# Shared witness bits and word probes between two SAT blocks

Let $A,B$ be subsets of $[D]$. Give two private Boolean formulas a common set of $k$ variables $z$. The first formula depends on $A$, the second on $B$, and their private variables are disjoint. With arbitrary private formulas, the least possible $k$ for a satisfiable conjunction exactly when $A\cap B\ne\varnothing$ is $\lceil\log_2 D\rceil$ for $D\ge1$. If a single bit on the second side can also request the opposite verdict, the least possible $k$ is $D$.

Each fixed assignment to the shared bits describes a rectangle of accepted pairs. The $D$ pairs of matching singletons require separate rectangles for intersection, and a common index attains that count. For the disjointness tag, use the $2^D$ pairs $(A,\bar A)$. For distinct $A,C$, at least one of $C\cap\bar A,A\cap\bar C$ is nonempty. Two such pairs cannot belong to one accepting rectangle. Sharing the $D$ bits of $A$ attains the bound. A local selector can be fixed by one physical bit using the two-name selector construction. These arguments count shared variables and allow arbitrary local formula size. Standard gate equivalences followed by proper ternary padding use only private auxiliaries. [Proper one-bit selector](a-sat-witness-before-one-semantic-continuation.md)

## Verifying a boundary assignment with probes

Represent $A$ by $m$ independent raw database words. The $j$th word carries $d_j$ bits of $A$, with $q_j=2^{d_j}$ possible contents and $D=\sum_jd_j$. Fixed bits in a physical word are free. A shared witness has $K$ possible values. On a proposed value, the first side can check any predicate of $A$. The second side knows $B$ and the tag, has unrestricted private guesses and computation, and reads at most $t$ *distinct original database words* on an accepting branch. Soundness and completeness are existential over the shared witness and private guesses. Work, witness description length on each private side, and formula size are uncharged. Related certificate models allow cell probes to certify a query after construction of a data structure. Here arbitrary extra data-dependent cells would change the theorem. [Wang and Yin, *Certificates in Data Structures*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5743)

Put
$$
 V_t=\sum_{S\subseteq[m],\,|S|\le t}\prod_{j\in S}(q_j-1).
$$
Assume $0\le t\le m$. Any verifier for the tagged relation obeys **$K V_t\ge 2^D$**. Exactly $V_t$ distinct databases can share one witness value that works on their complementary queries. A single deterministic class of that size admits answers on *every* query using $t$ probes.

For the lower bound, on each disjoint pair $(A,\bar A)$ choose a successful witness and a path. Fix a witness value and let $\mathcal C$ be the databases assigned to it. The path for $A$ checks a cylinder of at most $t$ equalities of raw words. Fix the private guesses and read replies on that path. Any $C\in\mathcal C$ in its cylinder gives the same accepting branch on $\bar A$ and therefore satisfies $C\subseteq A$. Order the databases by increasing cardinality. Evaluations of the chosen cylinder indicators give a triangular matrix on $\mathcal C$ with ones on the diagonal. Its rank is $|\mathcal C|$. For word $j$, the constant function and the point indicators for $q_j-1$ symbol values span all its point indicators. A product on at most $t$ words therefore lies in a space spanned by $V_t$ functions. This proves the bound, including for adaptive reads. Related private-point arguments appear in the literature on subcubes; the triangular matrix here permits containment by smaller databases. [Ellis, *Irredundant Families of Subcubes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.2960)

For equality in the single-class bound, take all databases in which at most $t$ words differ from the all-ones word in their variable slots. There are exactly $V_t$ such databases. If $B$ has set bits in more than $t$ words, an intersection is guaranteed. Otherwise read all words touched by $B$. This decides both tags and both verdicts.

The exact global minimum $K$ is the smallest number of classes covering all databases such that, for every member $A$ of a class, equality to $A$ on some $t$ or fewer cells forces any other class member matching there to be a subset of $A$. The lower-bound argument supplies such a partition for every verifier. Conversely, the shared witness selects a class containing the actual database. To prove disjointness the second side guesses a class member $A^*$ disjoint from $B$ and checks its specified cells. On a successful guess the real database is a subset of $A^*$, and guessing the real database always succeeds on a disjoint pair. To prove intersection one raw probe can verify a guessed common index. At $t=0$ the class condition forces singleton classes, so the second side already knows $A$. Overlaps in a covering can be resolved arbitrarily to make a partition.

For arbitrary cell alphabets, $\log_2 K$ differs from $D-\log_2 V_t$ by at most $O(\log(D+2))$. Choose $i\le t$ maximizing $E_i=\sum_{|S|=i}\prod_{j\in S}(q_j-1)$, so $E_i\ge V_t/(t+1)$. Every Hamming sphere of exact radius $i$ around a word-vector contains $E_i$ members, and each point belongs to $E_i$ such spheres. Independent uniform centers give a cover with at most $\lceil(1+D\ln2)2^D/E_i\rceil$ centers, since the expected number of missed points is less than one. Within a selected sphere the $i$ positions where a proposed database differs from the center uniquely determine it when their values are supplied. The second side can guess and verify an entire database, then compute either tag. This upper bound uses private nondeterminism. It does not give a deterministic fast method to find the exceptional words.

## Actual deterministic reads

Give a deterministic RAM the full query free of charge, all its ordinary instructions, unrestricted work space, and a database-dependent published summary of $b$ bits. Initially it has no other information about $A$ except its read-only word table. Call a database easy if every intersecting query costs at most $t$ distinct original database probes, or if every disjoint query costs at most $t$. Then
$$
                   \#\{A\text{ easy}\}\le 2^{b+1}V_t.
$$
On the easy databases add one bit to the summary indicating an easy verdict. Halt just before the $(t+1)$st distinct database probe and return the other verdict if the original program has not finished. The modified program is correct on the class assigned to each enlarged summary. The triangular bound applies to its complementary queries. Thus, outside the indicated exceptional sets, the *same* database has both an expensive positive and an expensive negative continuation. The two queries can differ.

There are compact SAT files for this statement. Use two private copies of the compact lookup block with the same free binary address, require the selected bit of each table to be one, and existentially quantify the address. On the compact construction's dimensions the encoded bit length is $N=\Theta(D)$, and every entry of the first table occupies one independent physical bit exactly once in a fixed layout. All other file bits depend only on the second table or are public. Partition the varying first-table bits according to their aligned RAM input words to get exactly the product alphabet above. Giving the second table for free lets a simulation reconstruct the rest of the file. Hence every *ordinary cold* correct deterministic SAT solver obeys the bound with $b=0$ on these files, counting distinct real input words carrying first-table bits. For length-$w$ words, $\log_2V_t\le tw+m$, with $m=O(D/w)$ on this layout for $w=\Theta(\log N)$. For example $t=\lfloor D/(2w)\rfloor$ gives hard continuations of both verdicts for all but a $2^{-\Omega(D)}$ fraction of databases. The case $b>0$ also holds at a checkpoint if its entire inherited database-specific information has at most $2^b$ states and all remaining such information is accessible only by probing the original words. A general RAM checkpoint can retain more information. A packed scan of the two raw tables decides the predicate; the earlier physical layout checker also handles the promised family within $O(N)$ time. [Compact physical lookup and size accounting](three-lookups-and-the-multilinear-sat-verdict.md)

The complementary queries illustrate a limit on forcing *adjacent* difficult executions. With just $\lceil\log_2(D+1)\rceil$ published bits for $|A|$, answer intersection immediately whenever $|B|+|A|>D$ and use a scan otherwise. Every positive one-bit neighbor of the complementary $B=\bar A$ is answered without a database probe. The disjointness proof can still force an expensive computation at the complement itself.

There is also an exact separation between finding and verifying raw cells. Restrict the database to a single set bit in one of $m$ designated cells. One private guess and one probe suffice to certify any answer. With $b$ bits of deterministic advice and no guesses, the optimal worst-case number of distinct probes for either polarity is
$$
                        \left\lfloor {\lceil m/2^b\rceil\over 2}\right\rfloor .
$$
Assign candidate positions as evenly as possible to advice classes. In a known class of size $g$, inspect the smaller of its positions inside and outside the query. For the reverse bound, choose a largest class and a query bisecting it as evenly as possible. Along the all-zero reply path there remain candidates on both sides until the program has tested at least $\lfloor g/2\rfloor$ positions. Both verdicts have executions needing that many probes when $g\ge2$. The two opposite databases then differ by moving their one set bit. The problem contains only $m$ possible databases and an unrestricted packed scan solves it. Large witness or snapshot counts alone do not supply a superlinear time induction.
