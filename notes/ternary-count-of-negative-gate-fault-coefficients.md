# Counting the negative gate-fault coefficients modulo three

Let the closed, locally indexed fan-in-two circuit at stage $j$ compute the genuine bit $b_j$, and let $f(r)$ be the sign of its output when a distinct residual bit flips each gate, constants included. Thus $f(0)=1-2b_j$. For each Fourier support $S$ write $c_S=\widehat f(S)$ with uniform normalized Walsh coefficients. Reduce dyadics in $\mathbb F_3$ by inverting their denominator. Define $N_-$ to be the **integer count** of sets $S$ with $c_S\equiv-1\pmod3$. A negative real coefficient and a coefficient with negative ternary residue are different tests. Then
$$
             |\{S:c_S\ne0\}|\equiv1,\qquad N_-\equiv b_j\pmod3 .
$$
In particular the negative count always has residue zero or one. An individual explicitly listed support can be tested in polynomial time in its list length and $j$ using only local wiring. Reducing the exact output probability of the fully buffered circuit at independent quarter-rate noise gives this same count modulo three. [Local quadratic coefficient algorithm](fourier-spectrum-of-gate-faults.md) · [Quarter-noise digit](modular-noisy-gate-evaluation.md)

Indeed each nonzero coefficient is a signed inverse power of two by the quadratic Walsh-sum calculation. Its square has ternary residue one. Parseval gives the first count. Write $N_+$ for the count of positive residues. Inversion gives $N_+-N_-\equiv f(0)$, whereas Parseval gives $N_++N_-\equiv1$. Subtraction proves the second claim. More generally at an arbitrary fault pattern $r$, the answer bit equals the number of nonzero coefficients whose ternary sign disagrees with $(-1)^{\langle S,r\rangle}$, reduced modulo three. The same argument applies to every Boolean sign function if its first count includes only coefficients that are nonzero *after* reduction. [Walsh inversion and Parseval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10386)

Buffers introduce no extra search choices for a nonzero coefficient. In a circuit buffered after every gate the Boolean function depends on the two residuals at each gate only through their parity. Switching both members of a pair cancels the computation and reverses a character that selects just one, giving coefficient zero. A union of complete pairs has exactly the original logical coefficient by grouping assignments. Thus the positive and negative counts are identical before and after buffering, even as integers. At the physical quarter-flip rate the mean sign is $\sum_S c_S4^{-|S|}$ in logical coordinates. All the weights in this last sum have residue one.

## An exact reduction by symmetries of the residual function

Suppose a finite group $G$ of order a power of three permutes the $m$ residual coordinates and preserves $f$ on every fault pattern. Let $h$ be its number of coordinate orbits and $g:\{0,1\}^h\to\{-1,1\}$ the restriction of $f$ in which residuals on each orbit are set equal. Then the two counts **separately**, for residue $+1$ and for $-1$, agree modulo three for $f$ and $g$. This gives a way to discard entire classes of supports before summing, without first computing $f(0)$.

Fourier coefficients are constant on the induced support orbits. A nontrivial orbit has size divisible by three. A fixed support is a union of coordinate orbits, indexed by $T\subseteq[h]$. In its unnormalized Walsh sum, permute the input patterns as well. Only patterns constant on each orbit contribute modulo three. Every coordinate orbit has odd size, so its repeated bits contribute the same inner-product parity as a single bit. Also $m\equiv h\pmod2$, hence $2^{-m}=2^{-h}$ in $\mathbb F_3$. The surviving coefficient is exactly $\widehat g(T)\bmod3$. This calculation does not require a quadratic form for $g$.

One concrete way to use the reduction is to have the action and an independently justified small circuit for its synchronized inputs. Given a truth table for $g$, a fast Walsh transform computes both counts in $O((h+1)2^h)$ field operations. Charge the cost of generating its table. Alternatively, if the quotient has the gate-fault form and a proved upper bound $d$ on the order of *all negative-residue* supports, the local $4^d\operatorname{poly}(d,j)$ bounded-order search suffices. Large positive coefficients need no enumeration under this hypothesis. A sparsity guess by itself gives no certificate for an omitted negative term.

There is a structural limit on applying the symmetry calculation to the native representation. Every finite group of three-power order acting by wiring automorphisms and fixing the output fixes every gate in its ancestor cone. At any fixed gate, the permutation action on its set of at most two parents is trivial by orbit sizes; recurse down the DAG. This also holds when gates allow exchanging their two ports. A semantic symmetry can be larger. For example the output of an XOR tree, with a residual on every internal gate, is the parity of all its residuals and its fixed noiseless answer; any permutation of those residuals preserves it. Finding a comparably effective semantic action on the actual nonlinear computation would require further information.

## Two opposite native pins with identical low-order signs and identical full power spectra

Start a disjoint flag with one constant gate $s$ whose value is the selected pin $t\in\{0,1\}$. Feed it through $L\ge1$ binary AND gates in series, at each gate using the preceding flag and a fresh constant-one gate as the two parents. XOR the final flag with the actual native core output at a new sink. Each gate has its own independent residual. With zero faults the outputs for the two circuits are $b_j$ and $1-b_j$. The added depth consists of genuine two-input nonlinear gates, and the added indexed cost is $2L+2$ logical gates. An indexable $L$ with $\log(L+1)=O(j)$ preserves polynomial-in-$j$ local access.

Flipping the public source pin is precisely the translation $r\mapsto r\oplus e_s$. It multiplies $c_S$ by $(-1)^{[s\in S]}$, so **all individual squared coefficients agree**, including the exponentially many high-order ones. If a nonzero coefficient contains $s$, its support must contain the whole directed path from $s$ to the sink: every gate in a nonzero support has a path to the output inside that support. This path has $L+2$ vertices. Hence all signed coefficients of orders $\le L+1$ agree. Under uniform faults the coupled paired outputs differ exactly when the $L$ fresh sibling bits are all one, independently with probability $1/2$ each. The XOR removes dependence of this difference on the arbitrary core. Therefore
$$
 \sum_S(\widehat f_0(S)-\widehat f_1(S))^2=4\,2^{-L},
 \qquad
 \sum_{S\ni s}\widehat f_0(S)^2=2^{-L}.
$$
In particular the number of differing real coefficients is $(-1)^L\bmod3$. Their ternary **signs** change the negative-count residue by one in one orientation and minus one in the other. Pair buffering doubles each surviving support size and preserves its coefficient. Low-order signed data, even augmented by the entire labelled power spectrum and exact tail mass, cannot determine the quarter-noise digit for both pins from those data alone. A procedure given the two netlists can inspect the source, exploit the public gadget or evaluate the core; this is a limitation on spectral truncation, not on all such procedures.

A balanced binary AND tree gives an adaptive query bound even for arbitrarily high-order coefficients. Use $n=2^a$ constant-one source gates as leaves, and XOR its output with the same native core. The alternative sets one hidden leaf to zero. Again its coefficient at $S$ is the baseline coefficient times $(-1)^{[i\in S]}$ for the hidden leaf $i$, and its entire labelled power spectrum is identical. Give an observer that spectrum for free and let it request exact signed coefficients by listing their gate names. On the all-one transcript, a deterministic observer using at most $q$ names in total mentions at most $q$ leaves. Every unmentioned choice has an identical transcript, by induction through the adaptive requests. Under equal priors for the baseline and a uniformly random changed leaf, even a randomized observer succeeds with probability at most $1/2+\min(q,n)/(2n)$, by fixing its coins and coupling the two transcripts. The outputs are complementary, and the AND tree has depth $a$. This lower bound charges the total lengths of explicit coefficient requests. Direct access to the source constants or succinct encodings of arbitrary support queries would give different observation models.

On the genuine stages, a fast exact evaluator of the signed count would report the actual native bit. The [native priority theorem](priority-pinned-sat.md) rules out a sound deterministic sign detector in $o(2^j)$ on infinitely many successful stages. A sound nondeterministic certificate costing $2^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}/(j+1)^C$ steps on infinitely many stages would exclude deterministic encoded SAT in $O(n^k)$ for sufficiently large fixed $C$ by the [bounded-acceptance reduction](packed-band-transfer.md). No requisite grouping, sparse negative tail or fast verifier for the actual circuit has been established.
