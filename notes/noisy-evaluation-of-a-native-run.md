# How accurately can we evaluate a noisy version of the actual gate computation?

Let a fixed, locally indexed, closed fan-in-two Boolean circuit at stage $j$ compute the [actual priority bit](priority-pinned-sat.md). Its gate identifiers have $O(j)$ bits, and querying a gate, its truth table and its predecessors costs $\operatorname{poly}(j)$. The known [gate-table construction](projected-parity-holes.md) has these properties. The noiseless execution costs $O(M)$ on the original RAM, $M=2^j$. We ask whether an evaluation at a non-Boolean point can give the noiseless sign early.

Add a separate residual bit $r_g$ to each gate, including sources, which flips its computed value. The noiseless point is $r=0$. At a table $y$ the corresponding residual is $r_g=y_g\oplus g(y_{\mathrm{parents}})$. This triangular map between full tables and residuals is bijective, by evaluating gates in order. Let $F_j(r)$ be the resulting output bit. At the common real point $a=(1-\rho)/2$ the canonical multilinear extension of $F_j$ is the output probability $p_j(\rho)$ under independent gate flips of probability $a$. This is a different polynomial from the indicator of one or two exceptional tables. Here $p_j(1)=b_j$. We obtain algorithms for the **actual value of this noisy polynomial** with access only to gates around the output. The missing step is authenticating an implication from its value to the noiseless sign.

## Bounding the error caused by cutting out most of the circuit

Use backward directed distance from the output. Keep the gates at distance less than $d$ and replace exterior values entering them by zero. The resulting noisy output probability is $p^{(d)}$. There are fewer than $2^d$ retained gates. For $0\le\rho\le1/\sqrt2$,
$$
 |p_j(\rho)-p^{(d)}|\le\sqrt{2\ln 2\, s_d},
 \qquad s_0=1,\quad s_{i+1}=\rho^2(2s_i-s_i^2).
$$
In particular $s_d\le(2\rho^2)^d$ and $s_d\le 2/(d+2)$. Shared gates are kept with their original identities. Replacing a shared gate by independently sampled copies could change the expectation.

For a proof, couple the two experiments by a uniform switch $X$. With $X=0$ compute the actual circuit, and with $X=1$ force every exterior gate to zero. Use independent gate noise in the interior in either case. Distinct branches of the dependency graph may reconverge, and an exterior gate can depend on an interior gate. The argument never conditions the whole interior on exterior values. Start from the set containing the output and remove its topologically latest gate $g$, retaining the other frontier variables $Z$. Their joint values and the parents of $g$ are independent of its fresh noise conditional on all the preceding gate values and $X$. The binary symmetric channel at $g$ satisfies
$$
 I(X;Z,g)\le(1-\rho^2) I(X;Z)+\rho^2 I(X;Z,\mathrm{parents}(g)).
$$
Indeed for the negative binary entropy $f(u)$ the second derivative is proportional to $1/[u(1-u)]$. The output bias parameter $a+\rho u$ is closer to $1/2$ than $u$, hence the second derivative of $f(a+\rho u)$ is at most $\rho^2 f''(u)$. The conditional Jensen gap contracts by $\rho^2$. Apply this given $Z$ and use data processing from the parent tuple to the noiseless gate. This is the Evans–Schulman information bound in the site-percolation formulation of Polyanskiy and Wu. [Evans and Schulman](https://users.cms.caltech.edu/~schulman/Papers/evansS99.pdf) · [Polyanskiy and Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06025)

Iterate this inequality, bounding mutual information by one bit at the first exterior gate and by zero at an empty frontier. The resulting bound is the probability of reaching the exterior when an interior gate exposes its parents with probability $\rho^2$. Processing the latest frontier gate ensures that it can never be added again. Equivalently draw independent site marks in advance and search the resulting open cluster. In breadth-first order its tree of first discoveries is dominated by a binary branching process with two children with probability $\rho^2$, otherwise none. A repeated or missing parent contributes no new node. Hitting any exterior gate requires depth at least $d$ in this tree. The survival probability to that depth is $s_d$. When $\rho^2\le1/2$, induction using the recurrence gives $s_d\le 2/(d+2)$. Finally the mutual information of a uniform switch is the average of the two divergences to the mixture. Pinsker's inequality gives $I(X;\text{out})\ge |p_j-p^{(d)}|^2/(2\ln 2)$ in bits.

An exact computation on the retained circuit enumerates its flip vectors once per gate, evaluating the DAG in topological order. For fixed rational $\rho$, or $\rho=1/\sqrt2$, the probability can be accumulated exactly using polynomial work per vector in the number of gates and $j$ (in the latter case in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$). Take $d=\lfloor(\log_2 j)/2\rfloor$. The time is $2^{O(\sqrt j)}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ and the certified additive error is $O(1/\sqrt{\log j})$ even at the endpoint. Below the endpoint the error instead decreases as a fixed negative power of $j$. The publicly computable error bound allows intervals, and only intervals that genuinely force an answer may be used as sound certificates.

## Faster sampling at a quarter flip rate

At $\rho=1/2$ there is also a randomized approximation to error $\eta$, with probability at least $5/6$, in worst-case time $\eta^{-2}\operatorname{poly}(j,\log(1/\eta))$ for $\log(1/\eta)=O(j)$. This holds at any circuit size covered by the indexing hypothesis, including shared gates. At each gate toss a fair coin: obey the gate on heads and return a fresh fair bit on tails. This implements flip probability $1/4$ conditional on the parents. Explore backwards and memoize each new gate so its noise is sampled only once; compute the output on the explored subgraph. The number $T$ of discovered gates is stochastically bounded by the total progeny of the critical binary process. A full ordered tree with $r$ branch vertices has $2r+1$ vertices and probability $2^{-(2r+1)}$. There are $\binom{2r}{r}/(r+1)$ such trees, so $\Pr(T>u)=O(u^{-1/2})$ and $\mathbb E\min(T,u)=O(\sqrt u)$. The majorizing process becomes extinct almost surely, also by the survival recurrence above. Balanced search trees on gate identifiers and a stack suffice for memoization within polynomial overhead in $j$ and $\log(1/\eta)$. [Total progeny and random walks](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-applied-probability/article/abs/total-progeny-in-a-branching-process-and-a-related-random-walk/B9505DF87F411C873D3AA511F21EBF8C)

For level $\ell$, let $Y_\ell$ be the true noisy output if $T\le 4^\ell$, and zero otherwise. Set $Y_{-1}=0$. A single capped exploration samples $D_\ell=Y_\ell-Y_{\ell-1}$ using the same choices for the pair. The variance is $O(2^{-\ell})$, since the difference vanishes unless $T>4^{\ell-1}$ for $\ell>0$. Expected work excluding indexing overhead is $O(2^\ell)$. Use levels $0\le\ell\le L$, $2^L$ the first power of two above $C/\eta$ for a sufficiently large absolute $C$. Take independently $\lceil C'(L+1)\eta^{-2}2^{-\ell}\rceil$ samples at each level. The sum of sample means has expectation $\mathbb E Y_L$, bias at most $\eta/4$, and variance at most an arbitrarily small fixed constant times $\eta^2$ by increasing $C'$. The expected work is $O((L+1)^2\eta^{-2}+2^L)$, in units of local gate operations. Chebyshev controls statistical error; aborting after a sufficiently large fixed multiple of the expected-work upper bound gives the claimed worst-case clock by Markov. Rounded sample means can be summed to error at most $\eta/8$ with $O(\log((L+1)/\eta))$ fractional bits. The sampling allocation is the standard multilevel method. [Giles, Multilevel Monte Carlo Path Simulation](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A0aa80ab7-9406-4778-8f1a-cdafc9c1ed9b)

## What would suffice for the unrestricted SAT clocks?

Here is one test with a concrete accuracy threshold on a noisy computation. Fix the circuit generator and one color $c$. At $\rho=1/2$, suppose $p_j(1/2)>1/2$ only on stages with $b_j=c$ (eventual soundness suffices after a finite hardwired cutoff). Let $\eta=2^{-\lceil\alpha j\rceil}$ for a fixed positive rational $\alpha$. If $p_j(1/2)\ge 1/2+4\eta$ infinitely often, these properties exclude deterministic $O(n^k)$ SAT whenever
$$
              2\alpha\min(2k^2,k^3)<1,\qquad k\in\{1,2,3\}.
$$
Indeed the sampler discriminates $p_j\le1/2$ from $p_j\ge1/2+4\eta$ at clock $T=2^{2\alpha j}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ with bounded error, including sampling, numerical and truncation costs. The [two- or three-block deterministic simulation under the SAT hypothesis](collision-forecasts-for-sat.md) separates the promised ranges in $T^{\min(2k^2,k^3)}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. Both cover arguments need density bounds only at the promised inputs; they define a fixed decision in the intervening gap. Any accepted gap stage still has the correct color by soundness. The resulting deterministic detector has infinitely many successes in $o(M)$, contradicting the native theorem. Thus the missing reliability property concerns the actual ideal output on individual stages. A better estimator for $p_j$ alone supplies no such implication.

Without the SAT assumption, the deterministic cut algorithm already shows that if $p_j(\rho)>1/2$ is sound for $c$ at $\rho=1/\sqrt2$, every sufficiently late positive excess over $1/2$ is $O(1/\sqrt{\log j})$. Otherwise the exact estimate minus a computable upper bound on its error would signal within $o(M)$. The same inference below that endpoint uses the sharper $s_d$. These are unconditional restrictions on a fixed locally indexed noisy encoding of the genuine bit, with no restriction on the original RAM workspace.

A different sufficient condition concerns cancellation between all Fourier terms of the same order. Put $B_j(\rho)=2p_j(\rho)-1$. The signed output has degree at most the number of gates along this scalar diagonal. Suppose a sound short description or an independently proved bound supplies a possibly much smaller **real scalar degree** $D$. For $D\ge1$, query $B_j$ at $\rho_i=\tfrac12\cos(i\pi/D)$, $0\le i\le D$. Lagrange interpolation at $1$ gives an exact linear statistic whose total absolute weight is $T_D(2)$, where $T_D$ is the Chebyshev polynomial. Indeed the weights alternate in sign in this decreasing order of nodes, and $T_D(2\rho_i)=(-1)^i$. Thus the weighted sum of these alternating values is simultaneously $T_D(2)$ and the sum of the absolute weights. This is the best possible norm among signed linear evaluation rules on the interval that are exact on all polynomials of degree at most $D$, by testing on $T_D(2\rho)$. Here $T_D(2)=((2+\sqrt3)^D+(2-\sqrt3)^D)/2$. The degree-zero case uses one observation.

The critical sampler works throughout this interval: obey the gate with probability $|\rho|$ (complement its result when $\rho<0$), otherwise resample a fair bit. A binary branching process with branching probability at most one half still dominates. To estimate the normalized interpolant, one may estimate each of the $D+1$ means to error $1/(8T_D(2))$ with simultaneous constant success probability, so its sign at one is recovered. When $D=O(j)$ all node values, weights and biased coins may be approximated with $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ bits. For example the barycentric weights at $x=2$ are proportional to $(-1)^i c_i/(2-\cos(i\pi/D))$, with $c_0=c_D=1/2$, $c_i=1$ otherwise. Normalize by their sum, retaining $O(D+\log(j+2))$ guard bits. Equivalently use products of node differences, whose inverses have bit length $O(D\log(D+2))$ for each interpolation weight computation. Couple the rounded coin sampler to the intended capped sampler, charging error probability at most the cap times the per-coin error. The per-level caps, including the amplification for simultaneous success, fit $T_D(2)^2\operatorname{poly}(j)$ worst-case work. A globally valid bound $D\le c j$ at every stage with $2c\log_2(2+\sqrt3)\min(2k^2,k^3)<1$ would therefore separate the chosen SAT clock. Merely having the degree bound on unrecognized stages does not validate the extrapolation there. [Chebyshev polynomials](https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.5) · [Exact low-order terms and the distinction from full Fourier degree](fourier-spectrum-of-gate-faults.md)

There is a specific obstruction to inferring color from bias alone. Append to the genuine output a fixed-height balanced tree of two-input AND gates, all of whose bottom input wires carry that same genuine output gate. In the absence of faults this preserves $b_j$. Conditional on the noisy value $z$ of the original output, the different branches use independent new noise. Each level in this *conditional* computation sends a Bernoulli success probability $u$ to $g(u)=a+\rho u^2$. Starting with $u=z$ gives the conditional output probability. For every fixed $0<\rho<1$, $g(0)>0$, $g(1)<1$, and $g(1/2)<1/2$. The smaller fixed point lies in $(0,1/2)$ and the other exceeds one; iteration from one decreases to the smaller point. A fixed height therefore makes the noisy success probability strictly below $1/2$ by a constant at **every stage**, including the infinitely many stages with true bit one. OR gates force the opposite bias in the same fashion. Even a locally indexed circuit computing the actual sign exactly can display either externally imposed noisy bias. A construction which certifies that its own bias is calibrated would be a separate mathematical advance.
