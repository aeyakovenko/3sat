# What a SAT decision trace can expose about a matrix product

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A fixed SAT solver can answer every rank-one checksum of a binary matrix product in $\widetilde O(n^2)$ time on explicit formulas. Even the complete execution transcript can be generated in that time without computing the product. A uniform method that recovered the entire product from one such trace, the formula and its public matrices in $\widetilde O(n^2)$ time would give a deterministic $\widetilde O(n^2)$ multiplication algorithm over $\mathbb F_2$. There is a corresponding distance-product example for weighted shortest paths. The solvers in this claim are genuinely correct on arbitrary encoded formulas. Their fast bound here concerns the specified family. Throughout, a tilde suppresses fixed logarithmic factors.

Under a near-linear SAT decision hypothesis one can go further. There are $\widetilde O(n^2)$-bit formulas whose **unique complete satisfying assignment** contains the full product. The same cheap checksum trace is then available from a solver that is fast on all inputs. Near-linear deterministic search on uniquely satisfiable formulas would then give near-quadratic weighted all-pairs shortest paths. For comparison the decision hypothesis alone gives $\widetilde O(n^{8/3})$ by a published negative-triangle reduction. These statements give a test for proposed witness-extraction arguments. They supply no unconditional time lower bound for explicit SAT.

## Ordinary SAT instances with a cheap bilinear decision

Take binary $n$-square matrices $A,B$ and Boolean column vectors $u,v$. Put $C=AB$ over $\mathbb F_2$. The verdict is the equality of a prescribed bit $p$ to
$$g(u,v)=u^\top C v=(u^\top A)(Bv).$$
On a logarithmic-word RAM the rightmost expression takes $O(n^2)$ steps by two matrix-vector multiplications and one dot product. It also has a uniform $O(n^2)$-gate XOR/AND circuit. Compile its **equality test** with ordinary Tseitin gate equivalences, fix the data, vectors and $p$ in unit clauses, and require success. Only the signs of the designated input pins vary at a fixed $n$. Every positive formula has a unique complete assignment to the inputs and intermediate wires. The ordinary encoded length is $\widetilde O(n^2)$. An accepting baseline always available without knowing $C$ is $u=v=0,p=0$. The auxiliary values in this first construction represent the scalar calculation. The formula need not contain $C$ as a designated witness.

A fixed program can recognize the family without a correctness promise. Decode the pins from their designated locations and compare the entire incoming formula against the regenerated fixed layout with those values. The dimension can be supplied in ordinary fixed header clauses, with their variables also pinned, or recovered from a strictly increasing padded length. Check that the declared dimension and size fit before regeneration. A mismatch goes to a general complete SAT algorithm. On a match, compute the displayed scalar and compare to $p$. This gives a globally correct solver with a $\widetilde O(n^2)$ real run on every family member, using exhaustive search as fallback if necessary. Its records, including accessed words, addresses and controllers, can be generated within the same soft bound. The statement does not rely on an oracle that hides the formula.

One rejected baseline can even have *exactly the same native trace for every pair of matrices* of a fixed dimension. Put two repeated-literal unit clauses on a fresh variable $z$ first. The first is positive. Make the second negative on $u=v=0,p=1$, and positive everywhere else. This syntactic adjustment preserves every verdict, since the exceptional query already asks whether $0=1$. Keep matrix pins outside the words containing this prefix. A global solver may immediately reject upon reading two contradictory unit clauses in the top-level conjunction. A malformed suffix is also rejected under the ordinary invalid-input convention. On this baseline it reads no matrix word, while the header and the contradictory pair are fixed. Transcript-only recovery on that particular call is impossible even with unlimited postprocessing. An extractor also given the matrices or the entire formula still has the computational problem below. The known positive baseline does not use this rejection shortcut.

A reconstruction proposed to work for **every** solver trace must work for this one. Simulate its run and hand the transcript and the actual pinned input to the reconstructor. The coefficients $g(e_i,e_j)$ it must return are all of $AB$. The reduction still costs $\widetilde O(n^2)$ for a polylogarithmic number of adaptive trace requests, counting formula construction and writing the traces. For the explicit arithmetic implementation the first formal derivatives at $(u,v)$ give $Cv$ and $u^\top C$. The mixed second derivatives give the entire $C$. Producing a row or column by the two matrix-vector operations accounts for the difference between these tasks. The matrix-product consequence here is a Boolean-work RAM computation over a fixed field. Its conclusion need not transfer to algebraic straight-line complexity over other fields. [Earlier parity trace and implicit matrix analysis](quadratic-trace-diagonals.md)

There is an exact information bound if the matrices and execution record are hidden. Choose $C$ uniformly from the $2^{n^2}$ binary matrices. With $b$ bits of arbitrary advice about $C$ and $q$ adaptive one-bit checksum answers, the success probability of returning $C$ is at most $\min(1,2^{b+q-n^2})$. For a fixed random tape there are at most $2^{b+q}$ transcripts and each outputs one matrix. The bound is attained by using advice on some entries and standard-basis queries on other entries, then filling unobserved entries with zeros. Every possible checksum matrix is realizable by choosing $A=I,B=C$. In the actual-trace reduction both matrices are public, so this query count yields no running-time lower bound for the reconstructor. Black-box witness-finding bounds likewise distinguish oracle answers from access to the formula itself. [Kawachi, Rossman and Watanabe on witness-finding queries](https://www.math.toronto.edu/rossman/witness.pdf)

## Every entry in a unique complete assignment

Suppose a fixed deterministic RAM $S$ decides ordinary SAT on $N$ encoded bits in $N\operatorname{polylog}N$ steps. Let $f(x)$ be a total string of a prescribed, efficiently computable length $m$, and suppose one specified bit of $f(x)$ can be computed deterministically in $T$ steps from $x$ and the bit index. Use a uniformly available budget for $T$. Put $K=|x|+m+T+2$. In $\widetilde O(K)$ deterministic time one can build a 3-CNF with $\widetilde O(K)$ encoded bits whose unique complete satisfying assignment has $f(x)$ in designated positions.

For any trial $y$ of the prescribed length, a mismatch is witnessed by an index $i<m$, one computation of $f(x)_i$, and the differing bit $y_i$. The bounded RAM-to-SAT compiler and then $S$ determine existence of a mismatch deterministically in $\widetilde O(K)$ time. The entire public $x,y$ is charged at this inner call. Consequently there is one fixed deterministic verifier of $y=f(x)$ on inputs $x,y$. Compile this verifier with a canonical deterministic-trace construction, fix just $x$, and leave the $y$ pins free. A valid run determines its chronological records, read replies and all inactive slot fields. A bitonic sorting circuit with fixed conventions for ties determines the address/time-ordered records used to check the reads. Deterministic gate equivalences, zero initialization and constrained padding give exactly one extension for every accepting $y$. A free-routing witness in a general equisatisfiable RAM compilation would need separate justification for uniqueness. Here only $y=f(x)$ is accepted, proving uniqueness of the entire assignment. Requiring in addition a deterministic predicate $J(x,y)$ computable in $\widetilde O(K)$ preserves this conclusion when it is true and gives an unsatisfiable formula otherwise. [Canonical deterministic transcripts](unique-checksum-witnesses.md) · [Packed RAM compilation](packed-band-transfer.md)

Apply this construction to $f(A,B)=AB$. One entry is a length-$n$ dot product. The public data and designated output occupy $O(n^2)$ bits. Add the test $u^\top yv=p$ to the outer verifier. Every positive instance now genuinely encodes $C$ in its unique assignment. The fixed enlarged syntax depends on the reference program $S$ and its clocks, but is still generatable in $\widetilde O(n^2)$ time uniformly in the public data. Recognize and specialize it by exactly the same comparison and scalar evaluation, with $S$ as fallback. This gives a globally near-linear solver whose run on the known positive baseline computes only that scalar and authenticates the layout. Recovering the witness from this run in near-linear time would in particular print $AB$ in near-quadratic time. This is a consequence about one actual run of a valid solver even when its public inputs and entire trace are available. It establishes no requirement that each individual solver actually multiplies the matrices to decide the baseline.

## Distance products and the decision-only benchmark

Let
$$C_{ij}=\min_k(A_{ik}+B_{kj})$$
with polynomially bounded integer entries or a distinguished $+\infty$. Use $O(\log n)$ bits per finite entry, increasing the fixed constant for sums and carrying infinity explicitly. One entry of $C$ takes $O(n)$ word steps. For selector potentials $u,v$ and target $\tau$ the decision predicate
$$
 \min_{ij}(u_i+C_{ij}+v_j)\le \tau
$$
can be evaluated without forming $C$. Compute $\min_i(u_i+A_{ik})$ and $\min_j(B_{kj}+v_j)$ for each $k$, add these two minima, and take the minimum over $k$. This uses $O(n^2)$ word steps. Potentials equal to zero at one chosen row and column and infinity elsewhere isolate an individual entry. All thresholds therefore characterize every $C_{ij}$. Alternatively allow the expected truth bit as another pin, giving known positive baselines. The uniform direct encoding and the recognition wrapper work as before using word comparisons, addition and a flag for infinity. Thus a promised scalar-family method that recovered all these threshold entries from a few actual decision traces in subcubic time would compute distance product in subcubic time, without a fast-SAT hypothesis.

Under the near-linear SAT hypothesis the unique-assignment construction places all of $C$ in a positive formula with $\widetilde O(n^2)$ encoded bits. Use a fixed canonical bit representation, including the payload for infinity. A general deterministic unique-SAT search method taking $O(N^{3/2-\epsilon})$ time for fixed $0<\epsilon<1/2$ would compute the distance product in $n^{3-2\epsilon}\operatorname{polylog}n$ time. The preprocessing and output cost $\widetilde O(n^2)$. On a weighted directed graph with no negative cycles, give each vertex a zero self-loop and encode missing edges by infinity. Repeated min-plus squaring gives all-pairs shortest paths after $\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ products, since a finite shortest path can be chosen simple. Each finite intermediate entry still has $O(\log n)$ bits for polynomially bounded input weights. This would contradict the usual *conjecture* of no truly subcubic weighted APSP algorithm. The matrix and shortest-path consequences leave both the conjecture and the unrestricted SAT question open. [Vassilevska Williams and Williams on path and matrix-product reductions](https://people.csail.mit.edu/virgi/tria-mmult-jv.pdf) · [Min-plus product and the APSP hypothesis](https://people.csail.mit.edu/virgi/trueminplus.pdf)

In fact near-linear SAT **alone** already gives a truly subcubic distance product by an existing reduction. A negative triangle in three input weight arrays has a certificate consisting of its three vertex indices. Look up its three weights and test their sum. Compiling with the $\widetilde O(n^2)$ public weight bits gives a deterministic $T(n)=\widetilde O(n^2)$ negative-triangle decision algorithm under the SAT hypothesis. Vassilevska Williams and Williams, Theorem 4.2, compute distance product in $O(n^2 T(n^{1/3})\log W)$ time, where $W$ bounds the integer magnitudes. This gives $\widetilde O(n^{8/3})$ and already conflicts with the usual APSP conjecture. The stricter conclusion of a near-linear *unique-search* transfer is $\widetilde O(n^2)$, and a search bound $O(N^{4/3-\epsilon})$ would improve the $n^{8/3}$ benchmark of this particular decision-only reduction. The comparison proves no optimality for that benchmark. [Negative-triangle to matrix-product reduction, Theorem 4.2](https://people.csail.mit.edu/virgi/tria-mmult-jv.pdf)

The trace reductions specify what a successful general extraction theorem must deliver without separating search from decision under the SAT hypothesis. I have not established priority for these particular deterministic unique-witness and actual-trace formulations.
