# A linear-time RAM solver for dense 3-SAT with missing triples

Let \(F\) be an ordinary proper ASCII DIMACS 3-CNF, \(N\) its encoded bit length, and \(v\) its number of distinct occurring variables. Let \(H\) be the hypergraph of distinct three-variable clause supports, ignoring signs and multiplicities. Write \(E\) for the number of vertex subsets of size at least three containing **no entire edge** of \(H\). For every fixed \(C\), one deterministic logarithmic-word RAM decides \(F\) and lists all its models on the occurring variables in \(O_C(N)\) native steps whenever \(E\le C v^2\). The procedure can recognize the condition and fall back to a general SAT solver outside it. A common name dictionary followed by one bit vector per model is a permitted output format.

The complete case \(E=0\) has a deterministic prefix algorithm reusing repeated states on pairs of dyadic intervals. An arbitrary fixed coordinate order can concentrate missing triples so that its per-level projection bound ceases to give the claimed clock. We construct the order from the input. Subsets counted by \(E\) may have more than three elements. Their maximum size can grow with \(v\). ## A weighted list of triples

Parse the input, intern decimal identifiers, normalize the literal order within each clause, and count distinct supports. Multiple signs on one support give one mask of forbidden assignments. There are at most \(C v^2\) missing triples on the promise. For a fixed \(C\), first check whether even the physical clause count can be as large as \(\binom v3-Cv^2\). Otherwise fall back before allocating any cubic array. Once this check passes, \(v^3=O_C(N)\). Allocate the direct support table and count missing triples. If more than \(Cv^2\) are absent, the promise fails. When at most \(Cv^2\) triples are missing, the spelling itself costs \(N=\Omega_C(v^3\log v)\) for large \(v\). Indeed, a positive fraction of the \(v\) distinct names have \(\Omega(\log v)\) digits, and in the complete hypergraph each appears \(\binom{v-1}{2}\) times. Removing the missing supports deletes at most \(3Cv^2\) of those occurrences. This latter bound also pays for testing the rest of the promise on dense inputs that fail it.

Enumerate the independent sets in increasing coordinate order by depth-first search. When \(S\) is the current set, a counter on each possible extension \(z\) records the number of pairs in \(S\) forming a present triple together with \(z\). Choose only a larger \(x\) whose counter vanishes. For each \(y\in S\) and each \(z>x\), increment the counter if \(\{y,x,z\}\) is present. Undo these updates on return. There are \(1+v+\binom v2+E\) nodes. The scan and updates for an accepted child cost \(O(v(1+|S|))\) steps. On a promised instance every independent \(s\)-set has \(2^s\) independent subsets, giving \(s=O_C(\log v)\). For a linear-time recognizer outside the promise, abort before visiting more than the allowed number of nodes or reaching a size \(s\) with \(2^s>1+v+\binom v2+Cv^2\). Both cutoffs are sound. The total preprocessing so far costs \(O_C(N)\). Assign each enumerated set of size at least three to its three least original coordinates. This gives a nonnegative integer weight on each such representative triple, of total weight \(E\).

## Choosing a coordinate order deterministically

Let \(p=2^h\) be the smallest power of two at least \(v\), and label the original variables by distinct elements of \(\mathbb F_{2^h}\) in a polynomial basis. For each nonzero \(a\), place variable \(x\) at the integer position coding \(ax\). All other positions in \([0,p)\) are fixed zero, including gaps between real variables. Keeping the gaps preserves the dyadic alignment.

At a level with block size \(\ell\), let \(b=p/\ell\). A representative triple occurs inside the union of exactly \(2b-1\), two, or zero ordered block pairs according as its variables occupy one, two, or three different blocks. For any two distinct field labels \(x,y\), their placed positions have the same high bits exactly when the code of \(a(x-y)\) has all those bits zero. As \(a\) runs over the nonzero field elements, this happens on exactly \(\ell-1\) of the \(p-1\) choices. Thus the average contribution of a triple at any fixed level is at most
\[
       (2b-1)\frac{\ell-1}{p-1}+2\le4 .
\]
The inequality uses only a pair of its labels to upper-bound the event that all three share a block. It holds at the root and the leaves as well. Some \(a\) has total weighted contribution over all levels at most \(4E(h+1)\).

Choose the minimum by evaluating the scores for all \(p-1\) multipliers and all levels using the weighted list. This costs \(O(vE\log v+v^2\operatorname{polylog}v)\). It suffices to loop over representatives of positive weight. A field modulus can be found by enumerating binary degree-\(h\) monic candidates and trial division by monic polynomials of degrees one through \(\lfloor h/2\rfloor\), in \(O(v^{3/2}\operatorname{polylog}v)\) bit operations. The tables of \(ax\) require only \(O(v^2\operatorname{polylog}v)\) further steps by ordinary shift-and-XOR arithmetic. Scores, weights and addresses have logarithmically many bits on the promise. Handle bounded small \(v\) directly.

## Paying for all the prefix checks

At a stage keep exactly the earlier assignments satisfying every earlier clause, with remaining and dummy coordinates still zero. For a fixed union \(U\) of two dyadic blocks of size \(\ell\), the number of distinct projection patterns is at most the number of independent subsets of \(H\) contained in \(U\). The elementary form of the Sauer inequality gives \(|\mathcal A|\le|\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal A)|\) for any binary family. Split by one coordinate. Sets shattered by the union of the two slices contribute at the old coordinates, and sets shattered by their intersection contribute again with the new coordinate present. This proves the inequality by induction. A shattered subset here must use only earlier real coordinates, and cannot contain all three variables of any present clause because that clause's forbidden signing has no satisfying extension in the prefix.

For subsets of size zero through two, summing over the \(b^2\) ordered block pairs costs \(O(b^2(1+\ell^2))=O(v^2)\) per level. If a larger independent subset is contained in a union, its representative is in that union. The weighted score therefore bounds the rest simultaneously over **all** levels at that stage. There are \(O((v^2+E)\log v)\) distinct states to visit during any one extension step. The number of prefix assignments is at most \(1+v+\binom v2+E\), by the same argument on the entire coordinate set.

Construct the corresponding rectangle graphs top down by fixed-radix sorting of constant-size identifier tuples, evaluate them bottom up, and update the surviving assignment trees as in the complete algorithm. A terminal check for a missing triple rejects neither possible next bit. Skip extension at dummy positions, retaining their forced zero bits. These operations cost \(O_C(v^2\log v)\) per stage, even when the chosen multiplier intersperses dummy positions. Fewer than \(2v\) positions are needed. Recovering \(O_C(v^2)\) full models and unpermuting each costs \(O_C(v^3)\). This proves the encoded bound.

For an example outside every fixed independence-number promise, omit all triples lying entirely within a set of size \(\lfloor 2\log_2 v\rfloor\) and include all other triples. The only independent subsets of size at least three lie inside that exceptional set. Their total is at most \(v^2\), regardless of the signs on the included triples. The algorithm also allows overlapping collections of missing triples without requiring a decomposition supplied with the input. The result gives a tested obstacle to an exhaustion proof that stays within this class. Sparse general SAT instances can have exponentially many independent subsets.

## A fixed coordinate order can take superlinear time on the same promise

For every order fixed at length \(v=2^h\), place \(t=2h\) exceptional variables first, for \(v\) large enough. Omit just the triples wholly in this set \(S\). On every remaining triple use the clause with all three literals negative. Here \(E=2^t-1-t-\binom t2\le v^2\). Every choice of bits on \(S\) with zeros elsewhere extends to a model. In particular all \(2^t=v^2\) different patterns survive in every late prefix.

At block size \(\ell=2^{\lceil\log_2 t\rceil}\le 4h\), the first block contains all of \(S\). Take this block as the first member of an ordered pair and any *later* block fully inside the first half of the variables as its second member. Their positions can contribute actual increasing coordinate pairs at any stage in the last half. At each such pair the constructed models have \(v^2\) distinct interval-pattern keys. The unpermuted shared-rectangle algorithm therefore processes \(\Omega(v^3/\log v)\) keys at each of \(v/2\) stages, or \(\Omega(v^4/\log v)\) keys in total. This lower bound counts actual distinct states of that enumeration algorithm. On dense consecutive decimal names the file has \(\Theta(v^3\log v)\) bits, and the input-dependent ordering proved above gives one fixed solver taking \(O(N)\) on all these files. The quantifier over fixed orders matters in a size-successor argument.

The finite audit enumerates all independent subsets independently on 84 support systems, estimates every multiplier's score, preserves every padded gap and compares the solver's output with the full truth table. It checked 3,549 independent sets and 175,810 shared rectangle states. These are correctness checks on small instances. The asymptotic proof is in the manuscript.
