# What a monomial substitution retains of the permanent lower bound

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** I tried to transfer the arithmetic lower bound by replacing each matrix entry with a signed power of an indeterminate. A tour is then a witness for its total weight, so a Boolean SAT solver could decide whether a prescribed weight occurs. The approach encounters a more basic obstacle: even the exact signed coefficient at a given weight admits a polynomial-depth, nonuniform decision tree over ordered real weights, unconditionally. All ties are included. This is an application of the point-location theorem of Kane, Lovett and Moran. The known arithmetic lower bound concerns a different computation on the matrix entries. [Kane, Lovett and Moran, Corollary 1.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.01720) · [Arithmetic theorem and formalization](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/vp-vnp) · [Permanent graph argument in PR 249](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/50e44b55f7f10adf6c8aa8f167b31dd5b23c2171/research/bss-permanent-graph/PROOF.md)

Take $m\ge2$ and $F=\operatorname{per}_m$ or the directed Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial. Let $\mathcal T$ be its family of permutation supports, each of size $m$. The input consists of weights $w_e$, signs $\epsilon_e\in\{-1,0,1\}$ and a target exponent $s$. The required answer is

$$
A(w,\epsilon,s)=\sum_{C\in\mathcal T:\ \sum_{e\in C}w_e=s}\ \prod_{e\in C}\epsilon_e.
$$

For integer exponents this is the coefficient of $t^s$ after substituting $x_e=\epsilon_e t^{w_e}$. The displayed finite sum also makes sense for arbitrary real weights. It permits cancellation between terms of the same weight. In the Hamiltonian case taking $w_e=s=0$ and $\epsilon_e$ the edge indicators gives the exact number of directed Hamiltonian cycles in the input graph, with cycles represented by cyclic permutations.

For each tour form the vector $h_C=(\mathbf1_C,-1)$ and put $H=\{h_C:C\in\mathcal T\}$. Compute the signs of all $\langle h_C,(w,s)\rangle$ by the deterministic comparison tree of Corollary 1.9. Its dimension is $m^2+1$, the maximum coordinate magnitude is one, and $\log |H|\le m\log m$. Its query depth is therefore $O(m^3\log^2 m)$. Queries test one such form or a difference of two such forms. Each uses at most $2m+2$ nonzero signed coordinates, so expanding each query into real additions, subtractions and a sign test gives depth $O(m^4\log^2 m)$. Reading the finite alphabet of input signs costs $O(m^2)$ further tests. At a leaf the tree has determined both the set of tied tours and every edge sign. Label that leaf by their signed count, a fixed integer in $[-m!,m!]$ using $O(m\log m)$ bits. Labels may be hardwired or printed at that additional cost. Each tour is represented by its edge set and each permutation monomial has coefficient one. [Definitions of comparison queries and the point-location bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.01720)

This argument pays for operations along a path. Constructing the tree and assigning its potentially exponentially many leaves is outside that cost. In particular the all-zero-weight example leaves a genuine counting problem for a uniform implementation. With arbitrary scalar amplitudes $a_e$ in place of the finite signs, all-zero weights and $s=0$ give $F(a)$ itself. Hardwiring the coefficient after reading the signs then ceases to apply. A route from linear SAT through weight comparisons still needs a uniform conversion from a Boolean decision procedure to an arithmetic circuit handling these amplitudes, or a proof that another property of the procedure suffices. Polynomial-depth comparisons of weights, even with exact counting labels, give no such conversion.
