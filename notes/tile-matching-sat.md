# An optimal single matching for the high-cut-rank SAT family

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** On the explicit Margulis–Gabber–Galil complement-quadratic family, a single globally correct word-RAM SAT solver has a fast path with the following properties on *every* assignment of the independent signs.

* It reads each of the $2m$ private sign sites exactly once, in a public order.
* Its only work-array accesses are $O(m/\log m)$ stores to distinct fresh addresses, followed by precisely one load from each of those addresses. Every stored word can be required to be nonzero.
* The fast path takes $O(m)$ native steps for $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ bits. All store-to-load pairs can cross the same aligned midpoint in native time.

This upper bound is on the family used to force retrieval from many disjoint discovery epochs in every solver. Thus the lower bound on the number of epochs at logarithmic width is sharp up to constants, even with the additional demand that all witness reads be distinct. A hierarchy argument would need to force where the information is consumed, in addition to where it originated. ## Square tiles keep the quotient degree bounded

On the core grid of side $l$, partition each coordinate into intervals of length $b$, with a possible shorter last interval. Take $b$ to be the largest power of two with $b^2+1\le w$, where the native width is $w=\Theta(\log N)$. Thus a square tile's $b^2$ signs and a marker fit in one word. For large $m$ there are $O(m/b^2)=O(m/\log m)$ tiles, including the boundary tiles. Pad missing cells within a tile with zero.

For any of the horizontal shear neighbor maps $(i,j)\mapsto(i\pm(2j+\delta),j)$, $\delta\in\{0,1\}$, an input tile retains its $j$-interval. Its possible unreduced first coordinates occupy at most $3b$ consecutive integers. For sufficiently large $m$ we have $3b<l$. Reduction modulo $l$ splits this interval into at most two ordinary intervals. Each piece meets at most five coordinate blocks since any proper interior block has length $b$. Hence each shear reaches at most ten tiles. The vertical shears obey the same bound. For a tile $A$ let $L(A)$ consist of itself and the tiles reached by the eight maps. The lists are symmetric because the maps occur with their inverses, and their cardinality is at most $D=81$. By enumerating a tile's at most $8b^2$ images and keeping a sorted list with repetitions removed in $D$ registers, the solver computes $L(A)$ and any desired list position in $O(b^2)$ operations. All of these are public calculations; the uniform program can use a fixed number of registers.

## Acquire the signs, then evaluate the edges

In the first phase, scan tiles, and within a tile acquire all its sign bits in registers. For each discovery of a bit $x$ update pair parity $e\gets e\oplus px$, $p\gets p\oplus x$. After the last sign, $e$ equals the sum modulo two of all pairwise products, irrespective of discovery order. The $O(\sqrt m)$ isolated sites outside the grid also contribute to these two registers and require no tile storage. Build the marked bit-packed word for tile $A$. For every $B\in L(A)$ store a separate copy at address $D\,\mathrm{id}(A)+\mathrm{position}_{L(A)}(B)$, with zero-based identifiers. Every such address is fresh. No work loads are performed.

In phase two, for each destination tile $B$ load its own packet, retaining it in registers. Take one load for each $A\in L(B)\setminus\{B\}$ to get the copy addressed to $B$. Symmetry ensures all these copies exist and every stored copy is consumed exactly once. For each $A$, including $A=B$, enumerate source vertices in $B$. Add the product at every simple edge with smaller global endpoint there and larger endpoint in $A$. Both relevant bits are in registers. Remove self-loops and duplicate endpoint names within the at most eight neighbors of any vertex. All sparse products are now included once. XOR their parity with $e$ to get the complement quadratic. There are only $O(1)$ neighbors per tile and $O(b^2)$ native steps per ordered tile pair, including address recomputation. This proves the time and work-access bounds.

Use the fixed layout from the expander construction with eight public gate slots per vertex, a regular pin bank and exactly one private sign occurrence per site. For strict width three, use a literal together with two publicly fixed false literals. Fix their public pins independently of private data. A fixed amount of padding between private sign sites suffices for the specified word width. On visiting a site the solver checks every public field in its sign-containing native input word against the expected local pin-bank syntax. The expected word takes constant word operations to compute in this regular bank. A separate public scan checks all remaining input words and the end of input, skipping those sign-containing words by their public addresses. Sequential generation of the gate slots uses the eight neighbor maps and public wire names in $O(m)$ total native steps. This validation needs only fixed registers. A format failure takes a full SAT fallback, so on all ordinary CNFs the combined solver is correct.

The two phases have a uniform $Cm$ word-step upper bound on this family for a constant $C$. Pad each phase to the same power of two $H\ge Cm$ comparable to $m$, including setup and validation. This can equivalently be done by a fixed power-of-two slot budget per padded loop index with a public clock counter in a register. The midpoint between them separates every store from its load. Therefore every pair whose time intervals are charged at their dyadic least common ancestor is charged at the root. In particular several discovery widths may have many separately charged source epochs while only one native-time level has any store-to-load transfers at all. For a solver that never rereads private pins, the disjoint-epoch lower bound on the number of genuine loads also applies at logarithmic width. The family permits both strong rank across *every* balanced cut and short geometric tile lists.
