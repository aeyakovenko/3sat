# Erasing private signs in a scan with an arbitrary fixed odd stride

Fix a nonzero odd integer $d$, a $w$-bit word, $Q=2^w$, and $S(p)=p+d\pmod Q$. A regular stage at $p$ reads $x=M[p]$, writes $M[x]\gets S(p)$ and advances to $S(p)$. The starting cursor may depend on the length and on a bounded prologue. The regular write uses the value latched by its stage read, including when an extra operation intervenes. The controller may compute between stages and may halt early. Count each skipped regular write and each extra word read or write as one exception. It must follow the public cursor order for the first two periods or until it halts. After two periods it may continue with arbitrary RAM instructions, for arbitrarily long.

**Theorem.** For infinitely many encoded lengths $n$ there is a family of proper ASCII 3-CNFs encoding equality of $k=2^{\lfloor(\log_2 n)/16\rfloor}$ bits per party. If the machine decides this family, then
$$
 k\ \le\ s+O((b+1)w).
$$
Here $b$ is its maximum exception count through the first two periods or halt, and $s$ bounds the number of bits encoding the entire controller state at the public cut specified below, on executions reaching that cut. The cut is a stage boundary in the first period. The controller has no separately visible elapsed clock or other uncounted state. In particular a fixed register controller on this schedule, with no exceptions, cannot decide all DIMACS SAT at any speed. A bounded exit path involving memory counts as a bounded number of extra probes before a halt. The theorem concerns the memory interface on a prefix of a run. A general RAM may access different cells on every instruction. ## A pair of private lists in scan order

Use the journal's $w=16\lceil D\log_2(n+2)/16\rceil$, with fixed $D\ge1$, and lengths divisible by $w$. There are infinitely many such lengths since each constant-width interval eventually has length greater than $w$. The file occupies $m=n/w=o(Q)$ words. Pack bytes little-endian. The two possible sign words $U_0,U_1$ consist of spaces with final byte respectively a space or a minus. The physically next word begins with the decimal name and the bytes for the rest of a clause, so the minus touches the first digit. On the chosen files every original physical word has top byte at least 9; the input addresses lie below all those word values at sufficiently large lengths.

Use public variables 2 and 3 forced false by the eight clauses $(\neg x_j\lor\pm x_6\lor\pm x_7)$ for $j=2,3$ and all four sign pairs. Each party's list contains one clause $(\pm x_j\lor x_2\lor x_3)$ for each $j\in\{1\}\cup\{8,\ldots,k+6\}$, followed in chronological order by two public clauses with positive $x_4$ and negative $x_5$. Each of these two clauses occurs once per list. Hence the header declares $k+6$ variables and $2k+12$ clauses. Every clause uses three distinct names. The two lists have a common satisfying assignment exactly when their sign strings agree. Put the header and the eight forcing clauses at the beginning; fill unused space between complete clauses with whitespace.

In the low physical body choose a stretch of consecutive scan stages, away from the starting cursor, without physical wraparound. Place the sign sites along it in the order Alice's list, Alice's public clauses, Bob's list, Bob's public clauses. For instance take successive sign sites eight stages apart. The occupied physical span is $O(|d|k)=o(m)$; this works for either sign of $d$. Each sign's decimal suffix goes at the next *physical* address even when the scan moves backwards. A fixed bounded prologue can first be simulated with a blank body and the final public header. Avoid its finitely many read and write addresses, the low addresses occurring as its memory values, and the resulting initial cursor when choosing the stretch. The resulting files produce exactly that prologue transcript. The bounded set of exclusions leaves a physical interval of length $\Omega_P(m)$, enough also to avoid overlap with the public prefix. A prologue already returning a verdict fails on one of the completions.

For each private sign site $i$, put a distinct public word $B_i$ at its scan predecessor $i-d$. Choose $B_i$ so that:

* $B_i$ is an initial memory value only at $i-d$. The address $B_i-d$ is absent from the initial memory image.
* $B_i$ and $B_i-d$ are high addresses initially containing zero. They are distinct from the hubs $U_0,U_1$ and from all prologue exceptions. The sets $\{B_i,B_i-d\}$ are disjoint between sites.
* $i$ and $i-d$ are absent from the initial image. The marker address $i-d$ is separate from every other private site.

Here the initial image includes the common changes made by the prologue and the zero outside the file. For $d\ne-1$ the marker can be entirely whitespace. For $d=-1$ it is the physical clause suffix, whose trailing whitespace supplies the choice. Reserve a final newline in the marker's top byte. Even with a fixed decimal prefix, at least $w/8-\log_{10}(k+7)-O(1)$ byte positions can freely be space or newline. Their number of settings is $\omega(k)$, since $w\ge\log_2 n$ and $\log_2 k\le(\log_2 n)/16$. Choose greedily, excluding the $O(k)$ already used or fixed words and their bounded number of required translates. Before choosing any markers exclude all ordinary non-marker values, including both sign variants. These choices also work if the initial cursor is high. The ordered physical word layout continues to be valid DIMACS in either direction. Define the communication cut immediately after Alice's two public clauses in scan order, before the first Bob sign.

## When the original sign is lost

Call site $i$ untouched by exceptions if no exception during the prefix through two periods or halt has a stage cursor, memory address, or written payload in
$$
 F_i=\{i,\ i-d,\ B_i,\ B_i-d\}.
$$
A skipped write has only its stage cursor for this definition. For an extra operation at a boundary use the adjoining stage at which it is logged. The sets have bounded overlap by construction, so at most $O(b)$ sites fail this condition. The following argument applies to each untouched site in any run that reaches the relevant stage.

A low address $a$ absent from the initial image cannot be a regular write destination before its first regular read, provided an extra write has neither supplied the value $a$ nor targeted $a$. Indeed a regular store to $a$ needs a read of $a$ as a *value*. Its sole regular producer is the preceding stage $a-d$, whose payload is $a$. In the first lap that producer either has not executed, or lies immediately before the regular read of address $a$, leaving no intervening regular read to fetch its payload. This argument also works for a high address absent from the initial image. In particular the scan obtains $B_i$ at its first visit to $i-d$ and writes $i$ at address $B_i$. The first visit to $i$ reads its original sign.

We check the stamp at $B=B_i$ until the next scan visit there. The only initial source of the word $B$ is at $i-d$, which the scan will not revisit during this open interval. The only regular producer of a new word $B$ is the stage $B-d$. Its occurrence immediately before the target read at $B$ cannot introduce an intervening read of $B$ as a value. There is one wraparound case: $B$ may have been visited once already before the stamping stage. If its predecessor $B-d$ ran on that earlier visit, then $B-d$ read its original zero by the preceding first-visit argument. The earlier read at $B$ also returned zero: the original source at $i-d$ had not yet been seen and there was no intervening normal read after the producer. The producer had put $B$ at address zero, and the very next regular store there replaced it by $S(B)$. If the scan began at $B$, there was no such earlier producer. In either case there is no surviving additional readable source of $B$ from the earlier turn. Extra operations cannot copy the value $B$ to another cell or directly alter the footprint of this site without charging it. Thus the stamp survives: the next regular read at $B$ yields $i$ and writes $S(B)$ to address $i$. This write occurs strictly before the second regular read at $i$, and during the first two periods.

## A deterministic equality protocol

Alice simulates the machine through the cut, or through an earlier halt. She knows the initial memory except for Bob's sign choices. No regular stage of the prefix visits a Bob sign address. For an extra read of an as-yet unresolved Bob sign she can send its address and receive one bit. Earlier writes can be kept locally. Alice sends a halt flag and verdict or the following data:

1. The $s$-bit controller state and the actual contents of the two hub addresses $U_0,U_1$.
2. An ordered log through the cut of all exceptions, including stage and phase, address, and actual written word where applicable. The stage has at most $2Q$ possibilities through two periods. Multiple exceptions at one phase stay in their actual order. A self-delimiting stream with a constant number of tags per record suffices.

Bob reconstructs the compulsory prefix writes from the initial memory with dummy Alice signs and his real signs, applying the logged stores and skips. The reconstruction need only replay memory; Alice supplied the real controller state. Before the first Alice sign the actual and reconstructed memories can differ only at original Alice sign sites. From that sign until the cut, all regular reads are on the low stretch and do not visit a hub. At a differing sign the two possible writes go to the two hubs; any common store has exactly the same effect on both memories. Logged stores have identical actual destinations and values in both replays. By induction the only possible disagreements at the cut are the hubs and Alice sign cells that still hold their original contents. Restore the transmitted hub values. Bob can track a symbolic unresolved original for each remaining such site.

Bob continues the actual controller. A store to an unresolved cell removes its uncertainty. A read of one can be answered by asking Alice for the bit, which Bob caches. At most $b$ extra reads require such queries. At an ordinary read, a surviving unknown Alice sign must belong to a charged site: each Alice site has already had its first regular read, and every untouched one is erased before its second. At the end of the second period, if reached, every still-unknown site is likewise charged. Bob requests the remaining unknowns, giving him the exact entire RAM for any subsequent computation. The total number of additional requests is $O(b)$; both parties know when a query or the terminal message ends. If $b>k$ the asserted inequality is immediate. Otherwise the entire deterministic protocol, including directions and variable-length records, costs at most $s+O((b+1)w)$ bits in the worst case.

On the $2^k$ diagonal inputs $(u,u)$, a correct deterministic equality protocol needs different accepting transcripts. If two had the same transcript, the rectangle property would give that transcript on $(u,v)$ too. A binary protocol with maximum length $C$ has at most $2^C$ leaves after making the output public. This proves the bound. It uses the labels of the files and the destruction of the *original* signs. Other memory locations may keep changing for many laps. ## Checks and scope

The [signed-stride audit](../audits/shifted_stride_erasure_audit.py) compares compressed simulation to 1,440 direct two-lap evolutions and tests four proper files for each of 248 choices of width, signed stride and starting point, including starts next to marker and hub addresses. In all 248 cases the four memories coalesced by the second lap, and in 71 by the first. Those are finite tests of the compulsory schedule. The [Lean proof of local erasure](../lean/ShiftedStrideOrigin.lean) verifies the early pair of writes to zero, survival of the stamp across an arbitrary list of safe events, and the final erasing store. The proof that the DIMACS layout supplies the hypotheses and the many-bit communication bound are manuscript proofs. The construction makes no claim about the computational cost of SAT when the cursor and the memory interface are unrestricted.
