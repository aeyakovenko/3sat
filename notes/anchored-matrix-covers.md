# Approximating arbitrary binary matrices with input-dependent column and row spaces

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** I obtained two upper bounds on the Hamming distance of an arbitrary rectangular binary matrix from bounded rank. Both improve the fixed column code used in the [lexicographic SAT test](lex-factor-responses.md). In the concrete test let $r=\lfloor J/16\rfloor$ and $E=\lfloor J^2/16\rfloor$.

* Every $J$-square matrix supported on its last $\lfloor0.2816J\rfloor$ rows has, for all sufficiently large $J$, a uniform deterministic procedure producing factors of inner dimension at most $r$ within $E$ edits. Its cost is $O(J^{\log_2 7}\mathrm{polylog}\,J)$. The earlier fixed-column-code limit was about $0.27522J$ rows.
* Allowing unbounded search for the factors, **all** matrices supported on their last $\lfloor0.2881J\rfloor$ rows have such factors. The proof allows an input-dependent set of generator columns to change sparsely while their span still approximates the other columns at the binary covering-code rate.
* Consequently the lexicographically first matrix farther than $E$ from rank $r$ must have at least $(0.2881-o(1))J$ nonzero rows. A further three-column construction raises the lower endpoint to $0.2883$. Counting gives at most $(0.298047+o(1))J$ nonzero rows. [Three columns per added generator](triple-anchor-cover.md) These bounds describe the mathematically defined matrix unconditionally. The finite-candidate program's agreement with it remains conditional on the hypothesized SAT clock.

The response procedure would accelerate the quadratic and cubic tests if it worked on every lexicographically earlier competitor. The first bound supplies it on a larger explicit set of competitors. The interval between the constructive bound and the counting bound still has linear width. Priority for the rectangular bounds has not been established. Finite-field rigidity and distance from subspaces are classical. Recent work on covering by random linear codes gives stronger uniform discrepancy estimates for fully random generators. [Alon and Cohen, *On Rigid Matrices and U-Polynomials*](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nalon/PDFS/rigid5.pdf) · [Recent random linear code discrepancy bounds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24471)

## A deterministic construction fitting both sides of a block

Write $k$ for the height, $J$ for the width and $R$ for the allowed rank. Columns and rows are over $\mathbb F_2$. One stage removes $a$ rows and $b$ columns of the residual matrix and spends $c$ rank dimensions. Fix a generator $G$ of shape $a\times c$ for a vertical covering code of radius $e_v$ and a generator $H$ of shape $c\times b$ for a horizontal one of radius $e_h$. For the new rank-$c$ term $UV$, set the top $a$ rows of $U$ to $G$ and the left $b$ columns of $V$ to $H$.

For each remaining column choose its coefficient vector in $V$ to approximate its top $a$ residual bits by a column of the form $Gv$. For each remaining row choose its coefficient vector in $U$ to approximate its left $b$ residual bits by a row of the form $uH$. These choices are independent. If the current dimensions are $k'\times J'$, the completed rows and columns have total error at most
$$
ab+e_v(J'-b)+e_h(k'-a).
$$
Subtract $UV$ on the remaining lower-right rectangle and continue. Embed subsequent factors with zeros on all completed rows and columns. The ranks add and later stages preserve the error accounting. In particular a later row can fit old columns by using the factors from their earlier stages. Restricting each column basis to a direct sum of small row blocks would remove that freedom.

For fixed blocks the coefficient choices use constant-sized nearest-codeword tables. To achieve the stated running time, avoid updating a full large rectangle after each constant-rank stage. Give a cell the deadline equal to the first stage at which its row or column is removed. Recursively split the ordered stages in half. After completing the left half, apply its entire factor product just to the cells whose deadline lies in the right half. These cells form two disjoint rectangles: the rows removed in the right half against columns still present at its beginning, and the rows remaining at its end against the columns removed during that half. Every earlier stage contributes to a cell's residual exactly once at the binary split separating that stage from the deadline. A leaf receives all needed residual entries before decoding its two sets of coefficient vectors.

At a node containing $u$ stages, the inner product dimension and one side of each rectangle are $O(u)$, and the long side is $O(J)$ when $k=O(J)$. Tile across that side and use square multiplication. At scale $u$ there are $O(1+J/u)$ nodes and total cost $O(J^2 u^{\omega-2})$ for $u\le O(J)$, with $\omega=\log_2 7>2$. The geometric sum is $O(J^\omega)$. Block lookup, advice or input copying and index encodings fit the stated polylogarithmic overhead. This computation returns both factors on arbitrary input. [Strassen's seven-product recursion](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02165411)

For the asymptotic calculation suppose a phase processes $uJ$ rows and $vJ$ columns with $sJ$ rank dimensions, beginning at remaining proportions $K,C$ of $J$. Use many fixed-size stages with common proportions and relative covering radii $d_v,d_h$. One may choose these constants whenever
$$
s/u+H_2(d_v)>1,\qquad s/v+H_2(d_h)>1.
$$
The existence of a fixed finite binary linear covering code at every strict pair of parameters follows from the covering argument in the earlier note. When the ratios are rational, use a sufficiently large common multiple for the two block lengths and generator dimension, concatenating fixed codes and padding generator coordinates if needed. Strict slack permits a slightly lower common rate. Incomplete final blocks and unused rank can be handled by dropping boundedly many rows or columns at $O(J)$ cost. Equivalently choose stage counts rounded down, so rank never rounds above the budget. The sum of the $ab$ corner costs is $O(J)$. Summing the other costs gives the normalized phase error bound
$$
u(C-v/2)d_v+v(K-u/2)d_h+o(1).
$$

Use two phases from initial dimensions $(K,C)=(0.2816,1)$. Their parameters $(u,v,s,d_v,d_h)$ are
$$
(0.1408,0.0525,0.0343,0.21795,0.06492),\qquad
(0.1408,0.04525,0.0282,0.24284,0.07294).
$$
Subtract the first phase's dimensions before applying the second bound. The rank proportions sum to $1/16$ and the leading error is $0.062457215040<1/16$. The linked script certifies the strict entropy inequalities with rational bounds for logarithms and verifies the factors on all 4,096 matrices of a small shape and 200 larger examples. The direct and dyadic schedules also return identical factors on every test. The finite script uses scalar products on the two dyadic rectangles. The asymptotic speed bound uses fast matrix multiplication there. Lean verifies that combining the explicit factors adds their widths and preserves exactly the residual error on the lower-right block. ## Keeping the data in the generators while covering the remaining columns

Here is a stronger existence bound without the same time guarantee. Write $k/J\to\lambda$, $R/J\to\rho$, with $0<\rho<\lambda\le1$. Fix $\rho<\alpha<1$. In an initial slab of $\lfloor\alpha J\rfloor$ columns use a fixed horizontal block covering code at rate just below $\rho/\alpha$ and relative radius $d_h$ with $H_2(d_h)>1-\rho/\alpha$. Round each row in small blocks. This gives a factorization $B V_0$ of that approximation with $R$ generator columns in $B$, padding unused generators if necessary. Every coefficient column of $V_0$ has bounded Hamming weight because it uses a single fixed code block. A possible leftover block costs $O(J)$ errors. The leading error inside the slab is at most $\lambda\alpha d_h J^2$.

Replace $B$ by $B+Z$, where the $kR$ entries of $Z$ are independent Bernoulli bits of mean $p=k^{-1/3}$. Each anchored coefficient uses at most a fixed $c_0$ generators, so the expected total change in its slab is at most $c_0p kJ=o(J^2)$. With probability tending to one the change is $o(J^2)$, for instance by using the cutoff $\sqrt p\,kJ$. The matrix $B$ may depend on the entire prescribed slab.

Fix the other target columns in advance and let $d_v<1/2$ satisfy $H_2(d_v)>1-\rho/\lambda$. Greedily pack coefficient strings of length $R$ so that they and every pairwise difference have weight at least $h=\lceil k^{2/3}\rceil$. Start with zero in an ordinary packing and remove zero afterwards. The radius-$(h-1)$ ball has size $2^{o(k)}$, so the resulting message set has $2^{R-o(k)}$ elements. For a message $a$, the bias of a single bit of $Za$ is at most $\beta=(1-2p)^h$. For distinct $a,a'$ all three nontrivial binary characters of one coordinate of $(Za,Za')$ have bias at most $\beta$, using also the weight of $a+a'$. Hence the probability of each two-bit outcome lies between $(1-3\beta)/4$ and $(1+3\beta)/4$. Independence across coordinates shows the whole pair is pointwise within a multiplicative factor $1\pm o(k^{-D})$ of the uniform pair for any fixed $D$. The same holds for individual messages. The fixed shifts $Ba,Ba'$ merely permute the tested outcomes.

For each fixed target column, the uniform radius-$\lfloor d_vk\rfloor$ ball has probability $2^{-k(1-H_2(d_v))+o(k)}$. The number $X$ of messages hitting it after applying $B+Z$ has exponentially large mean. Its ordered pair moment differs from the independent value by a relative $o(k^{-D})$. Thus $\Pr[X=0]\le\mathrm{Var}(X)/(\mathbb EX)^2=o(1/J)$, allowing a union bound on the at most $J$ prescribed columns. With positive probability the old slab is preserved to its edit budget and every other column has a nearby codeword. Choosing the corresponding coefficients proves
$$
\limsup_{J\to\infty}\ \max_{A\in\{0,1\}^{k\times J}}
\frac{\mathrm{dist}(A,\{\mathrm{rank}\le R\})}{J^2}
\ \le\ \lambda\bigl[\alpha d_h+(1-\alpha)d_v\bigr].
$$
The entropy conditions can be taken to equality by limits. The special case $\alpha=\rho$ uses the chosen $R$ data columns themselves with unit coefficient vectors and zero initial distortion. Finite weighted zero-hit and pair-moment inequalities have been checked in Lean. [Moment inequalities](../lean/AnchoredCoverMoments.lean)

With $\lambda=0.2881$, $\rho=1/16$, $\alpha=0.0947$, $d_v=0.23292$ and $d_h=0.06321$, both inequalities are strict and the normalized error is at most $0.062474042190+o(1)$. Thus even the worst choice of entries in these trailing rows fails to qualify for $R_J$. Lexicographic minimality sorts the rows, so initial all-zero rows account for all missing rows. The bound on the other side uses the number of rank-$j$ matrices, including the change-of-basis factor $|\mathrm{GL}_j(2)|$. [Counting and the upper bound on the number of active rows](lex-factor-responses.md)

There is a counting limit on this way of protecting the initial slab. Insist that every coefficient column in that slab have weight at most $w=o(R)$, allowing input-dependent factors. The slab approximants then number at most $2^{kR}(\sum_{i\le w}\binom Ri)^{\lfloor\alpha J\rfloor}=2^{kR+o(J^2)}$. Covering every possible slab at relative distance $d_h<1/2$ requires $H_2(d_h)\ge1-\rho/\alpha-o(1)$ by ball volume. The fixed-code choice already reaches this rate for a protected slab of the stated kind. It remains possible to distribute the errors differently or to allow dense coefficient columns.

The random perturbation preserves sparse anchor coefficients but generally makes the other nearest-codeword searches expensive. I do not know how to return those coefficients at the clock required by the direct-bit SAT test. Even a faster response for all matrices through $0.2881J$ rows would leave some earlier competitors untreated. The unrestricted SAT lower bound remains open.


## Using an existence theorem as a short response

An earlier competitor in the direct-bit test need not always be answered by listing actual factors. Extend its verifier with one sound alternative response: count the nonzero rows and accept the response when there are at most $\lfloor0.2883J\rfloor$. The three-column existence theorem proves that the competitor then fails qualification at all sufficiently large sizes. Counting its rows costs $O(J^2)$. This covers a larger family at the verifier's clock even though factor extraction on the whole family is open. The soundness and completeness proof for the direct-bit test still requires a small common response on **all** earlier competitors. [Direct-bit test](lex-factor-responses.md) · [Stronger existence theorem](triple-anchor-cover.md)

One can use this response even when no row is zero. Suppose all except $k$ rows have supplied rank-$s$ factors with $e$ errors. Extend the row space of these factors to dimension $s$ if needed ($s\le J$), adding unused coordinates. Choose $s$ pivot columns on which the coefficient matrix is invertible. For each of the $k$ remaining rows, choose its $s$ additional coefficients to fit those pivot entries exactly. Subtract their contribution on the other columns. Any rank-$(r-s)$ approximation for the arbitrary residual $k$-by-$(J-s)$ rectangle then combines with the first factors, by zero padding, into overall rank $r$. The errors are at most $e$ plus that rectangle's worst-case approximation error. The verifier only needs the list of exceptional rows and factors for the others when invoking the proved rectangular bound. This is a sufficient certificate and imposes no special position of the exceptional rows.

For example, uniformly in $s=o(J)$, $e=o(J^2)$ and $k\le0.2883J$, the strict rate and error margins at the certified point persist for the residual rectangle: its width is $(1-o(1))J$, its normalized rank budget tends to $1/16$, and padding smaller heights to the certified asymptotic height only increases the required work. Thus if the actual bit table of the bounded program agrees with some rank-$o(J)$ matrix off at most $0.2883J$ arbitrary rows and $o(J^2)$ other cells at infinitely many phases, the corresponding unrestricted SAT time bound fails. The approximants and row sets in this last sufficient condition need not be efficiently computable. I have not shown that the bounded program has this property.
