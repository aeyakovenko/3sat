# A short proof that one stage loses, with two long individual proofs

Fix a sound, effectively axiomatized classical theory $T$ extending Peano arithmetic. Use a standard binary arithmetic proof calculus in which PA proves the checker's correctness for the stipulated proof predicate and its **polynomial** worst-case clock on every candidate. PA must also verify the ordinary syntactic operations on these codes, including numeral substitution, embedding PA derivations in $T$, and combining proofs of opposite conclusions into a contradiction proof. Use the usual PA-verifiable arithmetic proof compiler for the fixed computation formula specified below. PA with a conventional binary proof presentation suffices. There is one designated rank $e$ in the actual [priority schedule](priority-pinned-sat.md) that never wins. Write $L_j$ for the closed arithmetic sentence that this rank loses at stage $j$, using a compact binary numeral.

* For all sufficiently large $j$, every $T$-proof of $L_j$ has at least $j-O_T(\log(j+2))$ bits. Each individual $L_j$ is provable in $T$.
* For every $j$, the same $T$ has an $O_T(\log(j+2))$-bit proof of $L_{2j}\lor L_{2j+1}$. Both disjuncts are true. Thus even the smaller of the two shortest individual proofs eventually exceeds $2j-O_T(\log(j+2))$ bits.
* $T$ proves that there exists a stage after which $e$ always loses. No standard numeral can be substituted as that cutoff and still give a theorem of $T$. In $T+\mathrm{Con}(T)$, one fixed proof establishes loss at every stage, giving $O_T(\log(j+2))$-bit proofs of the individual instances.

These are lengths of explicit proofs in a fixed arithmetic calculus. They are unconditional. The disjunction uses retirement at most once at two different stages; the [two-rank construction](disjoint-winner-certificates.md) instead uses at most one award at the same stage.

## The rank in the unmodified scheduler

Choose a self-describing program with one designated color occurrence of rank $e$. Its code can recover that fixed index by effective specialization as in the synchronized construction. Checking this particular compilation and enumeration identity requires only a fixed finite computation. The bit of interest is the actual award predicate $\mathrm{Win}(j,e)$, expressed by the existence of a valid finite record of the complete priority computation. It is $\Sigma_1$, and each fixed finite stage is decidable with termination provable in PA. The formula $L_j$ is its negation at the stipulated numeral. [Self-referencing ranks and native simulation](synchronized-priority-trials.md)

At scale $j$ enumerate all binary strings of length at most
$$
 \ell_j=\max(0,j-K\lceil\log_2(j+2)\rceil).
$$
Signal if a string verifies as a $T$-proof of either $L_j$ or a contradiction. Otherwise do not signal. Even during startup, before signaling, the trial must have checked the exact intended sentence and proof. Implement counters, numeral construction, clock arithmetic, and the specialized program and rank codes by exact binary operations. Before each scratch allocation or indexed access, check that its address is representable in the designated bank, using bit-length comparison or checked addition before forming the address. An unrepresentable allocation idles the trial. A timeout during startup or during a check emits no signal. This convention also applies to the SAT-assisted trial below, including formula construction and the simulated solver's word width. It keeps the signal implication valid at small stages where eventual time or space estimates need not apply.

Encoding the statement and checking each candidate takes at most $O_T((j+2)^a)$ native steps for a fixed $a$, including exact arithmetic and reuse or clearing of workspace. The counter needs $O(j+1)$ bits and polynomial-in-$j$ reusable scratch fits in the native address space for all sufficiently large $j$. For $K>a+2$ the exhaustive check costs $o(2^j)$, below this fixed rank's native allocation at every sufficiently large stage. These eventual comparisons follow in PA from the verified polynomial clock. The contradiction search uses the same candidates.

If $e$ wins, it has signaled. A proof of contradiction violates consistency immediately. In the other case there is a $T$-proof of $L_j$. The genuine winning computation gives a $T$-proof of $\mathrm{Win}(j,e)$ by checking its finite record. Combining them contradicts consistency. Thus $e$ never retires. After a finite stage it is active and no rank below it ever wins again. A timely signal by $e$ would then make it win. Hence neither search has a hit at any sufficiently late stage, establishing the proof-length lower bound. For each standard $j$ the true finite computation can be verified inside $T$, however large its record, proving that $e$ did lose.

Use binary numeral constructors $2x$ and $2x+1$ with a fixed representation for zero (handle small numerals separately). The two adjacent-stage disjunctions are instances of the single $T$-theorem saying that one rank cannot win at both stages $2x$ and $2x+1$. Substituting the numeral for $j$ costs $O_T(\log(j+2))$ bits in an ordinary finite-alphabet calculus with a fixed proof of that theorem. This choice of two adjacent stages avoids having to normalize an arbitrary successor numeral.

## The precise consistency strength of a cutoff

PA proves the following equivalence for this fixed indexed program:
$$
 \forall j\,\neg\mathrm{Win}(j,e)\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{Con}(T).
$$

For the right-to-left direction, write the fixed $\Sigma_1$ formula $\mathrm{Win}(j,e)$ as $\exists w\,R(j,e,w)$, with $w$ containing the finite computation record and any auxiliary witnesses for its bounded arithmetic check. Use a standard primitive-recursive arithmetic proof compiler $C$ for this fixed formula. PA proves that if $R(j,e,w)$ holds, then $C(j,e,w)$ checks as a PA proof of $\mathrm{Win}(\bar j,\bar e)$. This is formalized $\Sigma_1$ completeness for one formula. One construction evaluates the fixed bounded matrix on the supplied numerals, produces its PA derivation when it evaluates to true, and introduces the existential witness. PA verifies correctness by induction in the bounded evaluation and proof construction. Embedding this derivation in $T$ gives the needed $T$-proof code. No size bound on this proof is used. Parsing the emitted proof of $L_j$ and combining the two derivations then uses the syntactic operations assumed above.

For the reverse direction, a particular proof of contradiction eventually fits every $\ell_j$. At every sufficiently large stage, if still unretired and tested, $e$ signals within its deadline. On $e+1$ consecutive such stages, perpetual preemption would require $e+1$ distinct winning ranks below $e$. Hence it has won by the end of that interval. The proof uses the verified checker clock and the finite retirement rule and can be carried out in PA.

Classical $T$ proves $\exists J\,\forall j\ge J\, L(j,e)$ for *every* rank: either it never wins or the successor of its unique winning stage suffices. If $T$ proved the tail from some particular standard $J$, combine it with $T$-proofs of the finitely many earlier true losses to get $T\vdash\mathrm{Con}(T)$, contrary to Gödel's second theorem. This conclusion distinguishes a short proof of stabilization from a proof of an eligible cutoff. It also explains why adding precisely the consistency axiom yields the uniform short proofs. [Gödel, Theorem XI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01700692) · [Pudlák on the length of finite consistency proofs](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0049-237X(08)70462-2)

## What a hypothetical fast SAT solver changes

Suppose ordinary SAT has a correct deterministic $O(N^k)$ solver $S$. For this variant take $T$ to include a true axiom asserting that every positive answer by this particular solver on a well-formed CNF is correct. For example one can take PA plus that axiom. Build a new rank that uses $S$ on the bounded existence of a $T$-proof of its own stage loss, interleaving the contradiction monitor above. On a fixed pair $(T,S)$, a nondeterministic program guesses an $s$-bit-or-shorter proof and checks the stipulated conclusion in time $(s+1)^a(j+2)^{O(1)}$, for some fixed $a\ge1$. A sufficiently large constant $B$ allows
$$
 s=\left\lfloor 2^{j/(ka)}/(j+2)^B\right\rfloor
$$
with dyadic rounding. The packed compiler and the $k$-time solver then fit in $o(2^j)$ native steps since the clock and formula logarithms are $O(j)$. The two search branches may be dovetailed at fixed rates. On a positive SAT answer the rank signals. If it wins, $T$ can derive existence of the indicated $T$-proof from the positive-answer axiom and correctness of the bounded-acceptance encoding. This conditional rank has the same uniform consistency equivalence provable in $T$, using its positive-answer axiom; no PA proof of that axiom is required. The late individual $T$-proof lengths now exceed $s$. The opposite direction of the equivalence still uses the separate unconditional contradiction monitor. The short adjacent disjunction and short $T+\mathrm{Con}(T)$ proofs persist. The lower bound is conditional in this paragraph; the direct-enumeration rank above uses no SAT hypothesis. The stronger checker supplies short semantic loss proofs for an opponent that searches the weaker one. Changing the searching opponent to the stronger checker changes its designated rank and the consistency statement that the argument needs. The construction therefore exposes a failure of witness extraction from finite retirement, without giving an unrestricted SAT running-time lower bound or a new lower bound for standard propositional proof systems.
