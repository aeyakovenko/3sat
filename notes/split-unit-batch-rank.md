# When one bit of a SAT batch retains the entire communication rank

**Research journal, 25 November 2026.** Can an exact OR or AND of many small SAT instances compress away the part of a solver computation that reads distributed clauses? There is an explicit family of width-three formulas for which any deterministic two-party protocol needs a linear number of communicated bits for that one answer, even with interactive messages and free local computation. One ordinary word-RAM decides the same promised inputs in time linear in the number of encoded words. The distinction persists for full-degree Boolean operations on the batch under an explicit divisibility condition.

## Clauses that compare two private inputs

Alice and Bob each hold $s$ symbols in $[q]$, with $s\ge1$ and $q\ge2$. Alice contributes the units $X_{j,a_j}$ and Bob the units $\neg X_{j,b_j}$ for $1\le j\le s$. Their conjunction is satisfiable exactly when $a_j\ne b_j$ at every position. This predicate is the complement of the established **exists-equal** communication problem. The two-party results below concern exact deterministic protocols. Bounded-error interactive protocols for exists-equal have a different tradeoff. [Sağlam and Tardos](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.1217)

Take $t\ge1$ such blocks with disjoint variables. Conjoining their formulas gives AND. For OR, guard every clause in block $i$ with $\neg z_i$ and require $\bigvee_i z_i$, using a public binary OR circuit with a unit output assertion. Both are split width-at-most-three CNFs with $O(ts)$ clauses. Alice and Bob can each write their own clauses without access to the other input.

There is also a split width-three encoding of an arbitrary aggregate $h$ given by a public fan-in-two circuit of size $g$. Use the same guards to ensure $z_i$ implies all $s$ inequalities in block $i$. For the converse, introduce $s$ position selectors $c_{ij}$ and $q$ symbol markers $y_{iu}$ per block, with the following constraints.

- If $z_i$ is false, at least one $c_{ij}$ is true. A binary OR circuit enforces this in $O(s)$ clauses.
- Alice adds $(\neg c_{ij}\lor y_{i,a_{ij}})$, and Bob adds $(\neg c_{ij}\lor y_{i,b_{ij}})$. A public at-most-one constraint on the $q$ markers then makes a selected position certify equality. It costs $O(q)$ clauses using the running prefix OR and forbidding a marker when the preceding prefix is true.
- Assert the output of the exact Tseitin encoding of $h(z_1,\ldots,z_t)$. Each circuit gate costs a constant number of clauses of width at most three.

A colliding block can select one collision and mark its symbol. A collision-free block takes $z_i=1$ and selects nothing. Thus $h$ is applied to the actual SAT verdicts on every assignment satisfying the auxiliary checks, and witnesses always exist for the actual verdicts. The total is $O(t(s+q)+g)$ clauses. The encoding and rank results do not require $h$ to be monotone. [Lean verification of the private clauses and decoded verdicts](../lean/SplitUnitBatchRank.lean)

## Exact rank under arbitrary Boolean aggregation

Let $K=q^s$, $d=(q-1)^s$ and $p=d/K$. The real $0/1$ SAT communication matrix of one block is $D=(J_q-I_q)^{\otimes s}$, with $K$ rows, constant row sum $d$, and full rank. Write $J$ for the $K$-by-$K$ all-ones matrix. For $S\subseteq[t]$, let $P_S(p)$ be the expected discrete derivative $\Delta_S h$ over the remaining $t-|S|$ independent Bernoulli-$p$ bits. Equivalently, with multilinear coefficients $a_T$ defined by $h(z)=\sum_T a_T\prod_{i\in T}z_i$,

$$P_S(p)=\sum_{T\supseteq S}a_T p^{|T|-|S|}.$$

The real $0/1$ communication matrix $M_h$ for the aggregate has exact rank

$$\operatorname{rank} M_h=\sum_{S\subseteq[t]:\ P_S(p)\ne0}(K-1)^{|S|}.$$

To verify this, write $M_h=\sum_z h(z)\bigotimes_i B_{z_i}$ with $B_1=D$ and $B_0=J-D$. Decompose $\mathbb R^K$ into the constant line and its orthogonal complement. Both are invariant. On the line, the eigenvalues are $d$ and $K-d$. On its complement, the two matrices act by $D$ and $-D$, where $D$ is invertible. The tensor block with a complement in exactly the positions of $S$ therefore acts by the common invertible tensor operator times $K^{t-|S|}P_S(p)$. Its dimension is $(K-1)^{|S|}$. Summing the ranks of these invariant direct summands gives the formula. This is a biased, regular-matrix version of a known composition-rank argument. Lee and Zhang give the strongly balanced sign-matrix form in their Theorem 7. [Lee and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1443)

**Divisibility test.** Let $a=a_{[t]}$. If $K\nmid a$, then $M_h$ has full rank $K^t$. Each $P_S$ has integer coefficients and leading coefficient $a$ when $a\ne0$. The reduced denominator of $p$ is $K$ since $q$ and $q-1$ are coprime. For a positive-degree integer polynomial, clearing denominators at a rational root shows its reduced denominator divides the leading coefficient. A nonzero constant has no roots. Thus no summand vanishes. In the other direction, $a=0$ deletes the summand at $S=[t]$. Since $|a|\le2^{t-1}$ for Boolean $h$, when $K>2^{t-1}$ the aggregate has full rank exactly when $h$ has degree $t$. For both AND and OR, $a=\pm1$ and full rank holds for every $q,s,t$.

**Sharpness at a multiple of $K$.** For every $q,s$, put $r=1+\lceil\log_2\max(d,K-d)\rceil$ and $t=r+1$. Each parity class of the $r$-cube has at least $\max(d,K-d)$ vertices. On the last-bit-zero slice choose exactly $d$ vertices with negative sign in the top discrete derivative on $[r]$. On the last-bit-one slice choose exactly $K-d$ vertices with positive sign. Define $h$ to equal one on the chosen vertices only. The two $[r]$-derivatives are $-d$ and $K-d$, so $a_{[t]}=K$. Their biased average is $-(1-p)d+p(K-d)=0$. The aggregate loses at least $(K-1)^r$ dimensions despite having full degree. This needs $t\le\lceil\log_2 K\rceil+2$ batch bits. The audit solves 4,096 split width-three formulas by DPLL, compares the rank formula against 544 independently computed exact ranks, and checks ten parameter choices for this cancellation. ## What the lower bound charges

In a deterministic protocol tree with publicly determined output, each accepting transcript is a rank-one rectangle, so there are at least $\operatorname{rank}(M_h)$ accepting leaves. The same bound for $1-h$ counts rejecting leaves. Whenever $M_h$ has full rank, $h$ is nonconstant and $M_{1-h}$ also has full rank. Indeed the nonempty derivatives change sign and both functions have strictly positive expectation because $0<p<1$. Thus the tree has at least $2K^t$ leaves. Its worst-case depth is at least $1+\lceil st\log_2q\rceil$. Sending the private string as an integer in $[K^t]$, followed by the published answer, attains this exactly. If only Bob needs the answer, removing the last bit attains $\lceil st\log_2 q\rceil$; any shorter protocol could publish Bob's result with one more bit and violate the public-output bound. Both equalities apply to AND and OR for every $q,s,t$.

For a concrete asymptotic family take $t=s=q=2^k$. Number the clause variables by their tuple of indices, use a fixed-width binary literal representation, and put each side's $ts$ private slots in the public order of the block and position. These are ordinary explicitly listed CNFs. Each relevant literal identifier has $O(\log(tsq))$ bits, and the private slots have length $\Theta(ts\log q)$ overall. The total encoded length for AND or OR is $N=\Theta(ts\log q)$, and the communication bound is $\Omega(N)$. A single logarithmic-word RAM compares each private symbol with its partner in $O(1)$ steps and computes the aggregate in $O(ts)=O(N/\log N)$ steps on this promise. More generally, for a public $g$-gate aggregate the promised computation needs $O(ts+g)$ word steps given its fixed slot layout and a word-accessible gate list. In particular, neither the divisibility test nor a sharp $\Omega(N)$ interactive communication bound forces a superlinear step count for a RAM with access to both clause lists.

This tests distributed preprocessing. In ordinary instance compression the compressor can inspect the entire input. A fast SAT solver could emit the answer bit itself after joint computation. The known conditional compression barriers allow that model and make different complexity assumptions. A proof for the native hard family would still need to charge joint machine work beyond the cost of reading its input words.
