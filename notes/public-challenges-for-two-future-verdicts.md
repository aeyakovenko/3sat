# A public challenge about two genuine SAT verdicts

Fix $k\in\{1,2,3\}$. To rule out deterministic $O(n^k)$ SAT it would suffice to give the following verifier for the past-conditioned native family. At a start $i$ it is given the *true history before $i$* and two dates $i\le u<v\le i^C$ for fixed $C>1$. Its claim is that **at least one of the two specified positive-pin 3-CNFs is satisfiable**. It may fail to establish a true claim. No future words, winner identifiers or truthful current queries are supplied. A fixed verifier satisfying the following requirements would suffice.

- If both verdicts are negative, **at least one** public challenge must have no accepting reply.
- At infinitely many distinct starts there is some pair for which **every** public challenge has an accepting reply.
- Reading the challenge and reply, evaluating the verifier, and all other branch work fit $2^{\lfloor i/k^2\rfloor}/(i+1)^B$ for sufficiently large fixed $B$. Message caps and encodings are part of the verifier. In particular a bound with any constant exponent strictly less than $1/k^2$ suffices without knowing the needed logarithmic slack.

There is no lower bound on the probability of drawing the rejecting challenge in the first requirement. The verifier can be incomplete at every other true pair. The two dates must be distinct. The known complementary pins at *one* date would otherwise satisfy the promise trivially. For precision take $m=2\lfloor h/6\rfloor$ in the native family. Its verdict at date $j$ is $F(a_j)=\operatorname{Tr}(u_jv_jw_j)$ on the leading three field elements of its hidden word. The all-zero trial word gives a negative verdict at every date. The physical formulas are explicit, fixed-width 3-CNFs. [Native construction](learning-from-earlier-sat-verdicts.md) · [Two-date and longer disjunctions](winner-informed-positive-stages.md) · [Physical field pins](trace-product-positive-certificates.md)

Excluding the all-negative answer vector is an instance of the classical idea of *membership comparability*, where an algorithm rules out one vector of membership answers on several inputs. The particular features here are a partial checker, chronological dates with access to authenticated past words, and a native time budget below the physical instance length. Historical priority for this combination has not been established. [Ogihara](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539793258131) · [Membership comparability, selectivity and queries of different lengths](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/app-sel.pdf)

## Why the costs are charged only twice

First suppose a deterministic fast SAT solver exists. For each fixed pair the exact predicate tested is $\forall c\,\exists p\,V(i,u,v,\mathrm{past},c,p)$. A public prover can tailor its reply to the entire challenge. Compile the inner existential bounded computation to ordinary SAT and run the solver. A failed outer test has an existentially guessed challenge on which this deterministic computation rejects. Compile that test in turn and complement the answer. For a total bounded branch time $T_i$ the deterministic cost is $(T_i+\operatorname{poly}(i))^{k^2}\operatorname{poly}(i)$. The starting history and date encodings cost polynomially many bits in $i$. The packed bounded-computation reduction includes initialization, word widths, uniform input generation, and the solver's own unrestricted writable memory. Logarithmic factors are polynomial in $i$ on the stated clock. A test reading the output of the future native computation for free would violate the hypothesis. [Compilation on the target RAM](packed-band-transfer.md)

The deterministic tester accepts only true disjunctions and accepts some pair at infinitely many starts. If infinitely many accepted pairs have $F(a_u)=0$, a detector at $v$ can recognize such a pair and conclude $F(a_v)=1$. It uses the recorded word at $u$ and reconstructs only the history *before the original start* for running the tester. Otherwise discard a fixed finite prefix of starts, after which every accepted pair has $F(a_u)=1$. A detector at $u$ can use that fact even though $v$ has not arrived. In either case infinitely many different reporting dates occur, since every date is at least its originating start, and there are only finitely many candidate pairs per start.

At a putative reporting date $j$, enumerate $i\le j$ and the other date in the polynomial public range, keeping just pairs with the required reporting endpoint. This enumeration needs no general quantified search over solvers. Time amplification by a fixed polynomial in $j$ is absorbed by choosing $B$ sufficiently large. More explicitly, a sum over the earlier starts bounded by $2^i/(i+1)^b$ times a fixed polynomial in $j$ is $o(2^j)$ for large enough $b$, by splitting at $i=j/2$. The resulting positive detector never signals incorrectly and does so infinitely often. Its word reporter emits zero when it signals. On a true positive date this excludes its own proposed word. A timely reporter of fixed rank eventually wins whenever it emits at all, since each of its finitely many predecessors retires after one win. This contradiction proves the sufficient condition. A verified standalone upper bound for the required public-challenge computation on real pairs is still missing.

## Allowing error, a first prover message, and late replies

There is a bounded-error version. A prover can first supply a common message $w$ before seeing random bits. After a uniform $R$-bit public tape $s$ is revealed it can supply a reply $p$. For every false pair and every $w$, at most an $\alpha$ fraction of tapes should admit any accepting reply. On some pair at infinitely many starts, one common $w$ should allow replies on at least a $\gamma$ fraction of tapes, for fixed $0\le\alpha<\gamma\le1$. All message lengths and the verifier fit the charged bound $T_i$. A matrix with $T_i\le2^{\lfloor i/k^3\rfloor}/(i+1)^B$ in this model would suffice as well. Only recurrence on the actual histories is required. The guarantee for every false pair also applies on those histories, not on every counterfactual past.

Here are the quantitative details. Take $\ell=O_{\alpha,\gamma}(\log(R+2))$ independent copies with the same $w$ and fresh replies. Accept if the fraction of successes exceeds $(\alpha+\gamma)/2$. A Chernoff bound with a sufficiently large constant makes the soundness density less than $1/[4(R'+1)]$ and makes the completeness density at least $1/2$, where $R'=\ell R$. For each fixed tape vector, separate replies for the accepting coordinates exist precisely when enough of the single-tape claims admit replies. Zero random bits may also be handled directly.

On the amplified cube put $g=\lceil\log_2(R'+1)\rceil$. A subset of density at least $1/2$ has translates by the span of $g$ generators covering the cube. If $U$ is the uncovered set for the current span, adding a generator $z$ leaves $U\cap(U+z)$. Averaging over $z$ squares the uncovered density. After $g$ choices the uncovered count is at most $2^{R'-2^g}<1$. Conversely at density below $1/[4(R'+1)]$ no such span covers, since it has at most $2^g\le2(R'+1)$ members. This is a fine-grained use of the established translate-cover method. [Lautemann](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0020019083900443) · [Viola on probabilistic and alternating time](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/DagSemProc.06111.11) · [Earlier use for near-native counting](near-native-counting.md)

The deterministic predicate after eliminating the following three blocks is a sound, possibly partial test of the two-date disjunction:

1. Guess $w$ and the $g$ generators.
2. Universally choose one $R'$-bit tape.
3. Guess the $g$ selector bits for one span element and replies witnessing enough accepting coordinates of the translated tape.

Check one translated tape in the last branch. The generators cost $gR'$ bits and each of the $\ell$ verified runs costs at most $T_i$ before the fixed simulation overhead. All branch costs fit $(T_i+\operatorname{poly}(i))\operatorname{poly}(i)$. Three SAT eliminations give exponent $k^3$. The preceding two-date argument applies without an extra exponent. For linear SAT both public-proof models have the near-native exponent one. For quadratic SAT the perfect-completeness, two-message test uses exponent $1/4$ at the start and the bounded-error, three-message test uses $1/8$. For cubic SAT the corresponding exponents are $1/9$ and $1/27$. These exponents refer to the native clock $2^i$, not to the physical length of a guessed succinct future formula. ## A test whose public challenges are only polynomially numerous

For the simple positive tags in the window construction one can remove one SAT elimination altogether. These are ordinary designated word reporters that, having reached their public start, finish with a positive word whenever they run before retirement. Each wins once, and infinitely many distinct tags are available. At an actual stage $i$ when a designated rank $e$ is activated, past its start and unretired, consider the next two verdicts.

If both verdicts are negative then at dates $i,i+1$ two *distinct earlier ranks* $f,g<e$ must win, with words $y,z$ of negative sign. There can be no default while $e$ is ready; after the first negative win $e$ is still ready. Each rank can win only once. Test the specific event

$$
       (f,y)\text{ wins at }i\quad\text{and}\quad(g,z)\text{ wins at }i+1 .
$$

Suppose a local nondeterministic verifier can certify the **negation** of this event. Require soundness on the actual input and its genuine start history; the verifier may abstain. Different challenges can have entirely different certificates. The ranks are among at most $i+1$ active reporters and both words have $O(\log(i+2))$ bits, so all possible negative events together number only $\operatorname{poly}(i)$. Guess certificates for all those challenges and run the local verifications in sequence. This is **one** existential bounded computation, even though the condition is universal over all candidate histories. The local runtime $T_i$ is multiplied by only a fixed polynomial in $i$. Completeness at infinitely many eligible starts with some designated $e$ would now contradict $O(n^k)$ SAT at $T_i=2^{\lfloor i/k\rfloor}/(i+1)^B$. Guessing $e$ or enumerating the active ranks also costs at most a polynomial factor. Only the OR of the two positive signs has to follow. A checker is never asked to locate a winner or prove a whole transcript.

In particular, at each of the infinitely many genuine wins of simple positive tags, **every** proposed negative two-winner event is false. Under fast SAT, for every fixed sound verifier at the above clock, sufficiently late such wins still have at least one specific false event with no timely certificate. This holds even after combining any fixed finite list of sound verifiers into one by guessing the verifier index. For deterministic local tests the same conclusion holds unconditionally at $2^i/(i+1)^B$ by running all trials directly.

There is a dual implementation at the same exponent. Uniformly print a small CNF allowing some *possible* pair of negative preemptions. It may allow fictitious histories. Require only that every real double-negative outcome gives some satisfying assignment, and that the relaxation is unsatisfiable on infinitely many eligible starts. A deterministic printer taking less than the native budget, with enough polynomial slack for enumerating starts, and printing at most $2^{\lfloor i/k\rfloor}/(i+1)^B$ bits would suffice: run the hypothetical SAT solver once and signal the disjunction on its negative answer. This version needs no supplied certificate that the relaxation is unsatisfiable. Guessing an arbitrary small subformula and then asking the solver about it introduces another elimination unless there is a separate cheap proof of its obstruction. The coupling of the first winner word to the second reporter's history is one relationship such a relaxation could exploit.

I tried eliminating these events by prefix replay. For the first rank run it on the actual past for a bounded number of steps; for the second, condition on the first claimed win, appending $(y,f)$ to the past and retiring $f$. A complete run with an incompatible report eliminates the event. If the first claim was false there is no soundness risk in using its hypothetical history for the second conditional check. A truncated live run before its assigned budget expires gives no exclusion. The counting and retirement conditions themselves remove repeated or previously retired ranks but give no exclusion for two distinct live computations. At an actual designated win no earlier eligible reporter offers even the first claimed negative word. The theorem shows precisely why a fixed inexpensive collection of these tests must nevertheless leave a false event unresolved. An unconditional short exclusion for *every* such event on infinitely many genuine starts would meet the original time-lower-bound goal for the corresponding $k$. No coverage theorem of that kind has been proved here.
