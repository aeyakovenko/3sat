# Testing an algebraic refutation without writing a point in every variable

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A bounded computation on a short program may have exponentially many certificate variables. A proposed refutation can cover all Boolean assignments by violated clauses, taking the sum modulo two. We can check a particular representation of such covers using short algebraic challenges, even when evaluating one term requires exponentially many layers. Under linear SAT, one short program at the actual native first missing word has no cover within the bounds below. A separate family shows why ordering the variables favorably cannot ensure small width.

## A parity cover by ordered matrix products

For a CNF with M clauses on N variables let fᵢ(x) be one if clause i fails and zero otherwise. A refutation consists of Boolean functions gᵢ such that, on every Boolean x,

$$
\sum_i f_i(x)g_i(x)=1\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2.
$$

A satisfying assignment makes the left side zero. Conversely any unsatisfiable CNF has a cover by assigning each point to its first failing clause. The resource question concerns the complexity of the multipliers gᵢ. Represent each by a scalar entry of a width-w product of N binary matrices. Layer j chooses one of two matrices according to the bit at variable π(j), for a common ordering π. Initial and final standard basis vectors may be fixed by padding. One Boolean circuit gives any requested entry from the clause, layer, input bit and two state indices. A second circuit gives π. Both circuits are descriptions of the proof.

At bit b in a layer let cᵢⱼ(b) indicate that all literals of clause i on variable π(j) are false. It is one if the variable is absent. Multiply the b-matrix by that scalar. Interpolating **these two masked matrices** linearly in one indeterminate gives the layer polynomial. Their ordered product interpolates the full function fᵢgᵢ multilinearly. This order of operations matters because separately interpolating the two functions and multiplying them could introduce squares. Taking the direct sum across clauses, and adding one constant summand, gives a width W≤Mw+1 ordered read-once oblivious algebraic branching program for the discrepancy P. Boundary vectors on the direct sum permit the sum of all scalar outputs. Every variable has individual degree at most one. Vanishing on the Boolean cube is equivalent to the formal identity P=0, by induction on the number of variables using the two Boolean evaluations. For a true identity the discrepancy evaluates to zero in every commutative algebra of characteristic two.

## Short challenges for exponentially many variables

Assume N,M,w≤2^(C n) for a fixed C, and pad N to a power of two. The Forbes–Shpilka generator for a fixed order hits any nonzero P: some point over an extension field detects the failure. We need to generate *one coordinate at a time*. In the explicit formula a coordinate is a product of O(log N) matrices of width W² between endpoint vectors. The seed has O(log N) field elements. Choose the field of size 2^(n²) for all sufficiently large n. Its multiplicative group supplies an element of order greater than (NW²)², and the W² interpolation nodes can be the first distinct binary field representations. The formal generator has polynomial degree in N and W per coordinate and seed variable, by its displayed Lagrange formula. Thus the field also suffices to find a nonzero evaluation of the composed polynomial. A degree-n² irreducible polynomial, the chosen multiplicative element and the seed together need only poly(n) bits. [Forbes and Shpilka](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.2408) [Forbes, Shpilka and Volk, Lemma 7.1 and equation (7.1)](https://toc.cs.uchicago.edu/articles/v014a018/v014a018.pdf)

For each generator-matrix entry evaluate its designated univariate Lagrange polynomial by streaming through W² interpolation nodes, accumulating a numerator and denominator. Exponentiation and field operations use poly(n) space and a single entry costs 2^O(n) time. A balanced recursive scalar product evaluation for O(log N) matrices sums over W² intermediate indices at each split, with depth O(log log N). Its time is 2^O(n log(n+2)) and its space poly(n). This uses the large width W only to bound loop counters. In particular we do not allocate a W²-entry vector. The point coordinate uses the position in the common variable order, so the challenge supplies coordinates in layer order.

A sound universal check can allow even more challenges. Take every polynomial-size description for a coordinate-producing tape computation with sufficiently large fixed polynomial space cap, time cap 2^(n ceil(log₂(n+2))²), and n² output bits, using a default on failure. Each coordinate is obtained from its index with the same seed and program. Also allow every monic binary modulus of degree n² and compute modulo it. A valid cover survives even a reducible modulus. For an invalid cover the preceding actual field and generator are among these challenges. Thus the checker needs no test of a challenge's irreducibility or multiplicative order.

## Cost at the native first missing word

Use the uniform bounded-computation CNF on (n,p,z), exactly satisfiable when the native program of length less than n prints z by B=2^(20n). It suffices that clause counts and variable counts are 2^O(n) and that a clause, of at most poly(n) literals, can be generated in poly(n) time from its index. For example unroll a fixed tape simulation of the native machine in 2^O(n) time, including its native step clock, then use a local tableau. The simulated random-access memory may contain B distinct n²-bit addresses: scanning the earlier writes for each read still costs 2^O(n) total time on tapes. No workspace restriction is imposed on the hypothetical SAT solver. [Native source and first hole](native-missing-word.md)

Write g(n)=sqrt(n log₂(n+2)). There is a constant a such that, under linear SAT, for all sufficiently large n a single p at the actual first omitted vₙ has **no** such cover with both w and the combined bit lengths of the two circuits at most S=floor(B/2^(a g(n))). The common permutation can be chosen as part of each proof. The following clock calculation also applies with a uniform rounded-integer exponent in S as in the native source.

First check that the order circuit maps the variable indices injectively to themselves, by a universal pair of indices. It is then a permutation. Verify the syntax and the prescribed input and output arities of both descriptions. For one challenge, evaluate each clause's product in turn, maintaining a width-w field vector, evaluating its matrix entries by the guessed Boolean circuit. Add the scalar outputs. Repeated scans of the circuit description and of the vector are allowed. Read each clause explicitly to obtain its masks. This sweep has work space (S+1)poly(n) apart from the coordinate subroutine.

Under linear SAT a polynomial-space tape computation with clock 2^(n ceil(log₂(n+2))²) can be tested in deterministic time 2^o(n), uniformly given the challenge and index. Apply the existing bounded-reachability simulation to each output bit, including timeout and exact-output checks. Simulating this algorithm on tapes adds at most quadratic time and at most 2^o(n) space. Thus each coordinate can be recomputed within the sweep. The total sweep clock is 2^O(n): there are only exponentially many clauses, layers, matrix entries and circuit gates in these bounds. Its full tape configuration has (S+1)poly(n)+2^o(n) bits. Applying the bounded-reachability simulation a second time, now to the entire sweep, gives (S+1)2^O(g(n)) time. Both applications use fixed algorithms. Upper bounds on the hidden constants can be taken independently of a by using S≤B in the sweep's time and logarithmic factors. [Uniform linear-SAT bounded reachability](linear-quantifiers.md)

The outer guess of the proof descriptions, the universal coordinate challenge, the permutation check, and the short-program and prefix quantifiers add a fixed number of blocks. Their compilation and native simulation add polynomial factors in n. Choose a large enough that the resulting prefix search fits B. If every p had a certified cover at vₙ, find the first z where every p has a cover. Every predecessor has a timely producing p, hence has a satisfiable formula for that p and fails the universal test. This decoder would print vₙ by B using a constant-length name. The missing step for a linear SAT lower bound is to build the compact covers at every p there, on infinitely many n. Simply writing down multipliers as functions of assignments does not provide their matrix widths or circuit descriptions.

## A width obstruction that survives every common variable order

For any power of two m≥2 take two m-bit words x,y and a shift selector s of log₂ m bits. For each possible shift k, include the 2m clauses for the equalities xᵢ=y_(i+k mod m), guarded to apply only at s=k. Also include one clause for each k and each m-bit word u that fails precisely on the assignment with s=k, x=u and y equal to the corresponding shifted copy of u. The first group forces equality and the second excludes every equality pattern, so the formula is unsatisfiable. Its m(2m+2ᵐ) clauses of width at most 2m+log₂ m are uniformly indexed in poly(m) time.

Fix any variable order and make a cut after exactly m of the 2m word variables have appeared. If a of those m variables are in x, a uniformly chosen cyclic shift has expected (a²+(m−a)²)/m≥m/2 crossing pairs. Choose one shift with k′≥m/2 crossing pairs, set the selector to that shift and fix all noncrossing pairs to zero. Label the left and right choices on the crossing pairs by u,v in {0,1}^k′. For u=v the equality clauses all pass, so their cover contribution vanishes. For u≠v all pattern-exclusion clauses pass and the sum of the contributions from the 2m active equality clauses must be one. Their combined evaluation matrix on this restriction is precisely J+I in characteristic two. Its dimension D=2^k′ is even, so J²=0 and (J+I)²=I.

Each ordered multiplier has evaluation-matrix rank ≤w at the cut by its left-vector/right-vector factorization. Multiplication by its clause-violation indicator only scales rows and columns, since clause failure is a conjunction of literal values. Subadditivity of rank therefore gives 2mw≥2^k′≥2^(m/2), regardless of the order or the description cost of the multipliers. This is an unconditional width lower bound for the specified algebraic proof representation. The indexed formula itself has a particularly short semantic explanation of contradiction. It supplies an obstruction to the proposed proof transformation and no lower bound for deciding ordinary SAT.

The example uses exponentially many indexed clauses. Its width bound is polynomial in their explicit count. Related work proves CNF refutation lower bounds by rank-based feasible interpolation for ordered algebraic branching programs. Priority for the succinct special case here has not been established. Lean checks functional parity-cover soundness and the involution used to certify full rank of J+I; the generator simulation, diagonal clock and width argument are manuscript proofs. [Hakoniemi, Limaye and Tzameret](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/070/) [Lean verification](../lean/OrderedParityCovers.lean)

## Different variable orders for different multipliers

The native width and description bounds persist when clause i reads the variables in its own order πᵢ. A second proof circuit gives one reference order τ; the first may also give πᵢ on request. Impose the following weaker requirement. At each prefix of τ, its variables occupy at most b intervals in every πᵢ, where b=max(1,ceil(n/(log₂(n+2))³)). The orders, their inverses and the intervals need not be listed. Universally choose a clause and a reference prefix. Scan the clause's order, test membership in the given prefix, and count runs. Recover the reference rank of a variable by scanning τ if necessary. Universal index pairs check that each required order is a permutation. These exponential-time scans have the same small space as the proof circuit evaluation.

Why does the generator still hit the discrepancy? At a reference cut, clause i's matrix product switches between the two groups of variables at most 2b times. Fix the intermediate state at each switch. The corresponding term factors into a function of the left prefix times a function of the remaining variables. Summing over switch states gives rank at most w^(2b) for the Boolean evaluation matrix of the masked product at that cut. This bound holds simultaneously at every reference cut. The discrepancy's rank is therefore at most W=1+Mw^(2b). For each prefix length take the vector space spanned by all the discrepancy's residual functions after values are given to that prefix. Its dimension is the cut rank. Restricting a residual at the next variable to zero or one gives two linear maps to the next residual space. Choosing bases at each cut yields an ordered matrix product of width at most W in the reference order, and linear interpolation of each matrix pair computes the discrepancy's multilinear polynomial. This representation is used to establish that a detecting seed exists; the verifying computation still evaluates each original product in its own order.

Here log₂ W=O(n²/log³(n+2)+n). The same extension field of n²-bit elements eventually suffices. The balanced generator-coordinate computation has time 2^O(log(W+2) log(n+2)+n log(n+2)) and polynomial space. Enlarge the public coordinate time cap to 2^ceil(n²/(L max(1,floor(sqrt(L))))), with L=ceil(log₂(n+2)). This dominates its 2^O(n²/log²(n+2)) time at large n and its bounded-reachability decision still costs 2^o(n). During the full sweep, request each point at the reference rank of the variable actually read. All other bounds carry over. Thus one short program at the first missing word defeats this more general class with both original width and circuit description bounded by the same near-clock S. The cyclic-shift obstruction under this interval condition likewise gives 2m w^(2b)≥2^(m/2), by the same cut factorization. Arbitrarily unrelated orders remain outside the argument.
