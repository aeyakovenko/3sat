# Checking global polynomial invariants with sparse counterexamples to native RAM transitions

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A block of arbitrary RAM instructions may depend nonlinearly on incoming words. Its invariants can nevertheless be dense equations on the entire memory. If a few low-degree equations suffice for one inductive consequence, any failure has a complete counterexample state with few nonzero bits. This lets a hypothetical linear-time SAT solver check the implication without materializing an address-space-sized state. The resulting obstruction concerns actual native programs at the first missing output and its predecessors.

## Freezing the cells touched on a counterexample

Represent a finite RAM state by its register, controller and memory bits. One memory bit's name consists of an address and a word-bit position. Use an absorbing convention for halted or malformed states, and attach a bounded output controller that records whether the proposed word z is printed correctly by the cutoff. For the native machine on input n there are only poly(n) special controller bits, words have w=n² bits and memory has 2^w addresses. Public inputs remain fixed. Every block of C steps reads or writes O(C) words.

Let F be the exact transition across such a block. A proposed implication has h incoming polynomial equations Pⱼ(x)=0 over the binary field and one outgoing equation Q(F(x))=0. Take all polynomials multilinear on Boolean state bits. If the implication fails, it fails at an x of Hamming weight at most

$$
O(Cw+\mathrm{poly}(n)) + \deg Q + \sum_{j=1}^{h}\deg P_j .
$$

On an actual counterexample freeze the bits of all words it reads **or writes**, and the entire initial controller. Any filling of the other locations follows the same instructions, addresses and writes, by induction on the steps. Its outgoing controller and touched output words are fixed, and every untouched bit passes through unchanged. After substitution of these constants, the outgoing test is therefore a polynomial on the untouched *incoming* bits of degree at most deg Q, even when the instructions use multiplication or indirect jumps. The restricted product

$$
Q(F(x))\prod_{j=1}^h (1+P_j(x))
$$

takes value one at the original filling. Its multilinear reduction has degree at most the stated sum. A nonzero Boolean multilinear polynomial of degree D is nonzero at some assignment of weight ≤D. For example, the coefficient of a nonzero monomial is the sum of evaluations on the subsets of that monomial, by Möbius inversion in characteristic two. Use this sparse filling and restore the frozen words. The degree contribution is sharp: use the identity transition, demand h disjoint degree-k monomials equal one at entry, and demand a fresh, disjoint degree-k monomial equal zero on exit. Every counterexample has at least k(h+1) ones. [Existing Lean proof of the sparse evaluation lemma](../lean/LowWeightPolynomial.lean)

## Checking a circuit-indexed family of dense equations

Describe all invariant rows by a single Boolean circuit. It takes a boundary index, row name, monomial length and strictly increasing list of bit-coordinate names, and returns the binary coefficient. Fix a maximum degree k and pad monomial lists accordingly. Every such circuit defines actual polynomials. Unused rows may be zero. Row names can have poly(n) bits, so explicitly writing the set of all rows need never fit the machine clock. A proof can also supply a circuit for a schedule of boundaries t₀,…,tⱼ, with J≤B, where B=2^(20n). Check the endpoints and bound 1≤tᵢ₊₁−tᵢ≤C by a universal index. This includes using all B individual instruction boundaries with a binary counter.

Check every initial row on the specified genuine initial state. For every next boundary and each of its row names, existentially choose at most h previous row names. Require that the chosen rows imply that outgoing equation through the actual deterministic block. The support may depend on the row and the boundary; it is fixed before the challenger chooses the incoming state. The sparse counterexample lemma makes it sufficient to test all sparse incoming lists of length at most L=O(Cw+poly(n))+k(h+1). A challenged list is interpreted as a set of distinct nonzero coordinates, with padding and well-formedness checked. From such a list a replay is exact using an address dictionary; its resulting state is again sparse with at most L+O(Cw+poly(n)) nonzero bits. To check the implication, either the outgoing row vanishes or one chosen incoming row is nonzero. Only one incoming row need be evaluated in the latter case. Induction then gives *all* rows on the actual run. Require a designated terminal row expressing the desired verdict, either by fixing that row's coefficients publicly or by a final implication of the same kind into a prescribed verdict bit.

Evaluating a general dense quadratic on L nonzero coordinates by pair enumeration could cost L². Instead certify the value of its exclusive-or sum. Guess the L partial parities for the first position, check their aggregate and universally challenge one. Its second-position sum has L summands, whose bits can in turn be guessed and universally checked against the coefficient circuit. More generally, use a fixed number O(k+1) of such quantifier blocks to certify degree k, including the constant term. One can sum over k padded positions from the support plus a dummy, retaining precisely strictly increasing tuples followed by dummies. Each monomial occurs exactly once. Distinct challenged parities may use independent truthful subtables. Induction down this tree proves that the root verdict is exact. The resulting quantified circuit's initial encoded size is (L+h+S+1)poly(n) for fixed k and a total proof description of S bits. This includes sorting sparse coordinates, the RAM replay transcript, retrieving a chosen support row name, and checking the one indicated coefficient. The depth is fixed independently of n. The model needs no certificate evaluating a row efficiently at an arbitrary *dense* assignment. Lean verifies the exact parity game and the induction with a separately chosen support for every row. [Lean verification](../lean/SparseStateInduction.lean)

For example a parity equating the XOR of two or more cells to an observed bit may determine the output even while each of the cells takes both values on permitted states. Higher degree allows equations such as a dense quadratic relation between pairs of words. Exact state descriptions arise as a special case: take one unary equation per coordinate prescribing its real value. For any one next coordinate, fixing the true touched cells and the controller enforces the true replay; include the queried coordinate's initial value when it survives untouched. Thus these exact families always have O(Cw+poly(n))-row local supports. Obtaining a sufficiently short common coefficient circuit for the exact rows is a separate issue. Related inductive checks use affine constraints on hidden tags or predict incoming reads by address. The polynomials here are on the full RAM state itself. ## Consequences at the actual missing word

Fix k≥1. For sufficiently large fixed a take C,h,S≤floor(B/n^a), allowing the proof to choose any scheduled block lengths up to C. Fixed-block elimination under linear SAT, including the outer program and proof guesses, decides the existence of this proof in (C+h+S+1)poly(n) deterministic RAM time. The fixed polynomial exponent can be chosen independently of a. There is no J term because the verifier checks consecutive boundary indices universally against the same schedule and coefficient circuits. Choose a to leave room for native interpretation, prefix searches, and the poly(n) factors. [Uniform elimination](linear-quantifiers.md) [Native clock](native-missing-word.md)

For each sufficiently large n under this hypothesis, some single short program at the first omitted output vₙ has no such inductive certificate of nonproduction. Otherwise search for the first z for which every program has a nonproduction certificate: every predecessor has a real producer, and such a producer cannot pass this sound test. The bounded native decoder would print vₙ on a constant-length name.

There is also a statement about true outputs. Put z in the certified set if some program of length <n has an inductive proof that it really produces z by B. The set excludes vₙ. Apply the existing omission-ranking decoder: it guesses the entire 2ⁿ-bit membership table, universally checks one entry, computes all suffix omission scores in 2^(2n)poly(n) time and decodes a high-scoring omission by rank. Thus for a fixed c and all sufficiently large n every interval [vₙ−K,vₙ) with 0≤K≤vₙ has at least (K+1)/c−1 predecessors outside this certified set. Each really is printed by some short program; every timely producer there lacks the proposed invariant chain. [Ranking argument](native-first-reads.md)

A uniform construction giving these locally supported equations and compact coefficient circuits for all nonproducers at vₙ on infinitely many n, or covering almost all genuine predecessors on suitably growing trailing intervals, would rule out linear SAT. An ordinary one-line invariant stating that the memory equals its true contents has yet to meet the coefficient-access budget. The sparse counterexample theorem shortens the verification of a proposed invariant and supplies no such description by itself.

There is a coefficient-degree consequence requiring no size estimate for a separately supplied circuit. Fix the binary row-name format with at most n^u bits for some fixed u and use the unit-step schedule. Regard the coefficient table, jointly in time, row and monomial code, as a partially specified Boolean function: invalid monomial encodings are free. If it has an extension of binary algebraic degree r, its algebraic normal form on m=poly(n) bits can be implemented using at most (m+1)^r poly(m+r) encoded bits, by listing the nonzero monomials and folding their products and sum. For a sufficiently small fixed positive c₀, r≤c₀n/log₂(n+2) therefore gives a circuit strictly below the near-clock size cap. For the hard programs just identified, every locally supported degree-k invariant family within the unit-step row and support format must consequently have coefficient degree greater than c₀n/log₂(n+2) on every completion of its coding table. This degree is in the names of the coefficients; k was the separate degree in the RAM's state bits. A single isolated table entry already can have high degree and a tiny circuit. Here the obstruction also survives sparse exceptions: pick c₀ small enough that the low-degree circuit takes less than half the size cap. An exception at a valid m-bit query can be patched with an equality test using O(m log(B+2)) encoded bits. Consequently every valid family at a hard program differs on more than B/n^d valid entries from every polynomial of the indicated degree, for some fixed d depending on the name format and clock constants. This robust conclusion excludes an isolated-address explanation for the degree bound. Polynomial program invariants have a substantial prior literature. Priority for the quantitative RAM specialization has not been established. [Sankaranarayanan, Sipma and Manna](https://theory.stanford.edu/~sipma/papers/popl04.html)

## Which coefficient fields allow sparse counterexamples?

The same freezing argument works for equations over the finite field of p^r elements on **Boolean** state bits. Expand every coefficient in a basis over the prime field. Since a Boolean monomial evaluates to zero or one, each of the r coordinate functions has degree no greater than the original row. Each incoming zero constraint is the vanishing of all r coordinates; for an outgoing nonzero choose one failing coordinate. Multiplying the latter by the factors 1−P^(p−1) for each incoming coordinate gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most deg Q + (p−1)r·Σⱼ deg Pⱼ. Boolean-cube Möbius inversion works over the prime field as well. For fixed p and r=poly(n), the witness and coefficient entries grow only polynomially in n. The nested partial-sum checker simply compares the claimed modular sums coordinatewise.

Already for linear equations the field-size dependence is necessary. In r independent coordinate groups of p−1 Boolean variables each, require that the sum in every group be −1 modulo p. Packing the r conditions into one extension-field affine equation forces all r(p−1) variables to be one. Over the rationals even a single linear equation requiring N Boolean bits to sum to N forces weight N. Low degree alone would give an incorrect small-state test over that field.
