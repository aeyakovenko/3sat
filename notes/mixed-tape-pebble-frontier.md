# A square-root auxiliary-tape threshold for segregators and bounded-round pebbling

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Consider a run with one writable planar tape and a fixed number of additional writable linear tapes. Each has one head that stays or moves to a nearest neighbor. The graph has one vertex per step, chronological successor edges, and an edge between each two consecutive visits to any one work cell. Deleting vertices takes the induced subgraph, without reconnecting successive surviving visits. The input in the constructions is a read-only stream scanned strictly forward, so it adds no repeat-visit edges. Let $T$ count steps and $W$ count distinct auxiliary work cells visited, summed over tapes.

* If $W=o(\sqrt T)$, some $O(T^{3/4}\sqrt{W+1})$ deletions leave **every weak component** of size $O(T^{3/4}\sqrt{W+1})$. The two-round game of Paul, Pippenger, Szemerédi and Trotter (PPST) then costs $o(T)$ pebble placements.
* With one writable line visiting $W=\Theta(\sqrt T)$ cells, a fixed machine has arbitrarily long runs on which deleting $\gamma T$ vertices leaves $\Omega(T^2)$ ordered pairs connected by directed paths, for a constant $\gamma>0$. There is also one family of runs requiring $\Omega_R(T)$ placements for **every fixed** round bound $R$. Extra linear tapes may stay idle.

Thus $\sqrt T$ is the threshold, up to constant factors, for these grates and for a linear placement cost in any fixed number of rounds at least two. A chronological path alone can require linear cost in one round. A stronger separator bound counts live intervals, and the matching threshold becomes $T^{1/d}$ in fixed dimension $d\ge2$. [Dimension and activity theorem](mixed-tape-dimension-threshold.md) The commands can be supplied on a finite-alphabet input of length $O(T)$. The conclusions concern the graph of the given execution. The controller can ignore the data on its writable tapes, so this graph lower bound establishes no decision-time lower bound. PPST posed the multidimensional grate question for machines with tapes, and defined the challenger and placement costs used here in their §5. [PPST](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)

## Separator below the threshold

Let $p_t$ be the planar position at step $t$. For positive integers $u,q$, partition the plane into half-open $u\times u$ squares using a uniform random offset modulo $u$ in each coordinate. Two positions $p_i,p_j$ lie in distinct squares with probability at most $\|p_i-p_j\|_1/u\le (j-i)/u$. For all consecutive-visit edges of a fixed auxiliary cell, the sum of $j-i$ telescopes to at most $T-1$. Chronological edges also have total duration $T-1$, and planar memory edges have identical endpoint positions. Some offset consequently has at most $(W+1)T/u$ crossing edges. Delete their sources.

Within each square, list the remaining visits in time order and divide them into consecutive groups of at most $q$ by cutting after each full group except the last. At any such cut, at most one chronological edge crosses and each work cell contributes at most one crossing consecutive-visit edge. The square contains at most $u^2$ planar cells. Delete the sources of all edges crossing these cuts, at cost at most $(u^2+W+1)T/q$. Every weak component now lies in one group. Taking
$$
 u=\lceil T^{1/4}\sqrt{W+1}\rceil,\qquad
 q=\lceil T^{3/4}\sqrt{W+1}\rceil
$$
gives the asserted bounds for $W\le\sqrt T$. The positive argument even permits auxiliary heads with arbitrary movement on their respective cells. With $W=0$ it gives the single-plane $O(T^{3/4})$ bound. [One-head construction and separator](two-dimensional-segregators.md)

In PPST's game, first pebble the separator $J$. For the next challenge $x$, pebble every unpebbled ancestor reaching $x$ by a path with no internal vertex in $J$, excluding $x$ itself. There are at most $d q$ of them for maximum indegree $d$, by considering each immediate predecessor outside $J$ and its weak component. All parents of $x$, and all parents of every newly placed pebble, have then been pebbled. This wins after at most two placements of sets.

## A reusable line for a directed grate

The following layout implements an expander lift with a single permanent copy of each lane. Take a bounded-degree outer DAG on $O(s)$ vertices with $s$ designated inputs and $s$ designated outputs. Put $q=\lceil\sqrt s\rceil$ and $m=q^2$. For every outer vertex $v$ use a disjoint $q\times q$ tile on the plane, with one permanent cell $(v,i)$ for each lane $0\le i<m$ in row-major order. Pack tiles into a roughly square array of diameter $O(q\sqrt s)=O(m)$. The line uses exactly the cells $0,\ldots,m-1$.

Process vertices in topological order. At $v$ scan all its lanes and designate their distinct visit times as hubs $H(v,i)$. For each outgoing edge $v\to w$ and each of a fixed number of permutation labels $F$, do the following in its own time interval.

1. Visit $(v,i)$ together with line cell $i$, for $i=0,\ldots,m-1$. Call the meetings $L_i$.
2. Move the plane head to the destination while the line head waits. Visit $(w,F(i))$ together with line cell $i$ for $i=m-1,\ldots,0$. Call these meetings $D_i$.

Separate designated visits by a step as needed. If $F$ is an affine permutation of $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^2$ with fixed integer coefficients, its row-major sequence and its reverse cost $O(m)$ planar moves. Within each row every coordinate advances by a fixed increment modulo $q$, so there are $O(1)$ wraparounds of cost $O(q)$ per coordinate and ordinary moves cost $O(q)$ in total. Row changes cost $O(q)$ apiece. The line head may wait during the planar travels. The per-label interval, the travel between intervals, and each hub scan all cost $O(m)$. Thus $T=\Theta(sm)$, with $W=m=\Theta(\sqrt T)$.

For every deleted time mark the lane at the planar head's position when that position is in a tile. If the time belongs to a transfer interval for $v\to w$ and the line head is at cell $i$, also mark $(v,i)$. A deleted time marks at most two lanes, regardless of how often a line cell is reused. Whenever $(v,i)$ and $(w,F(i))$ are both unmarked, their hub-to-hub path survives. It follows the plane cell $(v,i)$ from $H(v,i)$ to $L_i$, line cell $i$ from $L_i$ to $D_i$, and plane cell $(w,F(i))$ from $D_i$ to $H(w,F(i))$. Each leg follows **all** intervening visits on that one cell, including waits and incidental crossings. The entire line leg lies within this one transfer interval. This time restriction permits reuse of the same buffer without charging a deletion to other transfers.

For completeness, the expander construction supplies the following stage. With $M=sm$ designated hub inputs and $M$ hub outputs, deleting $z\le aM$ steps leaves subsets of at least $7M/8$ on both sides with every retained input reaching every retained output. Here $a>0$ is absolute. A proof follows.

* Use Blocki and Cinkoske's Corollary 17, with $\epsilon=1/2$. The corollary indexes its family by every positive integer $s$, with constants independent of $s$. After deleting $k\le cs$ outer vertices, $s-k$ inputs and $s-k$ outputs have every pair joined by a surviving path of length at least $\lfloor\sqrt s\rfloor$. Replace unbounded fanout by outward binary trees of total size $O(s)$. Charge deletion of a tree vertex to its original owner and use original roots as terminals. Write $\Delta$ for the resulting constant bound on both degrees. [Blocki and Cinkoske, Definitions 1–2 and Corollary 17](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol185-itcs2021/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.64/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.64.pdf)
* Along every outer edge use the permutation labels of a fixed power of the lazy Margulis–Gabber–Galil walk on the $m$ lanes. Write $P$ for its symmetric, doubly stochastic matrix. Its mean-zero norm $\lambda$ can be made an arbitrarily small positive constant and its labels have fixed affine coefficients, uniformly in $q$. [Gabber and Galil](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4) · [Formalized spectral estimate](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Expander_Graphs.html)
* Put $\delta=1/64$. Discard each original owner containing a vertex with more than $\delta m$ marked lanes, including all its associated tree vertices. At most $2z/(\delta m)$ owners are discarded, at most $\min(c,1/32)s$ by the choice of $a$. In reverse topological order retain the unmarked lanes $F_v$ sending at least $3/4$ of their edges into $F_w$ for every surviving neighbor $v\to w$. Inductively $|F_v|\ge(1-2\delta)m$. Indeed, for an excluded neighbor set $E$ of density $u\le 2\delta$, the normalized variance of $P1_E$ is at most $\lambda^2u(1-u)$. The fraction of lanes sending more than $1/4$ into any excluded outneighbor set is at most $2\delta\Delta\lambda^2/(1/4-2\delta)^2\le\delta$ for small enough $\lambda$. Physical marks eliminate at most another $\delta$. Define $B_v$ analogously in the reverse graph.
* For a nonempty reached set $S\subseteq F_v$ and its neighbors $Y$ in $F_w$, mixing gives $3|S|/4\le |S||Y|/m+\lambda\sqrt{|S||Y|}$. Thus $|Y|\ge\min(m/4,2|S|)$, choosing $\lambda<1/8$. Starting from any retained input lane, $r=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ forward steps along a chosen outer path give a frontier of size at least $m/4$. The same holds backward from any retained output lane. For all large $s$ the outer path has at least $2r+1$ edges. Choose a middle edge with at least $r$ steps on either side. Its two frontiers meet across an edge by mixing, since their geometric mean size is at least $m/4>\lambda m$. The selected terminal lanes occupy at least $(1-1/32)(1-2\delta)M>7M/8$ on each side. All connections lift by the preceding three cell chains. [Earlier two-plane proof with explicit terminal losses](two-tape-robust-terminals.md) · [Klawe's expander amplification for abstract grates](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1992/TR-92-03.pdf)

The retained terminal sets give at least $(7M/8)^2$ connected pairs and establish the grate claim.

## Several rounds in the PPST game

The abstract adversary for stacked grates has precedents. De Rezende, Nordström and Vinyals give the strategy for stacked butterflies, prove their uniform-grate property in Observation 6.8, and explain the extension to stacked uniform grates, including Kalyanasundaram and Schnitger’s linear-size supergrates. Here the interfaces are paths on shared physical cells and the designated hubs may have incoming and outgoing tape dependencies. [De Rezende, Nordström and Vinyals, §6.2, Lemma 6.6 and Observation 6.8](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/006/download/) · [Kalyanasundaram and Schnitger](https://doi.org/10.1016/0890-5401(90)90002-Y)

Concatenate $k$ stages of the same size. Reassign tile locations at each stage so that its input lanes occupy precisely the permanent cells of the preceding outputs, bijectively. Each output hub then reaches its matched next input hub along the intervening visits to their shared planar cell. For a given interface these $M$ paths are vertex-disjoint because the plane head visits one cell at any time. A stage occupies $\Theta(M)$ time and moving between stages costs $O(m)$. The run length is $T=\Theta(kM)$.

Initially challenge the last time of the last stage. Every final output reaches it by chronology. Suppose the old pebbles form a set $P$ and more than $M/2$ output hubs of the current stage have paths to the current challenge $x$ avoiding $P$ except possibly at $x$. Let $A$ be the new placements, and suppose $|P\cup A|\le\min(a,1/8)M$. Apply stage robustness to the deleted times $P\cup A$. Its good output set intersects the candidate hubs. Pick a common hub $y$ and one of its previously surviving paths to $x$. Challenge the first vertex of $A$ along that path if it has one, or stay at $x$ otherwise. This is a legal PPST move. Every good input of the stage reaches this new challenge with all vertices except possibly the challenge outside $P\cup A$. The prefix from $y$ to the challenge avoids both old and new pebbles internally.

At most $|P\cup A|$ of the preceding interface paths meet any pebble, leaving at least $7M/8-|P\cup A|>M/2$ preceding output hubs with the same path property. This proves the induction. At the first stage there are still at least $7M/8$ unpebbled inputs reaching the new challenge. A path from an input distinct from the challenge supplies an unpebbled immediate predecessor. Thus a Pebbler with the stated total budget cannot win after $k$ placements of sets. In particular the cost for $k$ rounds is $\Omega(T/k)$ with an absolute implied constant. Pebbles outside the currently considered stages can only consume budget. PPST gave this scaling for general bounded-degree DAGs in Proposition 5.4. [PPST, §5](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)

A single family works simultaneously for all fixed $R$. For large $N$ concatenate the preceding runs for $k_j=2^j$, $0\le j\le\lfloor\tfrac12\log_2 N\rfloor$, taking $s_j=\lfloor\sqrt{N/[2^j(j+1)^2]}\rfloor$ and $M_j=s_j\lceil\sqrt{s_j}\rceil^2$. The minimum $s_j$ tends to infinity. Their total length is $T=\Theta(N)$ by summing $\Theta(N/(j+1)^2)$. Stage arrays can be anchored at the origin, and returning the two heads there between runs takes at most $O(m_j)$ extra steps per run. The line uses $\Theta(\sqrt T)$ cells across the whole execution. For $j=\lceil\log_2 R\rceil$ in range, initially challenge the last vertex of that particular run. The legal strategies just proved give
$$
 \text{placements in }R\text{ rounds}\ =\ \Omega\!\left(\frac{T}{R(1+\log R)^2}\right).
$$
All fixed $R$ are covered on the same family. One can also use the last vertex of the entire execution as the initial challenge for all $R$. Before any pebbles are placed, every final output hub of the selected run reaches that vertex by chronological edges, which is all the first stage of the induction requires. Kalyanasundaram and Schnitger subsequently proved the stronger $\Omega(n/\log R)$ trade-off on general bounded-degree graphs chosen for $R\ge2$ in their range, and studied the simultaneous quantifiers. The bound above concerns the explicit two-head tape schedules. Priority for the mixed-tape threshold and these physical realizations has not been established. [Kalyanasundaram and Schnitger, 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0890-5401(90)90002-Y)

## Geometric block graphs at PPST's clock

Each chosen native trajectory also occurs as the **epoch graph** of a legal block-respecting run with just the same two writable heads. Add one setup slot and at most one idle slot so that the slot count $p$ is odd. Take epochs of $b=p^2$ microsteps, $p$-by-$p$ planar blocks of volume $b$, and length-$b$ line blocks. The clock is $f=p^3$. Start a planar epoch at a face midpoint, go via the center to the desired exit midpoint in $p-1$ steps, wait $b-p$ steps, and cross or stay on the final step. Setup reaches the initial midpoint. Start a line epoch at an endpoint of its current block. For a crossing, go to the desired endpoint in $b-1$ steps or wait there for $b-1$ steps, then cross on the last step. On a stay, wait throughout. The spatial block visited by either head during an epoch is exactly its logical cell. An unrolled command input supplies legal directions on these runs. Consecutive block visits thus give every required logical edge, and chronological edges link adjacent epochs. The same game lower bounds hold with the epoch count as the graph size.

There is moreover a fixed machine respecting this volume convention on **every** input, using one writable plane and **three** writable lines, plus the read-only input. Give it $n$ direction-pair commands, set $p=2(n+1)$, $b=p^2$ and $f(n)=p^3$. Two small timer lines implement the period-$p$ and period-$p^2$ signals of the two-plane construction, staying in one length-$b$ block each. The planar payload starts at the center of its first square after setup. On later epochs the first two half-cycles of the fast timer move it to the center and then to the specified exit face, each leg using at most $p/2$ moves. It waits for the last microstep to cross. On a stay it finishes at the center. The line payload begins at the left endpoint of its first block. Finite control remembers the current endpoint and command, so it knows whether to wait or move on the first $b-1$ steps and whether to cross on the last. The command stream stays within one input block. After setup and $n$ command epochs the input head is at its right endmarker, position $n+1$. In each of the remaining $n+1$ epochs the payload heads stay and the input takes one leftward step at the start. Detect the left endmarker during the final epoch and halt on its last microstep. This gives exactly $p$ epochs. On the selected inputs the hard epochs occupy a constant fraction of $p$. The two timing lines add only chronological block edges. In this statement the square-root auxiliary threshold counts **distinct blocks per logical graph**, while the native threshold counts cells per physical step graph. [Timer initialization and epoch signals](two-plane-directed-grates.md) · [PPST's graph and clock, §3](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)

These schedules disprove a sublinear segregator or fixed-round pebbling bound quantified over all such block-respecting runs. A normal-form simulation selects a representation and execution for each language. Whether every language in the relevant time class admits a choice that avoids these graphs is a further question.
