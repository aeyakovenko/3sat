# Near-shortest late names that escape all efficiently listed replacements

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Can a native interpreter suppress late improvements simply by supplying a faster replacement for each late program? An unconditional diagonal defeats every polynomial list that can be generated cheaply from the proposed late name. It preserves a positive fraction of near-shortest words at a fixed exact **name length**. On a constructed interpreter, an early name outside the list actually produces the same word and has the same globally shortest length.

Fix any efficient literal native prefix interpreter V and a fixed ordinary optimal conditional prefix machine P. Write K for unbounded complexity on V and Kᵗ_P(q|w) for the shortest P-program printing q with auxiliary input w in time t. For every pair of fixed positive integers d,k, there is a fixed integer a such that at infinitely many lengths m there are exact V-names w of length m−d, publishing their m-bit output y during (t,T], with

    T=2^(3m),     t=⌊T/mᵃ⌋,     |w|≤K(y)+H,

where H can be fixed for V independently of d,k. At every such w, Kᵗ_P(y|w)>k⌈log₂(m+1)⌉, although K_P(y|w)=O_V(1). In addition, **every** exact V-name q' publishing y by t satisfies Kᵗ_P(q'|w)>k⌈log₂(m+1)⌉. If any early name exists, one has unbounded conditional complexity O_V(1) given w. Thus actual early coverage at these words would require finding a replacement whose short conditional descriptions need substantially more time. No SAT hypothesis enters the construction.

## Diagonalizing a whole block at once

One fixed native decoder M takes a prefix name q for the parameter tuple (s,d,k,a), then a conditional name p on that tuple and |q|. The field s is a bitstring. For the analysis take s to be the actual literal V compiler prefix for M. This is legitimate since M handles arbitrary s as data. Cache both consumed program strings. Form the usual integer record label r identifying the tuple, both names and both runtimes. Put m=2ʳ. Read enough raw rank bits z that s q p z has length exactly m−d. Skip invalid field sizes. At this tuple all ranks have the same length and exact parsing. [Native stream model](time-by-length.md) · [Record-length bound](programmable-clock-spectrum.md)

Put b=k⌈log₂(m+1)⌉, ℓ=b+2, and S=2^ℓ. Split z into a high part u and a low ℓ-bit integer j. There are S ranks j in the current block. For each i in this range reconstruct the full name wᵢ=s q p u binℓ(i). Simulate every conditional program of length at most b on auxiliary input wᵢ through t steps. For each complete output string add the string itself to the forbidden list if it has length m. Also simulate it as an exact V-name through t, adding its published m-bit word, if any, to that same row's list. Include wᵢ itself as an additional candidate V-name and perform the same simulation. There are at most 2(2^(b+1)−1)+1<S forbidden words per row, counting the extra candidate. All these are direct step-bounded simulations, including when the simulated candidate uses M.

The palette for this block consists of 2S words. Follow a fixed zero prefix by u and an arbitrary (ℓ+1)-bit tag, choosing the zero-prefix length to give total length m. The prefix has length d+|s|+|q|+|p|−1≥0; different high parts give disjoint palettes. In increasing row order assign the first palette word absent both from the row's forbidden list and from all preceding assignments in the block. Fewer than S+S words are excluded, so the assignment always succeeds. Emit the assignment for j. The construction is injective over **all** z at this tuple, although neighboring rows may prescribe entirely different candidate lists.

There are O_k(m^(2k)) pairs of row and conditional program. Each pair costs at most t·poly(m) native steps, including simulation on a candidate string of up to t bits. The interpreter's word width and all bounded clock registers have O(m) bits. Fixed-algorithm simulation, storing input and checking exact consumption have a polynomial overhead exponent independent of fixed k and a. The remaining ranking and comparison work is polynomial in m for fixed k. Thus one can choose a=2k+C with a fixed sufficiently large C so that V finishes by T at late lengths. The parameter a is supplied to the same decoder, so this choice does not change its compiler prefix. If wᵢ published by t, its own m-bit result would have been forbidden in row i. Consequently its actual V-time exceeds t, without requiring any lower bound on interpreter slowdown. Any other early name constructible from wᵢ by a conditional program of length at most b and time t was forbidden in the same manner.

## Keeping the assigned names close to shortest

Take q shortest on the ordinary reference machine. Choose p from successive strict conditional halt-time records at its length. Uniformly for these m, K₀(m)≥|q|+|p|−O(1) on the ordinary reference. Hence Σ_{|y|=m}2^(−K(y))≤C₁2^(−|q|−|p|). The constructed cohort has 2^(m−d−|s|−|q|−|p|) distinct outputs. A word with K(y)<m−d−H consumes at least 2ᴴ times its cohort weight 2^(d−m) in the shortest-name measure. Choose H once so C₁2^(−H)<2^(−|s|−1). At least half of each such cohort has |wᵢ|≤K(y)+H. The fixed C₁ and s are independent of d,k.

If an early producer does exist, an unbounded routine receiving w can run it, compute m,t from its parsed parameters, enumerate exact names through t, and print the first one producing the same y. At most t input bits can be consumed in t native steps. This gives the O_V(1) conditional description claimed above. The construction does not place K_T(y) at the exact length |w| or forbid all early names; it proves that any surviving early name escapes the stated conditional description bound.

## When the fast replacement really exists

There is a single efficient native interpreter on which the same conditional lower bound is attained by a pair of **globally shortest names** for the same word. One finishes before t and the other in (t,T]. These words have zero two-clock increment.

Give the fast branch header 0 and the diagonal branch header 10. Behind the latter use the algorithm above with the known prefix 10, giving the ordinary parameter name q the tuple (d,k,a). In its bounded simulations it interprets this same physical native machine, including the literal branch. This can be implemented with a fixed self-interpreter or a quoted copy of the kernel's finite machine index. Put a generic native interpreter behind a sufficiently long prefix of ones, disjoint from both branches.

For q,p and u at a valid tuple, the fast branch reads ℓ+1 tag digits instead of ℓ row digits. It immediately emits the corresponding word of the slow branch's palette. The one-bit shorter header pays for the extra tag digit. Thus the fast and late names both have length m−d. At large record lengths the fast literal is published well before t, since its setup and copying take poly(m). Bounded self-simulation still guarantees that the diagonal name publishes after t.

The total mass of the late cohort at this tuple is 2^(−2−|q|−|p|). The generic interpreter's entire mass of names at this length is at most C'2^(−|q|−|p|) before its extra compiler prefix. Choose that prefix sufficiently long that at most half of the assigned words can have a generic name of length at most m−d. At such a record no other special tuple shares the length, all the current special names have length m−d, and at least half the assigned words have the two indicated globally shortest names. The diagonal avoided every conditional candidate, including generic names and the actual early literal whenever a candidate could reconstruct it.

Given w and the correct ℓ+1 tag digits, a fixed conditional routine parses the short parameters, removes the row bits, and writes the fast code 0 q p u tag. This takes poly(m) time and b+O(1) program bits since ℓ=b+2. For this particular fast shortest name e we therefore have

    b < Kᵗ_P(e|w) ≤ b+O(1),      K_P(e|w)=O(1).

For the unbounded upper estimate one may simply run the diagonal computation and read off the resulting tag. All parameters needed by the conditional routines occur in w. This exhibits actual early replacement despite the prescribed lower bound on the cost of predicting it from the late name. A polynomial list of larger degree can cover this palette.
