# A time–treewidth lower bound for actual SAT computations

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Fix a deterministic packed logarithmic-word RAM that signals UNSAT only on unsatisfiable formulas and eventually signals on every such input. On a negative input of encoded bit length $N$, let $T$ count steps through its first signal. Let $w$ be the treewidth of the undirected graph on those actual steps, with edges for consecutive times and for writable loads from their last writing instructions. For every fixed $0<\lambda\le1$ its worst-case cost
$$
 (T+1)(w+1)^\lambda
$$
is outside $O(N^c)$ for every fixed $c<(1+\sqrt{1+4\lambda})/2$. At weight one the threshold is the golden ratio. More generally, a joint guarantee $T=O(N^a)$ and $w=O(N^v)$ is impossible if $a\ge1$, $v\ge0$, and $a+v<1+1/a$. The physical workspace is unrestricted. Treewidth can still be as large as $T-1$.

Both assertions also hold for the **minimum** width over the eligible anchor choices of the separator verifier. A read may use an earlier write to its address of its actual value if all intervening writes to that address stored that same value. A zero read may use initialization when there is no intervening different value. Work slots in one instruction have a fixed sequential order. Neither the solver nor the verifier needs to produce a tree decomposition. ## Paying for a cut down to a specified component size

Given a graph on $T$ vertices with treewidth $w$ and an integer $b\ge1$, a set of at most $2(w+1)T/b$ vertices leaves all components of order at most $b$. Restrict a width-$w$ decomposition to each component of order $u>b$. Assign every graph vertex to one bag that contains it and give the decomposition tree the resulting integer weights. It has a centroid bag with at most $u/2$ of the weight on each side. After its vertices are removed, the remaining graph components lie on single sides by edge coverage and connectedness of the bags containing each remaining vertex. Recurse on the oversized components. Charge every vertex present in a processed component of order $u$ at most $(w+1)/u$. Along any vertex's history the component size decreases by at least a factor of two. The reciprocal-size charges sum to at most $2(w+1)/b$, proving the claim. In particular this handles recursive cuts with arbitrarily many children.

The usable certificate lists cut times and their bounded instruction records, with one component label per surviving *chronological interval*. There are at most $|S|+1$ such intervals, even when a component consists of many nonadjacent pieces. A proposed defect selects at most two components. It replays their intervals using the cut records and private writes, checks controllers, and can challenge any proposed load by its real temporal order, address and an intervening different-value write. This forces one genuine trajectory if all challenges fail. On arbitrary proposed data the replay rule and size caps are deterministic. The certificate has $(1+|S|)\operatorname{polylog}H$ bits and the bounded existential defect test takes $(1+b+|S|)\operatorname{polylog}H$ time including preparation of its cut directory. The exponent here refers to a polynomial native time horizon in $H$. The verifier only needs *existence* of the economical cut on an honest run. No promise on an adversarial certificate or on runs on positive formulas is used. [Replay, checks and explicit input charges](slab-product-tradeoff.md)

## Charging the bounded source and the cut separately

Write $W(e,d)$ when nonacceptance of each fixed nondeterministic RAM computation with clock $R$ and $m$ public words has a sound nondeterministic certificate within $(R^e+m^d+1)H^{o(1)}$ steps, $H=\max(2,R,m)$. Its ordinary packed SAT instance has length $N=H^{1+o(1)}$ and individually accessible words from the source input. Assume for contradiction a product upper bound of exponent $c$, and set $d=c$. A separate time-$O(N^d)$ bound on all negative instances may be used for $d$ instead. Materializing the formula and clocking the recognizer on it gives $W(d,d)$, for $d\ge1$. A slightly larger exponent within a strict inequality absorbs logarithms, including when the stated bound has $c\le1$. [Bounded interpreter and packed formula](packed-band-transfer.md)

Suppose $W(e,d)$ holds with $1\le e\le d$. For short rejecting runs of at most $H^{g+o(1)}$ steps, replay the program on the virtual formula directly. For a longer run, since $\lambda\le1$, the product bound gives
$$
 T(w+1)\le H^{(c-(1-\lambda)g)/\lambda+o(1)}.
$$
Use components of at most $H^{x+o(1)}$ steps. Up to subpolynomial factors the cut payload has exponent $y=(c-(1-\lambda)g)/\lambda-x$. Choose $xe=yd=g$. This gives
$$
 g=\frac{c}{1-\lambda+\lambda/d+\lambda/e}.
$$
Here $x\ge y>0$. On the branch guessing the chart, use $W(e,d)$ for absence of a defect. The defect computation runs in $H^{x+o(1)}$ and its *physical* public input has $m+H^{y+o(1)}$ words. All original SAT input reads in replay use the local generator. The resulting bound is $(H^g+m^d+1)H^{o(1)}$. With $g\le d$, the case $H=m>R$ is paid by $m^d$, so this is $W(g,d)$. Rational rounding and fixed finite nesting permit arbitrarily small slack. If $\lambda$ is irrational, reduce it slightly and use a rational weight still satisfying the strict inequality, using monotonicity of the assumed product upper bound. [Resource accounting with short-run replay](weighted-time-minor.md)

Taking reciprocals yields an affine iteration of slope $\lambda/c$ with positive fixed-point exponent $(c-\lambda)/(1-\lambda+\lambda/d)$ for $c>1$. For $c<1+\lambda/d$ this fixed point lies below one. Starting with $e=d$, the iterates decrease, remain at most $d$, and reach $g<1$ after finitely many steps. Use the resulting certificate on the bounded interpreter's own padded descriptor, with public length $O(n_0)$ and horizon $R=n_0^D$ for fixed $D>d$. It certifies nonacceptance in $o(R)$ steps. A timely signal makes the very interpreter accept, while failure of every branch to accept would guarantee a timely sound signal. This contradiction proves the bound. Substituting $d=c$ yields $c^2-c<\lambda$ and the first threshold. Substituting $\lambda=1$, $d=a$, $c=a+v$ proves the joint exclusion. If a boundary parameter equals one, enlarge the assumed exponents infinitesimally while preserving the strict inequality. At $d=e=1$ the unrestricted envelope $c=1+\lambda$ returns $g=1$. Running this recurrence alone cannot exclude linear time. ## How wide the actual graphs of fast solvers must be

Assume a fixed reference solver decides SAT in $O(N\log^a N)$ deterministic time, and use its small-list, two-sign construction with seed length $L^2$, where $L=\lceil\log_2(H+2)\rceil$. The same good seeds support the following statement for every fixed correct near-linear SAT solver $A$, which may have its own time constants and logarithmic exponent $a_A$. At all sufficiently large scales for $A$, at **both** pins of **every good seed**, its actual graph has
$$
 w+1\ge N/\log^{D_A}N
$$
for some fixed $D_A$. This includes the minimum over valid same-value choices. The good-seed set is chosen by the fixed reference generator and has failure probability at most $2L/2^{L^2}$. Constants and admission thresholds in the conclusion depend on the target. [Common-distribution theorem](common-solver-seeds.md)

Indeed its fixed sign certifier guesses the cut directory, checks its size and component-size cap, and uses the reference to decide the existential defect test. This takes $(1+b+|S|+L^2)\operatorname{polylog}N$ time with the short seed and fully charged chart. A successful chart certifies the true *claimed* verdict on every well-formed input. The reference solver can check defects regardless of the sign of $A$'s final verdict. On a good pin, offers are impossible within $M=H/L^K$ and $N=\Theta(H L^3)$. Choose $b=\lfloor N/L^J\rfloor$ with $J$ larger than $K+3$ and the fixed verification losses combined. If $w+1<N/L^{D_A}$ then $|S|=O((w+1)T/b)=O(NL^{a_A+J-D_A})$. Taking $D_A>a_A+2J$ makes the honest offer fit $M$, a contradiction. The small-list construction keeps the physical cost of the public seed below this same bound.

The single-head pathwidth bound now has a consequence for these actual runs. If a single head on one fixed $d$-dimensional tape has a $U$-step chronology-and-consecutive-visit graph containing one of them as an undirected minor, treewidth monotonicity and the host's pathwidth $O_d(U^{d/(d+1)})$ imply $U\ge N^{1+1/d}/\operatorname{polylog}N$. For $d=1$ there can also be a planarity obstruction. A different algorithm can decide the same formula using a different dependency graph. [Weighted fragmentation and single-head path decompositions](spatial-grate-simulation.md)

There is a statement about the address sequence itself. Place the actually touched writable addresses of each of the fixed number $h$ of banks injectively in $\mathbb Z^d$, choosing even an optimal placement separately after each run. Choose arbitrary step anchors. Let $\rho_t$ be the sum of coordinate ranges of the locations touched at $t$ together with the current and previous anchors. Use the true last-write graph here. Its pathwidth is at most $1+hq^d+\sum_t\min(1,\rho_t/q)$ for each positive integer $q$ by the shifted-grid bound. Taking $q=\lfloor(N/\log^{D_A+2}N)^{1/d}\rfloor$ shows that the sum is $\Omega(N/\log^{D_A}N)$. Steps with $\rho_t<q/\log^{D_A+a_A+2}N$ contribute $O(N/\log^{D_A+2}N)$ altogether. At least $\Omega(N/\log^{D_A}N)$ steps therefore span $N^{1/d}/\operatorname{polylog}N$ or more in **every** such offline layout. This counts large jumps or widely separated accesses within one instruction. It imposes no cost for an individual jump in the RAM model. [Address placement and anchor definition](spatial-grate-simulation.md)

## Varying the host length independently of the actual RAM time

There is also an unconditional two-parameter restriction. Suppose every negative run of length $E=O(N^a)$ has an eligible access graph realized as a minor of some single-head $d$-dimensional host of length $U=O(N^u)$, with fixed $u\ge a\ge1$. Neither the hosts nor their minor models need be uniformly available. Write $\alpha=1/(d+1)$. Then one cannot have
$$
 (1-\alpha)u+\alpha a<\alpha+1/a .
$$
To see the fragment bound, mark one host vertex per target branch set. The shifted-grid lemma costs $U/L+(L^d+1)E/b$ deletions to leave at most $b$ marks per component. If $E>b$, choose integer $L\asymp_d(Ub/E)^\alpha$, valid since $U\ge E$. Mark every branch set struck by a deletion. The intact target components lift to intact host components and therefore have at most $b$ vertices. The target cut costs $O_d(U^{1-\alpha}(E/b)^\alpha)$, absorbing the $E/b$ term. For $E\le b$ use no cut. [Proof for general marked step sets](spatial-grate-simulation.md)

Put $p=(1-\alpha)u+\alpha a$. With component exponent $x=g/e$, the chart exponent is now $p-\alpha x$. Equating this to $g/a$ gives $1/g=(1/a+\alpha/e)/p$. Starting with $W(a,a)$, the fixed-point exponent is $a(p-\alpha)$, which is below one under the displayed strict inequality. The same bounded-source argument applies, including $x\ge g/a$ and the unbalanced public-input case. For $u=a$ the lower threshold on $a$ is $(\alpha+\sqrt{4+\alpha^2})/2$. A native single-head tape can write its current symbol back after every visit, with constant overhead, so consecutive visits give its memory dependencies. The offline minor premise also covers ordinary RAM programs using arbitrary addresses and an input-dependent host. Williams already obtained *higher* time exponents on actual geometric tape machines, including about 1.3009 and 1.1887 for dimensions one and two. The result here concerns graph preservation by a RAM on its own executions. [Williams, §7](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf) · [van Melkebeek and Raz](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dieter/Research/sat-t.html)
