# A two-dimensional sweep that breaks the PPST contraction estimate

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** In the chronological graph of a tape computation put a vertex at each step, an edge to the next step, and a directed edge from each visit to the next visit to the same writable cell. A position can be read and rewritten at each visit. PPST's one-dimensional argument bounds the **number of distinct arcs after contracting temporal intervals** via a decomposition into a fixed number of noncrossing edge classes. Their segregator theorem uses the resulting sparsity at its chosen scale. A single two-dimensional tape can already have a dense contraction at a specified scale. [Paul, Pippenger, Szemerédi and Trotter, proof of Theorem 2.1](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)

For odd $k$, visit every cell of a $k\times k$ square once in row order, snaking through alternate rows. End at a corner. Starting at that same corner, visit every cell once in column order, snaking through alternate columns. One stationary move joins the two sweeps. There are $n=2k^2$ visits. This is a nearest-neighbor head trajectory. Directions can be marked at the ends of rows and columns in the initial square for a uniform machine; a separate fixed head scanning direction instructions also realizes the sequence. For a graph question the trajectory suffices.

Contract the visits in each row of the first sweep to one vertex and each column of the second sweep to one vertex. These are $2k$ **consecutive time intervals of the same length**. For every pair $(i,j)$, the consecutive visits to cell $(i,j)$ give an edge from row interval $i$ to column interval $j$. Thus the contraction contains all $k^2$ arcs of the directed complete bipartite graph. Discarding $o(k^2)$ original vertices can destroy at most $o(k^2)$ of these particular arcs, since the witness edges have disjoint original endpoints. At this scale there can be no uniform $O(k)$ bound on the number of contracted arcs. The example concerns the ordinary cell-dependency graph and the indicated temporal contraction. Extrapolating it to every scale or to every possible block simulation would require another argument.

The same graph admits small segregators. Delete both visits to every cell in each $s$th row or $s$th column, where $s=\lceil k^{2/3}\rceil$. This removes $O(k^2/s)$ vertices. Every remaining connected component projects into a rectangle of side at most $s$, since a time edge moves by at most one lattice unit and a last-visit edge preserves the cell. A component has at most $2s^2$ vertices. Hence $O(n^{2/3})$ deletions bound even undirected component size by $O(n^{2/3})$. A method that tests only contracted density would misclassify this example as a candidate obstruction to segregation.

## A bound for every one-head cell graph

More generally take **any** $n$-visit trajectory of a single writable head on $\mathbb Z^d$, with fixed $d$, and use the same chronological and last-visit edges. Choose a grid of side $u$. Averaging over offsets modulo $u$ in each dimension, some choice places at most $dn/u$ visits on its coordinate hyperplanes. Delete those visits. Every remaining edge has both endpoints in one open grid box, including memory edges that jumped over a time of deletion.

For each nonempty box, list its remaining visits chronologically. Divide the list into groups of $q$ visits except possibly the last. At a cut between two groups, at most one chronological edge straddles the cut, since a time edge joins consecutive original visits. For each of the at most $u^d$ cells in the box, at most one surviving last-visit edge straddles the cut. Delete the source of every such edge. A box with $v$ visits has at most $v/q$ internal cuts, so the additional deletions cost at most $(u^d+1)n/q$. All components now have at most $q$ vertices. Taking $u=\lceil n^{1/(d+2)}\rceil$ and $q=\lceil n^{(d+1)/(d+2)}\rceil$ bounds the deletion count and residual component sizes by $O_d(n^{(d+1)/(d+2)})$. This is a statement about graphs with one head's cell dependencies. An independent input head contributes further edges if its history is included.

With two independently moving writable heads, a memory edge preserving the first head's cell can join times at which the second head occupies entirely different boxes. The box confinement step fails for the tuple of head positions. A subsequent construction gives two-head block graphs for which deleting a small linear fraction of the vertices still leaves quadratically many reachable ordered pairs. This answers the general segregator question negatively for those graphs. [Two-plane grates](two-plane-directed-grates.md) Neither of the examples gives the proposed separation of time classes.
