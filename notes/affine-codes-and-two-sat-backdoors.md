# Exact cost of reducing a reusable affine constraint to 2-SAT

A cycle with one Boolean sign per edge gives a dynamic 2-SAT test. For a fixed sign vector $s$ impose $x_u+x_v=s_e$ on each edge over $\mathbb F_2$. Each such equation needs only two binary clauses. A single sign flip toggles satisfiability, so after initialization a RAM updates the decision in constant time. Suppose instead that a bounded-run compiler builds *one* existential 3-CNF, and every sign must be selectable later by pinning its terminal bit. On a cycle with $n\ge 2$ edges, making every residual formula 2-CNF by fixing a common set of variables requires **at least $\lceil n/2\rceil-1$ variables, and this is attainable**, allowing arbitrary extension variables and arbitrary numbers of clauses. There is an exact formula for every affine relation.

Let $C=a+L\subseteq\mathbb F_2^n$ be a nonempty affine code, $r=\dim L$, and let $b$ be the largest number of nonzero words in $L$ with pairwise disjoint supports. An encoding $E(x,y)$ is required to satisfy $x\in C\iff\exists y\, E(x,y)$ for every terminal string $x$. A strong 2-CNF backdoor is a set of variables, terminal or auxiliary, whose every assignment simplifies every clause to width at most two or satisfies it. Count the total number of variables assigned across the whole formula. Then

* The largest subset of $C$ closed under coordinatewise majority has $2^b$ elements.
* The minimum number of 2-CNF solution sets whose union is $C$ is $2^{r-b}$. Allowing existential auxiliaries in the pieces gives the same minimum.
* The minimum strong 2-CNF backdoor over **all** existential 3-CNF encodings is exactly $r-b$. The same answer holds if the next variable is chosen adaptively along one global binary branching tree and every leaf must project from a 2-CNF. Measures permitting different connected components to be solved in parallel count a different resource.

**Why the affine target matters.** Translate a nonempty majority-closed subset $S$ of $C$ by $s\in S$. Per-coordinate complementation commutes with majority. The translate $S'=S+s$ contains zero, so it is closed under coordinatewise multiplication $xy=\operatorname{maj}(0,x,y)$. Its linear span $W\subseteq L$ is also closed under multiplication, by bilinearity. Choose a nonzero $u\in W$ of inclusion-minimal support. For every $v\in W$, $uv$ equals zero or $u$, since its support is a subset of the support of $u$. Hence every $v$ is uniquely the sum of a word vanishing on that support and either zero or $u$. The words vanishing there form a smaller subspace also closed under multiplication. Induction gives a basis of $W$ with disjoint supports. Consequently $|S|\le |W|\le 2^b$. Conversely choose $b$ disjoint words in $L$. In their span every coordinate is zero, or equals the free bit for its unique basis support. Each affine coset of this span is described by pins and equalities or disequalities, all 2-CNF. The $2^{r-b}$ cosets partition $C$. This proves the extremal claim, including $L=0$.

A binary clause satisfied by three assignments is satisfied by their majority: if both literals failed on the majority, at least one of the three assignments would fail both. Majority closure survives conjunction, fixed variable values and projection, taking majorities of three witnesses. Therefore any permitted leaf covers at most $2^b$ valid terminal words. A binary tree of depth $d$ has at most $2^d$ leaves, giving $d\ge r-b$.

For the matching *single encoding*, extend the disjoint words to a basis of $L$. Use one selector for each of the $r-b$ added words, and one free bit for each disjoint word. To compute a terminal coordinate start with its affine offset and, if present, the one free bit belonging to that support. XOR in the selectors whose added basis vectors contain this coordinate, one at a time. Encode each XOR step with the usual four ternary clauses. Every such clause contains the selector used at this step. Pins and signed equalities suffice at the two ends. Fixing all $r-b$ selectors leaves syntactic width at most two even before unit propagation. This construction uses $O(n(r-b+1))$ clauses given the chosen basis. The characterization makes no efficiency claim for finding an optimal disjoint family in a given code.

**Consequences for packed RAM steps.** For the cycle, the terminal signs form the even-weight code, with $r=n-1$ and $b=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. If branching may inspect only terminal signs, the cycle optimum is instead $n-2$. Fixing fewer signs leaves parity on at least three unknowns, which fails majority closure. An XOR chain starting at the first unbranched sign, XOR-ing in the $n-2$ selected signs, and equating the result to the second unbranched sign attains this bound syntactically. For the complete relation of the instruction $z=x\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}y$ on $w$ bit lanes, $r=2w$ and $b=w$. Each active triple of one codeword uses two of its three coordinates, preventing a second disjoint codeword from using that same triple. A fixed RAM can also check $T$ independent pairs of operand words against $T$ result words in $O(T)$ steps. Any reusable existential 3-CNF for all pins of that relation needs exactly $Tw$ variables in an optimal strong 2-CNF backdoor. More generally, if $q$ variable-disjoint width-three clauses form a maximal such family, after discarding tautologies and repeated literals, their at most $7^q$ satisfying joint assignments leave only binary clauses. Therefore $7^q\ge 2^{r-b}$ in every width-three encoding. This quantifies the limitation of the [native residual-2-SAT search](native-core-extensions.md) on reusable word relations. The cycle algorithm itself pays no such branching cost. A compiler specializing to already known fixed signs can simply print the corresponding binary clauses.

Paturi, Saks and Zane studied the same 2-CNF cover measure for depth-three circuits and used binary codes for lower bounds. Frankl, Gryaznov and Talebanfard sharpened bounds relating a general 2-CNF's solution count to the dimension of a signed-equality projection *inside* it. Here every leaf has an affine hull *inside the target code*, which permits the exact support-packing count. Standard SAT-backdoor terminology and lower bounds on the **total** size of parity encodings are also directly relevant. I have not established priority for the exact affine-code formula or the encoding-independent backdoor consequence.
