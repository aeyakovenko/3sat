# Estimating early-program prefix probabilities from the output payload

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Can an arbitrary fixed efficient universal prefix machine generate a near-incompressible word that gains a description between $t=\lfloor2^{3m}/m^a\rfloor$ and $T=2^{3m}$ without simulating every early program? A single payload can sample the early computations and still appear verbatim in the output. An explicit condition on the resulting prefix probabilities suffices. A fixed-point argument shows why this condition needs information about the machine. [Clocked increment series](clocked-complexity-increments.md)

## The statistic and the decoder

Use the reference halting names $p$, their times $t_p$, and distinct paired indices $r=r(p,t_p)$ from the [common grant experiment](one-bit-production.md). There are infinitely many tight indices with $w_r=2^{-|p|}\ge\delta\mathbf m(r)$, where $\mathbf m$ is the universal discrete semimeasure. Put $m=2^r$. At tight indices $|p|\le K(r)+O(1)=O(\log(r+2))$. The decoder can recover $r$ by running $p$, with $t_p\le r$. Take a fixed positive deficit $d$, a fixed sufficiently large header allowance $c$, $\ell=m-d$, and $k=|p|+d+c$. Discard finitely many small lengths as needed.

Give the native machine $V$ a uniform $\ell$-bit stream. Ignore any computation reading beyond the supplied bits. If it finishes by $t$ with an $m$-bit word, record its first $k$ bits. Write $\mu_r(j)$ for the probabilities of its tags $j\in\{0,1\}^k$. The total is at most $C\mathbf m(r)$, since the Kraft weights of *all* halting names outputting length $2^r$, without any time bound, form a lower semicomputable summable sequence in $r$. The total is also at least a fixed multiple of $w_r$ at all sufficiently late tight indices: a fixed decoder can read $p$, recover $r$, and print $m$ zeroes well before $t$. The choice of a low-mass **subset** of the tags matters.

Let a fixed deterministic rule $S$ take the public parameters and a rational estimated tag histogram and return a tag. Require time polynomial in $m$ on the sampler representations below. For $\varepsilon=1/m$ let $J_r$ contain all its answers on rational subprobability histograms within $\varepsilon$ of $\mu_r$ in every coordinate. Suppose along infinitely many tight indices

$$\mu_r(J_r)=o(w_r).\tag{1}$$

This condition would give an unconditional random exact-deficit increment series at some deficit between $d$ and $d+O(1)$, for this $V$ and clock pair, provided $a$ is large enough for the sampler. The constants can depend on the fixed rule, $d$, and machine. The premise concerns the law at the given early clock. It has not been proved for $V$.

To see the time and entropy budgets, after $p$ read a raw payload $u$ of length $m-k$. For an input $x$ of that length let $f_j(x)$ average the tag-$j$ indicator over all $2^{|p|+c}$ completions to an $\ell$-bit stream. Its uniform mean is $\mu_r(j)$. Average $f_j(u+s)$ over an explicit multiset of $\beta$-biased shifts $s$ on $m-k$ bits for $\beta=m^{-2}$. This yields one nonnegative rational subprobability vector $\widehat\mu(u)$, with no other random input. On nonconstant Walsh characters the convolution multiplies magnitudes by at most $\beta$. Hence

$$\mathbb E_u\sum_j |\widehat\mu_j(u)-\mu_r(j)|^2\le \beta^2\sum_j\operatorname{Var}(f_j)\le \beta^2,$$

since $0\le f_j\le1$ and their sum is at most one. All coordinates are within $1/m$ for at least $1-m^{-2}$ of the payloads.

For instance choose a binary field of size $q\ge m/\beta$ and enumerate all $(z,b)$ in its square, using bit $i$ equal to the binary inner product of $b$ and the representation of $z^i$, starting at $i=0$. A nonempty set of indices gives a nonzero polynomial of degree less than $m$. Averaging over $b$ makes its character zero unless that polynomial vanishes at $z$. This proves the bias bound by root counting. An irreducible defining polynomial of degree $\lceil\log_2(m/\beta)\rceil$ can be found by a polynomial-in-$m$ search and division. At the tight indices $q^2$, $2^{|p|+c}$, and the histogram length are all polynomial in $m$. Simulating each $t$-step native trial with polynomial-in-$m$ step overhead yields total cost $t\operatorname{poly}(m)$, below $T$ for sufficiently large $a$. A single routine taking $a$ as a fixed parameter can keep the exponent of the simulation overhead independent of $a$. If varying $a$ and allowing different selectors, their cost exponents must be paid separately. [Dickinson and Nayak, Appendix B, finite-field small-bias construction](https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~anayak/papers/DickinsonN06.pdf)

Print $S(\widehat\mu(u))$ followed by $u$. Including the fixed compiler, parameters, $p$ and $u$, the name length is at most $\ell$, by choosing $c$ larger than the header and its self-delimiting specification. Every word with a producing name of length at most $\ell$ by $t$ contributes at least $2^{-\ell}$ stream probability to its tag. Since the payloads give distinct outputs, the fraction with an accurate estimate whose outputs have such a name is at most

$$2^{\ell-(m-k)}\mu_r(J_r)=2^{c}\mu_r(J_r)/w_r.$$

By (1) this tends to zero. Also $\sum_{|y|=2^r}2^{-K(y)}\le C'\mathbf m(r)$ by another disjoint Kraft sum, now over shortest ordinary names. For a sufficiently large fixed $H$, fewer than a quarter of the payloads have output complexity at most $m-k+|p|-H=m-d-c-H$. Therefore a positive fraction have $m-d-c-H<K(y)\le K_T(y)\le m-d<K_t(y)$. Their increments are bounded below by a fixed multiple of $2^{-K(y)}$. There are only finitely many possible exact values of $m-K_T(y)$ in this interval. Some value recurs infinitely often, giving the stated randomness by the [Bienvenu–Shen term criterion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5028). Computing the nearby-answer set $J_r$ plays no part in the decoder.

## A limitation of selection from prefix probabilities alone

For any number $n$ of tags, any positive rational mass $b\le1$, any tolerance $\varepsilon>0$, and any deterministic rule on rational tag histograms, there exists a subprobability vector $\mu$ of total mass $b$ whose entire mass lies on the answers occurring within $\varepsilon$ of $\mu$. Triangulate the unit probability simplex with rational vertices and mesh smaller than $\varepsilon/(2b)$. Map each vertex $v$ to the unit vector of the answer at $bv$ and extend affinely on each simplex. Brouwer's theorem gives a fixed point $x$. In the simplex containing $x$, it is a convex combination of the unit vectors of the vertex answers. Thus every positive coordinate of $x$ occurs as an answer at a histogram within $\varepsilon/2$ of $bx$. One can choose $x$ rational, since in that rational simplex the fixed-point equations and inequalities are rational linear constraints. This shows that the ratio in (1) cannot be forced small for *all* possible histograms of total $w_r$, no matter how fine the estimation. The constructed vector need not be the early-program law of the fixed $V$. The outstanding question is whether its genuine histograms admit efficient low-mass selection, or whether correlations with the payload can be used directly without taking the union over nearby answers.
