# How many discovered input bits must be read again?

Fix a valid physical 3-SAT face on which a chosen verdict is exactly membership in a binary linear code $C\subseteq\{0,1\}^k$. Label its live bits by their code coordinates, absorbing any fixed bit complements. Let $d$ and $d_\perp$ be the minimum distances of $C$ and its dual. The dense fixed-record construction can place the pins in a prescribed collection of physical positions and realize either SAT sign. Fix any total correct deterministic solver, without an elapsed-time or workspace bound. Draw its actual input uniformly from the codewords. [Physical face and code choices](selected-input-rereads.md)

Choose $1\le\ell<\min(d,d_\perp)$. Cut the computation just after its $\ell$th *distinct* live bit is exposed. Let $P$ be the ordered string of first answers, $I$ the set of exposed live coordinates, and $S$ the complete mutable state at the cut. For a genuine suffix let $Q\subseteq I$ be the coordinates it exposes again, counting each at most once. A prefix is called cheap at $r$ if **some genuine codeword completion of that prefix** has $|Q|\le r$. Write $\pi_r$ for the probability of such a prefix. A prefix outside this event forces more than $r$ returns on every codeword completion.

For $0\le r\le\ell$, write $V_\ell(r)=\sum_{j=0}^r\binom\ell j$, let $D$ be the number of attainable pairs $(S,I)$, and take entropies in bits. Then

$$\pi_r\ \le\ \min\{1,D\,V_\ell(r)/2^\ell\},\qquad
\pi_r\big(\ell-\log_2 V_\ell(r)\big)\ \le\ H(S,I)+H_2(\pi_r).$$

In particular, if $H(S,I)=o(\ell)$ along a sequence with $\ell\to\infty$, almost every prefix forces at least $(1/2-o(1))\ell$ returns on every genuine completion. This includes $D=2^{o(\ell)}$. For any fixed $a<1/2$, the bound $\log_2 V_\ell(\lfloor a\ell\rfloor)\le H_2(a)\ell$ gives the claim with $a$ in place of $1/2-o(1)$; a diagonal choice of $a\uparrow1/2$ gives the stated form. The expected return count on an actual uniform codeword is also at least $(1/2-o(1))\ell$ under this hypothesis.

## Which information is fixed at the cut

Expand a load exposing several live pins into separate bit tests in a fixed order. The intermediate controller includes its current address, substep, and partial reply up to that substep. Raw pin values still to be tested are not available earlier in this mathematical expansion. The completed load returns exactly the native word. Each physical load on the specified layout exposes $O(1)$ live pins, so the distinction between these substeps and a cut rounded to the end of the physical load costs at most $O(1)$ returns in counting reads later than that load. A second exposure of a given pin requires a later physical load. Include all registers and control, and any clock information the machine can access, in $S$. Its immutable input remains the original physical formula.

Every codeword computation exposes each live bit: flipping one unseen bit would leave the verdict intact and take the input outside $C$. Until the prescribed cut, all transitions are fixed by past answers, since every other physical input bit is fixed on the face. The projection of $C$ onto fewer than $d_\perp$ coordinates is onto: a deficient projection would give a nonzero dual vector supported there. A uniform codeword therefore gives each adaptive sequence $P$ of $\ell$ answers probability $2^{-\ell}$, by following its fixed path and applying this projection fact. In particular $(S,I)$ is determined by $P$. Within a class of prefixes with the same pair $(S,I)$, the assignments on $I$ distinguish the prefixes. If two such assignments were equal, deterministic execution from the common initial state would give exactly the same observations and order up to the cut.

## One suffix gives a private restriction

Fix one class $(s,I)$, a prefix $p$ in it, and an actual extending codeword $a$. Let $Q$ be the bits of $I$ exposed in its suffix. No other prefix $q$ in the class agrees with the values $a|_Q$. Otherwise replace $a|_I$ by the assignment on $I$ from $q$ and keep every other coordinate of $a$. This hybrid follows the whole genuine prefix of $q$ to state $s$. Its suffix now follows the genuine suffix of $a$: outside $I$ the input is unchanged, and all coordinates in $I$ actually tested in that suffix are in $Q$. The hybrid gets the chosen verdict but differs from $a$ on between one and $\ell<d$ coordinates, so it cannot be in $C$. This contradiction proves the restriction is private. [Related codeword splicing in Jukna and Razborov, Proposition 3 and Theorem 1](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/few.pdf)

It follows directly that $(S,I,Q,a|_Q)$ determines $P$. Encoding $|Q|$, its subset of $I$, and the ordered values on that subset gives

$$\ell\ \le\ H(S)+H(I\mid S)+\log_2(\ell+1)
       +\mathbb E[\log_2\tbinom\ell{|Q|}+|Q|].$$

The rank argument removes the separate cost of sending the values when bounding the number of cheap prefixes. In one fixed class, choose for each cheap $p$ one codeword completion with $|Q_p|\le r$. The indicator that an assignment to $I$ matches its answers on $Q_p$ is the product of the corresponding literals $X_i$ or $1-X_i$. On the class's assignments it is one at $p$ and zero at every other prefix. Thus these indicators are linearly independent, while they lie in the span of the $V_\ell(r)$ squarefree monomials of degree at most $r$. Each class contains at most $V_\ell(r)$ cheap prefixes, independently of how its completions were chosen.

Summation over classes gives the support bound. For the entropy bound let $E$ indicate that the prefix is cheap. Conditional on $(S,I,E=1)$ the prefix entropy is at most $\log_2 V_\ell(r)$, and conditional on $(S,I,E=0)$ it is at most $\ell$. Hence

$$\ell=H(P)\le H(S,I)+H_2(\pi_r)+\pi_r\log_2 V_\ell(r)+(1-\pi_r)\ell.$$

Rearranging gives the second inequality, including distributions with many rare states or cut sets.

There is a classical sharpening of the finite class bound. For each cheap prefix extend its chosen restriction by arbitrary matching bits to fix exactly $r$ coordinates of $I$. The resulting $(\ell-r)$-dimensional subcube contains that prefix assignment, and none of the other such subcubes contains it. The family is irredundant. Meshulam's bound allows $V_\ell(r)$ above to be replaced by the smaller of itself and

$$\frac{2^\ell\binom\ell r}{\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-r}\binom\ell j}.$$

The polynomial count and this extremal bound concern private subcubes; the solver-specific step is obtaining them from honest completions at equal states and equal discovered sets. Unrestricted elapsed time allows a solver to retain the entire prefix. [Ellis, Theorem 4, giving Meshulam's bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.2960)

## Replacing the whole state with a description that works on prefix completions

The same bounds apply directly to certain locally decodable snapshots of the solver. For each first-answer history $p$, choose a string and controller information $Z_p$ for the cut. Allow arbitrary computation to choose it. The decoder still sees the *whole original formula*. Require that, with this same chart, replay gives the correct verdict on **every completion of $p$ on the face**, including noncodewords. A sufficient memory condition is that on every such completion the decoder reconstructs every incoming word actually used by suffix replay until that word is overwritten. A full-memory decoder correct throughout the prefix's completions suffices as well. Tag writes of zero in the replay overlay. Count cut times, registers, advice lengths and other variable metadata as part of $Z_p$, unless already determined by $I$ or the public parameters.

Replace $S$ by $Z_P$ in the support and entropy bounds. In $Q$ now count distinct old pins read **either by the suffix itself or in the decoder's queries while serving that suffix**. Indeed two histories in the same $(Z,I)$ class use the same replay program on the hybrid. Starting from its chart, replay follows the execution for $a$ if the hybrid agrees on its queried old bits: every other physical bit already agrees. Yet the hybrid is a completion of $q$ and its verdict differs. The stipulated correctness of $Z_q$ on that completion gives the contradiction. The private restrictions, rank and counting proof then apply verbatim. For $\pi_r$ take the cheap-completion event under the selected chart for each prefix. The support version also allows choosing any qualifying chart per cheap prefix, provided its class is counted.

Thus a short prefix-valid description at a low-entropy discovery set forces the decoder and native suffix together to reread nearly half the old bits on typical prefixes. This includes the example of a copied input prefix: its cheap decoder uses the original formula to supply a previously copied word. The unrestricted solver-snapshot theorem elsewhere permits advice chosen for one complete input and correct only there. In the splicing proof, the needed input is the noncodeword with prefix $q$ and the outside bits of $a$. A hindsight chart correct solely on a codeword completion of $q$ can fail exactly on that hybrid. On the two-bank equality face below, a program can copy the first bank and then compare it to the second. On equal inputs a constant-time hindsight decoder can return a copied data word by reading its corresponding second-bank word. It serves every copied word without a single original first-bank reread by either decoder or suffix. On a hybrid with unequal banks it would return the wrong copy. On a good-code face an unrestricted-time decoder can likewise recover erased parity pins from the later message; its per-word lookup time is a separate charge. This exposes the missing premise in the general hindsight case. [General snapshot interface](solver-snapshot-descriptions.md)

### An estimate that does allow hindsight advice

On the good-code face, consider an actual copy of the first bank, at a public cut after the copy. Ask any fixed decoder for the copied values at a predetermined set $J$ of $m$ old pins in separate words. It receives the whole genuine input and a shared string of at most $s$ bits chosen after seeing the whole codeword. Each of the $m$ queries can expose at most $q$ live input bits in total, including bits in returned words that are later masked off. If $m(q+1)<d_\perp$, then for every $0\le t\le m$,
$$
 \Pr[\text{some such string answers all }m\text{ correctly while leaving at least }t
      \text{ pins of }J\text{ unread}]\ \le\ 2^{s+1-t}.
$$
For a fixed string, condition on the entire adaptive transcript of the $m$ calls. Its leaf fixes only the values of the exposed coordinates. Together with $J$ there are fewer than $d_\perp$ coordinates, so the unprobed pins of $J$ remain independent fair bits at that leaf. The decoder's predictions are already fixed. Sum the leaf bounds and then count the strings. This argument also works for any fixed list of state-word requests whose correct outputs reveal those target bits without further input queries.

For $\ell=\Theta(k)$ and $(s+\log k+1)(q+1)=o(k)$, take consecutive disjoint batches of size at most $\min(\ell,\lfloor(d_\perp-1)/(q+1)\rfloor)$. Set $t=s+\lceil4\log_2 k\rceil+2$; batches of fewer than $t$ members lose fewer than $t$ pins trivially. Except on a fraction $O(k^{-3})$ of codewords, every description decoding the entire copy causes these requests collectively to reread $\ell-o(\ell)$ distinct original first-bank pins. This recovers a hindsight conclusion by charging total input exposures per decoded word and specifying the contents to be recovered. The copying program itself still fits a scan on the family. No claim that all SAT solvers copy such a bank is needed or implied.

## How much information buys a given number of returns?

At $r=\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor$, Meshulam's class bound is $O(2^\ell/\sqrt\ell)$ by the central binomial estimate. If every prefix admits a completion with at most this many returns, then every pair $(S,I)$ has probability $O(1/\sqrt\ell)$. In particular $H(S,I)\ge\tfrac12\log_2\ell-O(1)$. A count of the ones seen in a fixed first bank attains this entropy scale. If subsequent input uniquely predicts that bank as $p$, first check its weight against the stored count. Then compare the real bits only at the smaller of the sets of ones and zeros of $p$. Agreement there and equality of weight imply equality everywhere. The binomial count of a uniform prefix has entropy $\tfrac12\log_2\ell+O(1)$, by Stirling's formula or the entropy upper bound using its variance $\ell/4$. A constant number of pending raw bits at the last load changes entropy by $O(1)$.

For a radius $0\le r\le\ell$, the support lower bound for making **every** prefix cheap is $\log_2 D\ge\ell-\log_2 V_\ell(r)$. There is an upper bound within $O(\log(\ell+2))$ bits on a regular face. Cover the binary $\ell$-cube by radius-$r$ balls centered on a binary linear space of dimension at most
$$
 d_0=\min\{\ell,\lceil\log_2((\ell+1)2^\ell/V_\ell(r))\rceil\}.
$$
To see existence, begin with the ball at zero. If the uncovered fraction is $u$, its intersection with a uniformly chosen translate has mean fraction $u^2$. Adjoining a minimizing shift as a new generator therefore at least squares the decay. After $d$ additions the uncovered fraction is at most $\exp(-2^d V_\ell(r)/2^\ell)$, which is below $2^{-\ell}$ for the displayed choice before clipping. The whole space handles the clip. This covering argument is classical. [Delsarte and Piret](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1986.1057249) · [Averaging identity and attribution, Lemma 2.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.3170)

Retain the label of a covering center $c$ for the true prefix $x$ and the exact distance $t=d(x,c)\le r$. Given a predicted prefix $p$, reject equality immediately if $d(p,c)\ne t$. Otherwise read the $t$ positions where $p$ differs from $c$. If the answers coincide with $p$, those positions exhaust all disagreements of $x$ with $c$. Thus $p=x$. [Distance check in Lean](../lean/PrefixCoverVerification.lean)

This checks every possible prediction with at most $r$ old-bit returns. The center label and distance have at most $2^{d_0}(\ell+1)$ possibilities.

Here is a uniform realization on the good-code faces allowing unrestricted time. Use a fixed regular first bank of $\ell$ word-isolated data pins with all remaining live coordinates later. Stored syntax of the first bank is fixed across the face. On the first $\ell-1$ pins perform the covering construction at radius $\min(r,\ell-1)$, then clear the working copy and all data-dependent scratch **before** the last first exposure. Retain that last raw bit separately. This enforces the bound at the immediate cut itself. Replacing $\ell$ by $\ell-1$ changes the label bound by at most a constant, by Pascal's identity. One can find the generators and the center by exhaustive enumeration with polynomial workspace: for any candidate shift, enumerate all cube points and all current generator combinations to count uncovered intersections. The generators take $O(\ell^2)$ bits. The counters take $O(\ell)$ bits and can use several RAM words. No exponentially large address is needed.

After copying the other input positions, enumerate all fillings of the first bank on the **copy**, testing the selected SAT verdict with an ordinary complete solver. If there is no such filling, give the other verdict. If there is just one, it is the prediction to compare as above. If there are several, a complete fallback on the genuine input ensures correctness. On every codeword completion the predicted filling exists and is unique by $d(C)>\ell$. Fixed layout tests, word separation and masking ensure that only the prescribed comparisons revisit original first-bank pins. The schedule can use predetermined loops during the prefix, and erase scratch deterministically, so at the cut the controller and scratch add at most a constant number of possible states beyond the retained fields. A parameter that grows with length, such as $r=\lfloor\alpha\ell\rfloor$ for fixed rational $\alpha$, is computed from the public length. Thus both inequalities describe actual correct SAT programs on the constructed families.

## Scan time at fixed return fraction

For any fixed rational $0<\alpha<1/2$, the first-order trade is
$$
  \log_2 D = (1-H_2(\alpha))\ell+o(\ell),\qquad |Q|\le\alpha\ell.
$$
The upper bound can also be attained in scan time on a SAT equality face with $\ell=\Theta(h/\log h)$ and two $\ell$-bit banks. Equality has the two facts needed by the private-restriction proof: the prefix is uniform on equal inputs, and changing that prefix alone on such an input destroys equality. Use a fixed template where each bank supplies a unit sign on the same variable for each position, with two false fillers per unit. Its verdict is SAT exactly when the banks agree. Word-isolate the live signs using a constant number of spacer clauses. Validate the template in a packed scan, using a complete solver if the syntax fails. This is a single globally correct program. On well-formed inputs it can compute the predicted first-bank values from the second bank.

In the first bank, split the signs into blocks of $b=\max(1,\lfloor\log_2\ell/8\rfloor)$ positions. Precompute the small covering space and lookup table at radius $r_b=\lfloor\alpha b\rfloor$. With an indicator array of length $2^b$, trying all shifts and all points per addition costs $O(b4^b)$ word steps. Enumerating the centers in order for each possible block builds the lookup table in $O(\operatorname{poly}(b)4^b)$ further steps, including Hamming weights if the instruction set lacks popcount. These costs are $o(\ell)$, and all tables have polynomial addresses. Each finished block stores its center label and exact distance. Clear the raw block and temporary lookup fields, and mask live pins before retaining any copy of their syntax. No earlier block remains in scratch. At most $b$ bits of an unfinished block and a constant number of pending words contribute $O(b+\log h)$ bits to the logarithm of the cut support. Treat a short final block as literal data with zero later reads. The number of cut bits for the finished blocks is at most
$$
  (\ell/b)\big(b-\log_2 V_b(r_b)+O(\log b)\big)
  =(1-H_2(\alpha))\ell+o(\ell).
$$
Every original first-bank reread tests one of at most $r_b$ discrepancies in its block. Grouping the chosen positions and the final comparisons costs $O(\ell)$ word operations. The full physical cost on this face is $O(h/\log h)$, including syntax checks. At $\alpha=1/2$ the single count suffices; at zero the program can retain the whole prefix. This gives an attained trade between cut information and actual returns while the unrestricted elapsed-time exclusion remains open. [Finite cell, covering and codeword read checks](../audits/selected_prefix_rank_audit.py)

The same scan-time upper bound holds on faces satisfying the two linear-distance hypotheses themselves. In a systematic code with fast parity map $A$, put the parity pins first and the message pins later, so the first $\ell$ parity bits can be predicted by evaluating $A$ on the message. Take $\ell$ to be a sufficiently small fixed fraction of the code length; then $\ell<\min(d,d_\perp)$. Choose $O(k)$ records and available names, with fixed tautologies encoding the public bank parameters, so that $h=\Theta(k\log k)$. Separate active words and the bank boundary by fixed padding. Use a gate-equivalence 3-CNF that computes **into** the parity variables. The first-bank data clauses are signed units with two fixed-false fillers. A uniform solver validates these clause positions during its first scan, saves the count or covering fields, and omits the selected data clauses from its working copy. It copies all later data. On this copy unit propagation either finds a conflict, or, on each codeword completion, forces every missing signal and satisfies every remaining clause. In the latter case, after checking also that the fillers are forced false and the missing signals are fixed, the computed signs are the unique prediction. Comparing them to the original by the covering test decides SAT. A complete fallback handles a malformed bank or inconclusive propagation. A conflict in the smaller formula is sound even before the missing clauses are restored. Thus one globally correct SAT solver attains both the rate and $O(h/\log h)$ time on this good-code family. The successful code circuits may be chosen nonuniformly in constructing the formulas; their gates are presented to the solver in the ordinary formula. [Systematic linear-size codes with good duals](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITC.2026.8) · [Propagation and carrier details](arbitrary-sat-coordinate-faces.md)

## If either bank may come first

It is possible to arrange that either bank predicts the other in linear circuit size on one and the same code. Here is a construction using the published fast binary linear uniform-output families: for every nonzero $v$ of length $m$, a sampled matrix $F$ makes $Fv$ uniform on $m$ bits, and the transpose family has the same property. Fix three independent matrices $F,G,J$ from such a family. Define $L_F(u,v)=(v,u+Fv)$ over the binary field and put $A=L_J L_G L_F$. Each round and its inverse use $O(m)$ gates, and $L_F^T=L_{F^T}$. [Uniform-output families and transposition, Section 1.2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol385-itc2026/html/LIPIcs.ITC.2026.8/LIPIcs.ITC.2026.8.html)

For fixed $v\ne0$, after two rounds the pair is $(b,v+Gb)$ with $b=u+Fv$ uniform. Conditional on $b\ne0$ the second component is uniform. Thus its total variation distance to a uniform pair is at most $2^{-m}$. Another independent bijective round preserves the upper bound. If $v=0$ and $u\ne0$, the first round produces $(0,u)$ and the next two rounds give the same estimate. It applies to $A^T$ by reversing the three independent transpose rounds. Choose $0<2\delta<1/2$ with $H_2(2\delta)<1/2$. The ball of radius $4\delta m$ in $2m$ bits has size $B\le 2^{2mH_2(2\delta)}$. For each of $A$ and $A^T$, the probability that any nonzero input from the ball maps back into it is at most $B(B2^{-2m}+2^{-m})=o(1)$. Fix one successful sample. Its graph code has length $4m$, rate $1/2$ and both distances greater than $4\delta m$ up to integer rounding. Its inverse also has a linear-size circuit. Gate-equivalence constraints for the forward and inverse circuits may coexist, so after erasing the chosen prefix from either bank, the other bank forces all missing variables by propagation on codewords. This rules out a difficulty specific to reading the systematic bank first. Prior work uses Feistel rounds for efficient permutations with efficient inverses. The graph-distance quantity is also known as branch number. Priority of the three-linear-round application and the SAT tradeoff has not been established. [Kaplan, Naor and Reingold, Section 4](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~naor/PAPERS/kwise.pdf) · [Branch number and graph-code distance](https://doi.org/10.1049/iet-ifs.2016.0085)
