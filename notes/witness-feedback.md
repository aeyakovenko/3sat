# Can randomized local witness synthesis avoid a hard chronological table?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** A candidate generator reads a few bits of one shared random tape whenever it supplies a witness bit. Different output positions may read exactly the same random bits. The question is whether its chance of supplying an entire valid witness lets a fast SAT algorithm remove the long random tape. A sharp relation between success probability and Hamming distance gives one way to do this. A second check can exploit repeated constraints on a seed bit even when flipping it changes many output symbols.

## Convert completion probability into a short repair

Let a nonempty set A of binary strings be specified by forbidding partial assignments, each on at most b coordinates, with b≥1. Equivalently A is the satisfying set of a width-b CNF. For uniform independent input bits let ρ be its density. For **any** baseline z let r be its distance to A. Then

    ρ ≤ (1−2^(−b))^r.

To prove this, reveal a uniform string as needed. After each successful stage fill the unrevealed bits from z. If this completion is invalid, choose a forbidden pattern it matches. Its revealed positions already match. Inspect its unrevealed coordinates in a fixed order until the first bit differing from z. If no such bit exists, the random string violates the forbidden pattern and fails. Conditional on the transcript, failure in this stage has probability at least 2^(−b), including probability one when the pattern was entirely revealed. A successful stage adds exactly one disagreement with z. Until r such stages have succeeded, the baseline completion is necessarily invalid. The event that the random string belongs to A requires all of these successes. For equality take r disjoint groups of b bits, baseline zero, and require at least one one per group. Falsified-clause branching in a Hamming ball is classical; the early-stop exposure here gives the exact density constant. [Moser and Scheder](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.4067)

For a hierarchy formula F, let G(v,ω) produce the bit of variable v by adaptively probing a common R-bit seed ω. A whole local check, including all variables in one clause, may probe at most b seed bits. For each local check and each binary reply transcript that ends in rejection, record the queried positions and their values. Discard inconsistent repeated-address replies. Deduplicate equal coordinates and order by seed address. This gives a canonical forbidden pattern with at most b entries. Any seed matching it takes exactly that rejecting path. Conversely any failure supplies one such pattern. An index for a source check and b proposed replies suffice to generate a pattern; the determining procedure does not enumerate the exponentially many transcripts. R may be as large as a polynomial in the hierarchy clock T. The generation and canonical sorting must have the stated local cost below.

If G succeeds on a ρ fraction of uniform seeds, the lemma supplies a successful seed differing from zero on at most ln(1/ρ)/[−ln(1−2^(−b))] coordinates. This holds for any simple baseline in place of zero. The proof uses randomness only on the seed. There is no independence assumption on the output symbols. In particular exponentially many different chronological symbols can all change when one seed bit is repaired.

Let S bound the circuit sizes for computing a local forbidden pattern, the costs of decoding a candidate generator, the baseline bit circuit evaluated at every queried address, and the key length O(b log(R+2)). Include the size needed to check a purported probe transcript and to mask invalid or out-of-range source indices. A direct nondeterministic check guesses G and the h repaired seed coordinates H, implements baseline XOR membership in H, and asks whether any rejecting transcript matches the repaired seed. For fixed b or b=polylog(T), deterministic SAT time O(mᵈ) on m encoded bits gives Õ((S+h+1)ᵈ), plus the original formula generator construction. The exponent d is two or three. All tilde factors here are polynomial in log T with descriptions bounded by a polynomial in T. Guessing G is sound because its clocked semantics must give the same total bit function at every occurrence of v from the same seed, including across clauses. One implementation is a circuit with b ordered oracle accesses, next address computed from v and earlier replies, followed by an output bit. A clause tester runs fixed copies of this same circuit. Any stochastic choice must be supplied by the common seed.

## Batch by the distinct constraints induced on the seed

The direct check pays the SAT exponent on h. To reduce that charge, for a successful patch H define L to be the set of pairs (a,c), where a belongs to H and appears in a generated forbidden pattern c. Count distinct pairs, including each canonical pattern only once for an address. This count can be much smaller than the number of changed outputs or constraints. Under the same hypothesis a nondeterministic verifier checks the repair in

    Õ(Sᵈ + (h+|L|+1) S^(d−1)),

taking S≥2 and b=polylog(T). The machine caps each guessed description at the claimed bounds. Here are the completeness checks needed to prevent the prover from hiding a pattern.

1. Guess sorted H and a sorted list of pairs. Require each list element to have an address in H and a well-formed canonical pattern that mentions it. It is sound to overestimate the set if all listed patterns are subsequently avoided. Form the list J of distinct pattern keys in the guessed pairs.
2. Test whether some source index, transcript, and slot generate a pair (a,c) with a in H that is absent from the list. In lexicographic order on (a,c), add markers (a,bottom) and (a,top) for each a in H, reserving keys strictly outside the possible pattern codes. Partition the full ordered domain into O(1+(h+|L|)/S) consecutive segments, placing at most S records or markers of this augmented list in each, including leading and trailing gaps. For one segment, at most S+2 members of H have fibers intersecting it: except for the two endpoint fibers, any such fiber includes its markers in the segment. Compile the short list of these addresses and at most S genuine listed pairs into the existential violation query for this segment. Hardwire its two endpoints as well. A bad pair would be caught in its own segment. All segment descriptions can be made by a scan after sorting; testing intersection does not require scanning all of H per segment.
3. Evaluate each pattern in J directly under the corrected seed and reject if one matches. Every actual pattern outside J avoids H once the previous check passes. Test for an outside-J baseline violation by another SAT query series. Partition the space of pattern keys into consecutive segments with at most S keys of J each. For a generated pattern in the segment, test its absence from the local list and its match with the baseline. This covers every remaining source constraint.

Each query has Õ(S) explicit encoded bits. With packed polynomial-clock indices, guessing, sorting and writing the tables and directly evaluating the candidates fit the bound, including the baseline circuits on each record. Direct evaluations take Õ(S) per record, covered since d≥2. If a seed address belongs to at most Δ distinct generated patterns, then |L|≤hΔ. No occurrence bound on the source formula or generator outputs follows from this premise or is required by the check. The earlier [repair algorithm](restriction-histories.md) charged the number of source clauses containing a changed output variable.

For example, replicate each bit of an h-bit string in Q output rows and require each copy to equal its corresponding target bit. Use a generator mapping every copy directly to its seed coordinate. Half of the h targets can be one, in which case the zero seed needs h/2 repairs and the raw output changes on Qh/2 rows. There is just one distinct forbidden pattern per constrained seed coordinate, regardless of Q. This is a test of the verification cost. The cost of describing or generating the target bits is still charged in S.

## Replace the seed by a low-degree polynomial or a small-bias sample

For large b, the factor 2ᵇ in the sparse-repair radius can dominate. The number of distinct forbidden patterns is at most M·2ᵇ for M indexed local checks. This count is used only to apply a fooling theorem. The simulator never writes that many clauses. Let D≥log₂(1/ρ) and L=1+log₂(M+1)+b+D.

A t-wise independent seed distribution for t=O(L²), with a sufficiently large absolute constant, fools this induced CNF to additive error strictly below ρ. This is Bazzi's theorem with the number of clauses bounded as above. Assign each seed address a distinct element of a binary extension field with l=max(1,⌈log₂ R⌉) bits. A uniformly chosen polynomial of degree less than t over this field gives independent uniform values on any t distinct addresses by Vandermonde interpolation, or on all R addresses when t≥R. Taking a fixed nonzero coordinate bit preserves independence. Some polynomial thus gives a successful seed. [Bazzi](https://doi.org/10.1137/070691954)

The hierarchy simulator guesses polynomial coefficients and a monic degree-l modulus. For soundness even a reducible modulus defines a total seed word; completeness uses an irreducible one. To verify a mismatch with the local checks, guess the full sequence of at most b oracle replies on the putative rejecting path. The code for the path lists all query addresses before seed consistency is tested. Check the polynomial at **all** those addresses using fast multipoint evaluation, then compare the fixed bit against each proposed reply. This costs Õ(t+b) field operations: build a balanced product tree of the linear factors at the addresses, reduce the polynomial modulo the root, and propagate remainders down the tree. Monic division by reversal and Newton inversion and fast polynomial multiplication give this bound over fields or the quotient ring for a malformed candidate. Equivalently the verifier may test irreducibility in poly(l)=polylog(T) time and then use ordinary field arithmetic. The factor l in binary sizes costs polylog(T). Any genuine violation has a consistent guessed transcript, and inconsistent guesses cannot make a valid seed fail. The nondeterministic violation test has an ordinary SAT encoding of Õ(S+t+b) bits. A deterministic SAT call then certifies absence of a violation. This calculation still works when b is polynomial in T and S pays the actual cost of producing a complete transcript's keys. [Cantor and Kaltofen, fast multiplication](https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/91/CaKa91.pdf) · [Subproduct and remainder tree algorithm](https://fredrikj.net/fdoc/html/algorithms/polynomials/subproduct-tree.html)

There is a complementary bound for very small ρ. By De–Etesami–Trevisan–Tulsiani, a distribution of bias at most 2^(−O(log(R+1)+b log(b+1)L)), with a suitable constant in the exponent, fools this width-b CNF to additive error smaller than ρ. To apply their DNF theorem take the complement. Take u=O(log(R+1)+b log(b+1)L) sufficiently large and choose independent uniform a,c in the binary field of degree u. The bit at integer seed address i, starting at zero, is the binary dot product of a with the coefficient vector of cⁱ. For a nonempty set of addresses its parity has bias at most (R−1)/2ᵘ: average first over a, then bound the roots of the nonzero polynomial ΣᵢXⁱ. Thus some (a,c) works. Guess them and a monic modulus of degree u. No irreducibility check is needed: *every* candidate specifies a total word by polynomial reduction, and a genuine field is available for completeness. Each indexed bit takes O(log(R+1)) modular squarings and multiplications and one binary dot product. Quasilinear binary polynomial arithmetic, including monic division, makes the violation test cost Õ(S+b u). This avoids raising D to the second power when b is polylogarithmic. The size of the seed alone would not justify this time bound; the cost here includes random access to its output. [De, Etesami, Trevisan and Tulsiani](https://theory.stanford.edu/~trevisan/pubs/DETT10.pdf)

Both generator tests are uniform with integer size caps and a lower bound on density supplied as a clock parameter. Guessing a polynomial, field representation, or small-bias sample is part of the nondeterministic simulation, and checking its induced assignment uses one deterministic SAT call. Their underlying PRG theorems are unconditional. Fast SAT pays for checking an entire implicitly generated computation against the chosen sample.

## Apply the test to easy-witness feedback

Take T=2ᵏ and the fixed nondeterministic multitape hierarchy language outside time T/k² used in the [succinct witness argument](succinct.md). Its width-three history formulas have T poly(k) clauses with a poly(k)-size local clause generator constructible in poly(k) time. A valid witness generator here need only produce a satisfying assignment to that formula. The usual nondeterministic, address-sorted simulation of a polynomial-clock RAM adds polynomial factors in log T. [Jahanjou, Miles and Viola](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3171) · [Murray and Williams, easy-witness framework](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/easy-witness-nqp.pdf)

Fix positive α and β<1 with dα<1 and β+(d−1)α<1. If all but finitely many YES inputs had some G with cost S≤T^α and a successful patched seed with h+|L|≤T^β, the verifier above would decide the hierarchy language in o(T/k²), a contradiction. The generator and patch may both depend on the input. For fixed b and polylogarithmic S,Δ, there is a constant A such that on infinitely many satisfiable hierarchy formulas **every** such G has success probability less than exp(−T/(log T)^A). Otherwise the density lemma supplies h=O(T/(log T)^A) and the distinct-pair bound gives the required clock saving for sufficiently large A. The same conclusion applies for b=O(log log T) with a fixed implied constant: 2ᵇ remains polylogarithmic. With unrestricted pattern incidence the direct check still forces success below exp(−T^β) for fixed b, polylogarithmic S and each fixed β<1/d, on infinitely many YES inputs. These statements are conditional on the fast SAT hypothesis and count probability over uniform seed bits.

The polynomial replacement removes all incidence assumptions when d·max(α,2γ)<1, S≤T^α and both b and D are at most T^γ. Otherwise a successful polynomial-size guess at all but finitely many YES inputs contradicts the same hierarchy by the bound Õ((S+b+L²)ᵈ). In particular one can allow polynomially growing numbers of adaptive probes. The small-bias replacement shows that for *every* fixed polylogarithmic b and S, the success on infinitely many YES inputs is smaller than exp(−T^β) for any fixed β<1/d, without any control on sharing seed coordinates or on the number of their incident patterns. These parameter choices concern the class of all generators obeying the fixed caps simultaneously at the indicated hard inputs.

As a concrete generator, on each variable v propose the bit returned by a SAT query on its constant-radius bounded-degree neighborhood, using seed bits to order local choices or pin selected nearby variables, and a fixed fallback to handle infeasible pins. A shared seed address may supply advice at many distant centers. With fixed radius and a fixed number of queries per center, the construction uses a constant number of random probes per checked clause provided the rule for selecting pins has this bound. The assumed SAT algorithm gives small Boolean simulation circuits for the bounded neighborhood operations. The unrestricted-incidence conclusion already applies. With distinct seeds indexed by nearby centers, each seed can affect only constantly many source tests and paths, giving the stronger near-linear exponent. The same stronger conclusion is available for nonlocal sharing when the distinct-pattern bound is verified. Even selecting from a menu of G's after seeing the seed can increase the success bound only by the menu size, via the union bound, when the generators satisfy common bounds on the same hard input.

**Failure of the proposed final inference.** Nondeterministic hierarchy machines need only have one accepting choice tape. No lower bound on the above density follows. Even a large density of good *original computation choices* cannot be used as the density of a bounded-probe seed generator without proof. The predicate requiring at least half of R fair choice bits to be one accepts at least half its tapes but every accepted one differs from zero in at least R/2 bits. It has a small ordinary counting circuit and a bounded-width tableau with auxiliaries; the read complexity on the original choices after projecting those auxiliaries is a separate resource. Fast decision SAT on local neighborhoods supplies evaluators. It gives no lower bound yet on how often their outputs jointly satisfy the global computation. Priority of the exact density inequality and the distinct-pattern batching application has not been established.

Finite checks enumerated all width-b CNF solution sets on n=3,4 bits for 1≤b≤3 and all baselines. All 761,896 density comparisons for nonempty sets passed by integer arithmetic. A separate enumeration checked tuple batching on all 19,683 cases obtained from four possible addresses, three pattern keys, arbitrary claimed pairs on chosen addresses and three batch sizes. These tests cover only the indicated finite claims.
