# Easy promised verdicts with a common hard part in every satisfying assignment

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** The adjacent SAT instances in the matching construction can force every positive witness to contain an entire circuit-hard table. Can canonicality of that table itself convert witness hardness into decision time? There is an unconditional comparison where a visible pin gives the correct **promised** verdict, yet at infinitely many scales almost all the positive formulas force *the same* hard table. This is a corollary of a published circuit lower bound, with its advice and random strings accounted for. It is stronger than planting an independent random table in each input: the forced table here is independent of the random string on the successful event.

## Freezing public coins in two complementary proof systems

Chen, Li and Liang prove that for every fixed $0<\epsilon<1$, a language in $(\mathrm{AMEXP}\cap\mathrm{coAMEXP})/2^{b^\epsilon}$ is outside $\mathrm{SIZE}(2^b/b)$. Use their Theorem 1.1 and the perfect-completeness protocols in their Definition 3.1. Fix the language, its two verifiers, and one correct advice string $\alpha_b$ per input length $b$. Let $Q=2^b$, let $g_b$ be its $Q$-bit truth table, and choose a fixed integer $D$ such that both verifiers finish in at most $t_b=2^{(b+2)^D}$ operations on every branch, including reading their randomness and responses. At infinitely many $b$, a circuit computing $g_b$ from its $b$-bit address requires more than $Q/b$ gates in the paper's model. Fixed fan-in bases differing by constant simulations preserve $\Omega(Q/b)$. [Chen, Li and Liang, Theorem 1.1 and Definition 3.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/182/revision/1/download/)

**Prepare a two-message verifier before fixing the coins.** A response computed after seeing a full sequence of future public coins can cheat in a multiround protocol. Use the uniform constant-round reduction to a two-message bounded-error public-coin AM verifier stated in the paper's §3.2. Its cost is polynomial in the original time; apply it separately to the two signs on the correct advice. Here is an explicit restoration of perfect completeness if that normalization only guarantees bounded error. Independently repeat the two-message verifier and use majority, with a sufficiently large constant times $\log(t+2)$ repetitions for original normalized time $t$. For the resulting public randomness length $r=O(t\log(t+2))$, the probability that some reply accepts is at least $1/2$ on a true claim, and at most $1/[8(r+1)]$ on a false one. These are ordinary independent repetitions of the predicate “there exists an accepting reply.”

Put $d=r+1$. On a true claim there are $d$ shifts of the random $r$-bit string whose translated accepting sets cover the whole cube: the expected number of uncovered points for independent shifts is at most $2^r2^{-d}<1$. On a false claim any fixed shift list covers at most $1/8$ of the cube. For the new two-message test Arthur sends $B=rd+2$ independent uniform base points simultaneously. Merlin replies with **one common shift list**, and for each base point a shift index and valid amplified accepting reply. True claims win on every bundle. On false claims the chance that any common list works is at most $2^{rd}(1/8)^B<1/3$, including when Merlin sees the full bundle before choosing the shifts. Times, messages and coin lengths incur only polynomial overhead. Enlarge the fixed $D$ in $t_b$ so it bounds this normalized protocol too. Every use of frozen public coins below is for this Arthur-first verifier. [Round normalization in §3.2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/182/revision/1/download/) · [Perfect completeness for interactive protocols](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~oded/PSX/fgmsz.pdf)

Take $s=2b+2$ independent uniform $t_b$-bit public random strings $R_1,\ldots,R_s$ and reuse these strings across the $Q$ queries. On a public instance $(b,\alpha,R,p)$ with an extra bit $p$, compile the following nondeterministic predicate to packed 3-CNF. Require $p=1$, guess a bitmap $y$ of $Q$ bits, and for each $u\in\{0,1\}^b$ verify the sign $y[u]$ using the corresponding verifier on $u,\alpha$ with each $R_i$. Guess a separate response for each invocation. Fields unused by a shorter verifier are ignored. Formulas with different pins need only change one signed literal at initialization. Expose $y$ in an aligned range of variables, with equality constraints to the bitmap actually used. The work is $O(Qs\,t_b\operatorname{polylog}t_b)$, after ordinary uniform simulations and input access. One can use a fixed rectangular horizon and layout to obtain encoded size $N=2^{b^{O(1)}}$, with $\log N=\Theta((b+2)^D+b)$ using the stated clock up to polynomial factors in $t_b$. The generator given all the fields is uniform. The field $\alpha$ has at most $\lceil 2^{b^\epsilon}\rceil$ bits; it is essential to the following promise. [Explicit RAM encoding](packed-band-transfer.md)

On the correct advice $\alpha_b$, the formula has truth $p$ for **every** choice of the public random strings. For $p=1$ take the true $y=g_b$. Perfect completeness implies a response exists for every one of the finitely many coins used. For any one address $u$, the probability over the $s$ independent strings that the *wrong* sign has responses on every repetition is at most $3^{-s}$. In the normalized verifier the response is allowed to depend on the entire challenge bundle; this is precisely why the soundness bound is for existence of any such response. A union bound over $Q$ addresses shows that with probability at least $1-Q3^{-s}$, **every satisfying assignment** has $y=g_b$. Independence across different addresses is unnecessary. At the hard lengths each such formula requires $\Omega(Q/b)$ gates for lookup of the indicated bits in any satisfying assignment, while the promised decision is one pin read. Restriction to the aligned addresses is accomplished by fixing wires. The random strings are independent of the hard table. The finite implications from perfect completeness and a good coin tuple to the pinned verdict and forced bitmap are checked in Lean. [Finite witness proof](../lean/HallProbeColumns.lean) No computation on arbitrary wrong advice is assumed to decide by that pin. The bound is superpolynomial in $\log N$ but the AMEXP theorem gives no fixed positive power of $N$ here.

There is a uniform generator with no separately supplied advice if only an infinite subsequence of scales is required. Allocate a binary scale field with a delimited $b$, the advice bits as a literal payload and padding, of bit length $\Theta(2^{b^\epsilon}+b)$. Regard its integer value $H$ as the horizon and pad the output formula to $\Theta(H\log^3 H)$ bits. Along the subsequence encoding correct advice, this budget dominates $Qs\,t_b\operatorname{polylog}t_b$, since $(b+2)^D=o(2^{b^\epsilon})$. Also $Q/b$ still dominates $(\log H)^c$ for every fixed $c$. The successful subsequence is existential; a procedure identifying it or certifying that an advice field is correct does not follow. One may choose a rational exponent such as $\epsilon=1/2$ and round all schedule fields upward for exact computability.

## What carries to the matching construction

Keep $(b,\alpha_b,R)$ public for the entire matching graph at a scale, and include the probe code with literal payload $u$ for each $b$-bit address. Each such program ignores its row and offers iff successful sign-one responses exist on *all* $R_i$. Every true-one probe always offers. With probability at least $1-Q3^{-s}$ no false-one probe offers. Use a pool with $\ell=b+C$ and $P=\Theta(Q)$ and a clock $M$ sufficient for the repeated protocol simulations. Choose the source horizon large enough for $PM\operatorname{polylog}H$ and the physical public data; one can still take $\log H=b^{O(1)}$. The original unconditional matching source works. At a hard length on the good-coin event the fixed table $g_b$ occurs on the probe slice in *every* actual Hall witness of the first failed prefix and in *every* satisfying assignment of the preceding formula with exposed used-code flags. Here the index of the first failure may vary with the coins. The same table appears regardless. In contrast, the preceding pin-only example has no index to locate and an explicitly known promised verdict. [Extraction from all actual witnesses](first-failure-hall-witnesses.md)

This comparison isolates the missing premise in a proposed decision lower bound. One must relate the work of a globally correct **decision** program to producing or locally answering the mandatory witness bits. Canonicality across positive witnesses and across random inputs does not force that relation on an easy promise. The paper proves the underlying circuit lower bound; the SAT encodings and their restrictions are manuscript deductions.

## Removing the advice from the matching generator

The matching version admits a further quantifier improvement. Apply the published theorem with $\epsilon=1/3$, use a field of $s_b=2^{\lceil\sqrt b\rceil}$ bits, and enlarge the menu to include a separate probe $p_{\alpha,u}$ for **every** possible $s_b$-bit advice field and every $b$-bit address. For sufficiently large $b$ this field holds the actual advice with a self-delimiting length and padding. Malformed fields can be treated as no offer. All probes use the same public coins and interpret their own literal payload as the advice and query. Choose the code-length cutoff $s_b+b+C$ so that all probes fit. The number of codes $P$ is $2^{s_b+b+O(1)}$. With an offer clock sufficient for the uniform protocol and a serial matching clock $H$ of order $P$ times that clock times a fixed extra polynomial in $\log P$, we have $\log H=\Theta(s_b)$: the logarithm of the protocol time is only $b^{O(1)}=o(s_b)$. Take additional fixed clock and word-width margins for the universal interpreter. Entire code fields can be decoded in $O(s_b\operatorname{polylog}H)$ steps. A generator given $b,R$ alone can therefore write the list. No correct advice or advice-carrying subsequence is needed by this generator.

On the slice of columns with payload prefix $\alpha_b$, the preceding probability estimate forces the hard table. Events on the other advice slices need no union bound or correctness condition. Consequently at infinitely many ordinary address lengths $b$, with probability at least $1-2^b3^{-2b-2}$ over the public coins, every satisfying assignment of the last positive formula and every Hall witness at the first negative encode the same $g_b$ on that slice. The formulas have $\log N=\Theta(2^{\lceil\sqrt b\rceil})$. In particular the gate requirement $\Omega(2^b/b)$ is superpolynomial in $\log N$. Fixing $\alpha_b$ in the input wires of a proposed bitmap circuit adds no gates. These particular fixed wires are needed only in the mathematical reduction to the circuit lower bound. The first failed index can still depend on $R$ and no algorithm for finding it cheaply has been obtained. This removes advice from the *matching* generator; the earlier pin-only decision comparison uses the correct-advice promise.
