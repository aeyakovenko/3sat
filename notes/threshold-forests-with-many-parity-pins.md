# How many parity pins can a family of threshold forests preserve?

The minimum-spanning-tree preprint suggests preserving connected components at every weight threshold. For Boolean even-parity constraints on the incident edge bits at each vertex, a single forest can lose information already exposed by two pins. With several successive forests one can answer every bounded-pin query exactly. In the other direction, two graphs on the same vertices can give identical extension answers for *every* assignment to fewer than $m/3$ common edges, yet require exponentially different numbers of 2-CNF cases on $m$ edge variables. If one also permits pinning arbitrary aligned slots whose physical endpoints can differ, the first distinction occurs at exactly $\sqrt m$ pins. [Seven-vertex example and pin model](threshold-forests-and-parity-pins.md) · [Khanna, Putterman and Song, Fact 2.5](https://github.com/Louiealbp/DLMST/blob/0072335716b145032b418760883daed2a89809db/final.pdf)

## A positive transfer for all queries with at most k pins

Take a weighted undirected graph. Form $F_1$ as a minimum spanning forest, delete its edges, form $F_2$ on the remainder, and repeat through $F_{k+1}$. Retain their union $H$ with edge names, endpoints and weights. A query specifies a threshold, at most $k$ active edge identities and their assigned bits. The query supplies the endpoints of each pinned edge even if the edge was discarded. At every threshold the same $H$ suffices to decide whether the pins extend to an even edge set of the full active graph. It has at most $(k+1)(|V|-1)$ edges for nonempty $V$. Construction takes $O((k+1)(|V|+|E|))$ time using the preprint's MSF bound at each pass. The unweighted certificate argument is classical. [Nagamochi and Ibaraki](https://gi.cebitec.uni-bielefeld.de/_media/teaching/2013summer/936nagamochi-ibaraki-1992-sparse-k-connected-subgraph.pdf)

Fix a threshold and a vertex cut containing $c$ active crossing edges. Whenever at least one crossing edge remains, the next forest contains at least one active crossing edge, by threshold connectivity in that residual graph. Hence $H$ contains at least $\min(c,k+1)$ of them, including every one if $c\le k$. After deleting the pinned set $P$, every cut empty in $H-P$ is therefore empty in the full active graph minus $P$; here and below restrict $H$ to the same threshold. The two residual graphs have identical connected components.

Mark each vertex with the parity of its incident pins fixed to one. Sum these marks in each residual component. All sums must vanish. Conversely in any component of even total, send the marks toward a root along a spanning tree, assigning each child edge the parity of the child's accumulated marks. This constructs the remaining bits. Choose zero on unpinned edges outside $H$. The pinned one-edges themselves are kept at their prescribed values, including any outside $H$. This proves the simultaneous extension claim. With $k\ge1$ the literal rule that only uses unpinned edges in the union can require the extra forest on multigraphs. Take $k+1$ parallel edges on two vertices, retain the first $k$ one-edge forests, and pin these $k$ edges with odd total. The discarded edge can repair parity, while the retained unpinned set is empty. DLMST also applies to general weights through successive forest extraction, which is what makes the certificate simultaneous over thresholds.

The same certificate works for arbitrary linear equations over a field. Treat the variables as columns and compute $k+1$ successive minimum-weight column bases after deleting each basis. Consider an active column omitted from the union. By the greedy minimum-basis property, the omitted column is a linear combination of basis columns of no greater weight in each of the $k+1$ disjoint bases. At most $k$ pinned columns can meet at most $k$ of these bases. One representation therefore survives, and all remaining columns span exactly the same space as the unpinned retained ones at this threshold. Subtract the known pinned columns times their supplied values from the right-hand side, then test membership in that span. At rank $r$ this retains at most $(k+1)r$ columns; the columns themselves and the linear algebra also cost space and time. A Boolean lift that introduces a variable for each monomial still needs to enforce their products. For example $x=0$ and $xy=1$ become consistent linear equations if the coordinate for $xy$ is allowed to vary independently of $x$ and $y$.

Even for **simple graphs**, retaining just the first $k$ forests can give identical observations for opposite answers. Take two vertex clusters, each with $k$ edge-disjoint spanning trees. For an explicit choice use two parts of size $2k$ in each cluster, match labels cyclically at offsets $2j$ and $2j+1$ for $0\le j<k$, and delete one edge from each resulting Hamiltonian cycle. Add $k$ distinct cross-cluster edges joining vertices in the second parts. For $1\le j\le k$ give the $j$th trees in both clusters and the $j$th crossing edge weight $j$. This group alone is a spanning tree of all vertices, so it is the unique next residual minimum spanning tree. Finally choose two vertices in the first part of each cluster. At weight $k+1$ either join each selected pair inside its own cluster or join the two pairs by a cross-cluster matching. Edge counts, per-vertex degrees at every threshold, components, the first $k$ forests and their weights agree. Pin all $k$ light crossing edges with exactly one set to one. The residual graph in the first instance has an odd mark sum on each side. In the second instance it is connected. Thus the extra forest can supply an indispensable distinction even given these common statistics and the true endpoints of every pin. This sharpness concerns the certificate that retains those particular forests.

## Equal projections through square-root arity, different global costs

Start with the six-cycle in cyclic label order. Graph A adds edges 13, 25, 46, giving two triangles joined by a matching. Graph B adds 14, 25, 36, giving $K_{3,3}$. For any even $s\ge4$, replace each vertex by an independent set of $s$ twins and each base edge by the complete bipartite graph between its two sets. Each resulting simple graph has $6s$ vertices, $m=9s^2$ edges, and labeled degrees $3s$. Use the same Hamiltonian path in both graphs, visiting vertices 1 to 6 in each successive twin copy and then crossing from 6 to 1 of the next copy. Give exactly its edges weight zero, and every other edge weight one. Both graphs have that same unique minimum spanning tree and the same components and degree of every labeled vertex at every weight threshold. Identify tree edge names across graphs and use any bijection of the remaining slots.

Every assignment on the entire tree extends to an even edge set in either graph. Indeed the graph after deleting the tree is connected. In each consecutive pair of fibers on the base six-cycle at most a matching has been deleted from $K_{s,s}$, leaving a connected graph for $s\ge3$. Conversely every assignment on the complete complement of the tree extends in either graph since the tree itself remains connected. Each separate marginal projection is consequently the full cube. This statement is about the final active graph.

More strongly, any assignment on any **fewer than $3s=\sqrt m$ edge slots** extends in both graphs, even with tree and chord slots mixed. Both graphs are $3s$-edge-connected. If a vertex cut splits a fiber into parts of sizes $a,s-a$, then each of its three adjacent fibers contributes at least $s\min(a,s-a)$ crossing edges, since each adjacent vertex sees both parts. A nontrivial cut which consists of whole fibers crosses at least three complete bipartite bundles: both base graphs are 3-edge-connected. The star at a single vertex attains $3s$. After fewer deletions the graph is connected, and the marks induced by pins always have even total over the whole graph. The positive transfer above then gives an extension.

The pin threshold is sharp under an explicit slot correspondence. Match the common six-cycle bundles identically, match bundle 13 in A to 14 in B, bundle 25 to itself, and 46 to 36, preserving twin indices at each listed endpoint. Take the $3s$ slots of the star at twin $3_0$ in A. Give them the bits induced in B by the four-cycle $1_0,2_0,3_1,4_0,1_0$. The first three cycle edges have no incident $3_0$, and the final edge corresponds to $1_0 3_0$ in A. The pins therefore have odd parity on that star in A, and by construction extend in B.

## Pinning only edges with the same endpoints raises the threshold to m/3

Let $J$ be the ordinary intersection of the two graphs. Its edges are the six cycle bundles and bundle 25. It is connected. If pins are taken from $J$, both full graphs give identical answers for every pin assignment on fewer than $3s^2=m/3$ edges, and some query of that size distinguishes them. Some smaller queries are inconsistent in **both** graphs. The threshold counts when their answers can first differ.

For any set $P$ of common pinned edges, the dual of the cycle space projected onto $P$ consists of the cut vectors of the full graph supported inside $P$, restricted to those coordinates. Indeed a check on $P$ annihilates every projected solution exactly when its extension by zeros annihilates every even edge set. The orthogonal complement of the even edge sets is the span of the vertex incidence rows, whose binary sums are precisely cuts. Thus the smallest distinguishing number of pins is the minimum support size of a cut vector supported on common edges and present in exactly one of the two cut spaces.

A cut on $J$ determines its defining vertex set $U$ up to complementation because $J$ is connected. Its extension by zeros to A is a cut exactly when membership in $U$ is constant on the union of fibers 1 and 3 and on the union of fibers 4 and 6. These are the components joined by the two noncommon bundles. For B the corresponding unions are 1 with 4 and 3 with 6. A cut in A alone must therefore have fibers 1 and 3 on one side and 4 and 6 on the other. Orient the cut so 1 and 3 lie in $U$. Write $a,b$ for the number of twins in $U$ from fibers 2 and 5. Counting across the seven common bundles gives
$$
      4s^2-sa+3sb-2ab \;\ge\;3s^2.
$$
The expression is smallest at $a=s,b=0$. A cut in B alone has, up to complement, 1 and 4 in $U$ and 3 and 6 outside. Its size is $4s^2+s(a+b)-2ab\ge 4s^2$, since the last two terms count crossings in bundle 25. For a distinguishing assignment of size $3s^2$, pin the three full bundles 34, 61 and 25 (the cut separating fibers 1, 2, 3 in A). Use the bits induced in B by the four-cycle through twins $1_0,2_0,3_0,4_0,1_0$. It crosses the pinned set exactly once, so B admits the pins and A cannot. This query refers only to the original physical endpoints. The large common-edge agreement does not assert that the graphs have identical endpoint lists on their remaining slots.

## Exact difference in 2-CNF cover size

For the difference in global encoding size, let $r=m-6s+1$ be the dimension of either cycle space, and let $b$ be the maximum number of pairwise edge-disjoint simple cycles. This equals the maximum number of nonzero even edge sets with disjoint supports, because each nonempty even edge set contains a simple cycle. The exact projected 2-CNF cover size is $2^{r-b}$ and the optimal strong 2-CNF backdoor size over all existential 3-CNF encodings is $r-b$. The word *cover* requires every legal edge word and forbids every illegal one. [Affine-code theorem and proof](affine-codes-and-two-sat-backdoors.md)

For B the blowup is $K_{3s,3s}$. Every cycle uses at least four edges. Pair up the vertices on each side and take the four-cycle for each pair of pairs. This attains $b_B=9s^2/4$. For A, the blowups of its two vertex-disjoint base triangles each decompose into $s^2$ triangles. On three fibers use triples of indices $(i,j,i+j\bmod s)$ so each edge occurs exactly once. Each of the three remaining matching bundles decomposes into $s^2/4$ four-cycles by pairing its vertices on both sides. This gives $11s^2/4$ cycles. Every triangle uses three edges of the two triangle blowups, which have a total of $6s^2$ edges. Hence at most $2s^2$ cycles in any packing can be triangles. Every other cycle uses at least four edges, so a packing of $b$ cycles uses at least $4b-2s^2$ edges. This proves $b_A=11s^2/4$.

Therefore B requires a factor **$2^{m/18}$ more projected 2-CNF cases** than A and **$m/18$ more backdoor bits**, despite identical answers to every query below $m/3$ pins on common physical edges, and to every query below $\sqrt m$ pins on arbitrary aligned slots. A pinned parity query on the supplied full graph still takes $O(|V|+m)$ RAM operations by the component test. The gap separates two representation costs and gives no lower bound on unrestricted SAT decision time. The ingredients are graph connectivity, cycle packing and majority closure. Priority for their combination in this precise example has not been established. [Computational check of the two constructions](../audits/threshold_forest_blowup_audit.py)
