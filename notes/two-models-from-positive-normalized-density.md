# Two models agree on few variables at positive normalized density

For a proper ternary CNF $G$, let $q=7/8$, let $\mu(G)$ be its model fraction, and let $\tau(G)$ be the smallest number of variables meeting every ternary clause. Put $D(G)=\mu(G)/q^{\tau(G)}$. There is a single globally correct deterministic SAT solver with the following guarantee. For every fixed $d>0$ it takes $O_d(N)$ time on every file with $D(G)\ge d$, where $N$ is the encoded length in bits. It can produce **two satisfying assignments that agree in at most $O(\log(1/d))$ coordinates**. The model fraction itself may be exponentially small. For example, independent signed ternary clauses have $D=1$ and admit complementary assignments.

This also gives one globally correct solver for a one-bit edit with a fixed positive margin. If $E,F$ are proper files differing in the name of one variable in one clause and $D(E)\ge(4/7)^4+\delta$ for fixed $\delta>0$, the solver can decide $F$ in $O_\delta(N)$ using only $F$. For the grammar with canonical delimiters the statement also covers every legal one-bit edit, since changing the header alone preserves the formula on its occurring names. [Sharp edit threshold and canonical grammar](a-sharp-density-threshold-for-one-physical-sat-edit.md)

## Finding the two assignments

Write $L=\log(1/d)$, using natural logarithms. Greedily form a maximal packing of pairs of distinct intersecting ternary supports, requiring the unions from different pairs to be vertex-disjoint. Let $p$ be its size. The [overlap inequality](a-sharp-density-loss-for-intersecting-ternary-clauses.md) gives $D(G)\le(46/49)^p$, so $p\le L/\log(49/46)$. Mark all variables of the selected pairs in a set $S_0$ of size $s\le5p$. The distinct ternary supports lying wholly outside $S_0$ are disjoint by maximality.

Choose one signed clause for each of these $B$ disjoint supports. Call each triple a block, and give each other unmarked variable its own singleton block. All outside ternaries either are chosen or use the same support as a chosen clause. A transversal can take $S_0$ and one variable per triple block, hence $\tau(G)\le s+B$. There is a setting of $S_0$ for which the remaining satisfaction probability is at least $\mu(G)\ge d q^{s+B}$, by averaging over the settings. Conditional on satisfying the $B$ chosen clauses, the base distribution is independent across blocks. A triple block has seven uniformly distributed allowed patterns and a singleton has two. In this product distribution the residual acceptance probability is at least $d'=d q^s$.

Every further unsatisfied constraint at this point has width at most two, or is an additional signed ternary on a single triple block. Eliminate constraints identically true on the allowed block patterns. Each other constraint depends on at most two blocks and rejects at least one out of at most 49 equally likely local settings. Select a maximal collection of such constraints with disjoint sets of blocks. Its size $k$ satisfies $d'\le(48/49)^k$. Mark its at most $2k$ blocks and choose a setting of them with residual acceptance probability at least $d'$, again by averaging in the allowed product distribution.

Every remaining nontrivial constraint now concerns at most one unmarked block. Indeed, every nontrivial constraint before the second fixing met a selected block, and depended on at most two. The previously eliminated constraints stay satisfied. The solution set is therefore a product of allowed local subsets. Each block whose domain becomes strictly smaller loses at least a seventh of its base measure. At most $u\le\log(1/d')/\log(7/6)$ such blocks can occur. On each of the other unmarked blocks choose two complementary allowed assignments. On a triple only one of the eight patterns is forbidden, leaving three complementary pairs. Choose any valid value twice on the narrowed blocks. The resulting pair agrees on at most $s+6k+3u=O(L)$ variables. All constants are absolute, since $\log(1/d')=L+s\log(8/7)$.

## A uniform algorithm

The two settings obtained by averaging need not be supplied. Try increasing integer budgets $t=1,2,4,\ldots$. At one budget, abort this trial if the first greedy packing exceeds $t$. Each round of this packing requires one scan of the ternary supports outside the currently marked vertices, using one remembered incident support per vertex to find an overlap. Repeated occurrences of one support are grouped before the scan.

Enumerate the assignments on $S_0$. For each, build the disjoint base blocks and test the remaining constraints for redundancy by trying at most 49 local settings each. Abort this assignment if a constraint is identically false, or if the second greedy packing exceeds $t$. Enumerate the allowed assignments to its at most $2t$ marked blocks. Compute each remaining block's feasible local patterns. If all are nonempty, choose a pair maximizing disagreement separately in each block, including every free singleton. Retain a pair agreeing on at most $t$ variables and validate its two verdicts against the interned clauses. If none is found, increase the budget.

With budget $t$, at most $5t+6t$ variables are explicitly branched on in total, and each local alphabet has size at most eight. All data structures for a trial can be built by direct indexing after deterministic dense renaming in $O(N)$ time. Distinct ternary supports can be grouped once by a constant number of radix passes. A trial takes $O((t+1)2^{11t}(N+1))$ time. For $D(G)\ge d$, the proof gives a successful trial at $t=O(1+\log(1/d))$. Exhaustive SAT can be used if the next budget would exceed $\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil$, or for unsupported input syntax after a well-formedness check. The fallback ensures global correctness, and for each fixed $d$ it affects only finitely many lengths within the positive-density guarantee. The algorithm needs no estimate of $\mu$ or $\tau$.

### A margin that can decrease with the encoded length

There is also a universal $\eta>0$ for which the same solver takes $O_c(N)$ time whenever $D(G)\ge c(\log(N+2))^{-\eta}$ for fixed $c>0$. The distinction between physical bits and clause records pays for this improvement. Read, normalize and deduplicate once in $O(N)$, then run all trials and pair verification on the $m=O(1+N/\log(N+2))$ distinct clauses and their at most $3m$ occurring variables. The sum of the trial times through budget $T$ is $O((m+1)(T+1)2^{11T})$ in addition to the physical preprocessing. [Encoded clause-count proof](model-density-and-a-transversal-of-ternary-sat-clauses.md)

Take $L=\log(1/D(G))$. The displayed bounds on $p,s,k,u$ and doubling give a sufficient $T\le C_0(1+L)$ with an absolute constant $C_0$. For some absolute $C>0$, the resulting time is $O(N+(m+1)(1+L)e^{CL})$ whenever the sufficient budget fits the cutoff. Choose $\eta=1/(2C)$. In the claimed regime $L=O_c(1)+\eta\log\log(N+2)$, so the needed budget is $O_c(1+\log\log(N+2))$ and $e^{CL}=O_c(\sqrt{\log(N+2)})$. The total is $O_c(N)$. Neither this exponent nor the constants in the packing estimates are claimed optimal.

## After changing a digit

A model of $E$ that fails $F$ must give the four fixed variable values in the [edit threshold proof](a-sharp-density-threshold-for-one-physical-sat-edit.md). The model mass lost is at most $(4/7)^4 q^{\tau(E)}$. Replacing one clause changes $\tau$ by at most one, since adding a single variable to a transversal of the other file suffices. Hence
$$
D(F)\ge q\max\{0,D(E)-(4/7)^4\}.
$$
Any fixed positive margin in the parent file therefore supplies the solver with a fixed positive lower bound on the normalized density of the edited file, regardless of its ordinary model fraction. The same implication gives a globally linear guarantee for the vanishing margin $D(E)-(4/7)^4\ge c(\log(N+2))^{-\eta}$, with the absolute $\eta$ from the preceding section.

The pair also certifies answers simultaneously. If neither model witnesses a legal name edit, its old, new and two shared variables must all lie among the coordinates on which the pair agrees. With $a$ agreements there are $O((a+1)^4)$ possible signed clause-replacement types left, even if one clause occurs at many positions. Two explicit models have linear bit length and can be verified in $O(N)$ time. This counting statement gives no running-time guarantee on the exceptional edits at zero margin.
