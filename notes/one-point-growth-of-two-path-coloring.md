# A one-point ladder of simple permutations for two-path 3-colorability

Identify a permutation with a red path in increasing rank order and a blue path in the displayed order. Color all vertices properly along both paths with three colors. A common interval is a set occurring consecutively in both orders. A simple permutation has only the empty interval, the singletons, and the whole permutation as common intervals.

**Theorem.** For every simple order $P$ of size $m\ge4$, there is a computable sequence $F_1(P), F_2(P),\ldots$ of simple orders of respective sizes $m+4,m+5,\ldots$. Each has the same 3-colorability verdict as $P$. Every proper coloring of $P$ extends, and every proper coloring of an extension restricts to one of $P$. Each order in the sequence is obtained from the next by deleting one named point and standardizing.

Write the blue path of $P$ as $L v R$, for $v$ its greatest red vertex. Both $L$ and $R$ are nonempty because otherwise the remaining $m-1$ vertices would be a proper common interval. For $k\ge1$ append the fresh vertices $a,B_1,\ldots,B_k,c,d$, in that order, to the red path. Define the blue path by

$$
 (B_1,B_3,\ldots)\; L\; c\,a\,d\,v\; R\; (\ldots,B_4,B_2).
$$

The first padding run lists the odd subscripts in increasing order and the last lists the even subscripts in decreasing order. Erasing $B_{k+1}$ from the construction at $k+1$ gives the one at $k$ in both orders.

**Coloring.** The triangles $v,a,d$ and $c,a,d$ force $v,c$ to receive the same color. The sole old blue edge that disappears is from the end of $L$ to $v$. The new edge from that end to $c$ enforces its constraint. Conversely, fix an old coloring and give $c$ the color of $v$. The $B_i$ induce edges at distances one and two. When $k\ge2$, choose a period-three coloring of this square of a path as follows. Give $B_k$ a color avoiding both $v$ and the old blue end joined to $B_k$. Give $B_{k-1}$ a different color avoiding the opposite old blue end. Assign the remaining color to the third residue class. For $k=1$, give $B_1$ a color avoiding $v$ and the beginning of $L$. Give $a$ a color avoiding $v$ and $B_1$, then give $d$ the third color relative to $v,a$. This satisfies all edges.

**Common intervals.** Intersect a common interval with the old entries. Its intersection is a common interval of $P$ because deleting points preserves contiguity in each restricted order. If there are at least two old entries, the interval contains all old entries by simplicity. It contains the central word $c,a,d,v$, which lies between old blue endpoints. Since it contains both the smallest old and largest new red entries, the interval is the whole permutation. An interval with exactly one old entry and at least one new one must use $v$ by red contiguity. Its blue segment lies in $c,a,d,v$ because the old entries at the ends of $L$ and the beginning of $R$ prevent escape. A segment with no old entries lies within this same central word or one exterior padding run. The exterior runs have red-rank gaps of two. The central word has red ranks relative to $v$ equal to $(k+2,1,k+3,0)$. All adjacent differences are at least two, each length-three subword has rank span greater than two, and the full rank span $k+3$ exceeds three. Hence every proper interval has at most one vertex.

**Least possible start of an infinite negative ladder.** In one-based notation the simple uncolorable order $6257413$ is the deletion of the point of red rank seven in $62758413=F_1(2413)$. In the seven-point order the triangles $567,256$ force $7=2$ in color, while $123,134$ force $2=4$. The blue edge $74$ rules this out. The red and blue paths of $2413$ together give $K_4$. This supplies a literal one-point chain of simple uncolorable orders at every size starting at seven. The small census rules out any earlier infinite chain because there are no simple uncolorable orders of size six. It does not assert that every simple uncolorable order at seven admits a one-point successor. The previously enumerated $2463715$ is a counterexample to that stronger statement.

**Decoder and cost.** In native zero-based ranks keep doubly linked lists indexed by red rank for the current blue path. Its largest rank $d$ must have a next vertex $v$ and be preceded by $a=v+1$ and $c=d-1$. These determine $k=d-v-3$. Test $v\ge3$ and $k\ge1$. Walk the specified odd prefix and even suffix and check that at least one old blue vertex remains on each side of the central word. The tested segments together account for every fresh rank. On success remove ranks $v+1,\ldots,d$ and repeat. All successful tests and deletions take time proportional to the total number of removed points. The final failed test takes at most linear time in the surviving length. Including input validation and built-in permutation-array construction, the entire decoder is deterministic linear time on the word RAM in the number of points. Thus on a stack of lifts of a designated seed the seed can be recovered without a construction history. For example, the recognizer can peel all layers, return the precomputed verdict when it reaches $2413$, and run a general coloring algorithm otherwise. This supplies a total solver whose cost on the identified ladder is linear in the number of native words (and within linear time in its normalized explicit encoded length). Hardness amplification at each extension would need an additional argument about arbitrary solvers.

The independent finite checks in `constant_point_lift_audit.py` evaluate the endpoint coloring relation on all 27 normalized boundary assignments at each of 24 padding lengths, test the true common-interval definition and literal deletion on all 3,318 simple seeds of lengths four through eight at paddings one through four, and recover the original seed after 100 lifts of four points each. Local coloring checks treat the predecessor of $v$ and the two blue endpoints as separate variables, which also covers any coincidences of their vertices.

The earlier [simple-extension theorem of Brignall, Ruškuc and Vatter](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.4378) embeds arbitrary permutations in simple ones. Their result does not specify preservation of this pair-of-paths coloring relation. Priority for the colored construction here has not been established.

## A fixed physical contradiction on the size-seven chain

From size eight onward, take the one-based seed 2413 and vary the padding length. In zero-based notation the seven vertices `0,1,2,3,a,c,d` have `a=4,c=M-2,d=M-1` at order size $M$. Five selected red edges are $01,12,23,3a,cd$. The six blue edges are the consecutive pairs of the word $(1,c,a,d,3,0,2)$ starting at index $\lceil (M-7)/2\rceil$. The edge $02$ has common neighbors 1 and 3, forcing them to have the same color. The edge $ad$ has common neighbors 3 and $c$, forcing them to have the same color too. The blue edge $1c$ contradicts those equalities. These eleven edges form a Moser spindle. Their positions explain why this particular infinite succession of negative instances can be decided so quickly. [Graph and coloring description](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.6036) · [Lean proof for the eleven edges](../lean/OnePointColorCore.lean)

For the canonical proper DIMACS rendering, use the seven vertex clauses and both pin signs for each of the three colors on each selected edge. The resulting $7+6\cdot11=73$ clauses are already unsatisfiable. They are inclusion-minimal as a subformula. On the abstract spindle one can check all $3^7$ colorings to find a coloring after deleting any edge. In such a coloring the endpoints of the missing edge agree. Permute the colors and choose the common pin so that just the desired edge clause fails. To omit a vertex clause, use a coloring with an incident edge deleted and leave every flag at that vertex false. The same proof applies under the relabeling on every rung. Minimality here concerns these 73 selected clauses. [Clause renderer](../audits/simple_permutation_successor.py) · [Independent certificate audit](../audits/ladder_core_audit.py)

A single globally correct SAT program can reject all these files after probing a constant number of input words. Read the canonical header, compute the expected file length and field width, then compare the selected rows at their calculated offsets. For each selected row beyond the first, also check the line immediately before it. Require that line to start immediately after a newline and contain three signed integer tokens followed by the zero terminator and a newline. On any valid whole-file DIMACS input this ends any preceding clause, even if it began on an earlier line. It also rules out reading a zero in a line comment as a delimiter. The verified row is consequently a genuine complete clause. The first body row follows the verified header at the start of the file. If the checks pass, the selected subformula justifies rejection regardless of all unread bytes. On a failed check fall back to a total decider. This uses the whole-file language in which all numeric body tokens count and malformed inputs may be rejected. Each probed line occupies $O(\log M)$ bytes, hence $O(1)$ logarithmic words. Parsing and generating those words, including decimal spellings, takes polylogarithmic time in the file length. The exceptional seven-point input can be checked in constant time. [DIMACS suggested format, §2.1](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~babic/doc/dimacs_cnf.pdf)

## Two orientations with constant and linear uncolorable certificates

Use the four-point lift above, always with one padding vertex. All ranks below start at one. Start the red path at 1234 and write the old blue path $L v R$, with maximum $v$, blue predecessor $u$ and blue first vertex $h$. At step $i$, append $a_i,b_i,c_i,d_i$ to the red path and render the blue path as $b_i L c_i a_i d_i v R$. Thus $a_i=4i+1,b_i=4i+2,c_i=4i+3,d_i=4i+4$. Each orientation of the seed below has graph $K_4$. Every lift remains simple and uncolorable by the construction above.

**One orientation has a constant certificate.** With seed 2413 the first lift is 62758413. The triangles 123 and 134 force vertices 2 and 4 to have equal colors. The triangles 567 and 267 force 5 and 2 to have equal colors. The red edge 45 makes this impossible. The seven vertices and the eleven edges used here persist forever. The next lift erases just the blue edge 58, and all subsequent erased edges involve newer vertices.

**The reverse seed forces linearly large certificates.** With seed 3142, write $G_i$ for the graph after $i$ lifts. Let $f_0=14$, and $f_i=a_id_i$ for $i\ge1$. Let $e_i=u_ic_i=(4i-3,4i+3)$ be the new blue edge from the old predecessor $u_i$ of the largest old rank to $c_i$. For every $r\ge1$ and $1\le i\le r$, the graph $G_r-e_i$ is three-colorable. Consequently every uncolorable subgraph of $G_r$ must use all $r$ edges and at least $2r$ distinct vertices; the entire graph has $4r+4$ vertices.

To prove this, keep a coloring of $G_j-f_j$ in which the ends of $f_j$ have the same color and the first blue vertex has a different color from the largest red vertex. In the seed, use colors $(0,1,2,0)$ in red order. Given such a coloring at $j-1$, normalize the common color on $u,v$ to 0 and choose $c_j=1, a_j=d_j=2,b_j=0$ in color. The constraints $u c_j,b_jh$ hold by the choice of colors. All edges of the central diamond hold except $a_j d_j$; the new blue first vertex $b_j$ differs in color from the new red maximum $d_j$. This proves the invariant.

For a designated $i$, begin with the coloring of $G_{i-1}-f_{i-1}$. At step $i$, omit the edge $e_i$, which permits assigning $c_i$ the same color as $v$ even though $u$ also has that color. Give $b_i$ a color different from $v,h$, give $a_i$ a color different from $v,b_i$, and give $d_i$ the remaining color distinct from $v,a_i$. The old $f_{i-1}$ has been deleted by construction. Now perform every later ordinary extension of this coloring. The edge $e_i$ is never removed in those steps, so this gives a coloring of $G_r-e_i$. For distinct $i$ the specified edges are vertex-disjoint by their rank formula.

**Positive neighbors inside the simple-permutation promise.** The final blue order begins with $b_r,\ldots,b_1,3,1$, continues $c_1,a_1,\ldots,c_r,a_r$ and ends in $d_r,\ldots,d_1,4,2$. For any $1\le i\le r$, switch the adjacent pair $c_i,a_i$. Set $t=d_i$ if $i=r$, and $t=c_{i+1}$ otherwise. This removes the outside edges $u_i c_i,a_i t$ and adds $u_i a_i,c_i t$. In the exhibited coloring of $G_r-e_i$, vertices $u_i,c_i$ have the common color of the maximum before step $i$, while $a_i,d_i$ both differ from that color. For $i<r$, $c_{i+1}$ has the color of $d_i$ by the ordinary extension. Hence this is a proper coloring of the switched graph.

For $i<r$ the switched final order is also simple. Directly after switching at stage $i$, the argument by projection to the simple seed at that stage leaves only the nontrivial proper interval $\{c_i,d_i\}$. This follows by inspecting relative red ranks $(1,3,4,0)$ of the central word $(a_i,c_i,d_i,v)$; only the middle pair is a nontrivial proper red interval, and neither a triple nor the whole central word is a red interval. The sole exterior vertex is $b_i$. In the next lift, any interval projecting to that exceptional pair contains the entire inserted central word between $c_i$ and $d_i$, including the new largest rank. Red contiguity then includes the new blue-first vertex $b_{i+1}$, and blue contiguity reaches the old blue-first $b_i$, contrary to projection onto just $\{c_i,d_i\}$. The cases of an empty, singleton, or full projection are the same as in the simplicity proof for the unswitched lift. Later lifts preserve simplicity. Switching at stage $i$ and growing the order has precisely the same final result as switching in the displayed final array, since stage $i+1$ inserts its central word before $d_i$ and all later insertions precede newer maxima.

It follows that on the displayed uncolorable simple input with $4r+4$ entries, a deterministic exact procedure, even if promised a simple permutation, must inspect at least one entry in each of the $r-1$ disjoint pairs. This is an $\Omega(r)$ native word-probe lower bound for a flat array with one rank per word. Packing fields gives the same asymptotic statement when the input length is $N=\Theta(r\log r)$ bits and word size is $O(\log N)$ bits. A designated seed is recognized in $O(r)$ word time by checking the displayed blue order directly, or by stripping the lifts. A total solver can check that form and fall back to general three-coloring on other inputs. Thus the order of growth of the native probe lower bound on this family is attained.

**Fixed-width DIMACS.** In the two-path encoding, the blue record for $e_i=(u_i,c_i)$ has two clauses $(-X_{u_i,j},-X_{c_i,j},\pm p)$ for each color $j$. Flip the sign of the second endpoint in those six fixed-width fields. The constructed coloring of $G_r-e_i$ gives the two endpoints the same color, satisfying the new implication $X_{u_i,j}\Rightarrow X_{c_i,j}$ for every $j$. The modified formula is satisfiable and has the same length as the original. The $r$ possible modifications affect distinct blue edge records. Each record occupies $\Theta(\log r)$ bits. A word read of $O(\log N)$ consecutive bits intersects only $O(1)$ records, so an exact deterministic procedure must make $\Omega(r)=\Omega(N/\log N)$ input-word reads on this negative file. The sign-flipped files retain valid DIMACS syntax. The switched native orders above instead preserve the promise that the source permutation is simple. Because the native switched pairs at nonadjacent levels alter disjoint constant-size groups of adjacent blue edge records, their DIMACS renderings also give the same asymptotic lower bound under that rendering promise.

**The four inserted sites have no negative simple interpolation for this seed.** The previous displayed deficient coloring has $u=v$ in color. Adding only one of $a_i,c_i,d_i$ puts it between $u,v$ in blue and after $v$ in red. Giving it any other color makes the resulting instance positive. Adding $b_i$ alone retains all old points as a proper common interval. Hence every ordering of these four prescribed sites fails at its very first insertion. The separate exact one-point ladder at all sides starting at seven remains available for the other family.

Even overlap of a bounded number of upcoming four-point extensions forces a bounded contradiction to recur along any infinite simple negative one-point chain within those embeddings. A different family inserts one point in a fixed strip and makes the smallest contradiction grow linearly.
