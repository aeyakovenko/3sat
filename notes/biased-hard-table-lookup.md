# Near-random lookup accuracy for tables written in output time

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Suppose explicit SAT on the packed logarithmic-word RAM has a deterministic $O(n^k)$ algorithm, $k=1,2,3$, with unrestricted workspace. Write $H=1/k^2$. Fix the modeled deterministic word evaluator $V$ first. For every fixed $0<\eta<H$ there exist a fixed $\delta=\Theta_k(\eta^2)>0$ and a deterministic table generator with the following property. At every sufficiently large arity $m$, it writes a packed binary table of length $B=2^m$ from the unary input of that length in $O(B/\log B)$ native word steps. Even with arbitrary precomputed advice, any predictor on $V$ using $s$ advice bits and at most $q$ native steps on every index, with $s+q\le B^{H-\eta}$, has agreement at most
$$
  \tfrac12+B^{-\delta}
$$
on a **uniformly random index of the entire table**. The evaluator is given the arity and index. One can use a fixed bounded-ISA universal evaluator to charge per-arity code in the advice. A sign flag gives the corresponding two-sided bias bound for a predictor class allowing complements, with a fixed constant absorbed in the strict exponent slack of the proof. For circuits, encode a bounded-fan-in gate list; the same exponent follows by applying the RAM statement with a slightly smaller slack to cover this encoding overhead. The case $\eta\ge H$ follows by weakening any smaller positive slack.

We use the [exact hard-table generator](output-linear-hard-tables.md). Its source table has $M$ bits, can be written in $O(M/\log M)$ native steps, and has exact lookup cost at least $M^H/\operatorname{polylog} M$ for every sufficiently large power-of-two $M$. The source evaluator is fixed to perform the reconstruction below. The encoding and local list reconstruction follow the multivariate-polynomial and Hadamard hardness-amplification method. The parameters here charge an entire optimal packed bulk construction and each native lookup call. [Constant-error comparison](coded-hard-tables.md) · [Sudan, Trevisan and Vadhan](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/1999/stv-journ.pdf)

## The code and its packed generation

Fix a sufficiently large constant dimension $r$ depending on $k,\eta$; the bounds below allow $r=\Theta_k(1/\eta)$. Choose a positive rational $\delta$ between $\eta/(400Hr)$ and $\eta/(200Hr)$. Put
$$
 b=\lfloor m/(r+1)\rfloor,\qquad Q=2^b,\qquad
 u=\lceil\delta m\rceil,\qquad \tau=2^{-u}.
$$
Then $B^{-\delta}/2<\tau\le B^{-\delta}$ and $Q\tau^5$ grows as a positive power of $B$. Choose a fixed integer $c_r$ with $2^{c_r}\ge 2^{16}r$. Set $d=2^{b-5u-c_r}$, $M=d^r$, and handle small exceptional arities by a fixed output. These dimensions require only operations on integers of $O(m)$ bits. In particular
$$
 \log_B M=\frac{r}{r+1}-5r\delta+O_r(1/m).
$$
The grid alphabet is the first $d$ elements in a fixed polynomial-basis enumeration of the field $\mathbb F_Q$. Encode the $M$ source bits as the values of the unique polynomial $P$ of degree at most $d-1$ in each of $r$ variables on the resulting grid. Bit zero and one represent the corresponding field elements. For $z\in\mathbb F_Q^r$ and a $b$-bit mask $v$ output $\langle v,P(z)\rangle$ over $\mathbb F_2$ in the chosen basis. This gives $Q^{r+1}$ bits. Ignore the remaining $m-(r+1)b$ high query bits, thereby repeating this truth table by a fixed bounded factor. Repetition preserves uniform agreement on at least one copy for any given predictor.

The constructor finds the first degree-$b$ monic irreducible polynomial by deterministic enumeration, trial-dividing each candidate by the monic polynomials of degrees at most $b/2$. This costs $O(Q^{3/2}\operatorname{poly}(b))$ steps. Precompute field multiplication and the packed $Q$-bit Hadamard block for each of the $Q$ symbols in $O(Q^2\operatorname{poly}(b))$ time. Compute the $Q\times d$ univariate Lagrange evaluation weights directly in $O(Q^3\operatorname{poly}(b))$ time including field inversion. Extend the grid one coordinate at a time, using the same weights; the $r$ passes take $O_r(dQ^r)$ constant-time field operations with the tables. This is $O_r(B\tau^5)=o(B/\log B)$. All preceding precomputations are also $o(B/\log B)$ for the fixed large $r$. For each final field symbol copy its packed Hadamard block with $O(1+Q/\log B)$ operations; misaligned output boundaries require at most a fixed number of shifts and masks per packed piece. Repeat the bounded number of copies. The input source table costs $O(M)$ steps even when unpacked for the interpolation. The total is $O(B/\log B)$ word steps. All addresses have $O(m)$ bits.

## Local lists from a biased predictor

Assume an evaluator exceeds agreement $1/2+B^{-\delta}$. Fix high index bits giving a copy with at least this much agreement and include these $O(r)$ bits in the reconstruction advice. At each $z$ form a list of all $y\in\mathbb F_Q$ whose Hadamard block has sign correlation at least $\tau$ with the predictor on indices $(z,v)$ in that copy. Walsh orthogonality gives at most $\tau^{-2}$ list elements because the squares of all normalized correlations sum to one. The mean correlation of $P(z)$ is at least $2\tau$. Thus on at least a $\tau$ fraction of the field points, the list contains $P(z)$. Finding the list at one point uses $Q$ calls to the predictor, followed if desired by exhaustive calculation of all $Q$ correlations from those saved replies.

For a source grid point $x$, consider a random quadratic curve
$$
 C(T)=x+UT+VT^2,\qquad U,V\text{ independent uniform in }\mathbb F_Q^r .
$$
At two distinct nonzero parameter values the curve positions are independent uniform points: the two-by-two linear coefficient matrix has determinant $tt'(t'-t)\ne 0$, also in characteristic two. The number of nonzero positions with $P(C(t))$ in the list consequently has mean at least $\tau(Q-1)$ and variance at most its mean. Except with probability $O(1/(Q\tau))$, at least $\tau Q/3$ positions contain the true value. The restriction $f=P\circ C$ has degree at most $D=2r(d-1)\le\tau^4Q/10^4$.

Here is a deterministic list calculation for this curve. Set $A=\lfloor\tau Q/8\rfloor$, $J=\lfloor A/(2D)\rfloor$. In the vector space spanned by $T^iY^j$ for $0\le i\le\lfloor A/2\rfloor$, $0\le j\le J$, find a nonzero $G$ vanishing at all pairs $(t,y)$ with $y$ in the list at $C(t)$. There are at most $Q\tau^{-2}$ constraints. The number of coefficients exceeds this bound for all sufficiently large $m$, since it is at least $A^2/(4D)$ and $D\le\tau^4 Q/10^4$. Gaussian elimination suffices. Every candidate polynomial $g$ of degree at most $D$ agreeing at more than $A$ positions satisfies $G(T,g(T))=0$ identically, by the root bound and the weighted degree bound $\lfloor A/2\rfloor+DJ\le A$. Over the field $\mathbb F_Q(T)$ the nonzero polynomial in $Y$ has at most $J$ roots. In particular, on a good curve the true $f$ belongs to this short root list. [Sudan's interpolation algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcom.1997.0439)

For clarity, the roots can be enumerated with field arithmetic alone. Divide out the largest common power of $T$ from $G$. Try each root $c$ in the field of the nonzero constant slice $G(0,Y)$ as the next coefficient. Substitute $Y=c+TY$ and divide out the common power of $T$ again. If $c$ has multiplicity $j_0$, the $Y^{j_0}$ coefficient before division has a nonzero term of exact order $T^{j_0}$ and the $Y^j$ coefficient is divisible by $T^j$ for every $j$. The new constant slice therefore has degree at most $j_0$. The sum of the degrees of the child slices is at most the degree of the parent slice, and the number of new children is also bounded by that parent degree. Hence there are at most $J$ nodes at each positive level. After $D+1$ coefficients check $G(T,g(T))=0$ directly. Each genuine polynomial root survives all the substitutions, which are injective on bivariate polynomials. [Roth and Ruckenstein on reconstructing the codewords](https://www.site.uottawa.ca/~zhcheng/efficient.pdf)

These local calculations take $Q^{C_0}\operatorname{polylog}B$ steps plus $O_r((q+1)Q^2\operatorname{polylog}B)$ for the predictor calls, for an absolute constant $C_0$. For example $C_0=12$ suffices for straightforward dense algorithms. The linear system has at most $O(Q^2)$ rows and columns. At depth at most $D+1$ the substituted bivariate degrees are $O(Q^2)$ in $T$ and $O(Q)$ in $Y$, with at most $O(Q^2)$ nodes overall. Expanding substitutions and checking candidate roots by direct polynomial arithmetic stay within this bound. The local decoder needs only the $b+1$ coefficients of the defining irreducible polynomial as field advice. It can do each field operation in $\operatorname{poly}(b)$ time, independently of the bulk constructor's lookup tables. Time caps and scratch banks can be reset per predictor call using tags or by clearing the touched entries.

## Resolving ambiguity at every grid point with common advice

For one trial select distinct uniform nonzero parameters $a_1,a_2$ and independent uniform points $y_1,y_2$ in $\mathbb F_Q^r$. Store these four objects and $P(y_1)$. At query $x$ take the unique quadratic curve through $C(0)=x$, $C(a_1)=y_1$, $C(a_2)=y_2$. For each fixed $x$, conditioning on $a_1,a_2$ still gives independent uniform coefficients $U,V$. Consequently $a_1$ is independent of the curve and of the root list when that list is computed solely from the curve and the predictor, with deterministic tie breaking. For any spurious root $g\ne f$ of degree at most $D$, the test $g(a_1)=P(y_1)=f(a_1)$ passes at at most $D$ of the $Q-1$ nonzero parameters. Since the list has at most $J$ entries, a spurious match occurs with probability at most $JD/(Q-1)\le\tau Q/(16(Q-1))$. Keep the unique matching root and return its value at zero, using fixed defaults on ambiguity, failure, or a nonbinary value. The true polynomial is present on a good curve, so each source query succeeds with probability at least $3/4$ for all large arities.

Independent repetitions $O(\log M)$ and majority now give one set of advice that is correct **simultaneously** on every source grid point by a union bound. The stored objects for all trials occupy $O_r(\log^2 B)$ bits. The choice can depend on the predictor and on the table, as permitted for exact lookup. No search for anchors is charged to the bulk generator. Only these fixed objects, a single copy of the predictor's advice, its clock, the field polynomial, and the arity metadata are needed at reconstruction. The same trial descriptions serve every query point. A finite arithmetic check over $\mathbb F_4$ tested all 511 nonzero bivariates of bidegree at most two with binary coefficients against brute force on all 64 possible degree-two coefficient vectors, and checked the 24 anchor interpolation bijections at this field size. These checks test the coefficient extraction and anchor identities.

Take $r$ large enough that $(C_0+3)/(r+1)<\eta/10$. The lower bound from the exact table has exponent in $B$ at least $H-H/(r+1)-5Hr\delta-o(1)>H-\eta/5$. The reconstruction with $s+q\le B^{H-\eta}$ has combined advice and time at most
$$
 \big(s+1+Q^{C_0}+(q+1)Q^2\big)\operatorname{polylog}B.
$$
Its exponent is at most $\max\{C_0/(r+1),H-\eta+2/(r+1)\}+o(1)<H-\eta/5$. Fixed-factor word-width changes between the source and target arities can be handled by a fixed number of words and a polynomially bounded address trie with polylogarithmic overhead. This contradicts the exact-table theorem. The constants in $r$ and the stated interval for rational $\delta$ give the advertised quadratic dependence on the desired exponent slack. This is a conditional statement about decoding an explicit transformation of a solver's run. A uniform upper bound for such decoding at the proved parameters would give a route to an elapsed-time lower bound for SAT.
