# How many matrix borders recover a parity of inverse entries?

One extra variable and one extra equation can query an entry of an unknown inverse. Recovering a parity of \(r\) independent entries takes **exactly \(r\)** deterministic adaptive nonsingularity queries in the model below. Each query may attach an arbitrarily large, input-independent border. Its choice may depend on every earlier verdict and the final computation is unrestricted. The lower bound already has underlying matrices with only linearly many nonzero coefficients in their dimension.

There is a sharper sparsity distinction. For a family with at most two ones in **each** row and column, an XOR of nonadaptive border verdicts still requires \(r\). Adaptive queries on this last, sparser family can beat that number. On its three-index trace they need exactly two queries. An actual rank-\(k\) change has a different answer: its inverse parity takes \(O(k+1)\) expected two-copy queries. On ordinary proper clauses restricted to a rank-\(k\) affine image, exactly the fourth power in \(k\) is necessary and sufficient in the worst case for the number of locally probed coefficients in a negative certificate. All native costs below identify the required source accessors.

## The matrix and the SAT queries

All coefficients are in \(\mathbb F_2\). A call on an unknown invertible matrix \(M\) asks whether
\[
               \begin{pmatrix}M&U\\ V&D\end{pmatrix}
\]
is nonsingular. The new matrices are public at that node of the decision tree. By block elimination the answer is \(\det(D+VM^{-1}U)\). A single row and column, with \(D=0\), recover \(v^{\mathsf T}M^{-1}u\). Singularity is equivalent to the existence of a nonzero vector annihilated by the bordered matrix. Split the row XORs by auxiliary partial sums, encode a three-bit XOR by four proper width-three clauses, and enforce nonzero by an OR chain. Width-one and width-two constraints can be padded with a constant number of proper clauses. This uses \(O(d+\mathrm{nnz})\) variable occurrences for a matrix of dimension \(d\) and that many nonzeros, hence \(O((d+\mathrm{nnz})\log(d+\mathrm{nnz}+2))\) ordinary encoded bits. A SAT verdict gives the complement of the nonsingularity bit.

Take
\[
 M(X)=\begin{pmatrix}I&X\\0&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 \Phi_R(X)=\sum_{i,j}R_{ij}X_{ij}.
\]
Its inverse is \(M(X)\) itself. Fixed identity padding can add any desired dimension to the matrix. Arbitrary connections to the old rows and columns, including padding, give a query of the form \(f(X)=\det(D_0+V_0 X U_0)\), with constants absorbed into \(D_0\). A rank decomposition of \(R\) expresses the target as an XOR of \(r=\operatorname{rank} R\) bilinear queries and gives the upper bound.

For the adaptive lower allow all binary \(n\)-square matrices \(X\). Padding by \(n^2\) identity entries gives dimension \(d=n^2+2n\) and \(\mathrm{nnz}(M)\le2d\), uniformly over \(X\). With full-rank \(R\) the bound is \(\Theta(\sqrt d)\) queries on this linear-nonzero family. For the bounded-row-and-column lower take \(X\) to be a partial permutation, with at most one 1 per row and column. Now \(d=2n\) with no padding, and each row and column of \(M(X)\) has at most two ones. In all cases the restriction applies to the old core; the lower bounds grant arbitrary borders.

## Adaptive transversality

Let \(V=\mathbb F_2^n\). One query either gives a constant answer or tests whether the graph \(G_X=\{(u,Xu):u\in V\}\) meets a fixed \(n\)-plane \(K\subset V\oplus V\) trivially. To see this for a \(k\)-wide border, put
\[
             K=\{(U_0 z,y):D_0 z+V_0 y=0\}.
\]
If \(\ker U_0\cap\ker D_0\ne0\), the bordered test is constantly singular. The same holds if \([D_0\ V_0]\) lacks full row rank. Otherwise the displayed solution space in \((z,y)\) has dimension \(n\), and its map to \(K\) is injective. A nonzero kernel vector of \(D_0+V_0 X U_0\) gives a nonzero point of \(K\cap G_X\), and conversely. This proof includes singular \(D_0\).

An adversary specifies \(X\) as a linear map only on a growing subspace \(S\subset V\). If \(K\) already contains a nonzero matched \((u,Xu)\) with \(u\in S\), answer singular. If its entire horizontal projection lies in \(S\), the query is already determined. Otherwise take \((u,v)\in K\) with \(u\notin S\), assign \(Xu=v\), extend the map to \(S+\langle u\rangle\), and answer singular. Every completion of the new partial linear map gives that answer. At most one domain direction is spent per query.

As long as \(\dim S<r\), \(S^\perp\) contains \(\alpha\) with \(R\alpha\ne0\). Choose a coordinate vector \(e_i\) with \(e_i^{\mathsf T}R\alpha=1\). Replacing any completed \(X\) by \(X+e_i\alpha^{\mathsf T}\) preserves every value on \(S\) and flips \(\Phi_R\). Hence arbitrary adaptivity and arbitrary output postprocessing cannot beat \(r\) calls on this family. The completions also admit a refined sparsity bound. Select \(\dim S\) pivot coordinates on which \(S\) projects isomorphically and realize the partial map using only those columns. The standard systematic basis of \(S^\perp\) has one additional coordinate in each basis vector. Some such vector has nonzero image under \(R\); the opposite completion adds at most one nonpivot column.

The same direction argument persists if a step can instead inspect an entire row or column of \(X\). To handle both types together, parameterize the remaining matrix as \(X_0+P Y Q\), where \(Y\) is an unconstrained rectangular matrix. A fixed row or column measurement restricts at most one range or domain direction of \(Y\). Any border still tests \(\det(D'+V'YU')\). The transversality argument works with graph dimension equal to the current domain dimension and test-plane dimension equal to its range dimension. Each genuine restriction projects the target coefficient matrix on one side by codimension one, decreasing its rank by at most one. These are aggregate probe units; the native file representation and address costs depend on how its coefficients are stored.

## Paying for several copies inside one query

Count the number of copies even if a single bordered system includes \(s\) identical cores. On the triangular family a certificate of singularity fixes at most \(s\) applications \(Xu_j=v_j\). Indeed choose one compatible completion making the system singular, take one nonzero kernel vector and read its inputs and outputs in the \(s\) copies. Every completion preserving those replies retains this same kernel vector. If no currently compatible completion makes the system singular, the answer is already nonsingular. Extending the adversary's partial map at most \(s\) directions at this step proves the same lower bound of \(r\) on **total occurrences**. This works for arbitrary existential query predicates with a sound witness using at most \(s\) complete vector applications on that branch. The adversary may pick any witness computationally.

## What an actual low-rank change permits

Let \(X\) be the unknown projected linear map, accessible through an unknown invertible core \(M\) by \(X=P M^{-1}Q\), with public maps \(P,Q\). A fixed added offset can be handled by adding its output in the equations below. Suppose the **actual rank of \(X\) is known** to be \(k\), while its images and preimages are unknown. Write the target as \(\operatorname{tr}(RX)\), transposing the entrywise coefficient mask if necessary. Even for a full-rank public \(R\), there is a Las Vegas algorithm taking \(O(k+1)\) expected arbitrary nonsingularity calls when two unchanged copies are allowed per call. With one copy per call the expected bound is \(O(k^2+1)\). No entrywise inverse computation or model of the core is supplied.

Choose independent uniform binary matrices \(A\) of size \(n\times k\) and \(B\) of size \(k\times n\). The test \(\det T=1\) for \(T=BXA\) uses one border. On success \(XA\) is a basis of \(Y=\mathrm{im}\,X\), and \(B\) restricted to \(Y\) is an isomorphism. Write \(X=UV\) for analysis, with \(U,V\) of full column and row rank. The factors \(BU\) and \(VA\) are independent uniform \(k\)-square matrices, so the success probability is \(\prod_{j=1}^k(1-2^{-j})^2\), bounded below by a positive constant. For \(k=0\) the answer is zero immediately. On success,
\[
 X=(XA)T^{-1}(BX),\qquad S=T^{-1}BXRXA,\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}S=\operatorname{tr}(RX).
\]
This is an exact full-rank sketch factorization. Range projections also appear in trace estimation, where the real approximation and query models differ. [Meyer, Musco, Musco and Woodruff, *Hutch++*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09649)

For each coordinate \(i\), set \(y=XA e_i\) and impose \(BX(Az+Ry)=0\). These are linear equations using two copies of \(X\), and their unique \(z\) is \(S e_i\). Testing the bit \(z_i\) gives one query with either chosen polarity. To express this as nonsingularity, substitute \(X\) by solving \(M h=Qv\) and returning \(Ph\) on each occurrence. Before imposing the desired bit the equations form a square system with invertible coefficient matrix: eliminating the first core gives its unique \(y\), and then the lower square block has invertible Schur complement \(T\). A single last row and column extract the bit of its unique solution. Equivalently the displayed equations with the chosen unit pin compile directly to proper 3-CNF. Their size in coefficient occurrences is \(O(\mathrm{nnz}(M)+\mathrm{nnz}(P)+\mathrm{nnz}(Q)+\mathrm{nnz}(R)+nk+d+n+k)\) per call for a \(d\)-dimensional core and explicit sparse maps. Gate-encoding adds the ordinary logarithmic name factor. XOR the \(k\) answers.

For the one-copy variant, learn the \(k^2\) entries of the successful \(T\) by scalar borders. For every public \(k\)-square \(H\), a single further border gives
\[
   \det(I+X(R+AHB))=\det(I_k+S+HT).
\]
This follows from the factorization and \(\det(I+UV)=\det(I+VU)\). The factor \(AHB\) may be presented by extra sparse equations instead of writing its dense product. Since \(T\) is now known and invertible, the method can request any additive translate of the unknown \(S\). Choose random translates until an invertible \(W=S+J\) is found. Read the entries of its inverse using \(\det(W+e_i e_j^{\mathsf T})=1+(W^{-1})_{ji}\); invert locally and remove \(J\). This takes \(k^2\) further queries and constantly many expected trials. For just an upper bound \(h\) on the rank, use rectangular sketches with \(h+c\) columns and rows and learn the projected table. With probability at least \(1-2^{1-c}\) the two sketches preserve the actual rank, by a union bound on nonzero vectors of the unknown row and column spaces. Choose a nonsingular minor of full projected rank and apply the algorithm. This last version has bounded error.

## A sharp certificate at each input

A certificate prescribes complete vector applications of \(X\); the verifier may use unlimited other computation. Only matrices of the promised exact rank \(0\le k<n\) are compared. For **each individual** such \(X\), the minimum number of applications that determine \(\operatorname{tr}(RX)\) is
\[
  \min\{\operatorname{rank}R,\ \dim(RY)+k-\dim X(RY)\},\qquad Y=\mathrm{im}\,X.
\]
Subspaces and images here are over the binary field. To prove it, let \(D\) be the span of queried inputs and \(E=X(D)\). When \(\dim E=k\), all compatible completions have their images in \(Y=E\) and already attain rank \(k\). The remaining possible differences are exactly the maps vanishing on \(D\) with image in \(Y\). Their rank-one generators \(yf^{\mathsf T}\) give trace differences \(f^{\mathsf T}R y\); all vanish exactly when \(RY\subseteq D\).

When \(t=\dim E<k\), the rank-\(k\) completions **affinely span all extensions** of the fixed replies. In a complement of \(D\), choose arbitrary columns whose images modulo \(E\) form a matrix of rank \(s=k-t\). Its range ambient dimension \(n-t\) is strictly greater than \(s\). Every rank-one map \(uf^{\mathsf T}\) modulo \(E\) is a difference of two such rank-\(s\) matrices. Choose coordinates making \(f\) the first coordinate and choose \(s\) independent target vectors whose span excludes \(u\ne0\). Use these as the first \(s\) columns and change the first by \(u\), preserving rank. Changes into \(E\) are unrestricted. This proves the affine-span assertion, including zero changes. Consequently the observed images determine the target in this case exactly when \(\mathrm{im}\,R\subseteq D\). Querying that entire image costs \(\operatorname{rank}R\). The smallest \(D\) containing \(RY\) and satisfying \(X(D)=Y\) enlarges \(RY\) by preimages of a complementary basis, costing the formula's second term.

For \(R=I\) the number is \(2k-\operatorname{rank}(X^2)\). Square-zero maps of rank \(k\) need exactly \(2k\) applications when \(n\ge2k\). A one-dimensional identity block alongside \(k-1\) square-zero two-dimensional blocks gives trace one and needs \(2k-1\). Given the rank promise, one ordinary existential formula can handle either claimed value with at most \(2k\) applications. Guess \(k\) independent images and their preimages, witnessing independence with a left inverse. Evaluate \(XR\) on that image basis using at most \(k\) further applications and check its coefficients and trace. Every accepted model gives the true answer, since its image basis must span \(Y\). The verification of independence and coefficients has a separate polynomial size cost. In particular the promise does not furnish free native access to \(Y\): an arbitrary unknown column \(v\) can already be hidden in the rank-one map \(v e_1^{\mathsf T}\).

## Which proper-clause repairs can be batched on the RAM?

Suppose the permissible values of each name are given by \(x_i=\alpha_i+u_i^{\mathsf T}z\) for a common \(k\)-bit \(z\). There is no bound on the number of changed names. Assume each signed literal's row can be read in constant time after parsing, with \(k\)-bit masks fitting one native word. Then the **exact number of violated clauses for all \(2^k\) simultaneous repairs** can be computed in \(O(N+k2^k)\) native instructions for an \(N\)-bit proper 3-CNF on densely indexed names. Sparse canonical names can instead be routed to their provided rows, charging the cost of that lookup. A topologically supplied sparse binary XOR circuit defining the rows also suffices: propagate its \(k\)-bit masks and constant bits in one pass, adding its actual size to the cost. An arbitrary sparse system determining the signals need not supply such an elimination order.

To see the bound, write \(\chi_u(z)=(-1)^{u^{\mathsf T}z}\). Eight times the indicator of a violated clause is a product of three factors \(1+\epsilon_i\chi_{u_i}(z)\), where each sign includes the offset and its literal polarity. Add its eight signed terms to an integer table by XORing masks, even when several masks coincide. A single fast Walsh–Hadamard transform gives all the violation counts, scaled by eight. Every intermediate coefficient has magnitude at most \(8m\) for \(m\) clauses, since it is a signed sum over a subset of the original table whose total absolute weight is at most \(8m\). Constantly many native words suffice per coefficient. The transform and parse fit linear time whenever \(k2^k=O(N)\), including \(k\le\log_2 N-\log_2\log_2 N+O(1)\). The audit compares all 16,320 outputs of 256 sampled clause lists against direct evaluation, checks the degree-three cores at every assignment and solves 32 corresponding proper files. Fourier representations of SAT constraints are established. [Akyrillidis et al., *FourierSAT*](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/5515/5515-13-8740-1-10-20200512.pdf)

There is also an exact physical compression given these rows. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), each violated-clause indicator is a product of three affine linear forms in \(z\). On the Boolean cube its multilinear representative has degree at most three. A basis for the span of the indicators consists of at most \(D=\sum_{j=0}^3\binom{k}{j}\) original clauses. Their simultaneous zero set equals that of the original list. Compute this basis by Gaussian elimination on the explicitly generated coefficient vectors. To emit the \(p\le3D\) selected forms, split the \(k\) bits into blocks of \(s=\max(1,\lfloor\log_2(k+1)\rfloor)\). Compute every subset parity once per block and combine the selected parities for each form. This uses \(O((k/s)(2^s+p)+p)\) gates, and proper ternary gate encodings with dense decimal names occupy \(O((1+k)^4)\) **encoded bits** regardless of the original clause count. [Four Russians construction](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=dan&option_lang=eng&paperid=35675&wshow=paper) Finding the basis is a global preprocessing step, which a local compiler would have to implement or replace.

For a **negative** repair verdict, Gaussian elimination on all source clauses can be avoided even when the columns of \(X\) are unknown. Here \(X\) may be rectangular, with one row per name and one column per ambient source coordinate. Suppose the true rank \(k\) is given, and the source supplies individual entries of \(X\), the offsets and locally addressed original clauses. Let \(n\) bound both matrix dimensions. Guess a nonsingular \(k\)-square minor by row and column indices. Its selected columns span the entire image. Guess at most \(D\) actual clauses, read their three rows on just the selected columns, and test that their compressed formula is unsatisfiable. This needs \(O((k+1)^4)\) coefficient probes and \(O((k+1)^3\log(n+m+2))\) index bits, plus the cost of the final small UNSAT decision. No numerical values from other rows are needed. Soundness follows because any real repair satisfies every selected original clause. Completeness uses the degree-three basis. With a hypothetical linear SAT decider the final test is deterministic in \(O((1+k)^4)\) steps after the data are obtained; checking the minor by elimination and constructing the file fit this time with ordinary indexed word-sized masks and names. The affirmative direction simply guesses a choice of columns and coefficients, and can test one universally chosen clause by reading its three rows on those columns. These are uniform bounds when the source accessors themselves are uniform and their costs are included.

The fourth power of the **coefficient probe** bound is necessary on one negative input for each sufficiently large dimension, even given every clause and the **indices** of the pivot columns. Start with three groups of \(l\) Boolean choices, impose exactly one per group, and add a negative triple for each of the \(l^3\) possible choices. Pairwise prohibitions and an OR chain per group use \(k=\Theta(l)\) underlying Boolean parameters including auxiliaries, with constants padded to proper clauses. Each forbidden triple has a private assignment satisfying every other clause. Make every literal occurrence of the triple bank a **distinct fresh** variable in the output formula. A private row of \(X\) copies the corresponding choice parameter to that name; use an affine offset to translate the parameters by one on the \(3l\) choice coordinates. All the private assignments therefore have at least \(3l-3\) ones in parameter coordinates. Give every other ordinary name its own affine row implementing the public background. Include unused identity rows to ensure rank \(k\), with an arbitrary fixed zero column padding where a square ambient matrix is required. On this input there is no repair. For a chosen forbidden triple and one of its occurrence rows, toggling the coefficient in **any** of the at least \(3l-3\) indicated columns flips that literal at its private assignment. All other occurrences are unchanged, and the same assignment now satisfies the entire physical list. Identity rows protect the promised rank. Thus \(3l^3(3l-3)=\Omega(k^4)\) distinct original coefficients are individually pivotal. Every sound negative certificate must read them; a packed RAM needs at least their count divided by its word width in original-word loads. The finite audit checks 6,786 individual pivot edits and solves nine proper-CNF controls. A sparse or implicit encoding can expose information differently. The three-group private-clause construction has an established antecedent. [Lee, Claim 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.0427) · [Explicit one-hot background](sampled-clause-repair.md)

Replacing the exact rank hypothesis by an upper bound already changes the possibility of a short negative certificate at rank one. On the all-zero matrix take zero offsets and one clause \(x_1\lor x_2\lor x_3\). Turning on any one coefficient in one of the three rows gives a rank-one positive instance. Every negative certificate must read all three full rows. An oracle for a projected inverse which has no source-local entry implementation cannot be substituted for this access model.

These hypotheses matter for a hierarchy compiler. If two permutations \(P,Q\) on names differ by rank \(k\), the cycle decomposition gives \(k=n-\#\mathrm{cycles}(P^{-1}Q)\). There are at most \(2k\) moved names. Yet a swap of **two occurrences** with all other names fixed already suffices to turn a formula with a supplied model into an instance of unrestricted SAT when the remaining assignments are free. If the other names are explicitly pinned, the small repair algorithm applies. The rank of a change alone does not establish those pins or give the affine rows of the genuine model. ## Bounded-degree XOR queries

Use basis vectors \(e_1,\ldots,e_n\) for the first copy of \(V\), and \(f_1,\ldots,f_n\) for the second. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), an \(n\)-plane \(K\) has a unique nonzero exterior coordinate vector \(p_K\), the wedge of any basis. The bit testing transversality is the coefficient of the full volume element in \(p_{G_X}\wedge p_K\). The coordinates of \(p_{G_X}\) are the minors of \(X\), including the empty minor. This is the usual affine Grassmann construction. [Beelen, Ghorpade and Høholdt, *Affine Grassmann Codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.1298)

These minors remain linearly independent on just the **partial permutations**. For each minor on rows \(I\) and columns \(J\), fix a matching \(E\) from \(I\) to \(J\). Sum the claimed polynomial identity over every subset of \(E\). All monomials strictly below \(E\) cancel. The monomial using every edge of \(E\) belongs only to that minor. The empty case uses \(X=0\). This also detects every coefficient of the exterior vector defining the linear functional on graph wedges.

Consequently a nonadaptive XOR of \(k\) border answers, with a free output constant, gives an actual exterior identity. One side is a sum of at most \(k\) decomposable \(n\)-vectors. The other is a possible constant multiple of \(f_1\wedge\cdots\wedge f_n\) plus its first-order terms: replace one \(f_i\) by \(e_j\) with coefficient \(R_{ij}\), with the indexing convention from the graph columns. The resulting tangent coefficient, as a map from \(F=\langle f_i\rangle\) to \((V\oplus V)/F\), has rank \(r\). A transpose in the coefficient convention makes no difference to rank.

Here is a field-independent rank argument. If that tangent rank is positive, some decomposable summand must span a plane containing \(u\notin F\). Quotient the ambient space by \(\langle u\rangle\). This kills that summand; the images of all others are decomposable or zero. The quotient is injective on \(F\), and the tangent coefficient is followed by a quotient of the outside directions by at most one dimension. An additional constant multiple of the top wedge of \(F\) does not affect that coefficient. Its rank therefore drops by at most one. Continue until no summands remain. This proves \(k\ge r\) over the binary field. In particular a *single* unmodified-core border of any size cannot pack two independent inverse entries into their parity.

## An adaptive exception already at three

For a \(3\)-square partial permutation \(X\), let \(J\) be the all-ones matrix and \(E_{13}\) the single 1 in row 1, column 3. Query \(\det(I+X(I+J))\). On zero return \(\det X\), and on one return \(1+\det(I+X(I+E_{13}))\). All 34 partial permutations give \(\operatorname{tr}X\). Each displayed expression is one allowed border. The preceding lower bound excludes depth one. On arbitrary \(n\)-square partial permutations apply the same procedure on disjoint three-coordinate principal blocks and query leftover diagonal entries individually. This gives \(2\lfloor n/3\rfloor+(n\bmod 3)\) adaptive calls even though an XOR on the same family needs \(n\).

The audit enumerates all 35 two-planes in four binary coordinates and all 1,395 three-planes in six. On both the unrestricted and partial-permutation \(3\)-square tests, every one of the 512 linear targets has XOR optimum equal to its matrix rank. Only the sparse domain admits a two-level adaptive tree for the three-index trace. The checker also verifies 471,794 actual one-column extensions of the adversarial partial map.
