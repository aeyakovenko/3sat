# Checking a computation with explicit and circuit-described patches

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A quadratic or cubic SAT algorithm would let a nondeterministic verifier certify a computation using a different Boolean circuit in each region of its spacetime tableau. It pays the sum of the individual SAT costs. Regions with no short circuit can supply the actual head motions. A region of side length $L$ can replay its interior and produce its boundary in $\widetilde O(L)$ time. Verifying agreement with neighboring circuits carries a further cost localized to its boundary.

Take the fixed unary language $H\in\mathsf{NTIME}(n^{12})\setminus\mathsf{NTIME}(n^{23/2})$ on multitape machines used in the journal. On input $1^n$ its fixed verifier has a spacetime tableau of side $D$, where $D$ is a uniformly computable power of two of order $n^{12}$. Pad with absorbing states, mark the ends, and use enough tape cells that every legal head stays in range. All coordinates have $O(\log(n+2))$ bits. [Hierarchy source, Theorems 2.1–2.2](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf) · [Earlier certificates for repeated block transitions](epoch-dictionaries.md)

Partition the square into dyadic quadtree leaves. Two leaves touch if their closures meet, including at a corner, interpreting integer sites as unit squares. Require that touching leaves have side lengths within a factor of two. For each leaf $i$ choose one of two descriptions.

* An explicit leaf of side $L_i$ supplies its top row of tape contents and its local history of heads and global transitions.
* A circuit leaf supplies a bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit with $s_i$ gates giving the full cell symbol from its time and position. Circuits are independent across leaves.

Let $b$ be the number of leaves. Let $P$ denote the explicit leaves and $C$ the circuit leaves. Let $\mathcal I$ contain pairs $(i,j)\in P\times C$ that touch or have a common touching leaf. The latter pairs permit a local test reading symbols on opposite sides of its anchor. For $d=2$ or $3$, put

$$
W_d=b+\sum_{i\in P} L_i+\sum_{j\in C}(1+s_j)^d+
                    \sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal I} L_i(1+s_j).
$$

If every sufficiently large accepted $1^n$ has some accepting tableau with such a description satisfying $W_d\le n^{23/2-\epsilon}$, for a fixed $\epsilon>0$, then explicit SAT has no deterministic unrestricted-workspace $O(m^d)$ RAM algorithm on $m$ encoded bits. A sufficient, simpler premise replaces $W_d$ by

$$
b+\sum_{i\in P}L_i^2+\sum_{j\in C}(1+s_j)^d.
$$

Indeed, the touching graph and its distance-two neighborhoods have bounded degree under balance, and $L_i(1+s_j)\le (L_i^2+(1+s_j)^2)/2$. The first criterion can be significantly cheaper. In particular one explicit tile checks an entire real run for $O(D)$ before logarithmic overhead. This recovers the ordinary time scale and gives no hierarchy speedup by itself.

## Local witnesses on an explicit patch

Use one track per tape for its symbol and a head-presence bit. Each cell also carries a copy of its row's finite global state, proposed symbols read at the heads and chosen transition. Adjacent cells must agree on that row field. Check that the transition is enabled for the field, each marked cell has the declared read symbol, a write updates its marked cell, and the next head indicator equals the result of moving the previous markers according to the transition. These last tests need only the previous cell and its immediate horizontal neighbors. Check the next global state, the initial tape and heads, and final acceptance. A finite list of local test shapes suffices, with a constant-size stencil of coordinate differences at most one in each direction. The edges of the domain use the specified machine end convention. Unary input initialization at any given coordinate costs $\mathrm{polylog}(n+2)$. Agreement and induction from the initial row give exactly a genuine computation on all tapes simultaneously.

For an explicit leaf guess the physical symbol word along its first row on each tape. At each local time guess the common row field and, per tape, the head coordinate within this leaf's columns or a marker for absence. A real run has at most one such head. These data define every cell symbol in the patch. Maintain the current word in random-access memory. Advancing a row changes at most one tape symbol per tape. At its marked coordinate check the claimed read symbol and the transition. For the head-propagation equations at sites whose whole stencil is in the leaf, compare the predicted position from the old local head with the new declared position, restricted to the interior sites being checked. A move from outside can first reach only the boundary. The comparison of two singleton-or-empty position sets takes $O(\log D)$ bit work. Horizontal agreement is built into the row representation. The globally required stencil checks that cross a leaf boundary are performed separately. No head from another patch can enter undetected in the assembled tableau.

During this replay record the symbols in a fixed-width strip on all four sides, covering the full stencil diameter. The first and last constantly many rows are copied in $O(L_i)$ operations. In the other rows the constantly many boundary columns can be retrieved at their addresses. Small leaves may record every symbol. The output has $O(L_i)$ entries. Check initial or final row conditions on the explicit leaves where they apply. Thus every explicit interior, including its interface data, costs $\widetilde O(L_i)$ even when its symbols vary throughout the patch. Lean 4.19 checks the interior head-movement identity and the agreement of all local cell contents under replay of an arbitrary chronological write log. The geometric, complexity and hierarchy arguments are manuscript proofs. [Lean verification](../lean/MixedTableauPatches.lean)

## Amortized verification and a quantitative test

Guess the rooted ordered quadtree with four children at each internal node. Traversing it recovers the domains and guarantees a covering without overlaps. Compute all touching pairs in $\widetilde O(b)$ time. Sort vertical sides by line and merge oppositely oriented interval lists on each line, then repeat horizontally. In each list the interiors of same-side intervals are disjoint. Pairs meeting only corner to corner are found by sorting corners by their coordinates. Check the ratio at every pair. In a balanced partition each side of a leaf meets only constantly many neighbors, since each has side at least half its own. The same bounded-degree estimate applies at corners.

For each circuit leaf test for a local violation anchored within its domain whose stencil sees only circuit leaves. A neighbor coordinate is in that leaf or one of its constantly many touching leaves. Include their domains, query their circuits on the indicated coordinates, and guard against stencil positions belonging to explicit leaves. Include the appropriate exterior rule at a domain edge. The entire existential query has encoded Tseitin CNF length and construction time $\widetilde O(\mathrm{polylog}(n+2)+\sum_j(1+s_j))$, with $j$ ranging only over the anchor leaf and its circuit neighbors. Reject on any satisfiable violation query. The number of summands and the number of such neighborhoods containing any fixed leaf are bounded. Hence the supposed SAT algorithm costs $\widetilde O(b+\sum_{j\in C}(1+s_j)^d)$ over all leaves, including initialization and final conditions on circuit cells.

The remaining cross-leaf tests touch an explicit leaf. Enumerate anchors within the fixed stencil distance of its perimeter and keep those whose test actually enters that leaf and at least one other. There are $O(L_i)$ anchors per explicit leaf. Overlaps of these enumerations cause only constant repetition. Explicit symbols are available from their strips. Any required circuit leaf is at distance at most two in the touching graph, so direct circuit evaluations take in total $\widetilde O(\sum_{i\in P}L_i+\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal I}L_i(1+s_j))$. Every local check has now been performed. Consistency at seams is necessary for the assembled cells to describe one trajectory.

The verifier guesses descriptions with a constructible cap $n^{23/2-\epsilon'}$, choosing a rational $0<\epsilon'<\epsilon$, checks the budget and applies these procedures. Fixed description and sorting logarithms fit the remaining exponent gap. A nondeterministic multitape verifier can check a RAM execution by recording and sorting the finite access history with polylogarithmic overhead. Accepting branches therefore recognize $H$ within $O(n^{23/2})$ if the premise and the SAT hypothesis both hold, contradicting the hierarchy. Finitely many exceptional lengths may be hardwired. [RAM simulation bounds](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf)

For example, a uniform quadtree with $\Theta(n^6)$ leaves, all with circuits of at most $n^{7/4}$ gates, costs $\widetilde O(n^{45/4})$ under cubic SAT. The quadratic counterpart allows $n^{21/8}$ gates per leaf for the same cost. A single multiplexer for these independent circuits would have a description of order the total gates in the straightforward construction, putting that entire size inside the SAT exponent. The separate queries exploit locality across regions. A one-patch explicit witness uses order $n^{12}$ slots. I have no proof that this fixed verifier's accepting tableaux have mixed descriptions below the hierarchy threshold.
