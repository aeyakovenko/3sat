# Reading a unique witness from the diagonal of a decision computation

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** For a formula with a unique witness $w$ on $m$ listed bits, a linear-time SAT algorithm would compute $w\cdot s$ on any binary mask $s$ in quasilinear time. I tried to recover every coefficient by inspecting the computation of that single bit. With one layer of products between linear forms, this task is precisely the diagonal problem for an implicitly represented symmetric binary matrix. Whole matrix-vector queries can reveal only one bit of an arbitrary diagonal per query on average. The circuit representation gives stronger access when the off-diagonal blocks have low rank. A different choice of mask alphabet recovers the full witness with one reverse circuit pass under a degree condition. [Parity decision construction](unique-witness-batches.md)

## The symmetric matrix in one product layer

Suppose a supplied fixed-schedule XOR circuit computes the linear forms $a\cdot s$, $L_i(s)$ and $M_i(s)$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Its final answer is

$$
P(s)=a\cdot s+\sum_i L_i(s)M_i(s).
$$

Constants within the factors can be expanded into constant and linear terms. Include the input and all circuit wires in the size $T$. Write $B=L^\top M$. Testing the Boolean linearity identity on $e_j,e_k,e_j+e_k$ forces $B_{jk}=B_{kj}$ for $j\ne k$. Conversely a symmetric $B$ makes $s^\top B s=\operatorname{diag}(B)\cdot s$ on every Boolean mask. Hence the hidden coefficient vector is $a+\operatorname{diag}(B)$. One forward evaluation of $M$ followed by reverse accumulation through $L$ implements $x\mapsto Bx$ in $O(T)$ gates. The vector $a$ comes from another reverse pass. Conversely any supplied XOR circuit for a symmetric $B$ gives such a parity trace of comparable size by computing $s\cdot Bs$. A trace frozen on one baseline qualifies only if its result implements the promised parity on the whole cube. [Baur and Strassen on reverse accumulation](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(83)90110-X)

**Oracle obstruction.** Give a decoder matrix-vector access and unlimited local computation. On a uniform symmetric $m$-square binary matrix, the optimum probability of recovering its whole diagonal with $q$ adaptive queries and $b$ bits of arbitrary matrix-dependent advice is

$$
2^{-\max(0,m-q-b)}.
$$

For a fixed deterministic decoder without advice, condition on any transcript, let $V$ be the span of its query vectors and put $k=\dim V$. Matrices giving this transcript form a coset of the symmetric operators annihilating $V$, since preserving all answers preserves the adaptive query sequence. Their possible diagonal differences are exactly $V^\perp$. For an invisible symmetric $D$, each $x\in V$ satisfies $x^\top D x=0=x\cdot\operatorname{diag}D$. In the other direction each $z\in V^\perp$ occurs as the diagonal of the invisible matrix $zz^\top$, of rank at most one. Thus all $2^{m-k}$ possible diagonals occur equally often in the fiber of the linear transcript constraints. Internal random coins preserve the bound by averaging. Take a union bound over the $2^b$ advice strings. Reading up to $b$ diagonal bits as advice and querying additional coordinate vectors achieves equality with a fixed guess on the remaining positions. The distribution includes matrices needing large explicit circuits. [Lean proof of perturbation and exact adaptive replay](../lean/DiagonalQueryReplay.lean)

**Low total rank.** If columns of $Y=B[:,I]$ form a basis, the principal minor $G=B[I,I]$ is invertible. Indeed, if $Yc$ vanishes at $I$, symmetry makes it orthogonal to the span of the coordinate vectors indexed by $I$, so $Y^\top(\sum_{i\in I}c_i e_i)=0$. Since the columns of $Y$ span the image of $B$, their orthogonal complement is $\ker B$. This gives $Yc=0$ and then $c=0$. Matching those basis columns gives $B=YG^{-1}Y^\top$, whence $\operatorname{diag}B=Y\operatorname{diag}(G^{-1})$ in characteristic two. Under linear SAT one can find each new independent column by an existential search on its $\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$-bit index, using ordinary elimination for membership in the current span. Repeating until no such index remains is quasilinear in $T$ for polylogarithmic rank.

## Full rank with a public hierarchy

It suffices that the sibling off-diagonal blocks have rank at most $r=\mathrm{polylog}(m+2)$ along a supplied balanced binary tree of the coordinate set with singleton leaves. The factors and their bases may all be unknown. This condition admits full-rank matrices with dense off-diagonal blocks. The method adapts hierarchical matrix probing to exact finite-field arithmetic and uses a short seed search under linear SAT. [Lin, Lu and Ying](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0149)

Process the tree from the root down. Once ancestor interactions have been subtracted, the remaining operator is block diagonal on the current nodes. At a node with two children write $C$ for the block taking left-child inputs to right-child outputs. Apply $k$ shared masks supported on the left children of all current nodes, obtaining $Y=CV$ on each right child. Apply masks supported on all right children for $Z=C^\top W$ on each left child. If $\operatorname{rank}Y=\operatorname{rank}Z=\operatorname{rank}C$, then $W^\top$ is injective on the image of $C$. For every $x$, solve

$$
(W^\top Y)t=Z^\top x.
$$

Some solution exists since $Cx$ belongs to the image of $Y$. Every solution gives $Yt=Cx$ by injectivity. Equivalently, choosing independent rows and columns of $W^\top Y$ gives a nonsingular minor and an explicit factorization through its inverse. Store both directions of each sibling interaction and subtract them in subsequent probes. At singleton leaves a final probe on the all-ones vector returns the remaining diagonal.

Take $k=O(r+\log(m+2))$ independent masks from a distribution with absolute parity bias at most $1/2$. A nonzero row-space character of any $C$ extended by zeros to all $m$ coordinates vanishes on all masks with probability at most $(3/4)^k$. The same argument applies to column-space characters. There are at most $2m\,2^r$ nonzero characters to test across all sibling pairs. For a sufficiently large constant in $k$, one list captures the true ranks for every block. The same global masks can be restricted and reused at every level. A mask with $O(\log(m+2))$ seed bits is available using binary-field evaluations, with field order at least $2m$, as in the [small-bias calculation](multitrace-witnesses.md).

The known seed length makes the recovery deterministic under linear SAT. Guess the seeds, execute the peeling, and universally test a full vector $x$ against the original circuit for $Bx$ and the proposed representation. On insufficient-rank data the routine can take any nonsingular minor of maximal size in the small sketch, including an empty minor. A final claimed representation that passes this universal test has the right diagonal. There are $O(k\log m)$ matrix-vector calls. Applying previous factors and fitting the small minors costs $(T+m)\mathrm{poly}(k,\log(m+2))$ time over the entire algorithm, including straightforward Gaussian elimination. Testing prefix completions of the $O(k\log(m+2))$ seed bits uses a fixed number of quantifier blocks per decision. Their elimination and the bit-by-bit search preserve quasilinear time for polylogarithmic $r$. This uses the original circuit as a deterministic verifier, which an isolated adaptive oracle transcript cannot provide. [Fixed-block elimination](linear-quantifiers.md)

## Change the mask alphabet

For any fixed finite field $K$ a unique-witness formula allows coefficient masks $s\in K^m$. To determine $\sum_j w_j s_j$, add a field-sum circuit and test its value with SAT, using constantly many output bits or values. The answer on those masks is genuinely linear over $K$.

Suppose an explicit arithmetic circuit of total syntactic degree at most $D\ge2$ yields the field answer on $A^m$ for a public alphabet $A\subset K$ of exactly $D$ distinct elements. Set $g(t)=\prod_{a\in A}(t-a)$. The polynomial represented by the circuit has the identity

$$
C(s)=w\cdot s+\sum_j c_j g(s_j).
$$

In the difference from $w\cdot s$, only pure powers $s_j^D$ can have any variable's exponent at least $D$. Subtract the appropriate multiples of $g(s_j)$ to remove them. The remaining polynomial has individual degrees below $D$ and vanishes on $A^m$. Repeated univariate interpolation gives the zero polynomial. If $g'$ has a root $\theta$ in an efficiently represented extension, the formal gradient of $C$ at the common point $(\theta,\ldots,\theta)$ equals the entire witness. One reverse pass suffices for fixed $D,K$. In odd characteristic with $A=\{0,1\}$ and $D=2$, $\theta=1/2$ works. In characteristic two take $D=3$, $A=\{0,1,a\}\subset\mathbb F_4$ with $a\notin\mathbb F_2$. Here $g'(t)=t^2+a$ has the root $\sqrt a$ in the same field. The two output coordinates in this case even come from two ordinary binary parity queries.

In fact it is enough that $C$ agrees with the target off a fraction strictly below $\delta=(D-1)/D^2$ of a uniform grid. A nonzero reduced polynomial with lexicographic leading exponents $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ is nonzero at at least $\prod_j(D-a_j)$ points. Induct first on its leading coefficient in all but the first variable, then apply the univariate root bound in the first variable. Here $a_j\le D-1$ and $\sum a_j\le D$. Moving degree from a smaller positive exponent to a larger one decreases the product until all but at most one nonzero exponent equal $D-1$. The bound is at least $\delta D^m$. Thus the reduced difference must be zero. For $m\ge2$ the strict threshold is sharp for the gradient itself. The degree-$D$ defect $(y-b)\prod_{a\in A\setminus\{a_0\}}(x-a)$ differs from zero on exactly a $\delta$ fraction of the grid, and its $y$ derivative at $\theta$ is nonzero because no simple root of $g$ is a root of $g'$. This uses the finite-grid minimum distance underlying the Alon–Füredi bound. [Bishnoi, Clark, Potukuchi and Schmitt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06020)

This also treats some data-dependent RAM traces. Suppose one complete sequence of control decisions and indirect addresses occurs on more than $1-\delta$ of all masks. Freeze that sequence and retain arithmetic circuits for every guard bit and for the returned field value. If each guard has total degree at most $D$, it agrees with its recorded constant on more than $1-\delta$ of the grid. The same grid bound makes every guard constant on the whole grid. Replaying instructions from the start shows that the sequence is followed by every mask and hence can be obtained at any one public baseline. If the output circuit also meets the degree bound and the discrete solver gives the true mask answers, differentiating that trace suffices. Alternatively a supplied low-degree output trace alone already works if it computes the true field answer on more than $1-\delta$ of the grid, even when its frozen guards change elsewhere.

On the two-letter Boolean cube a cubic computation already defeats every *common scalar-weighted average of baseline gradients* in characteristic zero. Such an averaging functional $E$ would need $E[1]=1$. The null circuits $x^2-x$ and $x^3-x$ force $E[x]=1/2$ and $E[x^2]=1/3$. The derivative in $y$ of the null circuit $(x^2-x)y$ forces $E[x^2]=E[x]$. This contradiction permits signed weights and non-Boolean baselines. The third alphabet value in the characteristic-two example comes from an actual extension of the decision problem.

The SAT hypothesis supplies discrete programs for the mask answers. I found no proof that those programs have efficiently constructible fixed-schedule quadratic traces, low sibling ranks, or a low-degree arithmetic trace on the larger grid. A lookup, branch, or repeated multiplication can destroy those properties while preserving the same answers. These are extraction criteria for the unrestricted question.
