# A common constant width for exact savings at almost every exponential rate

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Fix an optimal native prefix machine V with the efficient interpreter used for the [native clock argument](near-clock-credits.md). Assume ordinary explicit SAT takes deterministic linear time on the unrestricted logarithmic-word RAM. For almost every real exponential rate in [3,4], **every** interval of a fixed number of successive positive description savings contains a saving attained by infinitely many near-shortest words inside a polynomial-factor time window at that rate. The width is independent of the initial saving and the rate.

For m≥2 write L=⌊log₂m⌋+1, Tₓ(m)=2^⌊xm⌋, and tₓ(m)=max(1,⌊Tₓ(m)/mᵃ⌋). Let K denote ordinary prefix complexity on V and Kₜ its clocked counterpart. There are fixed integers a₀,H such that for almost every x∈[3,4], simultaneously for every integer D≥1 some d in {D+1,…,D+H−1} has infinitely many witnesses y of length m=2ʳ satisfying

    m−D−H < K(y) ≤ K_Tₓ(y) = m−d < m−D ≤ K_tₓ(y).                  (1)

The same witnesses work for every integer a≥a₀. For each D at least one fixed d has such witnesses on a set of rates of measure at least 1/(H−1). For an appropriate such d, a normalized integral of the clock increments over the rates also has a Martin-Löf random sum.

## An output length identifying both the saving and a rate interval

Fix D and an arbitrary half-open dyadic interval I⊆[3,4) of depth s, with |I|=2^(−s). Canonically encode the pair z=(D,I). In this paragraph write K₀ for complexity on a fixed ordinary reference prefix machine. Take a shortest name q for z and put k=|q|=K₀(z). Conditional on (z,k), take a halting name p of length n witnessing a strict record in the maximum halting time among names of length at most n. There are infinitely many record lengths since the maximum halting time is unbounded. Use a uniform invertible integer coding of z,q,p and their two runtimes to form r exceeding their lengths and runtimes. Put m=2ʳ. Standard tuple pairing and recovering these parameters take polynomial time in r, apart from the original simulations, also bounded by r.

From m and (z,k) one can recover the record time and n, simulate all conditional names of length at most n to that time, and find a missing (n+1)-bit word. That word has conditional complexity greater than n. Since m determines z, the prefix-complexity addition theorem gives K₀(m)≥k+n−O(1), uniformly in D,I. The lower semicomputable mass Σ_{|y|=m}2^(−K(y)) is therefore at most C₁2^(−k−n). This is the total mass of all shortest V names at this one output length. [Record-length proof](programmable-clock-spectrum.md) · [Addition theorem, Chapter 4](https://mccme.ru/shen/kolmbook/kolmbook-eng-scan.pdf)

One fixed V decoder reads q followed by p and computes m. It does not check shortestness or the record property. Let A be a fixed large integer paying for native simulation and fixed-block counting, and choose C₀≥2A+8. Put j=⌊log₂m⌋ and

    b(m) = max(0, j − ⌈log₂(C₀(j+1))⌉).

On sufficiently late records b(m)≥s. Read b(m)−s further raw rate bits selecting a dyadic cell J⊆I of absolute depth b(m). Its width satisfies

    C₀ L ≤ m |J| < 4 C₀ L.                                           (2)

Write v for its left endpoint. Read ℓ=m−D−c−k−n−(b(m)−s) raw rank bits, with ℓ≥0 at sufficiently late records. These select one of the first 2^ℓ missing m-bit words in lexicographic order. A word is missing when it has no producer of length less than m−D by ⌊Tᵥ(m)/mᴬ⌋. Enough words are missing since at most 2^(m−D−1) distinct words can have such names at any time. The complete name has length m−D+|header|−c<m−D. A self-delimiting specification of the constant c, when needed, is included in the header.

A sorted list of distinct m-bit words with a given prefix, a universally selected row and one bounded native transcript suffice to count the already produced words with that prefix under linear SAT. The list occupies O(m2ᵐ) bits. Counting and bitwise unranking at this one length cost at most (2ᵐ+⌊Tᵥ(m)/mᴬ⌋)poly(m) altogether. A fixed sufficiently large A makes the output timely by Tᵥ. The verification algorithm treats D, the dyadic endpoints and its clock parameters as data. Its polynomial overhead exponent is independent of D,I and the cell. [Single-length verification](moving-saving-cutoffs.md) · [Native compilation](near-clock-credits.md)

A paid word's first completion on any name shorter than m−D lies in (⌊Tᵥ/mᴬ⌋,Tᵥ]. These intervals for different J are disjoint since neighboring deadline exponents differ by at least C₀ L−1. Also, for every x∈J we have Tₓ≥Tᵥ and tₓ≤⌊Tᵥ/mᴬ⌋ at large m with a₀=A+8C₀+8, by (2). Thus the first completion meets the required outer time window for every rate in J.

## Localizing the estimate on every dyadic interval

For a rate x∈I charge 2^(b(m)−s) 2^(D−m) for each paid word in its cell. The raw rank-field size gives exactly Q=2^(−c−k−n) on each rate path. With respect to **normalized Lebesgue measure on I**, the relative size of J is 2^(s−b(m)). The integrated charge per paid word is therefore exactly 2^(D−m). A word cannot be paid in two cells because its first short completion belongs to disjoint time intervals.

If a paid word has K(y)≤m−D−H, its integrated charge is at most 2^(−H−K(y)). The total integrated cost of all such words is at most C₁2^(−H−k−n) by the output-length mass estimate. Fix H once so that this is at most Q/8, independently of D,I. Each remaining word satisfies (1) throughout its cell for some d that may vary with x but has at most H−1 possible values. Discard the portions for any d occupying less than |J|/(8H). This discards at most another Q/8 in integrated cost. Since no rate has charge greater than Q, at least 3/4 of the rates in I retain a witness at this record. On a set of the same lower measure in I, retained witnesses recur infinitely often by taking the limsup over records. At each such rate a single d repeats at unbounded lengths.

Define retention intrinsically by (1) together with the requirement that its same d satisfies (1) on a subset of the absolute depth-b(m) cell of measure at least |J|/(8H). For fixed D the set of x with infinitely many retained witnesses is measurable and independent of the interval used in the proof. The argument gives it relative measure at least 3/4 in every dyadic I. By the Lebesgue density theorem its complement has measure zero. Intersect these full-measure sets over D. Increasing a preserves every witness and its retained portion since only the earlier cutoff decreases.

There is also a topological conclusion. For fixed D and a₀, the union of the open grid interiors on which any witness exists at length m≥N is open and full-measure for every N. Removing the grid boundaries over all m removes only countably many points. These open sets are enumerable relative to the halting problem, which decides the lower bound on K(y) for each particular word. Their intersection over D,N is comeagre and includes every rate Kurtz random relative to the halting problem (equivalently, avoiding every effectively closed null set relative to that oracle). Dropping the unclocked lower bound in (1) makes the unions effectively open without an oracle, so ordinary Kurtz-random rates already satisfy the resulting clock crossing.

## A computable numerical consequence

For each D at least one d in its band has infinitely many retained witnesses on a set of rates of measure at least 1/(H−1), by the full-measure conclusion and a finite union. The same d makes the following sum Martin-Löf random for all a≥a₀:

    Fₐ,𝒹 = Σ_y (m/L) ∫₃⁴ 1{K_Tₓ(y)=m−d}
                      (2^(−K_Tₓ(y))−2^(−K_tₓ(y))) dx,    m=|y|≥2.

The integrand for each word is constant between successive rational grid points j/m, so each summand is computable rational. Its positive support has measure at most (a log₂m+2)/m. Writing τ for the first time a name of length at most m−d produces y, positivity requires tₓ(m)<τ≤Tₓ(m). Hence the normalized summand is at most (a+2)2^(−K(y)), and the sum converges by Kraft. A retained witness has the same d on a portion of J of measure at least |J|/(8H), throughout which its increment is at least 2^(−H−1)2^(−K(y)). By (2) its normalized summand is at least C₀2^(−H−1)2^(−K(y))/(8H). Infinitely many such terms imply randomness by the computable-series term criterion. For noncomputable individual x we assert existence of witnesses. Integrating makes the summands of Fₐ,𝒹 computable. [Bienvenu and Shen, Theorem 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5028)

The same localized numerical conclusion holds on every fixed dyadic interval I⊆[3,4): choose d with repeated retained witnesses on a set of rates in I of measure at least |I|/(H−1). A rate in its interior lies in cells contained in I at every sufficiently large witness length. Consequently replacing the integral by ∫ᴵ preserves the tight subsequence for this d. The particular d may depend on I.

**Direct attack on the full-interval integral.** On the full unit interval the existence of a random integrated column somewhere in each constant-width band already follows unconditionally, possibly with different constants. Fix D and use record lengths as above with no rate bits and auxiliary parameter D. At length m give the 2^(m−D−c−k−n) ranks to words absent on names shorter than m−D by t₀=⌊2^(3m)/mᴬ⌋. Direct simulation of fewer than 2ᵐ names through t₀, sorting and unranking cost at most 2ᵐ(t₀+1)poly(m). Choose A so that every paid word appears by 2^(4m)/m². The output-length mass estimate still supplies a word with K(y)>m−D−H for a single H independent of D. Let τ be that word's actual first completion on a name shorter than m−D. It lies strictly after t₀.

For a≥A+3, all x with xm between max(3m,⌈log₂τ⌉) and that maximum plus ⌊log₂m⌋ satisfy tₓ<τ≤Tₓ and 3≤x≤4 at large m. The possible savings throughout this interval are among D+1,…,D+H−1. Some saving therefore persists on a subset of measure at least ⌊log₂m⌋/(mH). A fixed such saving repeats on infinitely many records, making its normalized full-interval integral random by the same term test. This proof pays a full exponential factor 2ᵐ to locate missing words. It gives no corresponding estimate in an arbitrarily short prescribed interval of rates, or a positive-measure set of individual rates repeatedly witnessing the same saving. An unconditional violation of the almost-everywhere witness conclusion, or of the numerical test for arbitrarily short prescribed intervals on one efficient native interpreter, would exclude linear SAT. No such violation is established here. Lean checks the program-length accounting, cancellation on a finite dyadic grid and uniqueness of a word's payment cell. The measure, simulation and randomness arguments are manuscript proofs. [Lean verification](../lean/ContinuumSavingRates.lean)
