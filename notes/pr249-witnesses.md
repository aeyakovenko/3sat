# Boolean witnesses and finite precision for the permanent graph

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Could a fast SAT algorithm help the linked proof by searching over short descriptions of a successful algebraic computation? The proof's generic-path argument gives a quantitative test for this proposal. Finite Boolean witnesses can change with the input, and the algebraic tests used to check different witnesses need not share a circuit. The cost below is the maximum depth of one test. Let f be the permanent at order m≥2 and let E be the minimum number of division-free arithmetic operations over ℂ to compute a nonzero polynomial divisible by g=Y−f(X). The two results imported by PR 249 give E≥2^(c(log₂ m)^(4/3)) for some c>0 and large m. Use finite complex arithmetic/equality trees with the PR's conventions and defined division on every execution. Bürgisser already treats randomized two-sided decision complexity. The calculation below concerns the size of the integer grid and the number of simultaneously available witness tests. No priority claim is made. [Bürgisser, The Complexity of Factors of Multivariate Polynomials](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.06828v1)

## What all discrete witness tests see at generic inputs

Suppose 4T+3<E. For a tree of depth at most T, compare its path on algebraically generic (X,Y) and (X,f(X)). These paths coincide. Before a first divergence, the denominators remain nonzero on both paths. Any identity on the ambient space remains an identity on the graph. A diverging equality must therefore have a nonzero ambient numerator that vanishes after substituting Y=f(X). Monic division in Y makes that numerator a multiple of g. Computing numerators and denominators of the arithmetic prefix costs at most four operations per instruction, plus three for the final cross multiplication and subtraction. This contradicts the definition of E. Generic paths can be taken in rational function fields or realized at algebraically independent complex inputs over the constants used.

More strongly, fix **any countable family** of such trees. The field generated by all of their constants over the rationals is countable. Choose all m² entries of X and one further Y algebraically independent over that field. One concrete graph input (X,f(X)) and one concrete off-graph input (X,Y) now give **identical answer vectors for the whole family**. Applying any fixed Boolean function of those vectors preserves equality, including a quantifier over all finite binary witnesses, several alternating discrete quantifiers, or unbounded discrete postprocessing. This statement concerns countably many *fixed primitive trees* with uniformly bounded depth and no further access to the field input. An arbitrary complex witness can vary over an uncountable domain, to which the common generic choice argument does not apply.

There is a measure-theoretic version when the referee draws arbitrary complex constants independently of the input. Assume measurability and fix a countable set of primitive test labels. Draw X,Y independently with absolutely continuous coordinate laws. For every fixed random seed and label, one common generic path is taken on both inputs except on the zeros of finitely many nonzero polynomials in the sampled coordinates. This exceptional set has measure zero. Tonelli's theorem and the countable union bound give a single graph/off-graph pair for which, for almost every seed, all labeled answers coincide simultaneously. Any input dependence of a purported witness is harmless here if it only chooses among the fixed labels. The entire vector is the same, so optimizing separately over available strategies gives the same value on the pair.

## A finite-grid calculation with a witness budget

Let S={0,…,B−1} for B≥2. Promise that X is in S^(m²) and the candidate value is an integer in [0,m!(B−1)ᵐ]. For each outcome of independent referee randomness, suppose the decision is a fixed Boolean function of at most K complex tree answers, each of depth ≤T. The Boolean function and the trees may depend on that randomness. K=2ᵇ covers enumerating all b-bit witnesses; possible acceptance rules include OR, alternation over that same list of leaves, and arbitrary voting. Assume the required expectations exist. If completeness on every graph input is at least c₁ and acceptance on every off-graph input at most s₀, then either 4T+3≥E or

    c₁−s₀ ≤ [1 + K(m+1)2^(T+1)] / B.                         (1)

To see this, fix the randomness and one primitive tree. On its common generic path multiply the numerator of each test whose generic answer is unequal and the numerator of each required nonzero divisor into Q. At each instruction at most one factor is included. By step t each factor has degree at most 2ᵗ by induction on the rational register expressions. Thus deg Q<2^(T+1). Both Q(X,Y) and Q(X,f(X)) are nonzero polynomials, and their total degrees are at most 2^(T+1) and m·2^(T+1), respectively. Points where the corresponding Q does not vanish take the common path, by induction on the instructions.

Take X uniform on S^(m²) and Y independently uniform on S. The polynomial zero bound on a product grid puts the probability of a deviation in either run at most (m+1)2^(T+1)/B. For completeness, a nonzero polynomial of total degree d in independent grid elements vanishes with probability at most d/B. Expand in its final variable to degree j, apply induction with d−j to the leading coefficient, and use the univariate j-root bound when the leading coefficient is nonzero. A union bound over K tests now controls disagreement of the two final answers. Average over the fixed randomness. The graph input is always a yes-instance, and the other input lies on the graph with probability at most 1/B. Subtracting their expected acceptances gives (1).

The same estimate covers a witness committed **before** the referee randomness, or a zero-sum game of competing witnesses. More generally, require the same sets of strategies on the paired inputs. For each fixed seed, all questions, decisions and payoffs for all pure strategy pairs must be functions of the same K primitive answers and input-independent data, with payoffs in [0,1]. The event that any answer differs therefore bounds the payoff discrepancy simultaneously for all strategies. Conditional on fixed inputs let e be its probability over seeds. Every expected payoff changes by at most e, and taking a maximum, minimum, or max–min over strategies changes the value by at most e. This remains true after mixing strategies. Average e over the grid coupling to obtain (1). The K budget counts all primitive field tests that may be needed across the transcripts, regardless of which witness is chosen. Lean checks the finite coupling inequality and the corresponding perturbation of witnessed guarantees. The polynomial and genericity arguments are manuscript proofs. [Lean](../lean/WitnessCoupling.lean)

At constant gap and B=2ᵖ this proves only a lower bound of order min(E, p−log₂K−log₂(m+1)−O(1)) on T when the second term is positive. The number of independent random bits does not enter (1). Publicly selecting the best among many witness tests costs log₂K bits of the precision guarantee. Each matrix entry alone uses p bits in the fixed-width encoding. A certificate search based on this finite-grid transfer therefore still falls short of even a superlinear bound in the encoded matrix input length. Ordinary SAT also has a different input language. No reduction from the unrestricted complex graph to ordinary encoded SAT has been obtained.

As a check at the opposite extreme, on Boolean matrices a depth-m²+1 equality tree can read each entry and compare Y to the permanent stored at the reached leaf. This finite restriction admits that bound even for arbitrary complex Y. Hardwiring the leaf values is legal in the nonuniform tree model.
