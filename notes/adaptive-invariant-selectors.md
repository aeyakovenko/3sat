# Checking a rejecting RAM invariant with one SAT call

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Suppose a fixed correct solver decides ordinary explicit SAT in $O(N^k)$ deterministic time on the packed logarithmic-word load/store RAM, with $k=1,2$ or $3$ and no workspace restriction. On an unsatisfiable formula at every sufficiently large source padding length, any rejecting invariant with the adaptive predecessor selection rule below has verification cost at least
$$
\begin{cases}
\Omega(N/L^5) & k=1,\\
\Omega\!\left(N^{1/k}/L^{1+3/k}\right) & k=2,3,
\end{cases}
\qquad L=\max(2,\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil).
$$
Here $N$ is the formula's encoded bit length. The bound concerns a proof allowed to use predicates true of many configurations. One explicit special case bounds the number of ordinary Boolean gates in a product of per-word invariants by the same quantities, giving $\Omega(N/L^5)$, $\Omega(\sqrt N/L^{5/2})$ and $\Omega(N^{1/3}/L^2)$ respectively. These are conditional statements about the solver on its own diagonal formulas. [Register-history theorem](chronological-solver-registers.md) · [Packed reduction](packed-band-transfer.md)

## The proof and its cost

Fix a formula $F$ and clock $T=\lceil CN^k\rceil$. Pad a halted solver by idling. A configuration $\sigma=(\rho,M)$ consists of its controller, including registers and PC, and its entire writable memory $M$. Fix a total convention on invalid controllers. The input $F$ is immutable. A proposed finite acyclic Boolean circuit $P(t,\sigma,y)$ has extra gates that read a word of $M$ at an address computed from earlier outputs. Its key $y$ is a binary string of a specified finite length. The intended invariant at $t$ says that $P$ holds for **every** key. The circuit and its selectors may depend on $F$.

A selector is a circuit with a finite ordered list of predecessor-row query slots. Each enabled slot specifies a key and receives the value of $P(t,\sigma,\mathrm{key})$. The chosen keys and their enable bits may depend on earlier row replies, on ordinary word probes to $M$, on $t,\rho$, and, for a transition, on the challenged outgoing key. No slot can call another selector. Define three obligations.

* Every row holds on the genuine initial zero-work-memory configuration.
* On every $t<T$, every $\sigma$ and every outgoing $y$, if all enabled predecessor rows passed, then $P(t+1,\operatorname{step}_F(\sigma),y)$ passes.
* At $T$, for every $\sigma$, if all predecessor rows enabled by the terminal selector passed, the controller is in a rejecting halt.

All three obligations are statements about ordinary deterministic evaluations. In the actual initial state every row passes. If they all pass at time $t$, every enabled old-row query in every invocation of the selector is true even when keys are chosen adaptively. The transition obligation gives all rows at $t+1$. Induction and the terminal obligation prove rejection.

Count the circuitry actually needed to check one challenge of each type, including a separate copy of the **same** $P$ at each row slot and at the outgoing challenge. Let $g$ count the ordinary bounded-fan-in gates, declared input fields and output bits of those checks and their supplied templates, charging unused proof data as well. Let $Q$ bound the combined number of memory-word queries after expanding all row slots, including the constant number needed for one native instruction. For the stated cost use
$$
s=g+QL\lceil\log_2(Q+2)\rceil^2+1.
$$
Reading a word through the *native solver's* input interface is implemented separately below. The proof templates themselves can hardwire formula-dependent logic. As stated they have oracle gates only for the mutable memory. Registers, times, addresses and words have $O(L)$ bits. Built-in local decoding, word arithmetic and a bounded number of multiplexers for the guest instruction add $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ gates independent of $g,Q$. In particular hiding a large row checker inside a succinct list of calls does not decrease $s$.

## Why a single small instance suffices

A defect guess specifies a challenge type, its free finite indices and controller, then a returned word for every old-memory query. Evaluate the circuits in gate order. Post-state requests use the proposed outputs of earlier native writes to that address, taking the latest if there is more than one. Otherwise use an old-memory query. The same convention applies inside the native instruction. In the initial check the old memory is actually zero. Elsewhere sort the records of all old-memory queries by address and check agreement of answers at adjacent equal addresses. A sorting network uses $O(Q\log^2(Q+2))$ comparators on $O(L)$-bit records. Records for inactive slots can either be masked or evaluated in full with consistent replies. The guesses then extend to one memory on the full address space by assigning zero to unqueried cells. Gate induction proves that this real memory gives the very computation that exposed the defect. Conversely any violated obligation provides all the guessed words. [Batcher's sorting networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/1468075.1468121)

Use the locally addressable bounded interpreter formula $F_x$ for source input $x$ of length $n_0$ and clock $h=n_0^{2k+2}$. Its padded encoded length is $N=\Theta(h\log^3 h)$. One native instruction needs at most constantly many immutable formula words. Each can be produced in a circuit of $O((n_0+1)\operatorname{poly}(L))$ gates by the fixed local generator, including a multiplexer for the true source bits. This implements every requested formula word even for a forged controller. The defect circuit has $O(s+(n_0+1)\operatorname{poly}(L))$ gates. Its explicit Tseitin 3-CNF has bit length $O((s+(n_0+1)\operatorname{poly}(L))L)$ when $s\le N$. A fixed verifier parses topological circuit codes, checks address and slot widths, copies the same row template into each call, and emits this CNF using the construction clock derived in the signed argument. It accepts the rejection proof precisely if its one deterministic SAT call returns UNSAT. There is no SAT subroutine inside the computation being converted to this CNF. ## The signed bound

On its own padded description $x$, the verifier's public $F_x$ is satisfiable exactly if some nondeterministic branch of that verifier signals success within $h$ steps. Any such signal certifies rejection of $F_x$ by the hypothesized correct SAT solver, a contradiction. Hence $F_x$ really is unsatisfiable and there is no success by that deadline. This proof allows an unsuccessful or overlong guess to run past the deadline. The source acceptance formula only tests timely success. Clock and word-width constants are fixed for the solver and the verifier.

Here is a conservative clock for the verifier's actual construction. Restrict its proof guesses to well-typed codes of cost $s\le N$, checking bounds before any corresponding allocation. Use topological lists of constant-arity gates, word read ports with their $O(L)$ wires, and explicit slot and interface wire lists. Each gate, port wire, or declared field bit takes $O(1)$ indices of $O(L)$ bits. Unused template data are charged in $g$. The verifier may guess and parse the entire code one bit at a time within $O(sL)$ steps. Parse each template once into indexed arrays, then clone it at each call in topological order using a table from local wire IDs to new IDs. Charge every clone in $g,Q$. Generate the padded bitonic sorting network by the usual nested stage, stride and record-position loops. Each comparator on $O(L)$ bits emits $O(L)$ constant-arity gate records, with a running table of wire IDs for the next stage. Padding $Q$ to the next power of two changes the bound by a constant. These operations produce $O(s)$ records for the proof-dependent part; they need a constant number of array accesses and operations on $O(L)$-bit indices per record, including the stage-loop overhead aggregated across records. Even allowing $O(L^2)$ elementary steps per record covers computing indices and writing each of the constantly many $O(L)$-bit literals of its Tseitin clauses bit by bit. A counting pass can supply fixed field widths and the header before the writing pass. The bounded interpreter's local input generator and native transition circuits occur constantly many times, independently of $s$. Including their construction and source-input multiplexers brings the total to
$$
 O\big((s+1)L^2+(n_0+1)L^{c_0}\big)
$$
for a fixed $c_0$. The materialized query still has only $O((s+(n_0+1)\operatorname{poly}(L))L)$ bits. This deliberately leaves slack in the linear-case bound.

The executing verifier and bounded universal interpreter use the same physical width $W=c\lceil\log_2(n_0+2)\rceil$. On inputs of this particular fixed verifier, the interpreter needs a constant number of steps per native instruction, including physical loads and stores. The largest possible *actual* query on a well-typed guess with $s\le N$ has at most $n_0^{2k+4}$ bits for sufficiently large $n_0$, since $N=\Theta(n_0^{2k+2}\log^3 n_0)$. The fixed SAT algorithm's numeric workspace addresses are then bounded by $n_0^{O_k(c_{\rm SAT}+1)}$ from its own width constant. Simulation uses a disjoint native bank for that store and masks arithmetic to the guest width. All other physical arrays have a fixed polynomial address bound as well. These address **exponents** are independent of $c$; increasing $c$ changes constants in the source transition encoding. In particular symbolic addresses belonging to the $W$-bit source interpreter are Boolean trace data, and never serve as physical indices into an array in the formula generator. Choose $c$ with headroom for all real addresses, bank tags, clocks and fixed arithmetic temporaries. Thus running the actual SAT call incurs constant time per instruction; no logarithmic factor multiplies its clock. Opponent register spills and instruction lookup on the padded fixed description also incur a fixed factor. [Same-width interpreter and sparse-address audit](packed-band-transfer.md)

For $k=2,3$, if $s\le c_1 h^{1/k}/L$, guessing and construction take $o(h)$. Solving costs $O(((s+(n_0+1)\operatorname{poly}(L))L)^k)$. The $n_0$ contribution is $o(h)$ since $h=n_0^{2k+2}$, and the $s$ contribution fits the remaining clock for sufficiently small fixed $c_1$. For $k=1$, take $s\le c_1 h/L^2$. Construction costs $O(c_1 h)+o(h)$ and solving costs $O(c_1 h/L)+o(h)$. Absorbing the fixed interpreter slowdown into $c_1$ would give a timely success in each case. Since $h=\Theta(N/L^3)$, this proves the three lower bounds above.

## Total product invariants and empty fibers

Let a standard Boolean circuit $R(t,\rho)$ describe the allowed controllers and a circuit $A(t,\rho,a,v)$ the allowed values at address $a$. Suppose for every allowed controller and address the set of allowed words is nonempty. Consider any semantically inductive rejecting invariant of the form
$$
R(t,\rho)\ \land\ \bigwedge_a A(t,\rho,a,M[a]).
$$
Use one row $P(t,\sigma,a)=R(t,\rho)\land A(t,\rho,a,M[a])$. For a transition challenge at $a$, query old rows at $a$ and at the bounded number of addresses accessed by the actual instruction. If these pass, preserve those old words and fill every other address independently with an allowed value. The completed state satisfies the whole old invariant, makes the same transition, and has the same outgoing controller and word at $a$. Semantic inductiveness on that completion therefore proves the challenged row. At the final time semantic rejection and nonempty fibers imply that every allowed controller is rejecting, so the terminal selector can query one fixed old row. The initial test follows immediately. This uses $Q=O(1)$ and $O(|R|+|A|+\operatorname{poly}(L))$ ordinary gates across the checks. It gives the advertised bounds on $|R|+|A|$. In particular an exact per-time circuit for the true registers and the true word at an arbitrary addressed location supplies singleton fibers.

The nonempty-fiber condition matters if one claims that arbitrary *semantic* inductiveness can be tested by this local verifier. With unrestricted fibers, deciding existence of a step violating such an invariant is $\Sigma_3^P$-complete in the succinct circuit representation, even for a fixed instruction that just changes phase and preserves memory. Give the old phase the test $\bigwedge_a D(\rho,a,M[a])$ and the new phase the constantly false test. A counterexample exists exactly when $\exists\rho\,\forall a\,\exists v\ D(\rho,a,v)$. Different addresses may independently choose their words. For membership with a general native instruction and a local outgoing failure, guess the controller, the touched addresses and words consistently with its execution, and the outgoing row. Check its failure and the relevant preconditions. For each remaining address universally challenge the address and existentially supply a word satisfying its old fiber. This yields the same three blocks. Here address and word widths are parameters of the succinct circuit instance and may encode the quantified strings. This is a classification of the semantic step test. The diagonal uses the stronger local implications, whose failure has the single existential certificate above. [Stockmeyer on the polynomial hierarchy](https://research.ibm.com/publications/the-polynomial-time-hierarchy) · [Independent predecessor-row selection gap](row-selection-gap.md)

The argument does not supply a short inductive proof for the true rejecting run of an arbitrary solver. Its conclusion allows the circuit or selector to be large even when the formula itself has a short uniform description.
