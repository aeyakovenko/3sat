# Predicting ordinary SAT on random checksums of a genuine RAM computation

For every stage $j$ there is a finitely described distribution of ordinary explicit 3-CNFs. Exactly half its draws are unsatisfiable and half have **exactly one complete satisfying assignment**. For $M=2^j$ each formula has $M\operatorname{poly}(j)$ encoded bits and can be read at a specified word address in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time from its query description. Completeness and uniqueness apply to the whole printed formula, including the checksum's auxiliary variables.

Fix $A\ge1$, $\epsilon=(j+1)^{-A}$, and one deterministic logarithmic-word predictor program. Its stage-dependent advice string is arbitrary and common to all query descriptions at that stage. It may inspect the actual formula by random access and receive its query description and stage as extra inputs. For each $k=1,2,3$, suppose explicit SAT has unrestricted deterministic worst-case time $O(n^k)$. There is a constant $B$ such that for every sufficiently late stage, spending at most
$$
 R_j=\left\lfloor 2^{\lfloor j/k^2\rfloor}/(j+1)^B\right\rfloor
$$
in **advice bits plus worst-case word-RAM steps per prediction** gives success probability at most $1/2+\epsilon$. The constants may depend on the fixed predictor, $A,k$ and the assumed solver. A weighted distribution on formula strings is meant; two descriptors giving the same string need not count as one draw. A bounded randomized predictor with at most one logarithmic word of fresh randomness per step has the same conclusion after adjusting $B$: at an advantageous stage fix a random tape achieving at least its expected success, charging $O((R_j+1)(j+1))$ bits in the deterministic simulation. The target is the decision bit of the printed CNF. The theorem is conditional on the SAT clock and the predictor budget is below the printed input length. A predictor within this budget with the stated advantage on infinitely many stages would rule out that clock; no effective set of good stages needs to be supplied.

## Physically printing the bit test

Let $C_j(X)$ be the parsimonious unpinned near-native 3-CNF for the genuine deterministic priority computation. It checks the chronological records and the deterministic gate values of a fixed verifier including its sorting network; exactly one full assignment $h\in\{0,1\}^{v}$ passes for each $j$. A specified slot in it gives $b_j$, the actual verdict. Clauses, their indices, $v$ and that slot are locally accessible from $j$ in polynomial-in-$j$ time. This uses the deterministic sorting-network construction with all unused slots fixed. A nondeterministically routed witness with several valid routings would not have the required uniqueness. [Chronology and parsimonious 3-CNF](projected-parity-holes.md#a-parsimonious-ordinary-3-cnf-near-the-native-clock) · [Native sign bound](priority-pinned-sat.md)

Put $d=\lceil\log_2 v\rceil$. In $K=\mathbb F_{2^s}$ choose $q=2^s$ to be the least power of two at least $2^{18}(d+1)/\epsilon^2$. Uniformly choose $p\in K^d$, $\lambda\in K$ and a fair pin $\tau\in\{0,1\}$, independently. Write $[z]$ for the $s$ binary coordinates of $z$ in a public polynomial basis, and use the ordinary binary dot product on coefficient vectors. Field arithmetic takes $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ time, including, if needed, deterministic construction of a modulus by enumerating monic polynomials and trial factors since $q=\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$. Define the actual message polynomial and public selector bits by
$$
 L_i(p)=\prod_{l:i_l=1}p_l\prod_{l:i_l=0}(1-p_l),\qquad
 P(p)=\sum_{i=0}^{v-1} h_i L_i(p),\qquad c_i=\langle[\lambda],[L_i(p)]\rangle .
$$
Here $i_l$ is a binary index digit; the multilinear message has value zero on padding indices $v,\ldots,2^d-1$. The following binary checksum is exactly a Hadamard encoding of the outer field symbol:
$$
 \bigoplus_{i=0}^{v-1} c_i h_i=\langle[\lambda],[P(p)]\rangle .
$$
One may instead use $c_i=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}(\lambda L_i(p))$. The trace pairing gives the same family of linear functionals up to a bijection of $\lambda$. Indeed the trace polynomial $z+z^2+\cdots+z^{2^{s-1}}$ is nonzero of degree smaller than $q$, so there exists an element of trace one; multiplying by any fixed nonzero field element is bijective. Both conventions have identical Hadamard distances.

Append to $C_j$ a new bit $u_i$ pinned to $c_i$ per slot, an AND gate $w_i=u_i\land X_i$, and a running XOR $r_{i+1}=r_i\oplus w_i$ with $r_0=0$. Assert $r_v=\tau$. Each AND and XOR is enforced in both directions by its ordinary width-at-most-three clauses. All new variables are determined once $X$ is known. Thus the result is satisfiable precisely when $\langle[\lambda],[P(p)]\rangle=\tau$, with one solution then and none otherwise. Equal lengths and fixed clause locations across all descriptions at the same stage are possible since only the signs of public pins vary. Fairness of $\tau$ gives the exact half-and-half conclusion even if a field symbol happens to vanish.

If exact three distinct variables per clause are required, use three fresh shared padding bits forced to $000$ by seven exact-three clauses forbidding the other seven assignments. Append distinct positive padding literals to each short clause. Simplify any repeated literal within a clause and replace a tautological slot by one always-true clause on the padding triple. A false empty clause can use the three positive padding literals. The added variables have unique values, preserving the assignment count. The number of clauses is linear in the original size plus $v$. Computing any public coefficient $c_i$ requires $O(d)$ field operations. These fixed arrays and the underlying native construction permit any packed formula word, including clause data crossing an alignment boundary, to be generated in $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ time from the short query description. Reading the entire query-dependent formula would cost its full encoded size.

The physical formula can optionally carry its entire public label in its first clause records. For a bit string of length $L$, write the binary digits of $L+1$ preceded by as many ones as there are digits and a zero delimiter, then write the string itself. Use this code for the binary numeral for $j$ and for the bit string listing the field coordinates of $p$, then $\lambda$, then $\tau$, in their public order. In the equality-padded version the point string also includes the extra coordinates. Put one fresh variable per encoded bit at the start of the clause list and pin it by a single signed literal. In the exact-three format complete the clause with the two common false padding bits, keeping the data literal first. These variables are outside the interpolated table. Each header has exactly one extension of its own, regardless of the pin, and all its strings have the same encoded length at a fixed stage. The standard binary CNF prefix gives the fixed literal width and clause stride. Starting at its first clause, a reader extracts one leading sign per record and stops after the two self-delimiting strings, using $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ physical word reads and work with no query input besides the CNF. The same records remain locally generable in that time, and their total encoded length is $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$. [Fixed-width binary CNF encoding](packed-band-transfer.md)

## Reconstructing the table even from two-sided errors

Suppose an advice string and a predictor have success greater than $1/2+\epsilon$ at a stage. Fix one of the two terminal pins that achieves this success conditionally. This adds one advice bit. Convert the predicted SAT answer back to a proposed checksum bit: on an affirmative answer use the selected pin, on a negative answer use its opposite. This gives a function $D(p,\lambda)$ correlated with $\langle[\lambda],[P(p)]\rangle$ by more than $2\epsilon$ in the signed convention, averaged over $p,\lambda$. Both kinds of decision error are permitted.

At each $p$ use the soft score
$$
 g_p(z)=\mathbb E_\lambda(-1)^{D(p,\lambda)+\langle[\lambda],[z]\rangle}
$$
for every $z\in K$. All scores take polynomial-in-$j$ work with oracle access to $D$, since all $q$ test vectors and $q$ candidate symbols can be enumerated. Walsh orthogonality gives $\sum_z g_p(z)^2=1$ by Parseval. The agreement hypothesis gives $\mathbb E_p g_p(P(p))>2\epsilon$. The binary version of the [soft-score decoding lemma](recovering-tables-from-field-values.md#beating-a-random-field-symbol-guess-on-fixed-scalar-queries) now applies at the displayed $q$.

In detail, to recover $P(x)$ at a Boolean index, use the quadratic curve $C(t)=x+At+Bt^2$. For independent uniform coefficients $A,B\in K^d$, its points at two distinct nonzero field parameters are independent: the determinant is $tu(u-t)\ne0$, including in characteristic two. The score average along the curve, including the point at zero, is at least $\epsilon/2$ with probability at least $31/32$ for all sufficiently large stages. This follows from the variance bound $1/(q-1)$ on the nonzero parameters and the uniform bound one on the magnitude of any single score. The true restriction $P(C(t))$ has degree at most $2d$. The cited multiplicity interpolation algorithm lists every degree-at-most-$2d$ polynomial whose score average on the curve is at least $\epsilon/2$ in $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ work. It uses all scores, and its filtered list has at most $8/\epsilon^2$ entries. For the size bound, the norm-one incidence vectors of two different candidate polynomials have pairwise inner product at most $2d/q$ by the root bound, whereas each listed vector has inner product at least $\epsilon/2$ with the single norm-at-most-one score array. [Koetter and Vardy on algebraic soft-decision decoding](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2003.819332)

Choose one uniform anchor $z\in K^d$, an independent uniform quadratic coefficient $B$, a uniform $T\in K^*$ and supply these with the true value $P(z)$. Set $A=(z-x-BT^2)/T$. For each fixed $x$ this gives independent uniform $A,B,T$, so conditional on the curve its unannounced anchor parameter is uniform on $K^*$. Build its list independently of that parameter. Return a candidate's constant term if it is the unique candidate matching $P(z)$ at $T$. Otherwise abstain. Even on curves that omitted the true candidate the chance a false candidate matches is at most $(8/\epsilon^2)2d/(q-1)<1/32$. Thus a single advised trial is correct with probability at least $15/16$. With $O(\log(v+2))$ independent advised tuples, majority recovery of $h_x$ fails with probability below $1/(2v)$. A union bound gives **one list of tuples** working for all original coordinates simultaneously. No decoder computes the true anchor values to generate its own advice. Those bits are allowed because the reconstructed table is independently verified. The remaining stage advice consists of the favorable pin and the predictor's advice. [Quadratic-curve correction](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/1992/gemmell.pdf)

## Cost on the RAM and soundness at unknown stages

The number of calls to the predictor per queried table bit is $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$: each anchor takes a polynomial number of outer evaluations, each requiring only polynomially many queries to $D$. Run the predictor itself using its declared read-only advice, clean work memory and a fixed bounded simulation. When it reads a word of the physical checksum formula, locally generate that word for its current description and pin. Normal logarithmic-word adaptation, formula access and bookkeeping cost at most a fixed polynomial in $j$ per simulated operation; clean banks can be reset by undoing touched writes. This directly gives a RAM evaluator for $h_x$ with total advice bits plus time per invocation at most $(a+t+1)\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ for $a$ predictor advice bits and $t$ worst-case steps. The exponential term in a circuit simulation of unrestricted mutable addresses is unnecessary. The budget gives no free preprocessed data depending on the sampled query, no nonuniform primitive instructions, and no free copy of an entire query-dependent formula into writable memory.

Guess that advice, check that the designated output bit evaluates to a chosen sign, and universally challenge one clause of the unpinned $C_j$. Evaluate the reconstructed bits of its at most three named variables. Every clause passing makes this a satisfying assignment of $C_j$, and uniqueness makes the sign check sound on *every* stage, including stages with arbitrary bad predictions, wrong anchor values, or malicious advice. Use fixed clock caps, total parsing and deterministic defaults in the evaluator to give every guessed string a defined table. At every stage with the required advantage, at least one of the two sign detectors accepts using the recovered table.

Under deterministic $O(n^k)$ SAT, the two ordinary bounded-computation eliminations cost $(a+t+1)^{k^2}\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$. The inner SAT call checks for a violated actual clause under the given description, and the outer call guesses the description and carries out this deterministic check. Receipt, storage and indexing of the guessed bits, local formula generation, and the final output pin all fit the stated budget. For sufficiently large fixed $B$ this is $o(M)$ whenever $a+t\le R_j$, contrary to the native $\Omega_D(M)$ bound on every sufficiently late successful signal. This covers infinitely often, unmarked good stages, since both sign procedures are sound regardless of the frequency of those stages.
