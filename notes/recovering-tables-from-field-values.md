# Recovering a complete RAM table from approximate finite-field evaluations

A single canonical multilinear-extension value distinguishes the number of holes in the [priority computation table](support-filtered-parity.md). Here evaluations at a distribution of succinctly specified points recover **every bit** of a genuine table. The coordinates are ordinary elements of the 16-element field away from the Boolean cube. A predictor may use a different Boolean circuit at every stage. It need not tell us which stages it predicts accurately.

Let $M=2^j$. Use the locally indexed closed Boolean circuit computing the actual priority bit $b_j$, with its acyclic, uniquely determined gate values $s_j$. In the [tagged construction](projected-parity-holes.md#long-primary-words-with-fast-local-positive-verification), the width-three violation DNF $W_j$ on $v$ bits has the mandatory missing word $h=(0,s_j)$. It may also miss $(1,\neg s_j)$, depending on the sign. We only use that any second hole has tag one. The width and gate indices have polynomial-in-$j$ generation cost, and $\log v=O(j)$. The target of a prediction is the four-bit field value of the **canonical** multilinear extension $\widetilde W_j$, so any circuit representation agreeing on Boolean inputs must be justified off the cube.

There is a polynomial-in-$j$ query description and distribution, defined below, with this consequence. If unrestricted deterministic SAT on $n$ encoded bits takes $O(n^k)$ time for $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, then there is a constant $B$ such that at **every sufficiently late stage** every Boolean circuit of size at most $2^{\lfloor j/k^2\rfloor}/(j+1)^B$ predicting that field value errs on more than $1/1024$ of these descriptions. Size can count gates, wires and output bits at bounded fan-in. The query circuit gets the stage as fixed data and receives only the short description, with a fixed binary encoding for field elements. The claim concerns no distribution over unrelated ordinary SAT formulas. Uniformity is needed in the verifier of the recovered table; the good predictor and its good-stage set can be nonuniform.

## Four canonical evaluations give a digit of an encoded table symbol

Number the $v$ genuine table coordinates from zero, with the tag at a specified index $i_0$. Let $d=\lceil\log_2 v\rceil$. Pad the **indexing of the message** by zero bits on $v,\ldots,2^d-1$. The original DNF still has exactly $v$ input variables. In $K=\mathbb F_{5^a}$, where $q=5^a$ is the least power of five at least $8(d+1)$, interpolate $h$ on the binary grid using
$$
 L_i(p)=\prod_{l:i_l=1}p_l\prod_{l:i_l=0}(1-p_l),\qquad P(p)=\sum_{0\le i<v}h_i L_i(p).
$$
Thus $P$ is a polynomial of total degree at most $d$. Its value at the binary digits of $i<v$ is $h_i$. For $p\in K^d$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb F_5^a$ put $c_i=\langle\lambda,[L_i(p)]\rangle$, the ordinary base-field dot product with the coefficient vector in a fixed basis for $K$. The digit we want is $\sum_{i<v}c_ih_i=\langle\lambda,[P(p)]\rangle\in\mathbb F_5$. Field conventions are uniform. One can construct the first irreducible degree-$a$ polynomial by trying monic polynomials and trial divisors, since $q=O(d+1)$.

Fix $u$ of order five in $F=\mathbb F_{16}$. For nonzero exponents $e_i\bmod5$ take $t_i=u^{e_i}/(1+u^{e_i})$. These fractions exist and lie outside $\{0,1\}$. From the full field answer obtain
$$
 Z(e)=\frac{1+\widetilde W_j(t_0,\ldots,t_{v-1})}{\prod_i(1+t_i)}
     =\sum_{x\text{ a hole}}u^{\sum_i e_ix_i}.
$$
This follows by expanding the multilinear indicators of the holes. To allow arbitrary digits without ever using exponent zero, put $b(c)=2$ for $c=4$ and $b(c)=1$ otherwise. Set $a(c)=b(c)+c\pmod5$. Both choices belong to $\{1,2,3,4\}$. Use either the vector $e_i=a(c_i)$ or $e_i=b(c_i)$. For each of these two vectors, use either the base vector or a vector shifted only at the tag coordinate by $g=2$ if $e_{i_0}=4$ and $g=1$ otherwise. The shifted exponent is again nonzero. Sample $p,\lambda$ independently uniformly, and the two choices of vector and shift independently uniformly, giving four equally likely query types.

For either vector, the normalized base and shifted answers are $X+Y$ and $X+u^gY$. Here $X=u^{\sum_i e_ih_i}$ is contributed by the mandatory tag-zero hole. The optional hole contributes $Y$, taken as zero when absent. These two equations determine $X$ because $u^g+1$ is nonzero. Dividing the $X$ for the $a$ vector by that for the $b$ vector gives $u^{\langle\lambda,[P(p)]\rangle}$. A table of five powers yields the digit. Malformed predictions can return any fixed default. They do not affect the calculation on four correct replies.

All individual coordinates $t_i$ and both normalization constants are computable in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ steps from the query description. For the global product, process the $d$ binary positions of indices in order. Maintain for each $z\in K$ a count modulo 15 of prefixes whose current product of interpolation factors is $z$. Multiply by the two possible factors at the next digit, adding counts reaching the same key. Retain the usual three comparison states against the largest allowed index $v-1$, then count only smaller or equal complete indices. The table size is $O(q)$ per layer. The multiplicity of each $c=\langle\lambda,[z]\rangle$ now determines the normalization for the base vector because $F^*$ has order 15. Correct the one changed factor at $i_0$ for the shifted query. This avoids a $v$-coordinate scan.

## Correcting imperfect predictions at unknown stages

Fix one stage and a circuit with field error at most $1/1024$. By a union bound over the four types the extracted inner digit is wrong on at most $1/256$ of pairs $(p,\lambda)$. For at least $63/64$ of $p$, at most $1/4$ of $\lambda$ answers are wrong. Distinct values $z,z'$ of $K$ have dot-product words $\langle\lambda,[z]\rangle$ and $\langle\lambda,[z']\rangle$ disagreeing on exactly $4/5$ of $\lambda$, since their difference is a nonzero linear form. Enumerate all $q$ symbol candidates and all $q$ test vectors, choosing a nearest word with fixed tie-breaking. On those good field points this recovers $P(p)$ exactly.

For a desired Boolean index $x$, take a uniform direction $y\in K^d$. Each $x+\tau y$ is uniform for $\tau\ne0$. The expected fraction of bad symbols on the $q-1$ nonzero positions is at most $1/64$, even if errors at different points are correlated. With probability at least $15/16$, at most a quarter are bad. Write $e=\lfloor(q-1)/4\rfloor$. Recover the line polynomial $\tau\mapsto P(x+\tau y)$ of degree at most $d$ by solving for a monic degree-$e$ error polynomial $E$ and a polynomial $N$ of degree at most $d+e$ with $N(\tau)=E(\tau)$ times the reported symbol at every nonzero position. With at most $e$ errors some solution exists, by taking all error locations as roots and multiplying by extra powers of $\tau$ when needed. For any solution, $N-E P|_{\rm line}$ has degree at most $d+e$ and at least $q-1-e>d+e$ roots, so division gives exactly the true polynomial. Use a fixed default on failure. Its constant term is $h_x$ for genuine indices.

Repeat with $O(\log(v+2))$ independent directions, taking the majority bit. For every fixed index the failure probability falls below $1/(2v)$ by elementary binomial amplification. By a union bound, one fixed list of directions works for every coordinate at once. Hardwiring those directions and the predictor gives a Boolean table circuit of size $(S+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ when the predictor has size $S$. Every field size, call count, bit operation in Gaussian elimination, and random direction length here is polynomial in $j$. Local correction of low-degree encodings from noisy symbol predictions is classical. The [journal's previous binary encoding](coded-hard-tables.md) constructs an encoded hard table. The four field probes above derive this type of encoding from canonical evaluations of the *original* genuine predicate. [Sudan, Trevisan and Vadhan](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/1999/stv-journ.pdf)

To check the recovered circuit, guess its description, require the designated output gate bit to be a chosen sign, and universally name a gate of the original locally indexed closed computation. Evaluate the claimed table bits at that gate and its predecessors and check the actual gate equation and input constants. Check the mandatory zero tag as well. The gate table is unique by acyclicity, so either sign detector is sound at every stage regardless of the predictor's behavior. An accurate predictor at any stage makes the corresponding detector accept. The circuit description and branch time are $(S+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$, including indices into the guessed circuit. Under $O(n^k)$ deterministic SAT, the two ordinary bounded-computation SAT eliminations cost $(S+1)^{k^2}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ as in the [packed reduction](packed-band-transfer.md). More explicitly the inner complement query handles one bad gate for the supplied description, and the outer query guesses the description and runs that deterministic inner test. Use a fixed constructible size cap slightly larger than the decoded size, absorbing polynomial overheads into $B$. Then each detector would cost $o(M)$, contradicting the [native sign lower bound](priority-pinned-sat.md) at any sufficiently late successful scale. No predicate deciding the set of accurate stages is used.

## One common scalar on a mildly enlarged formula

Alternatively keep the evaluation point literally identical across descriptions of a fixed stage. Use five primary slots per original bit. For a given query vector $e$, force its first $e_i$ slots to agree with the first slot and all its remaining slots to be zero. Here $e_i$ is its standard representative in $1,\ldots,4$. Add violation terms for these local equality and zero requirements to the DNF $W_j$ on the first slots. Every original hole extends to exactly one new hole. The new word has Hamming weight $\sum_i e_ix_i$. A field evaluation at the common diagonal $u/(1+u)$ now gives the same $Z(e)$ after normalization by $(1+u/(1+u))^{5v}$. The width remains at most three and every extra term is locally indexable in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. A fixed term layout can include disabled slots as identically false terms or use a dummy index convention in the local descriptor. Thus there is also an every-late-stage average-case lower bound under the same SAT hypothesis for circuits that take a **short description of a formula** and predict its extension at one fixed scalar. Such a predictor receives the query description, or a polynomial-time produced succinct generator of those terms. Feeding it the entire expanded DNF would change the input length.

## Beating a random field-symbol guess on fixed-scalar queries

For this improvement use only the tag-zero restriction of the original DNF, with its unique hole $h$ (fix the tag or enforce it by one violation term). The final gate in $h$ still gives $b_j$. For each digit $c_i$ choose $e_i$ from $1,\ldots,5$ congruent to $c_i$ modulo five, and use the preceding copied-bit construction. Its single hole extends $h$ with known copy positions. The normalized answer at the fixed diagonal is **exactly** $u^{\langle\lambda,[P(p)]\rangle}$. The public normalization factor and conversion between the five possible field answers and five digits cost $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ including the width. A uniform random digit is right with probability $1/5$ on every query.

Fix a positive integer $A$ and put $\epsilon=(j+1)^{-A}$. Choose the least power $q$ of five at least $2^{16}(d+1)/\epsilon^2$. This polynomial field size is for the $1/5+\epsilon$ theorem; the earlier high-accuracy theorem needs only the smaller field. For every $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, under its $O(n^k)$ SAT hypothesis there is a constant $B_A$ such that, at every sufficiently late stage, no circuit of size at most $2^{\lfloor j/k^2\rfloor}/(j+1)^{B_A}$ predicts the full fixed-scalar answer on more than $1/5+\epsilon$ of the uniformly sampled $(p,\lambda)$. This refers to the succinct, query-dependent copied formula and one common scalar. The unmodified table at four ordinary mixed points has the high-accuracy theorem above.

Regard any field prediction outside the five legitimate answers as an arbitrary digit. For each $p$ retain a real score for **every** candidate $z\in K$,
$$
 g_p(z)=\frac{\Pr_\lambda[\mathrm{guess}(p,\lambda)=\langle\lambda,[z]\rangle]-1/5}{4/5}.
$$
All scores follow by enumerating the $q$ possible $\lambda$ and all $q$ possible $z$. They lie in $[-1/4,1]$, with $\sum_z g_p(z)^2\le1$. Indeed the centered one-hot words $w_z(\lambda,b)=[\langle\lambda,[z]\rangle=b]-1/5$ are orthogonal across distinct candidates under the real inner product averaging over $\lambda$ and summing over digits $b$. They have squared norm $4/5$, as does the centered one-hot word of the predictor. The normalized coefficients of the latter on these orthogonal directions are the scores. Bessel's inequality gives the bound. If the predictor beats the stated agreement, $\mathbb E_p g_p(P(p))>5\epsilon/4$. This use of symbol reliabilities parallels algebraic soft-decision decoding. [Koetter and Vardy](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2003.819332)

For a Boolean index $x$, start with a quadratic curve $C(t)=x+At+Bt^2$ for independent uniform $A,B\in K^d$. For distinct nonzero $t,s$ the pair $C(t),C(s)$ is independent and uniform: the determinant $ts(s-t)$ is nonzero. Thus the average of $g_{C(t)}(P(C(t)))$ over the $q-1$ nonzero parameters has the global mean and variance at most $1/(q-1)$. Chebyshev's inequality puts this observed score above or equal to $\epsilon$ except with probability at most $16/((q-1)\epsilon^2)<1/32$. Including the parameter zero can lower the resulting average by at most $2/q$. Call a curve good if this last average is at least $\epsilon/2$. For every $x$ at least $31/32$ of random curves are good. The restriction of $P$ has degree at most $d'=2d$. Random polynomial curves for local correction are classical. [Gemmell and Sudan](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/1992/gemmell.pdf)

For usable nonuniform advice, choose independent uniform $z\in K^d$, $B\in K^d$, and $T\in K^*$. Supply these values and the true symbol $P(z)$. At each index $x$ compute $A=(z-x-BT^2)/T$. For this fixed $x$, every $(A,B,T)$ tuple occurs with the independent uniform law, since replacing $z$ by $A$ is a bijection for each $B,T$. Hence $C(T)=z$ and, conditional on the entire parameterized curve coefficients, the advised point is at a uniform nonzero parameter. This remains valid when the curve intersects itself or $z=x$. The decoder computes its lists from the parameterized curve independently of which nonzero parameter is to serve as the check.

On a given curve write $g_t(b)=g_{C(t)}(b)$ and enumerate all polynomials $f$ of degree at most $d'$ with mean score at least $\epsilon/2$, using the following polynomial-time procedure. At $(t,b)$ prescribe a zero of multiplicity $m_{tb}=\lfloor q\max(0,g_t(b))\rfloor$ for a bivariate interpolant $Q$. The number of homogeneous linear conditions is at most
$$
 \sum_{t,b}\frac{m_{tb}(m_{tb}+1)}2\le\frac{q^3+q^{5/2}}2\le q^3.
$$
This uses the squared-score bound and $\sum_b|g_t(b)|\le\sqrt q$. Multiplicities use the formal coefficients of $Q(t+X,b+Y)$ of total degree below $m_{tb}$, so they work in characteristic five. Put $D=4q\lceil\sqrt{d'q}\rceil$ and allow monomials $X^iY^r$ with $i+d'r\le D$. The rectangle $i\le\lfloor D/2\rfloor$, $r\le\lfloor D/(2d')\rfloor$ alone has more than $q^3$ positions for $d\ge1$. A nonzero solution exists by linear algebra. For every proposed $f$ satisfying the score test,
$$
     \sum_t m_{t,f(t)}\ \ge\ q^2\epsilon/2-q\ >\ D
$$
at the specified parameters. Divisibility by $(X-t)^{m_{t,f(t)}}$ after substituting $Y=f(X)$ follows by expanding at $t$, because $f(t+X)-f(t)$ has zero constant coefficient. The univariate polynomial $Q(X,f(X))$ has degree at most $D$ and vanishes with more than $D$ total multiplicity, so $Y-f(X)$ divides $Q$.

Its polynomial roots can be enumerated without a factoring oracle. At each coefficient, divide out the largest common power of $X$ from the current interpolant. Try every root $c$ of its nonzero constant-in-$X$ polynomial slice, then substitute $Y=c+XY$ and strip common powers of $X$ again. For a root of multiplicity $r$ in the slice, the substituted coefficient of $Y^r$ has $X$-valuation exactly $r$, while terms of $Y^l$ are divisible by $X^l$. Thus the new slice degree is at most $r$. Child degree budgets sum to at most the parent degree, and a constant nonzero slice generates no further children. All field trials and intermediate coefficients for $d'+1$ levels fit polynomial time in $q,d$. Filter completed polynomials by their required received-word score. [Coefficient extraction proof](recovering-longer-outputs.md)

The **filtered** list has at most $8/\epsilon^2$ polynomials, much fewer than the permitted $Y$-degree of the interpolant. With the inner product averaging over $t$ and summing over $b$, define $v_f(t,b)=[f(t)=b]$. Then $\|v_f\|=1$ and $\langle v_f,v_{f'}\rangle\le d'/q$ for distinct candidates. The score vector $g$ on the parameterized curve has norm at most one. For a list of length $L$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $(L\epsilon/2)^2\le\|\sum_fv_f\|^2\le L+L(L-1)d'/q$, which proves the list bound for our field size. Keep the candidates that agree with the advised true symbol at parameter $T$. Return their constant term if exactly one remains, and abstain otherwise. Conditional on the curve, a false candidate matches that check with probability at most $d'/(q-1)$ because the true restriction and that candidate differ as polynomials. Thus **even on a bad curve** the probability that any false candidate passes is at most $8d'/(\epsilon^2(q-1))<1/32$. With probability at least $15/16$ the true candidate was listed and is the unique survivor. Repeating with $O(\log(v+2))$ independent advised tuples, majority decoding succeeds simultaneously at every genuine index for one choice of these tuples, by amplification and a union bound. All enumerations and advice costs are polynomial in $j$ for fixed $A$. The resulting circuit for the true table has size $(S+1)\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$, so the same sound gate check yields the stated bound without identifying favorable stages beforehand.

## A binary prediction at a single scalar in the five-element field

Over a binary extension $K$ use the dot-product challenge $c_i=\langle\lambda,[L_i(p)]\rangle\in\{0,1\}$, so the intended answer is $b=\sum_i c_i h_i\bmod2$. The score $g_p(z)$ is the Walsh correlation with the dot-product word for $z$, with squared scores summing to one by Parseval. Agreement above $1/2+\epsilon$ supplies mean score above $2\epsilon$. With $q$ the least power of two at least the same bound, the curve arguments and anchor vote recover the genuine table.

These Boolean answers are themselves canonical field evaluations, up to the fixed relabeling $b\mapsto2b$. Use the tag-zero formula with one hole. For each genuine bit introduce four slots. The first is its genuine value, the second is its complement, and the last two each equal the first if $c_i=1$ and each equal zero otherwise. As before, add a violation term for each failed local requirement. Append $a=(-v)\bmod4$ extra groups, each of four slots forced to $(1,0,0,0)$. The unique extended hole has width $N=4(v+a)$ and Hamming weight $w=v+a+2\sum_i c_i h_i$. At the common scalar $t=-1\in\mathbb F_5$, one has $1-t=2$, $t/(1-t)=2$, and $2$ has multiplicative order four. The canonical extension of the positive DNF evaluates to
$$
  1-(1-t)^N\bigl(t/(1-t)\bigr)^w=1-2^{N+w}=1-(-1)^b=2b\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_5 .
$$
In particular the two possible raw field answers are exactly $0,2$, independent of the stage. Every new clause violation in the DNF has width at most two and is locally indexable in $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ on the short query. Consequently the same conditional every-late-stage circuit bound holds for predicting the raw value above $1/2+(j+1)^{-A}$ on the uniform binary-extension queries. The comparison concerns succinct descriptions of formulas each with one hole. They all have the same Boolean satisfiability status.

There is an exactly balanced choice of field queries on the same near-native table as the [ordinary checksum SAT instances](sat-on-random-checksums.md). Start with the width-three DNF $\neg C_j$ listing violations of the unpinned near-native 3-CNF. Its unique hole is the genuine complete assignment of $C_j$, and its designated output gives $b_j$. A universal clause challenge verifies any reconstructed table exactly as in the ordinary SAT argument. The width and encoded size of the resulting copied DNF are $M\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$, and each formula word is locally generable in $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$. For the unpinned field query above the raw zero answer has probability $(1+\Pr_p[P(p)=0])/2$, since every nonzero symbol has a balanced inner-product word. To balance the answers, sample an independent public fair bit $\tau$ and append one group of four coordinates forced to $(\tau,\tau,0,0)$. This increases $N$ by four and $w$ by $2\tau$, giving the raw field value $2(b\mathbin{\oplus}\tau)$, equally often zero and two at every stage. Any predictor exceeding the stated advantage on these queries attains that advantage for some fixed choice of $\tau$ at the stage. Store this one bit in the reconstruction and undo the known flip before decoding.

Neither the actual mixed-query distribution nor the fixed-scalar construction provides a fast predictor.
