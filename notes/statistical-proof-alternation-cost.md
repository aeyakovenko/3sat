# Exact alternating cost of a statistical proof for a RAM run

Consider a fixed interactive verifier with one computationally unbounded prover. On a public input of length $n$ the verifier takes at most $v$ native logarithmic-word steps, uses at most $r$ private fair bits and exchanges at most $m$ bits in a fixed number of messages. Write $w=\Theta(\log(n+2))$ for the word width. Use polynomially bounded public caps, computable within the stated running time, with random access to the original input. All interaction, including the verifier's random choices, is clocked even against a cheating prover. Length fields, end markers and padding can be included in the caps. With a fixed number of messages, padding each to the total cap costs at most a constant factor. Unused private bits can also be padded. Let $B=2+r+m$.

**Simulation theorem.** In either of the following cases there is a uniform deterministic alternating word RAM that decides the protocol's promised verdict with a constant number of quantifier blocks, in $O(v+1+B/w)$ steps with $O(B+w)$ choice bits per path.

1. On a positive input some strategy accepts with probability one. On a negative input every strategy accepts with probability strictly less than one. The soundness gap can be as small as a single private random tape.
2. For a fixed positive integer $a$, the optimal acceptance probability is at least $1/2$ on positives and at most $1/2-B^{-a}$ on negatives. In particular this covers constant two-sided error after a fixed small padding of the caps. The construction also works for a different known threshold if the corresponding integer threshold on the number of tapes is available at the stated cost.

The number of blocks and the constants may depend on the number of rounds and, in the second case, on $a$. An exponentially small positive completeness probability is also allowed by the same proof when it exceeds the soundness bound by a known multiplicative factor of at least $1+B^{-a}$ and the corresponding positive integer threshold is available. A verifier message may be any computed function of its private tape and the preceding public messages. Each computation path of the simulator replays the verifier at most once. Verifier work can include arbitrary access to its writable RAM.

## Perfect completeness with private coins

Universally choose each purported verifier message and existentially choose each prover reply, in public transcript order. Choose the entire private random tape universally **after** the last reply. Replay the verifier with this tape and the proposed prover messages. Accept if the actual verifier messages differ from the proposed ones, or if they agree and the verdict is positive. Check the original length and validity conventions when replaying. Use canonical encodings of the available actions at a prover node, so an illegal prover choice cannot create vacuous acceptance. One can give every fixed-width prover string a total interpretation as a legal action, or reject illegal replies on feasible prefixes. Variable termination can be normalized by padding the rest of the game with prescribed messages.

A perfectly complete strategy wins against every final tape on every feasible history. Conversely a winning existential strategy defines one prover function of the public history. For each fixed private tape, run this prover against the actual verifier. The universal player could have selected exactly these verifier messages and then this tape. Hence that ordinary interaction accepts. Finiteness makes probability one equivalent to acceptance on all tapes, and a successful randomized prover strategy may always be fixed to a deterministic strategy. Exposing the tape *before* the replies would give the prover extra information. For example, let the verifier keep one uniform secret bit, send a fixed challenge, and accept precisely when the reply equals the secret bit. Its true value is $1/2$.

In the converse direction replace the universal choices of a bounded-alternation game by uniform public coin choices, treating the existential choices as prover messages. For a fixed quantifier prefix, a true instance retains perfect completeness. On a false instance every prover strategy has a rejecting branch and the probability of following it is at least $2^{-b}$ when at most $b$ random bits are requested along a path. Consecutive choices by one player can be bundled into a bounded string. Fixed per-block caps and stream padding cost at most a constant factor for constantly many blocks. Thus proving that every incorrect claim has even one *detecting* private tape per prover suffices when completeness is perfect. A constant acceptance gap is stronger than this criterion.

## Counting the compatible private tapes

For two-sided error, fix a public history $h$ and let $F(h)$ be the private tapes consistent with the verifier messages in that history given the prover's earlier messages. Let $W(h)$ be the maximum number of those tapes accepted by a single continuation strategy. At a complete transcript, this is the number of tapes on which a deterministic replay agrees with all verifier messages and accepts. At a prover node take the maximum child weight. At a verifier node take the sum of the child weights. Indeed its different visible messages partition $F(h)$, and the prover may choose future replies separately on the different visible histories. This sum uses the **unnormalized** weights. A rare history may correspond to just one tape. Always $0\le W(h)\le 2^r$.

We use Ajtai's first-order approximate counting theorem. For each fixed $d>0$ there is a fixed formula $\varphi(t,S)$ over finite initial segments of the integers with addition, multiplication, order and unary predicate $S$. On a universe of size $2^L$ its value is true if $|S|\ge (1+L^{-d})t$ and false if $|S|\le (1-L^{-d})t$, for positive $t$ in the universe. The relational arithmetic has the ordinary integer interpretation. The bounded-gap case needs only constant relative accuracy. [Ajtai, Theorem 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1090/dimacs/013/01) · [First-order statement, appendix A of Keppeler, Schwentick and Spinrath](https://lmcs.episciences.org/18508/pdf) · [Goldwasser and Sipser on private coins and set size](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/tss/files/papers/goldwasser-Sipser.pdf)

Let $s$ be an upper bound of at least one on the number of verifier-message nodes along a path plus one final leaf count. Put $Q=16s B^a$, $\beta=1+4/Q$. We need a comparison for any candidate set of strings of $O(B)$ bits and $1\le j\le 2^r$ that accepts whenever its cardinality $C\ge j$ and rejects whenever $j>\beta C$. Encode $Q+1$ disjoint copies of this set in one augmented universe and use threshold $Qj$. The copy number is a field of $O(\log B)$ bits. Other elements of the universe fail the membership predicate. Choose its bit width $L=\Theta(B)$ large enough to contain all encodings and this threshold, with $L^{-d}\le 1/(4Q)$ for fixed $d>a$. For $C\ge j$ the ratio of the replicated size to threshold is at least $1+1/Q$. For $j>\beta C$ this ratio is less than $(Q+1)/(Q+4)\le 1-1/(4Q)$. The comparison is total even between these bounds.

Construct a total Boolean predicate $P(h,j)$ with the invariant
\[
 j\le W(h)\Longrightarrow P(h,j),\qquad
 j>\kappa W(h)\Longrightarrow \neg P(h,j).
\]
At a leaf apply the comparison to the set of accepting compatible tapes, with $\kappa=\beta$. At a prover node existentially choose a reply and use its child predicate. The same $\kappa$ works by taking the maximum. At a verifier node apply the comparison to the set of encoded pairs
\[
 (u,i),\qquad 1\le i\le 2^r,\qquad P(hu,i).
\]
Here $u$ represents one canonical possible public message. Every child of weight $z$ contributes at least $z$ and at most $\kappa z$ pairs, independently of how the predicate decides intermediate indices. Consequently the pair set has cardinality in $[W(h),\kappa W(h)]$. One application of the comparison yields the invariant with factor $\beta\kappa$. Impossible histories have weight zero and contribute zero. Replacing the unary predicate inside Ajtai's formula by the child predicate gives an ordinary fixed nested formula, even when it occurs negated.

At the root $\kappa\le (1+4/Q)^s\le \exp(4s/Q)\le 1+8s/Q$. The last inequality uses $4s/Q\le 1/4$. Test $j=\lceil 2^r/2\rceil$. In the positive case $W\ge j$. In the negative case $(1+8s/Q)(1/2-B^{-a})<1/2$, so $\kappa W<j$. This uses the fixed number of rounds to bound the accumulated relative error. It avoids repeating the protocol once per inverse gap.

## Packed arithmetic and the diagonal budget

The model checker follows the fixed formula syntax. A quantifier picks an $O(B)$-bit element in packed words. Boolean conjunction and disjunction can branch to one subformula universally or existentially. Negation dualizes these choices. Quantifier depth, formula size and nesting depth are constants. A branch needs the deterministic verification predicate only upon reaching a selected terminal membership assertion. Copy and transcript prefixes, threshold scaling by $Q$ (a constant number of machine words), comparisons and additions cost $O(1+B/w)$ word steps.

For a multiplication atom $X Y=Z$ with $L$-bit operands, universally choose an integer modulus $d$ from $2$ through $D=4L+8$ and check the equality modulo $d$. The least common multiple of all these moduli exceeds $2^{2L}$. Indeed every $\binom D i$ divides $\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,D)$ because the exponent of a prime $p$ in $\binom D i$ is a sum of at most $\lfloor\log_p D\rfloor$ terms, each zero or one, by the factorial valuation formula. The largest binomial coefficient is at least $2^D/(D+1)>2^{2L}$. Thus congruence for every $d$ forces the integer product equality. Its negation uses an existential modulus. For one chosen $d$ scan the packed operands by Horner reduction in a radix of $\Theta(w)$ bits. The modulus has $O(\log(B+2))$ bits. A fixed word-width coefficient large enough for the public polynomial caps, with ordinary multiply and division, accommodates the products of a residue with one radix digit. A fixed narrower source verifier runs directly on this host with masked source words. For the square-time native diagonal with $B=o(n^2)$ one can use its prescribed width $12\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil$ directly. There $4L+8<n^3$ for large $n$, and $Q$ uses only a fixed number of words even for large fixed $a$. At no point is a $B$-bit integer a unit-cost address or data word. These tests and the original replay prove the word-time and choice-bit bounds. The finite addition and maximum accounting for the continuation weights is checked in [Lean](../lean/TranscriptFiberWeights.lean).

For a $T$-step deterministic RAM computation, a protocol meeting $v+1+B/w=o(T)$ therefore gives an alternating time speedup. The [native unary-clock collapse](alternating-native-speedup.md) also charges the *bits* of the earlier choices. With a constant block count, $v+B=o(T)$ suffices on its diagonal. An explicit SAT slowdown separately pays for formula encoding and the number of blocks. No protocol of the required costs for general RAM histories is constructed here. The [two-word oracle example](two-word-interactive-barrier.md) concerns constant-gap statistical soundness. On an empty oracle row, testing one random index already has perfect completeness and detects a single mark with inverse-row-length probability, in agreement with the weak-gap version above. Fortnow and Lund studied interactive proofs in alternating time and space. Ajtai supplies the counting theorem used here. Historical priority for the packed private-coin accounting has not been established. [Fortnow and Lund](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/ipspace.pdf)
