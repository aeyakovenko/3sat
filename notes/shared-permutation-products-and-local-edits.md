# Three product orders do not force a hard join

A different candidate for a hard join puts a single product test on each order. Every row is a positive permutation of the same $p$ distinct unknown letters in $S_3$, followed by a common free anchor $z$. Its product must be a specified target $g_i$.

For at most **three rows**, equality of targets on repeated orders and equality of the signs of all targets are necessary and sufficient. This test takes linear time in the explicit input. Here is one proof of sufficiency. When there are three distinct orders, two pair comparisons already separate them. Take a pair that distinguishes the first two orders. The third agrees with one of the two on this pair, so one further pair separates those two. Restrict to the at most four letters in those comparisons and set all others to the identity. Three distinct orders require at least three retained letters. Rename them so the first retained row is the identity order. On three and four letters the finite lemma below exhausts the other two orders and realizes all nine possible pairs of even product differences $P_iP_1^{-1}$. Choose $z=P_1^{-1}g_1$. For two distinct orders, put noncommuting transpositions on one inverted pair to realize a nonidentity rotation difference in either direction, or use all identities for equal targets. The anchor again chooses the baseline. A single order is immediate.

Four rows already escape that criterion. On the letters $ABC$ take the orders $ABC,ACB,BCA,CBA$. Relative to the first order the other three products give a triple $(x,y,z')$ in $A_3$. In additive coordinates over $\mathbb F_3$ their exact constraint is $x=0$ if and only if $y=z'$. In particular every target can be even and the four equations can still be inconsistent. The following sparse construction uses precisely this constraint. Results about fixed numbers of general group equations already give polynomial-time algorithms for $S_3$. This argument concerns explicit shared positive permutations and the size of witnesses. [Nuspl, *Solving a fixed number of equations over finite groups*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00012-020-00701-6) · [Lean finite exhaustion and projection lemmas](../lean/ThreePermutationProducts.lean)

## A sharp interaction threshold for sparse permutation edits

Here is the input convention. A base is a sequence of disjoint consecutive blocks, each of two or three distinct variable names. Every variable occurs in exactly one block. Append a fresh anchor to the base and put its required product at the identity. Each further row specifies a target in the fixed group and lists a bounded number of distinct block indices together with their local permutations. Unlisted blocks retain the base order, and the same anchor always comes last. Thus every expanded row is a permutation of the same named letters, even though the file stores edits once. This is a sparse representation in its own right. Explicitly printing the full word at every row can increase the length quadratically on the reductions below.

Write $P_j$ for the base product of block $j$ and $L_j$ for the product of all preceding base blocks. For a replacement with product $P'_j$, its effective signal is $L_j(P'_jP_j^{-1})L_j^{-1}$ in $A_3$. The ratio of a modified row to the base product equals the product of its effective signals. Expand the replacement products before the anchor, inserting and canceling the base prefixes. Identify $A_3$ with additive $\mathbb F_3$. Every block allows a fixed relation on the signals of all its modes. The choices from different blocks are independent: conjugating the assignment to all letters of one block by $L_j^{-1}$ converts any desired tuple of local signals to one having that tuple of *effective* signals. Process blocks left to right so $L_j$ is already known. The final anchor enforces the base equation.

With at most one edited block in a row, reject odd targets, false unedited checks, and contradictory repeated requirements. All remaining rows impose requirements on individual modes of a single block. Enumerate at most $6^3$ assignments per block and accept iff every block admits one tuple meeting its requirements. This takes $O(N)$ native RAM steps for $N$ bits, including parsing names and indices. Equality of duplicate rows or repeated edits is unnecessary once the check for each block and mode is maintained.

Two edited blocks suffice for all the SAT clock milestones. Represent each graph vertex $v$ by a two-letter block $(U_v,V_v)$ whose swap has some effective signal $a_v\in\mathbb F_3$. Every signal can occur, by choosing equal or distinct transpositions. Represent each edge $uv$ by its own three-letter block $ABC$. The alternate orders $ACB,BCA,CBA$ have effective signals $x,y,z$ relative to $ABC$. Exactly these triples occur:
$$
                         x=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y=z .
$$
To check this, set $U=BC,V=CB$. The local differences satisfy $\delta=VU^{-1}$, $x=A\delta A^{-1}$, $y=[U,A]$ and $z=\delta y$ in multiplicative notation. For any requested $y$ and $\delta$ set $U$ to a transposition $t$ and $V=\delta t$ to another transposition. If $\delta\ne1$, choose the parity of $A$ to conjugate $\delta$ to the prescribed nonidentity $x$. If $\delta=1$ either parity suffices. Within either parity coset $[t,A]$ assumes every value in $A_3$. Indeed $[t,A]=t(AtA^{-1})$, and both parity cosets realize every transposition $AtA^{-1}$ since the centralizer of $t$ contains the odd element $t$. Choose $B$ with $B^{-1}UB=V$ and set $C=B^{-1}U$. This realizes all 15 triples, and conjugation by an earlier prefix preserves the displayed relation.

For this edge place a row requiring $x=1$ (the nonzero field element), a two-block row requiring $y+a_u=0$, and a two-block row requiring $z+a_v=0$. This enforces $a_u\ne a_v$ and allows all such pairs. The whole input is feasible exactly for graphs admitting a proper three-coloring. It uses one two-letter block per vertex and one three-letter block and three rows per edge. Both conversions to the explicit SAT clocks preserve physical bit length. In the forward direction the journal's 3-SAT-to-color reduction gives $m=O(N/\log(N+2))$ graph items, so printing all block indices and edit records costs $O(N)$ bits and steps. In the reverse direction guess one of the constantly many realizable signal tuples at each used block and check the sum in each row by a fixed Boolean circuit. For linear physical output on arbitrary badly padded or duplicate-heavy inputs, intern block names and coalesce duplicate row records (retaining targets). The $q$ distinct rows and $b$ explicitly declared named blocks obey $q\log(q+2)+b\log(b+2)=O(N)$ by finite-alphabet counting. Equivalently, use the fixed bit encoding with index width at least $\lceil\log_2(b+1)\rceil$ plus one target field per row, coalesce the constant rows, and use the bound above after coalescing. A linear-size constraint or truth-table circuit with dense names then costs $O(N)$ bits to render. Read model/encoding details for the prior graph reduction in [the S3 occurrence proof](branching-in-equations-over-six-permutations.md). This equivalence does not imply any lower bound for unrestricted SAT.

The Lean proof exhausts the relative products on three and four letters using native evaluation, checks the four-mode equivalence, and proves that setting omitted letters to the identity preserves a restricted product. The passage from two pair comparisons to a choice of at most four letters and the two-edit encoding are manuscript arguments.
