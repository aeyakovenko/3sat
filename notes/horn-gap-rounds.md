# Can gap amplification shorten a local refutation of a missing output?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Suppose a succinct CNF checks a bounded computation. Gap amplification can make every assignment to a false instance violate many clauses. A way to exploit the gap would be to show that it forces a *shallow reason for rejection* on the actual false outputs. The following calculation works if, after branching, enough of the violation must occur in clauses that are simultaneously Horn under some choice of variable signs. Both the corrupted clauses and all sign choices can be unknown to the checker.

## A quantitative bound on parallel unit derivations

Give the clauses nonnegative weights summing to one. Count an empty clause as Horn, and discard tautologies. A partial assignment pins some variables. Keep the original weights on the unsatisfied residual clauses, so a satisfied clause contributes zero violated mass. Suppose every assignment extending the pins violates weight at least ε. Fix any renaming of the unpinned variables, and put aside a set of residual clauses of mass δ such that the rest are Horn. Let W denote the total residual Horn weight retained, so W ≤ 1−δ. If ε>δ, the residual CNF has a signed unit refutation of parallel depth at most

$$
\left\lfloor\frac{W}{\epsilon-\delta}\right\rfloor .
$$

Write a Horn clause as a rule whose conclusion is a positive atom or a distinguished contradiction. Its premises are the atoms appearing negatively. Initially every renamed atom is false. At each step add every head whose premises are already true. Before deriving contradiction, the current set of true ordinary atoms describes a total assignment. At least ε−δ mass of retained rules have all premises true and the head false on this assignment. Each such rule receives its head at this step, so it can never be charged again. A fixed point without contradiction would have zero retained violations. Through and including the first step that derives contradiction, each step spends at least ε−δ out of the same W. Recording a supporting clause for each added head gives a proof at the stated depth. Undo the sign flips in that proof. Every inference is a sound unit inference on an actual residual clause, which can also be checked in the full CNF under its pins. No orientation or exceptional-clause list is needed as proof data. [Lean proof of the forward-step potential and cumulative charge for integer weights](../lean/HornGapRounds.lean)

The bound is exact for directed Horn inference on the uniformly weighted chain consisting of a positive unit, successive unary implications and a forbidden final atom. Each clause alone can be falsified and all others satisfied. With m clauses the gap is 1/m and the forward proof takes m steps. Bidirectional signed propagation can traverse from both ends and finish earlier. A Fibonacci variant explains the cost of explicitly unfolding a *directed* proof. Take facts for atoms 0 and 1, the unique rule from atoms i−1 and i−2 to i for each i=2,…,t, and forbid atom t. Every clause is necessary, so the gap is exactly 1/(t+2) with uniform weights. A forward proof tree of t has at least Fib(t+1) fact occurrences by its recurrence. The shared derivation has only t+2 records. This example bounds the cost of the specified proof representation.

Strong backdoors and backdoor trees already let different branches reach tractable classes. The observation here is the weighted depth bound in the presence of arbitrary residual clauses and arbitrary leaf-dependent renamings. Priority for that combination has not been established. [Dreier, Ordyniak and Szeider](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08326) [Gaspers et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.05725)

## Testing the property on the actual first missing word

Use the native machine of the journal with clock B=2^(20n) and n-bit first missing output v. Fix a uniform bounded-width CNF compiler for the statement that program p prints z by B. Clause and variable indices have poly(n) bits, and any clause can be generated in poly(n) time. Assume perfect completeness for timely outputs. A concrete compiler meeting this condition can be obtained by a local computation tableau of exponential size; the gap and residual structure below are additional requirements. [Native first missing word](native-missing-word.md)

Consider the following test for a short local *refutation*. Guess b distinct variables, universally choose their values, then ask whether the residual has a signed unit refutation of depth h. Allow padding to certify smaller b and h. A signed step deriving literal ℓ must exhibit a clause containing ℓ and derive the complement of every other literal; the root may derive contradiction by deriving complements of all literals of a clause. Pins may instead be used as initial facts. This convention also covers a clash of opposite units, by using the clause that derived one as the root and the proof of its opposite as a premise. Each clause may be used as it stands on the original input with pin facts at depth zero. A satisfiable instance fails the test since the universal block can match a satisfying assignment, and unit inference preserves truth under that assignment. If a b-variable backdoor exists such that *every* assignment to it meets the weighted lemma with bound h, the test succeeds. In particular, the sign choices in that hypothesis may depend on all b assigned bits and have unbounded description length.

Here are explicit parameter scales. Fix 0<σ<20, take b=2^floor(σn), and let h=floor(c n²/ceil(log₂(n+2))) for a sufficiently small fixed positive rational c. Use a fixed constant K≥2 bounding the clause width. In the signed-proof game, guess all rule identifiers for the next s levels of a tree and universally challenge one of the K^s frontier slots. Check every rule in the block against its parent's demands and verify terminal leaves against facts. Unused children are marked terminal and cannot cover a required premise. A challenge at a verified leaf terminates successfully. Recursively supply support for the chosen nonterminal frontier demand. Soundness and completeness follow by induction and finite choice over the disjoint subtrees. The guess for one block has O(K^s poly(n,h)) bits. Checking membership in the pins and rejecting inconsistent pins or duplicate variables can be done by one sorting network on the concatenation of all pin and requested identifiers, with tagged entries. This avoids a product of the pin count and the subtree size. Equivalently allow unused slots in the pin list, require distinct identifiers only on used slots, and treat the universal values of unused slots as immaterial. All occurrences can be batched at each level, including the final recursion level.

Choose s=max(1,floor(σn/log₂ K)). The initial quantified circuit has 2^(σn+o(n)) encoded bits and O(1+h/s) blocks. The uniform linear-SAT elimination estimate adds O(log n) to the log size at every block, as long as the log size is O(n). The latter follows by induction for fixed σ and small c since the sum of increments is O(cn/σ)+o(n), with a constant depending on the chosen compiler, K and SAT program. With c sufficiently small the whole test costs 2^(αn) for some α<20. A fixed number of further program, output and prefix quantifiers keeps slack below 20. A native program can run the resulting prefix search with polynomial overhead in n per RAM operation. [Uniform block elimination](linear-quantifiers.md) [Native interpretation and prefix search](native-missing-word.md)

Consequently, **if linear SAT exists**, for every sufficiently large n there is a single name p of length less than n at v such that for *every* choice of at most b pinned variables there is an assignment to them whose residual has **no** signed unit refutation of depth h. Otherwise the prefix search for the first z for which *every* short program passes the refutation test would print v before B. Every z<v has a timely producer with a satisfiable encoding and cannot be returned first. The search can ask whether an entire prefix interval contains any z that passes this universal test, without assuming the outcome is monotone in z.

If the encoding at this particular p,v has global violation gap at least ε, every surviving residual consequently leaves substantial non-Horn weight under *every* renaming. Indeed the depth lemma gives h < (1−δ)/(ε−δ) whenever δ<ε on such a residual, and hence δ > (h ε−1)/(h−1) for h>1. For δ≥ε this last inequality also holds if ε<1. The missing ingredient for a lower bound is a compiler with enough gap **and** a small strong backdoor on all the short programs at v, for infinitely many n. A gap by itself cannot do this. For example the four two-variable clauses with all four sign patterns have gap 1/4 and have no unit with which to start.

## Attacking black-box gap amplification

There is a separate obstacle to obtaining the gap through entirely local access to a long raw input. Let F_x be an implicitly indexed constraint family for a Boolean function f. Assume a fixed variable universe and index set, satisfiable on every YES input. Each constraint at an arbitrary fixed NO input x is generated by probing at most q input bits, adaptively. Put any probability distribution on clause indices at this x. For each YES neighbor y choose a satisfying assignment of F_y and set D_y={i : x_i ≠ y_i}. Any constraint violated by that assignment in F_x must have probed D_y during generation on x. Otherwise the same generator would give the same clause on y. Thus when the NO gap at x is ε, the distribution of probes on x hits *every* such D_y with probability at least ε.

More generally, assign the YES inputs nonnegative coefficients λ_y with sum over y differing at position i at most one, for each i. Sum the hitting inequalities with these weights. A single clause's at most q queried positions can hit YES neighbors of total weight at most q. Hence ε Σ_y λ_y ≤ q. On even-versus-odd parity of N raw bits, a NO input has N single-flip YES neighbors so ε ≤ q/N. This holds even if the index distribution depends on x and the proof can have arbitrary length. Standard proximity constructions avoid this demand by referring soundness to the distance from a YES input. The native first-hole instance has a short explicit description; applying the bound to long unverified trace bits would require justifying their role as independently changeable external data. [Ben-Sasson et al., robust PCPs of proximity](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/2004/bghsv-full.pdf)

## A stronger test using q-Horn clauses and short contradictory implication paths

In each residual let an arbitrary sign renaming partition variables into H (Horn) and Y (binary). In the retained clauses negative H literals may occur freely. Every retained clause either has one positive H conclusion and no Y literals, or has no positive H literal and at most two Y literals. This is the standard q-Horn class. Equivalently assign costs 0 and 1 to the opposite signs in H and cost 1/2 to both signs in Y, with total cost at most one per retained clause. Put weight δ of arbitrary exceptions aside. Write η=ε−δ>0. [Gaspers et al., definition of q-Horn and backdoors](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2013.67)

At a forward snapshot S on H, collect the second type of clauses for which all negative H literals have been falsified by S. What remains forms a weighted, activated 2CNF on Y. Let its minimum violation weight be β(S), measured in the original normalization. Realize that minimum by a Y assignment and extend S with it. If β(S)<η/2, the H-head rules currently violated must have mass greater than η/2. As above their heads all enter at this step, spending that mass permanently. Consequently by step floor(2/η) some activated binary system has minimum violation weight at least η/2. Every literal used to remove a guard from one of its defining clauses then has a signed unit proof of depth at most floor(2/η).

The binary contradictions can themselves be kept short. Suppose a weighted 2CNF on M≥1 variables has total weight ≤1 and minimum violated weight γ>0. An empty clause suffices as a contradiction. Otherwise there is a literal a with implication paths from a to its complement and back, each of length O(log(M+2)/γ). Here is a direct rounding argument with weights of arbitrary precision. Model a unit as a doubled literal, and equip each of the two implication arcs of a clause with its full weight. The sum of incoming plus outgoing weighted degrees of all literal vertices is at most four.

Assume no such pair of paths both has length ≤2t. This remains true in every induced residual graph. For any unassigned variable, choose its polarity a whose directed distance to its own complement exceeds 2t. Its outward ball of radius t contains no opposite pair. Otherwise paths from a to y and to its complement, together with reversal and complementation of the second path, would reach the complement of a in ≤2t steps. Give each ball volume 1/M plus the sum of the incident weighted degrees of its vertices. The ratio of final to initial volume is at most 1+4M, so for some 0≤i<t the volume increase at radius i+1 is at most θ times the volume at i, where θ=(1+4M)^(1/t)−1. This increase covers all arc weight leaving the ball of radius i, since every new arc endpoint lies in the next layer. Set all literals of this ball true and their opposites false, then remove those variables. A clause eventually falsified can be charged as soon as its first variable is removed. Its false literal's complement was in this true ball, and the implication toward its other false literal left the ball. The same applies to a false unit. Thus its weight is charged to this step's outgoing cut. Across all steps the balls have disjoint vertex sets, giving total degree at most four, and their additive volume terms total at most one. The resulting assignment violates weight ≤5θ. Taking t large enough that 5θ<γ proves the claim. The usual implication-graph characterization supplies soundness of each paired path. [Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(79)90002-4)

The succinct checker can guess the two paths explicitly, together with the clause defining each edge and the chosen set of guarded literals to remove. It checks adjacency, opposite endpoints, and the residual one- or two-literal forms. It then universally challenges any claimed guard deletion and demands a depth-h signed unit proof of the complement of the deleted literal. Guessed empty contradictions have the same guard check. Every edge established in this way is a genuine binary consequence of the residual formula, even when different edges use incompatible proposed structural partitions. A model would have to respect both paths, which is impossible. Thus the checker never encodes a candidate global q-Horn partition, its orientation, or the dropped mass.

If η≥2/h, the forward lemma gives depth ≤h and the paired paths have length O(h log(M+2)). With polynomial-bit variable names, the entire path-and-guard list has polynomial length in n for the h used above. Its existential block and the universal guard challenge preserve the clock calculation. Applying the prefix search with this stronger test gives, for each sufficiently large n under linear SAT, one short program at v for which **every** set of ≤b pins has a branch that admits no such guarded binary contradiction. In particular, if this program's encoding has gap ε, every deletion of clauses that leaves a q-Horn subformula on that branch must remove weight greater than ε−2/h (using a public polynomial path cap large enough for the displayed constants). This sharpens the structural requirement on a possible lower-bound construction to a lower bound on the violation of an actual q-Horn subset after branching. Proving that property for a uniform computation compiler is open. The Lean file formalizes the forward charging bound and the resulting terminal-gap dichotomy. The binary path rounding and diagonal application are mathematical proofs in this note.

## A constant-gap family that defeats even linear-size branching up to a constant

Every unsatisfiable width-K q-Horn formula has a resolution refutation of width at most max(K,2). Forward unit resolution derives the forced H heads, unit resolution on the guards gives the activated binary clauses, and resolution along both implication paths yields opposite units and the empty clause. Clause weights, proof length and propagation depth do not affect this width statement. The same conclusion holds if the residual simply *contains* an unsatisfiable q-Horn subformula.

Suppose a variable decision tree of depth b reaches such a residual on every branch. Put D equal to the disjunction of the negations of a branch's fixed literals. Lift the leaf refutation by adjoining D to all its clauses. A lifted initial clause is a weakening of the original version of its residual clause, and all resolved pivots at this leaf are unpinned. The result derives D in width at most b+max(K,2), allowing weakening. Resolve the clauses for opposite children on their decision variable to lift the entire tree. Hence resolution width greater than b+max(K,2) guarantees that **some leaf contains no unsatisfiable q-Horn subformula at all**, for every choice of decision tree and even if different branches choose their partitions independently.

Here this obstruction coexists with a genuine constant violation gap. Take m=12M independent 3-XOR equations on M variables with independent fair right sides. Sample three independent positions per equation and condition on the positions being distinct within each row, an event of probability bounded below by a positive constant as M grows. For each fixed assignment the number of failed equations is binomial with mean m/2. The probability of ≤m/4 failures is at most 2^(-m(1−H₂(1/4))), where H₂ is binary entropy. The union bound over all assignments tends to zero.

With probability tending to one, every s≤ρM equations involve more than 7s/4 distinct variables, for a sufficiently small fixed ρ>0. Indeed for j=floor(7s/4) the probability of a bad set of size s before conditioning is at most

$$
{12M\choose s}{M\choose j}(j/M)^{3s}
\le [C(s/M)^{1/4}]^s.
$$

Use the usual binomial upper bound and j≤7s/4. For 1≤s≤√M the sum is o(1) by geometric domination, and for larger s≤ρM choose ρ with Cρ^(1/4)<1/2. Both high-probability events survive the within-row conditioning. The expansion gives more than s/2 variables appearing exactly once in each such subsystem, by counting its 3s positions. It also gives linear independence of every ≤ρM rows since every nonempty subset of them has a variable occurring once.

Replace each equation by the four clauses forbidding its four failing assignments. Every assignment now violates at least a 1/16 fraction of the 3CNF clauses, and the formula requires resolution width Ω(M). For the latter statement, let μ(C) be the minimum number of original equations logically implying a clause C. Initial clauses have μ≤1, weakening never increases μ, and a resolvent has μ at most the sum for its parents. The empty clause has μ>floor(ρM) by linear independence. Trace backward to a clause with μ between floor(ρM)/2 and floor(ρM), or take a parent of the first line exceeding floor(ρM). For a minimum implying subsystem, every variable appearing exactly once must occur in C. Otherwise a counterassignment to C satisfying all equations except the unique incident equation can be changed on just that variable to satisfy the omitted equation, contradicting implication. Thus the width of C exceeds μ(C)/2. This is the standard expansion argument for resolution width. [Atserias and Dawar, locally consistent, far-from-satisfiable 3XOR](https://doi.org/10.1093/logcom/exz022) [Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, resolution width](https://doi.org/10.1145/375827.375835)

For the resulting formulas, **every adaptive tree of depth at most cM** has a branch on which every q-Horn subformula is satisfiable, for some absolute c>0. In particular no leafwise choice that covers all branches can retain a q-Horn subformula after dropping less than the original gap in clause weight. Gaussian elimination still decides the underlying parity system in polynomial time. The construction rules out a deduction from width three and constant gap alone; applying the positive q-Horn criterion to an actual program still requires structural information about its compiler.
