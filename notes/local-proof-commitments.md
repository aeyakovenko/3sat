# Exact costs for commitments before local proof checks

A short commitment can change how many PCP tests suffice to refute a proof after the verifier sees that commitment. The following minimax calculation distinguishes three kinds of commitment. It applies to a proposed compression of the [canonical SAT table](canonical-nonzero-cuts.md) by retaining local proof checks. Its conclusions concern the selected checks, even if arbitrary subsequent computation is free. The canonical bit protocol of Chen, Li and Liang computes the whole string before taking a bit. A PCP for that computation permits local access to a longer proof. The PCP soundness guarantee quantifies over proofs fixed before the verifier samples its coins. [Chen, Li and Liang, proof of Claim 4.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/182/revision/1/download/) · [Ben-Sasson, Goldreich, Harsha, Sudan and Vadhan](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/polylogPCP.pdf)

## A minimax characterization

Fix an unsatisfiable finite constraint system on a nonempty finite assignment space. Let $D$ be the minimum number of its constraints with no common satisfying assignment. Before the tests, the prover commits to $c=C(\pi)$ for some putative global assignment $\pi$. An admissible commitment value lies in $\mathrm{im}(C)$. For each $c$, let $s(c)$ be the minimum size of a set of tests with no satisfying assignment in that fiber. We minimize $\max_c s(c)$ over the allowed commitment maps. The checker has complete knowledge of the constraint system, including a minimum conflict, and can choose tests after seeing $c$. No computational efficiency is assumed for either party.

* For arbitrary commitments with at most $h\ge1$ possible values the optimum is exactly $\lceil D/h\rceil$. If every fiber has a blocker of at most $q$ constraints, the union of the blockers is a conflict of size at most $hq$. For the upper bound partition a minimum conflict into at most $h$ blocks of size at most $\lceil D/h\rceil$. The commitment names the first block containing a test violated by $\pi$. That block rules out its entire fiber.
* For an inconsistent affine system $A\pi=b$ over $\mathbb F_2$ and linear commitments $C(\pi)=B\pi$ of rank at most $p$, the optimum is exactly $\lceil D/(p+1)\rceil$. Choose a full-row-rank basis for $B$ and write $p'\le p$ for its rank. A chosen test set of size at most $q$ is inconsistent at $c$ precisely if a vector $z$ supported on that set and some $u\in\mathbb F_2^{p'}$ satisfy $A^Tz=B^Tu$ and $u\cdot c\ne z\cdot b$. This is the elementary consistency criterion for the combined linear equations. To survive every such test, $c$ must satisfy the system $u\cdot c=z\cdot b$ of all these implications with $|z|\le q$. If inconsistent, at most $p'+1$ implications sum to $0=1$, by taking a basis of their left-hand sides and one inconsistent row. Summing the associated vectors yields $A^Tz_*=0$, $b\cdot z_*=1$, $|z_*|\le(p'+1)q$. These source equations are unsatisfiable, so $D\le(p+1)q$. If the implications are consistent the surviving $c$ is admissible because $B$ has full row rank.

  For the matching upper bound, a minimum inconsistent subsystem consists precisely of the support of one parity contradiction. Partition this support into at most $p+1$ nonempty blocks of size at most $\lceil D/(p+1)\rceil$. Commit to the sums of the left-hand sides of all blocks except the last, evaluated at $\pi$. A mismatch with a block's right-hand-side sum rules out that block. When every committed sum matches, the last block has a forced mismatch because the total equation is $0=1$.

These upper bounds permit different blocked sets at different commitment values. A uniform challenge independent of the commitment need not choose the appropriate block. A nonlinear commitment here assumes membership in its prescribed fiber is enforceable. Implementing the first-violated-block map can itself require global verification.

## Linear many disclosed proof coordinates can still leave all small tests feasible

There are constants $\delta,\epsilon>0$ and, for each sufficiently large $n$, an $8n$-by-$n$ binary matrix $A$ with exactly three distinct ones per row such that $|A^T z|\ge\epsilon |z|$ for $0<|z|\le\delta n$. For this same $A$ there is $b$ such that every proof fails at least one quarter of the checks. The constants can be small. Stronger random-XOR proof-system lower bounds are known. [Schoenebeck](https://schoeneb.people.si.umich.edu/papers/Lasserre.html)

Here is a direct count sufficient for these parameters. Take $8n$ independent uniformly random triples. If $s$ rows have fewer than $s/2$ odd-degree neighbors, their union has at most $u=\lfloor 7s/4\rfloor$ neighbors by counting incidences. The probability of some such choice is bounded by $\binom{8n}s\binom nu(u/n)^{3s}$ when $u\le n$. With $\gamma=7/4$ this is at most $[K(s/n)^{2-\gamma}]^s$, $K=8e\,e^\gamma\gamma^{3-\gamma}$. Use monotonicity of $(en/u)^u$ up to $n$ to replace $u$ by $\gamma s$. Choose $\delta$ with $K\delta^{1/4}<1/2$ and $\gamma\delta<1$. The sum to $\sqrt n$ tends to zero by comparison to a geometric series with ratio $K n^{-1/8}$. The remaining sum through $\delta n$ is bounded by the tail of $\sum 2^{-s}$. This gives $\epsilon=1/2$. For a fixed $A$ and independent uniform $b$, the probability that any of the $2^n$ assignments satisfies more than three quarters of the $8n$ checks is at most $2^n e^{-n}$ by Hoeffding's inequality.

Fix any set $L$ of $a$ proof coordinates to disclose before testing. For any $q$ with $t=\max(q,\lceil a/\epsilon\rceil)$ and $2t\le\delta n$, there is one assignment on $L$ which extends to a satisfying proof for **every** choice of $q$ checks, with the extension permitted to depend on those checks. In fact this holds for every $b$. To see this, let $W$ consist of the vectors $z$ of weight at most $t$ such that $A^T z$ is supported in $L$. For two members their sum has weight at most $2t$, and its image is still supported in $L$. Expansion bounds its weight by $a/\epsilon\le t$, proving closure under addition. The map $A^T$ is injective on $W$ by expansion. Consequently $A^T z\mapsto b\cdot z$ is a well-defined linear functional on the image in $\mathbb F_2^L$. Extend it to the entire coordinate space and take its representing vector for the commitment on $L$. A failed extension on any $q$ checks would give a conflicting implied equation supported on $L$, derived using at most $q\le t$ rows. Its combination lies in $W$ and its forced parity agrees, a contradiction. Any fixed function of the named coordinates can likewise be precommitted by using the value on this same assignment.

The same result holds if the $a$ coordinates are queried adaptively before the test set is fixed. For every support $L'$ of size at most $a$, define the affine space of allowed replies by the $W$ construction with the **same** value of $t$. When $L\subseteq L'$, the implied forms on $L'$ supported on $L$ are precisely those from $W$ on $L$, by the definitions. Thus the projection of the larger affine space onto $L$ equals the smaller space, by linear duality. Choose arbitrary feasible answers sequentially. The resulting transcript admits a continuation for every $q$-test set. Here each complete sampled challenge names a set of nonadaptive checks. If the checks themselves are chosen after intermediate proof replies, the same projection argument on the union of all queried coordinates applies when $2\max(q,\lceil(a+3q)/\epsilon\rceil)\le\delta n$. Maintain a feasible assignment on the growing set of coordinates. Each fully disclosed tested row then has the correct parity since its singleton derivation belongs to $W$.

## A sharp tradeoff for parity commitments of bounded support

On the preceding gap-3XOR family, let $p_*(w,q)$ be the minimum number of precommitted parity bits, each depending on at most $w$ proof coordinates, such that **every** attainable value can be refuted by some $q$ original checks. For a sufficiently small absolute $c>0$, uniformly over $1\le q\le cn$ and $1\le w\le n$,
$$
 p_*(w,q)=\Theta\!\left(\frac{n}{\min(w,q)}\right).
$$
Expansion gives $D>\lfloor\delta n\rfloor$ since every contradiction has zero left-hand-side sum. The dense linear minimax identity forces $pq=\Omega(n)$ for $q\le cn$. The union of all positions underlying the $p$ committed parities has size at most $pw$. The coordinate extension result therefore forces $pw=\Omega(n)$ as well. For the upper bound, every minimum parity contradiction in $n$ variables has at most $n+1$ rows by linear elimination. If $w\ge3$, group its rows into blocks of size at most $s=\min(q,\lfloor w/3\rfloor)$, with all except possibly one of size $s$. Each block parity uses at most $3s\le w$ proof coordinates and each block has at most $q$ checks. Committing to all but the final block parity uses $O(n/\min(q,w))$ bits. For $w=1,2$ disclose the $n$ individual coordinates and choose any violated check. The upper bounds incorporate unlimited preprocessing of the original system.

Choosing each parity support after seeing earlier commitment values changes the tradeoff. With unconstrained support, a binary adaptive parity transcript of depth $p$ can achieve worst blocker size exactly $\lceil D/2^p\rceil$. Its at most $2^p$ transcript fibers give the lower bound for arbitrary commitments. For the upper bound, start with the minimum parity conflict, whose left-hand-side sum disagrees with its required sum on every assignment. Split the current mismatching group into two parts whose sizes differ by at most one and ask for the parity of the left-hand sides in the first half. Its answer determines a mismatching half in which to continue. The mismatch remains enforced on the entire transcript fiber. After at most $p$ answers the remaining block has size at most $\lceil D/2^p\rceil$.

With supports bounded by $w$, the minimum adaptive parity depth on the gap-3XOR family is
$$
  \Theta\!\left(\frac{n}{w}+\log_2(n/q)\right)
  \qquad(1\le q\le cn,\ 1\le w\le n).
$$
For the lower bound, a depth-$p$ strategy can be simulated by adaptively exposing at most $pw$ individual coordinates, so $pw=\Omega(n)$. The number of transcript fibers gives $2^p q\ge D$, hence $p=\Omega(\log(n/q))$ upon choosing $c$ sufficiently small as a function of $\delta$. For $w\ge3$, first split a minimum conflict into $O(n/w)$ blocks of at most $\lfloor w/3\rfloor$ rows. Their parities locate one mismatching block. Bisection then takes $O(1+\max(0,\log(w/q)))$ further queries, each still supported on at most $w$ coordinates. For $w=1,2$ direct coordinate disclosure suffices. The same adaptive asymptotic lower bound allows arbitrary Boolean functions of at most $w$ coordinates as commitment bits, and the upper bound already uses parities. This is an interactive selection rule for one refuting block.

## The number of adaptive rounds

For the odd-support element-extraction problem, Chakrabarti and Stoeckl already prove the tight deterministic round tradeoff, including the product-of-batch-factors argument. Their problem is the case of locating a single violated bit in an odd syndrome. The formulation here tracks the shortest refutation on each affine fiber, permits lists of tests, and accounts for the support width of measurements on the original proof. Priority of this formulation is unverified. [Chakrabarti and Stoeckl, Theorem 3.6 and its proof](https://mstoeckl.com/notes/papers/elemx.pdf)

More generally prescribe $r$ parity batches of sizes $p_1,\ldots,p_r$. Within a batch all supports are chosen before any answer from that batch is received. Later batches may depend on earlier answers. On **any inconsistent affine system** the optimum worst blocker size for unrestricted parity supports is exactly
$$
 \left\lceil D\Big/\prod_{i=1}^r(p_i+1)\right\rceil .
$$
For the lower bound restrict the assignment space to the affine fiber of all answers so far and parameterize it by independent binary variables. The original tests remain affine there. A commitment batch becomes a linear sketch plus known offsets. At this node the one-round minimax lower bound gives an attainable answer whose fiber has minimum conflict size at least the current size divided by $p_i+1$. Iterating and taking the integer ceiling proves the bound. For the upper bound take the mismatching group on the current transcript fiber and divide it into at most $p_i+1$ nonempty blocks of sizes differing by at most one. The parities of all but the final block identify one mismatching block. Recurse on that block. Nested positive-integer ceilings give the displayed value. The no-probe case in a batch simply passes the same fiber to the next round.

For the gap family and support cap $w$, let $G_r(D,q)$ be the smallest $\sum_i p_i$ with $p_i\ge0$ integers and $\prod_i(p_i+1)\ge D/q$. With at most $r\ge1$ rounds, the optimal total parity count is
$$
 \Theta\!\left(n/w+G_r(D,q)\right), \qquad 1\le q\le cn.
$$
The preceding lower bounds give both summands. They even allow the batch sizes to vary with the answers under a worst-case total budget $P$. Take the branch on which each successive minimum conflict decreases by a factor of at most $p_i+1$. On that realized branch success requires $\prod_i(p_i+1)\ge D/q$, so $P\ge G_r(D,q)$. For $w\ge3$, start with an optimal list of batch sizes and enlarge the number of first-round groups if necessary to at least $\lceil D/\lfloor w/3\rfloor\rceil$. The extra cost is $O(n/w)$, and every group and later subgroup uses at most $w$ proof coordinates. The original product budget still suffices. For $w=1,2$ disclose every coordinate in one batch. With one round, $G_1=\lceil D/q\rceil-1$; with as many rounds as desired, $G_r=\lceil\log_2\lceil D/q\rceil\rceil$ as soon as $r$ reaches that value.

## Effect on a canonical bit query

Fix a good outer tape for a canonical bit predicate and a local proof verifier with perfect completeness and soundness error $s<1$. At a given scale append the fixed affine system above as an alternative proof mode. Every test first reads one common mode bit. In the ordinary mode it runs the designated local verifier. In the other mode it checks one uniform XOR row on a separate proof. A true bit still admits a proof accepted on every test. On a false bit every fixed proof is accepted with probability at most $\max(s,3/4)$. The added data can be supplied nonuniformly as a public matrix and right-hand side independent of the address. For uniform public preprocessing they can instead be independently sampled explicitly and the argument conditioned on selecting a matrix and right-hand side with the displayed properties. This modification is a statement about a verifier presentation at fixed good outer coins.

Take $n$ comparable to the $V$ bit addresses. Any procedure that exposes $o(V)$ private proof coordinates beforehand and retains $o(V)$ original local checks still has a common extension in the XOR mode on every address. This holds even with unlimited computation and with the checks selected using the disclosed coordinates. Ordinary public-seed subsampling corresponds to zero disclosures. The same obstruction applies to a replacement test logically implied by the retained tests when the union of their supports contains only the allowed number of source checks. A global Gaussian elimination on the XOR data can remove the entire auxiliary branch. Thus this gives a sharp limitation for black-box commitments and local-test selection. A specialized compilation of the original canonical verifier to a short ordinary SAT template remains open.
