# A single local tensor can certify a missing RAM history

On the genuine closed gate-table construction at scale $j$, write $H_j$ for the indicator of the exceptional tables. On one priority verdict it is $\delta_s$, and on the other it is $\delta_s+\delta_{\bar s}$, where $s$ is the actual tagged history and the second word is its bitwise complement. The table has $v\ge2$ bits. The [finite-field readout](support-filtered-parity.md) evaluates a multilinear polynomial globally. An exact tree tensor representation of the same Boolean table can certify the one-hole verdict by examining **one local factor**. Every table in the family admits the required representation. What remains unknown is how quickly a sound verifier can obtain an appropriate factor of an exact representation. [Actual gate-table construction](projected-parity-holes.md#long-primary-words-with-fast-local-positive-verification)

## A local test for any tree of bond dimension two

Take a finite tree with at least one edge. An edge carries a summed binary index, called a bond. Vertices carry field-valued tensors on the incident bonds and their assigned physical bits. Every physical bit occurs once. Assume that deleting any edge places at least one physical bit on either side. Contraction sums over the internal indices and gives a function of all physical bits. Use any fixed field. One-dimensional bonds can be padded with zeros to dimension two. A local flattening towards a port is a matrix with that bond as its two columns and the vertex's remaining indices, including its physical bits, as rows.

**Test.** If the contracted tensor is promised to be either $\delta_s$ or $\delta_s+\delta_{\bar s}$, it is $\delta_s$ exactly when some vertex has a local flattening of rank less than two. Thus a certificate names one vertex and one port. For uniformly bounded valence and a bounded number of physical bits per vertex, a fixed number of field entries suffice. The tensor entries may be in an arbitrary basis on each bond. No particular canonical form is assumed.

To prove necessity and sufficiency, split at an edge. Contraction on each side gives a matrix with two columns indexed by the cut bond; the physical bits on that side index its rows. The global flattening there is $AB^{\mathsf T}$. If a local flattening at that port has rank at most one, attaching the remaining subtrees on its side applies a linear map to its row index and preserves this upper bound. The full tensor then has rank at most one at this cut. For two antipodes the words restrict to distinct rows and to distinct columns since each side has a physical bit. These two entries give rank two over every field. One local rank defect therefore excludes the two-hole case.

Conversely suppose all local port flattenings are injective as maps out of the two-dimensional port space. Root a component at an exposed port. Its map is obtained by applying the local map and then the tensor product of the child component maps and the identity on the local physical bits. Over a field, tensor products and compositions of injective maps are injective, as witnessed by products and compositions of left inverses. Induction gives both $A,B$ full column rank at every cut, so $AB^{\mathsf T}$ has rank two. A single point mass has rank one at every cut. This proves the test.

For a path with one physical bit at each site there is another short proof. Sum all physical bits except the first and last. The resulting $2\times2$ matrix has one nonzero entry for a single hole and is a permutation matrix for two antipodes. It is the product of the two endpoint matrices and one transfer matrix $T_i(0)+T_i(1)$ at each internal site. Its determinant vanishes precisely when one factor determinant vanishes. This is a different choice of local checks giving the same verdict on that promise.

## What this would buy on the actual RAM

An exact dimension-two tree exists on every scale, even on a prescribed binary tree on the bits. Copy a hidden bit along each bond, selecting $s$ or $\bar s$. At each physical position enforce the chosen bit value; in the single-hole case forbid the complementary selector at one specified vertex. This construction uses the actual history and verdict. It is an existence proof of the numerical ranks, with no efficient routine for supplying the actual local factors. A rank bound on the table alone therefore cannot decide the sign.

For the computational implication, take a fixed convention with valid vertex and port names of $O(j)$ bits (the original gate count is $2^{O(j)}$), including a way to reject invalid names and retrieve the needed entries. Work over a fixed finite field such as $\mathbb F_2$. Suppose a sound fixed ordinary nondeterministic verifier can certify a deficient port of an exact such representation, for example by computing its entries using a specified globally correct generator. An unauthenticated guess of tensor entries is insufficient: the represented tensor must be the actual $H_j$. The verifier can abstain everywhere it cannot establish the claim. The port gives a sound signal for one true sign. For $k=1,2,3$, if it accepts on infinitely many scales within $2^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}/(j+1)^B$ steps, with sufficiently large fixed $B$, a deterministic $O(n^k)$ SAT solver and the packed bounded-run compiler would give a sound $o(2^j)$ deterministic detector on those successes. This contradicts the native sign bound. In particular a polynomial-in-$j$ exact local generator on all stages would imply $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$: use polynomial-size explicit SAT instances for the existence of an accepted port; even an arbitrary polynomial SAT exponent on these instances would give polynomial-in-$j$ deterministic time. The two signs each occur infinitely often. [Priority detector and compiler](priority-pinned-sat.md) · [Encoded bit and word costs](packed-band-transfer.md)

## Higher bond dimensions inside regions

Cut a nonempty set of dimension-two edges of a tree. Assume each cut separates nonempty sets of physical bits. Each connected region is now one tensor on its physical coordinates and the boundary ports. The preceding proof applies unchanged to these regions, even when their internal bond dimensions are larger. In particular a single deficient *region port* certifies one hole. The region with many boundary ports need not output a table of exponential size.

Root the chosen region at that port. At each vertex compute a basis of the span of attainable row vectors on the parent bond. Here a row is obtained by fixing the physical bits and the remaining external ports throughout the subtree. Treat each unconstrained external port as a free input with its standard basis. For each local physical setting, contract the local tensor on each tuple of basis vectors from the children and collect a basis of the results by elimination. The recursion is exact: child subtrees have disjoint free coordinates, and multilinearity makes the span of contractions on child bases equal the span on all jointly attainable tuples. At the final port test whether the dimension of this span is less than two. With at most $d$ incident bonds and $a$ physical bits per vertex and bond dimension at most $D$ (including boundary ports), a region of $h$ vertices takes $h\,2^a(D+1)^{O(d+1)}$ field operations, plus enumeration and access to its local tensors. The running time counts the cost of naming, traversing and correctly delimiting the region. Under a fast-SAT hypothesis, infinitely many one-hole scales with a verifiably accessible deficient region fitting the preceding budget are excluded. This allows large or high-bond regions elsewhere on the same tree.

## Authenticating a nonuniform representation of the two-hole verdict

There is a different use of two-hole trees in which the verifier can receive an entirely untrusted proposed representation. Work over $\mathbb F_2$ with a trivalent unrooted tree, one physical bit at each leaf, no physical bits at internal vertices, and binary bonds. Each leaf supplies a $2\times2$ physical-by-bond matrix. On a promised two-hole table each internal tensor has an unordered decomposition
$$
 T=g_{1,0}\otimes g_{2,0}\otimes g_{3,0}
     +g_{1,1}\otimes g_{2,1}\otimes g_{3,1},
$$
where the pair of vectors at every port is a basis of $\mathbb F_2^2$.

Here is the justification even for unknown choices of bases. On a side of an edge, the component matrix $A$ has two independent columns spanning the column space of the true global flattening. If $P$ has as columns the two physical point indicators on that side, in a global order of the two words, then $A=P G$ for an invertible $G$. The two complementary sides have $G_L G_R^{\mathsf T}=I$ by contracting across the edge and canceling the two full-column-rank $P$ matrices. At a trivalent vertex, contract its tensor into the three incident side matrices $P_i G_i$. The global table in the three indicator bases has precisely the two diagonal entries $(0,0,0),(1,1,1)$ nonzero. Thus its vertex factors may be taken as $G_i^{-1} e_a$ for branch $a=0,1$. At neighboring vertices the pairing of their port vectors is the identity in the global branch order, by $G_L G_R^{\mathsf T}=I$. At a leaf its matrix applied to the neighbor's vector gives a physical unit vector. These arguments require no inner-product positivity.

The decomposition is unique up to a common swap of the two terms. Inverting the basis changes on any two ports identifies the span of their two paired rank-one matrices with the diagonal matrices. In dimension two the only nonzero rank-one matrices in that span over $\mathbb F_2$ are the two diagonal units. Hence those paired rank-one matrices and their individual nonzero vectors are fixed up to common permutation. Independence determines the two vectors on the third port as well. This is also the two-term case of tensor decomposition uniqueness. An actual decoder can enumerate the constant list of binary bases, try the two-term formula and use a fixed lexicographic ordering of its two terms. The dot products of adjacent port vectors then give a permutation telling which computed branch continues across that edge. Evaluating a leaf matrix identifies the physical bit on either branch. [Kruskal, uniqueness for three-way arrays](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795%2877%2990069-6)

Suppose on infinitely many two-hole scales there is a polynomial-in-$j$ bit description of such an exact tree representation, with polynomial-in-$j$ time for reading each named tensor, and for obtaining a root-to-leaf path to any requested leaf including its port numbers. It suffices that a tree of polynomial-in-$j$ diameter has efficiently navigable paths. The nonuniform descriptions need not be available or trustworthy at any other scales. Guess a description with its fixed polynomial cap and use the leaf for the tag to choose the branch with tag zero. Transport it along the indicated path to compute a proposed word's bit at any other leaf. Bounded timeouts, fixed default bits on malformed data and deterministic tie breaking make this a single proposed word even for a spurious descriptor. Check that this word **and its complement** satisfy every clause of the [actual locally indexed width-three hole CNF](projected-parity-holes.md#long-primary-words-with-fast-local-positive-verification). One universal clause challenge and a constant number of bit evaluations suffice. Passing this test entails the existence of two distinct holes irrespective of which tensors or claimed paths were guessed. Completeness on the indicated scales follows from the preceding reconstruction. This gives a polynomial-in-$j$ two-block detector on those scales; under $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ the polynomial hierarchy collapses to polynomial time, violating the native sign bound. Here the nonuniformity is in the polynomial-length description. Merely listing polynomially many entries *per site* for exponentially many sites does not meet this assumption.

There is a local variant without a diameter bound. Take a **public, correctly indexed tree** and require poly$(j)$-time access to the short paths connecting the at most three leaves of each actual clause. Give the verifier an untrusted bounded description of its local tensors. A universal challenge checks any vertex, adjacent pair of tensors, or leaf: each internal vertex must decompose as above, the two labels must match across each internal edge by a permutation under the dot product, and at a leaf the two mapped branch vectors must give the two distinct physical unit vectors. Include the trivial two-leaf case by direct evaluation, or restrict to at least three leaves. These local tests enforce two coherent global complementary words since the network is a tree. For a clause, orient its first variable arbitrarily, propagate the branch along its short paths to the other variables and evaluate the clause in *both* orientations. Every passed check is independent of the root. Passing all clause checks entails that both global words satisfy the original CNF. The same polynomial-hierarchy argument applies. This version does not reconstruct a distant individual bit, and the public tree condition is needed for soundness of the local checks.

For example, a caterpillar tree can enforce $y_{i+1}=y_i\oplus b_i$ for every consecutive pair. Use copy tensors and a swap of the bond basis across precisely the bonds marked by the bits $b_i$; alternatively use the ordinary two-state path keeping the current bit, and attach identity physical leaves. This is an exact two-antipodal-word representation for every choice of the $b_i$. Relative values for consecutive variables are locally accessible. Once $y_1$ is fixed, recovering $y_v$ requires the parity of all $v-1$ marks. A deterministic exact local-oracle procedure for that bit must read all of them. This example distinguishes local validation of clauses from a purported efficient random-access reconstruction on an arbitrary tall tree.

## Two sharp failures, already for nonnegative zero-one entries

1. **A dimension-three path.** Let $b_1,\ldots,b_v$ be bits governing the local factors, and $J$ the $2\times2$ matrix swapping zero and one. On one distinguished state use the scalar $1$ at a zero physical bit and $0$ at a one. Direct-sum this with a two-state component using the zero matrix at a zero physical bit and $J^{b_i}$ at a one. Its input state is zero and its accepted output state is one. Use boundary vectors that add the distinguished-state and two-state contributions; absorb those boundary vectors at the endpoints. Mixed physical strings get zero. The all-zero string has amplitude one and the all-one string has amplitude $\bigoplus_i b_i$. All entries and accepted amplitudes are zero or one over any field. The underlying contracted tensor has cut ranks at most two for every choice of $b$. The supplied three-state representation can hide the verdict in a product along the entire path.
2. **A single cycle with dimension-two bonds.** Give each site a physical bit $y_i$. Along the first $v-1$ edges require $y_{i+1}=y_i\oplus b_i$. At the closing edge accept $(y_v,y_1)$ in $\{00,01,11\}$. Each allowed relation $R_i$ gives the local tensor $T_i(y_i,u,w)=[u=y_i]R_i(u,w)$: the cyclic contraction sums to the Boolean indicator of the constraints. Each of its three local port or physical flattenings has rank two, since both input rows are nonempty with disjoint first-state supports as appropriate, and each $R_i$ is an invertible two-by-two matrix (for the output port, its columns remain independent after the copy). When the hidden parity is even, both complementary propagated strings survive. When it is odd the closing constraint lets precisely one of them survive. There is a physical bit at every vertex, and each fixed-bit tensor slice has rank one.

In either example an accepting witness that certifies a specified exact verdict, with opaque local access to the $b_i$, must inspect every hidden bit. Flipping an uninspected bit preserves the same accepting transcript with its witness fixed and changes the verdict. The local query examples are separate from the native sign family. They establish the need for additional hypotheses to use the same local-defect argument with a nonminimal path or a loop. They do not give a lower bound for an unrestricted RAM on explicit SAT.

Canonical forms of tree tensor networks and their cut ranks have a substantial literature. The local linear algebra here is an elementary field-independent specialization to these two promised tables. Historical priority for its combinations with the native certificate budget and the two local-query examples has not been established.
