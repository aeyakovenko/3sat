# Verifying a candidate read without computing it

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A fixed uniform relation gets the input formula, the true slot, the pre-load controller and the current address. It checks a proposed word $v$ and a private witness $u$ in $\operatorname{polylog}N$ native steps. Bound the witness length by that clock times word width. Assume that **every context admits some reply and witness**, including forged controller states. Witnesses can be different on every invocation. Multiple witnesses for the same word cost nothing extra. Their inspection time is charged. Only the solver's native history defines the memory and access times below.

Let $v_r$ be the word truly read at slot $r$ and $p(r)$ the preceding native touch to the same word, or zero at initialization. An error is a read at which the relation permits **at least one word different from $v_r$**. At every other read totality guarantees that the true word is the only possible output. It may take arbitrarily long to *find* a witness for it. For either sign of SAT, let $E$ be the first sound signal and charge
$$
 C_\lambda(E)=E+\sum_{r\le E\text{ an error}} (r-p(r))^\lambda,\qquad 0<\lambda\le1
$$
for fixed rational $\lambda$. No workspace bound and no negative-run time promise are imposed for a positive-sign solver, and vice versa.

**Claim.** For each fixed relation and solver, the worst $C_\lambda$ on the chosen sign is outside $O(N^c)$ for $c<(1+\lambda)^{1/3}$. For $\lambda=1$ this includes $c=1.3622$ by a deeper induction. With a separate deterministic near-linear SAT algorithm, the cost is at least $N^{1+\lambda}/\operatorname{polylog}N$ infinitely often on the chosen sign. For a linear solver and $\lambda=1$, on some inputs $N/\operatorname{polylog}N$ actual reads with gaps at least $N/\operatorname{polylog}N$ permit an incorrect reply. A total verifier allowed to be multivalued elsewhere cannot uniquely certify all those words. A relation always admitting the true answer but also admitting a different answer at a particular read incurs the charge there. This distinction matters for soundness. ## Finite totality from an inverse or a collision

Fix any forward map $f:[D]\to[D]$ computable in polylogarithmic time from the public context and a proposed argument. Its domain elements use a bounded number of words. Supply either a preimage of the requested target, with an output bit or word determined by that preimage, or two distinct arguments with equal image and an agreed default output. Every forward map admits a response: if injective it is onto, and otherwise it has a collision. When it is a permutation, all legal replies reveal the output attached to its unique preimage. The verifier makes one or two forward evaluations, with no inversion algorithm or certified promise on its input. The same construction works for words formed from a fixed number of such inversions. [Finite totality and uniqueness in Lean](../lean/CertifiedReadReplies.lean)

This separates the new test from deterministic forecasting on the [permutation example](long-gap-inverse-bits.md). On the valid guarded family, the designated read of bit $j$ of $\pi^{-1}(y)$, encoded as one plus that bit, admits only its true value by one forward lookup and the candidate inverse index. The relation can be made total on every formula: parse a bounded table with a public default for malformed entries, use a total square map on the finite domain, and allow the collision case. The deterministic lower bound on that family quantifies only over finding a bit from short common advice and a few probes. A code-based construction separately forces frequent errors even with total local verifiers. [Hardness with private certificates](verified-code-reads.md) The inverse certificate defeats the attempted transfer of that lower bound to locally checked answers. This observation concerns the designated hard loads, not the other operations of the wrapper. Total polynomial search and the pigeonhole principle have established complexity-theoretic formulations. ## A unique native segment and separately witnessed errors

Assume a cheap positive run through signal as in the deterministic result. On negative inputs choose true halt without that signal, or the first prefix whose integer cost exceeds a public budget $B=O(N^c)$ valid on all positives. Round fractional gap powers as in the deterministic proof. Exclude a positive signal at or before a purported negative endpoint, including a tie at the last slot. Though the score now uses existentially witnessed errors and need never be computed by the native machine, it is an intrinsic, nondecreasing function of the finite causal prefix. The overshoot is at most $1+(B+1)^\lambda$. Under a negative-sign hypothesis certify the cheap genuine UNSAT signal directly.

Average over phases of a grid of width $w$ on the chosen prefix. Supply one explicit correction $(p,v)$ for each **crossing error** requiring an external word. Supply the true controllers and cut times. At the genuine first block use initialized memory. In each later block a local last touch supplies its true value; an explicit correction supplies an incoming word when present; otherwise nondeterministically guess a legal reply with a witness. Evaluate native controls and arithmetic deterministically. For any syntactically valid proposed block, at least one such bounded replay exists by totality. A malformed correction gets a fixed total interpretation for replay and a separate syntactic defect. Absorbing halts and fixed block lengths keep every transcript bounded. Private reply witnesses belong to the short replay and never inflate the outer chart.

Include a defect for **two permitted replays of one block with different native transcripts**, ignoring private witness bits. On an honest chart there is no such pair: at the first genuine incoming read without a correction the relation admits only its true word, and induction within the block forces every native slot. On a forged chart without this defect there is exactly one native transcript for each block, by existence. A witness for a read, store, controller test or historical error can therefore materialize the relevant transcript by guessing one replay. No algorithm for deterministically constructing that log is needed.

Check the transitions between controllers and the terminal native conditions. Validate correction timestamps by addressing a previous touch and forbidding intervening touches, including the sentinel case. To challenge any wrong read, guess a previous store conflicting with its word and require no intervening store, or test for a nonzero value with no earlier store. The inner nondeterministic test asks if an intervening block **can** exhibit the forbidden intervening store; the parts in endpoint blocks are directly visible. In any full chart with no ambiguity defect this tests exactly the unique native logs. On an ambiguous forged chart the separate defect already suffices. With none of these defects, induction from the initialized RAM forces the genuine complete prefix.

It remains to check a positive watchdog's claim that the true error cost has crossed $B$. A chart cannot charge all its supplied corrections as errors: dishonest corrections can redundantly state the true value even where that value is uniquely certified. Instead give each block a nonnegative integer quota for error charges, and add the real prefix length separately. A **quota witness** replays the block, selects any subset of its reads and supplies, for every selected read, a legal certificate of a word different from the block's returned word. The preceding touch time for a selected read must be known inside this block, be fixed by genuine initialization in the first block, or be supplied by an explicit authenticated correction record. Sum the rounded gap charges and require at least the quota. There is at most one selected occurrence per slot. This witness costs $O(w\operatorname{polylog}N)$ time and private data.

A defect may choose a block for which **no quota witness exists**. This is a nonacceptance call of the same nesting depth and size as the intervening-store test. Comparing the sum of the publicly guessed quotas and the elapsed time with $B$ costs at most one outer scan of the chart. On an authentic negative prefix reaching the cutoff, take the actual contribution as each quota. Every selected crossing error has a supplied record, so witnesses exist. On any chart that passes, its unique native histories and true timestamps imply that each quota is a genuine lower bound. The alleged cutoff cannot pass on a positive prefix before its promised timely signal. For a negative halt below the cutoff no positive quota is needed. An intrinsic error check at every single time therefore needs neither a global list of certificates nor a deterministic algorithm for distinguishing correct from ambiguous replies.

The work of an inner nondeterministic test is $H^{x+o(1)}$ at $w=H^x$, and its public input is $(m+H^{c-\lambda x}+1)H^{o(1)}$. Each block quota is one logarithmic-word field and fits the chart bound. The two nonacceptance simulations now have exactly the cost of the [value-only recurrence](weighted-read-gaps.md). At $\lambda=1$ its 103-step rational certificate applies unchanged. With near-linear deterministic SAT, the fixed number of eliminations and polylogarithmic local verification overhead preserve the signed $H^{1+\lambda}/\operatorname{polylog}H$ cutoff. The asymptotic simulations here are manuscript arguments.

The same total-relation argument applies to a short per-cut advice string used for incoming reads in a finite forward window. Require every permitted word on every true required incoming context to equal the correct word. With an honest advice row, each block is unique along the genuine history. With any forged row, the existence and disagreement checks above suffice. Thus the [unannounced-width negative live-read theorem](live-value-replay.md) also forces substantial cumulative advice for this larger class of verifiers, which includes fast forward checks of hidden inverses. A corresponding unrestricted upper bound supplying short, locally checkable certificates for the reads of an arbitrary hypothetical linear solver is still missing.
