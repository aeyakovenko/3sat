# Parallel proof attempts of 28 November, round five, and why each stopped

On 28 November, a fifth round of parallel attempts took up the open steps left by round four, together with directions from two further external reviews and two questions raised directly in the project. Each attempt had about an hour and wrote a note. **None bridges the gap; the linear, quadratic and cubic questions remain open.** The results published as separate notes, listed below, were each checked by an independent referee and revised. This note records the other attempts, with the exact reason each stopped, so that later work does not repeat them. Of the attempts recorded here only one was reviewed, the transfer-matrix game folded into two blocks: the review confirmed its theorems, relabelled it partial and supplied its main finding, which no one else has checked. The others are attempt history, not verified theorems. [Round four](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-round-four.md)

The first of the two reviews proposed three directions: proving a verdict through compact semantic identities instead of reconstructing memory (recorded here), a verifier measure that keeps uniqueness (published), and sharing one repeated transition table in the compiler (recorded here, as a control). The second proposed four: closing black-box game shapes with the switching-lemma induction (recorded here), an exact-network argument that reads vertex tables and not only topology (published), the upstream Weisfeiler–Leman time bound as a positive control for the compiler route (published), and banking the alternation criterion's contrapositive as an unconditional theorem (published). The two questions raised in the project were whether the instruction count needed to decide SAT must grow (published: fixed linear-time programs decide several of the fooling families, two of them only in restricted forms, and growth at linear time is the target itself) and whether a one-instruction computer helps the program-size line (recorded here).

## Attempts recorded here

**Certifying a verdict through semantic identities (partial; the coverage theorem is missing).** The description-first note excludes games whose first block describes memory and leaves open first blocks that are not descriptions. The first review proposed certifying the verdict of that note's opponent p\* through identities instead: p\* writes a table into a fresh bank while XOR-accumulating, rescans the bank with a second accumulator and rejects on equality. A static certificate checker for the journal's RAM instruction set was built, with rules for read-over-write, range XORs, interval disjointness, confined loop-free blocks and cut-point induction. With a reserved-bank cancellation lemma it certifies the verdict of a p\* stand-in for arbitrary payloads: a 638-byte certificate checked in 199 rule applications at every tested n from 16 to 2^20, and every one of seven mutants that flip the verdict is rejected; the checker's soundness was tested on such mutants and random ones, not proved. The real p\* exists only under the hypothesis of a fast SAT program, and certifying it also needs the stores of its hosted simulation confined away from the bank, which was not verified. On the journal's standing opponents the same rules certify nothing shorter than replay: each would need its own new identity (a rotation series, a bit projection, a periodic sum), and for two of them none was found. By an argument on paper, not checked, no fixed rule list can close this. For any sound checker C, under the hypothesis, the recursion theorem gives an opponent that asks the hypothetical fast SAT program whether a short C-certificate of its own rejection exists and accepts if one does; by C's soundness none exists, so it always rejects, a constant verdict with no short C-certificate. This would be the criterion's contrapositive specialised to certificate-first games (a link not checked against the journal's notes), and it suggests that a coverage theorem needs reflection. A hypothesis-free version gives only s ≥ log₂(t/q) − O(1) (on paper). A certificate shared by all universal paths must sit entirely in the first block, as the guessed-field entry below also finds. [Description-first games](description-first-games-fail-on-one-hard-table-opponent.md) · [Standing opponents](../audits/standing_opponent_suite.py)

**One shared transition table in the compiler (partial; a control).** For a source that runs one procedure h times on fresh private workspace, carrying w bits of state between runs, the first review proposed storing the 2^w-entry transition table once and checking every step against it by one batched lookup. The compiler lemma is proved with exact variable and clause counts (22,851 variables and 89,590 clauses at w = 6 and h = 64), routing the table entries and the h queries through one Beneš network: the formula is a proper 3-CNF, satisfiable exactly when the runs halt and end in the proposed state. It was checked against CaDiCaL on 1,088 small formulas with no disagreement, every wrong final state was refuted, and mutations show which parts are load-bearing. Sharing disappears in the named cases: an independent map per block needs a key carrying the block index (h·2^w records), private memory that persists between runs enlarges the key by the persisting bits, and charged table construction costs 2^w·B steps. As a route to a bound it is only a control: tabulating and iterating decides the class deterministically with the same cost exponent as the charged compile, max(β + ω, 1 − β) for B = T^β and 2^w = T^ω, so no source in the class can carry the lower bound the budget needs. The review's budget calculation is already in the journal, in the few-coin transfer note and the notes that restate it, with four hypotheses that the one-line version omits. The missing step is the short-boundary note's open "uniform choice of ports": shared structure that a certificate can check but no deterministic o(T) computation can build. [Short boundaries](compiling-native-blocks-from-short-boundaries.md) · [Few-coin transfer](few-coin-sat-transfer.md)

**Guessed fields make the pebble game unsound; the named-writer game (partial).** The idea generator proposed combining a fast write-once array with round three's truth-claim game, to put the unit-cost RAM into word-priced alternating time O(t/log log t), and warned that guessed fields in memory might make the game unsound. A normal form whose memory records carry guessed fields can be sound as a nondeterministic machine and unsound in that game: on the program "store a←0; store a←1; load a" the guessed-next-pointer form wins a false root claim with two placements on every path. Exhaustively over the 516 straight-line programs with at most four steps on two addresses, that form is sequentially sound on all of them and game-unsound exactly where the final load has a stale earlier store of a different value; moving the same guesses into the root announcement, or using the deterministic write-once form, makes the game sound on all of them. A prover-named writer game handles programs in which, for every seed, each work cell is written at most once and never read before it is written: their language is in word-priced alternating time O(β/log β) for step budget β (proved on paper, modulo Dymond–Tompa's lemma as round three read it at source; on all 2,457 straight-line programs with T ≤ 4 it is sound and complete on the 332 write-once ones and unsound on 301 others). The speedup itself, DTIME_RAM[T] ⊆ ATIME_RAM[O(T/log log T)], is not proved: it needs the write-once array for every fixed operation sequence, in expectation over one multiply-shift seed and in word steps, which goes beyond the random workload that this round's write-once array addresses. A seed chosen in hindsight does not escape the journal's lower bound for online conversions, so log log t is this route's ceiling. No earlier statement of DTIME_RAM[t] ⊆ ATIME_RAM[o(t)] was found, from searches and abstracts only. [Mutable arrays on write-once memory](mutable-arrays-on-word-write-once-memory.md)

**A sound digest is an injective fingerprint (partial; one organising lemma, one correction).** Under the OR verdict, on independently falsifiable slots, a fixed digest is sound for at most N genuine reply vectors, because soundness leaves each such vector alone in its fibre of the load-sum map; this is proved and checked exhaustively at small sizes, and it is the journal's singleton-fibre count, not new. The step that this round's idea list proposed toward the digest bound was false as written: when both loads of a two-copy word are slots they are not independently falsifiable, and one digest is sound for every genuine table while every pair sum is 0 (checked for q ≤ 8). Using only the first copy's loads repairs it at manuscript level, under four unchecked assumptions, and the result is subsumed by the contrapositive of the round-three digest theorem. On round four's data-dependent-address residue, for one pointer load followed by one dependent load, the least range of a literally sound digest is 6 at q = 2 and 3 (7 with a public address; the lower bounds rest on CaDiCaL answers), and a hand-proved digest of group order 2,187 is sound for every address range (checked for q ≤ 3), so one dependent load costs nothing that grows with the range. Whether chains of pointer loads force a growing range is open. [Separate fingerprint witnesses](separate-fingerprint-witnesses.md) · [Round four](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-round-four.md)

**Cone caps on the chained overwrite table (partial).** Round four measured that a last-writer decoder with a greedy landmark rule stores 9–12% of all values on the chained overwrite table. A linear lower bound on stored values is false: landmark sets of expected size at most (1 + o(1))·m/ln Q cap every cone at Q (proved on an idealised chain DAG and transferred to the exact one), which is o(m) already at Q = √m. The right form is a lower bound of c·m/log Q for landmark sets chosen after the map; it is not proved, and on the lower side there is only the trivial ⌈n/(Q+1)⌉ and a first-moment bound for sets fixed in advance. The measured fractions belong to the greedy rule: a periodic rule with fix-ups beats it at large m (0.0628 against 0.0903 at m = 2^20, Q = m^{3/4}, on one map per size) and falls with m, and exact minima at m ≤ 128 lie 0–41% below greedy. In bits nothing changes: Θ(m/log Q) words of Θ(log m) bits are Θ(m) bits, so if the lower bound holds the charts of this family still have exponent u = 1. [Landmark chart](the-seed-note-runs-have-a-landmark-shared-bank-chart.md)

**The two-block opcode barrier as a density statement (partial; the chain only).** Round four left the two-block case needing a density statement over all classes of a verifier's cover. The density statement implies the two-block barrier by pigeonhole; there is no converse. On the chain it is exact: for every positive base table and q ≥ 2, the q-local closure of its relabelings contains a table with a flagged genuine key exactly when the base has a q-dangerous decoy start, a decoy cell whose background walk meets its first flag after at least q − 1 flag-0 steps (proved; checked on every positive base at T = 4 and 5 and on random bases). So the base must contain long clean decoy walks ending in a flag. The hidden-word note's uniform planting fails already at b = 0: one q-CNF with q = λ + log₂T + s rejects every flagged table and holds on all but a 2^{−s} fraction of positive tables, so under that measure the chain's barrier is invisible. One planted dangerous walk serves b = 0 for q up to about (1 − η)T but fails at b = 1; every base fails once b ≥ λ⌊T/q⌋; and a layered product base passes the necessary weight condition up to b ≈ (h + 1 − q)(λ − log₂h) (computed at one size), the base proposed for the next lemma. Whether some planted base on the depth-robust family defeats every family of density 2^{−b} with b, q ≤ δT is not settled. [Hidden-word opcode](hidden-word-opcode-and-local-verification-of-a-gap.md) · [Round four](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-round-four.md)

**A transfer-matrix game folded into two blocks cannot tell runs apart (partial, after review).** Round four priced two-block games with explicit linear claims and left round three's halving game, whose claims are transfer-matrix entries, uncovered. This attempt folded that game into two blocks on a fixed schedule, with prover-chosen split points and replay checks of at most s steps. Changing one step to A_ρ + y·zᵀ fakes every earlier covector consistently and passes every identity check, so every complete shape must contain base claims that detect each fake flipping the output, and the number of base claims is at least a sum of band weights over steps s apart. The attempt first concluded that the run's band weight is the price. Review found otherwise: the true values pass on every complete shape, and completeness depends only on the claim names and the support of the input, so the folded game's least cost is a function of the dimension, the length, s, the output index and that support alone; and its rules force all D² labels among the base claims at every interior step, at least D²·⌈(T − 2s)/s⌉ base claims even when the output is one input bit. That finding was made during review, checked by dynamic programming only at D ≤ 4, T ≤ 10 and s ≤ 3, and has not been checked by anyone else. The missing step is a class with long-range replay checks, where the covector-weight form already fails per input: for explicit covector claims the band must span the state, and a state-snapshot game beats the covector band weight by a factor D at |x| = 1. Games with three or more blocks were not attempted. [Round four](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-round-four.md)

**A Σ₂-query barrier by counting (partial; in the string model only).** The black-box note's threshold o(s) holds for NP queries, and its first next lemma asks for a barrier against Σ₂ queries, which compile the solver's run inside a query. In the string model, where a query sees the instance's structure only through its own bits, Tseitin structures on disjoint K₄'s in one fixed layout of M bits realize 2^{(3/4)n log₂n − O(n)} distinct verdict functions, so on some input every deterministic adaptive procedure asks queries of total length at least c·M when a query that depends on the hidden signs has at least ⌈log₂K⌉ bits, as in the black-box note's format, and Ω(M/log M) without that minimum. Σ₂ and Σ_k queries priced h·log^b h are special cases, so the threshold survives them in that model. The routes the barrier was meant for compile the solver's run and read the structure inside the query, and there counting gives nothing: one NP query of polylogarithmic size decides the same family. For one Σ₂ query on connected Tseitin structures, the Paturi–Pudlák–Zane isolated-model bound gives size at least √(m·log₂4m) − 1, tight up to polylogarithmic factors with the black-box note's one Σ₂ query of O(√K·polylog K) bits. Whether any structure forces Σ₂ queries with structure access beyond Θ̃(√M) is open. The other three items proposed with it (the worst case over files with repeated clauses, two reductions to read at source, and the indexer circuit and generator as a RAM program) were not attempted. **The switching-lemma induction for k-block opcode verifiers (dead).** The second review asked to prove round three's opcode barrier for every fixed number of blocks by the switching-lemma induction of the relativization-barrier notes. Writing a k-block verifier as a depth-(k+1) circuit over opcode answers works, but the induction's first step is false over opcode answers: with word-valued cells as variables, a branch that recomputes t chain steps keeps decision-tree depth at least t/2 with probability about 0.05 under a per-cell restriction at p = 1/(10t), where the switching form would give 2^{−t/2} (checked exactly at small size and by Monte Carlo). After the labels are fixed the induction does prove something, for the parity of the genuine flags, which the same O(T) run computes: q·(b + 3) > T/800 for two blocks, and a product form q·b^{k−1} ≳ T/C^k in general, the classical parity argument for constant depth. For the barrier's OR predicate it proves nothing. And the product form is the method's ceiling: the parity of T known flags has exact k-block verifiers with q·b^{k−1} = (k−1)^{k−1}·T·(1 + o(1)) (checked exhaustively up to T = 16), so no argument that fixes the labels can exclude (q, b) = (T^{0.9}, λ·T^{0.9}), a point that the pebbling-strength barrier, still unproved for two blocks, would exclude on the depth-robust family, or the criterion's points once T is large. This ceiling is proved for arguments that fix the labels, and derived, not proved, for switching with live label bits. The review's dichotomy gets neither answer: the theorem does not end the search over game shapes relative to the opcode, and the only non-pebbling game it exhibits needs a label per cut vertex on a hashed graph, which is the checkpoint game again. [Square-root floor](a-relativization-barrier-for-the-square-root-floor.md) · [Alternation criterion](alternating-native-speedup.md)

**A one-instruction computer for the program-size line (dead).** The question was whether a machine with one opcode, subtract-and-branch-if-not-positive with the journal's addressing modes, makes the time question easier or pushes the program-size exclusion past five instructions. It does neither. The two machines simulate each other: each one-opcode instruction is at most five RAM instructions, and each RAM instruction becomes one-opcode code with O(1) slowdown for arithmetic, memory and comparisons and O(w) for bitwise operations, multiplication and shifts (macros constructed and tested); with O(1) slowdown for every opcode the linear classes would coincide, which is Grandjean and Jachiet's theorem in their O(N)-value model, not transferred here to w-bit words. Translating the journal's instructions costs 1 to 42 one-opcode instructions each, for the twelve numerical operations and four branch tests compiled, so an exclusion at size C reaches only RAM programs whose compiled size is at most C; exhaustive search was complete only at C = 1 and reached C = 3 only as a capped statement for one operand set and fixed suites. The 43-instruction program compiles to a correct 469-instruction one-opcode program (tested on 52 CaDiCaL-checked files), so no quantity bounded by program size alone can be exhausted by SAT at every size. That no one-instruction program decides SAT at any speed follows from the five-slot theorem, and C = 2 is where the one-opcode machine leaves proved territory, its expansions falling in the open six-slot classes. [Five instructions cannot decide DIMACS SAT](five-instructions-cannot-decide-dimacs-sat.md) · [A 43-instruction SAT program](a-43-instruction-sat-program-in-the-five-slot-machine.md) · [Grandjean–Jachiet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13851)

**Nine or ten AND gates for one matrix-verification bit (open; 281 of 282 first-gate orbits excluded).** Round four left 166 of the 282 symmetry orbits of first gates open (285,547 planes). A new implementation of its restriction test reproduces every per-orbit survivor count and verdict it published at depths 2 to 4, and a run of round four's own program on five orbits agrees. Finishing the four-gate pass and going to five and six gates, with two reductions — gates whose plane lies in an orbit already excluded by an earlier run are dropped, and the second and third gates are reduced by the stabilizers of the first gate and of the first two — excludes 163 more orbits: 279 of 282, or 2,793,957 of 2,794,155 first-gate planes. Three orbits stay open, those of the first gates ae (54 planes), a(c+e) (108) and (a+e)(c+f) (36); no 9-AND circuit was found. The exclusions are finite computations, cross-checked but not formally verified, resting on round four's unrefereed restriction lemma, and the round's other finite and bibliographic leftovers were not attempted. A continuation closed two of the three, the orbits of (a+e)(c+f) and a(c+e), by searches run to completion to depth 9 that use only these reductions, so 281 of 282 orbits (2,794,101 of 2,794,155 planes) are excluded. It also stated and proved the restriction lemma, and a brute-force checker sharing no code with the searches found no violation of it in about 3.9 million restriction-path nodes. The orbit of ae stays open: 206 of its 3,413 second-gate representatives were searched to completion and no 9-AND circuit was found, so ten ANDs are not established. Either value is a finite circuit constant with no bearing on RAM time. [Seven ANDs for a 2×2 product](seven-ands-for-a-two-by-two-binary-matrix-product.md)

## What the routes were missing

The alternation route now has its contrapositive as an unconditional dichotomy with explicit parameters, and the upstream Weisfeiler–Leman bound shows where the route works: a hierarchy plus a compiler for a deterministic source, while for SAT the same step needs a one-block speedup of the diagonal. Black-box game shapes are not closed: the switching induction proves only product-form parity bounds, the two-block density statement is exact on the chain but open on the depth-robust family, guessed fields must sit in the root announcement, a certificate-first game needs a coverage theorem with reflection, and a transfer-matrix game folded into two blocks prices claim names rather than runs.

The circuit route now has a uniform cap: the vertex-site price inequality and its table-reading dual certify at most O(N log N) for every target, leaving price inequalities with multi-vertex pieces and diagonal counting as the escapes named. A T-step SAT program gives unique-witness verifiers of size O(T log²N) for both verdicts, and the quadratic exact-network construction does not cap that size. The Σ₂ counting barrier holds only for queries that cannot read the structure.

On the program-size line, fixed linear-time programs decide several of the fooling families that the small-program exclusions use, two of them only in restricted forms, and growth of the needed instruction count at linear time is equivalent to the target; a one-instruction machine changes nothing. Six-slot scans with an odd multiplier or a lookbehind payload need Ω(N^{3/2}/log²N) steps at standard width to answer every equality file correctly, even multipliers with odd strides fail at every speed under native initialization, and the six-instruction map stands at 932 classes excluded and 14 open (969 after a round-six note on one-verdict loops, exit code and LOAD loops without arithmetic). The truth-claim route's frame game is o(T) on ladders touching o(T) cells. The compiler route's shared-table compile is a control. Digests remain at their injection lemma, and charts of the chained overwrite table at a lower bound not yet proved. [Barriers](what-any-proof-of-superlinear-sat-time-must-get-past.md)

## The idea generator's judgment

The idea generator that proposed this round's residue entries recorded its own judgment with its lists of proposals for rounds five and six. It is reproduced here as that judgment, not as a result.

Before this round it judged, from rounds three and four, that the loop had stopped producing steps toward the target: the attempts return correct, checkable statements, namely exact maps of restricted move classes, barriers for criteria and finite work on the program-size line, which measure the commitment obstacle without removing it. It put the chance that anything on its list was a step toward the SAT bound proper at about one percent. It named three kinds of idea that would be needed instead. For the alternation route, a structural theorem about all RAM runs: a commitment of o(t) bits that cuts every run and is neither a value, a description, a digest nor a label-closed proof. For the circuit route, a lower-bound method that is not routing; here it suggested a method using the repetition of the simulated circuit, T copies of one gadget plus a sorter. For the program-size line, a quantity monotone in the number of instructions that SAT provably exhausts.

After this round it judged that the round had confirmed that view, and withdrew the circuit hint: a method using the repetition of the simulated circuit would not be a circuit method, because the trace network of a fixed program has a description of O(log N) bits, and a bound against networks with such descriptions is the machine statement again. So the circuit route needs a non-uniform technique that is not a vertex-site price inequality and that fails for the list-distinctness family D_w, which has an O(N/log N) RAM solver. For the program-size line it judged that the quantity has to be joint in size and time, since no size-only quantity works and unbounded growth at linear time is the target itself; every quantity it could name with a provable increment on the unrestricted machine stops at a constant number of steps per input word or at linear space. As a yardstick it noted that even SAT ∉ DTIME_RAM[O(N/log N)] is open, and that on the circuit side the same yardstick needs an exact-network bound ω(N log N), exactly the order of the price-inequality cap.
