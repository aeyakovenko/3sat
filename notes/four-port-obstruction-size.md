# Sharp size of an unsatisfiable sublist for four-port rows

Fix one threshold in the [port formulation](mst-forests-and-four-port-coloring.md). Each variable has a head and a tail of different colors in $\{0,1,2\}$. An active row equates a head with a tail. Suppose every equality component has at most four ports, and let $k$ count components of size four at this threshold. **Every unsatisfiable instance contains an unsatisfiable sublist of at most $7k+8$ original active rows. For each $k\ge0$ this bound is attained.** A deterministic preprocessing algorithm also gives an equivalent instance on $O(k+1)$ densely named variables; it can inspect the whole input. The sharp smaller bound is existential.

## Original-row sublists

Contract equality components. Each original variable creates an inequality edge. A quotient loop has a certificate of at most three rows along a simple path between its ports. Otherwise discard parallel edges for the graph analysis. In an uncolorable simple quotient take an inclusion-minimal non-three-colorable subgraph $H$. It is 4-critical: all proper subgraphs are 3-colorable, and coloring after deleting one vertex and giving it a fourth color proves the upper bound of four. Every vertex has degree three or four. Write $v=|V(H)|$, $e=|E(H)|$ and $b$ for the number of degree-four vertices in $H$. Four distinct incidences consume four ports, so $b\le k$ and $2e=3v+b$. Kostochka and Yancey proved $3e\ge5v-2$. Consequently $v\le3b+4$ and $e\le5b+6$. [Kostochka and Yancey](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1173)

For each vertex of $H$, retain a spanning tree of original active rows inside its equality component. A size-three group costs two rows, and a size-four group costs three. The chosen groups have size at least three. If $a$ of them have four ports, the total is at most $2v+a\le6b+a+8\le7k+8$. Some of the chosen four-port groups can have degree three in $H$. This count allows the extra port to lie between two needed ports. Each edge of $H$ has its original variable enforcing the inequality unconditionally. These retained rows therefore suffice by themselves regardless of the other variables. If the full sequence of thresholds also has final component size at most four, there is a sublist preserving the SAT bit at *every* threshold. At the first failing threshold retain the small original obstruction with its weights. Earlier prefixes of this sublist are satisfiable because the full prefixes were; later prefixes contain the obstruction. If no threshold fails, the empty sublist suffices. This gives the same sharp worst-case bound, with $k$ measured at the first failure. Locating that threshold can still require solving three-colorability.

In the capped chain of $L$ diamonds, the $L-1$ internal terminals have degree four, the others degree three. The path presentation has $k=L-1$, $v=3k+4$, $e=5k+6$ and $7k+8$ rows. Deleting any one row makes it satisfiable, as proved by splitting the incidences in the original chain. Taking $L=k+1$ proves sharpness at every integer $k$. [Explicit sharp family and deletion proof](mst-forests-and-four-port-coloring.md#sometimes-every-path-row-is-necessary-even-for-the-final-verdict)

If the loopless quotient is triangle-free, Moore and Smith-Roberge give $3e\ge5v+2$. Thus $v\le3b-4$, $e\le5b-6$, and at most $6b+a-8\le7k-8$ original rows suffice. A density of $(5v+4)/3$ in Davies' examples is an upper bound for the minimum possible edge count at their sizes. This calculation uses the proved lower bound $(5v+2)/3$; sharpness of the four-port triangle-free constants is undetermined here. [Moore and Smith-Roberge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01503)

## A checkable proof at the sharp end

Kostochka and Yancey give a stronger alternative for $H$. Either it is a **4-Ore graph**, assembled from copies of $K_4$ by Ore compositions, or $3e\ge5v-1$. In the latter case $v\le3b+2$, and $6b+a+4\le7k+4$ rows suffice. In particular, if every uncolorable sublist needs more than $7k+4$ rows, the instance admits a short purely existential proof of noncolorability. [Kostochka and Yancey, Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.0846#page=3)

In a composition of graphs $A,B$, delete an edge $xy$ of $A$, split a vertex $z$ of $B$ into two nonempty neighbor groups, and identify the two results with $x,y$. A putative coloring with different colors on $x,y$ colors the original $A$. Equal colors would color the original $B$. This proves soundness by induction from $K_4$. If the root has degree bound four then each child does too: each of $x,y$ gains at least one neighbor in the split side, so restoring the edge $xy$ cannot increase their degrees above the root bound. Each already has at least two neighbors on its own side, since the critical parent $A$ has minimum degree three. Each can thus have at most two neighbors in the split side, and $z$ has degree at most four. This reasoning applies recursively down an actual 4-Ore decomposition.

A certificate lists the binary composition tree, its $K_4$ leaves, the edge and split choice at each internal node, and the correspondence of each final inequality edge to an original variable. There are $(v-1)/3$ leaves because each composition loses one vertex from the sum of its children. Give every leaf distinct initial vertex names. A node deletes $xy$ and $z$ and redirects $z$'s at most four neighbor incidences. With bounded-degree adjacency slots these updates take constant work per node. Tree intervals of the initial leaf numbers verify that $x,y$ and $z$ belong to the indicated children, and each named vertex must still be live. Reject any oversized intermediate degree. Finally check the named original rows really connect the ports for each final vertex, by grouping these $O(k+1)$ entries and checking connectedness. Extra equalities in the full input can only preserve the obstruction. Row indices and original variable names cost $O(\log(N+2))$ bits each for encoded input length $N$. This gives $O((k+1)\log(N+2))$ guessed bits and $O((k+1)\operatorname{polylog}(N+2))$ verification time with random access. The verifier can accept such a proof without deciding whether its sublist is smallest. In a general sublist the assertion of noncolorability still has to be established.

## Deterministic preprocessing

On the simple quotient immediately return a fixed NO instance for a loop. Peel vertices of degree at most two; each removed vertex can be colored in reverse order. In the resulting graph freeze the set of $h\le k$ degree-four vertices. The other vertices have degree three. For each connected component $D$ induced by these degree-three vertices, examine its blocks. If it is **not** a Gallai tree, discard it. Any coloring outside $D$ leaves at least $\deg_D(x)$ available colors at each $x$, and the degree-choosability theorem extends it to $D$. Different such components have no edges between them. Here a Gallai tree is connected and every block is complete or an odd cycle. A $K_4$ block is an isolated $K_4$ by the degree-three condition, so return a fixed NO when one occurs. [Erdős, Rubin and Taylor, *Choosability in Graphs*](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1980-07.pdf)

Every remaining low-degree component consists of bridges, isolated vertices, and vertex-disjoint odd cycle blocks. Two cyclic blocks meeting at a vertex would give it at least four incident edges. Write $l$ for their total vertex count, $c$ for their number of components, $r$ for their number of cycles and $b_0$ for the total edge boundary to the frozen vertices. There are $l-c+r$ internal edges, $3r\le l$ and $b_0\le4h$. Hence

$$3l=2(l-c+r)+b_0,\qquad l+6c\le3b_0\le12h.$$

The equivalent retained graph has at most $13h-6c$ vertices and $20h-9c$ edges. If no exceptional NO was returned, it is an ordinary maximum-degree-four graph. Rebuild the four-port path presentation on dense names: each frozen vertex uses at most three rows, each of the other vertices at most two, so at most $3h+2l\le27h-12c$ rows are needed. Isolated vertices may simply be omitted. A fixed exceptional NO can be presented by $K_4$ with six variables and eight equality rows. Thus the densely encoded output needs $O((k+1)\log(k+2))$ bits. The original row positions can also be preserved for the single-threshold decision. For each retained quotient vertex keep an original active spanning tree of its whole port component. Each costs at most two rows plus one if it has four ports. There are at most $k$ such extra charges globally; the retained subgraph is still embedded in the contracted result. If the original is satisfiable, every sublist is satisfiable. If it is unsatisfiable, the retained graph is unsatisfiable by the list-extension proof, so its selected trees give an unsatisfiable original sublist. A loop costs at most three rows. For a $K_4$ obstruction this construction uses at most $8+\min(4,k)$ rows. Therefore a deterministic scan produces a verdict-equivalent original sublist of at most $\max(8,27k)$ rows; densely renaming its mentioned variables also gives $O((k+1)\log(k+2))$ encoded bits. It need not attain the existential $7k+8$ bound.

Taking connected components, enumerating blocks, and peeling use linear graph work. One can simplify bounded-degree parallel edges locally. For arbitrary binary variable identifiers group the active row occurrences by radix sorting in $O(N)$ word operations; variables with neither port mentioned are free to take any admissible pair. For word-indexed dense graph input the graph operations cost linear word time. The input can present an obstruction at any position, so this preprocessing still charges a scan. The [separate locally generated analysis](locally-generated-four-port-refutations.md) examines what a small obstruction means on a native hard computation. These results give no unrestricted time separation for SAT.

The audit uses the NetworkX graph atlas to check all 685 simple graphs through seven vertices with maximum degree four. An independent coloring search verifies the kernel verdicts and checks the row sublists for all 85 uncolorable graphs. Six additional bounded-degree Ore compositions and every one of the 260 row deletions over the first eight sharp examples also pass. The cited graph-density and degree-choosability theorems enter the mathematical proof as external results. Priority for the quantitative port-row formulation and kernel constants has not been established.
