# Maximum rank at every pinned cut in a linear-size network

For every $m\ge1$ and every infinite coefficient field $K$, a single trivalent tensor network with $O(m)$ sites and binary wires realizes an $m$-bit tensor with the following property. Pin any exterior bits to any values, and partition the remaining bits into sets $S,T$. The resulting matrix has rank
$$
                            2^{\min(|S|,|T|)}.
$$
The tables in the network are the same for every choice of pins and cut. They can also be chosen over any finite field of size greater than $(2+2\sqrt2)^m$.

For the binary multiplication graph $G(A,B,C)=[AB=C]$, put $m=3d^2$. The control tensor meets or exceeds every flattening-rank lower bound that $G$ could have on every pinned subproblem. Consequently even a hierarchy of these lower bounds, taken together, cannot force more than $O(d^2)$ unrestricted network sites by cut capacity alone. The zero support, relationships between the actual minors, and algebra of prescribed routing are additional information. The small network constructed here has arbitrary scalar tables and need not give a Boolean verdict. [Balanced cuts for $G$](balanced-cuts-of-the-binary-product-graph.md)

## One topology for all tests

Use a bounded-degree directed $m$-superconcentrator with $O(m)$ edges. For any equally large subsets of its inputs and outputs, it provides vertex-disjoint directed paths between those sets. The permutation of their endpoints can depend on the paths. For each exterior bit $i$, make a fresh trivalent terminal with its exterior leg and two wires, one to core input $i$ and one to core output $i$. Use separate input and output ports, adjoining a single private lead if necessary. [Pippenger, *Superconcentrators*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0206022)

Replace any core vertex of degree above three by a tree of trivalent vertices with one port per former incidence. Degrees are bounded and the total size remains $O(m)$. A path entering one port and leaving another now follows the unique internal tree path. Each routed core path was simple, and distinct routed paths used disjoint core vertices. Each visited tree therefore carries one simple internal subpath, and no other routed path uses that tree. The directed paths start and end on distinct chosen terminals and never use an unchosen exterior terminal in their interiors.

Assume $|S|\le |T|$, choose a subset $T'\subseteq T$ of size $|S|$, and route the core inputs of $S$ to the core outputs of $T'$. Extend each path through its endpoint terminals to the exterior legs. On each path vertex impose equality of the two path bits. On all unused internal wires force zero. At unused terminals require the prescribed exterior bit if one was pinned, or freely accept both exterior values if it was left unmatched. These constraints factor into individual local tables. Given matching endpoint values, exactly one internal wire assignment contributes, with weight one. Mismatched endpoints receive zero. Fixing the unmatched column bits to zero selects a square permutation matrix of side $2^{|S|}$. The case of no routed path gives a nonzero scalar minor by the same assignments. This is the binary routing argument of the quantum max-flow/min-cut theorem. [Cui, Freedman, Sattath, Stong and Minton, Theorem 3.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04644)

## One choice of tables

Use a different indeterminate for each entry of each local table. For every pinning and cut, choose the full-size minor from the routing calculation. Its determinant is a nonzero polynomial over $K$, since the corresponding zero-one routing assignment gives determinant $1$ or $-1$. These assignments can differ across tests. The product of the finitely many determinant polynomials is nonzero and hence has a nonvanishing evaluation over every infinite field. That evaluation supplies a common table assignment passing every test.

The same infinite-field argument allows any fixed finite catalogue of tests that attach a nonzero tensor to a discarded set of exterior legs before splitting the remaining legs. The attached tensor can couple all discarded bits. Choose one discarded assignment where its coefficient is nonzero, and force that assignment at the unused terminals in the routing specialization. The chosen minor is then a nonzero scalar times a permutation matrix. Taking the product over the catalogue again gives simultaneous full ranks. This includes summing discarded indices and imposing equalities among them. The finite-field estimate below counts just the pinning tests.

A contracted exterior entry has degree at most one in each table parameter. Each minor for a cut of sizes $s,t$ has individual degrees at most $2^{\min(s,t)}\le(\sqrt2)^{s+t}$. Summing over the four statuses of each exterior bit, pinned zero, pinned one, row, or column, bounds every individual degree of the product by $(2+2\sqrt2)^m$. A nonzero polynomial of individual degrees less than the field size cannot vanish on the whole field grid, by successive univariate interpolation. This proves the stated finite-field bound. Over the rationals the same interpolation gives positive integer gate entries from a set of $2^{O(m)}$ choices.

## Scope at small fields and on Boolean functions

Pinning every exterior bit makes the tested rank the indicator of a nonzero table entry. Maximal rank on all these tests requires full support. For a Boolean-valued tensor this forces the constant-one tensor, which already fails a two-bit cut. In particular a universal maximizing assignment over $\mathbb F_2$ is impossible when $m\ge2$. For each fixed test, the underlying routing topology still supports its maximum rank over that field.

Over $\mathbb F_3$, even arbitrary scalar entries fail at $m\ge4$. Full support makes every value a sign $(-1)^{g(x)}$. Full rank on each pinned two-dimensional face forces the mixed Boolean difference of $g$ on that face to be one. Thus $g(x)-\sum_{i<j}x_ix_j$ has zero mixed differences and is affine over $\mathbb F_2$, as follows from its unique multilinear expansion. Fix four bits and partition them two against two. Removing row-only and column-only signs leaves the phase
$$
                  (-1)^{(x_1+x_2)(x_3+x_4)}.
$$
It has at most two distinct rows. The required rank was four. At $m\le3$ the original all-pairs phase does attain the maximum, since every cut with both sides nonempty has a singleton side and at least one crossing interaction.

This control applies to arguments using lower bounds on the ranks of pinned flattenings and the number of bits carried by a cut. A test that also uses which fully pinned values vanish can distinguish $G$ from the control. A prescribed pairing must likewise be justified independently of the superconcentrator property, which only promises some pairing.

A reproducible finite example contracts four explicit trivalent tables around a four-cycle over $\mathbb F_{1009}$. Direct modular row reduction gives full rank for all $4^4=256$ combinations of pinnings and cuts on that one tensor. The audit also checks the sign-phase examples at three and four bits over $\mathbb F_3$. The general argument is a consequence of classical superconcentration and routing, with finite generic specialization. [Finite audit](../audits/universal_flattening_control_audit.py)

## The determinant restriction loses the list parameter

There is a concrete projection from the binary product graph to nonsingularity. Sum over every bit of $B$, pin $C=I$, and use coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$. For each fixed binary $A$ the result is $\det A$. An invertible $A$ has a unique inverse and a singular $A$ has none. Even if a different $C$ is prescribed for each input, the sum still equals $\det A$. For singular $A$ the number of solutions is either zero or $2^{d(d-\operatorname{rank}A)}$, which is even.

Suppose one tries to obtain the hard list predicate $D_w$ this way, substituting affine binary forms in the entries of $A$. There are $n=2^w$ records of $h=2w$ bits, with $w\ge1$. A simple restriction partitions the records into $n/2$ pairs, assigns the two members of each pair a common $(w-1)$-bit prefix unique to that pair, and fixes the suffix in the first member to zero. The test is the AND of $n/2$ independent nonzero tests on $w+1$ suffix bits. Its multilinear degree over $\mathbb F_2$ is exactly $(n/2)(w+1)$, since each nonzero test has a full-degree monomial. The determinant of a $d$-square affine matrix has degree at most $d$, even after reducing on the Boolean cube. This inverse check already needs side $\Omega(nw)$. The near-input-size parameter for the full graph would have $d^2$ of order $nw$. [List predicate and source network lower bound](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md)

In fact $\deg_{\mathbb F_2}D_w=nh/2=nw$. Sum over all matchings of the record positions, weighting each matching by the product of the equality indicators of its edges. On each assignment, matching counts factor over the classes of equal records. The count $a_s$ for a class of size $s$ satisfies $a_0=a_1=1$ and $a_s=a_{s-1}+(s-1)a_{s-2}$. It is even for every $s\ge2$. The matching sum therefore gives precisely $D_w$ modulo two. A single equality is $\prod_{k=1}^h(1+x_{ik}+x_{jk})$, and a matching has at most $n/2$ edges. This gives the upper degree bound. For the reverse direction, choose $n/2$ different unordered complementary pairs of subsets of $[h]$, available since $n\le2^h$. Assign their $n$ distinct subsets as masks on the $n$ records. The monomial selecting exactly those bits has degree $nh/2$. A contributing matching must be perfect and can only join records with complementary masks. Exactly one matching does this, and its top monomial has coefficient one.

The matching expansion in characteristic two also follows from the classical signed permutation identity for distinctness. A permutation and its inverse give the same equality indicator. The non-involutions cancel in inverse pairs modulo two, leaving exactly the matchings. The complementary-mask argument identifies a top monomial for the binary encoding. [Naslund, distinctness indicator](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcta.2019.105190)

Consequently $d\ge nw$ for the affine determinant projection. The same degree calculation also limits a direct affine projection in which only $s$ entries of $AB=C$ are tested. Its degree is at most $2s$, by multiplying the $s$ binary equality indicators, so it needs $s\ge nw/2$. The latter inequality permits a sufficiently large dense graph at the desired side. Neither calculation limits general tensor contractions with auxiliary constraints.

With $k$ shared Boolean AND gates and otherwise arbitrary affine preprocessing over $\mathbb F_2$, the determinant result has degree at most $d+k$. Initially regard the $k$ gate outputs as independent bits. The determinant has degree at most $d$ in them and the original data. Substitute for one gate output at a time in reverse topological order, reducing to multilinear form after each substitution. Replacing a variable with the product of two affine forms in earlier variables raises degree by at most one. Thus at $d=O(\sqrt{nh})$ this reduction needs $\Omega(nh)$ nonlinear gates, including with unrestricted sharing. A linear-size preprocessing network remains possible under this bound. The finite audit recovers degrees two and eight by a complete Boolean Möbius transform for $w=1,2$. [Audit](../audits/universal_flattening_control_audit.py)
