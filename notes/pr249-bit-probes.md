# Adaptive binary access on a finite permanent grid

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** PR #249 extracts a nonzero polynomial multiple of the permanent graph equation from an equality test on a generic path. On a finite grid, enumerating the possible answers to digital probes would consume the precision budget after reading a single entire matrix entry. Conditioning only on the bits actually inspected gives a different tradeoff. [Pinned proof, especially §§4–6](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/50e44b55f7f10adf6c8aa8f167b31dd5b23c2171/research/bss-permanent-graph/PROOF.md) · [Earlier finite-witness estimate](pr249-witnesses.md)

Choose integers p≥r≥1. Let B=2ᵖ and encode each entry Xᵢⱼ∈{1,…,B} by the p bits of Xᵢⱼ−1. The candidate y is an integer in [0,m!Bᵐ]. A primitive tree may interleave complex arithmetic and equality tests with free adaptive probes of original matrix-input bits. It may choose arbitrary constants and discrete operations from the bit transcript. Its access to y is algebraic. Each execution uses at most T field operations and tests in total. The finite unrolled tree has these resource bounds even on syntactic paths, and actual divisions are defined on promised inputs.

Call a cell exposed once fewer than r of its bits remain unread. Assume every prefix leaves a k-by-k submatrix containing no exposed cells. The row and column sets may change adversarially along the execution. For t=1,…,T let Dₜ bound the total degree of the unreduced numerator for an equality at the t-th field instruction, treating already exposed cells as scalars and the remaining entries and y as variables. The universal bound Dₜ=2ᵗ suffices. For a family of K such trees, suppose an input-independent random seed chooses the trees and their Boolean aggregation. Let Δ be the difference between the worst-case graph acceptance guarantee and the worst-case off-graph acceptance upper bound.

There is a fixed polynomial P such that, writing S̲(perₖ) for the imported all-entry border complexity, one has

    S̲(perₖ) ≤ P(m)(T+1)    or    Δ ≤ 1/B + K(m+1)(Σₜ Dₜ)/2ʳ.

The digital transcript does not incur a factor of two per probed bit in this estimate. An additional b-bit binary witness whose value can depend arbitrarily on the instance still costs K=2ᵇ if its verifier is simply enumerated as in the earlier note.

## A hard polynomial after adaptive exposure

Fix any pattern of exposed entries to their observed values and let fₐ be the permanent polynomial in the remaining entries. Pick an unexposed k-by-k block. Fix every free entry outside it to 1 and substitute λZ for the block. The coefficient of λᵏ is c·perₖ(Z). Indeed, a term of that degree must match every selected row within the selected columns. Its other edges form an arbitrary perfect matching on the complementary rows and columns. Their weights are positive, so their summed weight c is nonzero, including c=1 for an empty complement. This is where the positive grid permits arbitrarily many exposed entries outside the block.

If a nonzero multiple of y−fₐ has an arithmetic program of length 4T+3, apply the PR's characteristic-zero graph-factor bound to get a border circuit for fₐ with at most poly(m)(T+1) entries. It applies to this specialized graph polynomial, of degree at most m. Evaluate that circuit at k+1 distinct scalar choices of λ and interpolate the top coefficient, then multiply by 1/c. Scalar substitutions and repeated evaluations use at most a further polynomial factor in m. Approximating outputs have regular coefficients, so the fixed linear combination specializes to the required coefficient. This proves the first alternative in that case. The argument uses the PR's imported border lower bound and factor theorem. [PR #249, §5](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/50e44b55f7f10adf6c8aa8f167b31dd5b23c2171/research/bss-permanent-graph/PROOF.md#5-from-the-multiple-to-a-compatible-border-circuit)

## Coupling a virtual path to two executions

Sample X with independent uniform entries and Y independently uniform on {1,…,B}. Compare the graph input (X,perₘ(X)) with (X,Y). Fix the seed and one tree. A virtual run follows every digital query using X. When a cell becomes exposed, privately reveal its remaining bits to this run. Its formal field registers carry numerator and denominator polynomials, with the values of exposed cells substituted. Computing these polynomial pairs never requires division by a polynomial, even if an unreduced denominator specializes to zero on a formal path.

For an equality let E be the usual cross-multiplied numerator. The virtual run takes equality exactly when E is identically zero in the remaining variables and y. Conditional on its transcript up to a field test, all unrevealed bits are still independent fair bits. The virtual field decisions are functions only of the previously revealed values and program, so conditioning on them reveals no more information. Each unexposed cell is thus uniform on its own set of at least 2ʳ integers. These sets can differ across cells; they need no algebraic structure.

If E is the zero polynomial, it evaluates to zero on both coupled inputs. Otherwise, if E vanishes after y=fₐ(X), monic division in y makes it a nonzero multiple of y−fₐ. The formal arithmetic prefix with the exposed coordinates and transcript constants fixed computes E in at most 4T+3 division-free operations, since symbolic numerator/denominator pairs use at most four operations per source instruction. The preceding extraction then gives the first alternative. In the remaining case both E and E(X,fₐ(X)) are nonzero. Their total degrees are at most Dₜ and mDₜ. The polynomial zero bound for a product of independently uniform finite sets of size at least 2ʳ bounds the chance of either evaluating to zero by (m+1)Dₜ/2ʳ.

At the first divergence from a real run the virtual and actual paths have agreed so far. All actual divisions on this prefix are defined, hence its evaluated formal denominators are nonzero by induction. A first divergence must therefore be an equality with nonzero generic numerator evaluating to zero. Digital probes on the common prefix always receive identical bits. Sum the conditional bounds over field instructions and take the union over K trees. Finally, (X,Y) is on the graph with probability at most 1/B. This proves the estimate after averaging the seed. The stepwise argument avoids conditioning on earlier events of agreement with the real inputs, which could otherwise invalidate independence.

## Consequence at ordinary bit lengths

If at most q cells are exposed, choose k=⌊m²/(m+q)⌋ rows of smallest exposure count. They contain at most kq/m exposed cells, hence at least m−kq/m≥k columns have no exposed cell in these rows. For fixed 0<δ≤1 and q≤m^(2−δ), this leaves k=Ω(m^δ). The PR's imported lower bound for perₖ exceeds every polynomial in m.

For example, fix u,v≥0 and suppose every test has Dₜ≤mᵘ and K≤mᵛ. Choose r=⌈(u+v+10)log₂m⌉ and p=2r. Every tree may read half the binary digits of **every** matrix entry, and all the digits of up to m^(2−δ) exceptional entries, in adaptive order. The complete fixed-width bit encoding has length L=Θ(m²log m), including y. If T=O(L³), the first alternative eventually fails, and the second yields Δ=o(1). This excludes a cubic field-operation budget at constant gap for this finite-grid model. Iterated squaring can violate the polynomial-degree premise. An unrestricted bit RAM can read the other half of every cell, read the bits of y, and continue computing. No reduction of ordinary SAT to or from the resulting restricted lower bound has been proved here.

## A fixed binary candidate value and one hidden rational entry

There is a variant for the original permanent graph that permits unrestricted binary access to the candidate value. Set the candidate to the public integer C=m!Bᵐ+1 on *both* distributions. A NO matrix U consists of independent uniform integers in {1,…,B}. For a uniform secret position j independent of U write perₘ=aUⱼ+b, with a and b evaluated on the other cells. Form the YES matrix by replacing just Uⱼ by (C−b)/a. Here a≥1, 0≤b<C and both numerator and denominator have O(m(log B+log m)) bits. Each coordinate gets the same fixed-width rational encoding, with denominator one for ordinary integers and a fixed canonical representation for the replacement. Thus length metadata does not identify its position. This statement concerns rational matrix entries with finite binary encodings.

Suppose a primitive tree may inspect all the bits of any q adaptively chosen matrix cells, with arbitrary algebraic access to every entry as before. Use the virtual run on the digits of U with candidate C. Its transcript and its set of digitally inspected cells are independent of j, so the chance it probes the replacement is at most q/m². Before this event, the observed entries and the virtual decisions are the same in the two coupled runs, except possibly for the equality-test errors bounded below. For unprobed j and fixed virtual observations write f for the partially specialized permanent and f=a(X')Xⱼ+b(X').

The polynomial F=f−C is irreducible over the complex numbers. Its constant term c is negative because evaluating every remaining variable at zero yields at most m!Bᵐ, and every other coefficient is nonnegative. The unprobed j has a positive cofactor polynomial, so F is nonconstant. If F=GH with both factors nonconstant, multiaffinity forces the sets of variables of G and H to be disjoint, since degrees in each variable add. Both constant terms are nonzero. Pick one nonconstant monomial from each, with supports A and D respectively. Their corresponding pure coefficients F_A and F_D in F are nonzero and hence positive. The mixed coefficient must be F_A F_D/c<0, contradicting nonnegativity.

At a virtual equality with ambient-nonzero numerator E, substitute Xⱼ=(C−b)/a. If the result is identically zero, then f−C divides E by the preceding irreducibility and polynomial division over the fraction field. Use the **general** characteristic-zero irreducible factor theorem cited in §7 of the PR to extract a border circuit for f−C of size poly(m)(T+1). The untouched block and interpolation argument then recovers perₖ. If the substitution is nonzero, multiplying it by a to the power Dₜ clears denominators and gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most (m+1)Dₜ. Indeed each monomial of E has degree at most Dₜ and each of a,b has degree at most m. The NO evaluation has degree at most Dₜ. Conditional on the virtual prefix and an unprobed j, the other undecoded entries are independent uniform grid integers. Summing the first-divergence bounds gives, for one primitive tree,

    S̲(perₖ) ≤ poly(m)(T+1)    or    Δ ≤ q/m² + (m+2)(Σₜ Dₜ)/B,
    where k = ⌊m²/(m+q)⌋.

For K primitive trees, charge the number Q of distinct cells in the **union** of their virtual probe sets, so Q≤min(m²,Kq) always works. The probability that any tree sees the pivot is at most Q/m² for a worst-case union bound Q. The equality-error term is multiplied by K, and the same individual-tree value of k suffices for the factor extraction. The probability includes all seed choices independent of the sampled inputs. The estimate grants full access to the bits of the candidate value. For a single tree with q≤m^(2−δ), Dₜ≤mᵘ and B a polynomial-sized power of two at least m^(u+15), even T=O(L³) gives gap o(1) on these finite rational encodings, where L=O(m³(log B+log m)). Locating the changed cell by reading all coordinates is allowed on the unrestricted RAM. Clearing denominators converts rational graph instances into integer ones, but doing so can spread information about that changed cell to many matrix entries or to the candidate value. The hybrid probe bound is therefore not preserved automatically by denominator clearing.
