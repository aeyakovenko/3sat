# How many assignments witness every SAT threshold?

Khanna, Putterman and Song use maxima of fixed subsets under a common order to count minimum-spanning-tree histories. For SAT such an order exists if the elements being ordered are **total Boolean assignments**. This gives a sharp count of witnesses one can keep, even when different thresholds and pins require different witnesses. [DLMST, Lemmas 3.9–3.10](https://github.com/Louiealbp/DLMST/blob/0072335716b145032b418760883daed2a89809db/final.pdf)

Designate $v$ variables on which queries may pin at most $p$ bits, with $0\le p\le v$. A stream has $m$ clauses on these and any auxiliary variables. Queries specify a prefix length $0\le t\le m$ and a consistent partial assignment. A list chosen after seeing the entire stream, before the query, answers YES if a listed full assignment satisfies the pins and the prefix. For every stream, a list of size
$$
               D=\sum_{i=0}^p \binom vi
$$
suffices. Some width-three clause streams with $O(v(p+1)+1)$ clauses force this many distinct witnesses. Both statements count stored assignments. They give no lower bound on the size of a program that generates those assignments on demand.

## A shared order and a basis

Give each full assignment $a$ its lifetime $L(a)$, the largest prefix it satisfies. This exists since the empty prefix is true. For each set of pins $q$ the last satisfiable time equals $\max_{a\supset q} L(a)$. All menus $\{a:a\supset q\}$ are fixed before the clauses are known. Break lifetime ties arbitrarily in one common total order.

Visit assignments in decreasing lifetime. The row of $a$ over $\mathbb F_2$ contains the evaluations at its designated coordinates of the $D$ squarefree monomials of degree at most $p$, including one. Save $a$ if its row is outside the linear span of the saved rows. There can be at most $D$ saves. For any $q$ for which a witness survives to $t$, consider one such witness $a$. The pin indicator is the product of the appropriate $x_i$ and $1+x_i$, so it pairs to one with the row of $a$. If the witness was skipped, write its row as a binary sum of earlier saved rows. At least one summand pairs to one with the same indicator, giving a saved witness for $q$ whose lifetime is at least $L(a)\ge t$. The listed assignments thus preserve every positive answer at its original threshold. An arbitrary suffix of the original stream need never be evaluated by the query procedure to test a known lifetime. Constructing the basis this way can enumerate every full assignment.

## A linear-size clause stream forcing the exact bound

Use $v$ designated bits and an acyclic Boolean circuit whose wires $T_{i,r}$ record whether at least $r$ ones occur among the first $i$ bits. Include $1\le r\le p+1$, use constants $T_{i,0}=1$, $T_{0,r}=0$ for $r>0$, and compute
$$
 T_{i,r}=T_{i-1,r}\lor(x_i\land T_{i-1,r-1}).
$$
Print the gate equivalences as clauses of width at most three. Initial constants may be enforced by unit clauses. Then append in this order $\neg T_{v,p+1},\neg T_{v,p},\ldots,\neg T_{v,1}$. The marked prefix ending in $\neg T_{v,r+1}$ permits exactly the assignments of data weight at most $r$. Every data vector satisfying that bound has a unique completion of all live counter wires. This is the familiar sequential cardinality counter. [Sinz, Boolean cardinality constraints](https://www.carstensinz.de/papers/CP-2005.pdf)

For each $r$-subset $S$, pin its $r$ variables to one at this marked prefix. The query is satisfiable and every witness has **exactly** $S$ as its data support. Thus the queries for all $S$ of sizes zero through $p$ require $D$ different assignments, even if their auxiliary bits are free to vary in a proposed list. Width exactly three with distinct variables, if desired, follows by replacing a short clause by its constant-size block of all sign choices on fresh padding variables and using block boundaries. The designated pin variables stay the same. With $p=3$ and growing $v$, the stream has $O(v)$ clauses and $O(v\log v)$ encoded bits. Its required list has $1+v+\binom v2+\binom v3$ members. Each of the displayed positive queries has an easy on-demand witness determined by its pins.

For $1\le p\le3$, the same $D$ can be forced with **no auxiliary variables** if the clause stream may have $D$ entries. Use the [exact-width chain](prefix-certificates-for-cnf.md#the-exact-number-of-clauses-that-can-matter-at-their-own-thresholds) of $D$ clauses of width $p$ on the data. Each new clause has a falsifying assignment satisfying all earlier clauses. Just before the clause arrives, query its $p$ falsifying pins. Every answer witness there must fail this new clause and cannot survive to any later marked query. The witnesses of these $D$ queries are pairwise distinct. The sequential counter achieves the same obstruction using a much shorter stream and extra variables.

There is an exact randomized version. A procedure may choose a random list of at most $h$ witnesses in advance and promises to report a positive answer only by finding one in its list. The best success probability guaranteed simultaneously *per positive query* in the worst stream is $\min(1,h/D)$. For the guarantee, sample $\min(h,s)$ different members uniformly from a valid list of size $s\le D$. Each true query has at least one member that would certify it. In the counter stream, each fixed list hits at most $h$ of the $D$ displayed queries on account of their incompatible data supports. Average first over these queries and then over the procedure's randomness. This covers arbitrary correlated choices of the $h$ witnesses. It also shows how a sampler with permission to depend on the query could escape the list bound.

## What changes if the query specifies only one possible threshold?

For a single fixed formula let $F$ be its projection onto the $v$ designated bits. A witness list amounts to selecting points from $F$ meeting every subcube of at most $p$ pins that intersects $F$, with one satisfying lift per selected point. The extremal minimum list size over all $F$ is exactly the maximum size of an **irredundant family of codimension-$p$ subcubes** of the Boolean $v$-cube. Here irredundant means each member contains a vertex belonging to none of the other members.

Indeed, in an inclusion-minimal list, each selected point has a query hit by that point alone among the list. Shrink the corresponding subcube to codimension $p$ through that point by setting additional pins if necessary. The resulting private points certify irredundancy. Conversely, given any irredundant family, take $F$ to be a set of private points, one per member. Each corresponding query forces its own point in every list. Any finite $F$ can be realized by a Boolean membership circuit and width-three Tseitin clauses with auxiliaries. An empty $F$ simply needs no witnesses.

Meshulam's theorem gives the upper bound $2^v\binom vp /\sum_{i=0}^{v-p}\binom vi$. For fixed $p$ and large $v$ this is less than $\binom vp+1$, since the missing tail in the denominator is polynomial in $v$. The weight-at-most-$p$ counter and the positive $p$-pins give the matching lower bound. Thus the single-threshold extremum is $\binom vp$ for fixed $p$ and large $v$, while allowing every prefix increases it by exactly $\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\binom vi$ for $p\ge1$. This single-threshold step uses a known extremal theorem. [Ellis, proof of Meshulam's bound, Theorem 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.2960)

## An exact characterization with only $L$ permitted thresholds

Fix $L\ge1$ possible prefix lengths in increasing order and take the worst case over clause streams and such choices. On the data cube, color an initial set of vertices. In each parallel round color the last uncolored vertex of every codimension-$p$ face with exactly one uncolored vertex at the start of that round. Let $s_L(v,p)$ be the fewest initially colored vertices needed to color the whole cube within $L$ rounds. The worst-case minimum witness-list size for the $L$ thresholds is exactly
$$
                         2^v-s_L(v,p).
$$

To prove the upper bound, take a minimum list preserving the queries at these thresholds. Where several full assignments have the same data vector, keep the one surviving longest. One can likewise replace a listed assignment by the longest-lived lift of its data. Write $W$ for the resulting distinct data vectors. Each $w\in W$ is needed by some positive query it alone witnesses, or it could be dropped. Let $i$ be the last allowed threshold at which its lift survives. The private query occurred at threshold $i$ or earlier. Refine its pins to exactly $p$ using values of $w$. Every other listed data vector in this face must have expired before $i$. Start with all of $W^c$ colored. Inductively by round $i$ all points with last surviving threshold $i$ can be colored, so $s_L(v,p)\le 2^v-|W|$.

Conversely start with an optimal seed. At threshold $i$ prescribe the data domain to be the set of vertices still uncolored just before round $i$. These domains shrink and can be realized by a single stream. For example print definitions of width-two-fan-in Boolean circuits for all domain indicators, then append the output pins in order. Their width-three gate equivalences have one common lift per data vector satisfying the indicators pinned so far. For every point first colored in round $i$, the face that colors it intersects domain $i$ only in that point. The corresponding pin query forces a separate witness. All $2^v-s_L(v,p)$ data vectors outside the seed are needed. Realizing an arbitrary prescribed domain this way can take exponential space on the page.

For two thresholds and two pins the extremum is $v+\binom v2$ for $v\ge3$. The extra one available at three thresholds cannot be attained at two. [Two-threshold proof](two-threshold-pair-pins.md)

For $p=v-1$ and $v\ge1$ the faces are edges. A seed of covering radius at most $L$ is necessary and sufficient, since in one round the process travels exactly one step in Hamming distance. With four designated variables, three pins and two thresholds, two antipodal seeds suffice. A single radius-two ball contains only eleven of the sixteen vertices. The optimum list size is exactly fourteen. At one threshold Meshulam's formula above applies, while $L\ge p+1$ already permits the counter construction achieving $D$. In general, the unlimited-round minimum seed size $2^v-D=\sum_{i=0}^{v-p-1}\binom vi$ for positive-dimensional faces is the Boolean case of an existing theorem of Balogh, Bollobás, Morris and Riordan. This supplies independent prior art for the unrestricted numerical extremum. For $p=v$ a singleton face can be colored from the empty seed. [Balogh et al., Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.1410)

## On a genuine hard computation all pinned verdicts can agree while the list sizes diverge

Take the [unconditionally hard bit $b_j$ of the fixed native RAM](priority-pinned-sat.md) and its [near-native, uniquely satisfiable gate-table formula](projected-parity-holes.md). Introduce $v=j+4$ fresh designated data bits. Append the definitions of a cardinality counter and enforce one implication: when the computed bit is one, the data must have weight exactly two. For instance use the two width-two clauses $\neg b_j\lor T_{v,2}$ and $\neg b_j\lor\neg T_{v,3}$ after the gate equivalences. Here $b_j$ in the formula is the genuine output variable of the checked computation, never a hardwired guess. The added clauses have polynomial-in-$j$ size and the whole stream is still locally generated. The restricted data assignments have one full satisfying lift each.

Every partial assignment on at most two designated bits extends to a data word of weight two when $v\ge4$. Consequently **every allowed query is satisfiable at every prefix for both signs**. The optimal honest witness-list size over all prefixes equals the optimum at the final formula, because a list covering all pins at the final threshold automatically covers all earlier ones.

* If $b_j=1$, every positive pair of pins forces its own two-element data support. Conversely all such supports cover every permitted pin, so the optimum is exactly $\binom v2$.
* If $b_j=0$, the data range over the full Boolean cube. Two constant words and, for each binary digit of the data index $0,\ldots,v-1$, its row of digit values and the complementary row cover every two-pin pattern. Two distinct indices differ in some digit. The optimum is at most $2\lceil\log_2 v\rceil+2$.

At all sufficiently large stages the ranges are disjoint. A sound procedure that distinguishes even one specified side of this gap from the public stage on infinitely many occasions encounters the native sign detector lower bound: every sufficiently late success costs $\Omega_D(2^j)$ deterministic native steps for the fixed detector. This is a cost of learning an optimum from succinct metadata. In particular a two-sided factor-$\rho$ estimate of the optimum would distinguish the two branches when $\rho^2(2\lceil\log_2 v\rceil+2)<\binom v2$. An unrestricted solver can answer every one of the designated SAT queries by the constant YES on this promise. A single final query setting three different data bits to one is satisfiable exactly on the free-data branch, which shows precisely where the pin limit matters. Even unlimited access to those **decision answers alone** gives the same observations on the two sides. This example distinguishes a mathematical obstacle to constructing witnesses from the actual goal of deciding satisfiability.

The same construction with weight exactly $p$ works for each fixed $p\ge2$ and $v\ge2p$. All $\binom vp$ weight-$p$ words are forced in that branch. In the full-cube branch $O_p(\log v)$ honest assignments suffice: a uniform data word matches any given partial assignment with probability at least $2^{-p}$, and a union bound over $\sum_{i\le p} 2^i\binom vi$ such patterns proves existence. Auxiliary count gates keep the clauses of width at most three.

The comparison has an exact fractional form. Assign nonnegative mass to full assignments and require mass at least one witnessing each allowed query. In the free-data branch the minimum total mass is exactly $2^p$. The $2^p$ patterns on any fixed $p$ coordinates partition the final models; conversely total mass $2^p$ distributed uniformly on the data and lifted to satisfying assignments suffices. In the weight-$p$ branch the $\binom vp$ all-positive $p$-pin queries require disjoint final witnesses. Mass one per support suffices. Equivalently, the optimal worst-query probability of success from one random preselected witness is exactly $2^{-p}$ or $1/\binom vp$ according to the actual native bit, even though every requested SAT verdict is YES. The argument holds for $v\ge2p$ with $p\ge1$ even when $p$ grows. With $p=\lfloor v/3\rfloor$, the ratio of fractional optima is $\Theta((3/2)^v/\sqrt v)$ by Stirling's bounds, and the counter still costs only $O(v^2)$ gates. For integral lists, the same uniform sampling proof gives at most $\lceil2^p(v\ln3+1)\rceil$ free-data representatives, since there are at most $3^v$ pin patterns in all.

At a single fixed threshold this type of coverage is studied under constrained covering arrays. Equivalently, pins isolating a data vector in a residual family constitute a teaching set. Without a bound on the number of removal rounds, Samei and coauthors already gave the corresponding Sauer-type upper bound for recursive teaching dimension by an algebraic argument. [Samei et al., algebraic bounds for teaching complexity](https://www2.cs.uregina.ca/~zilles/sameiSYZ12.pdf) Priority for the SAT witness formulation and its bounded-threshold equivalence is unverified. A fast SAT proof would need a way to construct or certify a relevant representative at an unknown hard threshold. Giving the solver the full formula allows it to exploit the counter structure in the example.
