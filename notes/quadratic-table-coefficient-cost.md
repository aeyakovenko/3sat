# Coefficient size in a quadratic-table proof of a native output

Suppose a proposed history check supplies signed tables for all its Boolean columns, as in the [quadratic-table verifier](quadratic-lookup-tables-for-native-traces.md). An explicit native computation can force a large product of the number of tables and their total length, even if the verifier permits arbitrarily many different observation maps and overlapping supports. The bound depends on the mean-square size of the table entries. This dependence matters because the verifier permits integers with a full word of bits.

Let $q=2^m$ and let $f(u,v,w)=(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(uvw)}$ on the uniform binary coordinates of $\mathbb F_q^3$. Represent a real function $g$ as a sum of $R$ blocks $1_{H_i} A_i(L_i x,Q_i(x))$. Here $H_i$ is affine, $L_i$ has $r_i$ affine output bits, and $Q_i$ is any binary quadratic. Write $s_i=2^{r_i+1}$, $S=\sum_i s_i$, and $K_i=(\sum_z A_i(z)^2/s_i)^{1/2}$. For either fixed sign $\sigma$, the condition $\mathbb E|g-\sigma f|\le\epsilon<1$ implies

$$
  1-\epsilon\ \le\ \frac{2q-1}{q^2}\sum_i \sqrt{s_i}\,K_i
             \ \le\ \frac{2q-1}{q^2}\sqrt{S\sum_i K_i^2}.
$$

In particular, if every table has root mean square at most $K$, then $K^2 RS\ge (1-\epsilon)^2 q^4/(2q-1)^2$. For exact sign columns with entries bounded by one, **the optimal order of $RS$ is $q^2$**. The upper bound uses $q$ tables with two entries each. On $u=a$ use the phase $(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(avw)}$ with the desired sign. These disjoint supports partition the domain.

For the lower bound, expand $A_i$ in its normalized binary Walsh basis. Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the sum of the absolute Fourier coefficients by $\sqrt{s_i}K_i$. After composition, each character in this expansion becomes a quadratic phase. The indicator of a nonempty affine subspace is the average of the signed affine characters from its defining equations. Its coefficients have absolute sum one, so multiplying by the indicator preserves the preceding upper bound. The zero indicator can simply be discarded. Every quadratic phase has absolute correlation at most $(2q-1)/q^2$ with $f$. Thus the first right-hand side bounds $|\mathbb E[fg]|$. The approximation assumption gives $\mathbb E[\sigma fg]\ge 1-\epsilon$. Cauchy–Schwarz over the blocks proves the last inequality. For a Boolean column representing $(1-\sigma f)/2$, pass to $1-2g$ using one constant block and double the remaining entries. This changes the exact bound only by constant factors for bounded nonzero coefficients. [Exact quadratic correlation, with a field-specific proof](trace-product-quadratic-correlation.md) · [Kolokotronis and Limniotis, Corollary 1](https://eprint.iacr.org/2011/212) · [Walsh expansion and Parseval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.10386)

The coefficient bound against individual quadratic phases also has a matching decomposition. The minimum total absolute coefficient in a real linear combination of unrestricted quadratic phases equal to $f$ is exactly $1/\beta$ for $\beta=(2q-1)/q^2$. The correlation inequality proves the lower bound. For the upper bound, $\mathbb E_z f(x)f(x+z)=\beta f(x)$ at every $x$. For each fixed $z$, the product on the left is a quadratic phase, since it takes a derivative of a cubic. This decomposition permits real coefficients and uses up to $q^3$ terms. It optimizes a different cost than the number of entries in an integer chart. The symmetry argument is related to extent and fidelity for Clifford magic states. [Bravyi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00128)

## A bound allowing entries of arbitrary magnitude

Suppose the same chart approximates $\sigma f$ with root mean square error at most a fixed $\delta<1$, and allow arbitrary complex entries. Then

$$
                        S=\Omega_\delta(m^2/\log(m+1)).
$$

View each function as a vector with amplitudes given by its values divided by $\sqrt{q^3}$. Each block's Walsh expansion gives at most $s_i$ multiples of stabilizer vectors. Indeed every nonzero $1_H(-1)^Q$ is a scalar times a stabilizer state, using the uniform superposition on an affine subspace with a quadratic phase. Thus the whole chart belongs to the span $V$ of at most $S$ such vectors.

Every normalized qubit stabilizer is, up to global phase, supported on an affine subspace $H$ of density $p$ and has phase $(-1)^Q i^\ell$ there. In affine coordinates, $Q$ is binary quadratic and $\ell$ can be taken to be an affine Boolean bit. For the usual mod-four linear exponent, its low bit is affine and the high bit is quadratic: adding bits contributes pairwise products to the carry. Both polynomials on affine coordinates extend to the ambient binary space by an affine left inverse. Set $a=\mathbb E [f1_H(-1)^Q]$ and $b=\mathbb E[f1_H(-1)^{Q+\ell}]$. Both are real of magnitude at most $\beta$ by the affine-indicator expansion. The complex unnormalized correlation has magnitude $|(1+i)a/2+(1-i)b/2|=\sqrt{(a^2+b^2)/2}\le\beta$; it is also at most $p$. Conjugating the phase gives the same bound. The squared normalized overlap is at most $\min(p,\beta^2/p)\le\beta$. Conversely the uniform state on $u=0$ has squared overlap $1/q$. Thus the largest squared overlap is within a factor two of $1/q$. [Dehaene and De Moor on stabilizer normal form](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0304125)

By the Sinha–Lovitz stabilizer rank–extent inequality, the squared projection of a unit vector onto a span of $S$ stabilizers is at most $2^{O(\sqrt{S\log(S+1)})}$ times its largest squared stabilizer overlap. Here approximation gives squared projection at least $1-\delta^2$. Since $\beta<2^{1-m}$ this forces the stated lower bound (if $S>m^2$ it already holds). Large table entries cannot remove this polylogarithmic obstruction. The imported projection inequality does not give an exponential lower bound on $S$. [Sinha and Lovitz, Theorem 1.1 and §1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06819v1)

## Exact stabilizer fidelity and its equality cases

For $m\ge2$ the largest squared stabilizer overlap is exactly $1/q$. The maximizers, up to global phase, are precisely the normalized restrictions of $f$ to the affine planes of $K^3$ over $K=\mathbb F_q$. This also gives stabilizer extent $q$, where extent is the square of the minimum absolute coefficient sum in a decomposition into normalized stabilizers.

Indeed $p<1/q$ gives strict inequality from the support bound. For $p\ge4/q$ we have $\beta^2/p<1/q$. Apart from $p=1/q$, the only remaining dyadic support density is $2/q$. Write this affine support as $x_0+W$ with $\dim_{\mathbb F_2}W=2m+1$. Write $F=\operatorname{Tr}(uvw)$ and choose any binary quadratic $Q$. The square of the conditional mean of $(-1)^{F+Q}$ on the support is the average over $z\in W$ of the conditional means of its derivative signs. For $z\ne0$, the polar form of $D_z(F+Q)$ on the ambient space is $(s,t)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\det(z,s,t))$. Its radical is $Kz$, since for $s\notin Kz$ the determinant runs through all of $K$ as $t$ varies. The quotient by $Kz$ is symplectic of binary dimension $2m$. If $t_z=\dim_{\mathbb F_2}(W\cap Kz)$, the image of $W$ has dimension $2m+1-t_z$. Its restricted rank is at least $2(m+1-t_z)$, since its radical is contained in its symplectic orthogonal complement. The affine translate changes no polar rank. [Derivative and quadratic bias calculation](trace-product-quadratic-correlation.md)

Thus every nonzero derivative sign has absolute conditional mean at most $1/2$, by the quadratic Gauss sum. When $Kz\not\subset W$ the bound is $1/4$. The set $C=\{z:Kz\subset W\}$ is a $K$-linear subspace inside $W$. Its binary dimension is a multiple of $m$, hence at most $2m$ for $m\ge2$. At least half the shifts therefore give the stronger bound. Including $z=0$ gives a squared conditional mean at most $3/8+1/(2|W|)<1/2$. For the complex phase the square is the average of the squares for the two real phases above. The squared normalized overlap at $p=2/q$ is consequently strictly below $p/2=1/q$.

Equality now requires $p=1/q$ and agreement with $f$ everywhere on $x_0+W$ up to one scalar of unit magnitude, by equality in the triangle inequality. In particular the binary polynomial $F$ restricts to a quadratic there: the imaginary bit of the stabilizer phase must be constant for it to agree with a real sign. Here $W$ has dimension $2m$. Vanishing of each third difference forces the restricted polar form of $D_zF$ to have rank zero for $z\in W$. Its rank lower bound is now $2(m-t_z)$, so $Kz\subset W$ for every nonzero $z\in W$. Thus $W$ itself is a $K$-plane. Conversely every triple of directions in a $K$-plane has determinant zero. All third differences vanish, and the restricted Boolean polynomial has degree at most two. This proves the equality classification. For $m=1$ the state is the three-qubit CCZ state and its squared overlap with the constant phase is $9/16>1/2$. [Bravyi et al., stabilizer extent and CCZ](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.00128)

The inner product of the target with an exact decomposition gives coefficient absolute sum at least $\sqrt q$. Any parallel class of $q$ planes attains this bound, using coefficient $1/\sqrt q$ on each normalized restriction. Moreover, every minimizing decomposition is a mixture of parallel classes after adjusting global phases and merging identical vectors. Equality of the coefficient bound forces each summand to maximize overlap and align with the target. Factoring out the target's pointwise sign gives weights $a_H\ge0$ with $\sum_{H\ni x} a_H=1$ for every $x$. For each parallel class, its contribution is a function of one nonzero $K$-linear form. Condition on that form. Every form from another parallel class is uniform under this conditioning, because the two are linearly independent. Their expectations are constant, forcing the first class's function to be constant too. Thus the weights are equal across every plane of each class and the class weights sum to one. The minimum number of terms among these minimizing decompositions is $q$. A decomposition minimizing the number of terms can have larger coefficient mass.

## A column containing the actual output

Take the ordinary priority calculation of $b_j$ in $O(M)$ native steps, $M=2^j$. Choose $m=\lfloor j/3\rfloor$, so $M/4\le q^3\le M$. Extend the fixed program as follows. In a fixed dyadic time window, run the priority calculation and set up multiplication and absolute-trace tables for $\mathbb F_q$. Choose the first irreducible monic binary polynomial of degree $m$. Testing all $q$ candidates by division by all monics through degree $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$ takes $O(q^{3/2}\operatorname{poly}(j))$ word steps. Polynomial multiplication and reduction for every pair takes $O(q^2\operatorname{poly}(j))$ steps in total, including time to evaluate the trace of every element by repeated squaring. This is $o(M)$. The finitely many small stages can use a fixed convention. Work arrays for the extension can use a separate tagged bank. A fixed program can be run for a prescribed power-of-two number of macro-steps by padding each instruction's wrapper to a constant power of two and idling after halt.

At a dyadically aligned boundary, enumerate the $q^3$ triples in binary order. Each iteration takes a constant power-of-two number of native ticks, including two table lookups for multiplication, the trace lookup, XOR with $b_j$ and a write of the resulting bit $h$ to the entry for that triple. The masks and shifts to split the loop index are fixed during this window. Return the entry for the zero triple as the program's output. The computation still reports $b_j$ in $O(M)$ native time. On the fixed write slot, the genuine chronological data bit satisfies $1-2h=(-1)^{b_j} f$. Fixing the high window bits and the low instruction and slot bits of the chronological row index gives an affine copy of the $3m$ variable bits. Restriction to this copy pulls back an affine support, an affine observation and a quadratic to objects of the same types, preserving the supplied table and its root mean square. The bound therefore applies to every chart of this column on the whole history. [Native priority computation](priority-pinned-sat.md) · [Chronological row and slot layout](ordered-diagrams-for-native-checksums.md)

For this output-preserving extension, a chart for the Boolean column with $RS\le M^{1/k}$ must contain a table with root mean square $\Omega(M^{1/3-1/(2k)})$ for $k=2,3$, taking $R,S\ge1$. These are powers $1/12$ and $1/6$ respectively. The word-sized coefficients allowed by the verifier can reach these levels. Its same $RS$ running-time guarantee does not establish a lower bound on the cost of an arbitrary verifier or even rule out a faster check of these specific tables. A prover who uses the program's semantics can ignore the nonzero triples altogether and certify the original $b_j$. Obtaining such a certificate within the rooted clock on infinitely many genuine stages remains the missing implication. The argument gives no superlinear SAT bound and makes no historical priority claim for the chart tradeoff.
