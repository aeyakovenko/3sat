# Can a short list catch one of two addresses?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** There is an ordinary language in deterministic time 2^{O(n)} with exactly two yes strings at each length n≥2, one in either half of the address space, with the following property. Suppose a fixed nondeterministic 2ⁿ poly(n)-time predicate defines lists Lₙ of n-bit strings. If, on an infinite sequence, Lₙ contains at least one of the two yes strings and |Lₙ|/2ⁿ tends to zero, then cubic SAT on the unrestricted word RAM is impossible. Lists may contain false positives, the good lengths need not be recognizable, and the density may converge arbitrarily slowly. The construction exposes where a proof would have to obtain information about an actual computation.

## Two marks and a list that can be mostly wrong

Use an efficient deterministic universal multitape machine U with n furnished as a separate input. A description consists of a self-delimiting **fixed decoder** followed by an arbitrary binary payload with a known end. Include direct simulation of ordinary fixed multitape programs with logarithmic-factor or polynomial overhead of degree at most two. At n≥2, let d=2(n−1). Choose the first length-d string w omitted by all runs of U(1ⁿ,p) that halt within 2^(20n) steps and have a raw description |p|<d. There are at most 2ᵈ−1 such descriptions. Exhaustive simulation and selection are uniform in deterministic 2^{O(n)} tape time, even by repeated scans. Small lengths can be specified separately. Write w=xy with |x|=|y|=n−1. Mark 0x and 1y. This is the time-bounded incompressibility argument applied to the **joint** address string. The proof of Lemma 5 of Antunes et al. explicitly selects the lexicographically first time-bounded incompressible string with a different cutoff. Here the new question is how to hit a selected address within a much shorter witness clock. [Antunes et al., Lemma 5](https://publications.hse.ru/pubs/share/folder/41na4ak4ou/200942046.pdf) [Buhrman, Fortnow and Laplante](https://doi.org/10.1137/S009753979834388X) · [Allender, Avoiding Simplicity is Complex](https://people.cs.rutgers.edu/~allender/papers/simplicity.pdf)

Under a cubic SAT hypothesis, a deterministic tape decoder can test membership in Lₙ in 2^(6n+O(log n)) steps. Compile the fixed bounded nondeterministic computation into quasilinear-size explicit 3-CNF, run cubic SAT, and simulate the deterministic RAM on tapes with at most quadratic overhead and polylogarithmic word costs. A fixed polynomial factor in the original verifier clock gives a polynomial factor in the result. Enumerate the 2ⁿ candidates separately, so the total tape time is 2^(7n+O(log n)). Even a quadratic universal simulation lies within 2^(20n). This calculation charges for writing every formula and allows the SAT decider unrestricted workspace. [Quasilinear SAT completeness, Theorem 3.1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf)

At a good length, describe w using one bit to choose a marked half, the other mark's n−1 address bits, and the zero-based index of a genuine mark in the lexicographic list. The index takes ⌈log₂ |Lₙ|⌉ bits, including zero bits for a singleton. The fixed decoder reads its final field to the end of the description. Total length is at most n+⌈log₂ |Lₙ|⌉+O(1). If the density tends to zero, the saving versus d tends to infinity. This contradicts the construction at every sufficiently advanced good length. The same reasoning gives a constant lower bound on list density depending on the list decider whenever a sufficiently fast deterministic list hits a mark. For the stronger one-sided premise that a verifier accepts only genuine marks at those lengths and accepts at least one, it is enough to find a single accepted address by n prefix-existence queries under cubic SAT.

The marks can also represent the earlier parity proposal. For an arbitrary 2(n−1)-bit mask (a,b), give its query normal value a·z at position 0z and b·z at position 1z, over the binary field. On the two-mark table its answer is exactly a·x+b·y. Every independent family of these normals has perfectly uniform answers when the two addresses are uniform. For a fixed polytime-computable advice length A(n), a fixed fast verifier giving h sound independent rows can be converted under cubic SAT into a description of length d−h+A(n)+O(1). Scan all at most 2ᵈ masks and both signs, evaluate the verifier on each, and row-reduce a consistent affine system. Keep the advice and the bits on its free columns. Choose h to be the rank of the whole accepted menu. This requires 2^(8n+O(log n)) tape steps before universal simulation and still fits the cutoff. Thus any unbounded excess h−A(n) would suffice. The field-valued moment construction addresses untagged points and a narrower query family. [Untagged moments](advised-claims.md)

There is a one-mark endpoint. Choose w directly at length d=n by the same rule and mark only w. A list hitting this mark with density approaching zero would compress it using just its index plus the fixed decoder. This simplification exhibits the cost of the construction: recognizing its unique missing output is itself the unresolved task. Merely guessing a short description proves that a candidate **was** output, giving the opposite sign. Exhaustively validating a claim that it was omitted by all short programs exceeds the proposed witness clock.

## The actual first hole has many late predecessors at every scale

Lexicographic minimality gives information specific to w. Every length-d string preceding it has a description of fewer than d bits running within the long cutoff B=2^(20n). This suggests a candidate witness: give executions printing the predecessors in a short window, perhaps just the ones still missing after a fast preliminary enumeration. The following calculation tests that idea on the actual U and w, for either d=n or d=2(n−1).

Use the intermediate cutoff F=2^(2n). Call a length-d output fast if a description shorter than d prints it within F steps of U and halts. Fix a uniformly computable suffix width k=k(n)≤d, with polynomial-time evaluation, and let a be w's prefix of length d−k. Enumerate in order the length-d strings starting with a that have no fast description. This can be done by a fixed decoder inside the long cutoff, even on our choice of universal simulator: simulate fewer than 2ᵈ programs for F steps of the fixed machine U, sort the output records on tapes, and merge with the sorted list of candidates. This takes 2^(4n+O(log n)) native tape steps for both d choices. Even a quadratic universal slowdown leaves slack. Use the convention that an output counts when the generating program halts, at both cutoffs.

Let c exceed the fixed decoder prefix length by one. With a payload consisting of the d−k prefix bits and a rank of k−c bits, this decoder has length less than d. It can print any of the first 2^(k−c) fast omissions in the prefix block, if that many exist. Since w is itself a fast omission and cannot be printed by this decoder, at least 2^(k−c) fast omissions precede w in its block, whenever k≥c. All of them eventually have short descriptions by the definition of w. Every such description takes more than F steps before halting with its answer.

There is a simultaneous version even for k chosen after seeing w. Include k in the payload using a self-delimiting string of length at most 2⌈log₂(k+2)⌉+1, and deduct this length from the rank budget. The same fixed decoder gives at least

    2^(k−c−2⌈log₂(k+2)⌉)

such late predecessors in [w−2ᵏ,w) for every k≤d with nonnegative exponent, for a single machine-dependent constant c. In particular choose one sufficiently large **constant** k. Every sufficiently long selected string has a predecessor within a constant distance for which every short output computation costs more than 2^(2n). Consequently the proposed 2ⁿ poly(n)-time witness already fails if it individually replays the descriptions for all predecessors in this constant window. The argument still permits a shorter joint proof of their outputs that avoids those replays. Finding one that also identifies a sparse list containing w is unresolved.

## How much does a witness that probes the generator reveal?

Consider a finite model where a verifier for a candidate sees only forward evaluations of a generator. The generator is an injection G from M−1 addresses into M outputs, with unique hole y. A nondeterministic verifier accepts a set L(G); each accepting path reads at most q≤M−1 generator entries. Let p be the probability that the hole belongs to L(G) for a uniformly random injection. Then

    E[|L(G)|] ≥ p(M−q).

Complete G to a permutation σ of M values by putting its hole in the final slot. For every σ accepting its hole, fix one accepting path for this candidate, and select any of the at least M−q slots j outside the path's probes. Swap entries j and the final slot to form τ. Every probed value is unchanged, so the verifier on τ still accepts the old hole, now τ(j). The map (σ,j)↦(τ,j) is injective because swapping twice recovers σ. For a fixed τ there are exactly |L(τ)| slots whose values it accepts. Counting gives the inequality, including branches whose probes depend on earlier replies. When p=1 the bound is sharp: inspect a fixed q slots and accept every value not found there. Thus a universally correct black-box witness cannot restrict the hole to a small list using few probes, even if false positives are allowed. A fixed tiny list can still hit on rare instances.

This oracle example isolates evaluation access and has exactly one hole. The universal decoder in the actual diagonal has known code, collisions, timeouts and potentially many holes. Exploiting that structure to recognize a sparse set containing its selected mark remains a possible route. Prior work studies range avoidance for explicit stretching circuits and draws further distinctions by circuit class. [Ren, Santhanam and Wang](https://hanlin-ren.github.io/files/pdf/focs22_avoidance.pdf) · [Kleinberg, Korten, Mitropolsky and Papadimitriou](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.44)
