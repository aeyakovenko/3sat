# Adaptive affine queries for a hard truth table

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Let $E_{m,d,T}$ be the length-$m$ words printed by time $T$ by exact prefix programs of length at most $d$ on one deterministic native reference. An affine query chooses any affine flat $F\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m$. An ideal answer returns its sole member outside $E_{m,d,T}$ if there is exactly one. Giving the search these answers for free allows it to depend on previous answers, the entire random hash, and the program producing the queries.

Suppose a query returns $y$ and its flat has dimension $r\ge\lfloor d/2\rfloor+2$. For each fixed $a$ and $d\le a\log_2(\log_2(T+2)+2)$, **$y$ itself has an ordinary deterministic program of $d+O_a(1)$ bits running in $T\,\mathrm{poly}_a(\log(T+2))$ time**. This holds for every such flat, including one chosen using unlimited auxiliary computation. The output program receives neither the flat nor $m,d,T$. The proof uses the known finite-degree closure bound of Golovnev, Guo, Hatami, Nagargoje and Yan and gives a way to enumerate and name the candidate words within the claimed clock. [Golovnev et al., Corollary 7](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2024.41)

## A small set containing all the missing points

For a finite $E\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m$ let $\operatorname{cl}_k(E)$ contain the points where every polynomial of degree at most $k$ vanishing on $E$ also vanishes. A degree less than $r$ polynomial sums to zero in $\mathbb F_2$ on every affine $r$-flat. Indeed parametrize the flat by $r$ independent binary variables. After affine substitution and multilinearization each resulting monomial omits at least one variable, and its two contributions along that variable cancel. Thus if $F\setminus\{y\}\subseteq E$, then $y\in\operatorname{cl}_{r-1}(E)$.

Set $k_d=\lfloor d/2\rfloor+1$ and $L=|E|$, where $1\le L\le 2^d$. Take $s=\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor+2$. If $s>m$ the whole ambient cube already has fewer than $2^s$ points. Otherwise Corollary 7 of the cited paper bounds the closure by fewer than $2^s$ points as soon as $L<\sum_{i=0}^{k_d}\binom{s}{i}$. Here $s\le d+2$ and $k_d\ge\lfloor s/2\rfloor$. Complementing subsets gives a binomial sum at least $2^{s-1}>L$. In particular
$$
 |\operatorname{cl}_{k_d}(E)|<2^s\le4L.
$$
The empty set has empty closure because the constant polynomial one vanishes on it vacuously. The estimate works separately at every output length, even when the available short programs concentrate at just a few lengths. [Golovnev et al., sharp finite-grid closure theorem](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2024.41)

The same coding conclusion holds for every fixed integer $C\ge0$ throughout the larger range $r\ge\max(2,d/2-C\sqrt d)$, with constants also depending on $C$. Use the computable integer degree $k=\max(1,\lfloor d/2\rfloor-C\lceil\sqrt d\rceil-1)<r$. For each fixed $A>0$ the fraction of the $s$-cube at weights at most $s/2-A\sqrt s$ exceeds a constant $c_A>0$ for all sufficiently large $s$. For instance Stirling's formula gives probability at least $c'_A/\sqrt s$ at each integer weight between $s/2-(A+2)\sqrt s$ and $s/2-(A+1)\sqrt s$. Fix $A=2C+3$, then choose a fixed $b\ge2$ with $2^{b-1}c_A>1$ and put $s=\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor+b$. When $s\ge d/2$ and $s$ is large, $s\le d+b$ gives $k\ge s/2-A\sqrt s$. When $s<d/2$ and $d$ is large, $k\ge\lfloor s/2\rfloor$. In either case the low-weight binomial count exceeds $L$ (if $s$ stays bounded as $d$ grows, eventually $k\ge s$). Thus the closure has size at most $2^b L$. At bounded $d$ even the affine span of $E$ has size $O_C(L)$, since $L$ is bounded in terms of $C$. The rest of the enumeration and allocation proof applies with this degree and a larger fixed cell overhead.

The oracle may even return an entire list of missing words. If an $r$-flat has $h\ge1$ missing words, each one has the coding guarantee whenever $r-\lceil\log_2 h\rceil$ meets the rank threshold. Fix a missing $y$. A random linear map on the direction space with $c=\lceil\log_2 h\rceil$ output bits maps at least one of the other $h-1$ differences to zero with probability at most $(h-1)2^{-c}<1$. Some map therefore avoids them all. Its kernel coset through $y$ has dimension at least $r-c$ and contains no other missing word. The eventual program needs no description of that map.

## Finding the entire closure quickly

For $r\ge2$ choose distinct nonzero directions $u,v$ inside the flat. Then
$$
 y=(y+u)+(y+v)+(y+u+v).
$$
All three summands belong to $E$ and are distinct. This supplies a pool of at most $L^3$ candidates for the isolated points. In fact the same clock allows enumeration of **every** point of $\operatorname{cl}_{k_d}(E)$, including points outside this pool.

Since $k_d\ge1$, the closure lies in the affine span of $E$. Gaussian elimination on the differences from one member of $E$ finds $n\le L-1$ pivot coordinates. Their projection $\pi$ maps the affine span bijectively onto $\mathbb F_2^n$, with a computable affine inverse. The image of the closure is exactly $\operatorname{cl}_{k_d}(\pi E)$ there, since substitution by either affine map preserves the degree upper bound. Computing and evaluating the inverse takes $(m+1)\,\mathrm{poly}(L+1)$ operations. A singleton has $n=0$.

In pivot coordinates grow a binary prefix tree. At depth $i$ keep precisely the visited prefixes in the degree-$k_d$ closure of the projection of $E$ onto the first $i$ pivot coordinates. A genuine closure point survives every test because any separating polynomial on a projection also separates on the full cube. At final depth the test is exact. At each depth the size theorem applies in that prefix cube to at most $L$ distinct projected words, so at most $4L$ prefixes survive. For the wider fixed-$C$ range the bound is $O_C(L)$ as well: for bounded $d$ and any degree at least one, even the whole affine span has at most $2^{L-1}=O_C(L)$ points. The tree has depth at most $L-1$, independent of $m$.

For each test form $W$ from the projected easy words and the candidate prefix. To compute degree-at-most-$k_d$ evaluations on $W$, start with the constant row of ones. At each of $k_d$ rounds retain the current row space and add every previous basis row multiplied componentwise by each coordinate row on $W$. Each degree-at-most-$j+1$ monomial is constant or a coordinate times a degree-at-most-$j$ monomial, so row reduction gives the full evaluation space. The candidate column belongs to the span of the easy columns exactly when all polynomials vanishing on those words also vanish there. Each test costs $\mathrm{poly}(L+1,d+1)$ operations. Including reconstruction, the entire calculation takes $(m+1)\,\mathrm{poly}(L+1,d+1)$ bit operations up to address bookkeeping.

At epoch $j\ge1$ simulate every exact reference program of length at most $B\lceil\log_2(j+2)\rceil$ through $2^j$ native steps, for fixed sufficiently large $B$ depending on $a$. For each $d$ up to this cutoff, group the observed outputs of programs of length at most $d$ by their actual length $m$. On each nonempty group enumerate the closure just described. There are polynomially many names and lengths in $j$. Explicit output length is at most $2^j\,\mathrm{poly}(j+2)$, so all simulations, word comparisons and closure computations through this epoch cost $2^j\,\mathrm{poly}_a(j+2)$ native steps. Numerical factors depending on the fixed reference can be absorbed by increasing the exponent.

## Naming all discoveries across clocks and lengths

Issue one request of width $2^{-d-5}$ at the first epoch reporting $(y,d)$, in fixed order. For fixed $d,m$, all reported words belong to $\operatorname{cl}_{k_d}(E_{m,d,\infty})$ by monotonicity. There are at most $4|E_{m,d,\infty}|$ such words. Each eventually produced word with shortest exact name length $b$ contributes $\sum_{d\ge b}2^{-d}=2^{1-b}$ to $\sum_{m,d}2^{-d}|E_{m,d,\infty}|$. Choosing one shortest program per word and applying Kraft bounds that sum by two. Total requested width is therefore at most $1/4$.

Place the successive requests in adjacent intervals and give each an aligned dyadic cell of half its width. A fixed decoder repeats the epoch computations, remembers discoveries, and compares the supplied prefix to the new cell addresses, retaining previously read bits. It reads fresh bits only to the first mismatch or the end of the current address. On a valid name, disjointness from each earlier cell is certified by the end of that name. Its length is $d+6$ plus the fixed interpreter header. At $j=\max(1,\lceil\log_2 T\rceil)$ all the companions are present and the closure contains $y$. This proves the native description bound. The deadline includes a polylogarithmic factor. The proof permits $y$ to lack any $d$-bit program at the original cutoff $T$.

As a necessary condition on a persistent coding failure, a word longer in description than $d+O_a(1)$ at that later clock must admit a degree-at-most-$k_d$ polynomial taking value one there and zero on every $d$-bit timely output of the same length. A separating polynomial can be chosen with at most $L+1$ monomials, since monomial evaluation rows on $E\cup\{y\}$ span a space of dimension at most $L+1$. The coefficients or variable positions of this certificate may themselves require long descriptions.

A weaker estimate works at every rank $r\ge2$. Each of the $(2^r-1)(2^r-2)$ ordered choices of distinct nonzero directions gives a distinct ordered triple of easy words with XOR $y$. Simulate three prefix programs in succession on one fair stream and publish their XOR when the lengths match. Selecting one witnessing name per companion gives total mass at least $(2^r-1)(2^r-2)2^{-3d}$. These contributions come entirely from triples with at most $3d$ program bits. At each epoch the preceding polynomial enumeration of short programs computes their product weights exactly. For every word request a name at each dyadic mass threshold it first crosses in this monotone short-triple enumeration, retaining only thresholds of depth $O_a(\log(j+2))$ at epoch $j$. Total width is bounded by a constant because the product program domain is prefix-free and the geometric threshold sum for each output is at most twice its eventual mass. The same interval decoder gives length $3d-2r+O_a(1)$ at the nearby clock. If the flat is a linear subspace, each of its $2^r-2$ words $x\notin\{0,y\}$ gives the ordered pair $x,x+y$ of easy words whenever $y\ne0$. The pair construction gives $2d-r+O_a(1)$ in that case.

## What reusing a single random affine chart buys

There is also a bound on the chance of *ever* isolating a fixed $y$ under many correlated queries. Let $A$ be a uniform invertible binary $m$-by-$m$ matrix. In the chart given by $A$ and any translation, suppose every permitted positive-dimensional query has a direction space containing a member of a fixed dictionary $D\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m\setminus\{0\}$ in chart coordinates. The algorithm may see $A$, reuse all its randomness, query arbitrary offsets and adapt to every previous reply. If it ever isolates $y$ with a permitted query, there is a $v\in D$ with $y+Av\in E_{m,d,T}$. Each fixed $Av$ is uniform on the nonzero native vectors. For $y\notin E_{m,d,T}$ this gives
$$
 \Pr[\text{some permitted query isolates }y]\le\min\left(1,\frac{|D|\,|E_{m,d,T}|}{2^m-1}\right).
$$
The dictionary can depend on the fixed easy set and target but is chosen independently of $A$. The number of queries does not enter. All positive-dimensional coordinate subcubes in the same chart have a dictionary of just $m$ coordinate vectors. All proper prefix cuts use only the last coordinate vector. More generally take the dictionary of directions with descriptions shorter than $b$, conditional on $m,E_{m,d,T},y$ on any fixed prefix machine. It has at most $2^b$ members. If a search isolates $y$ with probability at least $p$, then on probability at least $p-2^{b+d}/(2^m-1)$ it succeeds without any successfully isolating kernel containing such a direction. Over a high-mass family with $d=O(\log q)$ and $m=q$, this forces long chart-coordinate descriptions for every nonzero direction of those successful kernels.

Allowing arbitrary affine lines gives an exact escape from the dictionary restriction. For phases where the known word $0^m$ has a $d$-bit program by $T$, the line $\{0^m,x\}$ for $x\ne0^m$ returns $x$ exactly when $x\notin E_{m,d,T}$. Testing the first $2^d+1$ words thus finds the first hard word deterministically with this oracle when the segment fits inside the cube. For the explicit phases in the [initial-segment tester](dense-hard-table-sampling.md), $0^m$ has the phase header plus fixed printing code and meets both bounds. Realizing these answers with ordinary native computations is exactly the remaining membership problem on that segment. A large-flat isolation at an earlier clock gives an unconditional short name at a later clock; the unrestricted linear SAT lower bound still requires an actual family with a persistent coding failure. Priority for the native coding consequence is unestablished.
