# A finite-field product checker with low-degree equality tests

Let $T:F^a\times F^b\to F^m$ be bilinear, where $F=\mathbb F_q$. Consider an arithmetic decision tree with constants in $F$, at most $s$ scalar arithmetic operations and tests along every path, and a fixed yes/no result at each leaf. The arithmetic is division-free and the only branches test equality. The tree must decide $Y=T(U,V)$ on **all triples over this one field**. No correctness on field extensions is assumed.

Follow the path on formal inputs $(U,V,T(U,V))$ in the function field $F(U,V)$. A comparison whose operands agree there contributes its difference to a list $h_1,\ldots,h_k$. Call these the equalities. All other differences on this path are the inequalities. Assume all the differences, formed before making the substitution $Y=T(U,V)$, have total input degree at most $D$, and assume $q>2sD+1$.

* If every equality is affine in the proposed output $Y$, then $T$ has exact tensor rank at most $2s$ over $F$. This holds in every characteristic.
* More generally, in odd characteristic with $q>m$, each equality may instead have $Y$-degree at most two and satisfy $\nabla_Y h_j(U,V,T(U,V))=0$ as a polynomial identity. Arbitrary mixtures of the two types are allowed. Then the exact tensor rank of $T$ over $F$ is $O(s)$.

There is no restriction on the output-degree of the inequalities beyond their total-degree bound. Both dimensions, the tree and the field may vary. The complexity counts arithmetic gates on one path and permits arbitrary constant-coefficient linear decoding of the resulting rank-one forms. Earlier work in the journal gives the final mixed-coefficient step under a full-rank Jacobian hypothesis. ## A valid finite-field specialization

The product of the inequalities restricted to the graph is a nonzero formal polynomial of total degree at most $2sD$. A nonzero polynomial of individual degrees less than $q$ cannot vanish everywhere on $F^k$, by induction using the univariate root bound. Choose $x_0=(u_0,v_0)$ where the product is nonzero and put $y_0=T(u_0,v_0)$. This honest point follows the chosen path, which therefore accepts.

Form the matrix $J$ of $Y$-gradients of the affine equalities at $(x_0,y_0)$ and let $W=\ker J$. At this point each stationary quadratic equality, evaluated at $y_0+t z$, is exactly $t^2 Q_j(z)$ for a quadratic form $Q_j$; its constant and linear coefficients vanish. If $0\ne z\in W$ and all $Q_j(z)=0$, all equalities on that line hold for all $t$. Every inequality there was nonzero at zero and has at most $D$ roots. Since $q-1>sD$, there is a nonzero $t$ where all inequalities remain nonzero. This false $F$-point would reach the accepting leaf. Consequently the $Q_j$ have no common nonzero isotropic vector in $W$. If there are only affine tests this says $W=0$, directly yielding the full Jacobian.

## Extracting nonsingular linear information from stationary quadratics

In odd characteristic let $H_j$ be the symmetric $Y$-Hessian of each stationary test at the chosen point, so $z^{\mathsf T}H_jz=2Q_j(z)$. Some linear combination of the $H_j$ restricts to a nonsingular form on $W$. To see this, choose a form $A$ of largest rank $r$ in their span on $W$. Split an invertible principal $r$-by-$r$ block from its radical by a change of basis. If that radical contains a nonzero $z$, some form $B=H_j|_W$ has $B(z,z)\ne0$. Include $z$ as the next basis vector. The corresponding $(r+1)$-minor of $A+tB$ has linear coefficient $\det(A_r) B(z,z)\ne0$. Its degree is at most $r+1\le m<q$, so some $t\in F$ gives rank greater than $r$. This contradicts maximality. The empty $W$ case requires no quadratic tests.

Choose coefficients $w_j$ for this nonsingular restriction and put $G=\sum_j w_jh_j$. The list of affine equalities together with all components of $\nabla_Y G$ vanishes formally on the graph. Its $Y$-Jacobian has full column rank at $(x_0,y_0)$: a kernel vector must lie in $W$ and in the kernel of $\sum_jw_jH_j$, which meets $W$ trivially. Adding the scalar $G$ to the path circuit takes $O(s)$ operations. Reverse differentiation computes *all* its $Y$-partials with $O(s)$ operations on a shared circuit. For each product gate it sends the adjoint to both factors multiplied by the other factor, and for sums it sends the adjoint unchanged. Input coordinates absent from the circuit have derivative zero. This works over the stated finite fields. [Baur and Strassen](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(83)90110-X)

For completeness, feed this circuit $u_0+tU,v_0+wV$ and $y_0+tT(U,v_0)+wT(u_0,V)$ with $t^2=w^2=0$. Adding $tw T(U,V)$ in the output slot makes all tests and derived gradient components vanish by their polynomial identities. Without that addition their mixed coefficients therefore equal minus their $Y$-Jacobian applied to $T(U,V)$. Each product gate introduces at most two products of a linear form in $U$ and a linear form in $V$ into the joint span of the mixed coefficients. A fixed left inverse of the Jacobian recovers $T$, proving both rank bounds. Computing the base point, the weights and this left inverse efficiently is unnecessary for the tensor-rank assertion.

When the bilinear map has fixed integer structure constants, a uniform rank bound $r$ at a fixed dimension for infinitely many distinct primes also implies complex rank at most $r$. The equations for the entries of a sum of $r$ rank-one tensors have integer coefficients. If they have no complex solution, the weak Nullstellensatz gives a polynomial combination equal to 1 over $\mathbb C$; the finitely many coefficients involved solve rational linear equations, hence a rational solution of those linear equations exists. Clearing denominators gives a combination equal to a fixed nonzero integer. All but finitely many primes then admit no solution even over their algebraic closures. This transfer requires the indicated uniformity across primes.

## The price of reading the bits of a field element

On a scalar parameter $u\in F$, follow a degree-$D$ equality tree on the transcendental value $u$ over $F$. All but at most $sD$ actual field elements follow the same path. Indeed every deviation requires the first vanishing of one of the path's nonzero univariate tests. Thus if the output is a Boolean leaf label with fibers of sizes $a,b$, one has $sD\ge\min(a,b)$. If the leaf labels distinguish every scalar, $sD\ge q-1$. These claims continue to hold with divisions when numerator and denominator degrees and a joint budget for the exceptional roots are tracked; no division is needed in the stated theorem. The injective version concerns *leaf labels*, including a full branch transcript; simply returning the input scalar as a register is free. For $F=\mathbb F_p$ the unique reduced polynomial of a nonconstant Boolean bit has degree exactly $p-1$. In fact its top coefficient is minus the sum of its values, and its number of ones is between 1 and $p-1$. This is an additional obstruction for branch-free bit extraction. It applies to every nonconstant bit in every scalar encoding, even if that bit has a very small fiber.

The physical SAT reduction that pins the binary digits of arbitrary field elements in unit clauses requires an encoding step in addition to an arithmetic verifier. Replaying each exposed digit or low-bit instruction as a bounded-degree field test runs into these inequalities. A uniform Boolean verifier may also sort addresses and perform table lookups. A transfer that avoids exposing digits at all would have to recover a low-cost algebraic computation of what the whole verifier does on the product graph. Correctness on the Boolean files asserts no degree bound or derivative identity for such a computation. The rank statements above provide separate conclusions for verifiers obeying the displayed conditions. The unrestricted SAT-on-RAM problem remains open.

The quadratic case needs its stationarity condition. For example over an odd field with a nonsquare $a$, the equalities $e_1-e_2^2=0$, $e_1^2-ae_3^2=0$ isolate the origin over that field. The first has linear gradient of rank one and is genuinely quadratic; the second is stationary. Eliminating $e_1$ yields a degree-four equation. This example lies outside both alternatives and does not refute a stronger transfer. Priority for this finite-field criterion has not been established. ## The effect of indirect addressing on a single scalar input

Now supply a scalar $x$ in a register and permit direct field arithmetic, equality branches, and loads and stores at addresses in $F$. Memory starts at zero, with no uncharged precomputed table. Constants in the program are permitted. Define $E,R,W$ to be the numbers of equality branches, loads and stores along the trace with formal scalar $x$ transcendental over $F$. Addresses are compared as formal expressions, and a load returns its last matching write or zero. Assume a worst-case step bound $s$ on actual inputs; truncate the symbolic trace at $s$ if necessary. Let $D$ bound the degrees of tested differences and address polynomials along that trace.

At most $D(E+RW)$ actual scalars can deviate from the symbolic control-flow and last-writer choices. Protect each nonidentically-zero branch difference and each nonidentically-zero difference of a load address and an earlier store address. Outside the union of their roots the branch outcomes and all prior write matches are unchanged, recursively at every step. If $D(E+RW)<q$ there is a genuine input following the full trace, which thus halts by $s$. Otherwise the following lower bounds hold trivially. For a fixed Boolean verdict per leaf with fiber sizes $a,b$, we obtain $D(E+RW)\ge\min(a,b)$. An injective branch transcript requires $D(E+RW)\ge q-1$. For a verdict returned in a field register include its final zero test. Returning the unrestricted scalar itself in a register does not expose it as a branch transcript.

Over an odd prime field, a balanced bit of one scalar actually takes only $O(\sqrt p)$ steps in this model. Choose $h=\lceil\sqrt p\rceil$. Store marker information at $a=0,h,2h,\ldots<p$, then probe $x+k$ for $0\le k<h$, using the first hit. Arbitrary fixed $h,p$ and table information may be unrolled; all stores, initialization and probes are charged. One nonzero table symbol can distinguish an odd point, a nonzero even point, and the zero point; for the finitely many tiny primes handle them directly. The cyclic gap argument ensures a hit. The canonical representative of $x$ equals $a-k$ unless $a=0$ and $k>0$, in which case add $p$. Consequently the parity of the representative is the parity at the hit combined with the parity of $k$ and the wrap flag. Only equality tests of constant-size status symbols are needed, and the addresses have degree one. To identify $x$ by the complete branch transcript instead, store the table index plus one, and after the hit compare the returned value to the $O(p/h)$ possible nonzero indices using equality. The transcript then identifies $a$ and $k$. Both constructions cost $O(p/h+h)$ operations and realize the square-root scale. This witness uses field addition and sparse random access; the collision budget is genuinely quadratic even before ordinary word operations enter.
