# Testing short-program outputs by dimensions of column subspaces

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Can a small collection of exceptional paths in each column account for the last-bit matrices in the [fixed program test](fixed-program-parity.md)? A randomized linear map gives a sufficient condition. Its dimension depends on the number of exceptions in a column even when the row uses arbitrarily many paths and the labels need arbitrarily many bits. For the actual set of all short-program outputs there is a stronger formulation using subspaces. Under each SAT time hypothesis, any such test that includes a constant fraction of genuinely missed words must also include a constant fraction of actual late outputs. I have no construction separating the two on the full interpreter.

## Compressing an incidence test

Work over a fixed finite field of order $p$. In an arbitrary finite-dimensional space assign a point $u_y$ to each row and a linear space $V_z$ of dimension at most $b$ to each column. All the data may depend on the matrix size. Let $k\ge1$ and choose a uniform random linear map $H$ into $p$-ary dimension $D=b+k$. Choose $k$ independent linear forms annihilating $H(V_z)$, by a fixed rule depending only on this image. Store $Hu_y$ at each row and the $k$ forms at each column. Declare membership when all $k$ evaluations vanish.

Every member passes. For $u_y\notin V_z$, conditional on all values of $H$ on $V_z$, the value $Hu_y$ is uniform in the full output space. This follows by extending a basis of $V_z$ by $u_y$ before sampling the map on a complete basis. The $k$ evaluations are therefore jointly uniform and the false membership probability is exactly $p^{-k}$. The conditional argument matters because the forms are chosen after seeing the map. A single map serves the entire array by averaging the total number of errors. This is the probabilistic rank method applied to subspace incidence. Ambient dimension and the time for finding the map have disappeared from the size of the representation. [Alman and Williams on probabilistic rank](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05558) · [Aaronson et al. on one-way group membership protocols](https://arxiv.org/abs/0902.3175) · [Finite projection experiment](../audits/word_path_flat_experiment.py)

Over the binary field the membership indicator for a fixed column after the projection is a multilinear polynomial of degree at most $k$ in the same $b+k$ row bits, the product of one plus each form. Its binary matrix rank is at most
$$
\sum_{i=0}^k\binom{b+k}{i}.
$$
In particular the original fixed program's bit table of side $J$, defined independently of the SAT hypothesis, would have an approximation with at most $J^2/16$ errors and rank $o(J)$ if it were exactly such an incidence matrix with $b=o(J^{1/4})$, by using $k=4$. Infinitely many such phases suffice for the respective unrestricted SAT lower bound. With at most $J^2/32$ initial discrepancies, use $k=5$ and $b=o(J^{1/5})$. Floors at the error threshold follow from integrality. For arbitrary sets $A_y,B_z$ take $u_y=1_{A_y}$ and $V_z$ spanned by coordinate axes in $B_z$. This handles $A_y\subseteq B_z$ with no bound on the size of $A_y$. Here $b$ is the number of distinct axes in $B_z$. [Exact program criterion](fixed-program-parity.md)

## A bound for the genuine late outputs

Use the [dense-advice qualification](spectra-of-program-counts.md). On an even $q$-bit cube put $N=2^q$, $L=2^{q/2}$, and $T=N(q+2)^{\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil}$. A hard word has no name of length at most $q-2$ by $T$. A late word has such a name by $T$ and none by the designated early deadline $\tau$. For $d=1$ there is one block of side $J=L$ and $\tau=N$. For $d=2,3$ use blocks of side $J=2^s$ with $s$ maximal subject to $2^{2s}7^{sd^2}\le N$, and take $\tau$ maximal with $J^2\tau^d\le7^s$. The dense-advice theorem under $O(n^d)$ SAT says that any fixed verifier with sufficiently small constant times $J^2$ bits of advice per block, within its specified clock, that accepts at least a fixed positive density must accept a positive density of easy words. Its early-word check can exclude the early words.

Fix $\delta>0$. Suppose that at infinitely many such lengths one can choose, **in every block separately**, binary row points and binary column spaces of dimension $o(J)$ uniformly over blocks. Suppose their incidences contain $\delta N$ hard words and only $o(N)$ late words globally. This would exclude deterministic $O(n^d)$ SAT on the unrestricted target RAM. Indeed fix the verifier for a variable number $k$ of normal tests, parsing bounded advice and capping $k$ at $q$ in the block algorithm. Obtain its positive contamination threshold $\gamma$ at density $\delta$ first. Choose a fixed $k$ with $2^{-k}<\gamma/3$. Project as above independently per block. All selected hard words still pass. Choose maps with at most $2^{-k}N$ spurious acceptances in total. This is possible even if the selected words, rows and spaces depend on all the executions. The lists occupy at most $(k+1)J(b+k)$ bits per block plus framing, hence $o(J^2)$. A single-cell verifier evaluates $k$ dot products. For the stronger exponents multiply each of the $k$ factor pairs across a whole block using Strassen and intersect their zero sets. These costs fit the respective qualification clocks including malformed advice after the stated caps. Reject early words. The required lower bound $\gamma N$ on accepted easy words now contradicts the count.

This also gives a concrete search on graphs. Assign each row two arbitrary vertex names $u,v$ and the vector $e_u+e_v$. In a column supply an arbitrary undirected graph of spanning-forest size at most $b$. The span of its edge vectors contains the row vector exactly when $u,v$ are connected, including the case $u=v$. A connecting path sums to the row vector; a cut between distinct components annihilates every edge and separates the two vertices. Thus $o(J)$ independent merges per column, over any shared universe within the block, would suffice if these connectivity relations included enough hard words and few late ones. Each column can contain arbitrarily many redundant edges. A bound only on the **number of equations defining a space** gives no such restriction. With row points $e_y$, one hyperplane per column can realize any binary table by choosing its normal from that column's zero/nonzero pattern (an extra unused coordinate makes every normal nonzero).

## Allowing a few independent unsatisfied conditions

There is a more general test for sets $U_y,V_z$ that are both subspaces. Declare a pair acceptable when
$$
\dim((U_y+V_z)/V_z)\le h.
$$
Only $\dim V_z\le b$ is restricted. For coordinate spaces this says that at most $h$ distinct row requirements lie outside the column's permitted set. Choose $k=h+t$ with $t\ge2$ and $D=b+k$. For each row sample $k$ independent uniform draws from $U_y$ (with replacement). Project them by a common random binary $H$ into dimension $D$. At each column choose $k$ independent normals as before. Test the rank of the $k$-by-$k$ matrix of row evaluations against $h$.

An acceptable pair always passes. Otherwise choose any $(h+1)$-dimensional quotient of $U_y/(U_y\cap V_z)$. The $k$ row samples span this quotient except with probability less than $2^{h+1-k}$, by testing all nonzero linear forms on it. On success select $h+1$ sampled vectors independent modulo $V_z$, with no reference to $H$. Given the map on $V_z$, their vectors of normal evaluations are independent uniform $k$-bit strings. They are linearly independent except with probability less than $2^{h+1-k}$, by testing nontrivial linear combinations. Hence the total false acceptance probability is less than $2^{2-t}$. A fixed $t$ can make this smaller than the contamination threshold.

The bit advice is now $2JkD$ plus framing. For the linear target **$kD=o(J)$ suffices**, even when $h$ increases. On one cell computing the entries and their rank costs $O(k^2D+k^3)$ operations, within $J^2\mathrm{poly}(q)$ since $D\ge k$. For the other two exponents a sufficient whole-block condition is $kD=o(J)$ and
$$
k^2D^{\omega-2}+k^3=O(J^{\omega-2}),\qquad \omega=\log_2 7.
$$
To obtain this time multiply the $(Jk)$-by-$D$ row matrix with the $D$-by-$(Jk)$ column matrix by tiling into $D$-square products (pad to the next power of two). Their total cost is $O(J^2 k^2D^{\omega-2})$, since $D\le Jk$ eventually. Ordinary binary elimination on all $J^2$ small matrices costs $O(J^2k^3)$. A verifier can clock each stage, independently of the advice. For either stronger exponent the theorem applies to all fixed $h$ and $b=o(J)$. These conditions concern the size of an independently supplied incidence representation. No bound here on actual SAT computation forces it to exist.

A proposed use of these tests is to share one collection of routed paths across an entire block. Let the row specify the paths on which the endpoint bits conflict and the column specify which paths contain a sign break. Feasibility is containment for disjoint paths. Even a known pairing of the ends can hide the location of one marked break, and a mark depending on both the row and the column defeats this separation. [Path test and the mirrored two-track counterexample](one-break-paths.md)

## An explicit dense incidence family with nearly full binary rank

The advice calculation for actual short-program outputs also works over any fixed finite field. Encode field elements in a constant number of bits and compute the block products in that field. Here is a genuine native example showing that small column dimension and large exact binary rank can occur together on constant-density sets of zeros and ones.

Take one representative of each one-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb F_3^v$, with its first nonzero coordinate one. There are $P=(3^v-1)/2$ such points. Use the same representatives as normals of the column hyperplanes and record zero dot product. The ternary column dimension is $v-1$. Each row has $a=(3^{v-1}-1)/2$ ones, and any two distinct rows have $c=(3^{v-2}-1)/2$ common ones. Thus over $\mathbb F_2$ the incidence matrix $M$ satisfies
$$
MM^{\mathsf T}=(a-c)I+c\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}=I+c\,\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T},
$$
because $a-c=3^{v-2}$ is odd. Its binary rank is at least $P-1$ and its density approaches $1/3$. Both statements refer to one fixed, elementary family. A native program on $m$-bit row and column addresses takes the largest $v$ with $P\le 2^m$, names the first $P$ indices by these representatives, and reuses the first representative for unused addresses. Unranking by the position of the first one and base-three suffix digits takes $\mathrm{poly}(m)$ time. The original square is a submatrix with $P>(2^m-1)/3$, so the binary rank stays a positive fraction of the side and each bit value has positive density. One may place this test in the ordinary systematic program branch. [Finite projective incidence calculation](../audits/projective_incidence_experiment.py)

This supplies an unconditional representation on a real branch even at nearly maximal exact binary rank. Its systematic outputs also have early descriptions when the padding pays for copying both possible tags. Neither the rank calculation nor its ternary incidence description controls other branches of the interpreter on the remaining late words.
