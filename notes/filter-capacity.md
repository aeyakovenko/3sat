# How many consistent records can one streaming verifier accept?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Consider a stream with a public fixed layout. Each of K independent data bits occurs at two or more prescribed positions. A consistent stream uses the same value at all occurrences of each bit. Any non-data positions are fixed. This models the equality checks on a raw history. A sound filter may accept any subset of the consistent streams, and may take nondeterministic branches. Its maximum possible coverage has an exact formula, including when the available memory changes during the scan.

Between consecutive positions put a cut c, with a nonnegative integer memory budget s_c. The verifier is a layered one-pass nondeterministic branching program, with at most 2^{s_c} states after the positions to the left of c. Transitions may depend on the position, and may have no successor. Count every state component that can affect subsequent reading in this bound. The transition descriptions are unrestricted in this extremal question. The single start state and accepting end states are fixed. Write I_j for the cuts from the first to the last occurrence of bit j, including the cut immediately after its first occurrence and excluding the cut after its last.

Choose the largest possible number ν of the intervals I_j subject to at most s_c chosen intervals containing each c. Then

    maximum number of consistent streams accepted by a sound filter = 2^ν.     (1)

In particular, arbitrary correlations, nondeterminism and position-dependent transition tables can all be allowed when proving this upper bound.

## The cut argument

Select any set D of cuts and partition the stream into consecutive pieces there. Fix the states on an accepting path at these cuts. The readable pieces leading between each prescribed pair of states form a Cartesian product: one can concatenate any such local paths. The same statement holds for the initial and final pieces with the corresponding acceptance conditions.

If a data bit occurs in two of the pieces, its value must be fixed over the whole nonempty product. Otherwise take two streams with different values of this bit, copying one piece from the first and another from the second. The concatenated path accepts an inconsistent stream. The bits whose intervals miss D are the only remaining choices. There are at most 2^{Σ_{c∈D} s_c} state tuples. With u(D) the number of intervals missed by D, every sound filter therefore accepts at most

    2^{u(D) + Σ_{c∈D} s_c}                                                (2)

streams. This argument allows a stream to have several accepting paths. Lean checks the path concatenation and fixed-value argument. [Lean proofs](../lean/FilterRectangle.lean)

The minimum exponent in (2) equals ν. For completeness let A have rows indexed by successive cuts, with A_cj equal to one when c lies in I_j. The packing linear program maximizes Σ_j x_j with Ax≤s and 0≤x≤1. Its dual minimizes Σ_c s_c y_c + Σ_j z_j with Σ_{c∈I_j} y_c + z_j≥1 and y,z≥0. The ones in every column of A are consecutive. Every square submatrix retains this property. Subtracting each preceding row from its successor, from bottom to top, keeps the determinant and leaves at most one +1 and one −1 per column. If every column has both signs the column sums are zero. Otherwise expansion in a column of at most one nonzero proves by induction on size that the determinant is 0 or ±1 (the same inductive bound holds for any matrix with this signed-column property). Appending identity rows or columns and transposing preserve total unimodularity. Thus integer right-hand sides and costs give integral optimal vertices on both sides by Cramer's rule. By linear programming duality the optima agree. A dual optimum can be clipped to zeroes and ones, with z_j paying exactly for intervals missed by D={c:y_c=1}. This gives the asserted minimum. The interval matrix and scheduling ingredients are classical. [Fulkerson and Gross](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1965.15.835) · [Interval scheduling formulation](https://sites.gatech.edu/alejandro-toriello/files/2022/07/interval_scheduling_revision.pdf)

For attainability take an optimal packing and fix all other bits to zero. Test those zeroes at every occurrence. Store the chosen bits from their first appearance through their last, and compare every repeated occurrence. The layout determines the live bits and their order in each layer, so their values use at most s_c bits at c. Failed comparisons have no successor. The 2^ν choices of the packed bits all pass. This proves (1).

## What this says about short descriptions

If the prover may pick one of P sound filters of these widths, covering *all* consistent streams requires P≥2^{K−ν}. This exact menu size suffices. Use the same optimal packing and let the prover specify the bits outside it. Each such specification covers one of the disjoint sets of 2^ν streams. For several possible orders, one per filter, the corresponding counting bound uses the sum of 2^{ν(order)} over the menu. In a uniform model one must also account for computing the transitions, including how to find each stored bit.

A single cut can miss almost all of this cost. Take h disjoint consecutive groups, each containing p independent bits in the order 1,…,p,1,…,p, with s_c=s≤p. A group has an internal cut crossed by every one of its p intervals. Exactly s intervals per group can be selected. Thus one filter accepts at most 2^{hs} streams, and a covering menu needs at least h(p−s) bits to name a filter. The worst single cut forces only p−s bits. For a unary hierarchy input the verifier only needs to preserve one legal accepting run. Its local transition constraints might force the bits outside the packing to be zero, or to be computable from a short rule. In the all-zero case the optimal family for coverage is unnecessary: one fixed filter already contains a legal run. Conversely, the capacity formula alone says nothing about which member of the menu contains a run for a hard input. That intersection is the outstanding mathematical question.
