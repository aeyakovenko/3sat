# Checking a Nullstellensatz refutation in nearly linear time in its number of multiplier monomials

An $r$-term multilinear Nullstellensatz refutation of a 3-CNF over $\mathbb F_2$ gives a sound UNSAT certificate of $O(r\log(n+m+r+2))$ bits, even when individual multiplier monomials mention nearly all $n$ variables. Here $m$ is the clause count, and a term is one nonzero monomial in the multiplier of one input axiom. With indexed clause access the deterministic check costs $r\,\operatorname{polylog}(n+m+r+2)$ bit operations on a logarithmic-word RAM, plus the cost of $O(r)$ clause lookups. The construction keeps the connected component of the constant monomial in the coefficient equations. Adjacent multipliers in this component differ in at most six variable indices. This gives a verification bound for any 3-CNF admitting such a sparse identity.

## Encoding wide multipliers by their differences

Work in the Boolean quotient $K[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(x_i^2-x_i)_i$. Every element has a unique expansion in the squarefree basis $x^S=\prod_{i\in S}x_i$. For a clause $C$, its violation polynomial $f_C$ is the product of $1-x_i$ for its positive literals and $x_i$ for its negative literals. It equals zero on any assignment satisfying $C$. Repetitions and tautological clauses are allowed. Write the proposed identity as

$$
  \sum_{i=1}^r a_i x^{S_i}f_{C_i}=1 .
$$

The multiplier basis uses the original positive variables $x_i$. A product containing many complementary factors written without expansion can have exponentially many terms in this basis. A width-three axiom still has at most eight raw terms in its expansion. After multiplication and multilinearization, every resulting support in row $i$ has the form $S_i\cup T$ for $T\subseteq\operatorname{vars}(C_i)$ and $|T|\le3$.

Append the formal row $-1$ with multiplier support $\varnothing$. Build a bipartite graph between rows and monomial supports, with an incidence when a row has a nonzero coefficient at that support. The total coefficient at every support is zero, so the connected component of the appended row also sums to zero. Discarding all other components leaves a refutation. Two rows incident to the same support have multiplier supports at Hamming distance at most six. Indeed, every element of $S_i\setminus S_j$ belongs to the at most three extra variables of row $j$, and the reverse difference obeys the symmetric bound. This applies to the appended constant as well. A spanning tree in the row graph therefore lists at most $r$ ordinary rows in an order in which each support is obtainable from an earlier one using at most six specified bit flips. The first available support is empty. Over $\mathbb F_2$ one can alternatively pair equal raw occurrences before taking the component. Neither argument requires computing the spanning tree from the formula.

Each certificate row gives a previous row or the root, up to six variable indices to toggle, its clause index and its coefficient. In a fixed finite prime field the coefficient occupies a constant number of bits. The checker constructs the claimed supports in a persistent binary tree indexed by variables, with duplicate nodes interned in a deterministic ordered dictionary keyed by height and the two child identifiers. At height zero use two fixed identifiers. Build the empty root once in $O(\log(n+2))$ operations without visiting all leaves. Updating one bit of any old root changes only one root-to-leaf path. Equal bit sets have identical root identifiers by induction on the height, independent of update order. For each raw term of the axiom set its at most three variables to one in a temporary copy of the multiplier. Accumulate the signed coefficient at the resulting root in a second ordered dictionary. Accept exactly when the final sparse coefficient vector is the unit at the empty support. All operations, including the at most eight raw terms per row, take polylogarithmic time per record. No randomized hash or polynomial identity test is used. Any accepting transcript contradicts evaluation at a Boolean satisfying assignment.

For example, take the units $x_1,\neg x_h$ and the implications $x_i\Rightarrow x_{i+1}$. Writing $P_i=x_1\cdots x_i$ and $P_0=1$, the row contributions $1-P_1$, $P_i-P_{i+1}$ for $1\le i<h$, and $P_h$ telescope to one. The multipliers of the implication rows are $P_{i-1}$, and the final unit uses $P_{h-1}$. This particular identity lists $\Theta(h^2)$ variable names if each multiplier is written out. Its supports use only $O(h)$ single-bit changes.

## Coefficients and fields

For fixed positive characteristic $p$, the same $r$ clause-and-support choices work over $\mathbb F_p$ whenever they work over an extension field. The coefficient system has entries in the prime field, and its ranks do not change on field extension. For characteristic zero, there is a short certificate of **Boolean unsatisfiability** even if the supplied identity uses coefficients of unbounded height. First keep the component above. Its integer matrix $B$ has one column per remaining multiplier and one coordinate per resulting support, including the constant. Each column has $\ell_1$ norm at most eight. A characteristic-zero solution of $Ba=e_{\varnothing}$ implies a rational solution supported on a column basis. If its rank is $t$, select $t$ rows giving a nonsingular $t$-by-$t$ minor of that basis, with signed integer determinant $D$. Hadamard's inequality gives $1\le |D|\le 8^r$. By Cramer's rule the rational coefficients have common denominator dividing $D$.

Some prime $p$ of magnitude $(r+2)^{O(1)}$ does not divide $D$. The determinant has at most $3r$ distinct prime divisors, whereas the number of primes below $u$ is $\Omega(u/\log u)$. Reduction modulo such a prime gives the required unit identity with at most $r$ rows. One may keep the old support-difference description even for rows whose new coefficient is zero, since they can serve as intermediate supports. The verifier can guess $p$ in binary. For sound Boolean UNSAT checking it suffices to require an integer modulus of at least two and check the identity exactly modulo it. This accepts only unsatisfiable Boolean formulas, whether or not a particular submitted modulus is prime. It verifies a short *consequence* of having a characteristic-zero proof. A Boolean UNSAT certificate of this kind is not a test for the existence of a rational identity on particular supports. For positive characteristic the bit bound has the extra charge $O((r+1)\log(p+1))$ when $p$ is variable.

## Projection to one of two independent copies

For any field, the minimum number of multiplier terms for disjoint axiom systems is exactly the smaller of their two individual minima, assigning infinity if no refutation exists. The statement preserves the *specific projected multiplier rows*, useful when different degrees or proof representations are allowed. Consider a joint refutation on $x,y$. Each row has one local axiom, and its multiplier factors as $x^S y^T$. In the $y$-coordinate space take the span of the local rows $y^T g(y)$ from the $y$-axiom occurrences. If the constant belongs to this span the $y$-side already has a proof with at most the number of its original rows. Otherwise choose a linear functional $L$ annihilating this span with $L(1)=1$. The space may be restricted to the finite span of all needed $y$-monomials and the constant. Applying $\mathrm{id}\otimes L$ to the joint identity kills the $y$-axiom rows. On every $x$-axiom row it multiplies $x^S f(x)$ by the scalar $a_i L(y^T)$. The resulting $x$-identity uses a subset of the projected original rows. The converse embeds a component proof into the union. One can compute the projection by linear algebra. Its running time is unnecessary for an existentially guessed component certificate.

## Checking a DAG-like multilinear polynomial calculus proof over $\mathbb F_2$

Use the same squarefree basis. A line is a polynomial written as a set of monomials. The rules admit a clause axiom $f_C$, addition of two earlier lines, and multiplication of an earlier line by one variable with immediate multilinearization. The last line must be the constant one. Copies or zero scalar multiples can be checked directly if included. Write $s$ for the number of lines plus the total number of monomial occurrences across them. Degrees and the number of uses of a line are unrestricted. There is a compact certificate of $O(s\log(n+m+s+2))$ bits checkable deterministically in $s\,\operatorname{polylog}(n+m+s+2)$ time with indexed access to the referenced axioms. This statement applies to individual refutations. The same checker permits explicit twin variables $y_i$ with axioms $x_i+y_i-1=0$ and clause violations written as products of the appropriate positive or twin variables. Perform multilinearization separately on all $2n$ coordinates and check the twin axioms as local polynomials. This convention explicitly pays for their use in the proof. Quotienting for free by $y_i=1-x_i$ during arbitrary monomial comparisons would require a different checker.

First provide a dictionary of all support sets used on lines. An entry specifies an earlier entry and one variable to insert, with a distinguished initially empty set. Every actual proof admits such a dictionary of length $O(s)$. Each monomial in a width-three axiom needs at most three insertions. Every monomial of a sum already occurs in a parent. Every surviving monomial of $x_i A$ is obtained by inserting $i$ into a parent support. The verifier decodes the supplied dictionary in its order, interns the sets exactly as above, and turns each claimed line into a list of distinct canonical support identifiers. Also intern the line itself as a persistent binary set of these identifiers. Dictionary-generated identifier values have $O(\log(n+m+s+2))$ bits even counting internal trie nodes. Use a common fixed key width throughout, obtained from the declared parameters and a conservative upper bound on the number of nodes. Axiom checks expand the local width-three clause. Canonical line identifiers give exact equality of polynomials.

To check $C=A+B$, start at the persistent root for $B$, toggle each listed support of $C$, and compare the resulting root with $A$. This costs $(1+|C|)\operatorname{polylog}(n+m+s+2)$ even when $A$ and $B$ are wide and repeatedly reused. Here $|C|$ counts monomials, independently of the degree of each.

For $C=x_i A$, scan the list of $A$, inserting variable $i$ in each support and toggling the resulting support identifier in a fresh polynomial. Compare the result with $C$ and cache the product by **parent line index and variable**. Repeated checks of that same pair need only a root comparison. Let $a=|A|$, and write $e_i$ for the number of edges in coordinate $i$ induced by the supports of $A$ as a subset of the Boolean cube. Each such edge is one pair of monomials which merge and cancel under multiplication by $x_i$. All remaining images are distinct, so $|x_i A|=a-2e_i$.

Let $J$ consist of the distinct multipliers $i$ checked at this parent. For $a>0$, the classical cube edge bound gives
$$
  \sum_{i\in J} a = \sum_{i\in J}|x_i A|+2\sum_{i\in J} e_i
  \ \le\ \sum_{i\in J}|x_i A|+a\log_2 a.
$$
One short proof of the edge bound splits on the last coordinate into sets of sizes $a_0,a_1$. Induction bounds the internal edges in each part, and at most $\min(a_0,a_1)$ edges cross. The binary entropy satisfies $H_2(t)\ge 2\min(t,1-t)$ by concavity on each half of the unit interval. Substitution gives $2\sum_i e_i\le a\log_2 a$, with $0\log 0=0$. The scan of an empty parent costs constant work per instruction. For valid steps, charge the first sum to one output line per distinct pair and the second to that parent. Summing over parent line indices bounds the total scan work, apart from dictionary costs, by $O(s\log(s+2))$. A checker stops on the first invalid inference, so one extra uncompensated scan costs at most $s$. This gives the claimed time bound on all well-formed certificate lists meeting the size cap. The final equality to one proves Boolean unsatisfiability.

The scan overhead can occur. For $A=\prod_{i=1}^d(1+x_i)$ there are $a=2^d$ monomials but all $d$ distinct products $x_i A$ vanish. The scans cost $a\log_2 a$ for this parent even though their output lists are empty. The time bound for individual polynomial calculus proofs gives a further sound branch in the two-rank verifier. The projection argument above used a *static* sum of axiom multiples and only asserts the exact disjoint minimum for Nullstellensatz. Matching equal surviving monomials has appeared previously in polynomial calculus reflection encodings. [Buss, Fleming and Impagliazzo, section on polynomial calculus](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/TfnpProofSystems/TFNP.pdf)

### Linear combinations over a fixed prime field

The same bit and time bounds hold over any fixed prime field $\mathbb F_p$ when the addition rule becomes $C=\alpha A+\beta B$. List the nonzero coefficient at each claimed support on a line, using canonical field representatives. To make scaling a polynomial cheap, represent a nonzero coefficient vector by its first nonzero coefficient $c$ in increasing index order and an interned identifier for the vector divided by $c$. Give the zero vector a distinguished handle $(0,0)$. A nonzero leaf has one fixed normalized identifier. At an internal node choose $c$ from the first nonzero child and divide both child handles' scalars by $c$. Intern the height, the two normalized child identifiers and their two relative scalars. Recursion proves equality of full handles exactly when the entire vectors agree. Scaling a root by a nonzero field element changes only its scalar. Scaling by zero returns the zero handle. A point addition descends one path, recovering the absolute scalars of the children at each level, then normalizes and interns the modified path on the way back. This handles cancellation of the old leading entry, including a return to the zero vector.

For a linear combination start from the handle of $-\beta B$, add the listed coefficients of $C$ at their support keys, and compare with the handle of $\alpha A$. The cost remains proportional to $1+|C|$ times a polylogarithmic factor, including the cases $\alpha=0$ or $\beta=0$. For a multiplication, aggregate parent coefficients at the image support. In direction $i$ each colliding pair has one surviving term if its coefficients sum to a nonzero element, and zero terms otherwise. The unpaired $a-2e_i$ terms always survive. Hence $a\le |x_i A|+2e_i$, which suffices for the same amortized scan bound. The example $A=\prod_i(1-x_i)$ gives the tight $a\log_2 a$ term at every prime. The field is fixed in this statement, so field arithmetic, inversion and coefficient encoding use fixed overhead. The characteristic-zero argument above concerns static identities.

## What the checker gives for the priority experiment

For the priority construction, use the single verifier which guesses a modulus fitting a native word and checks the difference-encoded proof of the named pin through local clause generation. The [two-rank theorem](disjoint-winner-certificates.md) then applies under the hypothesis that ordinary encoded SAT takes $O(N^k)$ deterministic time. For the resulting fixed losing pair and a sufficiently large fixed $B$, both pins and their disjoint union require more than $M^{1/k}/(\log M)^B$ multiplier terms at all sufficiently late stages, over characteristic zero and over each fixed positive characteristic. The starting stage may depend on that characteristic. The same verifier can be added to another sound checker behind a format bit. Adding the polynomial calculus branch gives the same lower threshold for the number of lines plus monomial occurrences in an individual pin refutation over a specified fixed prime field. This branch may fix its prime in its program. The factor $k$ occurs only once because each certificate is checked directly. For the original full history encoding, any independent lower bound on distinct *axioms* also bounds this term count, since rows mention axioms individually. The general checking lemma applies equally to a different locally indexed encoding. Obtaining a small algebraic identity on such an encoding remains the missing upper bound in the SAT argument.

The formal statements take the incidence coefficients or the coordinate fibers as input.
