# Charging word values over every coefficient field on the same SAT files

The same family of proper ASCII 3-CNFs can demand a linear total degree of Boolean observations in **every field**, on **every** file of either verdict. The coefficient field may be selected after the particular file and its run are known. All observations on the resulting path use that one field. The sum of the degrees of all observations on any individually certifying transcript is $\Omega(N)$. The native consequence at any fixed degree cap \(d\ge1\) is again \(\Omega_d(N/\log N)\) charged instructions on each file. This extends the earlier quadratic argument for a packed inner product to arbitrarily prescribed bit-degree caps on a family that still carries the original SAT decision. The degree here is that of the restriction of a machine bit to the original input controls. [Earlier quadratic trace result](quadratic-explanations-of-packed-sat-runs.md) · [Packed control fields and native RAM model](processing-packed-control-words-in-dimacs.md)

## The same legal inputs for all fields

Use the packed decimal construction, with a cyclic accumulator of size fifteen. For total binary Hamming weight \(S\) set \(a=S\bmod3\) and \(b=S\bmod5\), using representatives \(0\le b<5\). Force a positive verdict whenever
\[
                    a+(b\bmod3)\equiv1\pmod3.
\]
Additionally accept at \((a,b)=(0,0)\) exactly when the original payload \(F\) is satisfiable. Force a negative verdict everywhere else. A fixed finite table controls the final state, with the source clauses guarded only at residue zero. The all-zero records transfer arbitrary SAT in linear encoded length and native time. The fifteen lookup tags have fixed width, and the physical file again contains \(m=\Theta(N)\) independent binary digit switches. [Renderer for both automata](../audits/packed_residue_dimacs.py)

Each fixed \(b\) column and each fixed \(a\) row contains both verdicts for either truth value of the payload. For a column there is one prescribed yes of the three \(a\)'s, with at most the additional yes at \((0,0)\). For a row, the residues of \(b\bmod3\) range over all three values. In the row \(a=0\), adding a yes at \(b=0\) still leaves, for example, \(b=2\) negative. A single modulo-three guard would lose its nonlinear-weight obstruction in characteristic three, where the residue itself is linear in the input bits.

Fix any file and any coefficient field \(E\). At least half the control digits on that file have one common bit value. If \(\operatorname{char}E\ne3\), partition such digits into groups of five, dropping fewer than five. Toggle all members of each group with its own fresh switch. Their net change in the total weight is \(+5\) or \(-5\) per toggle, with the same sign throughout. This holds \(b\) fixed and makes the verdict a nonconstant function of the new weight modulo \(q=3\). For \(\operatorname{char}E=3\) use groups of three instead. They hold \(a\) fixed and vary the new weight modulo \(q=5\). In both cases there are \(k\ge\lfloor m/10\rfloor\) switches through the designated file. All physical bits are affine over \(E\) in those switches on the Boolean cube. In both cases \(q\) is nonzero in the field. The witness cube may vary with the characteristic and with the designated file.

## A one-sided degree bound for modular weight

Let \(q\ge2\) be invertible in \(E\). If a polynomial \(P\) on the Boolean \(k\)-cube is nonzero somewhere and vanishes on all inputs with one specified weight residue modulo \(q\), then its multilinear degree \(D\) is at least \(\lfloor k/q\rfloor\). This estimate is a form of the established immunity bounds for modular counting. Beck and Li prove sharper, side-specific bounds in finite characteristic. Here is an elementary proof sufficient for the word-operation application. [Beck and Li, *Represent MOD function by low degree polynomial with unbounded one-sided error*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.0713) · [Related triple-flip proof for MOD3, Propositions 7–8](https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/68335/1/eprint.pdf)

For \(k\le2q-2\) the claim is immediate. When the claimed bound is one the cube already includes all residues, so a nonzero constant is impossible. Take \(k\ge2q-1\) and suppose \(D<\lfloor k/q\rfloor\). For any \(q\) selected coordinates subtract the restrictions with all of them zero from the restrictions with all of them one. Terms independent of those coordinates cancel. The difference has degree at most \(D-1\) and still vanishes on the same weight residue in \(k-q\) variables. The constant case is immediate. Otherwise induction implies this difference vanishes identically. Thus flipping any \(q\) identical zero coordinates to ones, or reversing such a move, preserves \(P\).

This also permits swapping any unequal coordinate pair. Among the other \(k-2\) coordinates are at least \(q-1\) spare zeros or \(q-1\) spare ones. With zeros, first turn on the initially zero member and those spares, then turn off the initially one member and the same spares. With ones, reverse the two moves. Permutations and the weight changes by \(q\) show that \(P\) is given by a single sequence \(f\) on the \(q\) weight residues.

Let \(\Delta f(i)=f(i+1)-f(i)\) cyclically. For every \(0\le i<q\) the mixed difference in \(D+1\) coordinates, with \(i\) other ones, is zero by the degree bound. There are enough coordinates since \(D+q\le k\). Symmetry identifies this difference with \(\Delta^{D+1}f(i)\). A cyclic difference sums to zero over its \(q\) entries. Thus no *nonzero* constant sequence can be a difference, since \(q\) is invertible. Taking the least positive power of \(\Delta\) annihilating \(f\) shows that \(f\) is already constant. This contradicts nonzero support together with the omitted residue, proving the estimate.

There is also an exact calculation for the omitted residue. For \(q\ge3\), \(k\ge q\) and \(0\le r<q\), let \(L\) count the weights in \([0,k]\) congruent to \(r\). The minimum degree of a nonzero polynomial vanishing on that residue is \(L\), except that it is \(L-1\) when \(r=0\) and \(q\mid k\). This is the shifted-residue calculation from the symmetrization method of Beck and Li. At zero residue it gives their \(\lceil k/q\rceil\). [Beck and Li, Theorem 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.0713)

When the polynomial is nonsymmetric, choose a transposition changing its value, subtract its restrictions on the two assignments \(01,10\) to that pair, and keep the nonzero difference. The degree drops by at least one. After \(j\) such steps a nonzero symmetric polynomial remains. The relevant original weight interval is now \([j,k-j]\). Let \(L_j\) count its target weights. The remaining polynomial has degree at least \(L_j\). Indeed the first \(L_j\) elementary symmetric polynomials starting with degree zero have values \(\binom{u}{i}\) at residual weight \(u\), for \(0\le i<L_j\). At the \(L_j\) target weights their determinant over the integers is \(q^{L_j(L_j-1)/2}\) by the Vandermonde determinant and the leading coefficients \(1/i!\) over the rationals. This integer is invertible in \(E\). The multilinear monomials of each degree have the same coefficient in a symmetric polynomial, so these evaluations detect every symmetric polynomial of smaller degree. The case \(L_j=0\) requires only nonnegativity of degree.

The original degree is at least \(j+L_j\). For \(j\ge2\), the two removed end intervals contain at most \(2\lceil j/q\rceil\le j\) target weights. For \(j=1\) the only way to lose two targets is the stated endpoint exception. Conversely a homogeneous linear system imposing \(L\) evaluations on the \(L+1\) independent symmetric polynomials of degrees zero through \(L\) has a nonzero solution. In the exceptional case use the factor \(x_1-x_2\) and a nonzero symmetric annihilator of degree at most \(L-2\) on the remaining \(k-2\) coordinates. When the two bits differ the discarded endpoints have removed two target levels from the remaining interpolation problem. Their disjoint variables ensure the product is nonzero somewhere. This proves the upper bounds.

## What is measured on a run

On the cube through the designated file, consider any adaptive transcript of Boolean answers \(b_i\in\{0,1\}\). Before the \(i\)th answer, suppose its queried bit agrees on the surviving assignments with a polynomial \(Q_i\) in the original switches of degree at most \(d_i\) over \(E\). Representatives may depend on the earlier answers and can be chosen specifically for this one path. They need only take the correct Boolean values on the then-surviving set. If the final verdict is fixed throughout the surviving set, then
\[
                            \sum_i d_i\ge\lfloor k/q\rfloor.
\]
Indeed form a product using \(Q_i\) for answer one and \(1-Q_i\) for answer zero. At each step this product remains the exact indicator of the matching partial transcript, by induction. It is one on the designated input. Its support omits at least one entire weight residue because the verdict is a nonconstant function of the residue and is constant on the support. Multilinear reduction never increases degree. The one-sided bound applies. A terminal output represented by a degree-\(d\) polynomial can be fixed by one additional Boolean observation of that degree.

For the RAM application, maintain polynomial representatives of degree at most \(d\) for the bits at fixed addresses and in registers on the current surviving set. A known-control step that preserves such representations may be free, even when its mathematical operation is nonlinear. Otherwise freeze bits of its bounded number of operands, branch conditions, addresses or selected words to their actual values. After resolving addresses the selected contents have known representatives. At most \(Cw\) such observations per charged step suffice to fix all newly computed words when necessary. Free word operations may only use the transcript already determined to select their actions. This gives \(d(Cws+1)\ge\lfloor k/q\rfloor\) on every returning run with \(s\) charged instructions. The register and memory hypotheses describe a relaxation that grants arbitrary bounded-degree features in the original controls along that path. Free composition of quadratic gates without a bound on the resulting degrees would be a different model.

This statement does not assume that a native SAT solver computes over any field or selects any polynomial. It restricts how much of its actual execution can be accounted for by bounded-degree word values without fixing additional operand bits. Setting \(d=1\) gives scan-order processing in every single characteristic. Disclosing the whole input a word at a time costs \(O(N/w)\) batches. With the truth of the payload supplied, word-table lookups compute the residue in that many charged instructions. Increasing the count to more than linear **time in the bit length** remains open.

In the bounded-degree relaxation with the payload verdict given, the dependence on \(d\) is also tight. Group \(d\) raw switches at a time. Each of the four binary bits of a group's weight modulo fifteen is a Boolean function of at most \(d\) bits, hence has a multilinear representative of degree at most \(d\) over any field by Boolean interpolation. Make these bits available for free. Fill a word with the residues of \(\Theta(w)\) groups, look up their combined residue in one fixed table, and accumulate. This costs \(O(1+N/(dw))\) charged operations. For \(1\le d=o(N)\), the lower inequality also forces at least one charged operation and gives the matching order for each known-payload cube. For \(d\) large enough to represent the entire decision even the final bit can be free.

Using a common coefficient field is essential. Over characteristic three the three indicators \(1-(S-i)^2\) give the residue of the total, and over characteristic five the five indicators \(1-(S-j)^4\) do the same modulo five. Here each \(S\) is the sum of all original binary controls in the indicated field. These are eight Boolean-valued features of degree at most four. A single constant-size truth table on their bits and the supplied payload verdict decides the entire fixed-payload cube. Thus even the all-fields statement has a concrete escape if different features can be taken in different characteristics at no cost.

The mod-15 audit checks 190 physical SAT decisions, including total weights zero through thirty under both payload answers. It checks exact byte locations for 225 disjoint copies of each digit bit on the exhaustively varied small cubes. The general all-fields immunity argument and native simulation are proved above.
