# Quadratic variable names and the two verdicts in matrix verification

Suppose a reduction to ordinary SAT fixes the clauses, signs and namespaces of its variable occurrences. The name at an occurrence is a tagged scalar polynomial of the inputs over a finite field. SAT can observe identifications of names within a namespace. We ask how cheaply this mechanism can verify an entire proposed matrix product, allowing arbitrary auxiliary SAT variables and even free SAT decisions.

Let $A,B,C$ be $d$-square matrices over $F=\mathbb F_q$, $m=d^2$, and $R_F(d)$ the exact bilinear tensor rank of matrix multiplication over this particular field at this particular dimension. Write $K$ for the number of distinct tagged name polynomials and $u$ for the number of used namespaces. Charge $S$ shared nonscalar multiplication gates in a division-free straight-line circuit producing all the names. Arbitrary affine operations are free. The degree hypothesis below is on the resulting formal names, even if intermediate degrees are larger. All results require correctness on every triple of field matrices.

* When **SAT means a correct product**, names of degree at most $D\ge1$ satisfy $D(K-u)\ge m$ and $D\sum_t\lfloor K_t^2/4\rfloor\ge(q-1)m$, where $K_t$ counts the tagged names in namespace $t$. These bounds hold even with unbounded arithmetic cost. For quadratic names over a prime field, $\Theta(d^2+d\sqrt p)$ names are necessary and sufficient.
* When **SAT means a product mismatch**, quadratic names satisfy $2(K-u)\ge m$. Let $M_0$ be the number of same-tag pairs whose polynomials differ *on the formal product graph*. There is a space of output functionals of dimension $r$ on which the joint bilinear product has tensor rank at most $2S$, with
  $$
                     q(m-r)\le 8M_0.
  $$
  For $q>d$ and $r\ge m/2$, this gives $R_F(d)\le 2S(1+\lceil\log_2 m\rceil)$. Thus over prime fields the minimum of $K+S$ is within logarithmic factors of
  $$
                        \min\{R_{\mathbb F_p}(d),\ d^2+d\sqrt p\}.
  $$
  Both bounds are finite-dimensional. They use no numerical assertion about the matrix-multiplication exponent.

The distinction comes from what happens to a satisfying assignment when names merge. The previous affine compiler theorem treated both verdicts using preservation of the entire partition. The earlier binary theorem classified quadratic equations required to vanish together. Here SAT may use the names in an arbitrary fixed CNF. ## One assignment on a correct product

Fix $A,B$, and write $Z=C-AB$. In every namespace subtract its first name from all its names. This common translation preserves the partition. When all $K-u$ resulting nontrivial differences retain their values at $Z=0$, SAT has the same answer as at zero. Thus the product of the indicators $1-(f(Z)-f(0))^{q-1}$ for these differences is nonzero precisely at zero. Every polynomial supported only at zero and nonzero there has degree at least $(q-1)m$. Indeed reduction by $Z_i^q=Z_i$ preserves its values and never increases degree. Its unique reduced representative is a nonzero scalar times $\prod_i(1-Z_i^{q-1})$, by univariate interpolation in each variable. This proves $D(K-u)\ge m$ for **either** labeling. [Finite-grid polynomial estimates of Bishnoi, Clark, Potukuchi and Schmitt](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06020)

Now assume the formula at zero is satisfiable. Color each tagged name by the bit of one satisfying assignment there. At any $Z$ where no oppositely colored same-tag names collide, these same bits give a well-defined assignment satisfying every clause. Correctness requires that every nonzero $Z$ cause at least one such collision. The product of the corresponding differences is therefore supported only at zero. Its factors are nonzero at zero. If there are $a_t$ names of one color in namespace $t$, its degree is at most $D\sum_t a_t(K_t-a_t)$. The finite-grid bound proves the claim for this individual witness.

For the matching prime-field construction compute the $m$ residuals $x_i=C_i-(AB)_i$, with $S\le R_{\mathbb F_p}(d)$. Choose $h=\max(1,\lceil\sqrt{p/m}\rceil)$ capped at $p-1$. Put $H=\{0,\dots,h-1\}$ and take $L$ to consist of the positive multiples of $h$ through $p-1$, together with $p-1$. Then $H\cap L=\emptyset$ and $L-H$ contains every integer from 1 to $p-1$. The successive endpoints in $L$ leave gaps of at most $h$. Use positive units at names in $L$ and negative units at every $x_i+a$ with $a\in H$, all in one namespace. These pins are compatible exactly when every residual vanishes. The count is $O(mh+p/h+1)=O(m+\sqrt{mp})$. A unit pin can be expanded into four proper width-three clauses with two auxiliary variables from other namespaces, using the same two auxiliary variables for every pin. This also bounds the number of literal occurrences to within a constant factor.

The positive case also has a sharp degree tradeoff. For $2\le D\le 2m$ and a prime field, the optimal number of names, allowing unlimited arithmetic, is
$$
            \Theta\!\left(m/D+\sqrt{mp/D}\right).
$$
Partition the residuals into groups of at most $b=\lfloor D/2\rfloor$. In a group of size $j$, regard its entries as the coordinates of one element of $\mathbb F_{p^j}$ in a fixed basis. Its field norm is zero precisely if the group vanishes, and is a homogeneous degree-$j$ formal polynomial in those coordinates over $\mathbb F_p$. Indeed it is the determinant of the $j$-by-$j$ matrix for multiplication by that element, whose entries are linear forms. Its degree in $A,B,C$ is at most $2j$. Apply the same pin construction to these $\lceil m/b\rceil$ scalars. The two witness lower bounds give the reverse order including $p=2$. This allows a single degree increase to combine independent error coordinates; it also exhibits exactly how much that buys in this degree range.

## A true point with few accidental equalities

For mismatch SAT a correct product gives an unsatisfiable formula. Preserving every name equality at this point suffices to preserve unsatisfiability, even if additional names merge. Otherwise any satisfying assignment to the more identified formula would pull back to the original one.

A formal polynomial $h(A,B,C)$ of total degree at most two that vanishes after $C=AB$ equals $\ell(C-AB)$ for a fixed linear functional $\ell$. To see this, decompose the substitution by bidegree in $A,B$. The quadratic part in $C$ is the only source of bidegree $(2,2)$. Its substitution vanishes formally, so setting $A=I$ kills that entire quadratic part as a polynomial. The mixed part in $A,C$ is the only source of $(2,1)$. Over the rational function field of a generic $A$, the linear change $B\mapsto AB$ is invertible, so this mixed part vanishes identically. The same argument eliminates $B,C$ in degree $(1,2)$. The remaining identity is $g(A,B)+\ell(AB)=0$, as required. This argument uses formal indeterminates and works in characteristic two.

Let $U$ be the span of the $\ell$ obtained from same-tag differences that vanish formally on the graph. Set $r=\dim U$ and $W=U^\perp$, of dimension $m-r$. The other $M_0$ differences restrict to nonzero polynomials of total degree at most four on the graph. Each vanishes on a uniformly sampled product with probability at most $4/q$, by the polynomial root bound. Choose one product with $E\le 4M_0/q$ accidental equalities. The inequality is valid, though weak, when $q\le4$.

Keep these factors fixed. On $Z\in W$ all formal equalities still hold. The $E$ accidental equalities have differences $h_j(Z)$ with $h_j(0)=0$. If all of them held at any nonzero $Z$, a wrong product would still give an unsatisfiable formula. In coordinates on $W$ the polynomial $\prod_{j=1}^E(1-h_j(Z)^{q-1})$ is consequently supported only at zero and nonzero there. Its degree is at most $2E(q-1)$. Hence $m-r\le2E\le8M_0/q$.

At $C=0$ the formal differences give $-\ell(AB)$ by free linear operations on the name outputs. Compute the coefficient of $tw$ at inputs $A=tX,B=wY$ modulo $t^2=w^2=0$. Each nonscalar product gate introduces at most two products of an $X$-linear form and a $Y$-linear form. The mixed outputs for the *whole* space $U$ have a common set of at most $2S$ such generators. ## Recovering the full product over the same finite field

Here is a finite-rank accounting for any subspace $U$ of requested matrix output functionals of dimension $r>0$. If its joint rank is $s$, the full product has rank at most $ks$ over any field of size $q>d$ whenever
$$
                         m(1-r/m)^k<1.
$$
For $r\ge m/2$, take $k=1+\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$. The restriction $q>d$ applies to this exact same-field construction.

Order the coefficient positions $(i,j)$ lexicographically, indexing them from zero. Take a row-echelon basis of $U$, with normalized leading positions $P$, $|P|=r$. Independent uniform row and column permutations send each given position into the image of $P$ with probability $r/m$. The expected number missed by $k$ independent translated copies is $m(1-r/m)^k$. Fix copies that cover all positions. Select one basis row per position from a copy where its permuted pivot is that position.

Before each copy's permutation, multiply the coefficients at $(i,j)$ by $a_i b_j$, using separate scalar indeterminates for each copy. Such coefficients are obtained by scaling input rows of the left factor and columns of the right factor and permuting inputs. The determinant of the $m$ selected coefficient rows is a nonzero polynomial. For a witness, specialize $a_i=t^{di}$ and $b_j=t^j$. Divide each selected row by the power of $t$ corresponding to its original pivot position. In the limit at zero one gets precisely the $m$ distinct chosen coordinate rows. Equivalently the normalized determinant has constant term $\pm1$. In the original determinant each $a_i$ or $b_j$ of each particular copy has individual degree at most $d$. A determinant term uses each output coordinate once and the copy's permuted row or column contains only $d$ positions. Since $q>d$, there is a specialization over $F$ with nonzero determinant. Zero scaling factors are allowed. Linear output decoding now recovers all $m$ entries, using at most $ks$ separable products. For context, partial matrix multiplication with selected summands in inner products is also studied in a different tensor model. [Vrana, partial and weighted matrix multiplication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15728)

For the cost lower bound, $2(K-u)\ge m$ always. If $r<m/2$, then $8\binom K2\ge8M_0>qm/2$. Otherwise use the preceding finite-rank bound when $q>d$. When $q\le d$, the $\Omega(m)$ bound already covers $m+\sqrt{mq}$. This proves the claimed minimum up to an $O(\log(m+1))$ lower-bound loss. For the first upper bound compute the residuals with $R_F(d)$ multiplications and give each residual its own namespace containing names $0,x_i$. Use an auxiliary choice of at least one index. At each chosen index enforce a positive pin on one name and a negative pin on the other. Such a choice exists precisely for a mismatch. The choice and guards have $O(m)$ width-three CNF size and $R_F(d)\ge m$ by output dimension. For the second upper bound the prime-field affine table construction uses $O((m+d\sqrt p)\log(p+1)+d\log^2(p+1))$ occurrences and zero nonscalar name products. In using this upper bound for the minimum one may take $p\le d^4$ since $R_F(d)\le d^3$, so the logarithmic factors can also be bounded in terms of $d+1$ alone. ## Consequence for the SAT search

The positive case rents no time from the solver at all. Even precomputing the product does not remove its name-count lower bound. In the mismatch case there is a choice between many names and supplying bilinear work before the SAT call. The costs refer to the compiler, with all linear arithmetic free. A deterministic word RAM receiving the resulting file can inspect every bit of an integer name, branch on it, and address arbitrary memory. Neither proof bounds any of those operations. In particular it does not show that a cheap Boolean verifier of binary matrix products has a quadratic-name representation. Establishing such a representation with the quantitative parameters above would be an additional theorem. Historical priority for these name-compiler bounds has not been established.
