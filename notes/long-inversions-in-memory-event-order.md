# Disjoint long inversions in the memory-event order of a SAT solver

Fix a correct deterministic RAM program for ordinary DIMACS SAT with unrestricted code size and workspace. Use the [dense list projection and bounded-trace construction](sparse-errors-in-memory-event-order.md). At bit length $n$, word width $w=\Theta(\log n)$ and polynomial deadline $T$, there are $m=\lceil n/w\rceil+O(T)$ word-sized rows: physical initializers, chronological access slots and labeled idle slots. Their unique IDs distinguish every row. The canonical key orders active rows by bank, address and time, then idle rows by ID. Let $g(w)$ be the Boolean gate cost of one deterministic CPU step given its memory replies. A predictor of size $s$ must always permute its supplied chronological rows, including for fabricated replies. It may use the input and the reply guesses. In the main theorem it has no oracle for the sorted table. Any fixed permutation costs zero gates.

Write $P$ for its predicted row sequence. A **long inversion** at distance $b$ is a pair of positions $i<j$ with $j-i>b$ and canonical key $P_i>P_j$. Let $\nu_b(P)$ be the maximum number of such pairs with all endpoints distinct.

**Theorem.** Fix one target verdict, $1\le b\le m$ and $0\le r\le m$. If every projected input of that verdict halts by $T$ and has $\nu_b(P)\le r$, then
$$
 n+Tg(w)+s+ mw\log_2(b+2)+rw\log_2(r+2)
       =\Omega(nw^\sigma).
$$
Here $0<\sigma<1$ is any sufficiently small exponent supplied by the November 24 list-distinctness tensor theorem. The statement holds for each sufficiently large byte-aligned length in the projection, and for either verdict separately. It uses no upper bound on the work RAM beyond its word addressing. [List theorem](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/build/obstruction.tex#L40-L79) · [SAT projection and encoding](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md)

In particular suppose $T=O(n/w)$, $g(w)=w^{1+o(1)}$, and $s=O(n)$. For some constants $c,c'>0$, for every such predictor and each target verdict there is a projected input of that verdict which either takes longer than $T$ or has at least $c'm/w^{1-\sigma}$ **pairwise disjoint inversions of span greater than $2^{c w^\sigma}$ on a single run**. Choose $b=\lfloor 2^{c w^\sigma}\rfloor$ and $r=\lfloor c'm/w^{1-\sigma}\rfloor$, taking the constants small in the displayed bound. Small adjustments to $c$ absorb rounding. Each of these pairs has at least one endpoint displaced by more than $b/2$ from its canonical rank. The sum of both endpoint displacements in the pair is greater than $b$. Hence total absolute rank displacement is $\Omega(m2^{c_1w^\sigma})$ for a smaller $c_1>0$. Adjacent transpositions can produce maximum positional Hamming distance while having zero long inversions at $b=1$.

## Reducing long inversions to deletions by two local sorts

Divide positions into blocks of length $b$, padding at the end with distinct keys above every genuine key to a multiple of $2b$. First sort the disjoint block pairs $(0,1),(2,3),\ldots$. Then sort the staggered pairs $(1,2),(3,4),\ldots$. The increasing padding stays at the end. Let $P'$ be the true output restricted to the original length. The two stages are deterministic circuits of $O(mw\log(b+2))$ gates by word comparators and the AKS sorting network. They act on entire rows. [Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808726)

Suppose $d$ rows meet every long inversion of $P$; call the others good. A good pair from two nonadjacent original blocks is increasing, because its positions differ by at least $b+1$. After the first stage the only possible inversions among good rows are across consecutive sorted blocks of length $2b$. For such a block $A$, let $d_L,d_R$ count designated rows in its original left and right halves. Every good row of its original left half is smaller than every good row in the next $2b$-block. All good rows of $A$ that invert with that next block therefore come after at least $b-d_L$ rows in sorted $A$. At most $d_L$ offenders can lie in its first half. Symmetrically at most $d_R$ good rows in its last half can invert with the preceding block. Remove the original designated rows and these offenders for the purpose of the argument. This costs at most $2d$ across all blocks. Each remaining inversion lies between the right half of one block and the left half of its successor. The second stage sorts those halves together, so deleting the same row identities from $P'$ leaves an increasing subsequence. An endpoint set of any maximal disjoint family of long inversions meets all long inversions. Thus the minimum deletions needed to sort $P'$ are at most $4\nu_b(P)$. The factor two for a given cover is sharp: at $b=1$ the sequence $(1,3,2,0)$ has the long inversions covered by its last row, while the two stages produce $(1,0,3,2)$ of deletion distance two.

## Checking a nearly increasing event sequence with a unique certificate

Here is the verifier that gives a linear base cost. For $m$ distinct rows of $O(w)$ bits, suppose removing $k$ of them would make their canonical keys increase. A deterministic streaming check on the true sorted sequence with $O(w)$ bits of state and $O(w)$ Boolean gates per row can be verified by a circuit of
$$
 O(mw+kw\log(k+2))
$$
gates and guessed bits in total, with one witness on the covered successful inputs. On arbitrary sequences soundness still holds. The following construction accepts when the streaming check succeeds and a particular deterministic stack process has at most $k$ pops. That process has at most $k$ pops under the deletion hypothesis.

1. Scan the supplied sequence with a stack of increasing keys, initially empty. Push the current row if it exceeds the top or the stack is empty. Otherwise pop the top and discard both that top and the current row. Each pop identifies a disjoint pair of rows inverted in their original sequence. Any deletion set making the sequence increasing must meet every such pair. The survivors, read in input order, are increasing. Thus the stack discards at most twice the minimum deletion count. The deletion count is the classical distance to monotonicity. [Ergün and Jowhari](https://homes.luddy.indiana.edu/fergun/PUBLICATIONS/lis.pdf)
2. Implement this stack with sparse receipts. Record the current top as its input position and key, or an empty sentinel. Only a pop needs an unknown restored top. At each push guess whether it eventually gets popped. For a flagged push at position $i$, emit the receipt $(i,\mathrm{top\ before\ pushing})$. For each pop, emit $(\mathrm{index\ of\ top},\mathrm{guessed\ restored\ top})$. Bound each number of emitted receipts by $k$, then check the two multisets for equality. One-bit-key compaction followed by deterministic sorting of at most $k$ complete receipts costs $O(mw+kw\log(k+2))$. At every unused guess require the sentinel or zero encoding. To prove uniqueness, consider the first incorrect restoration. The top about to be popped is still genuine, so equality of receipts forces exactly the top that preceded its unique push. Induction now fixes all restorations, and multiset equality forces exactly the flags on pushes. A zero cap amounts to requiring that neither side emits a receipt. [Asharov, Lin and Shi, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09884#page=4)
3. Keep the unflagged pushes as the increasing subsequence, in place in the original $m$ slots. Compact the at most $2k$ discarded rows. The subsequence has a gap before its first row, and one after every retained row, including its last. Name each gap by its preceding retained row's original position, using zero before the first. For each discarded row guess one gap and sort the small array by gap then key. Each nonempty group thereby arrives in order. Give the streaming checker a state component enforcing strict increase of the complete canonical key, including across group boundaries, and propagate any rejection.
4. Run two deterministic scans with matching boundary receipts. The main scan processes the retained rows in place, skipping all discarded slots. At the initial sentinel and after each retained row guess a flag for a nonempty group there. If flagged, emit its gap label, the current checker state, and its guessed state after that gap; continue in the latter state. In the small array start each nonempty group with a guessed incoming state, process the whole group, and emit its label, incoming state and computed outgoing state. Match these two receipt multisets by the same sparse sorter. Force inactive flags, gap labels, state guesses and padding bits to their specified defaults. The main scan also checks its final state.

An accepted witness puts every original row in the combined traversal exactly once. Equality of boundary triples stitches the two scans into the deterministic execution on that traversal. The strict key test forces its order to be canonical. There is only one possible gap for each removed row in the canonical order, even with no retained rows. Every flag and boundary state is now forced. The stack-pop and gap checks both use only sparse sorting. This avoids a stable global compaction of the retained rows, which would require a separate circuit bound.

Apply this verifier to $P'$ with cap $\min(m,4r)$. The checker in canonical order verifies every read reply against the last write or its physical initialization as in the previous event-order construction. Each CPU step with guessed replies is deterministic. Therefore every successfully checked transcript is the genuine run, and the entire witness on that run is unique. For the accepted verdict of the list projection the Boolean indicator is the duplicate predicate; for the rejecting verdict it is distinctness. Over $\mathbb F_2$ the trivalent tensor for the unique verifier sums to its indicator. On the duplicate side subtract that value from one by a selector with a single all-zero dummy witness on the constant branch. Under the coverage premise this computes the list-distinctness tensor in both cases. Applying its lower bound gives the theorem.

The predictor here cannot require a supplied canonical sorted array as an input. The verifier checks the canonical traversal without materializing that array. The next test covers that stronger predictor interface.

## Long displacements even for a predictor that sees a candidate sorted array

Suppose the predictor may also see the guessed canonical array $B$, as in the [earlier positional error test](sparse-errors-in-memory-event-order.md). The same tradeoff holds if $r$ bounds the number of predicted rows whose true destination is more than $b$ positions away. For each predicted index $i$ guess a destination $j$, and for each canonical index $j$ guess the inverse source $i$, rejecting out-of-range integers. Form the two sets of triples $(i,j,\mathrm{whole\ row})$. A near triple has $|i-j|\le b$. Its two length-$b$ block numbers differ by at most one. For each ordered pair of equal or neighboring blocks, use fixed wires from those two blocks, mask absent triples to an inactive symbol, and compare their full sorted lists padded to length $b$. Each block serves at most three pairs on each side. The remaining far triples are compacted globally, checked against the cap $r$, sorted and compared. This costs $O(mw\log(b+2)+rw\log(r+2))$. Initial and final arrays may use a shorter last block, padded for this comparison.

Equality of all bucket and far lists forces equality of the complete triple multisets. The distinct IDs uniquely determine both maps on a genuine match. The direct checker of sorted $B$ and the CPU transcript therefore retains soundness and one witness even when the predictor reads $B$. This gives the long-displacement and total-displacement conclusions above for that interface. It does not establish the long disjoint inversion count with that additional oracle.

## Limit of the native-time inference

A fixed program writing addresses $m-1,m-2,\ldots,0$ in order realizes a fully reversed order on those writes in $O(m)$ native steps. This exhibits linear native cost for a sequence containing $\Theta(m)$ disjoint inversions of span $\Theta(m)$ against chronological order. A predictor hardwiring this particular reversal handles it exactly. The theorem quantifies separately over every cheap predictor for SAT and the input witnessing its failure. Neither reversal alone nor the bit cost of verifying the order implies an extra native step. The unrestricted linear-time SAT milestone remains open. [Earlier offset family against fixed packet routing](offset-route-tax.md)

The [finite audit](../audits/long_inversion_event_audit.py) tested the two staggered sorts against the exact minimum long-inversion cover on 40,319 permutation/block-width pairs through size seven. It exhaustively checked that only the true stack witness passes the receipt test on 153 permutations through size five, including guesses that restore a future position, and tested 2,065 assignments to gaps. [Lean](../lean/LongInversionEvents.lean) proves the greedy pop count for arbitrary-length sequences and any proposed deletion cover. The sparse-circuit construction and full RAM consequence remain manuscript arguments. Priority of the combined verifier and application is unestablished.
