# Learning the true winner at positive stages

The [past-conditioned native program](learning-from-earlier-sat-verdicts.md) chooses an $h$-bit word $a_j$ at stage $j$. Its word reporters are ordinary deterministic RAM programs. The first active, unretired reporter to finish within its own budget wins, supplies $a_j$, and retires for good. Otherwise the stage takes a default word. The entire genuine earlier history is available to the reporters. Write $M=2^j$ and fix a polynomial-in-$j$ procedure producing a trial word $p_j$ with $F(p_j)=1$, where $F(u,v,w)=\operatorname{Tr}(uvw)$ on the leading three field elements. To use the even-degree certificate classification on every late stage, take $m=2\lfloor h/6\rfloor$, and let $F$ ignore the remaining hidden bits. For example take $u=v=1$, choose a trace-one basis element for $w$, and zero the remaining bits. The field degree is $O(\log j)$, so these choices, including the defining modulus from the [field-query construction](trace-product-quadratic-correlation.md), fit the budget. All statements concern sufficiently large stages with positive field degree.

Give a verifier the **true current winning rank** and the entire genuine past at every override. It may ask truthful quadratic questions about the current word and then certify a conclusion, or abstain. Soundness is required only when the supplied rank really wins, the answers are truthful, and the verifier signals; it covers every allowed nondeterministic choice at such inputs. In particular there is no requirement at a default stage, nor on a false proposed winner. Use finite, explicitly encoded conclusions and queries with polynomial-in-$j$ trial evaluation, for example a quadratic question, the cubic output sign, or exclusion of one specified word.

**Assume an unrestricted deterministic $O(N^k)$ explicit SAT solver for $k\in\{1,2,3\}$. There is a single increasing subsequence of actual override stages at which $a_j=p_j$ such that every fixed sound verifier, on all sufficiently late stages of this subsequence, can certify only conclusions forced by its transcript at the clock $\lfloor 2^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}/(j+1)^{B_V}\rfloor$.** Here $B_V$ may depend on the verifier and the assumed solver. More precisely, if one fixes the correct past, the true winner identifier, the physical formula, and the verifier's choices, every timely claim holds for every trial word giving the recorded current replies. The same statement is unconditional for fixed deterministic verifiers at $2^j/(j+1)^{B_V}$. The subsequence for simultaneous coverage is existential. The construction below also gives a computable family of designated ranks for each supplied finite collection of sound verifiers and fixed solver.

In particular, without current field queries even the weak statement that one specified word is excluded cannot be certified there. At an even field degree $m\ge4$, a positive certificate using quadratic answers still needs at least $m/2+1$ of them. The supplied winner identifier already resolves whether the stage is an override and which candidate outranked the other reporters. This tests an approach to authentic positive stages without charging anything for those scheduler decisions. [Quadratic positive-certificate bound](trace-product-positive-certificates.md)

## A word reporter that actually wins

Fix a finite list of sound verifiers. Put a tagged family of specialized programs into the original word-reporter enumeration. Each obtains its own actual rank $e$ by comparing its specialized text with the active texts, using the same finite-template self-application construction as the [synchronized trial programs](synchronized-priority-trials.md). Distinct literal tags give distinct indices in length order. A rank acts only on a legal stage, using the actual past passed to it. Every copy uses the following search before it emits **any** word.

Ask whether some trial word $y$ and some bounded branch of a listed verifier lead to a claimed conclusion which is *false of $y$*. Feed the verifier the actual past, the stage, and $e$ as the alleged genuine winner. Respond to its queries using $y$. Generate its physical stage formula words from their genuine public layout without changing them when the trial changes. Fix a bounded universal verifier for the list, with cap $T=\lfloor 2^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}/(j+1)^B\rfloor$ on its simulated work. Include all transcript simulation and predicate evaluation inside the bounded source computation or in a fixed polynomial-in-$j$ overhead per step. A cap it cannot implement, or an incompletely evaluated SAT test, may cause the rank to idle. On a completed negative existence test, emit $p_j$. On a completed positive test, recover a falsifying trial $y$ by at most $h$ further existence queries with successively longer prescribed prefixes, and emit exactly $y$.

These are ordinary SAT calls implemented by the hypothesized deterministic program. The [packed bounded-computation compiler](packed-band-transfer.md) produces encoded length $(T+\operatorname{poly}(j))(j+1)^{O(1)}$ for the existence test, with short public parameters and prefix. Public data include the past, the verifier codes, and $e$. One can charge virtual formula-word generation at polynomial cost in $j$ per access. For a fixed list and a sufficiently large $B$, total running time is $o(M)$. Equivalently use one bounded universal interpreter with a literal list of normalized program texts as data. Active reporter text has length $O(\log(j+2))$, since its index is at most $j$. Decoding that list, bounded-program simulation, and the fixed query and conclusion syntax have uniform polynomial overhead in $j$. This permits one conservative $B$ for such lists, with a fixed verifier's interpretation cost absorbed by increasing its $B_V$. Alternatively, for each list one can use separate per-verifier cutoffs with a fixed power chosen for each verifier, running finitely many existence searches in sequence. The sum of finitely many $o(M)$ clocks is $o(M)$ for that copy. The latter implementation covers distinct fixed polynomial-time trial evaluators without requiring a single evaluation exponent for all verifier syntax. For a deterministic verifier enumerate the polynomially many trial words instead of calling SAT, charging its own fixed interpretation overhead.

If $e$ wins on the positive-search branch, the word it found becomes the actual current word. Given the actual winning rank, the corresponding verifier branch sees precisely those trial replies and genuine physical inputs. The conclusion it claims is false. This contradicts the stipulated soundness, so no win by this copy can occur on that branch. In particular, even proving only a conditional of the form “if this rank wins, it cannot return $y$” is covered. At every win of this copy the negative-search result is genuine, it returns $p_j$, and no tested path on *any* trial word leads to a false conclusion.

Every fixed copy eventually wins. Otherwise it is unretired forever. Its index is eventually activated. Only finitely many smaller indices ever win, as each retires permanently, so after their last such win this copy is reached unless it itself wins. At all sufficiently large stages its entire search and emission fit its own $\lfloor M/2^{e+1}\rfloor$ interpreter ticks. Thus it would win at one of those stages, a contradiction. Each copy wins only once. Infinitely many copies therefore give infinitely many distinct genuine positive overrides, even when the activation rule grows arbitrarily slowly. This does not give a fast stage selector: the winning date of a tagged copy depends on the other active ranks.

## Confining the positive date to a short public window

For any fixed finite list of verifiers the tags can locate the winning date to within $e+1$ explicit stages. Use the same instruction body across all its tags, with the literal tag as data. Wait without emitting until stage $J_e$, the first $j$ with $e<R(j)$, $e+1\le\lfloor\log_2(j+2)\rfloor$, $G(j)\ge e+1$, and $j\ge J_0$. Here $R$ is the original activation bound and $G$ is any prescribed positive, nondecreasing, unbounded function computable in polynomial time in $j$. All conditions are public and persist. Recognizing specialized text, computing its rank and operating with that index costs at most a uniform polynomial in $j$ in this family. Its active text has logarithmic length and the tagged template is fixed with its finite list. Choose the time exponents so that the full search and emission cost at most $M/(j+2)^2$ interpreter ticks for sufficiently large $j$, uniformly in the tag satisfying these start tests. This follows from the same compiler bound, taking fixed slack to absorb uniform interpretation and prefix recovery. A constant $J_0$ can cover the finite startup. Such a cutoff exists for each fixed solver and finite list; the construction does not compute a runtime coefficient from a black-box assertion that an arbitrary solver is fast. Under the separate-search implementation for the simultaneous theorem one can reserve a summable part of the $M/(j+2)^2$ budget for each successive verifier, increasing $J_0$ for the whole fixed list to absorb its constants.

The rank retains its ordinary budget of at least $M/(j+2)$ after $J_e$. From then on its raw trial always finishes and offers a word. It has never offered before $J_e$. On every successive stage at which it is still unretired and does not win, a distinct smaller rank wins and retires. There are only $e$ smaller indices. Thus it wins **exactly once** during

$$
                         J_e,J_e+1,\ldots,J_e+e .
$$

This identifies a public search problem on at most $G(J_e)$ dates, with a unique correct answer to the question of which date this rank wins. Other dates in the window may also have positive cubic sign. The starts can be recognized from their stage parameters in polynomial time in the stage, or found by scanning from zero through $J_e$ in time polynomial in $J_e$. A deadline from the future is not part of the trusted history at a candidate earlier date.

There is a version of this window with **no SAT hypothesis**: use tagged reporters which, after the same public start, simply return $p_j$. They all genuinely win in their windows by the identical counting proof. For this family, any uniformly sound nondeterministic certificate *naming the date that a designated rank wins* on infinitely many distinct tags, checked **at the named date with only its true past** in the rooted clock, would exclude $O(N^k)$ SAT. To see this, at stage $j$ guess an active tag, check its template and date condition, then run that certificate. Each accepted branch certifies the actual positive cubic sign with no current teacher answers, contradicting the [unconditional retirement after SAT elimination](learning-from-earlier-sat-verdicts.md). This is a search among an arbitrarily slowly growing public number of candidates, all belonging to genuine computations. The pigeonhole argument certifies the disjunction and uniqueness without giving a timely membership certificate at the winning date.

The candidates can also be concentrated near their public starts. To make the windows disjoint in rank order, let $A_e$ be the first stage satisfying the start conditions above and take $J_e=(e+2)^2(A_e+1)$ instead. The $A_e$ are nondecreasing, so the intervals $[J_e,J_e+e]$ are pairwise disjoint. A reporter can determine whether its new starting condition has been met by scanning the start tests through the current stage, still in polynomial time in $j$. For a prefix of ranks $0,\ldots,E$, let $H$ be the number of designated tags in it and $d_e$ the actual offset of each designated winner. Then

$$
                  \sum_{e\le E\ \mathrm{designated}}d_e\le E+1-H .
$$

Each date before the designated win inside its window consumes a distinct smaller rank. No designated rank can consume any such date, since each already wins in its own disjoint window and can win only once. In particular the number of designated wins occurring at offset at least $L\ge1$ is at most $(E+1-H)/L$. This bound holds for the given rank numbering without a density assumption. In an externally delimited length-lex encoding reserving a fixed prefix followed by arbitrary tag data for this template, $H\ge c(E+1)$ for a constant $c>0$ in every sufficiently long prefix. A constant initial number of dates then suffices for any prescribed fraction below one of the designated wins, by choosing $L$ accordingly. Density here requires the stated program encoding; the unique-winner bound per window does not. Certifying that a particular date in the initial portion is the actual designated win remains unresolved. [Prefix families of program texts](first-failure-hall-witnesses.md)

One can check precisely what the retirement count alone contributes. For designated ranks $e_1<\cdots<e_s$ in disjoint windows, the possible delay sequences in an abstract schedule with freely chosen offers from all other ranks are exactly the nonnegative integers satisfying $\sum_{i\le h}d_i\le e_h+1-h$ for every $h$. For sufficiency, at window $h$ use $d_h$ as-yet unused nondesignated ranks below $e_h$, one on each initial date; all other nondesignated ranks idle on this window. The prefix inequality guarantees enough ranks. If the actual history at $J_e$ is already known, let $U_e$ be the number of smaller ranks still unretired there, excluding any designated ranks known to wait past this window. The same argument reduces the deadline to $J_e+U_e$. Every offset from zero to $U_e$ is consistent with that history if further offers are allowed to vary freely. Selecting an earlier date from the actual candidate set requires using facts about the values or timing of the real programs. This finite completion argument makes no claim that a fixed real program can arbitrarily vary its future offers.

**Unconditional cost of locating the date.** In the simple-reporter version, give a deterministic finder $A$ the rank $e$, the public start $J_e$, and the true history through stage $J_e-1$. Use the native start scale with $\Theta(J_e)$-bit words, equivalently giving an inert zero array of length $2^{J_e}$ with its length available. It may abstain and must always be correct when it names a date. On every sufficiently late successful tag, it uses at least $c_A 2^{J_e+d_e}$ native steps, with fixed $c_A>0$. A single exact finder on the same promise attains $O(2^{J_e+d_e})$ on every tag. Thus the lower bound matches the cost of progressing to the winning date even when the window is very short.

For the lower bound, a fixed ordinary word reporter at stage $j$ enumerates the at most $j$ active indices, tests the delayed template and finds the unique window containing $j$, if any. The construction of the disjoint public starts and all history-prefix preparation cost polynomial time in $j$. Run $A$ at the width of its stated start scale on that tag and the actual prefix through $J_e-1$. Give it no subsequent history. A fixed-program simulation can mask to the smaller word width at constant native cost per instruction, using the ordinary sparse-bank convention. If $A$ names $j$, report the zero word, for which $F=0$. This reporter can never win: the correctness promise on the genuine prefix would force that very stage to be the designated positive win. After activation and the last preceding-rank award it is reached at every stage, with a fixed positive fraction of $2^j$ interpreter ticks. Its polynomial setup and emission time are $o(2^j)$. Thus a successful run of $A$ on a sufficiently late tag takes $\Omega_A(2^j)$ at its true winning date $j=J_e+d_e$. The argument uses one fixed contradictory word without enumerating counterfactuals. For the upper bound, start with the given history and simulate the actual scheduler on successive stages, stopping on the first award to $e$. The sum of their clocks is $O(2^{J_e+d_e})$. At most $e\le\log_2(J_e+2)$ future stage increments are needed, so all host word widths can be represented by a fixed number of start-width limbs. A sparse bank with a fixed number of radix levels simulates the later addresses; the start-width host needs space for the corresponding allocated nodes. If necessary choose the host word-width coefficient larger than the fixed scheduler coefficient, which is permitted for the finder and suffices since $j/J_e\to1$. The lower bound applies to each such fixed finder. This search theorem measures native time on stage parameters and a supplied true history. It does not measure SAT time on the encoded formula.

## A short list suffices even if it keeps every earlier date

Stay with the unconditional simple positive tags and their disjoint windows. At a start $J_e$ allow an ordinary nondeterministic verifier, given that start's true past, to certify a set $L\subseteq[0,e]$ of offsets. Soundness requires $d_e\in L$ on every accepted branch; the verifier may have no accepting branch on any number of tags. The set can be guessed as an explicit bit mask of length $e+1$. There are no current field answers or future history at issuance. Use the start clock $T_e=\lfloor 2^{\lfloor J_e/k\rfloor}/(J_e+1)^B\rfloor$ with a sufficiently large fixed $B$ for the verifier and the reduction below.

It suffices, to exclude unrestricted $O(N^k)$ SAT, that a uniformly sound verifier have a timely accepted list with **at most $K$ elements larger than $d_e$** on infinitely many distinct tags, for any one constant $K$. In particular, any bounded-size sound shortlist suffices. So does certifying a valid deadline $J_e+h$ that exceeds the true date by at most a constant infinitely often: use $L=\{0,\ldots,h\}\cap[0,e]$. These conditions do not require the verifier to mark the winner, recognize a tight deadline, or ever know how many dates in its list precede the win. Equivalently, under the SAT hypothesis, for every fixed $K$ only finitely many tags have *any* timely accepted sound list with at most $K$ later elements.

For the proof, let $s$ be the least tail size that occurs on infinitely many tags among timely accepted lists. Under the sufficient condition such an $s\le K$ exists by finite pigeonhole. Each smaller tail size occurs on only finitely many tags. A single fixed program can omit their finite union by a fixed initial cutoff; no uniform algorithm to find this cutoff is asserted. On every remaining tag, every timely accepted list has at least $s$ offsets strictly after $d_e$. Deleting its largest $s$ elements always leaves $d_e$ in the list, and on infinitely many tags some timely list now has $d_e$ as its largest element.

At any candidate date $j$ in the unique public window, read the genuine past to check that $e$ has not already won. Nondeterministically guess $L$ and a timely successful start-verifier branch, replaying it only with the history prefix through $J_e-1$. Check the cutoff and the start clock even when $j>J_e$. Delete the largest $s$ elements and accept when the last remaining offset is $j-J_e$. The start certificate forces the win to occur no later than $j$, while the genuine past rules out every earlier date. Thus every accepting branch identifies the actual positive win. On the infinitely many tags with a minimum-tail list, its branch accepts at the true date. The wrapper guesses and handles only $O(e+1)$ mask bits besides the original verifier, and its start-clock simulation fits the corresponding rooted clock at $j$ after reserving a fixed power of $j+1$ for interpretation and compilation. Under the fast-SAT hypothesis one ordinary bounded-acceptance SAT call at $j$ detects this possible branch in $o(2^j)$ time. A word reporter can return zero when the call accepts, contradicting positivity exactly as above. For deterministic lists at a sufficiently subnative start clock the same contradiction needs no SAT assumption. The unconditional concentration estimate for the first $L$ dates across tags does not certify coverage of any particular accepted list.

## Disjunctions about future ordinary SAT formulas

The same proof gives a test on the original past-conditioned cubic family with **no tagged reporters in the statement**. At start $i$, given its genuine history through $i-1$, a verifier may certify a list of distinct dates $i\le j_1<\cdots<j_b$ with the claim that at least one of their actual positive cubic pins is satisfiable. Every accepted branch must give a true disjunction. It may choose the dates adaptively using the history or guesses. No current or future replies are supplied. The dates are explicitly written in binary within the start verifier's budget, and the family generates the indicated ordinary formula from each date. There need not be a unique positive or a recognizable class of winning programs.

It suffices for the $O(N^k)$ SAT exclusion that such a verifier certify a disjunction of **two**, or any bounded number of, future positive pins on infinitely many distinct starts at clock $2^{\lfloor i/k\rfloor}/(i+1)^B$, for a sufficiently large fixed $B$. More generally it suffices that a timely accepted disjunction on infinitely many starts have only a bounded number of list entries after its *first* true disjunct. The length and position of its initial false segment can both grow.

Take the smallest tail count recurring on infinitely many distinct starts among all timely accepted lists. Hardwire out the finite initial segment containing all occurrences of smaller counts, and remove that many largest dates. Every resulting list still contains its original first true disjunct. At a candidate final date $j$, a bounded verifier guesses $i\le j$ and a branch of the start verifier, reproducing its behavior with just the true history through $i-1$ and the proper start clock. Check that the last retained date is $j$ and that *every earlier retained date has a false pin*, using the real past words supplied at $j$. Soundness of the reduced disjunction now implies $F(a_j)=1$. For the infinitely recurring minimum tail, the retained last date is the first true one, so a successful branch exists there. Unbounded starts give unbounded such dates. Even if some discarded date is far beyond $j$, it is only handled as binary text; its future formula and verdict are never evaluated. Sorting can be avoided by requiring increasing order and checking it. Checking all dates uses their total length times a polynomial in $j$, including evaluation at prior words, and fits the rooted clock with fixed logarithmic slack. SAT elimination and the one-word contradiction proceed as before. The same reasoning permits a specified zero or one pin at each date, provided there is only one per date and the reporter can choose a contrary word for that pin.

This leaves a weaker proof obligation than identifying a particular satisfiable formula at its date: a single uniformly sound proof system may certify, infinitely often and with ordinary bounded witnesses, that *one of two later positive pins* is satisfiable. The scarcity of exceptions or the empirical frequency of the positive stages alone does not certify those two-formula disjunctions. The use of the authentic earlier words at the final date is essential; the statement here is for the past-conditioned family.

One can also use the disjuncts $a_{j_r}\ne u_r$ for explicit proposed words. Each earlier disjunct is testable on the actual past, and when the remaining claim concerns the present word the reporter can emit $u_r$ to contradict it. Thus **a sound short certificate that a specified pair of words fails to occur jointly on two specified different future dates** would suffice on infinitely many starts. Neither word need have a candidate proof of exclusion by itself. For example, if both target words are known to be unavailable on their respective default branches, a sound certificate that the same single rank would have to win both dates to produce both words suffices, because a rank can win only once. Establishing that restriction on possible sources for real reports remains necessary.

There is an unconditional recurrence consequence on the genuine word sequence. Fix any constant $K\ge1$. A polynomial-in-$i$-time rule at start $i$ can use the actual past to select or skip the start, and on a selected start specify $K$ distinct increasing future dates (allowing $i$ itself) and one target word of the correct length for each. If infinitely many starts are selected, **all $K$ target words occur together on infinitely many selected lists**. Otherwise, past a fixed cutoff the deterministic rule would supply a sound disjunction of $K$ word exclusions on every selected start. Apply the minimum-tail argument above. At stage $j$ enumerate all starts $i\le j$, using only the genuine prefix belonging to each start and its polynomial-time rule. This costs polynomial time in $j$, including reading the binary dates and words the rule can print. A reporter gets a sound current-word exclusion on infinitely many stages, contradicting retirement unconditionally. This extends the [single-word recurrence](learning-from-earlier-sat-verdicts.md) to finite lists. It also follows by induction: at the final date one can test the earlier target hits from the genuine past, then apply single-word recurrence on the resulting selected dates.

In particular choose $p_j$ by the public trace-one rule above. One fixed mask reporter prints the singleton $\{p_j\}$ on the no-advice channel at every late stage. Its density eventually falls below that reporter's fixed admission threshold, and writing the whole mask takes only polynomial time in $j$. The default therefore never equals $p_j$ at late stages. Taking $K$ consecutive dates in the recurrence theorem gives infinitely many runs of $K$ **consecutive genuine positive overrides**, with the entire word prescribed in advance on each date. This holds for every fixed $K$, even with an arbitrarily slowly growing bound $R(J)$ on the cumulative number of overrides. The recurrence supplies neither a future start date nor a short witness identifying one.

Prescribing finitely many future values is an established operation in resource-bounded genericity. Here the prescribed values are full varying-length native words, the history includes actual winner identifiers, and the nondeterministic exclusion consequence uses the assumed encoded SAT clock. The recurrence alone does not establish historical novelty. [Ambos-Spies on resource-bounded genericity and finite extensions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/computability-enumerability-unsolvability/resource-bounded-genericity/D01C9FD0FE33A6AADFA19B240542421C)

## A subsequence for all sound verifiers

Semantically sound verifiers are countable. Fix an enumeration as a mathematical sequence, with no effectiveness assumption on recognizing soundness. For its first $s$ members use a finite-list family of copies. Assign each member its own sufficiently conservative cutoff exponent once, retaining that exponent on all larger lists; the separate-search implementation above has this property. Always finish all necessary tests before emitting $p_j$. If any test succeeds, emit one certified falsifying trial instead. For every fixed list infinitely many copies have distinct winning stages, and all sufficiently late such wins meet the intended cutoffs for that finite list. Choose one later than the preceding selected stage for each successive $s$. On the resulting subsequence each fixed sound procedure is covered eventually. Alternatively, one can include both a target word pattern and an admissibility predicate for the stage in a tagged family and idle off that predicate. The winning argument then requires infinitely many admissible stages on the actual history. For example restricting to even $m$ works if the chosen public scale sequence attains even field degree infinitely often.

These are authentic stages with a known, prescribed positive word *in the mathematical construction*. For any particular tagged list, an external argument using the soundness of its members proves this. A verifier that recognizes only that particular list can exploit the promise there; the increasing-list subsequence accounts for every fixed sound verifier in turn. Supplying a short uniform certificate for the target using the true winner's identity on this subsequence would still require information beyond that identity and the logical contents of the current query replies. The result gives no unrestricted SAT running-time separation.
