# How much correlation can sampled replies certify on a genuine RAM run?

There is a fixed native RAM run whose last block has $q=2^h$ named data reads, for $h=\lceil\log_2(j+2)\rceil$ at stage $j$. Even after learning some genuine replies of its choice, a verifier faces a sharp limit on the correlation it can certify between that block and a list of binary forecasts. The claim may say merely that **some** forecast agrees unusually often **or** disagrees unusually often. Neither the forecast nor the direction needs to be identified. At even residual dimension we give forecasts attaining the full boundary on every stage.

Use the [hidden word](collision-forecasts-for-sat.md) $a_j\in\mathbb F_2^h$. A fixed deterministic program which successfully reports only words different from $a_j$ needs $\Omega_D(2^j)$ native steps on all sufficiently late successes. The priority construction computes $a_j$ in $O(2^j)$ steps. In two fresh cells store zero and one. For each public $x\in\mathbb F_2^h$ in binary order, compute $a_j\cdot x$ and load from that numbered cell. This appends $q$ genuine named reads in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ additional time. Each reply also equals the SAT verdict of the corresponding [physical 3-CNF](collision-forecasts-for-sat.md#the-native-program-and-its-formula-inputs). The decoder sees stage metadata and may query these particular replies. Other internal registers of the genuine run are unavailable. This is the training step for which the [minimum-spanning-tree paper](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/339/download/) uses path maxima on an already specified forest.

For a center $y:\mathbb F_2^h\to\{0,1\}$ define its signed correlation with a word $a$ by
$$
 C_y(a)=q^{-1}\sum_x(-1)^{y(x)+a\cdot x}.
$$
Agreement on a fraction $p$ of the reads means $C_y(a)=2p-1$. On one accepting path let $W$ be the linear span of the masks actually queried, let $r=\dim W$, and put $d=h-r$, $K=2^d$. A verifier may adapt the masks, centers and thresholds to the observed replies. It may abstain.

## When every compatible word must be covered

Say the verifier is sound if every accepted claim with its **actual** named replies holds of $a_j$ at every scale. Allow polynomially many explicit centers and binary rational thresholds of polynomial description length in $j$, with a fixed polynomial for the verifier. A center may equivalently have a representation evaluable at all $q$ positions in total polynomial time in $j$. Consider a report that at least one of its centers satisfies $|C_{y_i}(a_j)|\ge\gamma_i$, for positive thresholds $\gamma_i\le1$. The following assertions concern every sufficiently late successful path within the indicated clocks.

* For deterministic procedures there is a fixed $B$ for which paths costing at most $2^j/(j+1)^B$ have a report true for **every** word compatible with that transcript.
* If explicit SAT on the unrestricted logarithmic-word RAM takes $O(n^k)$ time for fixed $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, the same holds for every fixed nondeterministic verifier within $R_j=\lfloor 2^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}/(j+1)^B\rfloor$ steps for sufficiently large fixed $B$. Free access to genuine named replies is permitted in this assertion. Providing those replies as ordinary input outside this theorem can cost the native clock.

Indeed the replies on a fixed transcript constrain $a$ to one affine coset of $W^\perp$. Each word in this coset reproduces the adaptive path when the same internal witness decisions are taken. If any compatible $a$ violates the report, replaying on that proposed $a$ supplies an accepting path whose assertion is false of $a$. Global soundness entails $a\ne a_j$. A deterministic reporter can try all $q=O(j+2)$ words up to the stated cap and check the polynomial-size results, paying $o(2^j)$ in total for large $B$. For the nondeterministic version guess a proposed $a$ and a path, simulate alleged oracle replies by the known $a\cdot x$, and verify acceptance and falsity by exact arithmetic. The simulation runs in $(R_j+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. Under the SAT assumption, the packed bounded-acceptance calculation and prefix queries in the [false-word theorem](collision-forecasts-for-sat.md) recover such an $a$ in $o(2^j)$ time. The polynomial covers interpreter, input-construction and word-width costs and the bit length of the numerical output. For fixed verifier and independent polynomial overhead one chooses $B$ large enough. The contradiction applies even if the verifier infrequently accepts and its actual accepting branch happens to be true only for a strict subset of compatible words. The same argument applies to a Hamming-radius report by testing the proposed inequality directly.

## Sharper bounds for deterministic training on the genuine generator

There are stronger native bounds for a deterministic verifier $A$. Call a success *incomplete* here if its accepted claim is false for at least one hidden word compatible with the named replies on that successful path. The claim may be true for the actual word, as soundness requires. In the following simulation the masks have just $h$ bits, there are $q=O(j+2)$ of them, and the output list and its truth tests on all hidden words have the polynomial size and checking cost specified above. A synthetic reply for any fixed $a$ can be returned in $O(1)$ native steps after building its $q$-entry table. Record a queried-mask indicator in $O(1)$ additional steps, regardless of the number of times a mask is repeated. This lets us check all compatible words in polynomial time after a success. A fixed implementation of $A$ can be run with this virtual access at constant cost per instruction using the native word convention and the [fixed-limb and sparse bank simulation](priority-pinned-sat.md). Any $O(2^{j/4}\operatorname{poly}(j))$ one-time arithmetic-table initialization pays $o(M)$. The following costs are for identifying a falsely covered word from stage metadata.

*If at most a fixed $r_0$ independent masks were asked on the genuine path*, every sufficiently late incomplete success costs $\Omega_{A,r_0}(M)$. For a cap $T$ enumerate a binary tree which splits only on a mask outside the span of the independently fixed answers, stopping a branch if it would need more than $r_0$ independent answers. A node fixes the answers to its previous independent masks. Precompute their linear closure with the resulting reply on every dependent mask. Rerun $A$ from its start up to $T$ at this node, using the table until an independent query requires a split or an output is reached. There are at most $2^{r_0+1}-1$ nodes; the method never copies a long partially executed guest state. An accepted report followed by any compatible violation gives a sound excluded word. Doubling $T$ searches cost $O_{r_0}(T+\operatorname{poly}(j)\log(T+2))$ through a true incomplete success of clock $T\le M$, apart from the single initialization. Infinite subnative successes would contradict the exclusion theorem. The same full native bound holds at **every sufficiently late default stage** $a_j=0$ with an incomplete success, without a rank restriction. Run just the simulation that answers zero at every mask, then search its accepted claim for a compatible violation. Any word found is genuinely excluded whether or not zero is actual. On a default stage this simulation follows the true path.

For **arbitrary** query rank an incomplete success at any sufficiently late stage costs
$$
                   \Omega_A\!\left(\frac{2^j}{1+L(j)}\right),
\qquad L(j)=\left\lfloor\log_2\!\bigl(2+\lfloor\log_2(j+4)\rfloor\bigr)\right\rfloor .
$$
This uses the actual generation of the hidden word. At stage $j$ there are at most $L(j)$ active priority ranks before exclusions for retirement. At a nonzero stage, its winning rank $e$ emits the word within $\lfloor M/2^{e+1}\rfloor$ universal ticks on the canonical zero input, independently of the results of the other trials. The host's retirement test decides whether to run that trial but does not change its contents or initial bank. Since a winning rank is retired permanently, $e\to\infty$ along nonzero stages.

Use outer budgets $Z=1,2,4,\ldots$ through a fixed constant multiple of $M$. At budget $Z$ run each of the $L(j)$ isolated trials for at most the lesser of its genuine tick cap and $\lfloor Z/(e+1)^2\rfloor$ ticks. Retired ranks may be run too. Collect zero and every well-formed word emitted within the cap, then run $A$ on the synthetic replies of each such candidate with cap $\lfloor Z/(L(j)+1)\rfloor$. Inspect any report on its actual synthetic query set. Output only a **compatible** word whose universal replay would make the very same report false. This is sound for the original hidden word because that violation would contradict $A$'s ground-truth soundness if the word were actual. The isolated trials share the same stage width and their counted steps are interpreter ticks. The fixed universal interpreter can be run tick-by-tick with fixed host overhead, clearing touched cells in time proportional to the trial length. Testing all $L(j)$ branches even at small budgets, generating the short active program texts, creating the $q$-entry synthetic reply tables, and checking the polynomial-size reports cost only $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ per budget outside simulations. Total work through $Z$ is $O(Z+\operatorname{poly}(j)\log(Z+2))$ plus the single initialization.

If the genuine incomplete success costs $T$ and the true word is nonzero, a falsely covered word is found by the first dyadic $Z$ at least
$$
   \max\big\{(e+1)^2\lfloor M/2^{e+1}\rfloor,\ (L(j)+1)\lceil T\rceil\big\}.
$$
The first term is $o(M)$ along sufficiently late nonzero stages. No such term is needed on zero stages. Therefore $T=o(M/(L(j)+1))$ on any infinite subsequence of incomplete successes would yield sound excluded words in $o(M)$ time on that subsequence. This avoids the loss from enumerating all $q$ possible hidden words in separate long training runs. It supplies no procedure for quickly testing whether a genuine sample has any specified value.

## The correlation and Hamming bounds

Choose one solution $a_0$ to the observed linear equations. In coordinates adapted to the decomposition into cosets of $W$ in the *query* space, absorb the sign $(-1)^{a_0\cdot x}$ into the center's sign. Average that modified sign within each coset, obtaining numbers $g(z)\in[-1,1]$ on the quotient of size $K$. Each $a-a_0\in W^\perp$ defines one distinct quotient character. Thus the $K$ correlations on compatible words are precisely the normalized Walsh coefficients $\widehat g(u)=K^{-1}\sum_z g(z)(-1)^{u\cdot z}$. By character orthogonality,
$$
        \sum_u\widehat g(u)^2=K^{-1}\sum_z g(z)^2\le1.
$$
At most $\lfloor\gamma_i^{-2}\rfloor$ compatible words can have $|C_{y_i}|\ge\gamma_i$. Hence every timely accepted report satisfies
$$
                  \sum_i\lfloor\gamma_i^{-2}\rfloor\ \ge\ K.
$$
In particular a common threshold for $t$ centers is at most $\sqrt{t/K}$, independent of the unknown index or correlation sign. With $t$ fixed and a positive constant threshold, a successful verifier needs $h-O(1)$ independent genuine replies.

For an ordinary Hamming-radius report about a single center, the average distance over the $K$ candidates is at least $(q-2^r)/2$. Every queried-span coordinate is determined; on the others the true bit is equally often zero and one. Consequently the certified radius is at least $\lceil(q-2^r)/2\rceil$. This radius is attainable on all transcripts. Predict the known values on $W$. If $r\ge1$, each other coset carries a known relative pattern with an unknown common flip. Choose a center matching one orientation on half the coset and its complement on the rest. Both orientations incur exactly $2^{r-1}$ errors there. For $r=0$, the constant-zero center has worst-case distance $q/2$ (here $q\ge2$). These constructions use just finite vector-space operations in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time.

## Exact list boundary from quadratic Boolean functions

Suppose the residual dimension $d$ is even and the thresholds are $\gamma_i=2^{-k_i}$ with integers $0\le 2k_i\le d$. There exists a list achieving the claimed disjunction on **every** compatible word if and only if
$$
                  \sum_i 4^{k_i}\ge K.
$$
The upper obstruction was just proved. For attainability, view quotient frequencies as strings of $d/2$ base-four digits, each digit a pair of binary coordinates. Take a subcollection of the specified capacities summing exactly to $K$ by repeatedly merging four equal capacities into the next power of four. If no capacity $K$ were produced and none originally equaled $K$, the residual base-four digits would total less than $K$. Expand one capacity $K$ back through its merges. Each four-way split specifies one further fixed base-four digit, so the resulting disjoint prefix cylinders partition the whole frequency space. Each chosen cylinder of size $4^{k_i}$ fixes $d-2k_i$ binary frequency coordinates to a prefix $c$.

For this cylinder let the **quotient center sign** at spatial coordinates $(s,z)$ be
$$
              (-1)^{c\cdot s+Q(z)},\qquad
              Q(z)=\sum_{b=1}^{k_i} z_{2b-1}z_{2b}.
$$
Its coefficient at frequency $(u,v)$ vanishes unless $u=c$. For $u=c$, the sum factors over pairs. Each pair's unnormalized contribution is $2(-1)^{v_{2b-1}v_{2b}}$, giving absolute normalized coefficient exactly $2^{-k_i}$. Lift the center back to the original block by undoing the $a_0$ phase and repeating its quotient sign on each coset. Set any unused centers arbitrarily. This is polynomial work in $j$ and the stated list length, with a constructive schedule of any $r$ linearly independent sample masks. The checker can establish the disjunction from the construction using only those replies. Quadratic bent functions and their flat Walsh spectra are classical. [Rothaus, *On “bent” functions*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(76)90024-8)

There is also rigidity for any fully covered transcript at exact capacity $\sum_i\gamma_i^{-2}=K$. Write $A_i=\{u:|\widehat g_i(u)|\ge\gamma_i\}$. The bounds $K\le\sum_i|A_i|\le\sum_i\gamma_i^{-2}=K$ force the sets to partition the compatible words. In every center all coset averages then have absolute value one, and its Walsh coefficients vanish off $A_i$ and have magnitude exactly $\gamma_i$ on it. Thus even at the numerical threshold, an accepted sound claim for a list visibly violating these conditions would separate the stated SAT clock if it occurred infinitely often within budget.

The one-center even-dimensional baseline permits a specific test. With no samples take $h=2k\ge2$ and center sign $(-1)^{Q(x)}$. Alter its sign at one fixed $x_0$ with $Q(x_0)=1$. The unnormalized coefficient at hidden word $a$ changes from $2^k(-1)^{Q(a)}$ by $-2(-1)^{Q(x_0)+a\cdot x_0}$. Its absolute magnitude is $2^k+2$ or $2^k-2$, and at the default word $a=0$ it is $2^k+2$. The default occurs at all but $O(\log\log(J+4))$ stages through $J$. Among the stages with even $h$, an ordinary short certificate that this one specified forecast's absolute correlation has the larger value infinitely often would exclude the chosen unrestricted SAT clock, although it need not give a correlation sign or one true named reply. The unconditional bent construction reaches the baseline exactly; it does not single out a stage with the extra $2/q$. This is the unresolved step.
