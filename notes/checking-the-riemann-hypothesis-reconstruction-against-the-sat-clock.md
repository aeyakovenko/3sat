# Checking the Riemann-hypothesis reconstruction against the SAT clock

The linked reconstruction supplies a useful check on an induction: all the estimates used at one step must hold for the same family, and that family must still satisfy the hypotheses of the next step. I found no implication from its prime-sum estimates to the running time of a deterministic RAM on ordinary SAT.

The Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative certified the statement and proof of the separate original Alpöge library. Their report describes statements compared with Mathlib and independent kernel checks. I relied on that report for the status of the original proof. The linked human-readable manuscript, inspected at commit `68f45d0`, labels its own deductions conditional. Its Section 2 criterion turns a uniform upper bound for a smoothed prime sum on arbitrarily large windows into a zero-free half-plane. Its Section 18 assembly assumes that the required arithmetic data can all be chosen on one window. The status file gives a concrete example. Section 7 chooses a spacing $2\lceil x^{402}\rceil$, while a standard record in the source requires at least $4\lceil x^{377}\rceil^2+2t_r$. For $x\ge2$ the proposed spacing is already less than $4x^{754}$. This identifies a mismatch in an attempted correspondence between the manuscript's family and the source's family. On the SAT side the analogous test is to retain the actual images of the original sixteen files. The complete five- and six-bit catalogs both have clock six; every affine extension loses that bound on our five-bit suite. A particular quadratic extension passes the stronger same-suite test. Two potential numerical transfers have specific limits.

* The journal's translated-history proof already obtains a prime $p\equiv1\pmod{2^b}$ with $O(\log(T+2^b))$ bits from Linnik's theorem. The prime certifies a difference of two supplied multiset descriptions. A better bound for locating a prime does not supply a short description of an arbitrary solver's trace. For the simpler idea of identifying an arbitrary $H$-bit integer by a fixed list of integer moduli, their least common multiple must be at least $2^H$; otherwise zero and the least common multiple collide. This counting test only concerns exact identification on the whole indicated integer range. [Translated RAM histories](sorting-address-translated-ram-histories.md)
* The cyclic selector already has exact spectral counts. For a fixed $2\times2$ transfer product $M$, Cayley–Hamilton gives $\sum_{j\ge0}\mathrm{tr}(M^j)z^j=(2-\mathrm{tr}(M)z)/(1-\mathrm{tr}(M)z+\det(M)z^2)$. On an unmarked cycle this gives $(2-z)/(1-z-z^2)$ and exponential growth of the number of covers. One native program still decides the predicate in constant time per packed word. Pole information here counts masks; to infer RAM time we would need a bound that every fast computation violates.
