# Two permutation assertions make a one-load defect NEXP-complete

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Consider a bank of $D=2^s$ words. Two assertions about its starting state require the normalized tables $P$ and $Q$ to be even permutations of the same word set. A designated read tests just one value of $P$. Given a *succinct description* of the assertions, deciding whether some permissible state triggers this read is NEXP-complete. This holds when each row of the second normalization differs from the first by at most two disjoint transpositions. In the bank size parameter there is also an unconditional near-linear lower bound for a nondeterministic tester, quantified below. The state under test is existential. The all-zero state satisfies both assertions on the hard instances and never triggers the read. [Single-assertion sparse witnesses and the two-assertion parity example](signed-permutation-checkpoints.md)

## The test and its certificate

Let $P(i)=i\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}M[i]$ and $Q(i)=h_i(P(i))$. A public circuit on the $s$ address bits returns up to two pairs of $s$-bit endpoints. The permutation $h_i$ swaps those pairs and fixes all other labels. A total encoding can treat an ill-formed list of pairs as the identity. Valid endpoints in one row are distinct and differ from $i$, so $h_i(i)=i$ and both normalizers send a native zero to the row name. Specify the closing row $e_*$ and the trigger value $t$. The block loads $M[e_*]$ and triggers if its normalized value equals $t$. The endpoint circuit and its ports are part of the description, of length $z$ with $s\le z$.

One nondeterministic tester guesses $P$ and derives the native memory by XOR. It evaluates all $Q(i)$, checks range and uniqueness of the two tables, and checks the closing read. For a permutation of $D$ symbols its sign is $(-1)^{D-c}$, where $c$ is the number of cycles including fixed points. Cycle traversal with visited bits checks both signs. This takes $D\operatorname{poly}(s+z)$ steps on a bounded-word RAM and establishes membership in NEXP in the short input length. With an explicitly listed table of row operations the same verification is polynomial in the expanded input length.

## Encoding a path by two tables

Take an undirected graph with terminals $s_0,t$ and edge colors, each color appearing at most twice. Replace every ordinary edge by a path of four edges through three fresh internal vertices. Put its original color on the middle link between the first two internal vertices. Use fresh, globally distinct colors on the three other links. Consequently the two edges of any repeated color have disjoint endpoints, including when the original edges met. Add a single edge from $t$ to $s_0$ of fresh color $e_*$. A simple path from $s_0$ to $t$ in the original graph is rainbow if its colors are distinct. Subdivision preserves this condition. Closing the subdivided path gives a cycle of odd length.

Give the colors and vertices disjoint word names. On the row for color $i$ let $h_i$ swap the two endpoints of each edge of that color. Vertex rows, two spare rows, and unused addresses are identities. Color $e_*$ has just its closing pair. In a permissible state, draw one directed arc from $P(i)$ to $Q(i)$ for every row where they differ. Both endpoints of such an arc are incident to the selected edge of color $i$. After canceling all unchanged rows, each symbol has equal indegree and outdegree, each at most one by injectivity. Thus the arcs form vertex-disjoint directed cycles and each color occurs at most once among **all** active arcs. A triggering read selects the arc $t\to s_0$. Removing it from its cycle yields a rainbow simple path from $s_0$ to $t$.

Conversely orient a rainbow path from $s_0$ to $t$, followed by $t\to s_0$. Starting from the identity, construct $P$ by swapping each cycle color row's name with the vertex word for its arc's tail. These transpositions are disjoint since the color names are distinct, the tails are distinct, and the namespaces are disjoint. Flip the two spare names if necessary to make $P$ even. All inactive color rows still carry their own names. On active rows $Q$ equals $P$ followed by the single cycle on the vertex outputs. Its odd length implies this change has even sign. Therefore both tables are even, the closing row has the required value, and the construction fills the entire bank. ## Clause occurrences as colors

Make a series chain of two-terminal gadgets. For each variable use two internally disjoint parallel paths indexed by its proposed truth value. For each clause use three parallel branches, one per literal occurrence. All gadgets share just consecutive articulation terminals. An undirected simple path between the ends traverses exactly one full branch in each gadget. Parallel single-edge branches can be regarded as distinct edges before the private subdivisions.

Give occurrence $o=(j,k)$ a distinct color. Its clause branch uses that color. In its variable gadget the path for the value that makes this literal false also uses it, and the other path omits it. All other branch-edge colors occur only once. An assignment and one selected literal per clause therefore specify a rainbow path exactly when all selected literals are true. Repeated literals and clauses containing both signs are covered by the occurrence indexing. This gives explicit NP-hardness and, with table verification, NP-completeness for the explicitly expanded one-load test. Rainbow-path hardness and the disjoint forbidden-pairs path problem have earlier reductions. The private subdivisions, full even permutations and local indexing are the steps needed for this test. [Chakraborty, Fischer, Matsliah and Yuster, Theorem 2.3](https://www.cmi.ac.in/~sourav/papers/rainbow.pdf) · [Kolman and Pangrác on forbidden pairs](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2009.03.018)

For a succinct clause circuit returning variable and sign from $(j,k)$, with $V$ possible variables and $M$ clauses, a first layout gives every variable branch one slot for every occurrence. At its slot $(j,k)$ a branch takes the occurrence color only if both the variable and its false value match. Otherwise it uses a private color. There are $O(VM)$ slots after the usual nonempty padding. Given an occurrence color, evaluate the circuit to locate its unique variable-side slot and its clause branch. A private color contains its slot and branch tags, so the reverse lookup is also local. A private-color tag superseded by an occurrence color induces an identity row. Fourfold subdivision, edge and vertex tags, and power-of-two padding cost only a constant factor with packed contiguous indices. The reduction does not search for occurrences of a given variable. If $V,M$ are exponential, the descriptions of the two normalizers are still polynomial in the clause circuit description. This already proves the claimed NEXP-hardness under polynomial-time many-one reductions. [Succinct 3SAT and efficient Cook reductions, Fact 1 and §3.1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/acc-lbs-journal-final.pdf)

## Removing the factor for the variable universe

For a nearly linear expanded bank it matters that the program can compute both an occurrence's variable and a local slot for that occurrence. The source succinct 3SAT format promises only the former. The following preprocessing supplies both. Set $w=\lceil\log_2(V+M+2)\rceil$. Use a power-of-two number of sorting lanes between $4M$ and $8M$. In lanes $4j+k$, $0\le k<3$, place a real record with the variable ID obtained from the clause circuit and a freely guessed assignment bit for that occurrence. Make the other lanes dummy. Check separately that the three bits of each clause satisfy it using the given signs.

Sort the records lexicographically by dummy flag, variable ID, and bit. Require equal bits on every pair of adjacent real records with the same ID. Since all equal IDs become contiguous, these tests enforce one global value per variable that actually occurs. Sorting the complete record also gives deterministic outputs for comparators when keys agree. The preprocessing CNF is satisfiable exactly when the source formula is. The input keys and signs are fixed by local evaluations of the given circuit, implemented as locally generated constants. No inverse of the source circuit is used.

Here are layout details ensuring a succinct reduction with a near-linear bank. Use Batcher's bitonic network on the power-of-two lanes, with $O(\log^2 M)$ comparator stages (using $\log(M+2)$ for small $M$). In a stage the partner of lane $\ell$ is $\ell\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}2^j$ for a stage-dependent $j$. The direction and the choice of min or max are computable from the stage and lane bits. Allocate a block to each stage and lane. It computes that lane's sorted output from its own and its partner's records in the preceding stage. The two outputs of a comparator can be computed in separate blocks. Comparing and multiplexing $O(w)$ bits uses a uniform Boolean circuit of $\operatorname{poly}(w)$ size, with all internal variables and their constant-width gate clauses stored in its block. Use separate initial and final blocks for record initialization, clause checks on quadruples, and adjacent consistency checks. Pad with local tautologies and use local constant pins so the layout introduces no unbounded-fanout global constant variable. All clauses can have exactly three slots by repeating literals. [Batcher's network](https://doi.org/10.1145/1468075.1468121)

For each new variable the blocks that can mention it are a *computable constant-size list*. An internal gate is used in its own block. An output bit can additionally be read by the two next-stage lane blocks using that lane, obtained by the next stage's XOR partner. Initial occurrence bits may also enter their quadruple's clause block. Final outputs may enter the checks for the adjacent lane pairs on either side. Local constant pins and padding stay in their own block. Enumerate each candidate block once, then all its $\operatorname{poly}(w)$ clause slots and three literal positions, testing the actual variable ID. Thus each variable truth branch needs just $\operatorname{poly}(w)$ slots, even if the *original* variable had exponentially many occurrences. For a desired occurrence, compute its new variable, enumerate that variable's candidate slots, and locate the unique match by its clause and literal position. Padding and false-branch selection are handled as in the first layout. These operations are polynomial in the short descriptor and $w$, and their algorithms compile to polynomial-size circuits in polynomial time.

There are $M\operatorname{poly}(w)$ blocks, new variables, and slots altogether. Using fixed-width local operation and slot fields of $O(\log(w+2))$ bits, plus a lane field of $\lceil\log_2(8M)\rceil$ bits, still gives only $M\operatorname{poly}(w)$ reserved addresses per fixed family of tags. In particular a color or a vertex name never contains two independent global lane indices. An occurrence color stores its clause block and local literal position. A private color stores its variable block, local variable, chosen branch and candidate-slot index, or its clause branch and local edge. A subdivision point stores one base edge name and position. Private link colors likewise store a base edge and link number. Gadget articulation vertices use successive variable or clause indices, computable by fixed layout arithmetic. Endpoints are obtained from one tag at a time, with out-of-range or unused color tags acting as identities. Final padding to a power of two and two spare words preserve
$$
 D=O\big(M\operatorname{poly}(w)\big).
$$

## Short-input lower bounds and their scope

Since NP is properly contained in NEXP by the nondeterministic time hierarchy, no sound and complete nondeterministic polynomial-time tester on the short descriptors decides this one-load question. Complementing gives coNEXP-completeness of universal safety for the same read. That safety question also has no NP decision procedure. If it did, NEXP would be contained in coNP, forcing NP = coNP and then NEXP = NP. These are uniform complexity statements. They do not bound unrestricted circuit size for a single witnessing state.

There is a finer bank-size statement. Use a $k$-input circuit of $\operatorname{poly}(k)$ gates for a succinct 3-CNF in the efficient Cook reduction. Its regular clause fields can be read with $\operatorname{poly}(k)$ probes to that circuit, and $V,M\le 2^k\operatorname{poly}(k)$. The regular layout gives a descriptor of size $z=\operatorname{poly}(k)$ and a bank of size $D\le 2^k\operatorname{poly}(k)$. If necessary add inert identity rows in a fresh tag range to ensure $D\ge 2^k$. Then $\log D=\Theta(k)$. Williams's nondeterministic hierarchy consequence excludes time $2^k/k^{\omega(1)}$ on succinct formulas with $k$ address bits and polynomial-size circuits. Equivalently one can apply the reduction directly to the hard nondeterministic $2^n$-time languages, whose clause descriptors have $k\le n+O(\log n)$ and may be padded to $k\ge n$. This direct argument requires local clause access only on the regular hard instances. [Williams, Theorems 2.2–2.3](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/tss/files/papers/1111.1261.pdf) · [Regular clause access, Fact 1 and §3.1](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/acc-lbs-journal-final.pdf)

In particular, for any fixed $b\ge0$ and any function $f(D)\to\infty$, a sound complete nondeterministic tester cannot have the uniform worst-case bound
$$
 O\!\left(D(z+2)^b/(\log D)^{f(D)}\right)
$$
on all the generated instances. The same conclusion holds for $D^{1-\delta}(z+2)^b$ at any fixed $\delta>0$. A hypothetical tester would solve the hard succinct instances in $2^k/k^{\omega(1)}$ time. To move a bounded-word RAM tester to ordinary nondeterministic multitape time, guess its chronological records together with read replies and the claimed nondeterministic choices, verify transitions, and sort the memory records by bank, address, time and access slot. Verify last writes and initialization in this order. Use merge passes on multitapes and charge bit-polynomial implementations of native instructions. Immutable descriptor reads can be treated as initialized memory. For a polynomially bounded word width in $k$, and runtime at most $2^{O(k)}$, the cost is $(T+z+1)\operatorname{poly}(k)$ on these instances. This also pays for evaluating a succinct row circuit if the tester is given a unit-cost row query on this generated family. All the fixed polynomial losses are absorbed by the assumed superpolynomial-in-$k$ saving. For the fixed instruction set one can also fix a sufficiently large *constant* exponent. Choose one language in nondeterministic time $2^n$ outside nondeterministic time $2^n/n$ by the time hierarchy and fix its efficient generator. On its instances write $D\le 2^n n^g$, $z\le n^h$, and bound the simulation overhead by $n^q$, increasing fixed constants as needed. Padding gives $\log D=\Theta(n)$. For this fixed $b$, any $K>g+hb+q+2$ is already impossible in place of $f(D)$. This argument supplies neither a numerical threshold for an arbitrary implementation nor an exclusion for every positive fixed $K$.

## An unconditional restriction on every triggering memory

There is a family on which **every** memory triggering the load is hard for constant-depth modular circuits. Fix a depth $d$ and modulus $m_0\ge2$. For some $\gamma>0$, at infinitely many bank sizes $D$ there is a succinct affirmative instance such that no triggering memory has an address-to-word circuit of depth $d$ and size at most $2^{(\log_2 D)^\gamma}$ over AND, OR, NOT and $\mathrm{MOD}_{m_0}$. This includes memories satisfying only the unsigned permutation requirements. The short instance parameters may be hardwired into the circuit. No hypothesis about the running time of ordinary SAT enters this statement.

Use the first, rectangular layout with one slot for each clause occurrence on each variable branch. For each variable $v$, let $c_v$ be the **fresh** color on the first subdivided link of its true branch, incident to its entrance junction $a_v$. These names are fixed tag-and-field strings in $v$ with the branch, slot and link numbers fixed. If the closing row triggers, its directed cycle traverses the chain from source to target and visits every entrance junction. Its unique outgoing arc at $a_v$ uses the true branch precisely when $P(c_v)=a_v$. No other active arc can leave that vertex. Thus one probe of the memory word at $c_v$, an XOR with $c_v$, and an equality comparison give the selected truth value. In particular the bits recovered from **any** unsigned triggering state satisfy every original clause. Applying the same rule to an arbitrary circuit for tentative memory still defines one assignment. If it satisfies the formula, the formula really is satisfiable regardless of the proposed table's defects.

Choose a language accepted in nondeterministic time $2^r$ outside nondeterministic time $2^r/r^2$. By the local Cook reduction, in time $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ its input gives a 3-CNF clause generator with $\ell=r+O(\log r)$ input bits. Each output bit of the generator has constant locality, and its variable names use $O(\ell)$ bits. Consequently the rectangular layout has address width $s=\log_2 D=O(r)$ even though it can have many more words than the original formula has clauses. Tagging and padding the variable names makes $c_v$ and $a_v$ computable by wiring and constants. Fixing the Cook input, its clause bits can be written as constant-depth bounded-fan-in circuits. For a polynomial-size supplied circuit with at most $K$ relevant bits per output for fixed $K$, one can also find each support by evaluating all inputs of weight at most $K$ and comparing pairs differing in one bit. The fixed local truth tables then give an explicit depth-two representation. [Jahanjou, Miles and Viola, Theorem 1 and §6](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/home/viola/papers/explicit.pdf)

Suppose all sufficiently large affirmative instances had a word-output circuit of the stated size. Guess its gate list. Build a circuit on **$\ell$ clause-index bits** which reads the three literal names and signs, applies the preceding extraction to each, and returns one if the chosen clause is false. Three copies of the memory circuit and the address operations have depth $d+O(1)$ with the same fixed modulus. The description and construction cost $2^{O(s^\gamma)}\operatorname{poly}(r)$, even accounting for explicit wires and output gates. On a negative input every guessed circuit gives a false clause. On an affirmative input a genuine triggering circuit gives none. Williams's ACC satisfiability algorithm at the resulting fixed depth and modulus takes time $2^{\ell-\Omega(\ell^\delta)}$ on circuits of size $2^{O(\ell^\epsilon)}$ for suitable positive constants. Choose $\gamma$ strictly smaller than the allowed size exponent and one. Testing absence of a false clause, including the guess and fixed-polynomial simulation losses, would take $O(2^r/r^2)$. This contradicts the choice of language. Checking collisions by guessing two bank addresses would have introduced too many index bits for this bound. Extracting one assignment bit per literal avoids that loss. The ACC satisfiability algorithm, the local Cook reduction and their use for witness lower bounds have precedents. The additional transfer here is from *every triggering memory* of the two-permutation test. [Williams, Theorem 4.5](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/acc-lbs-journal-final.pdf)

There is also a consequence for **actual assignments produced on ordinary explicit inputs**. In the same local Cook construction, if every sufficiently large affirmative input had even one small ACC circuit for some satisfying assignment, that circuit itself could replace the memory extractor in the three-copy clause test. The same hierarchy contradiction gives arbitrarily large, succinctly named satisfiable 3-CNFs for which every satisfying assignment needs more than $2^{(\log N)^{\gamma'}}$ gates for the fixed depth and modulus, for some $\gamma'>0$. Here $N$ counts encoded bits in the expanded formula with fixed-width literals, so $\log N=\Theta(r)$. The short name can be hardwired. On these formulas, any correct search solver that leaves a direct-address table of assignment bits has a real output snapshot with this decoding lower bound. A decision solver can also be used to produce such a table by successively testing and fixing variables. That entails a sequence of increasingly constrained SAT calls. The result gives no lower bound on one such call, and no evidence that a decision solver must materialize this table. This assignment corollary follows from the published local Cook reduction and ACC satisfiability bound independently of the two-permutation encoding.

## Putting the check at the entry of an ordinary SAT solver

One can put this same obligation at an actual program location without relying on any expensive computation by the solver. Relabel the closing color as word zero and the target vertex as word one. Prefix any deterministic SAT solver by a fresh-memory load from address zero and a comparison with one. An unexpected one branches away from the solver entry. A zero proceeds to the original solver with the initial registers restored. The native branch always proceeds on genuine initialization and costs a constant number of steps. Its behavior as a SAT decider is preserved on all ordinary inputs.

For the research test, encode the polynomial-length descriptor in tautological clauses of a satisfiable formula and pad it with tautological clauses to between $D$ and $D\operatorname{poly}(\log D)$ bits. For example a signed first literal in a tautological clause whose other literals are $y,\neg y$ stores one descriptor bit. Use fixed-width fields and a length prefix with a locally computable layout. The resulting logarithmic machine word can address the bank. If its address space is larger, all other rows have identity as their relative relabeling. This adds no active edges. A fixed checker can obtain the row description by reading the short descriptor portion of the formula. The whole padded formula also has a locally computable word generator from the descriptor. The beginning pair of assertions holds at the true initialized state. Propagating their full intersection through the one-load branch is the hard test above. The exact zero initial row gives a constant-size proof of what the program really does. Thus even an authentic program location and a reachable starting state do not turn hardness of *auditing this proposed relaxation* into a lower bound on the best proof of that run.
