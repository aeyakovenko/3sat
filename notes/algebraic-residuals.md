# How many root comparisons can be indispensable?

This attempt removes the constant-gap assumption from the [rooted propagation argument](rooted-propagation.md). A seed is a string of b bits. On a NO input fix one permitted path per vertex as in that argument. Each comparison now induces a scalar function Pₑ on seeds. Zero means the comparison passes. Assume no seed passes them all. On YES one common seed must pass for every valid choice of comparisons and paths.

## An exact bound without a gap

If the functions Pₑ lie in a vector space of dimension R over a field, at most R comparisons already have no common zero. Take a basis from among the comparisons. At a point where every basis member vanishes, so does every linear combination. A nondeterministic NO certificate can simply specify those comparisons and paths. The checker validates them and calls SAT once to ask whether any common seed passes. It need not compute the basis, prove independence, or expand a coefficient vector. A scalar comparison and its chosen paths must still admit a small, uniformly constructible circuit. The rank refers to functions *after following the paths*. The coefficient-basis argument for explicitly listed polynomial constraints appears in Theorem 3.1 of Jansen and Pieterse. [Optimal Sparsification for Some Binary CSPs Using Low-Degree Polynomials](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03233)

The sharp Boolean-cube bound below is a direct case of Dol’nikov and Igonin’s 1999 Theorem 2.4. Set their transversal size k to one and take L to be the space of degree-at-most-r polynomial functions on the cube. Its dimension is M(b,r), and it contains the constant one. There are M(b,r) independent point evaluations because a function vanishing at every point is zero. These evaluations satisfy the theorem’s general-position hypothesis at k=1. The authors’ sharpness construction uses one equation for each omitted evaluation point. Earlier work of Deza and Frankl treats projective hypersurfaces, and De Loera, Petrović and Stasi apply rank bounds to sampling polynomial systems. [Dol’nikov and Igonin, Theorem 2.4, p. 1219](https://www.mathnet.ru/links/0b3ea410267754f1f5ca11ffb17bc496/fpm427.pdf#page=11) · [Deza and Frankl](https://www.renyi.hu/~pfrankl/j44.pdf) · [De Loera, Petrović and Stasi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.08804)

For b Boolean seeds and multilinear polynomials of degree at most r over the binary field, put

    M(b,r) = sum_{j=0}^{min(r,b)} binom(b,j).

The monomials give R ≤ M(b,r). This is the **exact worst-case number of original comparisons needed** even on the entire Boolean cube. For every set A of at most r coordinates use the residual

    P_A(x) = sum_{A ⊆ U ⊆ [b], |U| ≤ r} product_{i ∈ U} x_i      (mod 2).

At the indicator vector of any X of size at most r, the sum is zero if A is not contained in X. Otherwise it counts the 2^|X∖A| sets between A and X, giving one exactly when X=A. Globally the XOR of all P_A is the constant one. The coefficient of each monomial indexed by U is 2^|U| modulo two. Consequently all comparisons together are inconsistent, while deleting any one leaves the private satisfying seed 1_A. Under **any** probability distribution on the tests, some private seed violates mass at most 1/M(b,r). Uniform weighting attains this bound on the minimum. An individual test admits a small circuit even though its expanded polynomial can be large. Multiply the bits in A and compute the parity of the elementary symmetric functions of degrees 0 through r−|A| on the remaining bits by the usual descending-degree coefficient update for ∏(1+xᵢt).

One can independently enable or disable each comparison. On the input enabling all of them, a sound NO certificate in a bit-query interface must inspect all M enable bits. Deleting the uninspected comparison would create a satisfying assignment with the same transcript. This is a linear lower bound in the explicit enable-string length. The fully enabled instance also has the short symbolic identity ΣP_A=1, so the result concerns selecting original comparisons. It does not lower-bound arbitrary algebraic refutations.

There is a limit to pursuing degree one as a route to SAT hardness. When the legal seeds are the full cube and the residuals are affine, guess at most b+1 comparisons. Evaluate each at zero and at the b unit vectors to recover its coefficients. Gaussian elimination checks inconsistency in O(b³) further bit operations up to logarithmic factors. This procedure never calls the hypothetical SAT solver. Degree two already accommodates general SAT with fresh variables. A clause on literals l₁,l₂,l₃ imposes y=(1−l₁)(1−l₂) and y(1−l₃)=0 for a fresh bit y. The cost of these new bits matters when applying a bound in b.

## Does recursive composition keep the rank small?

Consider a complete binary tree of depth b and a single state bit z, initially one. All paths share seed x. On an edge choosing c at level i set z′=z·[xᵢ=c]. Every update has degree two. At leaf a the state equals [x=a]. Requiring zero at every leaf gives 2ᵇ independent residuals, each with a private seed. In particular a one-bit intermediate state and quadratic local updates place no polynomial bound on the transported rank. A length-b path can use b distinct bits of shared information. A root-to-leaf list certificate really can require every leaf of this tree.

Two restrictions on computation survive this example.

* Suppose a computation program has a publicly indexed set of a Boolean AND outputs whose removal leaves only affine gates over the binary field. Use one additional seed bit per such output, shared wherever it is reused. After cutting these wires, the inputs to each AND are affine forms in b+a bits. Its consistency equation is yᵢ+LᵢL′ᵢ=0. Enforce all a equations once. If the terminal comparisons are affine in the cut circuit, at most b+a+1 terminal comparisons suffice in addition: their span has that dimension bound regardless of the AND equations. This gives at most **b+2a+1** total constraints. The point is stronger than merely bounding the final degree by two. The a nonlinear polynomials are a named list and can all be retained. Any solution of the cut system lifts gate by gate along the original acyclic program. If each needed affine form, including its coefficients, can be constructed in Õ(b+a+1) time, the explicit query costs Õ((b+a+1)²), plus the cost of locating the selected tests and fixed setup. For a T-time hierarchy language, a reduction satisfying these restrictions with b+a≤T^β, negligible setup and β<1/(2d) would suffice against a degree-d SAT clock. An unrestricted accepting computation supplies neither this bound on its independent nonlinearity and choices nor the requisite construction algorithm.
* Some programs keep a small feature space under arbitrarily many nonlinear steps. Split the registers into u and v and allow u′=Au+c, v′=Bv+Q(u) for degree-two Q. The features 1, uᵢ, uᵢuⱼ, vₗ transform linearly. If a word of transitions is given by a straight-line program with g leaves and concatenations in total, compose its feature matrices in O(gR³) field operations for feature dimension R, once the generator matrices are available. This permits long jumps even though v updates are nonlinear. A short circuit returning the next instruction at an arbitrary time does not by itself supply a short concatenation program or the matrix product.

## Change the variables in which low degree is required

Suppose the valid comparisons on consistent fixed paths have a common index i of h bits (unused indices may denote identically zero residuals). Consider P(i,x) as a Boolean function of **the index** at fixed seed x. Over a field of q>2h elements the unique multilinear extension in i has degree at most h, with no condition on degree in x. Assume the reduction supplies a uniformly constructible circuit evaluating this exact extension at arbitrary field points, with the original YES seed giving zero at every Boolean index. On NO every seed has a nonzero index polynomial. At a uniform field point its probability of evaluating to zero is at most h/q by the polynomial root bound. Independently choose b+1 field points. The probability any of the 2ᵇ seeds vanishes at all of them is less than one. These points therefore give a certificate with one SAT query for a seed vanishing everywhere on the list. The list costs O((b+1)h log q) bits and the SAT instance includes b+1 extension evaluations and field arithmetic at their actual bit costs. Consistent paths at all indices and correctness of the extension must be justified by the reduction.

This can beat the full-rank tree. For P(i,x)=[i=x] the extension is ∏ⱼ(xⱼiⱼ+(1−xⱼ)(1−iⱼ)). Assign every index coordinate one field element different from zero and one. That single point is nonzero for every Boolean seed x. Thus an exponential list lower bound for the original comparisons coexists with a one-point refutation in the extended domain.

For a general succinct Boolean circuit C(i), the exact extension at (1/2,…,1/2) over a characteristic-three field equals 2^(−h) times the number of accepting indices, as an element of that field. An efficient uniform evaluator at even this one point would also evaluate the count modulo three. Replacing Boolean gates by arithmetic gates does not guarantee the exact extension. The circuit i(1−i) is identically zero on Boolean inputs but is nonzero at field points outside {0,1}. A Boolean YES seed could be falsely refuted in that way. A circuit already syntactically multilinear in the index bits avoids this problem. Establishing such an evaluator within the hierarchy budget, or finding a different efficiently checkable family of aggregate comparisons, is the remaining positive step in this approach.

A constructive case allows repeated index reads. Suppose the residual is computed by a width-w deterministic branching program with ℓ steps in a fixed order of index probes. For a fixed seed x, the transition on index bit zero or one is known. Weight bit j by 1−zⱼ or zⱼ respectively **only at its first probe**. Carry its chosen Boolean value in the state until its final probe. Between steps there are at most c coordinates whose first probe is past and whose last probe is still to come. Dynamic programming over the w·2ᶜ resulting states sums the path weights for accepting final states. Unread index bits contribute the factor (1−zⱼ)+zⱼ=1. This is exactly the interpolation formula, including at non-Boolean z. For explicit transition matrices the straightforward arithmetic cost is O(ℓw²2ᶜ), in addition to constructing the transition entries from x and processing the fixed probe schedule. Sparse, fixed destinations allow a cheaper update. In the read-once case c=0. A small-space program can repeatedly inspect all the literals of an arbitrary 3CNF and retain only running satisfaction bits, so small control state alone does not bound c. The extension at the midpoint still computes its satisfying count modulo three.

The November 23 VP–VNP result supplies an unconditional obstruction to a general formal arithmetic construction here, even for monotone 2-CNF predicates. The proof and its scope for finite-field bit evaluators are in [VP, VNP and the multilinear extension of SAT](sat-extension.md).

## Can a small algebraic proof replace the exact extension?

Let Q(i,x) be any compact arithmetic circuit for the correct Boolean residual at each Boolean index i and Boolean seed x. Permit the seed to supply additional circuits Hⱼ, and evaluate

    Q(z,x) − Σⱼ (zⱼ²−zⱼ) Hⱼ(z).

On NO every seed x fails somewhere on the Boolean cube. That point remains a nonzero evaluation for every choice of H. On YES it suffices that one passing x have a small certificate expressing Q in the ideal generated by the Boolean axioms zⱼ²−zⱼ. Its corrected polynomial then vanishes identically. Such coefficients exist without size restrictions by reducing each variable degree until the remainder is multilinear. The remainder has zero Boolean values on YES and is therefore zero. The circuit-size question is precisely where this repair can fail. This is the Boolean-ideal certificate used in algebraic proof complexity. [Grochow and Pitassi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3820)

For a charged version, restrict the finite seed encoding, including all H descriptions, to B bits. Predetermine a field and a degree cap Δ for every valid corrected circuit, with field size greater than 2Δ. Enforce the syntactic caps. On NO the same polynomial root bound and union bound give B+1 field points ruling out every candidate. The Boolean SAT query has to decode and evaluate the guessed arithmetic circuits at all points, paying the actual bit cost of field operations and indirect wiring. For instance, Q=z(1−z) from the failed gatewise construction has H=−1, giving an identically zero corrected polynomial. Extending this one-gate repair to an arbitrary true succinct tableau assertion requires a uniform size and degree bound for its ideal certificates. Mere membership in the Boolean ideal gives no such quantitative estimate.
