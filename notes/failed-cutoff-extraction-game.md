# Extracting an opposite-verdict pair from a failed cutoff search

Given a satisfying assignment and a rejecting [ordered-cutoff pruning trace](ordered-cutoffs-for-two-bit-ram-exhaustion.md), one can name two queried files with identical timed replies and opposite verdicts using at most $\lceil\log_2(Kv)\rceil$ additional SAT decisions. Here $v$ is the number of assignment bits and $K$ the size of the reply alphabet for the whole list of machines. The decisions may all be cutoff queries on the original formula. The number depends on the recorded pairs that could certify failure. The extractor does not have to find a complete least satisfying assignment. On a fixed list it uses $O(\log(v+1))$ SAT decisions. Each cutoff can be compiled into an ordinary proper 3-CNF of $O(n)$ encoded bits as in the original construction. A polynomial-time computation on the trace and its cutoffs generates the questions and names the two original output files.

There is a sharp query bound, including parallel rounds and bounded error. The lower bounds below concern an extractor given the timed reply table, endpoint order, one verified satisfying assignment and failed terminal assignment checks. It may consult the source's truth function only through the specified oracle. A RAM reading the source clauses has additional information. The existence of a failure for the complete unrestricted census remains open. A separate [reduction on explicit SAT files](locating-a-mixed-sat-batch.md) studies the computational hardness of naming the mixed batch from a supplied failure.

## Which pairs can certify the failure?

Number assignments from zero. Let $w$ be the supplied satisfying assignment and let $Z$ be the terminal assignments checked and rejected. The set of feasible values of the least witness from this information is $P=\{0,\ldots,w\}\setminus Z$. It contains $w$. Every $x\in P$ can be the least witness in the abstract game, by taking the witness set to be $\{x,w\}$. Truth of an endpoint test at $e$ is $[\mu\le e]$ where $\mu$ is the actual least witness. Each pair of endpoints $a<b$ with the same observed color at the same depth supplies the interval $(a,b]$. Returning that pair in this order is correct exactly when $\mu$ belongs to its interval. If the chosen colors are heavy, one such interval supplies all the corresponding copies of both verdicts. One may also use any other shared color recorded at two cutoffs. For each color at a depth its first and last occurrence suffice, using the first and last *heavy* occurrence if multiplicity is required. This replacement can only enlarge its useful interval.

The available intervals cover $P$. Every $x\in P$ begins in a live prefix and is absent from the rejected final list. At its first lost prefix, an earlier disjoint prefix had the same selected color. The earlier endpoint is less than $x$ and the dropped endpoint is at least $x$. At most $Kv$ intervals are required when extremal endpoints are used. The domain of $P$ can be exponential, but its holes consist of at most $K$ checked assignments. Finding the first uncovered point, the successive cover endpoints and threshold queries requires arithmetic on $v$-bit integers and lists of polynomial size in the trace.

Let $\kappa\ge1$ be the smallest number of available intervals covering $P$. Start at its smallest point $p_1$, choose a containing interval reaching furthest right, remove the points it covers, and repeat. The sequence $p_1<\cdots<p_k$ of first uncovered points has no two in any one available interval. Otherwise a later point would already have been covered when the earlier one was first considered. The greedy intervals therefore attain the minimum, $k=\kappa$. They partition $P$ into $\kappa$ consecutive nonempty blocks, each with a recorded pair valid throughout it. This is the classical interval edge-cover and independent-set duality. [Aharoni, Holzman and Zerbib, Theorem 1.1(b)](https://holzman.technion.ac.il/files/2017/08/edge-covers_dcg.pdf)

## Exact adaptive and parallel query costs

A query specifies any subset of assignments and asks whether it contains a witness. Give the extractor $q_1,\ldots,q_s$ such queries in successive rounds. Within each round all queries must be chosen before the replies arrive. Define $R=\prod_{h=1}^s(q_h+1)$, taking $R=1$ for zero rounds. The optimal worst-case probability of returning a valid pair is
$$
                      \min(1,R/\kappa).
$$
In particular the exact deterministic sequential cost is $\lceil\log_2\kappa\rceil$, the exact nonadaptive cost is $\kappa-1$, and deterministic extraction without further decisions is possible when $\kappa=1$.

For the upper bound, ordered cutoff tests at block boundaries determine which group of blocks contains $\mu$. A round of $q$ threshold questions can partition an ordered list into $q+1$ groups. Recursing gives exact identification when the number of blocks is at most $R$. If $R<\kappa$, choose $R$ blocks uniformly without replacement. Use the same procedure on their ordered positions, assigning gaps to adjacent leaves. It is correct whenever the actual block is sampled.

For the lower bound use the compatible witness sets
$$
        W_i=\{p_i,p_{i+1},\ldots,p_\kappa,w\},\qquad 1\le i\le\kappa .
$$
Their least witnesses are $p_i$. Each permissible output interval succeeds for at most one of these inputs. Along the ordered sequence $W_i$ any existential question, even with an overlapping or disconnected filter, switches from YES to NO at most once. At a fixed history $q$ such questions can split the surviving chain into at most $q+1$ response classes. Induction across rounds gives at most $R$ leaves on the chain. A deterministic strategy succeeds on at most $R$ members. Averaging over uniform $i$ gives the same upper bound on the worst-case success probability of a randomized strategy. The [Lean proof](../lean/FailedCutoffExtraction.lean) checks the one-round transition count and the point-indistinguishability statements. The full interval and multi-round minimax proofs are manuscript arguments.

## If only complete assignments may be tested

An assignment test asks whether one specified point is a witness. Its optimal deterministic worst-case cost is
$$
                    \min_{I\ni w}|P\setminus I|,
$$
where $I$ ranges over available intervals containing the planted point. On the witness set $\{w\}$ the returned interval must contain $w$. Every point of $P$ outside this chosen interval must have been tested. Otherwise adding that point preserves the entire transcript and makes the answer wrong. Conversely choose the interval attaining the minimum. All missing points of $P$ lie to its left because $w=\max P$. Test them from smallest to largest. The first YES is the least witness and is covered by some available interval. If no test returns YES the chosen interval works.

## Sharpness on explicit proper formulas

One observer returning a constant verdict always retains only the leftmost child, at every depth. The final point is zero. Give $w=2^v-1$ for $v\ge3$ and take a source rejecting zero. On the single-pair-per-depth transcript the intervals are the $v$ disjoint bands $[2^{h-1},2^h-1]$, $1\le h\le v$. If all replies are the same there are exactly two distinct cutoff values at each depth, regardless of how many identical-color copies were used. Only the two files from the same physical cutoff family are offered as an output pair. Thus $\kappa=v$. Extracting a pair takes exactly $\lceil\log_2 v\rceil$ adaptive existential decisions or $v-1$ nonadaptive decisions. Using complete assignment tests alone requires $2^{v-1}-1$ additional tests. Any sequence of $q<v-1$ nonadaptive existential tests has optimum worst-case success $(q+1)/v$.

The nested hard worlds for the existential-query lower bound have actual short source formulas. Choose a different packing point in each band, $p_h=2^h-1$, and name bit one as the least significant bit. Impose $x_{j+1}\Rightarrow x_j$ for all $1\le j<v$, and pin $x_i$ true. The possible assignments are exactly $p_i,\ldots,p_v$. Implement each binary implication as a pair of proper clauses using the two signs of any third variable, and the pin as four proper clauses using two other variables. The formulas have $O(v\log(v+2))$ encoded bits. They have the same supplied $w=p_v$, the same rejected terminal assignment and the same constant observer replies. For the point-test lower bound, each possible pair $\{x,w\}$ also has a proper formula of this size. Pin to one all bits on which $x$ and $w$ agree, and constrain the remaining bits to be equal by a chain of binary implications in both directions. When there are no remaining bits, only the pins are needed. Every unconstrained singleton bit with a missing identifier can be included by a redundant proper clause implied by a pin, or by allowing the declared but unused identifier. In the point-only lower bound the extra point $x$ lies in $1,\ldots,2^{v-1}-1$, so at least one pin is present. The formulas can expose their simple structure to a clause-reading algorithm.

The factor $K$ in the count can also matter in an abstract pruning transcript. For $K=2^h$ with $0\le h<v$, give every child a different color through depth $h$. At all later depths give the two children of each surviving parent a common color private to that parent at that depth. There are $K$ parents, each retaining its left child. The $K(v-h)$ right-child intervals lost over all later rounds are disjoint and cover every nonterminal point. With the maximum planted assignment, each is needed. This construction prescribes observations in the abstract game. The constant program above implements the $K=1$ palette case on actual files, even when the ambient set of timed replies includes unused colors.

The [finite audit](../audits/failed_cutoff_extraction_audit.py) exhausts 948 complete interval families on up to four ordered candidates. It computes the optimal adaptive decision tree and nonadaptive batch over all existential filters, and the optimal point-query tree. It checks 3,576 direct source assignments and solves 18 actual degree-four cutoff files for the nested three-bit example, verifying every positive assignment on the parsed clauses.
