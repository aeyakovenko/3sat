# Counting a circuit's Boolean parity when its high-degree monomials omit few variables

The parity of a polynomial on the Boolean cube can be recovered from its values on a collection of lines over characteristic two. The number of lines depends on how many coordinates each high-degree monomial misses. The missing coordinates can be different in every monomial. There is an exact lower bound for these particular lines and a rank lower bound even for evaluations in arbitrary finite-dimensional algebras. For the journal's actual one-or-two missing computation tables, the canonical multilinear extension reveals the answer at one fixed scalar diagonal over the sixteen-element field. Duplicating coordinates also permits a four-element-field readout for arbitrary parity. Computing its value is the outstanding issue.

## Masking the diagonal

Let $P$ be a division-free arithmetic circuit polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$ on $v\ge1$ variables. Put $0\le b\le v$. Require that **each formal monomial with nonzero coefficient and total degree at least $v$ omits at most $b$ variables**. This is a condition on the final polynomial after cancellations. For instance it holds if in every monomial the sum of exponents in excess of one on its support is at most $b$. No fixed set of exceptional variables is required.

For each $S\subseteq[v]$ of size at most $b$, set coordinates in $S$ to zero and every other coordinate to an indeterminate $t$, obtaining $p_S(t)$. Write $H_v(p)=\sum_{d\ge v}[t^d]p$. Then
$$
\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^v} P(x)=\sum_{|S|\le b} H_v(p_S)\qquad\text{in }\mathbb F_2 .
$$
On the left, a monomial contributes one precisely when it uses every variable. On the right, no low-degree monomial contributes. A high-degree monomial missing $m\le b$ variables survives precisely the $2^m$ masks contained in its missing set. This number is odd precisely for $m=0$. The proof uses linearity, including cancellations caused by specialization.

For $B(v,b)=\sum_{i=0}^b\binom vi$, this takes $B(v,b)$ circuit evaluations in $\mathbb F_2\times \mathbb F_2[t]/(t^v)$. The two components evaluate $p_S(1)$ and its residue modulo $t^v$. Add to the first component the sum of the coefficients of the residue. This computes $H_v$ regardless of the degrees occurring in intermediate gates or at the output. Schoolbook truncated convolution takes $O(v^2)$ bit operations per gate. Enumerating masks, reading the circuit description and copying its gate values cost at most $B(v,b)(s+v+1)\operatorname{poly}(v,\log(s+2))$ word steps for a circuit description of $s$ bits, with ordinary random-access storage for the gates. If only scalar evaluations are available and a formal degree cap $D$ is supplied, $D+1$ scalar evaluations on each line suffice by interpolation over a large enough binary extension; field representation and arithmetic must then also be charged. For $v=O(j)$ and $b=o(j)$, $B(v,b)=2^{o(j)}$, by the standard binomial entropy bound when $b\le v/2$ and by $2^v$ when $v=o(j)$.

## Optimality on masked lines and a general evaluation lower bound

Even an oracle returning the **whole univariate polynomial** on each requested masked line requires all $B(v,b)$ different masks on the zero input if $b<v$. Indeed fix a possible missed mask $S$ and choose $h\notin S$. Define
$$
Q_S(x)=\prod_{j\notin S} x_j\prod_{i\in S}(x_i+x_h).
$$
It is homogeneous of degree $v$, and every monomial has at most $|S|$ missing variables. Its sole full-support monomial has coefficient one. On the line with mask $S$ it gives $t^v$. On every other masked line an outside factor is zero or some inside factor is $t+t$. An adaptive algorithm missing the line for $S$ on zero has the same transcript on $Q_S$. If $b\le v/2$, choose distinct anchors $h_i\notin S$ for the different inside factors. This keeps every variable's individual degree at most two and proves the same line lower bound.

There is also a lower bound on the total dimension of arbitrary evaluation replies. The polynomial is hidden. A deterministic exact algorithm may query points in any finite-dimensional unital commutative $\mathbb F_2$-algebras, choosing both algebra and point adaptively. On the all-zero transcript it must receive total binary dimension at least
$$
B(v,\min(b+1,v)).
$$
For $b=0$ the $v+1$ dimensional upper construction above is therefore exactly optimal. With fixed $b$ and growing $v$, the lower and upper bounds are both $\Theta_b(v^{b+1})$. If **all** monomials, including the low-degree ones, omit at most $b$ coordinates, the optimal total dimension is precisely $B(v,b)$.

Here is the rank argument. The queries on zero give a joint evaluation map $E$ into the direct product algebra of the replies. On the binary linear space of polynomials allowed by the promise, the kernel of $E$ is contained in the kernel of cube sum: any polynomial in the common kernel produces the zero transcript. Thus equal images of two promised polynomials imply equal cube sums, even for an adaptive nonlinear decoder.

For the claim with all monomials constrained, use $x^A x^{[v]\setminus B}$ for all subsets $A,B$ of size at most $b$, where $x^A=\prod_{i\in A}x_i$. The products have individual degree at most two and miss only $B\setminus A$. Their cube-sum matrix is $[B\subseteq A]$, unit triangular in order of increasing cardinality. The evaluation vectors $E(x^A)$ must therefore be linearly independent. A linear relation on them, multiplied in the reply algebra by each $E(x^{[v]\setminus B})$, would give a left kernel vector of that triangular matrix. For the matching upper bound, query the ordinary Boolean mask points $1_{[v]\setminus S}$ for $|S|\le b$. The $2^m$ cancellation applies at every degree. This exact result permits a different missing set in every monomial.

For the stronger bound allowing arbitrary low degrees, let $V_r$ be the span in the reply algebra of evaluations of all monomials of degree at most $r$, with $V_{-1}=0$. For $0\le r\le b$, take all $r$-subsets as a family $\mathcal A_r$. For $b<r\le v$ take all $r$-subsets containing one fixed core of $r-b$ coordinates. For distinct $A,B$ within either family, $|B\setminus A|\le b$. Thus $x^A x^{[v]\setminus B}$ is promised, homogeneous of degree $v$, and has cube sum $[A=B]$. The vectors $E(x^A)$ for $A\in\mathcal A_r$ are independent **modulo $V_{r-1}$**. Indeed multiply a proposed relation modulo $V_{r-1}$ by $E(x^{[v]\setminus B})$ and take the cube sum. Each lower-degree term in the relation now has total degree less than $v$, is promised, and has zero cube sum. The identity matrix forces every row coefficient to vanish. Summing the successive dimension increases gives
$$
\dim V_v\ \ge\ \sum_{r=0}^b\binom vr+\sum_{r=b+1}^v\binom{v-r+b}{b}
= B(v,b)+\binom{v}{b+1},
$$
with the last binomial zero for $b=v$. The argument includes jet evaluations represented by finite quotient algebras. It uses the moment-matrix method for cubature, here with complementary subset monomials and the filtration by degree. The lower bounds concern evaluation access. Inspecting the supplied gate list permits other algorithms. [Fialkow and Petrović](https://cs.newpaltz.edu/~fialkowl/files/cubature.pdf) · [Earlier sumcheck calculation](succinct-prover.md)

## Verifying the formal condition

Failure of the missing-variable condition has an NP witness given the circuit, $v$ in unary or explicitly as its input count, and $b$. For $b=v$ failure is impossible. Otherwise guess a set $S$ of exactly $b+1$ zero coordinates. The restriction setting these variables to zero and leaving the others formal retains precisely the monomials avoiding $S$. A failure occurs iff some such restriction has a nonzero tail of total degree at least $v$.

For a circuit of description length $s$ with binary gates, total degree is at most $2^s$. In a binary extension of degree $s+2$, a nonzero tail polynomial of that degree has some point $u$ with nonzero value, by the polynomial identity bound. Supply $u$ with zeros on $S$ and an irreducible binary modulus of the specified degree. Verify irreducibility, evaluate $P(u)$, compute $P(tu)\bmod t^v$ over the extension, and subtract the sum of the low-degree coefficients from $P(u)$. Every check costs polynomial time in $s+v$. An accepted point proves failure, even if terms at many gates cancel. [Schwartz's polynomial identity bound](https://doi.org/10.1145/322217.322225) · [Rabin's irreducibility test](https://doi.org/10.1137/0209024)

For an application to the [actual sign](priority-pinned-sat.md), suppose a fixed polynomial-time verifier on the unary scale $1^j$ recognizes membership in a set $W_j$ of $v=\operatorname{poly}(j)$ primary bits using an existential polynomial-length witness, with constructible $v$. Assume odd cardinality implies a specified true sign at all scales. Fix a polynomial cap on the size of a circuit agreeing with $W_j$ on the Boolean cube. Under $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ we can find an agreeing circuit minimizing its eligible $b$ within that cap, or determine there is none, in time polynomial in $j$. A violation of agreement has a fixed polynomial-hierarchy test using the verifier for $W_j$ and ordinary circuit evaluation. The preceding test handles the formal condition. Polynomial-hierarchy collapse, binary search over $b$ and bitwise prefix search give the assertion.

Compute $B(v,b)$ and perform the masking algorithm only if $B(v,b)\le 2^{\lfloor(1-\delta)j\rfloor}$ for fixed rational $0<\delta<1$. The resulting detector runs in $o(2^j)$ on every scale. Every odd report is sound. Infinitely many successes contradict the deterministic $\Omega(2^j)$ [native sign bound](priority-pinned-sat.md). Thus under that hypothesis every sufficiently late odd stage has either no agreeing circuit within the cap or $B(v,b)>2^{\lfloor(1-\delta)j\rfloor}$ for **each** such circuit. In particular polynomial-size representatives with $v=O(j)$ and $b=o(j)$ on infinitely many genuine odd stages would imply $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$, which suffices for the three requested SAT clock exclusions. The procedure does not need the favorable stages, circuits or an effective rate for $b/j$ in advance. Even a short verifier giving these actual odd stages on short primary words would already imply $\mathsf P\ne\oplus\mathsf P$ if membership is deterministic and its parity is globally signed as above. ## One fixed off-cube point for the genuine long tables

For a Boolean predicate $H$ on $m$ bits, create a Boolean predicate $G$ on $2m$ bits. Its zeros are exactly $(x,\neg x)$ with $H(x)=1$. The unique multilinear extension of a point indicator has value $\prod_{a_i=1}z_i\prod_{a_i=0}(1+z_i)$ at $z$ for the Boolean point $a$. Take $z_i=\alpha$ for every coordinate, where $\alpha^2+\alpha+1=0$ in $\mathbb F_4$. Each zero of $G$ has equally many ones and zeros and contributes $(\alpha(1+\alpha))^m=1$. Therefore the genuine multilinear extension satisfies
$$
\widetilde G(\alpha,\ldots,\alpha)=1+\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^m} H(x)\ \in\mathbb F_2 .
$$
In particular the value is zero for one exceptional word and one for two. The fixed-point identity exploits the complementary pairs in this particular class of Boolean functions. For an explicit 3-CNF $H$, $G$ is an explicit width-three DNF: include each original clause violation and each equality $x_i=y_i$ expressed by its two length-two terms. Thus computing this one fixed evaluation already recovers parity-3SAT by a linear-size transformation in the conventional literal-list encoding with declared variables used in the formula or charged to its length. In the reverse direction each coefficient bit of the canonical evaluation of a given Boolean circuit or DNF is a parity count: guess a Boolean input, evaluate its predicate and its point-indicator weight over $\mathbb F_4$, and accept exactly if the selected coefficient bit is one and the predicate holds.

Apply the construction to the ordinary width-three constraints with one or two missing genuine gate tables in [Projected parity holes](projected-parity-holes.md), taking $H$ to indicate a missing table. The added violation terms preserve cheap indexed access and the table width only doubles. This gives a uniform **specified point**, with a constant description independently of $j$, whose true evaluation always determines the native sign. On this promised family either field answer therefore costs $\Omega_D(2^j)$ native work on all sufficiently late successful determinations by a fixed sound procedure on stage metadata. The canonical MLE has individual degree one. A syntactic arithmetization of the DNF gives no guarantee of computing it away from the cube. The original closed-circuit gate table has $2^{O(j)}$ bits. The dual-order RAM history has $O(2^j j)$ bits, and the near-native parsimonious circuit version has $2^j\operatorname{poly}(j)$ bits. Each paired encoding doubles the respective width. Writing or scanning that table, supplying an extension oracle, or assuming a short circuit for the canonical extension would change the input cost. A sound inexpensive evaluation on infinitely many particular stages is still missing.

## A sharp field size on the unmodified table

Leave every gate-table bit unchanged. Let $W$ be the original positive predicate on $v$ bits, with a promise of either one or two holes. The two holes may be at arbitrary distinct positions. Choose an element $u$ of order five in $\mathbb F_{16}$ and fix $t=u/(1+u)$. At the scalar diagonal $(t,\ldots,t)$ define
$$
Y=(1+t)^{-v}\big(1+\widetilde W(t,\ldots,t)\big).
$$
A hole of Hamming weight $h$ contributes exactly $u^h$ to $Y$, since $t/(1+t)=u$. The normalization depends only on the public bit width and takes $O(\log(v+1))$ operations by repeated squaring. **There is one hole exactly when $Y^5=1$.** This tests membership in the subgroup of fifth roots of unity.

Indeed, the only nontrivial binary linear relation among $1,u,u^2,u^3,u^4$ sums all five to zero. The order of $2$ modulo five is four, so $1+X+X^2+X^3+X^4$ is the degree-four minimal polynomial of $u$. A singleton contributes one of the five roots. Two equal residues contribute zero, while the sum of two distinct roots cannot be another root. In fact the ten sums of two distinct roots are all different, by the same relation, and fill the ten nonzero elements outside the subgroup. This fixed sample works for the ordinary gate table as well as the shorter dual-order chronology, directly with their original bits. It recovers the actual sign from a canonical-extension evaluation at every scale.

No smaller finite binary field supports a scalar diagonal separating **all** one-hole tables from all two-antipodal-hole tables of the same sufficiently large width. At $t=0$ or $1$ only the selected Boolean corner is visible; use noncorner holes. In $\mathbb F_4$ and $\mathbb F_8$, every nonzero $u=t/(1+t)$ with $u\ne1$ generates the full multiplicative group of prime order $p=3$ or $7$. Give the antipodal pair weights $r,v-r$, choosing $r$ from $\{0,1\}$ so $2r\not\equiv v\pmod p$. Their normalized sum is nonzero and therefore equals $u^h$ for some $0\le h<p$. For $v\ge p-1$ this is attainable by a single hole of weight $h$, giving the identical evaluation. The argument covers every choice of the common scalar separately even if it depends on the width. The minimality assertion is for unrestricted promised hole positions and scalar diagonals. The actual history may have extra structure, and sampling different coordinates at different field elements is a different problem.
