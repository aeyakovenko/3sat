# Large transfer graphs at every block size from one time cut

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Charging a read to the lowest common ancestor of its writer and its reader in a binary tree of time intervals counts it only once. Could the large transfer graphs required of a hypothetical near-linear SAT solver force charges at many different levels? The following construction tests that inference on actual computations of an ordinary SAT solver. The formula’s truth changes if a selected bit of any one transferred word is flipped in the formula. The family has opposite verdicts on toggling a single final pin. [Hierarchy lower bound on actual transfer graphs](pebbling-live-producers.md) · [Information transfer for online problems, Pătraşcu and Demaine](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/DynamicConnectivity_SICOMP/paper.pdf)

There is a fixed globally correct SAT solver and a family of ordinary 3-CNFs of length $N=\Theta(m\log m)$, with $m$ a power of two, on which it takes $T=\Theta(m)$ native steps. On both signs simultaneously the following properties hold for a suitable choice of data at each sufficiently large scale.

* There are precisely $m$ writable loads, with distinct, genuine writers. All their store-to-load intervals cover the same middle half of the main computation. In the aligned dyadic time tree all transfers belong to its root. In any translated dyadic tree, every level of width less than half the main computation contains zero transfers between its children. Each of the middle input bits can independently flip the SAT verdict.
* For some absolute $\gamma,\varepsilon>0$, erase any $\varepsilon m$ transfer edges. For every hindsight partition into $P\le\gamma m$ nonempty consecutive blocks of maximum length $CT/P$, for any fixed $C$, the simple graph on blocks given by the remaining transfer edges alone has treewidth $\Omega_C(P)$ and a clique minor of order $\Omega_C(\sqrt P)$ for large $P$. A repeated block pair gives only one edge. In particular these conclusions hold simultaneously at every polynomial scale $P=N^\alpha$, $0<\alpha<1$. The native graph, with chronology, has treewidth $\Omega(m)$ and a clique minor of order $\Omega(\sqrt m)$ after the same erasures.
* One may further require that a fixed deterministic policy, sound whenever it supplies a word on this run, can supply fewer than $\varepsilon m$ of the loads. The policy gets a shared hindsight string of $s$ bits and up to $q$ adaptive probes of forward input entries per call, with $m/(q+1)\to\infty$ and $s(q+1)=o(m)$. It can inspect all genuine earlier scan replies and even their preimage indices for free. Calls start with empty private scratch. Uncharged advice accumulated by a separate preprocessing pass or by earlier private probes would change this test. A policy with arbitrary polynomial time to scan the entire formula can find every writer on this family.

The graph claim concerns a single family on which *some* globally correct solver runs quickly. The lower bound for every fast solver in the earlier journal article applies on other hard inputs. Its graph conclusion by itself does not force positive information transfer at a growing number of scales. The present counterexample concerns this inference even when the graph is robust, most individual transfers resist short advice with cheap probes, and the stored words affect the true verdict.

## A paired, uniquely satisfiable scatter and scan

Choose a permutation $\pi$ of $[m]=\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$ and independent data $a_i\in\{0,1\}^2$. First encode two unit pins on $x_{\pi(i),0},x_{\pi(i),1}$ for each $i$, with the signs given by $a_i$. Add $2m$ pinned middle bits $d_t$. A chain of XOR equivalences accumulates first all the $d_t$, then $x_{j,j\bmod2}$ for $j=0,\ldots,m-1$. Pin the initial parity to zero and the final parity to $c$. Each XOR uses four clauses and short clauses can repeat literals. Use fixed-width names for only the declared wires, and consecutive records in each phase. The two variants have unique-SAT and UNSAT verdicts since every wire is determined. For each $j$, changing the selected bit of $a_{\pi^{-1}(j)}$ in its pin flips satisfiability. A single-occurrence unit-sign encoding can also use a pair of fixed-false literals. Each $d_t$ has the same pivotal property. Consequently **every** deterministic solver correct on all inputs of this prescribed formula format takes $\Omega(m)=\Omega(N/\log N)$ native steps on each member, including both signs and the hard data selected below. If none of the words it reads covers the sign occurrences for one middle bit, toggling that pin changes the correct answer while preserving its entire run. Each packed word meets only a constant number of different middle pins because each record occupies $\Theta(\log m)$ bits. Thus the specialized algorithm attains the optimal order on each member within this promise. All middle bits remain allowed to vary for this solver-correctness requirement. Which lane the chain uses depends on the target key, so permuting two targets of opposite parities can change the verdict.

The solver first tests the dimensions and header of this layout. In the first pass read each left record, check its syntax, and store the two-bit payload and a presence flag at the encoded target $\pi(i)$. No prior content is needed on a store. In the middle pass use registers to verify all the pinned middle records and XOR templates and accumulate their bits. In the last pass, for each $j$ load the payload and flag at $j$, check the successive XOR templates and accumulate its selected lane in a register. Check the output pin and end of input. A missing flag prevents using this branch. Seeing every flag proves surjectivity, hence bijectivity, since exactly $m$ in-range stores were made. On any format or flag failure use a complete ordinary SAT solver with a fresh work bank. On any input where the branch returns directly, the CNF is exactly the formula just analyzed. This gives global correctness without an earlier seen-table scan. Header and program arithmetic, counters and masks fit in a fixed number of registers. At fixed field widths a constant number of packed input words encode each record. Comparing the expected names and gate clauses and unpacking each tuple takes constant native work.

Use a fixed power-of-two number $B$ of native slots per record in each pass, with short fixed padding after the actual work. The three passes take $Bm,2Bm,Bm$ slots respectively. Only the first pass writes writable memory and only the last one reads it. This proves the scale assertion on the main computation; the $O(\log m)$ setup and final slots change its length by $o(m)$. Every right record's controller before its load is reconstructible from public counters and the parity of the preceding answers and middle bits, after clearing temporary registers. Formula words, including words straddling fields, depend on constantly many forward entries $(\pi(i),a_i)$ and public fields. The middle bits may be fixed to zero on the selected family. Their flips remain valid inputs.

## Two optimal solvers on identical inputs

A second layout gives the same conclusions and also has a globally correct solver with **zero** writable transfers on these very same inputs. This addresses whether verdict sensitivity and optimal elapsed time force the transfer profile to be stable under changing the algorithm.

Pin two source-lane variables at each $i$ to $a_i$, and pin a public variable $h_j$ to $j\bmod2$ at every $j$. For each source $i$ use the literal name $h_{\pi(i)}$ as the selector of a multiplexer on its two pinned lanes. Four width-three clauses enforce the selected output. Feed the $m$ multiplexer outputs, in source order, and the $2m$ pinned middle bits to an XOR chain with the requested output pin. All variables have uniquely determined values before that test. Now the field supplying $\pi(i)$ participates in the logical selection itself. A physical formula read still reveals at most constantly many forward entries. Every $\pi:[m]\to[m]$, including a map with duplicates, yields verdict $[\bigoplus_i a_{i,\pi(i)\bmod2}\mathbin{\oplus}\bigoplus_t d_t=c]$.

The first solver checks the left syntax and scatters exactly as before. It checks the middle bits and its templates in registers, and checks any remaining public chain and parity-pin templates during the final scan. When every target is present, gathering the selected lanes in target order gives precisely the XOR in source order. On other layouts or a missing target it uses its general fallback. The second solver checks all the same syntax and computes the selected lane immediately at each source record from the name of its selector. In the middle pass it adds the middle parity; it verifies all remaining public templates and checks the requested output. Neither the computation nor validation of this layout needs a work array on a valid member. The register-only scan is sound even with duplicated target names. On malformed inputs use a complete SAT algorithm. The lower bound from the middle pins applies to both, so each is optimal up to a constant on every family member, in both signs. On the hard permutations the former solver retains the expanding matching even after the specified forecasts, whereas the latter has an empty transfer graph. Its successful elimination uses commutativity of XOR without reconstructing the original loads. If the selector pins $h_j$ themselves become independent right-hand data, the register-only program can read the named right pin at each source step and obtain the chosen lane directly. With these early right reads forbidden, the identity permutation and left lanes $(0,x_i)$ give all parity queries, with an exact cut-information tradeoff up to logarithmic terms. ## Expansion across hindsight intervals

For a uniform permutation $\pi$ there are absolute $\gamma,\tau>0$ such that, with probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(m))$, every pair $L,R\subseteq[m]$ that are individually unions of at most $\gamma m$ intervals and have sizes at least $m/6$ satisfies
$$
 |\{i\in L:\pi(i)\in R\}|\ge\tau m.                                      \tag{1}
$$
For fixed $L,R$ take $k=\lfloor m/12\rfloor$ distinct elements of $L$ and expose their images consecutively. At each trial the conditional probability of an image in $R$ is at least $1/12$. The number of hits dominates a binomial of parameters $k,1/12$ by coupling the draws with independent uniform numbers. The probability of fewer than $k/24$ hits is $\exp(-\Omega(m))$. A union of at most $r$ intervals has at most $2r$ endpoints among the $m+1$ boundaries, so the number of candidate pairs is at most $(\sum_{i\le2r}\binom{m+1}{i})^2$. Choose $\gamma$ small enough that its exponential rate is below the preceding tail bound and choose a fixed $\tau<1/288$. This proves (1). This elementary interval requirement permits choosing the partition after inspecting the whole run.

Fix any erasure of at most $\tau m/2$ native transfer edges. In the block graph weight each block by its number of load and store slots, with total weight $2m$. A graph of treewidth $w$ has a bag of at most $w+1$ vertices leaving each component with at most half the weight. This follows by assigning each vertex's weight to one bag containing it and taking a centroid of the decomposition tree. If the bag consists of $o(P)$ blocks it removes $o(m)$ endpoints, since every block has at most $CT/P$ slots. More quantitatively choose $w+1<c_CP$ to remove fewer than $m/12$ endpoints. Group the remaining components in two colors, each of total weight at least $m/3$. One component with weight between $m/3$ and $m$ can form the first color. If none has that size, accumulate components to obtain weight between $m/3$ and $2m/3$.

Some opposite-color pair, on opposite sides of the matching, then has each side of size at least $m/6$. Indeed one side of the first color has at least $m/6$. If the other side of the first color also has $m/6$, use the large side of the second color. If it has less, the second color occupies at least $m-m/6-m/12$ there. The chosen color sets are unions of at most $P$ intervals on their own timelines. By (1) more pairs cross than can have been erased. A surviving pair cannot lie in a single block and supplies an edge between two different components, a contradiction. This proves $w=\Omega_C(P)$ up to the additive one.

For the native graph use the same vertex weights, now one per endpoint. Removing $u$ native slots from the chronological path leaves at most $u+1$ intervals. If a weighted balanced bag had $u+1\le\gamma m$ and $u<m/12$, group the remaining components in two colors as above. Their endpoint sets are unions of at most $u+1$ intervals, and (1) again forbids separation. This gives native treewidth $\Omega(m)$.

For either graph with $v$ vertices, the uniform $K_h$-minor-free separator theorem gives treewidth $O(h\sqrt v)$: recursively remove an unweighted balanced separator, build decompositions in the components, and attach the separator to every child bag. The sum of the resulting bounds along any recursive path is geometric in the square roots of the component sizes. Applying the contrapositive gives the two clique-minor bounds. The transfer-only argument uses coarse blocks. At single-slot resolution the matching itself has treewidth one and the chronological edges then matter. [Conroy, Le and Spalding-Jamieson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00663)

## Few correct offers from short preprocessing

Here $\pi$ is uniform and the $m$ two-bit payloads are independent uniform words, independent of $\pi$. Fix any constant $0<\delta<1/4$ and a policy. Each call can probe at most $q$ forward entries after receiving all earlier true inverse/payload pairs in target order. Treat the middle data and final pin as fixed public choices. Fix both choices of final pin separately in the following probability argument. A run that is sound and supplies at least $\delta m$ words has at least $\delta m/2$ offers among the first $\lfloor(1-\delta/4)m\rfloor$ targets for large $m$. Independently sample $r=\lfloor c_\delta m/(q+1)\rfloor$ distinct targets uniformly there and test them in increasing order. For a fixed successful input and its chosen advice, with probability at least $1/2$ there are at least $\delta r/4$ offers and all are correct. This follows from sampling without replacement with variance at most $r$, since $r\to\infty$.

Now fix the advice and sampled targets. Release true inverse/payload pairs for passed target labels, with no special access to the scatter phase beyond the charged forward probes. Before completing the sampled tests there are at most $(1-\delta/4)m+qr$ pairs exposed. On any new forward probe the conditional probability of discovering a still undisclosed sampled target (including the current target) is at most $r/(\delta m/4-qr)$. This follows from the uniform bijection between the unexposed positions and labels. Previously queried positions cannot discover another target. Pad the at most $qr$ probe slots by zero hits. A union bound on designated slots shows that for $k=\lceil\delta r/8\rceil$,
$$
 \Pr[\text{at least }k\text{ targets discovered by these probes}]
 \le \binom{qr}{k}\left(\frac{r}{\delta m/4-qr}\right)^k
 \le 2^{-\Omega_\delta(r)}
$$
for sufficiently small fixed $c_\delta$. When $k>qr$ the probability is zero. Free releases concern only the past and current labels after each prediction. They cannot discover a future sampled target.

At any sampled offer whose target was never found by a forward probe up to and including that test, the target's two payload bits remain fair and independent of the prediction. One can condition on the full permutation and all exposed payloads for this last assertion. On paths reaching the next such offer, multiplying a stake by four for the exact payload and by zero for any other payload preserves expected stake. Leave it unchanged on abstentions and on targets found by forward probes. Starting at one, Markov's inequality bounds the probability of no erroneous offer and at least $u$ such correct offers by $4^{-u}$. Taking $u=\lfloor\delta r/8\rfloor$ and allowing the preceding hit event bounds the desired probability by $2^{-\Omega_\delta(r)}$ for each advice and fixed sample. Averaging the sample, taking a union over fewer than $2^{s+1}$ advice strings, and using the sampling probability $1/2$ gives a bound tending to zero if $s(q+1)=o(m)$. This accounts for advice selected after seeing the permutation and all payloads. The two possible output pins cost only a factor of two. Taking $\delta<\tau/2$ and intersecting with (1) proves the simultaneous construction. The same choice can satisfy any fixed finite list of policies under these budgets. This argument allows complete true prior scan histories but charges all probes that locate input entries in the first pass. A straddling block may expose its own preceding scatter events for free; in a partition with maximum block size $o(m)$ no block spans the middle half.

The finite checks cover 3,136 fully propagated CNFs, 27,616 colored cuts and 49,152 adaptive offer trajectories. Lean proves the color arithmetic, the interval-width obstruction, parity sensitivity and permutation invariance, and the one-step fair-stake identity. The asymptotic probability and minor arguments are manuscript proofs.

The program can be studied with forward queries of polylogarithmic cost and $s=m^{1-o(1)}$ satisfying the displayed product. Giving it one complete scan of the left source for each forecast defeats this bound. Extending an actual fast solver’s information-transfer lower bound to many disjoint scales still requires a property beyond weighted separator size, genuine input-bit sensitivity, and the above local unpredictability. The matrix of source-to-target connections here is a single permutation at one cut.
