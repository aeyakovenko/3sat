# Identifying a transmitted word by its possible local values

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A packet of stack symbols can be long even if only a few words can appear at one of its endpoints. The endpoint need not be able to compute the right word, or enumerate its possible words quickly. An input-specific modular residue can distinguish the right value within that implicit set. This gives an entropy bound on actual *accepting* computations under the linear SAT hypothesis, complementary to the fault bound on rejecting inputs.

Use the fixed unary hierarchy verifier and its stack packet directory. For a concrete legal computation, fix its public directory and CPU boundary information. Each block v has a finite local relation Rᵥ of possible readings and emissions on its incident packets: replay its bounded sequence of CPU transitions, permitting arbitrary external pop symbols, enforcing its controllers and counts and matching its internal stack pairs. A public parameter θ may further restrict Rᵥ via a fixed uniform local test. Only choices of θ retaining the concrete computation are used to obtain an upper bound. For this account the restriction must check one local block in Õ(|θ|+J+L+n) RAM time, including access to the directory. A global tuple of local realizations agreeing on all packets glues to a legal run. [Directory and gluing](stack-packet-faults.md)

Assign each packet to one of its two endpoints, the *owner*. Concatenate all packets owned by v in directory order, in their canonical orientation and a fixed-width binary symbol encoding. Its length Wᵥ is O(L) for a fixed verifier. Write Pᵥ for the projection of Rᵥ to this entire string. If v owns no packets its projection is the singleton empty string. Let xᵥ be the projected word in the legal run. The following cost suffices to identify all these xᵥ simultaneously for use in a fixed-alternation verification:

    |θ| + O(J log(T+2)) + O(Σᵥ log₂ |Pᵥ|).                          (1)

The sum concerns only the owner endpoint, and all words it owns are counted jointly. This allows unrelated values and correlations at the other endpoint.

Interpret fixed-length binary strings as nonnegative integers. If x lies in a set P⊆{0,1}ᵂ with M=|P|≥2, the nonzero positive integer product of |x−y| over the other y has bit length at most (M−1)W+1. For D=4MW+4 the least common multiple of 1 through D exceeds this product, by the binomial argument below. Hence some 2≤d≤D does not divide the product. For this d no other y has residue x mod d. When M=1 use d=1. The modulus and residue cost O(log M+log(W+2)) bits, even if finding them computationally is expensive. For the least-common-multiple inequality, every binomial coefficient in row D divides lcm(1,…,D) by the factorial valuation formula. Its maximum is at least 2ᴰ/(D+1). Modular isolation of a member of a finite set is classical perfect hashing. [Fredman, Komlós and Szemerédi](https://doi.org/10.1145/828.1884)

Guess θ, the public data and one such modulus and residue per owner. A local block qualifies if it belongs to its original relation and its owned projection has the claimed residue. Impose two tests.

1. For every owner, no two qualifying local realizations have different projections on its owned packets. This is the complement of a search over an owner and two concrete blocks.
2. For every block v there exists a qualifying local realization at v such that, for every bit on every incident packet, there exists a qualifying realization at that bit's owner giving the same bit. The choices of the local realization at v precede the universal bit selection.

The second test gives existence at each owner. The first then fixes a single value of its entire projection. For each v, choose its realization from the second test. Every packet agrees across its two chosen endpoints because both agree with each bit of its unique owner value. This proves soundness even if the moduli were picked maliciously. On the concrete accepting computation, the isolating residues give completeness. No argument about fast counting or enumeration of Pᵥ is needed. To test a residue efficiently on explicit W-bit local data, supply a quotient q and check x=qd+r, 0≤r<d; the bounded quotient is unique whenever it exists. Alternatively use fast integer division. Quasilinear multiplication, RAM transcript checks and a fixed number of quantifier eliminations suffice. [Integer multiplication](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2021/193-2/p04) · [Linear-SAT simulation](linear-quantifiers.md)

## A shared sampler with different per-block indices

A second way to supply the owner's value handles a possibly huge support when the right value has substantial probability. Fix a uniform local sampler using at most W random bits at each block (pad unused bits). Fix its directory and common parameter, and let pᵥ>0 be the probability of outputting the actual full owned tuple xᵥ. The sampler can output failure. Only the particular chosen sample at a queried owner will be evaluated. Assume that this one evaluation, including reading its context and extracting the canonical tuple, costs Õ(|θ|+J+L+W+n). Then it suffices to spend

    O(W + |θ| + J log(T+2) + Σᵥ log₂(1/pᵥ))                         (2)

bits on the common data and per-block indices when W≤poly(T). This bound needs no randomness in the final SAT decision.

Choose one shared uniform affine Toeplitz map on W bits. Its matrix diagonals and its additive shift use 3W−1 random bits for W≥1. For distinct inputs the outputs are pairwise independent and uniform. Indeed for a nonzero difference with first nonzero coordinate i, the j-th output of the linear part contains the fresh diagonal j−i and only earlier diagonals for its other terms, in row order. The independent shift makes the first output uniform conditional on the difference. Applying the matrix is binary convolution. Quasilinear bit time follows by integer multiplication with O(log(W+2))-bit slots followed by parity extraction, with the appropriate ordering for the Toeplitz product. [Universal hashing](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(79)90044-8) · [Time-bounded coding theorems for sampled strings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08312)

For owner v try Nᵥ=2^{⌈log₂(2J/pᵥ)⌉} distinct indices of this map if Nᵥ≤2ᵂ. Its number of successful samples has expectation Nᵥpᵥ and variance at most that expectation. The chance of zero is at most 1/(2J). By a union bound one choice of the common map gives a successful index for all these owners. If Nᵥ>2ᵂ, give one successful W-bit random tape literally; W≤log₂(1/pᵥ)+log₂(2J)+1 in this case. Only one successful index is ever stored or evaluated, regardless of the number of trials in the existence proof. A tag and an index length fit the claimed overhead. The case W=0 is deterministic. As in the second test above, fix each block's candidate realization before selecting a packet bit, and then ask the deterministic owner sampler for that bit. Validation at all blocks forces the generated values to form a legal execution. Even if a guessed selector generates a word outside its owner's local relation, it cannot produce a false global acceptance.

## What this would force on the hierarchy source

Take L=⌈√T⌉ and J=O(√T). With linear SAT the first verifier runs in time (n+L+J+|θ|+Σ log₂|Pᵥ|)log^{O(1)}(T+2), using the preceding uniform cost assumptions. The second has the corresponding bound with W and Σ log₂(1/pᵥ). A budget may be padded and enforced independently of the witnesses. For each fixed restriction and sampler algorithm and W=O(√T polylog(T+2)), choose a sufficiently large constant a. At infinitely many accepted lengths of H, **no accepting run, owner assignment and allowed parameter** reaches either cost below T/logᵃ(T+2), including its charged inputs. Otherwise take the union of the two fixed verification modes, use the hypothetical SAT solver, convert to a nondeterministic tape computation and hardwire finitely many exceptions, contradicting the unary hierarchy clock. Thus it is enough to establish such a saving by either route on every sufficiently large accepted length, with one fixed set of local algorithms. Neither saving has been proved for that verifier.

As a scale check, let a local relation require all incident W-bit packets to carry the same arbitrary word, at every vertex of a graph with at least one edge. The cost Σ log₂|Pᵥ| minimized over orientations is W times the minimum vertex-cover size: the vertices assigned an edge cover all edges, and any cover can receive all edges. Even a connected graph with a large minimum cover can have just one free global W-bit word in its legal executions. Choosing this word as a shared parameter then gives singleton local projections at every owner. This example shows why optimizing local support counts without shared information can miss arbitrarily large savings. It gives no bound on how much shared information genuine hierarchy inputs need.
