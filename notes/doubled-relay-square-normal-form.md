# Four-sheet squares in both layers suffice for 3-SAT

In the two-layer, two-bit family, impose a further condition on the four matching tables. Before the designated edit, the two matchings in **each** layer must partition the sheets into disjoint alternating squares, with four different physical sheets in each square. Deciding whether the edit preserves SAT is still NP-complete, under a deterministic reduction linear in the encoded bit length. If the two first-layer matchings instead coincide and the restored second-layer components each have two or four sites, the same decision takes linear time. These statements concern a particular family of proper 3-CNF files. Its hard corner has the same open linear-time question as unrestricted proper 3-SAT. ## Replacing a long relay

Let $P(c,a,b)=\neg c\lor a\lor\neg b$ denote the projected local constraint. A regular four-sheet box imposes $P(c,a,b),P(a,c,z),P(b,z,c),P(z,b,a)$. Their conjunction is exactly
$$
                              c\land b=z\land a.
$$
Indeed each side implies both bits of the other side. The first-layer relays make the physical left occurrence of a name $X$ equal to $X$ and its physical right occurrence equal to $1-X$. With negative edges from left to right, a regular four-sheet box therefore equates two directed descent indicators $D(X,Y)=X(1-Y)$. Each of its two left occurrences is the source of one descent, and each right occurrence the target of one.

Start from the two-layer reduction for a signed proper 3-CNF. Keep its pin box, the one whose restored positive edge will be cut. Make a copy of each old use on a new physical sheet of the same side and logical name. On these copies repeat every regular four-sheet job. The copy of the pin box uses regular $P$ at all centers and retains its crossing edge. Its negative edges join left ONE to right ONE and left ZERO to right ZERO, so it tests $D(\mathrm{ONE},\mathrm{ONE})=D(\mathrm{ZERO},\mathrm{ZERO})$ and imposes no condition. At this stage every name has even left and right occurrence counts. Balance its two sides by adding missing sites in pairs. Match a pair of unused left sites of name $X$ with two unused right sites of name $Y$ in a regular four-sheet box, which enforces only $D(X,Y)=D(X,Y)$. There are equally many unused sites on the two sides because the old jobs and the pin box are individually balanced.

For each old name pair its left and right sites arbitrarily, giving clones $X_0,\ldots,X_{m-1}$ with one old occurrence on each side. Add a regular box for every link $i\to i+1$ cyclically. Equate $D(X_i,X_{i+1})$ to $D(W_i,W_i)$ for a fresh name $W_i$. This forces $X_i\Rightarrow X_{i+1}$, and the cycle forces all the clones to agree. Conversely any old assignment extends, with each $W_i$ arbitrary. This occurrence-splitting argument is the familiar cyclic implication construction, implemented here by the same four-sheet box used everywhere else. Each clone now has exactly two left and two right occurrences. Each $W_i$ has one occurrence on each side. Add its second pair. The number of $W_i$ is even because every old balanced name had even multiplicity. For two new pairs belonging to $W,U$ use a regular box testing $D(W,W)=D(U,U)$. Now every first-layer relay uses exactly two sites on either side. Its two alternating matchings form one genuine square. All second-layer components are genuine squares before the one edge is cut. Every square uses four distinct physical sites even when a logical name appears several times. [Tovey's bounded-occurrence construction](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7) · [Lean proof for arbitrary old constraints on the occurrences](../lean/RelaySquareClones.lean)

The cloning costs a constant number of sites and jobs per original occurrence. In the implementation it uses eight times as many sites per side as the preceding two-layer compiler. Thus the construction still takes $O(N)$ deterministic time and produces $O(N)$ bits using its duplicate-clause normalization on a proper $N$-bit source. Name the original two cut endpoints 10 and 11 as before. Of the four settings obtained by independently restoring either of the two affected physical bits, three have explicit satisfying assignments and the fourth has the source verdict. The duplicate pin box does not introduce another exceptional center. At the hard corner, tightening every regular guard to intact would reject immediately because the second positive edges and its two positive self-loops would require at least $a+1$ physical ones on $a$ sheets per side, while its negative matching permits at most $a$. Testing only the tightened binary constraints therefore misses every satisfiable hard-corner input. [Implementation of the reduction](../audits/sheet_guard_deletions_audit.py)

## How many exceptional relay positions can a scan absorb?

Normalize the first positive matching to identity. Let $t$ count indices in nontrivial cycles of the first relative permutation, and $k$ count indices in cycles of length at least three of the second relative permutation. On $a$ sheets per side there is a deterministic algorithm with running time
$$
                            O(a)+2^{O(t+k+1)}
$$
on the named word layout. The input scan and construction of the reduced instance take $O(a)$ steps. This gives a linear worst-case algorithm even for $t+k\le c\log_2 a$ for some absolute $c>0$. The square reduction has $k=0$ and $t=a$. [Construction of the reduced instance and its proof](singleton-relay-matching-cuts.md)

Here are details of the exponential term. The scan leaves $m\le t+2k+4$ marked vertices and $M=O((m+1)^3)$ candidate descent orbits. Enumerate the $2^m$ pin assignments, testing all prescribed equalities and fixed descents. For a given assignment discard candidates with inconsistent endpoints. Each remaining candidate covers at most two required arcs. Two candidates that cannot be taken together have adjacent descent edges on a directed cycle, so each has at most four conflicting neighbors. Feasibility is equivalent to covering all required arcs by mutually compatible candidates, and any feasible cover contains one of size at most $r\le m$, the number of required arcs.

Use a family of red/blue colorings of the $M$ candidates that realizes every bit pattern on up to $5r$ prescribed coordinates. Given a coloring retain only red candidates with exclusively blue neighbors. They are mutually compatible, so one pass tests whether their union covers. Conversely for any compatible cover of size at most $r$, choose a coloring putting all its members in red and all their neighbors in blue. At most $5r$ colors have been prescribed. When $r=0$ the fixed descents suffice.

For explicit deterministic construction number the candidates $0,\ldots,M-1$ and choose a binary field of order $Q$ with $M2^{5r}<Q\le 2(M+1)2^{5r}$ when $M>0$. For every $u,v$ in the field use the colors $\langle v,u^j\rangle$ in a fixed binary basis. For any nonempty subset of indices the bias of their parity is at most $(M-1)/Q$, because the nonzero polynomial $\sum_j u^j$ over those indices has at most $M-1$ roots and averaging over $v$ kills every other value. Expanding the indicator of $b\le5r$ prescribed bits in parities lower-bounds its probability by $2^{-b}(1-(2^b-1)(M-1)/Q)>0$. Thus enumeration of these $Q^2$ seeds suffices. A defining irreducible polynomial can be found within $2^{O(\log Q)}$ bit operations by trying monic polynomials and trial division through half their degree. Each seed and its current colors can be processed with polynomially many bits in $m+1$, keeping the computation within standard RAM addressing. The scan is performed only once. Combining all guesses and colorings gives the stated time. A missing cover with $M=0$ rejects directly. [Alon, Goldreich, Håstad and Peralta on explicit small-bias spaces](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~oded/p_aghp.html)

## Verification and scope

The singleton algorithm agrees with direct evaluation of the projected center constraints on all 54,720 normalized six-per-side layouts satisfying its hypotheses. Of these, 22,320 are SAT. Every smaller such layout through size five also agrees. The doubled-relay compiler was checked on all 256 subsets of the eight signed clauses on three fixed variables. A further 40 sources include a seven-variable minimally unsatisfiable decision tree, each single-clause deletion of it, and seeded random formulas. All 1,184 emitted ASCII settings had the predicted verdict under CaDiCaL (15 UNSAT) and distances $0,1,1,2$ in actual bits, and a separate structural check recovers all four matchings from the unprojected clauses and verifies that **both** restored layers consist exclusively of squares. Lean proves that the relay replacement preserves satisfiability for an arbitrary predicate on the old occurrences. The scan bound, full DIMACS compiler and running-time analysis are manuscript arguments. Bounded-occurrence SAT and degree-two Boolean constraint problems have an existing literature. Priority for this specific matching restriction and cut algorithm is unestablished. [Finite census and raw DIMACS checks](../audits/bounded_relay_sheet_audit.py) · [Edge constraint satisfaction and bounded occurrence](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03124)

To prove the requested lower bound using this normal form, it would suffice to show that for each correct fixed RAM program and each constant $C$, arbitrarily large square layouts take more than $C N$ executed steps at encoded length $N$. The cut test rules out all candidate families with only logarithmically many exceptional positions at a sufficiently small constant. Its exponential dependence on the number of positions is an upper bound. It supplies no lower bound on solvers that process those positions in some other way.
