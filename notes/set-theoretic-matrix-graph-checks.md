# Multiplicative complexity of verifying a matrix product over an algebraically closed field

Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic zero, let $A,B,C$ be $n$-square matrices, and write $M_n$ for the matrix multiplication tensor. Suppose a straight-line circuit outputs a finite tuple $g(A,B,C)$ whose simultaneous vanishing means exactly $C=AB$ on $k$-points. If it uses $s$ nonscalar multiplication gates, then

$$
                         \underline R_k(M_n)\le 2s.
$$

The proof allows repeated factors and equations whose entire Jacobian vanishes along the graph. It suffices that $C=AB$ is an isolated solution in the $C$ variables at the generic pair $A,B$. Divisions whose denominators are nonzero at that solution count as one nonlinear gate each. The same inequality holds on a generic accepting path of a finite deterministic algebraic equality tree, and on an accepting path for an exact existential graph verifier as specified below. Applying the November 24 preprint's claimed geometric border-rank bound gives $s\ge\lceil n^{9/4}/2\rceil$ in these models. The rank comparison itself is independent of that claimed exponent. [Matrix multiplication preprint and its stated formalization scope](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026)

## Deforming an isolated solution

Regard the entries of $A,B$ as independent indeterminates and put $K=\overline{k(A,B)}$, $p=n^2$. For polynomial outputs, set-theoretic correctness over $k$ implies that the radical of the output ideal equals the graph ideal, by the Nullstellensatz. In particular every $C_{ij}-(AB)_{ij}$ has a power in the output ideal. These identities persist over $K$. Translate to $E=C-AB$ and work in $R=K[[E_1,\ldots,E_p]]$. The ideal generated by $g$ is primary to the maximal ideal. The same hypothesis holds for regular rational outputs whenever the solution is isolated in their common domain.

Choose $p$ constant $k$-linear combinations of the original circuit outputs, written $h(A,B,C)$. Their images $h(A,B,AB+E)$ in $R$ form a system of parameters. Indeed, after each choice the next combination can avoid the finitely many positive-dimensional minimal components of the current zero set. None of the corresponding primes contains every $g_i$. The forbidden coefficients lie in a finite union of proper linear subspaces over $K$, and $k^t$ is Zariski dense in $K^t$ for $t$ outputs. This drops the dimension to zero after at most $p$ choices. In particular the coefficients in these linear combinations are independent of $A,B$.

Make $R$ a module over $S=K[[Y_1,\ldots,Y_p]]$ by substituting these $h_i$ for $Y_i$. It is finite. To see this, lift a finite $K$-basis of $R/(h)$ and express any element modulo $(h)$ in that basis. Iterating on the remainders expands it in the same generators with coefficients in $K[[Y]]$. This converges because the $(h)$-adic and maximal-ideal topologies on $R$ agree. The map $S\to R$ is injective since the integral extension of its image has dimension $p$ and any nonzero ideal in the power-series domain $S$ lowers dimension. Both rings are regular of dimension $p$ and the closed fiber has dimension zero. Miracle flatness makes the finite module flat, hence free of positive rank over the local ring $S$. [Stacks Project, miracle flatness](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00R4)

Let $J=\det(\partial h/\partial C)$, evaluated at $C=AB+E$. The $h_i$ are algebraically independent rational functions of $E$ over $K$ by injectivity. Their Jacobian determinant is nonzero because in characteristic zero $K(E)$ is a finite separable extension of $K(h)$, in which the $dh_i$ remain linearly independent. Multiplication by $J$ on the finite free $S$-module $R$ has a nonzero determinant $\Delta(Y)$, since it is injective and remains injective over $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$. Choose $v\in k^p$ avoiding the zero set of the first nonzero homogeneous part of $\Delta$. Then $\Delta(\epsilon v)\ne0$ in $K[[\epsilon]]$. This choice also stays constant under differentiation in $A,B$.

Base change by $Y_i\mapsto\epsilon v_i$. The resulting algebra is finite free of positive rank over $K[[\epsilon]]$. Its generic fiber has a point over an algebraic closure of $K((\epsilon))$ and $J$ is invertible there. The images of the $E_i$ are integral over $K[[\epsilon]]$ and reduce to zero because their images are nilpotent in the closed fiber $R/(h)$ and hence zero in the residue field of the branch. By Newton–Puiseux, all coordinates of this one point lie in $K[[\epsilon^{1/e}]]$ for one positive integer $e$. We obtain a matrix $C(\epsilon)$ satisfying

$$
 h(A,B,C(\epsilon))=\epsilon v,\qquad C(0)=AB,\qquad
 \det h_C(A,B,C(\epsilon))\ne0 .
$$

In the rational case the original denominators are units at the central point, so their evaluations on the branch remain defined. The equations at $\epsilon\ne0$ are imposed on $h$, allowing the original outputs to take nonzero values there.

## The mixed derivative of the branch

Each coordinate derivation in $A,B$ extends uniquely to $K$ by separability. These derivations commute, and on $P=K((\epsilon^{1/e}))$ they act on the coefficients with $\epsilon^{1/e}$ fixed. They preserve the valuation ring and its maximal ideal, so mixed differentiation of $C(\epsilon)$ commutes with taking its constant term. For directions $a$ in the $A$ entries and $b$ in the $B$ entries, write $T(\epsilon)(a,b)=D_aD_b C(\epsilon)$. Here the directions stand for fixed coordinate directions, extended bilinearly after forming the derivative entries. Reduction at $\epsilon=0$ gives $T(0)(a,b)=ab$.

Evaluate the circuit on the branch. For each nonlinear gate, its mixed derivative is a linear combination of earlier mixed derivatives plus at most two rank-one bilinear forms in $(a,b)$. For a product $w=uv$, the new terms are $(D_a u)(D_b v)$ and $(D_a v)(D_b u)$. For a division $w=u/v$, differentiating $vw=u$ gives the two new terms $-(D_a v)(D_b w)/v$ and $-(D_a w)(D_b v)/v$. The coefficients and the first derivatives are taken at the same branch point in $P$. Affine operations introduce no new terms.

Propagate these identities to the $p$ outputs $h$. The inputs $A,B$ have zero mixed derivative. The coefficient matrix multiplying the unknown $T(\epsilon)$ from input $C$ is exactly the ordinary Jacobian $h_C$ with $A,B$ held fixed, by the first-order chain rule along circuit wires. Consequently

$$
 0=D_aD_b h=h_C\, T(\epsilon)(a,b)
            +\sum_{\nu=1}^{2s}v_\nu\,\alpha_\nu(a)\beta_\nu(b)
$$

for vectors $v_\nu$ and linear forms $\alpha_\nu,\beta_\nu$ over $P$. The left side vanishes because the target $\epsilon v$ is constant under both derivations. Multiplication by $h_C^{-1}$ only changes the output vectors of the displayed simple tensors. Thus $T(\epsilon)$ has tensor rank at most $2s$ over $P$, even if individual decomposition coefficients have poles at $\epsilon=0$.

Every $k$-polynomial vanishing on sums of $2s$ simple tensors vanishes identically after substituting the universal parameters of such a sum, because $k$ is infinite. Hence it vanishes on $T(\epsilon)$. Its entries are integral power series, so the same polynomial vanishes on $T(0)=M_n$. This is precisely membership in the $k$-Zariski closure defining border rank.

## Equality paths and existential witnesses

For a deterministic computation tree with field constants, arithmetic and equality tests, follow its path at $(A,B,AB)$ over $K$. Correctness transfers to the algebraically closed extension by the Nullstellensatz for constructible sets, or quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields. The accepting path requires its zero tests to vanish while its nonzero tests and denominators remain nonzero. These latter conditions hold in a Zariski-open neighborhood of the point in $C$-space. In that neighborhood only $AB$ can satisfy all path conditions. Apply the preceding argument to the path's zero-test differences, which use the original shared arithmetic gates.

Suppose an exact verifier may additionally guess $m$ field elements $W$, with acceptance for **some** $W$ precisely on the graph. Its finitely many arithmetic paths may test equality and nonvanishing. At generic $A,B$ at least one accepting path has nonempty witness set. In the open set where its nonvanishing tests and denominators hold, all its zeros have $C=AB$. Thus the path's fiber is a nonempty locally closed subset $Z$ of witness space $K^m$. Let $t=\dim Z$. Intersect with $t$ generic affine hyperplanes in the $W$ coordinates, chosen independently of $A,B$. The intersection with $Z$ is finite and nonempty. For detail, a generic complementary projective linear space meets the closure of a component of dimension $t$ in finitely many points and avoids its lower-dimensional boundary, including points at infinity. It also misses all lower-dimensional components. The admissible slices contain a nonempty open in the hyperplane parameters over $K$, so density allows coefficients in $k$. For $t=0$ use the whole witness space.

Choose one point $(AB,W_0)$ in this intersection. The path equations together with the slice equations now have an isolated zero in the joint $(C,W)$ variables, with all guards nonzero there. Use the same deformation argument in $K[[C-AB,W-W_0]]$. The auxiliary slice equations are affine and cost no nonlinear gates. The center $W_0$ may depend algebraically on $A,B$ because we differentiate the unshifted circuit and the branch $(C(\epsilon),W(\epsilon))$. The larger invertible Jacobian gives rank at most $2s$ for its complete mixed derivative, and projection onto the $C$ coordinates retains that bound. Here $s$ is the number of nonlinear gates along the selected path. A worst-case path bound supplies the same conclusion for the verifier.
