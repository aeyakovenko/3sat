# Certifying a long parity history without computing its prefixes

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** A running bit updated by an indexed sequence of XORs is already a test for chronology compression. A certificate for its final value can describe edges of a two-dimensional grid. This allows small certificates for some sequences with expensive prefix queries. The number of occupied edges that cross a cut measures precisely how much this construction can save in one oracle model.

## A local certificate with no prefix circuit

For a length-M boundary word y, place one terminal below each column of an M-by-H grid of vertices. Its bottom half-edge has value yᵢ. All other exterior half-edges have value zero. A filling assigns bits to interior edges, with even incident parity at every vertex. Summing the vertex equations cancels every internal edge twice, so a filling forces y to have even parity. The converse needs no size bound: on one row, put the successive prefix parities on the horizontal edges. These are the standard parity equations of a graph. [Håstad, grid Tseitin formulas](https://www.csc.kth.se/~johanh/jhgridfull.pdf)

Suppose n is the length of a hierarchy input, the boundary bits are given by a circuit C computable from the input in time U, and log M and log H are charged as part of the descriptions. Guess a circuit for an edge of the filling. Give an edge one canonical name so both endpoints use the same bit. A violation is witnessed by a grid index and a test of at most four circuit outputs or a boundary condition. If the combined circuit size and index lengths are S, then one explicit SAT instance of length Õ(S) checks for any violation. Under deterministic SAT time O(mᵈ) this gives a nondeterministic test costing U+Õ(Sᵈ) on instances having such a succinct filling. A guessed circuit cannot certify odd parity. To test a desired final bit, append it to the boundary. This is one possible way to eliminate an exponentially long *existential* running-bit history. It still needs an upper bound on certificate size.

**Construct a filling from cancellation partners.** Suppose a circuit π pairs every i with yᵢ=1 to another active index, with π(π(i))=i and π(i)≠i on those indices. Take H=M. For a pair i<j route a path from the two bottom ports up their own columns to row i, joining them along that row. Distinct pairs use distinct columns and distinct horizontal rows; paths may cross at a vertex. Each crossing has even degree. To evaluate a vertical edge in column k, test yₖ and compare its height with min(k,π(k)). For a horizontal edge of row i, test yᵢ, i<π(i) and membership of the edge in [i,π(i)). End conditions follow by the same convention, with the bottom ports given and row zero allowed. Each edge costs O(1) circuit evaluations plus index arithmetic. Pairing circuits may thus replace prefix circuits entirely. An assumed SAT solver finds the next active index for a small y circuit by an ordinary existential search and binary search. Pairing consecutive active indices also requires choosing which alternating edges of this successor list to retain. Correct decisions for prefix *nonemptiness* do not give parity of the prefix count.

**Try a canonical partner computed by decision SAT.** Take two copies of the word 1x, with x an arbitrary N-bit block. Pairing matching positions of the two copies works. Instead pair consecutive active positions in sorted order. The central marker has 1+|x| active positions before it, where |x| counts ones. Its canonical partner is earlier exactly when that number is odd. Thus even the direction of this partner requires N deterministic probes to x. An existential test can find the nearest active position in either direction given a succinct x. Choosing the correct direction in this example gives the parity of its full truth table.

There is a positive repair on sparse fibers. Suppose a small relation C(v,i) marks at most k active M-indices for each key v, with k polynomially bounded in the circuit description length. Under SAT∈P, for a given v one can enumerate the active indices in polynomial time: test if an active index remains above the last one, then find the smallest by binary search. This takes O((k+1)log(M+1)) SAT calls, each copying C a constant number of times with binary comparisons. A polynomial-time procedure thus returns the canonical partner on each nonempty even fiber, uniformly in v, and its Boolean simulation supplies a polynomial-size pairing and filling circuit. There is no comparable bound here when a single key has exponentially many active indices. In a RAM reconciliation proof, tagging records by a unique timestamp can keep the multiplicities small, but selecting the record across many independently described blocks still has to fit the time budget. ## Exact tradeoff on duplicated words

Let M=2N and y=xx for an arbitrary N-bit string x presented by bit probes. Fix a deterministic edge evaluator that gives a valid filling for every x. Allow arbitrary preprocessing independent of x and up to b≥1 adaptive probes to x for each edge. Suppose at most w horizontal edges crossing the middle vertical cut are occupied, for each x. Then

    b w ≥ N.

Sum the equations over the left half of the grid. The resulting parity of the middle-cut edges equals parity(x). Each crossing bit is a Boolean decision tree of depth at most b in x, so it equals a real multilinear polynomial of degree at most b on the cube. This follows by interpolating the two subtrees at each query. Let K(x) be the sum of the crossing bits. It is integer-valued between 0 and w. A univariate polynomial of degree at most w interpolates k↦(−1)ᵏ on these w+1 values. Its composition with K computes (−1)^parity(x) in degree at most bw. Multilinearization cannot raise degree. The unique multilinear expansion of that sign is ∏ᵢ(1−2xᵢ), of degree N.

For 1≤b≤N there is a matching construction with at most ⌈N/b⌉ occupied edges on **every** vertical cut. Break x into consecutive groups of at most b bits. Give each group one grid row. Route each of its two copies of each bottom bit straight up to that group's row. Along the row set each horizontal edge to the parity of the group's bits encountered to its left, counting both copies. Each original bit occurs twice on the row, so the exterior right edge is zero. The vertical bit uses one query. The horizontal bit uses at most b by reading each distinct group bit once. At every vertex the two horizontal edges differ precisely at an active port that terminates its vertical path there. In particular, a single-probe filling exists for every duplicated word, whereas its chronological midpoint can require N probes. The integer optimum for the worst-case width is exactly ⌈N/b⌉ when the height is unrestricted.

## Allow a guessed proof

Suppose an a-bit advice string may depend arbitrarily on x. For every x suppose some advice gives a valid filling whose middle cut has at most w occupied edges. For each fixed advice the edge evaluator still makes at most b queries, including on invalid inputs. There are H possible cut edges. Then

    a + log₂(Σ_{0≤j≤min(w,H), j even} binom(H,j)) ≥ N/(4b+1) − 1.

To prove this, restrict to even x. Fix one advice and one cut pattern of even weight ≤w. Require all grid constraints and require every cut bit to match that pattern. Each individual check has a decision tree querying at most 4b+1 input bits, including a possible prescribed boundary bit. Forbid each rejecting branch of each tree by a clause. The conjunction is a CNF of width at most 4b+1, with no bound needed on its number of clauses. Every satisfying x is even by the cut identity. Each is therefore isolated among the satisfying assignments. The satisfiability coding lemma bounds the size of this set by 2^(N−N/(4b+1)). Union over the advice strings and cut patterns must cover all 2^(N−1) even inputs, which gives the bound. It allows the verifier to behave arbitrarily on a wrong guess. [Paturi, Pudlák and Zane](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/PaturiPudlakZane_1997_focs.pdf) · [Earlier local-check accounting](batch-verification.md)

The sum needs only even cut patterns. At w=1 this gives a≥N/(4b+1)−1, independently of height. Conversely store as advice the parities of the ⌈N/b⌉ groups. A one-row filling evaluates any prefix by combining advice for complete groups with at most b probes for the partial group, also handling the second copy. Thus for height one the advice cost is of order N/b when that ratio grows. This case is closely related to the systematic prefix-parity data structure of Gál and Miltersen. [Gál and Miltersen](https://dodona.be/nl/exercises/241382809/media/gal2007.pdf)

There is also a height-independent bound for general w:

    a + 2bw ≥ ⌈N/2⌉/(4b+1) − 1.

Here is the coding argument. For uniform even x, choose one passing narrow advice A(x). Collect the queried input coordinates on the actual decision paths of all occupied middle edges under that advice. Their union Dₓ has at most bw coordinates. Reveal the values on R=⌊N/2⌋ uniformly chosen coordinates. If bw≤N/4, the probability that all of Dₓ are revealed is at least 4^(−bw), by sampling without replacement, since R−|Dₓ|+1≥N/4 whenever Dₓ is nonempty. If bw>N/4 the displayed conclusion is already immediate.

For any fixed restriction and fixed advice, simulate each cut-edge tree until it either finishes or first requests an unrevealed variable. The set F of completed positive trees is now known. On the event just counted F is exactly the set of occupied middle edges and has even size. Require every cut edge outside F to be zero and require validity everywhere. The fixed positive edges stay positive on all completions. Every passing completion therefore has the same total parity, so all satisfying assignments of this width-(4b+1) CNF on the N−R free variables are isolated. Conditional on the restriction, a uniform even x is uniform on 2^(N−R−1) completions. The probability of successful revelation for some advice is at most 2^(a+1−(N−R)/(4b+1)), by the coding lemma and a union bound. Comparing the two probabilities proves the inequality, even if H is too large to encode a cut pattern economically. The decoder's computation of F is only a counting argument and has no running-time constraint.

## Can fast decision SAT always supply the filling?

For general succinct boundary words, under SAT∈P the claim that *every even truth table of a Boolean circuit has a polynomial-size filling circuit* is equivalent to P=⊕P. Use one common polynomial in the full input circuit description length for the filling descriptions and index lengths. In the forward direction, guess the circuit and dimensions, then deterministically check for a violation using SAT. Under P=NP this is a polynomial-time test for even #CircuitSAT, whose complement is ⊕P-complete by the definition of parity acceptance. Conversely, if P=⊕P, prefix parity is computable in polynomial time because a comparison with the binary prefix endpoint can be included in the nondeterministic predicate. Hardwire the input circuit in the resulting Boolean circuit and use the one-row construction. This equivalence does not require short fillings on odd inputs.

The universal premise is stronger than needed for a hierarchy argument. The paired example supplies a constructive route on selected inputs even when a prefix query is expensive. A remaining task is to build comparable local certificates for the specific changing machine states of a hierarchy verifier, and derive their size from a quadratic or cubic SAT algorithm. The two cut bounds concern black-box bit access. They do not apply to a RAM free to inspect and exploit a succinct description of its input family. Priority of these particular cut bounds and their application has not been established.
