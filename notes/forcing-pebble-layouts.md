# Verifying a missing output's predecessors with local Boolean proofs

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** The earlier pathwidth test checks an entire computation and authenticates coverage of every instruction. A deterministic computation also has a Boolean value circuit. Here the verifier checks just the gate claims sufficient for the desired output. A gate may appear repeatedly, with different local proofs. This removes the requirement for a connected public path through the records. Under linear SAT, many genuine predecessors of the first missing output require high width or more than $2^{\epsilon n^2/\log n}$ records on every short generating program, in the sense below. A true AND pyramid explains why duplicating claims cannot in general make the width small. [Full-trace verifier](trace-pathwidth.md)

## Sound local supports, with repetition

For the [first-hole source](address-diagonal.md), take $d=n$ or $2(n-1)$, $B=2^{20n}$, and the fixed deterministic multitape machine $U$. Given $n,p,z$ with $|p|<d$, $|z|=d$, fix a uniformly indexed, fan-in-two Boolean circuit whose output states that $U$ halts with $z$ by $B$. It has $N=2^{O(n)}$ gates. Gate types and predecessor indices are computable in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ time. For example, simulate the fixed multitape machine by sweeps on a single tape in $B^2\mathrm{poly}(n)$ time, retaining a clock that tests the original $B$-step limit. Unroll the local single-tape update on a rectangular time-by-cell tableau with $2^{O(n)}$ cells and rows, including the initial input and terminal acceptance check. The uniformly generated time, position, and local-gate indices use $O(n)$ bits. No restriction is made on the original machine's work space; this circuit is used by the verifier, not charged to $p$ as advice. Related space simulations also use the uniformity of circuits for bounded tape computation. [Williams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17779)

An occurrence claims a value for a named gate. It chooses some input ports and their values which force that claim for **every** setting of the remaining ports. An input claim checks the actual input. A true AND needs both true inputs. A false AND can use a single false input. Form a finite proof by supplying a supporting parent occurrence for each chosen port and designating a true output occurrence. All edges from conclusions to their prerequisites strictly decrease the publicly assigned gate index. Induction on this index proves the correctness of every claim, even if the same gate index occurs many times or a large part of the computation circuit is absent. [Lean proof](../lean/ForcingLayouts.lean)

For limits $W,L$ there is a nondeterministic verifier that accepts exactly when such a proof has at most $L$ occurrences and admits a path decomposition with bags of at most $W$ live occurrences. Introduce a record for its gate, sign, chosen port labels and a two-bit discharge mask. To check a port, exhibit a live parent with the required gate and value. Forget a record only after all its chosen ports have been checked. Count introductions, cap the live space, require the designated output claim and end empty. A fixed cap of $4L+O(1)$ operations suffices. In an accepting schedule each introduction is a distinct mathematical occurrence; slot reuse after forgetting cannot switch the identity of a parent already checked. The live intervals give the decomposition. Conversely introduce and forget a proof's occurrences according to their first and last bags, checking each port while its two endpoints coexist. A parent can support arbitrarily many children without recording their number.

The unsigned graph can be much smaller than a full computation trace if many transitions do not matter to the final bit. An unrolled forcing tree of $L$ occurrences always meets a logarithmic bag bound: recursively remove a centroid, decompose the remaining subtrees sequentially, and add the centroid to every resulting bag. The largest subtree size at least halves with each level. The verifier also admits arbitrary sharing and different amounts of unfolding at different gates.

## How long a proof can the diagonal afford?

For fixed rational $0<\sigma<5$, let $W=2^{\lfloor\sigma n\rfloor}$. There is $\epsilon>0$ such that we may use
$$
 L=2^{\lfloor\epsilon n^2/\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil\rfloor}.
$$
Let $S_n$ be the set of $d$-bit outputs certified by some $p$ of length less than $d$ within these bounds, for the fixed value-circuit family above. Every member is genuinely printed by $U$ by $B$. If explicit SAT takes deterministic linear time on the unrestricted-space logarithmic-word RAM, then there is a constant $C$ such that for all sufficiently large $n$ and all $0\le K\le w$,
$$
 |[w-K,w)\setminus S_n|\ \ge\ (K+1)/C-1,
$$
where $w$ is the actual first omitted $d$-bit output at $B$. Every predecessor counted on the left really does have a timely short program; every such program fails to have a proof within both limits **on its chosen input and claimed output**.

To check the clocks, the multitape verifier guesses $p$ once. It scans its live array to test each operation. Its branch time is at most $(L+1)\,\mathrm{poly}(n,W,\log(L+2))$ and its work space is $O(nW+\log(L+2)+\mathrm{poly}(n))$. Size and width caps can be supplied as binary inputs to one uniform machine. The [linear-SAT bounded reachability simulation](linear-quantifiers.md) therefore gives deterministic RAM decision time
$$
 2^{\sigma n+O(\sqrt{\epsilon}\, n)+o(n)}.
$$
Indeed the logarithm of branch time is at most $O(\epsilon n^2/\log(n+2)+n)$ and the second logarithmic factor inside the simulation's square root is $O(\log(n+2))$. The uniform verifier lets us first fix $\sigma$, then choose rational $\epsilon$ small enough that the RAM exponent has a fixed upper bound $\alpha<5$ for all large $n$.

Finally the missing $w$ is outside $S_n$. Use the [rank of a scored omission](predecessor-batches.md) to describe $w$. The membership table with $2^d$ bits can be guessed and universally checked at one entry against the deterministic decision procedure. Exact scoring by interval start and end costs $O(2^{2d}\mathrm{poly}(n))$. A fixed number of alternating quantifier blocks under linear SAT gives a rank decoder on the RAM in time $2^{\max(4,\alpha)n+o(n)}$. Quadratic conversion to tapes and quadratic outer universal simulation fit $B$ since $4\max(4,\alpha)<20$. Thus $w$ has bounded omission score, giving the interval estimate as in the full-trace calculation. A vanishing fraction of uncertified true predecessors on one sequence of unbounded trailing intervals would refute linear SAT. The required upper bound on this fraction remains open.

The same verification principle allows semantic lemmas. Fix any sound, bounded-arity calculus on claims of $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ bits, with local axiom and inference syntax checkable in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ time and a distinguished claim for the bounded output statement. Store an additional guessed rank of $O(\log(L+2))$ bits per occurrence and require all its used premises to have smaller rank. A sound root follows even when labels repeat. Any genuine acyclic derivation within $L$ records supplies such ranks. The polynomial-in-$n$ cost per live record preserves the exponent above. The constant $C$ may depend on this calculus. Soundness of its rules in the intended interpretation is a premise of this extension.

## An obstruction that survives arbitrary unfolding

Consider the height-$h$ pyramid circuit with all leaves true and an AND at every internal vertex. Every proof of its true sink must support both inputs at each occurrence. Suppose a proof has a path decomposition with bag size $W$, with arbitrarily many duplicate occurrences permitted. Expand every bag by adjoining the (at most two) supporting parent occurrences of each original member. The new bag size is at most $3W$. The bags of a given occurrence still form an interval: each added interval is that of a child and overlaps the parent's original interval because the proof edge was covered. In some expanded bag for each occurrence we can validate it with both parents simultaneously present.

Sweep these enlarged bags. Initially give every newly live occurrence a white pebble. At its chosen validation point switch it to a black pebble with all prerequisites live. Once it leaves, delete its black pebble. Project to an ordinary black-white pebbling of the original pyramid by keeping black at a gate if any black occurrence is live there, and otherwise white if any white occurrence is live. A white-to-black conversion that changes the projection can legally remove white and place black since the prerequisite labels are pebbled. If the last black occurrence expires while whites remain at that label, delete black and place white freely. Occurrences are validated before removal, so an unsupported final white removal is never necessary. A source has no prerequisites. The sweep reaches a black sink and finishes empty; keeping the validated sink until the end yields a persistent pebbling in at most $3W+1$ pebbles.

The standard black-white pebbling price of a pyramid is $h/2+O(1)$. It follows that **every** forcing proof in this format has $W\ge h/6-O(1)$, independently of its length. Thus duplication and arbitrary presentation order do not guarantee low width. These pebbling lower bounds are classical. [Klawe's pyramid bound, as stated in Nordström, Theorem 3.15](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2010/045/revision/1/download/)

For example, conjoin a height-$2^{6n}$ all-true pyramid to the value-circuit output used above. Its size and local indexing cost still obey the stated bounds, and the recognized relation is exactly the same. Every true instance of this *modified circuit* has a mandatory pyramid subproof, so its minimum width exceeds $2^{5n}$ at all large $n$. The conjunction can instead be eliminated by its definition or by a uniform induction proving that every pyramid level is all true. This separates the width of proofs for a chosen gate encoding from any lower bound on the underlying output problem across encodings. I have no encoding or sound calculus meeting the needed coverage on the actual predecessors.
