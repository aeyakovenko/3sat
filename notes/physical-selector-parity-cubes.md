# Parity of physical one-bit edits selects between two arbitrary SAT instances

Let $F_0,F_1$ be proper explicit 3-CNFs of total encoded length $n$ bits. Let $r\ge1$ and put $S=n+r\lceil\log_2(2+n+r)\rceil$. There is a uniform construction of a dense, sorted, duplicate-free proper ASCII DIMACS template and $r$ different byte positions. For each $z\in\{0,1\}^r$, XOR the least significant bit of the designated bytes by $z_1,\ldots,z_r$. The resulting formula is satisfiable exactly when $F_{z_1\oplus\cdots\oplus z_r}$ is satisfiable. At **every** corner, each variable occurs at most four times. The common bit length is $\Theta(S)$. The positions can be put in distinct packed input words simultaneously for any fixed finite set of logarithmic word-width coefficients.

One can request each byte length in a whole interval $[K\lceil S/8\rceil,(K+1)\lceil S/8\rceil]$, for a sufficiently large constant $K$. Within any one length, changing coordinate $i$ changes exactly its one specified bit. Across lengths all coordinate offsets move by the same header-length displacement. The construction reads the sources and emits the template in $O(1+n/\log(n+2)+r)$ word operations with words sized for the output and the journal's packed builder. For polynomially bounded $r$ on the source RAM a sufficiently large fixed width coefficient suffices. The base template and its offsets suffice to describe every corner. Producing all $2^r$ files costs extra. Constructions of bounded-occurrence 3-SAT using cycles of copies and forced literals are classical. Historical priority for the physical placement and exact-length refinements is unestablished. [Tovey, 1984](https://cedric.cnam.fr/~bentzc/INITREC/Files/CB11.pdf) · [Packed normalization and construction](dense-sorted-cores-for-ram-exhaustion.md)

## A single clause pins a signed occurrence copy

Normalize and remove duplicate clauses in each source. The resulting total number $c$ of clauses is $O(1+n/\log(n+2))$. Use disjoint logical variable sets for the sources. For fresh input bits $t_i$, compute their parity $P$ by $r-1$ XOR gates. The four proper clauses excluding the four incorrect triples specify each gate. For $r=1$ use $P=t_1$. For each source clause $(A\lor B\lor C)$ on side zero require $(P\lor A\lor B\lor C)$, and on side one use $\neg P$ as the guard. With a fresh $y$ for this clause, replace the four-literal disjunction by $(\mathrm{guard}\lor A\lor y)\land(\neg y\lor B\lor C)$. The resulting relation, after existentially choosing gate and splitting variables, imposes exactly the selected source. Empty source conjunctions are allowed.

Give each ordinary literal occurrence its own physical variable. For each logical variable link its physical copies into a directed implication cycle; leave a singleton unlinked. The signed literal attached to a copy denotes the *logical* variable, so ordinarily this link literal is positive and the ordinary clause retains the polarity of its original occurrence. For input bit $t_i$ add two otherwise unused copies with link literals $-a_i$ and $+b_i$. In any model the link literals are all equal. They force $a_i=\neg t_i$ and $b_i=t_i$. If the input bit has no ordinary occurrence, the cycle still has these two vertices.

A link $U\Rightarrow V$ is the proper clause $(\neg U\lor V\lor f)$, with its own fresh $f$ forced false. Use Tovey's thirteen proper clauses on a distinguished literal and nine fresh auxiliaries to force $\neg f$. The distinguished variable occurs three times in the pin and once in the link. Every pin auxiliary occurs at most four times. Explicitly, in each of three independent triples of auxiliaries $(u_j,v_j,d_j)$, put $(\neg f\lor u_j\lor v_j)$ and the three clauses forcing $d_j$ if $(u_j,v_j)\ne(0,0)$. Append $(\neg d_1\lor\neg d_2\lor\neg d_3)$. Some $d_j$ is false, forcing $u_j=v_j=0$ and $f=0$. The all-zero auxiliaries work when $f=0$. Each link has its own such pin. [Original bounded-occurrence construction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0166218X84900817) · [Earlier journal implementation](bounded-occurrence-words-in-dense-sat-cores.md)

For coordinate $i$ introduce $p_i,q_i$ forced false by two disjoint pins of the same kind. To set the bit to zero add $(p_i\lor q_i\lor a_i)$. To set it to one substitute $b_i$ for $a_i$ in this single clause. Every ordinary occurrence copy belongs to one ordinary row and at most two links. Each of $a_i,b_i$ belongs to two links and at most one selector. The pins on $p_i,q_i$ leave room for exactly this one additional occurrence. All auxiliary and marker names occur on every corner. Each link and pin has its private names, and the ordinary clauses use disjoint occurrence copies. The selector is the only clause containing both $p_i$ and $q_i$. All clauses are distinct regardless of the corner.

## Placing the edited bits

Let $d(x)$ count the decimal digits of $x>0$. Choose a multiple $L$ of ten with $L=\Theta(1+S/\log(S+2))$. Number coordinates from zero in this layout. Fix a constant $h$ and set the stride $E=10\lceil(h+20)/10\rceil$. In window $u=L+iE$ set
$$
 (p_i,q_i,a_i,b_i)=(u+1,u+2,u+12,u+13).
$$
The selector is already in canonical literal order. Its two possible last tokens differ only in ASCII `2` versus `3`, whose XOR is one. Neither replacement changes its sorted position since no other row shares its first two literals.

In each window add $h$ independent positive filler clauses whose respective first names are $u+20+j$ for $0\le j<h$. Each of their other two names is fresh and assigned a distinct integer above every window. These rows lie strictly between successive selector rows when sorted, except that the last group lies after the last selector. For dense numbering, first reserve all displayed names and the high filler names. Create additional disjoint all-positive clauses on new triples until the count of available variables is at least the largest reserved name. Assign every unreserved variable a different unused integer in the interval from 1 through this count. The reserved maximum and the number of new variables are $O_h(L+r+c)$, and every integer in this interval occurs. No dummy clause can change satisfiability.

Each intervening filler row occupies at least $d(L)$ bytes, and $d(L)=\Theta(\log(S+2))$. For sufficiently large $S$, its asymptotic ratio to $\log_2 S$ is $1/\log_2 10$. Thus a sufficiently large fixed $h$ gives distance at least $W$ bytes between successive edited bytes for word length $W=2\lceil D\log_2(N+2)/16\rceil$ bytes, simultaneously for all prescribed coefficients $D$, whenever $N=\Theta(S)$. The leading coefficient of $W/\log_2 S$ is $D/8$. Constants and finitely many small lengths can be covered by increasing the window padding or constructing fixed larger templates. No assumption on the alignment of the first byte is needed.

There are $O_h(L+c+r)$ clauses and variables before final length padding. All names have $O(\log(S+2))$ digits. The bound $(c+r)\log(S+2)=O(S)$ follows by separating $r\le n$ and $r>n$. Conversely the dense names through $L$ alone require $\Omega(L\log(L+2))=\Omega(S)$ encoded bits for large $S$. Normalization, creating the gate and pin rows, digit emission and radix sorting take $O_h(1+n/\log(n+2)+r)$ packed RAM steps. The selected byte offsets can be recorded during serialization.

## Exact lengths with occurrence budgets already full

This padding works on any nonempty dense proper core with $v$ variables and $c'$ clauses, preserving its maximum occurrence bound whenever that bound is at least one. Append $t$ disjoint triples of consecutive new variables, one clause per triple. Let $D_t$ be the minimum byte increment, with all signs positive. With a separator newline between adjacent records,
$$
 D_t=d(v+3t)-d(v)+d(c'+t)-d(c')+5t+\sum_{k=1}^{3t}d(v+k).
$$
Changing the signs of any $j$ distinct new literals adds exactly $j$ bytes for $0\le j\le3t$. Each fresh clause can still be satisfied independently. Its first name is greater than all old names, so every appended row follows the entire original body, even after a sign change.

Consecutive minima have gap at most $3d(v+3t+3)+7$. For $t_0=4d(v+c'+10)+20$ and all $t\ge t_0$, we have $d(v+3t+3)\le t-2$. This holds initially by the decimal bound on $v$, and increasing $t$ by one increases the digit count by at most one. Thus $D_{t+1}-1\le D_t+3t$. Every target increment from $D_{t_0}=O(\log^2(v+c'+2))$ onward is attainable. Use the largest minimum not exceeding the desired increment and the requisite number of sign flips. Triple count times its logarithm is bounded by the output bit length, so selecting and spelling the padding obeys the previous word-time estimate on $\Theta(S)$-bit files. Choosing $K$ beyond the uniform base-length bound and this slack yields the entire claimed interval. At any new length, comparing the unpadded body to the padded one gives the same translation of every old byte by the change in header width.

### The smallest universal padding threshold at degree four

Fix $d\ge1$ and require the old signed clauses and numerical identifiers to stay in the new dense core. Among all nonempty cores of maximum occurrence four with $d$ digits in their largest name, the smallest common threshold beyond which **every byte increment** is attainable is exactly
$$
 B_d=1+(d+3)(3d+8).
$$
The extension remains at occurrence bound four and every old satisfying assignment extends. The lower example is itself satisfiable.

For the upper bound put $t=d+3$. All $3t$ fresh names have at most $d+1$ digits. Both header counts can grow by at most one digit: for the clause count use $d\le v\le3c'$ and $d+3<9c'$. Hence $D_t\le2+t(3d+8)=B_d+1$. All increments from $D_t$ on work by the preceding gap argument, now starting at $t=d+3$ because $d(v+3t+3)\le d+1=t-2$. The only exceptional target is $B_d$ if $D_t=B_d+1$. Equality in that bound requires $v=10^d-1$ (even the very first fresh name has $d+1$ digits) and a carry in the clause count. Here $v/3\le c'\le4v/3$ and $c'+t<10^{d+1}$. The only power of ten that $c'$ can cross is $10^d$, giving $c'\le v$. The sum of the unused occurrence capacities of the old names is $4v-3c'\ge v$. Old names of fewer than $d$ digits contribute at most $3(10^{d-1}-1)<v$ to this sum because each name already occurs. Thus one old $d$-digit name has a free occurrence. For the $t$ disjoint positive new triples replace the last fresh name $v+3t$ by this old name, then sort. The two remaining fresh literals in that clause still permit any original assignment to extend. The largest name is now $v+3t-1$, still of length $d+1$, all names occur, and the signed clause count is the same. The saving is exactly one byte as needed.

For the lower bound use $v=10^d-1$, a multiple of three. Partition its names into triples and insert the four clauses on each triple whose first literal is positive and whose other two signs run through all choices. Every name now occurs four times, all clauses are distinct, and setting each first variable true satisfies the formula. There are $c'=4v/3$ clauses. An extension retaining them cannot use **any** old name in an added clause. For $1\le s\le d+2$ added clauses, all used fresh names have $d+1$ digits since there are at most $3s$ and the names must be dense. The variable header grows by one byte; the clause header size stays fixed. The byte increment is at most $1+s(3d+11)\le B_d-2$. An extension with $s\ge d+3$ has increment at least $1+s(3d+8)\ge B_d$, since every fresh name has at least $d+1$ digits and the variable header must grow. Therefore $B_d-1$ is unattainable. This exact threshold concerns additive extensions preserving the old clause set at degree four. A renaming or a replacement of old clauses has more freedom.

### A one-byte successor must sometimes replace all added clauses

On that same saturated source take $t=d+3$, $a=3d+8$ and $B=B_d=1+at$. Choose *any* admissible extension separately at each of the consecutive byte increments $B,B+1,\ldots,B+3t+1$. Initially it must have exactly $t$ added clauses. At the final increment it must have more than $t$, since even $t$ all-negative clauses cost at most $B+3t$. Throughout the interval there is room for at most $t+1$ clauses, since $2a>3t+1$. No smaller count than $t$ is possible by the preceding gap. Hence one adjacent step goes from $t$ to $t+1$ new clauses. At these counts all appearing new names have $d+1$ digits and both headers have fixed digit widths. If the two increments differ by one byte, the total number of minus signs among the new clauses must drop by $a-1=3d+7$.

Every common clause preserves its own number of minus signs. If $k$ former extension clauses disappear, the old total minus the new total is at most $3k$. Thus $k\ge\lceil (3d+7)/3\rceil=d+3=t$. **Every one of the previous extension clauses disappears** on that one-byte step, and $t+1$ new ones take their places. This holds even when the extensions are chosen with advance knowledge of the entire interval. It explains why the constant-clause successor on the journal's earlier special family cannot be imposed simultaneously on all saturated degree-four cores at this threshold. All vertices of the cube can themselves be easy. For instance take both sources satisfiable. Even when the two source verdicts differ, querying all $r$ selector positions only costs $O(r)$ once their locations and orientation are known. The compiler gives no unconditional superlinear lower bound. The [executable audit](../audits/selector_parity_cube_audit.py) parses serialized corners, checks the complete XOR difference mask, counts occurrences and solves the resulting CNFs. It verified 364 actual files at coefficients 1, 2 and 4, including opposite and equal source verdicts. [Lean](../lean/PhysicalSelectorParity.lean) checks equality around an arbitrary signed-copy cycle, the selector projection at every coordinate, the resulting abstract parity-selected verdict and the guarded-clause split. The byte layout and asymptotic construction costs are manuscript proofs.
