# Two interpreters with different first arrivals at the same individual rates

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Fix an integer B≥2. Two efficient literal native prefix interpreters can agree on their complete program-to-output map while having the following different spectra, unconditionally. Under **every** rate-digit law satisfying log₂(F(j)+2)=o(j2ʲ) in the [cylinder-law test](rate-cylinder-laws.md), almost every individual rate x∈[3,4] simultaneously has these properties:

* On the first interpreter, each d≥1 has infinitely many y with K(y)=K_Tₓ(y)=m−d<K_tₓ(y).
* On the second, the same holds for each positive multiple of B. For every other d, only finitely many words make a positive exact-d clock increment, at **every** rate x.

Here Tₓ(m)=2^⌊xm⌋ and tₓ(m)=max(1,⌊Tₓ(m)/mᵃ⌋). The claims hold for all integers a≥1. Thus for a point mass with efficiently computable binary digits the same computable rate has random sums in every column on the first machine, while the nonmultiple columns on the second have dyadic sums. The construction extends the [paired integer-rate construction](programmable-clock-spectrum.md) to clock properties at individual rates under singular, continuous or atomic laws.

## Generic and exceptional programs

Use the same generic branch on the two machines. Reserve a prefix of H ones, with H fixed below. Behind it use B headers of local lengths 1,…,B on the identical payload and select the one whose total consumed length agrees with output length m modulo B. A longer unconditional header simulates the same payload. Synchronize the successful short header to publish no later than the unconditional one. This gives a literal compiler; whenever a shortest timely generic program exists on the generic branch, its deficit is divisible by B. Total generic program mass at length m is at most 2^(−H)C2^(−K₀(m)) by the length-marginal estimate. [Precise headers and timing](exact-saving-blocks.md)

On prefix 0 read an ordinary name q for z=(d,μ-algorithm,I), k=|q|, followed by a conditional halting name p on (z,k), n=|p|. The finite parameter I is a binary parent cylinder, and its depth is s. Use the injective tuple code r for these data, names and runtimes, and put m=2ʳ. Set b=r−⌈log₂(r+1)⌉+4. For sufficiently late records s≤b, and the depth-b descendants J of I have numerical exponent width

    m2^(−b) ≤ (r+1)/8.

Use N=32·2^(b−s) tickets. Conservative lower rounding of μ(J|I) gives each cell a consecutive range of nⱼ tickets with nⱼ/N≤μ(J|I) and Σnⱼ/N≥29/32. Apply the same β lower-bound search and absolute-precision estimates as in the cylinder-law proof. The first computations of q,p and r cost poly(r). Cap subsequent table preparation at 2ᵐ native steps, diverging in both variants on invalid parameters, cap exhaustion or unused tickets. At each fixed qualifying μ and positive I the table takes 2^o(m) eventually: cell depth and precision are r−log₂r+O_{μ,I}(1). Thus the cap never binds on the late records used below, even though arbitrary input programs for putative laws can run for unbounded time.

For a used ticket read ℓ=m−d−1−k−n−log₂N further bits, skipping negative ℓ. The complete name has length m−d. Publish d zeroes followed by the complete name, including its leading zero. Distinct programs at this tuple give distinct words, and distinct tuples have distinct m. On the first machine delay publication until a fixed positive fraction below 2^⌊vm⌋, where v is its cell's lower endpoint. On the second do the same only for B|d; otherwise publish promptly after preparation and copying the literal. The bounded-frame native delay and finite preparation fit the stated upper clock at large m. Prompt outputs of arbitrary successful tuples take at most 2^(2m) eventually, including the cap and copying. The two partial functions agree on every input.

## Why the timing changes are genuine

Fix d,μ and a positive-mass parent I, take q shortest, and take successive conditional halt-time records for p. At the resulting lengths K₀(m)≥k+n−O(1) uniformly in the parameters. On a path in J, assign cost 2^(d−m)/μ(J|I) to each exceptional output assigned to the cell, counting zero on cells without tickets. Total cost on a path is at most Q=2^(−1−k−n), and its average over μ conditional on I is at least 29Q/32. Each assigned output has integrated cost exactly 2^(d−m). This argument uses unique literal outputs, so neighboring release intervals need not be disjoint.

If an assigned word has any generic name of length at most m−d, its cost is covered by the actual mass of that name. The total such cost is at most C'2^(−H−k−n), less than Q/8 for a single sufficiently large fixed H. For every remaining word its exceptional name is its unique shortest name. A delayed release occurs after even Tₓ/m and by Tₓ uniformly on the closed real image of J, since x−v≤2^(−b) and m=2ʳ. These estimates apply at dyadic endpoint atoms as well. At least 25/32 of the normalized law on I consequently has a protected word at each late record. The limsup preserves this lower measure, and the cylinder monotone-class argument makes the recurrence full-measure globally. Intersect over d. At nonmultiple d on the second machine no generic name can realize a positive exact-d increment; its strictly shorter timely companion would contradict minimality. All eligible exceptional names there were published before tₓ for each fixed a at large m. This proves finite support. At computable individual rates the random-series assertions follow from the [computable-series term criterion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5028).

The two machines exhibit a genuine gap at each nonmultiple saving even at permitted individual rates. The largest gap is bounded by B−1 on the full-measure set. This does not produce the unbounded gaps on one interpreter needed for the SAT lower bound.
