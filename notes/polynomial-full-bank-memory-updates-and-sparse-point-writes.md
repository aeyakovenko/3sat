# Polynomial updates to an entire word-RAM memory bank

Let \(R=\mathbb Z/2^b\mathbb Z\), with \(b=\Theta(\log(N+2))\) for an \(N\)-bit input. In addition to ordinary native registers, read-only input, point loads and point stores, allow a single instruction to change all \(2^b\) bank words simultaneously. For every original address \(x\), its content \(v\) moves to address \(\pi(x)\) with new value \(G(x,v)\). Here \(\pi\) is an integer-polynomial permutation of \(R\), and \(G\) is a bivariate integer polynomial. The coefficients can be computed by the ongoing program and depend on the input and earlier reads. For unit-cost instructions use bounded-degree, bounded-length descriptors contained in constantly many registers. More generally charge the cost of supplying and evaluating descriptors of size polynomial in \(b\). The payload map can be irreversible.

After \(h\) native or bulk instructions, track the logical **origin** of a point-store address by pulling it back through all previous address permutations. If their distinct origins number \(m\), the simulation uses \((m+1)\operatorname{poly}(b,\log(h+2))\) bits and \(h\operatorname{poly}(b,\log(h+2))\) ordinary RAM steps. The implicit initial word at an origin is zero or can be obtained from an immutable input view in the ordinary way. The construction supports online reads, newly computed coefficients and repeated point overwrites of a given origin. In particular time and space do not scale with the total number of virtual words changed by all-bank instructions.

## Compositions of input-dependent word polynomials

Put \(L=2b\). The falling factorial \((X)_L=X(X-1)\cdots(X-L+1)\) vanishes as a function on \(R\), since \(L!\) divides its integer value and \(2^b\mid L!\). In a fixed number \(d\) of coordinates, reduce the degree separately in each coordinate using this monic identity. Every resulting polynomial function has a representative with fewer than \(L\) powers in each coordinate. Multiplying reduced polynomials, discarding high degrees by monic division and substituting reduced representations into each other take \(\operatorname{poly}_d(b)\) word operations. Equality of the stored monomial coefficient arrays is unnecessary.

An alternative canonical representation, used in the finite audit, records mixed forward differences at the origin. The Newton basis is \(\prod_i\binom{x_i}{u_i}\). In an integer polynomial, the coefficient of this basis element is divisible by \(\prod_i u_i!\), by converting monomials to falling factorials in each variable. Thus all indices satisfy \(0\le u_i<L\) after reduction modulo \(2^b\). Finite differences on the \(L^d\) grid determine the coefficients, even if the original formal degree was much larger. The ordinary binomials used for evaluation may be calculated with integer arithmetic of polynomial bit length before reduction. For one coordinate and \(u<L\), even the unreduced numerator product has \(O(b^2)\) bits. These finite-ring polynomial identities are classical. [Bhargava, polynomial functions on residue rings](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/p-orderings-and-polynomial-functions-on-arbitrary-subsets-of-dede/) · [Null polynomials in the falling-factorial basis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1364.pdf)

A chronological segment of bulk instructions acts on a cell by a triangular map
\[
                (x,v)\longmapsto \bigl(U(x),V(x,v)\bigr).
\]
Its composite with a later map \((Y,Z)\) has coordinates \(Y(U(x))\) and \(Z(U(x),V(x,v))\). Thus any segment has polynomial-in-\(b\) representation and evaluation cost, however many instructions occurred within it. The permutation \(U\) can be inverted at a requested point by lifting its low bits successively. An integer polynomial is compatible with every congruence modulo \(2^j\). If its map modulo \(2^b\) is bijective, the induced maps on all these quotients are bijective by equal fiber cardinalities. Therefore exactly one of the two next lifts maps to the target's next bit. Invalid descriptors, if part of the language, can be detected. For \(b\ge2\) require a permutation modulo two and odd derivative on the two parity classes. Necessity follows from the two lifts differing by \(2^{b-1}\), and sufficiency follows by the same lifting. For \(b=1\) test the two residues directly. [Rivest, permutation polynomials modulo \(2^b\)](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/Riv01c.pdf)

## One marker per distinct individually overwritten origin

Maintain the cumulative address permutation \(U\) and a dictionary indexed by origin. For every origin that received a scalar write, keep only its latest such write, including its physical address and word at that time. View those surviving event times as markers dividing the history into at most \(m+1\) segments. Each segment stores the composite of its bulk maps. Other point writes in the segment are excluded from the composite. A point write on another origin has no effect on the current cell's history.

* A bulk operation composes its map into the final segment and its address part into \(U\).
* For a point store at \(x\), determine \(o=U^{-1}(x)\). If its previous marker exists, remove it and replace the adjoining segments by their chronological composite. Insert the new marker and create an identity terminal segment.
* For a load at \(x\), again compute \(o=U^{-1}(x)\). If the dictionary has no marker for \(o\), apply the whole bulk composite to \((o,\mathrm{initial}(o))\). Otherwise apply the chronological suffix composite after that marker to its stored (physical address, word). The resulting address is \(x\) and its second coordinate is the requested value.

One balanced ordered tree augments its nodes with their subtree composite, with separate origin-to-marker lookup. Suffix queries and marker/segment changes use \(O(\log(m+2))\) compositions by the ordinary balanced-tree operations. Scalar times provide ordered labels of \(O(\log(h+2))\) bits, including when several stores have consecutive times with no bulk operation between them. Recycle removed storage and use the usual bounded-width sparse bookkeeping for simulation. For polynomial \(h\), all addresses and times use \(O(\log N)\) bits up to constant factors. The result also follows for finitely many banks by maintaining separate dictionaries and aggregates when each bulk instruction reads only its own earlier value at the selected source. Leaf maps, queries and scratch require only polynomially many words in \(b\) per live tree record. The key invariant uses injectivity only for the address permutation. ## Consequences for SAT and the boundary in the instruction set

For every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1\) there is \(\delta>0\) such that no deterministic machine deciding ordinary SAT has both \(O(N^{1+\delta})\) bulk/ordinary steps and \(O(N^{1-\epsilon})\) distinct scalar-store origins on all inputs. The standard simulation would take \(O(N^{1+2\delta})\) time and \(O(N^{1-\epsilon/2})\) auxiliary bits after polylogarithmic model transfers. Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas, Theorem 5.7 and the proof of Theorem 1.1, exclude time exponent \(c>1\) and space exponent \(d(c)\) with \(d(c)\to1\) as \(c\downarrow1\). Take \(c=1+2\delta\) sufficiently near one that \(1-\epsilon/2<d(c)\). This also excludes simultaneous time \(N^{1+o(1)}\) and the stated origin cap. The machine's virtual bank can have all \(2^b\) words nonzero after the very first instruction. The unrestricted number of individual stores available to an ordinary RAM lies outside the cap. [Fortnow's time–space tradeoff](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/npvsnl.pdf) · [Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf) · [Packed word-model accounting](sat-time-space-band.md)

Without any limit on origins, the priority-pinned formulas give a separate almost-everywhere fact. Let \(M=2^j\) and \(N=\Theta(M(j+1)^3)\). Any fixed solver correct on the two pinned formulas requires at least \(M/\operatorname{polylog}M\) bulk/ordinary operations on both at every sufficiently late scale. A shorter execution could be simulated to completion within \(o(M)\) ordinary steps using the local formula generator and the data structure above, contradicting the sound color-tester bound. This pins the scale of a compressed native execution below the requested linear SAT lower bound. [Priority-pinned theorem](priority-pinned-sat.md)

The following specific changes invalidate the small-exception argument.

* Permit the most significant bit \(H(u)\) inside a bounded-length bulk payload expression. With arithmetic modulo \(2^b\), \((1-H(x-a))(1-H(a-x))=[x=a]\), including \(x-a=2^{b-1}\). With the identity address permutation, \(v+(z-v)[x=a]\) simulates any point write in one whole-bank instruction, with no scalar-store origins.
* Even preserving every congruence modulo every power of two allows arbitrary point writes. Give the payload expression one bitwise XOR with a power-of-two mask fixed at each width. Embed a \(w\)-bit guest in host width \(b=3w+1\), using guest addresses \(0\le x<2^w\) and storing a guest word \(v\) as \(Sv\), where \(K=2^{2w}\) and \(S=2K\). For target \(a\) put \(u=-(x-a)^2\) modulo \(2^b\). On guest addresses the square is smaller than \(K\), so bit \(2w\) of \(u\) is set exactly when \(x\ne a\). The ring expression \(E(x)=S-[(u+K)-(u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}K)]\) equals \(S[x=a]\) there, by \(u+K-(u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}K)=2(u\mathbin{\mathrm{AND}}K)\). Scalar-read the old guest word \(y\), then use the identity address permutation and bulk payload \(v_{\rm host}+(z-y)E(x)\). This sets the selected guest word to \(z\), preserves all other guest words, and uses zero individual stores. Values outside the embedded bank may be arbitrary. Every numerical operation in the bulk expression preserves all low-bit congruences. The uniform simulator packs and unpacks guest registers with ordinary scalar word operations and uses a constant number of steps per guest step. The word-width promise here permits the stated constant-factor expansion.
* Permit a second translated old-cell read in each output and a constant number of scalar lanes per cell. Reserve a marker lane initialized to one only at address zero, by one scalar store, and preserve it. At every \(x\), update the data lane to \(v+\mathrm{marker}(x-a)(z-v)\). Two affine address maps and a quadratic expression perform the same arbitrary point write. Ordinary loads read the data lane. This simulation needs only one scalar-write origin in its entire life.
* In a scatter where unhit addresses retain their old words, a non-surjective allowed address map can itself select a single word. The constant map \(x\mapsto a\) with constant payload \(z\) implements precisely the point write. The separate historical predecessor index for such scatters charges space for the phases. [Polynomial scatter images](two-adic-images-for-sequences-of-ram-store-phases.md)

In each of these extended models a proof excluding linear SAT solely from the small count of scalar origins would already exclude ordinary unrestricted linear-time RAM SAT.

## How many low bits can a bulk payload inspect?

Allow the address and payload to use arbitrary boundedly many XORs with masks supported in the lowest \(r\) positions of the host word. Once its operand's bottom \(r\) bits are fixed, each such XOR is an integer translation. Consequently each bulk map is given by an integer polynomial on every low-residue class of its inputs. Compositions preserve this representation: the output residues of a chart depend only on its input residues, so the next chart is determined before substituting its polynomials. A triangular segment needs at most \(2^{2r}\) pairs of bounded-size reduced polynomials. The cumulative address permutation can be inverted by first finding the source residue modulo \(2^r\), then lifting one bit at a time. Bijection on the whole bank gives bijection on all quotients of precisions \(j\ge r\), since the charts respect these congruences and their fibers have equal size.

This multiplies the marker simulator's time and space estimates by at most \(2^{2r}\operatorname{poly}(b)\). In particular, for every \(0<\epsilon<1\) there is \(\eta>0\) for which SAT still has no simultaneous \(O(N^{1+\eta})\) time and \(O(N^{1-\epsilon})\) scalar-store origins, even allowing masks in the bottom \(\lfloor\eta\log_2 N\rfloor\) positions at every bulk step. Choose \(\eta\) small enough that the simulated space exponent is less than \(1-\epsilon/2\) and FLMV's excluded exponent at time \(c=1+4\eta\) exceeds \(1-\epsilon/2\). The same proof permits any bulk primitive whose chart polynomials are constructible at the stated cost and whose outputs modulo \(2^r\) depend only on the input residues. Finitely many point reads may determine the next coefficients without modifying the blank bank.

There is a precision obstruction to using even unlimited such passes to implement one guest write. Embed all \(2^w\) guest addresses as the consecutive physical positions \([0,2^w)\) of an initially zero \(b\)-bit host bank, where \(0\le r\le w\le b\). Fix all choices on one actual execution with zero scalar writes. Its final value as a function of the physical address is a univariate integer polynomial separately on each residue modulo \(2^r\). Indeed the origin-to-physical permutation and the origin's final payload have such charts. The inverse permutation lies in the same class: on a finite set it is a positive power of the original permutation, or the identity. Substitution therefore gives the claim on physical addresses regardless of how the coefficients were selected.

Suppose every guest word of a chart function is zero except at one address, and let \(z\) be its one surviving host word. Put \(n=2^{w-r}\). Then \(z\) is divisible modulo \(2^b\) by
\[
               2^{\min(b,\lambda)},\qquad
               \lambda=r(n-1)+\nu_2((n-1)!).
\]
On its distinguished residue write the polynomial as \(P(a_0+2^r t)\), \(0\le t<n\), with \(a_0\) the least residue. Its \((n-1)\)-st forward difference at zero equals \(\pm\binom{n-1}{i}z\) at the surviving index \(i\). The binomial is odd since \(n-1\) has all its low bits set. The same difference, term by term in the formal polynomial, is divisible by \(2^{r(n-1)}(n-1)!\): only monomials in \(t\) of degree at least \(n-1\) contribute, and their \((n-1)\)-st differences are multiples of \((n-1)!\). This proves the bound. It is exact for arbitrary independently chosen chart polynomials: multiply \(x-z'\) over the other \(n-1\) guest addresses \(z'\) in the residue and use zero on the other residues. The surviving product has valuation exactly \(r(n-1)+\nu_2(i!(n-1-i)!)=\lambda\). When \(\lambda\ge b\), every such marker vanishes. This exact sharpness concerns the chart class; the following explicit circuit handles the XOR primitive.

For any fixed host expansion \(b\le Cw\), a nonzero marker for a single guest word requires \(r\ge w-O_C(1)\). Indeed \(\lambda\ge (r+1)(2^{w-r}-1)-(w-r)\). If \(r<w/2\) the bound already exceeds \(Cw\) for large \(w\). Otherwise \(r\ge w/2\) and \(2^{w-r}\) is bounded in terms of \(C\). At \(r=w\) two uses of the *same* permitted mask suffice for arbitrary point updates. Set \(K=2^{w-1}\), and for \(u_\pm=\pm(x-a)\) let \(h_\pm=(u_\pm+K)-(u_\pm\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}K)\). Each is \(2^w\) times bit \(w-1\) of its operand. Both bits are zero exactly when \(x=a\bmod 2^w\): a nonzero residue and its negative cannot both be in \([0,2^{w-1})\). Thus \((2^w-h_+)(2^w-h_-)=2^{2w}[x=a\bmod 2^w]\). With host width \(3w\), this gives the additive overwrite simulation above with data scaled by \(2^{2w}\). This obstruction and construction use the stated consecutive-address, scalar-read guest representation. Other memory encodings may change the necessary precision.

## Exact minimum support and ordinary sparse RAM snapshots

For a nonzero integer-polynomial function \(f:R^d\to R\), the exact minimum possible number of points where \(f\neq0\) is
\[
  2^{bd-D},\qquad
  D=\max\left\{\sum_{i=1}^d h_i:\quad 0\leq h_i\leq b,\quad
               \sum_{i=1}^d(2^{h_i}-1-h_i)\leq b-1\right\}.
\]

Let \(v\) be the lowest 2-adic valuation of any output. All mixed Newton coefficients are divisible by \(2^v\), since differences are integer linear combinations of output representatives. Divide their representatives by \(2^v\) and reduce modulo two. For a nonzero coefficient at multi-index \(u\), the factorial divisibility above forces \(\sum_i \nu_2(u_i!)\leq v\). For nonnegative integer \(x\), \(\binom{x}{u}\) is odd exactly when the set bits of \(u\) are all set in \(x\), by expanding \((1+t)^x\) over \(\mathbb F_2\). Thus the lowest nonzero output bit is a nonzero multilinear Boolean polynomial in the \(bd\) address bits. Each such monomial has degree \(\sum_i\operatorname{popcount}(u_i)\). The smallest index with \(h\) set bits is \(2^h-1\), with factorial valuation \(2^h-1-h\). Indices beyond the finite input domain have zero basis function there. This bounds the degree by \(D\).

A nonzero Boolean polynomial of degree at most \(D\) on \(n\) bits has at least \(2^{n-D}\) ones. Fix one monomial of maximum degree \(D'\). For every setting of the other \(n-D'\) variables its coefficient is still one, so the remaining polynomial has an input of value one. For sharpness choose maximizing \(h_i\), set \(u_i=2^{h_i}-1\), let \(c=\sum_i\nu_2(u_i!)\), and use
\[
                 2^{b-1-c}\prod_{i=1}^d (x_i)_{u_i}.
\]
It is nonzero exactly when each prescribed group of \(h_i\) low bits is all ones. This gives the claimed support. The minimum-distance argument uses standard finite-ring Newton expansions and the binary Reed–Muller weight bound. Priority for this exact expression has not been established.

For one coordinate \(D=\max\{h\leq b:2^h-h\leq b\}\), so nonzero support is at least \(2^b/(4b)\). On a zero-initialized ordinary \(T\)-step RAM snapshot \(W\), at most \(T\) addresses contain nonzero words. Whenever \(2^b/(4b)>2T\), **zero is its unique closest integer-polynomial function** in word Hamming distance. Every other polynomial function differs even outside \(\mathrm{supp}(W)\) at more than \(T\) addresses. In particular, at the journal's native width on a near-linear run, an optimization which chooses the best global polynomial plus a sparse correction list pays for every nonzero word. The conclusion concerns the entire address space. A description charged only on a promised subset of addresses is a different approximation problem. The multivariate formula likewise limits how sparse a nonzero word-valued polynomial opcode on two input words can be.

The audit composes actual polynomial maps, checks full bank states with interleaved point operations, and exhausts polynomial-function evaluation tables over small rings against the minimum-support formula.
