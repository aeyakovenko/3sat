# An upstream preprint's Weisfeiler–Leman time bound is a hierarchy plus a deterministic-source compiler, and for SAT the same step needs a one-block speedup of the diagonal

An unrefereed upstream preprint (September 2026) proves that k-dimensional Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence needs n^{ck} time on the log-word RAM at every sufficiently large order, for every fixed k ≥ k₀, on explicit adjacency matrices. The proof's constant is c₀ = 10⁻⁹; the statement's c and k₀ are existential. The proof is the deterministic time hierarchy plus a compiler from a deterministic one-tape run into the bijective pebble game, followed by one non-adaptive round of cutoff calls to the hypothesized decider, with no quantifier elimination. Mapped onto the journal's criteria, the same step applied to SAT needs the existential player. A CNF lists every clause, so heuristically a compressed run can live only in the assignment. Provably, a compiler of this kind into SAT that is short and fast enough to exclude O(N^{k₀})-time SAT gives a one-block nondeterministic procedure that decides the diagonal J in o(n²) steps. For k₀ = 1 such a compiler sits between a strong and a weak form of the journal's open one-block speedup premise for J, up to polylogarithmic factors, the polarity of the block and the packed encoding. For k₀ = 2 or 3 it would need a polynomial one-block speedup. This map restates, in one-block terms, the journal's diagonal-only compiler criterion; the preprint adds a worked instance of a succinct compiler from a deterministic source, into a target that has an exponent dial. If the preprint is correct, it is a second instance of the template "hierarchy plus succinct compiler from a deterministic source", beside the journal's forecast theorem. Its TeX source was read in full at the pinned commit, but it was not refereed. The direction came from an external review. Nothing here is a SAT bound; the linear, quadratic and cubic questions remain open. ## 1. The preprint and its statement

**Source.** "Unconditional time lower bounds for Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence", dated September 25, 2026, author "OpenAI", in the upstream repository at the commit the journal pins: https://github.com/openai/math/tree/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Unconditional-time-lower-bounds-for-Weisfeiler-Leman-equivalence-September-25-2026 . The TeX source (`build/main.tex`, `build/sections/01-game.tex` to `05-transfer.tex`, `build/references.tex`) and the README were read in full. The 27-page `paper.pdf` (sha256 `5d58d0f7cee1b9ed468a083efaf94357885e251b203171139132d1643960c904`) was read in part, and its theorem numbering and constants match the TeX. During review four of the files were fetched again from the pinned commit and matched byte for byte. The proofs were read but not refereed, in particular Lemmas 3.5–3.7 and Theorem 4.3. Every statement attributed to the preprint below is the preprint's claim.

**Theorem 1.1**, verbatim from `build/main.tex`:

> There are absolute constants $c>0$ and $k_0$ with the following property. For every fixed integer $k\ge k_0$ and every correct deterministic decider $A$ for $k$-Weisfeiler–Leman equivalence, there is an integer $n_0=n_0(A,k)$ such that $T_A(n)\ge n^{ck}$ for every integer $n\ge n_0$. This holds for multitape Turing machines and for sequential random-access machines with $O(\log(n+2))$-bit addresses and words, boundedly many memory accesses per instruction, and fixed instructions computable in time polynomial in the word length. The same bound holds if $A$ is required to decide correctly only on pairs of simple connected uncolored graphs of diameter at most two, with $T_A(n)$ maximized over that class. It applies both to the joint replacement-multiset convention for $k$-dimensional refinement and to the separate-multiset convention defined below.

- T_A(n) is the worst-case running time on pairs of graphs with n vertices each. A decider is "uniform in the input size; it may depend arbitrarily on the fixed dimension k".
- Equivalence means equal stable k-tuple colour histograms (Definition 2.1). By Lemma 2.3 this is the same as Duplicator winning the infinite bijective game (Definition 2.2) with K = k + 1 pebble pairs under the joint convention, or K = k under the separate one.
- The input layout (§6.2): "the order encoding and the two row-major adjacency bit strings are stored directly in a fixed packed or unpacked layout of $O_A(n^2)$ input words. Every other memory cell initially has a fixed default value."
- **Constants.** The statement's c and k₀ are existential, and 10⁻⁹ is not in it. The proof sets c₀ = 10⁻⁹ in eq. (6.1) and ends with "Taking $c=c_0$". The paper gives no numerical value for k₀, nor for the absolute constant D of Lemma 6.2.
- **Remark 6.3** extends the bound to the finitely many smaller positive k, on unrestricted inputs, by lowering c. It rests on every equivalence test inspecting a matrix entry for every possible hidden edge, that is Ω(n²) bits or Ω_A(n²/log(n+2)) steps with logarithmic-word access, and it "does not assert the promised-class extension for small k".

**Fit with the journal's model.** The journal's log-word RAM has a fixed instruction basis including multiplication and division, word width Θ(log N) and unrestricted workspace in its address range, and it meets the stated hypotheses; Lemma 6.2 handles unrestricted workspace by a list of touched cells. In journal units the input has N = Θ(n²) bits, on valid lengths only, so the bound reads T_A ≥ n^{c₀k} with n = Θ(N^{1/2}), at every sufficiently large order. The preprint excludes randomized algorithms, advice and unit-cost operations on unbounded integers.

**Derived numbers** (not stated in the paper; [constants script](../audits/weisfeiler_leman_preprint_constants.py)).
- The proof of Lemma 6.2 displays a one-tape exponent 760 + 12p. It fits m^{D+50p} for every p ≥ 1 once D ≥ 722; the script uses D = 760.
- The least k satisfying the inequalities displayed in (6.6) and in the proof of Lemma 6.1 is 10⁶ under the joint convention and 10⁶ + 1 under the separate one, for every D ≤ 949. It is least because 20c₀k < p forces p ≥ 1, hence s ≥ 1000 and r ≥ 10⁶. At that k, r = 10⁶, s = 1000 and p = 1, and the lower-bound exponent is c₀k = 0.001.
- n^{c₀k} exceeds the input-reading scale n² only when k ≥ 2,000,000,001.

So the theorem has content above input reading only for astronomically large k. What it provides is the shape n^{Ω(k)} at every large order. None of these numbers bounds the theorem's own k₀, which the paper does not compute.

## 2. The proof as a chain

1. **Game (Lemma 2.3).** WL equivalence is a win for Duplicator in the bijective game with K = r + 1 pairs, where r = k under the joint convention and r = k − 1 under the separate one (Remark 2.5).
2. **Circuit to consistency (Proposition 4.1).** A finite acyclic monotone circuit with seeds and terminal tests becomes an individual-edge arc-consistency instance over F₂, with vector dimension at most two. It has nonempty arc-consistent sets iff every test gate is false. A two-coordinate buffer (4.1) makes propagation one-way: a true source forces {0}, while a false source keeps its full image. Product-schema descriptions are preserved.
3. **Computation to circuit (Theorem 4.3).** Its statement already outputs the graphs; its proof applies Proposition 4.1, Theorem 3.2 and Lemma 4.2. Fix a deterministic one-tape M and r ≥ 3, and put d = ⌊r/3⌋. For every m with T₀(m) < m^d − 1 and 2T₀(m) + m + 2 < m^{r−d}, the construction maps w ∈ {0,1}^m to two simple uncoloured graphs of order O_{r,M}(m⁶), and M accepts w iff the graphs are inequivalent in the (r + 1)-pebble bijective game.
   - A tableau cell (time, position) has address [m]^d × [m]^{r−d} = [m]^r. The successor maps of time and the cyclic position maps are unions of O(dq) product schemas with coordinatewise partial bijections. The description is O_{r,M}(1) one-coordinate tables of O(m) entries, "constructed from w in O_{r,M}(m³) time without running M on w".
   - The graphs can be padded to any order n ≥ m^{20} and built in O_{r,M}(n⁴) multitape time.
4. **Consistency to graphs (Theorem 3.2).** Each block stores two adjacent address coordinates. Lemma 3.5 (address recovery): a wrapping collection of r blocks among at most r + 1 determines one full address in [m]^r. Lemma 3.6 (exact projection): $\mathcal H_U|_V=\mathcal H_V$ for |U| ≤ r + 1. Lemma 3.3 gives necessity and Lemma 3.7 sufficiency.
5. **Diagonal (Theorem 5.2).** The language H_s has a one-tape decider M_s running in time at most C_s(m + m^s + 2)^{20}, and every correct one-tape decider for H_s takes more than m^s steps at every sufficiently large m.
6. **Transfer (Lemmas 6.1 and 6.2).** With s = ⌊r/1000⌋, p = ⌊s/1000⌋ and I_m = [m^{20}, (m + 1)^{20}), every n ∈ I_m has a pair of graphs that is inequivalent iff w ∈ H_s. The deterministic one-tape procedure B tries every n ∈ I_m, giving A at most m^p steps on each, at total cost O_{A,r}(m^{D+50p}). The gaps D + 50p < s and 20c₀k < p give a contradiction at any n ∈ I_m with T_A(n) < n^{c₀k}.

**The parameters the transfer actually uses.**
- The source is M_s, deterministic, one tape, with run length at most C′_s·m^{20s}.
- Each instance has order n ∈ I_m, so Θ(m^{40}) adjacency bits, built in O_r(n⁴) = O(m^{80}) time.
- The decider is called as a black box once per order n ∈ I_m with cutoff m^p, that is |I_m| = (m + 1)^{20} − m^{20} ≈ 20m^{19} calls per word (ratio 1.0096 to 20m^{19} at m = 1000). The calls are non-adaptive and not nested, and the first completed answer is returned.
- The compression takes run length m^{20s} to m^{40} output bits, an exponent ratio of 2/s, which is 2·10⁻³ at k = 10⁶. Theorem 4.3 alone takes runs shorter than m^{⌊r/3⌋} to O(m^{12}) unpadded bits.

## 3. The map onto the journal's criteria

| | Compiler criterion | Alternation criterion (k blocks) | The WL preprint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hard source | J, the deterministic RAM diagonal at clock Cn²; **from the second iteration on, the source is V, which is nondeterministic** | J, by an alternating procedure with k blocks | H_s, decided by the deterministic one-tape M_s |
| Hierarchy | J ∉ DTIME_RAM(o(n²)), tight up to a constant factor | the same | one-tape DTIME: more than m^s at every large m (Theorem 5.2) |
| Compiler | U → proper DIMACS 3-CNF of length M = o(s log s), with a polylogarithmic local bit indexer | none; s + b = O(n²/(log n)^{3k+ε}) | one-tape run → (r + 1)-pebble game on graphs of order O(m⁶); explicit O(n⁴) construction, no local indexer stated |
| Output length | M = o(s log s) bits, which may exceed s; only the relevant assignment, O(1 + M/log M) = o(s) bits, is shorter than s | a saving of (log n)^{3k+ε} in path length | polynomial compression: run length m^{20s} → m^{40} bits |
| Use of the hypothesized solver | inside V, on the failure test of a guessed assignment, over O(log n) rounds each halving the clock | one nested call per block, each costing a factor L³ | one non-adaptive round of cutoff calls: one per order n ∈ I_m, about 20m^{19} per word, each capped at m^p steps; the first completed answer is returned and B is deterministic |
| Overheads absorbed | polylogarithmic only (β + a < 1) | C^{k+1} or L^{3k} | polynomial overheads, absorbed by choosing k large: one-tape squaring (Lemma 5.1), the (n³ + t + 2)⁶ RAM simulation (6.5), the n⁴ construction, the m^{20} padding and an absolute D |

**The paper's step.** Theorem 4.3, applied in Lemma 6.1, is a decision-preserving map from a deterministic run to a target instance of polynomial size in m with an absolute exponent: Θ(m^{40}) bits in the transfer, while the run may last up to C′_s·m^{20s} steps. It is followed by one non-adaptive round of cutoff calls, one per order in I_m, any single completed call deciding w. There is no quantifier-elimination step.

**The journal's iterated form.** The compiler criterion needs a nondeterministic source because it iterates. Its fixed nondeterministic program V receives (P, x′, t) as payload, computes the compiler's output length M, "guesses the assignment, physically constructs the failure test and runs the SAT machine", and after the first iteration "the program is always the same" V. The iteration is forced because the compiler's output need not be shorter than its input: M = o(s log s) may exceed s, so calling the solver on the formula directly costs O(M), which need not be o(s). The criterion therefore guesses the O(1 + M/log M)-bit assignment, which is o(s). That guess is the existential player, and every later round compiles a program that contains it. The WL compiler's output is polynomially shorter than the run it encodes, so the preprint needs neither a guess nor iteration.

**The non-iterated form, stated for SAT.** Fix k₀ ∈ {1, 2, 3} and a fixed RAM program C. On an input x of length n, C writes a proper DIMACS CNF F_x of at most M(n) bits in at most τ(n) steps, with J(x) = 1 ⇔ F_x ∈ SAT.
- **(S) Sufficiency.** If τ(n) + M(n)^{k₀} = o(n²), composing C with an O(N^{k₀}) solver decides J in o(n²) steps, contradicting the diagonal. It can be weakened to the good extensions of each program prefix, as in the alternation note's section "Accuracy needed on padded programs", with the composed tester capped at a constructible o(n²) budget. This is not new. For k₀ = 1 it is the journal's diagonal-only compiler criterion: the round-three entry on that criterion notes that writing the compiled file and running the solver once works at length o(s) with constant-time packed access, and the refereed [black-box routes](black-box-routes-through-the-diagonal-compile.md) note proves that a packed compile of length o(s) would exclude linear SAT. The last section of the forecast note states the same for the forecast language: a uniform compiler with work o(W/n) and output length o((W/n)^{1/k₀}) would rule out O(N^{k₀}) deterministic SAT; and the k = 1 case of the alternation criterion gives the same for J in its narrower range.
- **(N) The existential player is necessary.** This is guess-and-check, since SAT ∈ NTIME_RAM(O(N)). Any such C gives a nondeterministic RAM procedure for J with one existential block of O(τ(n) + M(n)) steps: run C, intern the occurring names in a digit trie, guess one bit per occurring name, and evaluate every clause. Under the hypothesis of (S) with k₀ = 1 it puts J in NTIME_RAM(o(n²)), a Σ₁ speedup of the deterministic diagonal. For k₀ = 2 or 3 the procedure guesses fewer bits, O(1 + M/log M) with M = o(n^{2/k₀}), but its time is still only O(τ + M) = o(n²).
- **Converse, for k₀ = 1 only (the Cook–Levin direction).** The packed reduction turns a one-block procedure into a formula: the alternation note compiles a nondeterministic program into "a virtual explicit 3-CNF" of O(N log³ N) "encoded bits with constant-time packed reads after polylogarithmic setup", citing the [packed-band transfer](packed-band-transfer.md), which was not read here. So the k = 1 premise of the alternation criterion, s(x) = O(n²/(log n)^{3+ε}) on good extensions, with a block of either polarity, gives back a compiler of the kind in (S), as virtual output with packed reads in that note's fixed-width binary literal encoding rather than written DIMACS.

The round-three entry on the diagonal-only criterion already relates it to nondeterministic time for J: the criterion at length o(s log s) would imply that linear SAT excludes J from nondeterministic time O(T/log^α T) for every α > 2, while excluding J from nondeterministic time o(T) would suffice. (N) and the sandwich below restate that relation in one-block terms. So for k₀ = 1, a WL-style compiler into SAT sits between two one-block statements: a one-block procedure with s = O(n²/(log n)^{3+ε}) on good extensions gives (S), and (S) gives J ∈ NTIME_RAM(o(n²)). Up to the (log n)^{3+ε} margin, the polarity and the packed encoding, the two are the same premise. For k₀ = 2 or 3, (S) needs M = o(n^{2/k₀}), which is a polynomial one-block speedup with a short guess, not a polylogarithmic one. **This is the step at which SAT needs the second player.**

## 4. Why WL equivalence escapes that step

- **The target has a quantifier range larger than its input, and that range decompresses the run.** By Lemma 2.3, WL equivalence asks whether Duplicator wins an infinite game whose positions are partial correspondences with at most K pairs; there are n^{O(K)} of them, while the input has Θ(n²) bits. By Lemma 3.5 one position with r wrapping pebbled blocks names one full address in [m]^r, one tableau cell of the m^{Θ(r)}-cell run, and no part of the input lists that cell. Duplicator wins exactly when the consistency instance has a witness (Theorem 3.2), which happens exactly when no test gate of the tableau circuit is true (Proposition 4.1). No witness is guessed in the reduction ("without running M on w"). The price is the target's own upper bound n^{O(k)}, which is why the theorem has the shape n^{Θ(k)} and why the dial k can absorb every polynomial overhead of the table.
- **SAT has no such range.** An explicit CNF lists every clause its universal quantifier ranges over; its only unlisted object is the assignment, and only O(1 + M/log M) bits of it matter. Heuristically, a compiler that writes o(t) bits for a t-step deterministic run must carry the run's content in the assignment, and the provable content of this is (N). SAT's nondeterministic exponent is fixed at 1, so the source side has no dial to borrow either, and compressing nondeterministic sources is itself limited: at the quadratic and cubic deadlines, among compilers of g source guesses with a uniformly small first term, some instances need at least (2.4094… − o(1))g occurring variables ([one-guess compiler barrier](short-branch-verification-and-the-one-guess-compiler-barrier.md)).
- **Each call absorbs every alternation of its game.** The game alternates between Spoiler and Duplicator without bound, and each call settles the whole game on its instance; the calls are not nested, and B never removes a block. In the journal's alternation criterion each block costs a multiplicative factor, C in the native version, where only o(log log t) blocks are affordable, and L³ in the SAT version with k fixed; SAT itself settles exactly one existential block per call. No claim is made about how many rounds the game takes on these instances.
- **The commitments are local.** In the strategy of Lemma 3.7, Duplicator never commits to more than the offsets of at most r + 1 blocks, and the exact projection of Lemma 3.6 lets every retained offset extend to any new block. The commitment obstacle of the alternation route, that the existential player must write whatever the universal player checks against, does not arise, because the target's own decider evaluates the game and no hypothesized collapse has to remove a quantifier.

**The detour through WL itself does not help.** To get a SAT bound from Theorem 1.1 one would compile k-WL (in)equivalence on order n into explicit SAT of L(n) = o(n^{c₀k}) bits in o(n^{c₀k}) time. Composed with Theorem 4.3 and Lemma 6.1, that compiles the deterministic run of M_s, of length about m^{20s} with s ≈ k/1000, into a SAT instance of L(m^{20}) bits built in O(m^{80}) + o(m^{20c₀k}) time. By (N), H_s would then have nondeterministic time O(m^{80}) + o(m^{20c₀k}), where 20c₀k < p ≤ s/1000, while it needs more than m^s deterministic one-tape time. That is a polynomial nondeterministic speedup of a deterministic diagonal, the same premise at polynomial scale. The obvious certificates for WL (in)equivalence, such as the stable colouring of the n^k tuples, have size n^{Θ(k)}; no shorter one is known here, and the literature was not checked.

## 5. What this gives toward the RAM bound for SAT

**It gives** a citable unconditional theorem, the preprint's claim and unrefereed here: for every fixed k ≥ k₀, every deterministic log-word RAM deciding k-WL equivalence on explicit adjacency matrices takes at least n^{ck} steps at every order n ≥ n₀(A, k), with c = c₀ = 10⁻⁹ from the proof, with unrestricted workspace and on the promised class of connected uncoloured pairs of diameter at most two. If correct, it is an unconditional RAM time bound with an arbitrarily large polynomial exponent for a natural problem in P, at every sufficiently large length and not only infinitely often.

It follows the template of the journal's own forecast theorem: a deterministic hierarchy plus a succinct compiler from a deterministic source, with the exponent dial on the target or language side. That theorem ([forecast note](one-variable-delay-and-one-binary-product.md), section "A polynomial-time language on the standard logarithmic-word RAM", and the RESULTS.md entry on a padded prediction problem in P) says that for each fixed q > 1 a padded forecast language has an O(M^{5q})-time log-word RAM decider, while every correct decider takes Ω_P(M^q/log M) steps with unrestricted workspace, with slow inputs at every sufficiently large supported length M = 2^{⌈n/q⌉}; RESULTS.md states the case q = 4. The WL bound holds at every large order, with lower exponent 10⁻⁹k against an unspecified O(k) upper exponent. If correct, the preprint is a second instance of the template, on a natural target whose deterministic exponent grows with k.

**It does not give** a lower bound for SAT at any exponent. Applied to SAT, the provable content of the template's compiler step is (N), a one-block speedup of the deterministic diagonal, which is the journal's existing open premise (route B/C of the [barrier note](what-any-proof-of-superlinear-sat-time-must-get-past.md)). The compression technique (product schemas, address recovery, exact projection) works through a target whose game arena has n^{Θ(k)} positions, and explicit SAT has no such arena. The preprint has a dial only because its target family has one; for SAT the dial would have to come from the source, as Σ₁ compressions of DTIME(t) into explicit SAT of o(t^{1/a}) bits for each a, and none is known.

## 6. The three standard tests

These are run on the cited WL theorem and on the transfer premise.
- **(a) A fast, globally correct solver on the hard family?** The hard family is {(G_{w,n}, G′_{w,n}) : n ∈ I_m}, whose verdicts encode H_s. Theorem 5.2 forces every one-tape decider to be slow at every large length, since each fixed machine designates itself on a whole cylinder of inputs, and Lemma 6.2 tries every order in I_m, which excludes isolated fast orders. No packed-scan solver exists for this family unless the diagonal fails. Pass, as cited and unrefereed. For the transfer premise the family would be the compiled formulas F_x of J, and the same reasoning applies.
- **(b) Does the same argument give a bound for 2-SAT?** For an easy target the argument breaks at the compiler step. A deterministic-source compiler with polynomial compression, output T^γ with γ < 1 built in T^{γ′} time, into a language decidable in linear time would contradict the deterministic hierarchy, so no such compiler into 2-SAT exists, and premise (S) is false for 2-SAT. The preprint's compiler can exist because the target's exponent grows with k. A construction of (S) for SAT must use something 2-SAT lacks; the witness check of (N) is common to both, so it cannot be that ingredient. Pass: no false bound for 2-SAT results, for the preprint as cited.
- **(c) Does it survive the oracle of [the relativized clock](relativized-clock.md)?** That opcode reads a contiguous packed query of up to Θ(n log n) bits or more in one step, which violates two hypotheses of Lemma 6.2: "boundedly many memory accesses per instruction" and "fixed instructions computable in time polynomial in the word length". Theorem 5.2 diagonalizes against oracle-free one-tape machines, and Theorem 4.3 compiles only a local update rule, which an oracle answer is not. So the proof uses the feature the journal requires, that one instruction reads O(w) bits, both for the decider in Lemma 6.2 and for the source's tableau in Theorem 4.3, and neither step relativizes to that opcode; whether the WL statement holds for oracle deciders is not examined. For the transfer premise, route B/C of the barrier note already requires a non-relativizing step: its property (i) says the transfer fails when one instruction reads Θ(n log n) bits, and property (ii) adds the packed-query algebrization requirement. (N) inherits both.

Two method barriers bear directly on (S). The refereed black-box routes note shows that in a black-box model of the diagonal-only criterion with hidden clause signs, where a procedure reads the compiled structure and calls a SAT solver at a stated price, no route passes compile length o(s) under linear SAT; so (S) at k₀ = 1, which needs M = o(s), is exactly where black-box routes stop. Second, the [round-three summary](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-round-three.md) records "The opcode barrier for a constant number of blocks (partial)": relative to the two-word opcode of the hidden-word note, black-box arguments, which treat an instruction as an arbitrary map on two words, are barred from three cases of the speedup premise, including one existential block. By (N), any construction of (S) for k₀ = 1 is a one-existential-block procedure for J, so if that entry holds, (S) cannot be obtained by such a black-box argument and must use the algebra of the real instructions. The entry is unrefereed attempt history, marked partial, and "a method barrier, not a counter-model".

## 7. Where the route lands, and the next step

- SAT ∈ NTIME_RAM(O(N)), and an explicit CNF has no quantifier range beyond its listed clauses and an assignment of O(1 + M/log M) relevant bits.
- In the WL proof the step that compresses a deterministic run polynomially is Theorem 4.3, and its decompression happens inside the target's n^{Θ(k)} game semantics, through Lemma 3.5, Theorem 3.2 and Proposition 4.1.
- With SAT as the target, the provable content of the same step is (N), a Σ₁ speedup of J. For k₀ = 1 it is the k = 1 premise of the alternation criterion, up to the (log n)^{3+ε} margin, the polarity and the packed encoding; for k₀ = 2 or 3 it is a polynomial one-block speedup with a short guess.
- No impossibility is shown; the step reduces to an open premise, and any proof of it faces the two method barriers above: the refereed black-box limit at compile length o(s), and the unrefereed opcode barrier.
- The journal's compiler criterion avoids polynomial compression only by guessing the assignment and iterating, which puts the existential player into the source from the second iteration on.

The preprint offers no step past this point. For the journal it is a second instance, beside the forecast theorem, of "hierarchy plus deterministic-source succinct compiler", to be cited with the conventions of Section 1 and the qualifier that it is an unrefereed preprint. Reusing its machinery (product-schema tableaux, address recovery, exact projection) would first need an explicit-SAT target property that, like the bijective game arena, ranges over more objects than the formula lists without being an assignment. In ordinary CNF semantics there is none; this is a definitional fact, not a theorem proved here.

## 8. What is not proved

- Theorem 1.1 itself: its proof was read in the TeX source, not refereed.
- The derived numbers of Section 1 are computed here, not stated in the paper, and none bounds the theorem's k₀.
- A local bit indexer for the WL compiler: the paper states none, and only the explicit O(n⁴) construction is claimed.
- (N) is a one-line observation. The sandwich holds for k₀ = 1 only, up to the (log n)^{3+ε} margin, the polarity of the block, the packed encoding and the good-extension weakening. For k₀ = 2 or 3 the converse fails as stated. Whether (N) is new was checked only by a narrow grep during review.
- No round count for the bijective game on the constructed instances, and nothing about oracle deciders.
- Adachi, Iwata and Kasai (JACM 1984), the classical instance of the same template for combinatorial games, is named from memory and was not accessed; the preprint does not cite it. Berkholz (LMCS 2013), Grohe (Combinatorica 1999) and Grohe, Lichter, Neuen and Schweitzer (JACM 2025), which the preprint cites, were not accessed.
- No SAT lower bound of any kind.
