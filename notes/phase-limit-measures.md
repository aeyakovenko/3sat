# Which limits can the distributions of first production times approach?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** If SAT on the unrestricted logarithmic-word RAM has a deterministic linear-time algorithm, the distributions in the synchronized phase experiment have a stringent property. Every one of their weak limits along the specified phases has a positive atom at **every limit-computable rate in [3,4]**. Their cluster set is perfect and contains no limit-computable measure. Consequently it would suffice to prove that this specific computable sequence of distributions has even one limit-computable cluster point. Compactness alone does not deliver one.

## An exactly observable measure

Use the native prefix-free stream machine, full cutoff B(m)=2^(20m), feedback phases, first h selected lengths at each offered block, and phase totals P_r and I_r defined in the [phase identity](near-clock-credits.md). These definitions do not use a SAT solver. For every target word at a selected length m on a **failed** window of phase r, let T be the first step by which any program of length <m has printed it. Count only T≤B(m). At the rational rate x=⌊log₂(max(1,T))⌋/m put mass 2^(−m)/P_r. Sum over these distinct words and call the resulting finite measure μ_r. Its support is in [0,20] and its mass is at most 2. Since each phase terminates, simulation through its deadlines computes the finite rational measure without an efficiency requirement. Using the unrounded log-rate changes its location by at most 1/m and gives the same weak limits.

For a sufficiently large fixed integer k, infinitely many phases meet the two rational tests

    G_k = { r : I_r ≥ 2^(−k) P_r and I_r ≥ 1/r² }.

Indeed the term test for Ω gives I_r at least a constant times the universal discrete semimeasure on infinitely many r, while this semimeasure dominates both a constant multiple of P_r and a constant multiple of 1/[r(log(r+2))²]. The tests defining G_k are exactly computable after finding the end of phase r. The grant decoder need not predict membership before the phase or know k. Fix any such k. ## Spending grants at an arbitrary limit-computable rate

Let x∈[3,4] be the limit of a computable sequence of rationals. Give the stream decoder the fixed program enumerating these approximations; no convergence rate is required. At the start of phase r simulate this program for r steps and use its most recent complete output, clamped to [3,4], defaulting to 3. Call this rational x_r. Thus x_r→x and its bit length is O(r). At every selected length m of that phase use the exact integer deadline D_m=2^⌊x_r m⌋ and early cutoff t_m=⌊D_m/m^a⌋. The rate is constant within each phase. Across successive windows belonging to different phases the jump in minimum length makes the time for the previous phase negligible even if the rate decreases. All arithmetic deciding the rates and deadlines is polynomial in m.

Under linear SAT, the counted words in the early table can be ranked and the remaining words unranked before D_m for one sufficiently large fixed a. Use the word allocator with this early table. Its grant arrivals are independent of x_r. Its queue empties on the last successful window of every phase. Thus in every sufficiently late phase it assigns exactly 2^(−d)I_r distinct-word mass for a decoder-dependent constant d. Each of these words first appears during (t_m,D_m]. At most 2^(1−r) of this mass can fall on the final success. In every sufficiently late phase r∈G_k the resulting contribution to μ_r has mass at least 2^(−d−k−1), since 2^(1−r)=o(I_r) there. Its log-rates all lie within (a log₂ m+3)/m of x_r. These neighborhoods shrink uniformly as the phase increases. [Native clock calculation](near-clock-credits.md) · [Exact allocation](fifo-producer-credits.md)

Now let μ be the weak limit along *any* convergent subsequence of μ_r with r∈G_k. For each fixed closed interval about x with x in its relative interior, the preceding mass belongs to that interval eventually along the subsequence. The closed-set inequality for weak convergence gives μ(interval)≥2^(−d−k−1). Intersect shrinking closed intervals to obtain

    μ({x}) ≥ 2^(−d−k−1) > 0.

The high phases G_k were fixed independently of x. Therefore this statement holds for every limit-computable x at the same weak limit μ. The lower bound can depend on the program approximating x. Since those programs are countable, the conclusion respects finite total mass. On this same G_k the success cost times 2^⌊r/2⌋ is o(I_r) by the test I_r≥1/r². The separate experiment dividing each grant among that many rates therefore gives a positive Lebesgue component across [3,4]. [Simultaneous disjoint intervals](rate-bin-credits.md)

## A sufficient condition for a SAT lower bound

For a finite measure on this computably compact interval, take a name to provide arbitrary-precision weak approximations, including integrals of rational piecewise-linear functions. Every atom z of such a measure is computable from its name, allowing constants for the particular atom. Choose a rational q strictly between zero and μ({z}), and a rational neighborhood V of z with μ(V∖{z})<q. Search over rational nonnegative bumps bounded by one, supported inside V on intervals shorter than 2^(−n), until an integral exceeds q. A bump equal to one at z exists on such a short interval, and every bump passing the test has z in its support. Its center approximates z to 2^(−n).

Conversely, from a name of μ one can construct at least one nonatom in (3,4). Inside a current rational interval search for a smaller closed rational interval in its interior, of diameter at most 2^(−n), whose mass is <2^(−n). There are arbitrarily many disjoint candidates, and finite total mass ensures one passes. Strict upper mass bounds on a closed interval can be recognized by continuous rational upper approximations to its indicator. Nested intervals found this way specify a point of mass zero.

In particular if μ had a name computable from the ordinary halting problem, its nonatom constructed above would be a limit-computable real, contradicting the atomic conclusion. Also the limit-computable rate 3+Ω/2 encodes the halting problem and is an atom, so every name of μ computes the halting problem. Thus the degree of any such name is strictly above that of the halting problem. Here the equivalence between limit computability of a real and computability relative to the halting problem uses the ordinary limit lemma. An analogous all-computable-points atom property is known for Levin’s neutral measures. The assertion here derives the atoms at limit-computable rates for a particular family of first-time distributions under the SAT hypothesis. [Bienvenu, Gács, Hoyrup, Rojas and Shen on neutral measures](https://www.lirmm.fr/~ashen/nafit/2011-bghrs-eng.pdf)

The atom bounds are preserved under convex combinations and weak limits of those combinations, since each bound on the mass of a closed singleton defines a weakly closed convex set. The closed convex hull of the high-phase cluster measures also has no limit-computable member under linear SAT.

The high-phase cluster set is a nonempty compact metrizable space, by bounded mass on a compact interval. An isolated cluster point would have a basic rational weak neighborhood whose closure contains no other cluster point. Use rational piecewise-linear tests and rational strict bounds. For these finite rational μ_r membership is decidable after phase r. The subsequence of high phases visiting that neighborhood is infinite, computable and converges weakly to the isolated point. The limit of a computable convergent sequence has a name computable from the halting problem, even without an effective convergence rate. This excludes isolated points. A nonempty perfect compact metric space has uncountably many points by binary splitting into disjoint shrinking neighborhoods.

**Attempt to complete the proof.** An infinite computably enumerable subsequence of G_k along which μ_r converges, or any independently constructed limit-computable measure in the closed convex hull of its cluster points, would now exclude linear SAT. The known numerical inputs here are bounded mass, computability of individual phase tables and random convergence of a *scalar* series. They do not establish either sufficient condition. In particular there is a computable bounded rational sequence with no limit-computable accumulation point, as shown by Le Roux and Ziegler. Placing unit atoms at its values gives the same obstruction even for the closed convex hull of the weak cluster points. Every probability measure in that hull is supported on the closed set C of numerical accumulation points. From a name for any such probability, search for nested rational open intervals of shrinking diameter and positive mass, each with closure inside its predecessor. Positivity is semidecidable from the measure, and the limit point belongs to C. A limit-computable name for a probability in the hull would therefore give a limit-computable point of C. An argument exploiting the first production times of this particular universal machine is still required. [Le Roux and Ziegler, Theorem 3.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cs/0610080) · [Kreuzer on computable subsequences without a convergence rate](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.5316)
