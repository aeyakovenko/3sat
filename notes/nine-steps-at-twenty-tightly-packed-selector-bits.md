# Nine instructions at twenty genuinely packed SAT selector bits

On the five-bit native RAM, **nine executed instructions are necessary and sufficient** for ten-variable unit-selector SAT on four completely occupied words. This remains true on the 19-selector subcatalog in which the tenth variable has an optional negative unit and its positive-unit selector is fixed zero. Arbitrary program length, registers, writes and indirect addresses are allowed. Every correct program has a satisfiable file requiring nine or more instructions among the 64 files specified below, even if rejection has no time limit.

The four words are $P,Q,M,N$, in that physical address order. At 20 bits the ten positive selectors are followed immediately by the ten negative selectors. No padding intervenes anywhere. At 19 independent selectors this arrangement is word-aligned by reserving the high bit of $Q$ as zero. The latter occupies 20 physical bits and differs from the tight 19-bit sign string. The formula has a fixed positive disjunction of the ten variables. It is satisfiable exactly when $P\mathbin{\&}M=Q\mathbin{\&}N=0$ and $(M,N)\ne(31,31)$. The ordinary nine-step algorithm tests the two overlaps and the exceptional pair. The scalar checker ran it on all 524,288 and 1,048,576 files of the two respective catalogs. ## Which extra inputs the lower bound needs

Use the common target set

$$
S=\{(p,q,31-p,31-q):4\le p,q\le 11\}.
$$

Changing an underlying nibble to 15 in any original word forces a conflict with its nonzero mate. All four originals must therefore be read before their first overwrite on these targets. The branch-count argument, store erasure and terminal reductions are those of the 17- and 18-selector theorem. Here both negative masks on the initial hard slice range over $16,\ldots,31$. Varying an underlying nibble within that half preserves unsigned and signed order. A last fresh negative word mutated to underlying 15 becomes physical 31. [Earlier five-bit proof and reductions](the-nine-step-clock-with-a-seventeenth-selector.md)

One cannot copy its two-comparison bound by forcing *both* new variables false on all the files. On that narrow slice let $j=Q\mathbin{\&}N$, $x=\operatorname{mem}[j]$, $y=31\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}M$. The two tests $Q<y$ and $x=y$ give an eight-instruction accepting path there. If $j=1$, then $x=Q<y$. If $j=2,3$, the result is an upper-half word, while $y\le15$. An address at least four returns zero, which contradicts $Q<y=x$. Hence acceptance forces $j=0$, $P=y$ and both pairs are disjoint. The exceptional negative masks would give $y=0$, also impossible. On the complementary targets this path accepts precisely $q<p$, giving **28 targets**. For the legal 19- and 20-selector file $(0,3,24,7)$, the code instead reads $j=3$ and obtains $x=7=y$. It accepts a real conflict. The narrow slice therefore gives the wrong per-leaf capacity.

Keep $P,Q<16$ and $M\ge16$ as before, but allow $N$ to span $0,\ldots,31$. The original 58,976 negative files with upper-half $N$ stay in the test. The lower-half values contribute exactly 58,975 files having an actual overlapping bit. The old exceptional nibble pattern at lower-half $N$ is satisfiable, because variable ten is free; it is omitted. This supplies **117,951 genuine negative files** on a 131,072-file subdomain of both catalogs. Every narrow-slice exclusion remains necessary for the larger catalogs. The additional negatives are required for the genuinely indirect two-ALU, two-comparison paths.

## Finite capacities

* Four comparisons leave only the four fresh reads. The high-band raw comparison test has 117 eligible guard masks and excludes all 2,016 unordered target pairs. A three-comparison leaf covers at most eight targets. The five one-ALU direct or single-indirect layouts exhaust 7,052 first tables and 35,721,228 guard pairs. The no-arithmetic reductions use the same high band.
* For two comparisons the direct and low-arithmetic reductions give capacities at most sixteen. In the two-ALU, four-fresh-load case, the expanded primary computation covers all 17,837 distinct first tables of fourteen genuinely indirect layouts. It retains 7,626 adequate first tables, 13,963,084 second-table representatives on the quick lanes and 20,449 candidate implementations. All 497,393 concrete final guard pairs that passed the quick test meet one of the physical negatives. The resulting cap is eighteen. An eight-target positive control was also evaluated over the full 1,048,576 files.
* If the first comparison occurs before instruction eight, repeat it on a one-comparison accepting leaf after dropping its fixed data-only tail. This path has the two-comparison capacity at most eighteen. With leaf bounds $(18,18,8,1)$ the marked-rejection tree covers at most 63 targets. If the first comparison occurs at instruction eight, every target must take the same immediately accepting outcome. [First-branch timing](../lean/SharedFirstComparisonAtDeadline.lean) · [Marked-tree arithmetic](../lean/EightStepMarkedCapacity.lean)
* To rule out a single last comparison covering all 64 targets, the one-store and read-only low-arithmetic cases run on the original narrow domain. An unprocessed last input word gives the single-fiber order or raw equality bounds as in the previous proof. The remaining eight guarded-store, sixteen terminal-read and fourteen terminal-arithmetic layouts exhaust 908,907,086 first-table/second-operation pairs. Their respective areas are 232,904,648, 331,116,730 and 344,885,708. No candidate covers all 64 targets. [Store erasure](../lean/FreshReadStoreErasure.lean)

An optional fifth bit in a new input word now gives a concrete *adjacent* equality of optimal clocks. In the variant in which this bit enables a new unconstrained disjunct, its zero restriction preserves the full 20-bit input memory, including address 4 and all scratch. The lower bound thus transfers to 21 bits in this convention, and the explicit nine-instruction program supplies the reverse inequality. For the negative-unit convention the nine-step upper is the established conclusion here.
