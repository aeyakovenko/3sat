# Adding an independent dot product costs exactly one AND per coordinate

Let $F(X)$ be any Boolean map, with any number of output bits. Give an unrestricted XOR–AND circuit disjoint inputs $X,U,V$ and require the output vector $(F(X),\sum_{i=1}^s U_iV_i)$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Its minimum number of binary AND gates is exactly
$$\operatorname{MC}(F)+s.$$
XOR, constants and fanout are free. Intermediate gates may have arbitrary Boolean degree. In particular, computing the four entries of one binary $2\times2$ matrix product together with an independent $s$-bit dot product costs exactly $7+s$ ANDs, for every $s\ge0$. Appending one fresh coordinate pair to the dot product increases the optimum by precisely one. [Independent finite check for the seven-AND base](../audits/mm2_kernel_check.py)

## A restriction that preserves every input to the first output

For a Boolean map $G(Y)$ let $D_{\mathrm{aff}}(G)$ be the minimum worst-case depth of a binary decision tree whose tests are parities of the input bits and whose leaf outputs are affine maps of $Y$. Each leaf's affine map need only agree on the inputs reaching it. Then
$$\operatorname{MC}(F(X),G(Y))\ \ge\ \operatorname{MC}(F)+D_{\mathrm{aff}}(G).$$
The roles of $F$ and $G$ can be exchanged. The task supplies them as separate outputs. The proof uses that its first output still equals $F(X)$ after a substitution in the second input.

Take an $M$-AND circuit. Write every AND operand and every final output as an affine combination of the original inputs and preceding AND outputs. At any stage let $Z$ denote the remaining free coordinates from $Y$. If some AND operand has a nonzero coefficient on a $Z$ coordinate, take the earliest such gate and choose one of its operands with this property. Every preceding gate is now a function solely of $X$, since its operands contain only $X$, constants and earlier such gates. Thus the chosen operand is $\ell(Z)+B(X)$ for a nonconstant linear form $\ell$ and an affine combination $B$ of $X$, constants and existing earlier gate outputs. The function $B(X)$ itself can be nonlinear.

Branch on the value $c\in\{0,1\}$ of the chosen operand. Solve $\ell(Z)=c+B(X)$ for one coordinate of $Z$ and substitute into the remaining circuit. The chosen AND becomes zero or its other operand, so it can be deleted. Substitution adds no AND: $B(X)$ uses only wires already available before the deleted gate. Substituting into earlier ANDs is unnecessary because none mention the eliminated coordinate. Both branches remain feasible for **every** $X$, with all uneliminated coordinates of $Y$ free. Along a fixed branch sequence, the original $Y$ has the form $LZ+b(X)$, where $L$ is injective and independent of $X$. Each branch removes exactly one free coordinate.

Stop when every remaining AND depends only on $X$ through its operands and earlier gates. After $t$ deletions at most $M-t$ ANDs remain. Fixing the remaining $Z$ to zero gives a circuit computing $F$ on its full domain, so $t\le M-\operatorname{MC}(F)$ on **every** leaf. Fix one $X=x_0$ throughout the whole tree. At each node the test above is then a parity of the free original $Y$ coordinates plus a fixed bit, which can be absorbed by exchanging the two branches. At each leaf all surviving ANDs are constants at $x_0$, and the output for $G$ is affine in the surviving coordinates of $Y$. This constructs the claimed tree of depth at most $M-\operatorname{MC}(F)$. The resource being bounded is its depth. Its size can grow exponentially with depth.

## Dot products, monomials and the limit for a second matrix block

For $G(U,V)=U\cdot V$, the polar form on $2s$ bits is $(u,v),(u',v')\mapsto u\cdot v'+u'\cdot v$ and is nondegenerate. If $G$ is affine on a coset with direction space $L$, polarization gives $L\subseteq L^\perp$, hence $\dim L\le s$. A path of $t$ parity tests reaches a coset of codimension at most $t$, so each nonempty affine-output leaf requires $t\ge s$. Querying the $s$ bits of $U$ attains depth $s$. The $s$ separate products and an XOR give the matching circuit upper bound. This also recovers the result for any independent scalar quadratic form of polar rank $2s$, using the standard affine normal form. Mirwald and Schnorr proved the scalar rank formula and additivity for disjoint scalar quadratic forms. The first component $F$ in the bound above can have arbitrary degree and any number of outputs. Weak normality is the established term for affinity on a large affine subspace. [Mirwald and Schnorr](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(92)90235-8) · [Boyar, Find and Peralta](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4957665/)

There is another exact successor. An affine-output parity tree of depth $t$ represents each output as the XOR over leaves of its affine label times its path indicator. That indicator is a product of at most $t$ affine tests or their complements. Thus every coordinate has Boolean algebraic degree at most $t+1$. For a fresh monomial on $r\ge1$ bits, the tree depth is at least $r-1$, and a chain of $r-1$ ANDs suffices. Appending this monomial as a separate output to any $F$ therefore costs exactly $r-1$ ANDs. The same argument works for any block whose AND count equals its maximum coordinate degree minus one. Both exact successors can be iterated on disjoint inputs.

For the full $d\times d$ binary product, $D_{\mathrm{aff}}$ is exactly $d^2$. Query all entries of $A$ to linearize $AB$. For the reverse inequality, the affine output at a leaf also gives the trace, a dot product of $d^2$ pairs in independently varying $A$ and $B$. Consequently the inequality adds four to an existing lower bound upon adjoining an independent full $2\times2$ product. Starting with seven yields $4r+3$ for $r\ge1$ independent such products. Determining whether every additional matrix block costs seven requires information beyond this decision-tree bound. These are circuit counts for separate Boolean outputs. No unrestricted SAT running-time inequality follows from them.
