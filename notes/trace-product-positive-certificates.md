# The cost of certifying a positive trace-product SAT verdict

Let $K=\mathbb F_q$, $q=2^m$, and $F(u,v,w)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}(uvw)$. On binary coordinates of $K^3$ an observation may return any quadratic polynomial, including affine and constant functions. A certificate is a sequence of truthful answers that forces the claimed bit on all inputs consistent with those answers. Questions may depend on previous answers. The argument applies to individual nondeterministic branches as well.

For **even $m\ge2$**, every input with $F=1$ needs exactly $m/2+1$ quadratic observations for a certificate. Some inputs with $F=0$ need only $m/2$. For even $m\ge4$ the latter are exactly the inputs with a zero field coordinate. ## Low-degree indicators supported on positive inputs

For $m\ge3$, every nonzero binary polynomial $P$ of degree at most $m$ supported on $\{F=1\}$ has relative Hamming weight at least $2/q$. This holds for any such polynomial before using the special form of a certificate.

First, no affine binary flat of dimension $2m-1$ lies inside $\{F=1\}$. Write a putative flat as $x_0+W$. At every $z\in W$ the alternating form $(s,t)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}(\det(z,s,t))$ vanishes on $W\times W$. This is the third derivative of $F$. For $z\ne0$ the radical on the full $3m$-space is $Kz$, since for $s\notin Kz$ the determinant ranges over $K$ as $t$ varies. The quotient by $Kz$ has a nondegenerate alternating form of binary dimension $2m$. An isotropic subspace there has dimension at most $m$. It follows that $\dim(W\cap Kz)\ge m-1$. If $W$ contained three $K$-independent vectors, these line intersections would span at least $3(m-1)>2m-1$ binary dimensions. Hence $W$ is contained in a $K$-plane. Two of the standard coordinate projections jointly identify this plane with $K^2$. If both projections of $W$ were proper binary subspaces of $K$, their joint image would have dimension at most $2m-2$. Thus one coordinate ranges over all of $K$ on $x_0+W$. At a point where it is zero we have $F=0$, a contradiction.

Second, on every affine binary flat $H=x_0+W$ of dimension $2m+2$, every quadratic $A$ satisfies
$$
             |\mathbb E_H(-1)^{F+A}| < 7/16 .
$$
Square the mean and average the signed means of the derivatives over shifts $z\in W$. For nonzero $z$, put $t_z=\dim(W\cap Kz)$. The image of $W$ in the symplectic quotient has dimension $2m+2-t_z$. Its orthogonal complement has dimension $t_z-2$. The polar rank of the derivative on $W$ is therefore at least $2(m+2-t_z)$. Translation does not affect this rank, and the derivative of $A$ is affine. A binary quadratic of polar rank $r$ has absolute bias at most $2^{-r/2}$, by squaring its bias and averaging its affine derivatives. Each nonzero shift therefore contributes at most $1/4$ in absolute value, or at most $1/8$ if $Kz\not\subset W$. The shifts with $Kz\subset W$ form a $K$-linear subspace of $W$, of binary dimension at most $2m$ since $m\ge3$. They make up at most one quarter of $W$. Correcting for the zero shift gives a squared bound $5/32+3/(4|W|)<(7/16)^2$. [Related calculation on a flat of dimension $2m+1$](quadratic-table-coefficient-cost.md#exact-stabilizer-fidelity-and-its-equality-cases)

Suppose $P\ne0$ has relative weight below $2/q$. The Kasami–Tokura normal-form theorem applied to $\mathrm{RM}(m,3m)$ gives two possibilities after an invertible affine change of coordinates. [Kasami and Tokura](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1970.1054545) · [Gopalan, Klivans and Zuckerman, Theorem 9](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~klivans/rm.pdf#page=6)

- The support is the symmetric difference of two codimension-$m$ flats. The normal form multiplies $m-\mu$ individual coordinates by the sum of two disjoint $\mu$-fold products, with $3\le\mu\le m$. Set the common and first-product coordinates to one and one second-product coordinate to zero. This gives a $(2m-1)$-flat inside the support.
- The support is the one-set of $D=\sum_{i=1}^{\mu} y_{2i-1}y_{2i}$ inside a $(2m+2)$-flat $H$ obtained by fixing $m-2$ other coordinates to one. For $\mu=1$ fix the first pair to one. For $\mu=2$ also fix one bit of the second pair to zero. Both supports contain a $(2m-1)$-flat. For $\mu\ge3$ the density of $D=1$ in $H$ is $(1-2^{-\mu})/2\ge7/16$. Write $f=(-1)^F$. If $F=1$ everywhere there, then $\mathbb E_H f(-1)^D-\mathbb E_H f=2\Pr_H[D=1]$. Each of the two means has magnitude strictly below $7/16$ by the derivative bound. This is impossible.

For the small even degree $m=2$, the positive set has 18 points out of 64. A nonzero quadratic supported there must have weight 16 by the same normal form. It must indicate a four-dimensional binary flat. For a direction space $W$ of dimension $2m$ on which the translated phase is constant, the isotropic quotient argument gives $\dim(W\cap Kz)\ge m$ for every nonzero $z\in W$, so $W$ is a $K$-plane. A coordinate projection of this plane is surjective and its affine translate has a point with $F=0$. Hence the proposed quadratic does not exist.

## From an indicator to a physical SAT certificate

Fix a transcript of $t\le m/2$ observations for even $m$. Its indicator is the product of the $t$ binary quadratic equality indicators, of degree at most $m$. As a real function it is an average of signed quadratic phases, obtained by expanding each equality indicator as $(1\pm(-1)^Q)/2$. Every quadratic phase has absolute correlation at most $\beta=(2q-1)/q^2$ with $f$. If the transcript forces one sign, its mass is $|\mathbb E[f1_{\rm transcript}]|\le\beta<2/q$. The preceding result excludes a nonempty positive transcript. Additional unused label bits cause no difficulty. Fix them in a slice meeting the transcript and apply the same bound there. [Exact quadratic correlation and its provenance](trace-product-quadratic-correlation.md#the-one-query-value-was-known)

For the upper bound, guess $u=a$ and test the guess two bits at a time. One quadratic checks that both bits of a pair equal their guesses. After these $m/2$ checks, $\operatorname{Tr}(avw)$ is a single quadratic query giving the sign. If $a=0$ there is no need for the final query to certify zero.

In the earlier past-conditioned native construction choose $m=2\lfloor h/6\rfloor$ for hidden word length $h$, using its first $3m$ bits. The even-$m$ result now applies at every sufficiently large supported stage, with $m=\Theta(\log\log N_j)$. A deterministic learner given all past words and verdicts may ask adaptive, genuine current SAT questions returning arbitrary quadratic functions of the hidden bits. The physical output formula tests $F=1$. By the journal's transcript theorem, every sufficiently late timely sound claim must be correct on every trial word consistent with its replies. Consequently a fixed uniformly sound learner using $N_j/(\log N_j)^C$ ordinary work for sufficiently large fixed $C$ cannot certify a positive verdict at such a stage with just $m/2$ questions. Under the hypothesis that SAT has an $O(N^k)$ deterministic solver, the same conclusion holds for uniformly sound nondeterministic learners on the clock $2^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}/(j+1)^B$ for sufficiently large fixed $B$ and $k=1,2,3$. Fix one accepting branch in applying the transcript theorem. There are infinitely many positive stages. Otherwise an eventually constant zero claim (with a finite initial cutoff) would be sound, contradicting the same theorem with no questions. The matching nondeterministic procedure above certifies every positive stage one query above the threshold. [Native transfer theorem and timing assumptions](learning-from-earlier-sat-verdicts.md)

An unrestricted lower bound would follow from producing a sound certificate with at most $m/2$ questions and within the specified nondeterministic work bound on infinitely many actual positive stages. An ordinary solver may pay to reconstruct the native run. Correctness and an $O(N^k)$ clock on the physical formulas have not yielded a certificate at the smaller native clock.
