# Correcting repeated variables in a Boolean arithmetization

**Question, 2026-11-23.** Suppose a small circuit computes the right decision bit on every Boolean input. Can one recover just enough of its formal multilinear extension to apply the Hamiltonian cycle lower bound? We derive a sufficient condition on individual monomials, give a nearly matching limit on correction by point queries, and test the condition on a linear-time RAM computation. The target throughout is ordinary SAT on bitstrings, even the open question of an O(n)-time algorithm with unrestricted workspace.

## A partial conversion that suffices for the low-degree part

Let P be the formal polynomial of a size-s arithmetic circuit in h variables over a field F. Its Boolean multilinearization ML(P) replaces every positive exponent in each monomial by one and adds like terms. For coordinate i define the linear operators

    IᵢP = (1−xᵢ)P|_{xᵢ=0} + xᵢP|_{xᵢ=1},       Dᵢ = Iᵢ − Id.

Operators at distinct coordinates commute. The product of all Iᵢ is ML. Expand ∏ᵢ(Id+Dᵢ) and keep only subsets of at most r distinct indices:

    Cᵣ(P) = Σ_{|S|≤r} (∏_{i∈S}Dᵢ)P.

**Claim.** If every monomial of P with support size at most d has at most r variables of exponent greater than one, then the components of total degree 0 through d of Cᵣ(P) and ML(P) coincide. There is no bound on the exponents themselves, the total degree of P, or the repeats in larger supports.

For a single monomial M, DᵢM is zero unless the exponent of xᵢ is at least two. Every surviving term of DᵢM has the same support as M, since xᵢᵉ is replaced by xᵢ−xᵢᵉ for e≥2. Thus a product of more than r distinct Dᵢ annihilates every monomial covered by the hypothesis. All terms produced from any other monomial have support size greater than d and hence degree greater than d. This proves the claim even if terms cancel in the field. With d=h it gives the entire multilinearization.

For a fixed S of size j, evaluating ∏Dᵢ requires at most 3ʲ copies of P with coordinates either unchanged, zero or one. Coefficient products and sums cost O((j+1)3ʲ) extra gates. Consequently Cᵣ has a circuit of size

    O((s+r+1) Σ_{j=0}^{min(r,h)} 3ʲ binom(h,j)).

Ordinary homogeneous extraction through degree d then costs an O((d+1)²) factor by truncated convolution. No circuit expansion into a monomial list is needed. One need not certify the support condition to construct the circuit, but it must be proved to deduce correctness. This operator expansion is related to the difference-operator construction of sparse-grid quadrature. General hardness of Boolean multilinearization and homogeneous extraction have explicit precedents. [Schillings and Schwab](https://www.sam.math.ethz.ch/sam_reports/reports_final/reports2012/2012-37_fp.pdf) · [Hrubeš, Theorem 1 and Lemma 4](https://users.math.cas.cz/~hrubes/PDFs/Mult.pdf)

## A consequence for every small representative of an easy predicate

Use h=m² and let P agree on the cube with the test that the selected edges *themselves* form one directed Hamiltonian cycle. The degree-m part of ML(P) is HCₘ. Any size-m^{O(1)} representative P therefore has a nonzero monomial of support size at most m that repeats Ω((log m)^{1/3}) distinct variables, for every sufficiently large m at fixed characteristic. Indeed, if r is the maximum number of repeats on those supports, the conversion gives HCₘ a circuit of size exp(O((r+1)log m)). The published lower bound is exp(Ω((log m)^{4/3})). This argument concerns all small representatives, including representations with high formal degree and cancellations. [Cycle test and extraction](sat-extension.md) · [Arithmetic lower bound](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00954v1)

This narrows the desired transfer for SAT. On the signed-input restriction of [the SAT encoding](sat-extension.md), a hypothetical fast solver would already give a small representative of this easy cycle predicate. For example a polynomial-time word RAM unrolls to polynomial-size Boolean circuits even with a naive multiplexer over its polynomially many touched locations indexed by address comparisons, and Boolean gates arithmetize on the cube. To contradict the arithmetic bound by the conversion above one would need a SAT-specific reason to produce a representative with fewer repeats **only on the supports of size at most m**. The algorithm need not be multilinear elsewhere.

**First test of that premise.** A deterministic RAM decides the cycle predicate by reading each matrix bit exactly once, recording a successor and counting indegrees, then following successors in writable memory. Its time is O(m²), linear in the number of input bits. Nonetheless every polynomial-size arithmetization correct for this predicate has the repeated-variable obstruction just proved. Limiting rereads of external input therefore cannot establish the desired premise. For a polynomial-size deterministic acyclic branching program that queries input variables directly, bounding repeats on *every syntactic accepting path* would suffice: its usual weighted-path polynomial has products of xᵢ and 1−xᵢ, and expanding a path cannot repeat a variable queried only once. An unrestricted RAM's writable memory can encode exponentially many branching states. Restricted branching programs with extra queries on selected variables have their own lower-bound literature. [Woelfel, A Lower Bound Technique for Restricted Branching Programs and Applications](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2001/101/)

**Retry using symmetry.** Over the rationals, take any small P for the cycle predicate. Replace every input by its square, then average the resulting polynomial over simultaneous permutations of vertex names. The Boolean test is invariant, so this average still represents it. Every exponent that occurs is even. Since multilinearization preserves monomial supports, each cycle monomial in HCₘ must receive a nonzero contribution from a support where all m variables repeat. Taking the average can be expensive. Even granting it for free, relabeling alone supplies no universal guarantee that small supports become free of repeated variables. This counterexample is about the proposed operation, not a lower bound against better ways to exploit the symmetry of SAT.

## Can all exceptions be confined to a short list of supports?

**Support-count theorem.** Fix a field and a constant B. There are positive η, δ and γ such that the following holds for every sufficiently large m. Put r=⌊η(log₂ m)^{1/3}⌋ and ℓ=⌊δ(log₂ m)^{4/3}⌋. For every specified set J of ℓ matrix rows, every circuit of size at most mᴮ computing the Boolean cycle predicate on the cube has at least

    m^{γ(log₂ m)^{4/3}}

distinct monomial supports of size at most m with all three properties below. Here the supports belong to the circuit's formal output polynomial P over the field.

1. The support meets every row in J.
2. Its directed graph of matrix entries has indegree and outdegree at most three.
3. There is a nonzero monomial on that support in which more than r distinct variables have exponent at least two.

Call a support with property 3 exceptional. Merely counting cycle monomials would not establish property 3. The proof first builds a sparse graph that retains a smaller cycle polynomial. Then it exploits the Boolean behavior on matrices with a zero row to make each exceptional support harder to retain under a random relabeling.

**A directed graph on m vertices that projects to HCₖ.** Take k=⌊√m/3⌋ and start with the complete loopless digraph on k vertices. Give arc (i,j) the label Xᵢⱼ. At a vertex with indegree d≥4, partition its d incoming arcs into two nearly equal groups. Replace the vertex by a,b,c, with a↔b, a→c, b→c. The two groups enter a and b respectively, and all outgoing arcs depart from c. In any spanning cycle the gadget has exactly one exit and hence one entrance. For either entrance the order through the three vertices is forced. This gives a weight-preserving bijection of spanning cycles if new internal arcs have weight one and all redirected arcs retain their labels. The sum over vertices of max(0,indegree−3) decreases by one at each split, since each group has size at least two. Outdegree excess stays unchanged. Reverse the split to reduce outdegree, preserving the achieved indegree bound. There are at most 4k² vertices after both stages. To reach exactly m, replace a vertex by a directed path, incoming arcs to its start and outgoing arcs from its end. Every edge on that path is forced. We now have a loopless directed graph G on m vertices with maximum indegree and outdegree three whose weighted cycle polynomial projects to HCₖ. These split gadgets and preservation of the number of cycles are established constructions. [Ejov, Haythorpe and Rossomakhine, §4](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/62/ajc_v62_p045.pdf#page=8)

**Force the exceptional supports to be large.** Let ρⱼ set every variable in row j to zero. Form Q=∏_{j∈J}(Id−ρⱼ)P. On a monomial this operation keeps its coefficient unchanged if its support meets every chosen row, and deletes it otherwise. On a Boolean input, each nonempty choice of zeroed rows makes a Hamiltonian cycle impossible, so every such evaluation of P is zero. Thus Q computes the same Boolean predicate, in all characteristics. Inclusion–exclusion implements Q with O(2^ℓ(s+1)) gates, regardless of its formal degree. It is important here that the filter creates no new monomials. Multiplying by a row-coverage predicate need not have that property.

**Survival probability.** Place G on the m matrix indices by a uniformly random permutation of its vertex labels. Set all disallowed edge variables to zero. If a support survives, it has indegree and outdegree at most three. Each surviving support of Q also has at least ℓ distinct edges. Choose ℓ of its edges in advance. For any fixed candidate of maximum indegree and outdegree three containing those edges, write v for the number of their incident vertices and a for their number of weak components. Loops cannot survive. Then ℓ≤3v and a≤v/2, so v−a≥ℓ/6. In a spanning forest of their undirected graph there are at most m choices for each component root and at most six choices for each following vertex given its parent. There are (m)ᵥ=m(m−1)⋯(m−v+1) possible injective placements. Since v≤2ℓ=o(√m), the chance that all selected edges survive is at most

    mᵃ 6^{v−a} / (m)ᵥ ≤ 2(6/m)^{ℓ/6}.

If the number of supports in the theorem were smaller than the claimed bound with γ sufficiently small relative to δ, a union bound would give a labeling of G in which none of them survives. Supports with larger degrees have survival probability zero. Setting variables to zero never shrinks a surviving monomial's support.

**Contradiction.** On that graph every surviving monomial of Q of support size ≤m has at most r repeated variables. The truncated correction followed by ordinary degree-m extraction therefore computes the cycle polynomial on G. Setting the internal gadget variables to one now gives HCₖ. The compiled circuit size has base-two logarithm at most

    ℓ + (2+o(1))r log₂ m + O_B(log m).

Here one may use the looser bound h≤m² for the number of variables in the correction, and truncated homogeneous extraction costs a polynomial factor. The arithmetic lower bound for HCₖ has base-two logarithm Ω((log m)^{4/3}). Choose δ and η small enough, then choose γ. This contradiction establishes the support count. The order of substitutions matters. Setting internal edges to one before the correction could shrink large supports into the degree-m range.

A weaker preliminary restriction simply partitions the vertices into k paths and allows every end-to-start edge. An exceptional support can then concentrate on a complete bipartite subgraph of the allowed arcs. The degree-three gadget avoids that concentration. The row filter further increases the number of edges that must survive from r+1 to ℓ. Random zero restrictions have precedent in restricted arithmetic circuit lower bounds, and functional lower bounds for bounded-depth representations have their own literature. We are applying the published general arithmetic lower bound to support structure. The Boolean predicate here already has a linear-time RAM algorithm. [Kumar and Saraf](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.5978) · [Forbes, Kumar and Saptharishi](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2016.33)

**Transfer to a SAT solver's output representation.** Choose the sign-input face of the CNF encoding so that live encoding bits are xₑ without any substitution by 1−xₑ. If the sign convention pins the logical wire to the complement of the encoding bit, have the fixed verifier test the cycle predicate on the complements of its logical wires. Now a small arithmetic circuit agreeing with the decision-SAT truth table produces a small representative P for the cycle test simply by fixing the other input bits to constants. The theorem applies to its surviving supports on this face. Producing very many expanded monomials costs little by itself. The polynomial ∏ᵢ(1+xᵢ²), for example, has all squarefree supports with doubled exponents in O(h) gates. A time proof must identify a property of a fast solver that constrains the particular support structure forced here. [Sign-restriction argument](sat-extension.md)

## How much can an adaptive evaluation oracle improve the conversion?

Fix z with every zᵢ outside {0,1}. Let L(P)=ML(P)(z) and M=Σ_{j≤r}binom(h,j), summing from zero through min(r,h). If P is supplied only by evaluations at points of the same field, **every exact deterministic algorithm needs at least M queries on the zero transcript** for the promise that every monomial repeats at most r distinct variables. This applies to adaptive queries and arbitrary computation on their values.

For S of size at most r put u_S=∏_{i∈S}(xᵢ−zᵢ). The M functions are linearly independent, e.g. by their leading squarefree monomials. The pairing (p,q)↦L(pq) is nonsingular on their span. Its matrix in this basis is diagonal: L factors over disjoint variable sets, a lone centered factor has weighted mean zero, and a squared one has weighted mean zᵢ(1−zᵢ)≠0. These are algebraic weights and need no ordering of the field. If fewer than M points were queried on input zero, some nonzero p in this span would vanish at all of them. Select q=u_T with L(pq)≠0. The input pq also returns zero at each queried point and meets the promise. In each product of a monomial of p and a monomial of q, only coordinates in their intersection can repeat, at most r. Adaptive replay now contradicts the answer on zero. The same argument applies to a sound zero-certificate whose verifier only reads such point evaluations.

The lower bound has a Boolean-valued variant. Work over E=GF(2ᵏ), k≥2, with z outside the Boolean domain in every coordinate. Seek p in the binary span of the M ordinary squarefree monomials of degree at most r. Each E-valued zero condition imposes at most k binary linear conditions, so k times the number of queries must reach M. Otherwise choose a nonzero binary p vanishing at all the queries. By the same nonsingular pairing over E there is an ordinary squarefree monomial q of degree at most r with L(pq)≠0. Now pq takes only Boolean values on the entire cube. At fixed k and r the lower bound has order hʳ, matching the order of the upper evaluation count. Circuit descriptions or access to their gates can reveal more than evaluations. The lower bound supplies no running-time lower bound on unrestricted SAT.
