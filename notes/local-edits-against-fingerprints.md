# How many edits can escape adaptive fingerprints?

Fix any list of $N$ distinct records. A verifier can inspect individual records and can ask for commutative aggregates of the whole input. On each query it chooses a finite commutative monoid $G$ and a map $f$ from record values to $G$, and receives $\prod_i f(x_i)$. Both choices may depend on the complete preceding transcript, the original reference list if supplied, and messages from a prover. The aggregate uses the same map on each occurrence. Charge $\log_2|G|$ bits, including when the response delivered to the verifier is a single predicate of a larger monoid state. A verifier can compute freely on its transcript. Its entire access to the candidate input must be through these operations. Consider an accepting execution on a distinct reference list $x$, with at most $P$ different inspected positions and at most $b$ aggregate bits.

* If $\binom{N-P}{h}>2^b$, some duplicate-containing list within $h$ row edits produces the identical transcript.
* With private randomness, suppose the honest prover achieves acceptance probability $c$ on $x$, with the above budgets on accepting paths. Put $K=\binom{N-P}{h}$, $H=\binom Nh>1$, and $D=\lfloor2^b\rfloor$. Write $K=Dd+r$, $0\le r<D$. Some fixed duplicate-containing list within $h$ edits admits a prover with acceptance probability at least
  $$
    c\,\frac{D d(d-1)+2dr}{H(H-1)}.
  $$
  In particular for $P=0$ and $H\gg 2^b$ the lower bound approaches $c/2^b$ for integer $b$. This is a statement about the soundness error of the verification model. A verifier using a few random fingerprints can have small error.

## Equal subset fingerprints on one transcript

Freeze a true execution. Choose exactly $N-P$ untouched positions, discarding extra eligible ones if necessary. Each $h$-subset has a signature consisting of the aggregates on just its records, in each monoid selected on this path. There are at most $D$ signatures. For equally sized position sets $A\ne B$ with equal signatures, replace the records in $A\setminus B$ by copies of the records in $B\setminus A$, paired in increasing position order. This rule is independent of the transcript. Every probed position is unchanged. In each monoid the original whole-list product factors as the product on the complement of $A$ times the product on $A$. The altered list instead contributes the same complement and the product on $B$. Thus each response along the original path persists, even with noncancellative multiplication. The altered list contains a duplicate because the copied records also occur outside $A$.

If the bucket sizes are $k_j$, there are $\sum_j k_j(k_j-1)$ ordered, distinct colliding pairs. This is minimized at balanced sizes: transferring a member from a bucket of size $u$ to one of size $v\le u-2$ decreases the sum by $2(u-v-1)$. The stated numerator follows. For a randomized verifier, sample an ordered pair of different $h$-subsets uniformly from *all* $H(H-1)$ possibilities independently of the coins. On each accepting true path at least the stated numerator of pairs reproduce it. A prover on the corrupted input can imitate its honest strategy on $x$ using only its observable transcript. Averaging gives a fixed pair with the stated acceptance probability; it need not depend on the private tape. The same reasoning permits randomized honest strategies.

The estimate at $P=0$ is close to attainable even if the reference list is public. Give each value a uniformly random independent $b$-bit tag and compare the candidate XOR with the reference XOR. On any length-$N$ candidate containing a duplicate, some distinct reference record is absent. Its independent tag makes the discrepancy uniform. This is an information-theoretic comparison allowing an arbitrary random tag table. For robustness to fewer than $h+1$ replacements without randomness, encode the record alphabet injectively in a finite field $F$. Evaluate the multiset product $\prod_i(\alpha-x_i)$ at an element of degree $\max(2,h)$ over $F$. Cancel common factors in the two candidate products. For $e\le h$ remaining factors on either side their monic polynomials have difference of degree at most $e-1$. A nonzero such difference cannot vanish at $\alpha$. Thus one field value of $\max(2,h)\log_2 |F|$ bits distinguishes unequal multisets in this radius, including every duplicate neighbor of a distinct reference. This checks equality to the reference multiset. Characteristic-polynomial reconciliation and randomized multiset hashing have classical antecedents. [Minsky, Trachtenberg and Zippel](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.2003.815784) · [Wegman and Carter](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0022000081900337)

## When a query includes the timestamp

For bit-valued memory replies there is an analogous result allowing the tag to depend on the full timestamp. Fix **any** true vector $x\in\{0,1\}^N$. A query can now return the XOR of position-specific functions $f_i(y_i)$ in a binary vector space. Each $f_i$ may depend arbitrarily on $x$ and on the preceding transcript. Count its vector dimension in the budget $b$. Candidate access also allows at most $P$ direct probes as above. For any fixed path the change on flipping a mask $A$ is the XOR of the columns $f_i(0)\oplus f_i(1)$ for $i\in A$, concatenated across the path's queries. Thus two equal mask signatures yield the same full responses on $x$ and on $x\oplus 1_{A\triangle B}$. The mask $A\triangle B$ is nonempty if $A\ne B$.

Taking masks of *all* weights at most $h$ gives exactly the previous deterministic criterion and private-coin probability bound with $K=\sum_{j=0}^h\binom{N-P}j$ and $H=\sum_{j=0}^h\binom Nj$. The adversarial candidate differs in at most $2h$ positions. This covers adaptive tests of equality between genuine and asserted binary load replies with full per-event tags, even if the verifier already knows the honest vector. A trusted value-dependent test of the individual replies or a position-dependent nonlinear aggregate falls outside these query hypotheses.

For comparison, choose $N$ distinct nonzero labels $a_i$ in $F=\mathbb F_{2^w}$, $2^w>N$. The $h$ fixed XOR aggregates $\sum_i y_i a_i^j$ for odd $j=1,3,\ldots,2h-1$ detect every nonempty edit of weight at most $2h$. Equal odd syndromes imply equal syndromes at all exponents $1,\ldots,2h$ by squaring. For changes at $e\le2h$ distinct labels, the matrix with rows of exponents $1,\ldots,e$ is a Vandermonde matrix multiplied by invertible column scalars. Its product with the all-ones column cannot vanish. This familiar binary BCH construction spends $hw$ bits, close to the necessary ball-volume bound for small $h$. For two arbitrary words in a larger alphabet, the squaring step would also square their difference coefficients. The binary-reply hypothesis is used here. [Hocquenghem, original construction](https://db.aconit.org/dbmedia_0/pdf_8/8514.pdf) · [Bose and Ray-Chaudhuri](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0019995860902874)

## Consequence for the SAT experiment

In the journal's [dense 3-CNF encoding](matrix-list-unit-sat.md), a distinct list maps to an unsatisfiable formula. Every edited list above maps to a satisfiable formula with at most $2h$ changed variable-name fields in the fixed layout, in the width-at-most-three encoding, since editing one row changes at most two fields. Padding the two varying binary clauses per row by pairs of exact-three clauses with a fresh variable duplicates those occurrences and gives at most $4h$ changed fields. The statements concern procedures whose access to the variable input is through the declared probes and aggregates of its list records. A general SAT solver can choose different operations. The result also quantifies the [earlier memory-fingerprint example](public-memory-fingerprints.md): a prover that supplies nearly correct timestamped binary replies can pass every test along a short deterministic XOR transcript with only a low-weight edit. Additional constraints checking the proposed CPU computation can use information beyond its memory-reply fingerprints.

These estimates identify which local corruptions a fingerprint proof must detect and a binary syndrome that detects them at nearly the counting cost. To use the positive result for the unrestricted time bound one would still need a short, sound certificate bounding the discrepancy of an actual proposed RAM history, as well as a fast way to certify its syndromes on the true run. I have not established either property for the priority computation.
