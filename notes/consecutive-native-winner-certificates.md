# Two consecutive native awards with both source identities disclosed

In the [past-conditioned native family](learning-from-earlier-sat-verdicts.md), the output at date $i+1$ can depend on the entire outcome of date $i$, including its winning program. Can this dependence certify a forbidden pair of words without evaluating either word? Consider a verifier at date $i$ given the genuine history through $i-1$ and, in advance, both indices $e,f$ promised to be the **actual consecutive winners**. Require soundness only when both awards occur as promised. It can certify that the pair $(a_i,a_{i+1})$ differs from a proposed pair $(u,v)$ of its choosing, or abstain. Every accepted nondeterministic branch under the promise must be sound. More generally it can ask truthful quadratic queries about the word pair and assert a fixed polynomial-time evaluable predicate of it. It never gets either word merely from the history supplied at the start.

Fix polynomial-in-$i$ computable target words $p_i,q_{i+1}$ of the respective lengths. They can both have positive cubic trace, or be two different words with zero trace when testing simultaneous failures of positive pins. For even field degree on all late dates use $m_i=2\lfloor h_i/6\rfloor$ and ignore unused bits. Assume deterministic unrestricted $O(N^k)$ SAT for $k=1,2,3$. **For every such sound verifier there are infinitely many pairs of consecutive genuine awards, with the prescribed words and both actual winners known to the verifier, at which it cannot certify even one excluded pair in $2^{\lfloor i/k\rfloor}/(i+1)^B$ steps without further replies**, for sufficiently large fixed $B$. With current queries, every timely claim on these pairs must hold for all trial word pairs consistent with its replies. For deterministic verifiers the statement at $2^i/(i+1)^B$ is unconditional. The two ranks are not interchangeable: the second program reads the identity of the first and reproduces the same deterministic choice of a pair.

## Programs for the first and second dates

Use two disjoint kinds of tagged ordinary word programs. For each terminal tag let $f$ be the rank of its terminal program, and assign it infinitely many potential first programs identified by that tag and a second tag. Each program recovers the relevant indices by the fixed-template self-application and finite active-list scan already available in the native interpreter. Number first programs by their actual ranks and select the first $f+1$ with rank strictly greater than $f$. Call these the partners of $f$, and let $E_f$ be their largest rank. A program can check whether the selection is complete by scanning the active prefix; before completion all its cohort members idle. There are infinitely many terminal tags, each with its own disjoint cohort. [Self-indexed native programs](synchronized-priority-trials.md)

For each date, genuine start history and proposed consecutive ranks $(e,f)$, define one deterministic choice. Search for a trial pair $(u,v)$ and a bounded verifier branch making a claim false on that trial with those winner identities. Compute any queried replies from $(u,v)$, leave the publicly generated physical formula words intact, and give the verifier only the genuine past available at the start. If the search succeeds choose the lexicographically first such pair. If it fails choose $(p_i,q_{i+1})$. In the exclusion-only case a falsifying trial is simply the exact pair the branch claims to exclude.

The bounded source guesses two words, whose combined length is $O(\log(i+2))$, and the verifier choices. One [packed bounded-acceptance formula](packed-band-transfer.md) tests existence and a logarithmic number of prefix queries recovers the chosen pair by ordinary deterministic SAT. The full computation costs $T^k(i+1)^{O(1)}+\operatorname{poly}(i)$ for the stated start clock $T$. The history, ranks and prefix are explicit short parameters. For a deterministic verifier simply enumerate the polynomially many trial pairs. Use a fixed instruction body across all tags, and emit only after obtaining a complete result. As in the [one-winner construction](winner-informed-positive-stages.md), the bounded interpretation and active-list work have uniform polynomial overhead in $i$ when the involved ranks are at most $i$. The exponents can give the whole search cost $O(2^i/(i+1)^2)$ in interpreter ticks. Searching the same pair in the next stage uses the *old* word width, clock, rank parameters and history prefix. The one-stage width change costs constant work per primitive fixed-word operation. Public startup and formula generation remain within the fixed overhead.

Choose a public common start $J_f$ beyond which the cohort is all active, $E_f+1\le\lfloor\log_2(i+2)\rfloor$ and the search, index scans and emission fit the individual budgets for every rank in the cohort. It suffices to use the first stage satisfying the two monotone numerical conditions and a fixed sufficiently large lower cutoff for the uniform instruction body. Inert early dates produce no report. At every subsequent eligible stage $i$, each unretired partner $e$ offers the first word of the selected pair for $(e,f)$ at that date. At date $i+1$ the terminal $f$ offers only if its genuine preceding winner was a selected partner $e$ at an eligible date. It recomputes the original choice and offers its second word. Nothing requires guessing the second date's history in the first computation. The later program has received precisely what is needed to reproduce the earlier inputs.

Whenever a partner $e$ wins and $f$ wins immediately afterwards, the actual two words are the deterministic choice for that pair of ranks. A success of the false-claim search would therefore make that verifier branch genuinely unsound with **both** promised source identities correct. Every such double win must use the fallback, and on it the search found no falsifying trial. In particular the restriction holds on every reachable counterfactual word-pair transcript at that date with those rank parameters.

## Why the two selected sources genuinely win in succession

Every terminal $f$ actually gets such a double win. Until all $f+1$ partners have retired, some eligible partner of rank at most $E_f$ offers a word every stage. An award must go to rank at most $E_f$, which retires permanently. Hence all partners win at distinct dates between $J_f$ and $J_f+E_f$, inclusive: otherwise during $E_f+1$ dates all ranks at most $E_f$ other than an unretired partner would have had to supply distinct winners.

If $f$ has not yet won, it offers after each partner award. A failure then requires a distinct winning rank strictly smaller than $f$. There are only $f$ such ranks but $f+1$ partner awards. Thus by $J_f+E_f+1$ the terminal has won, and its own emission rule ensures the preceding winner was one of its partners. This uses the *actual* first-stage history to trigger the second rank. The count $f+1$ cannot be reduced on retirement grounds alone: in an abstract offer schedule with at most $f$ partners all ranked above $f$, assign a fresh rank below $f$ to offer immediately after each partner wins. Those stages all preempt the terminal. Distinct terminal ranks yield infinitely many genuine pairs. A finite collection of verifiers can be tested in one deterministic pair search; any chosen false witness suffices.

The first winner can retire without removing every possible source of the second word. Here a different, lower-index source actually computes its response conditional on the first winner and returns a prescribed word on a genuine pair. Supplying both winning indices in advance does not itself yield a short certificate for an arbitrary uniformly sound verifier on these pairs. The choice of cohorts can depend on the verifier and the assumed SAT program. No trusted future index is available for free in the original two-date proof request, and no ordinary two-date verifier meeting that request was obtained. ## Every successful adjacent-disjunction proof spends a different small rank

Now use a start verifier $D$ without winner metadata or current-word replies, receiving the true earlier history. It certifies a claim about the two words on adjacent dates $i,i+1$. Require soundness on every actual accepted branch. For instance, the claim can be that one of the two actual positive cubic pins is satisfiable, or simply that an explicitly named pair of words cannot occur together. Every claim of either of these kinds has some falsifying pair of *trial* words at late stages. The proof below also applies to other claims with a timely trial-evaluation algorithm, counting the starts where at least one timely accepted claim is falsifiable.

Fix one terminal and its $f+1$ partners as above but change the emission rule for all of them. **If the false-claim search is negative, emit nothing.** An unretired partner offers only on a completed positive search, and the terminal offers only after a genuine partner win, using exactly the selected second word from the same search. The search no longer needs to depend on the partner identity. Gate all programs so that none ever offers before the common start $J_f$ and every search fits its individual budget thereafter. Such a gate can use a fixed numerical startup bound and the stage tests above. The numerical bound can be treated as literal data in the uniform instruction body, to avoid changing the interpreter estimate when installing it. Given explicit compiler, simulation and SAT runtime bounds, $J_f$ can be specified from these and activation, without knowing how late any other rank will win. A bare big-O hypothesis promises a sufficiently large runtime coefficient; it does not supply that coefficient as input.

The terminal can never win: if it did, the two actual consecutive words would be the falsifying witnesses for an accepted claim. For every genuine partner win at date $i$, the terminal is therefore live, reached or preempted at $i+1$, and has a complete eligible offer there. A smaller rank must win $i+1$. Distinct partner wins give distinct successor dates and distinct preempting ranks. Consequently **at most $f$ of the $f+1$ partners ever retire**. In particular there is a live partner at every date, including the ones where $D$ issues a proof. This conclusion requires no bound on how long another reporter can delay its first award.

For $i\ge J_f$, any timely accepted falsifiable claim makes every live partner offer. Hence the *actual* current winner has index at most $E_f$, whether or not it is one of the partners. These winning indices are all distinct, each rank having a single lifetime award. At least two indices in $0,\ldots,E_f$ are never winners: the terminal and one partner. There are **at most $E_f-1$ such proof starts** and no such start can be a default or an override by a higher-index source. After observing a true history prefix, one can subtract from the $E_f+1$ places every already retired rank in the prefix and still reserve the two never-winning places. This count concerns existence of a verified branch, even if no algorithm finds those branches online.

Thus a timing contradiction on adjacent dates would follow from exhibiting $E_f$ distinct successful starts beyond the resource-controlled gate for the self-tested verifier and reference SAT solver. This is a finite alternative to the unbounded-start hypothesis in the earlier disjunction reduction. Both the rank bound and the gate depend on the verifier and the reference implementation; there is no universal numerical threshold asserted for all programs. An unconditionally sound deterministic start rule has the same charge after replacing the SAT search by explicit trial-pair enumeration at the subnative clock. This accounts for how the scheduler can defeat a proposed correlated pair: a preemptor at the second date retires for good, and at least one fresh first partner survives all such preemptions.

The number $E_f-1$ is sharp if only retirement, these trigger rules and the truth of the adjacent positive disjunctions are used. In an abstract schedule, issue the positive disjunction at every date of a block. Give every partner the zero first word of a falsifying trial, and give the other winning ranks positive words. Let the first $f$ partners win in increasing order, letting the distinct ranks $0,\ldots,f-1$ preempt the terminal on the respective next dates. All unrequested outside ranks idle during these $2f$ dates. Only partner $E_f$ now remains. On successive dates let every still-unused outside rank below $E_f$ except the terminal win once, preempting $E_f$. This uses all ranks except $f$ and $E_f$, for $E_f-1$ dates total. Every zero is followed by a positive, the last word in a nonempty block is positive, and all the asserted disjunctions are true. For $f=0$ the alternating portion is empty. Improving the finite count would require additional restrictions on the real reports or the proof computations.
