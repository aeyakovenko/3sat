# Adaptive entries of a binary matrix power with an arbitrarily long exponent

Let $A$ be an explicitly supplied $d$-square binary matrix, $d\ge1$, and $k$ a nonnegative exponent given in $b$ bits. A Boolean controller makes $q$ successive queries about $A^k$ over $\mathbb F_2$. It may choose each entry from all previous answers. It can even submit two $d$-bit vectors $u,v$ and ask for $u^{\mathsf T}A^kv$. Let $s$ count the bounded-fan-in Boolean gates and wire uses of its unrolled address, control and verdict computation; include the bits it receives from any other input to that computation. For each coefficient field of characteristic two the resulting decision bit, or the graph of the whole answer transcript, has an exact binary-wire trivalent tensor network of volume
$$
                          \widetilde O(d^2+b+qd+s).
$$
Here the tilde hides powers of $\log(d+b+q+s+2)$. Thus $d$ adaptive queries about an arbitrarily distant iterate still cost essentially the number of input bits when the controller fits that budget. The construction routes all its input-dependent matrix lookups together.

This addresses a possible amplification of the [one-master memory tradeoff](one-master-for-many-ram-programs.md). A proof through exact network volume would have to use another family, field or query regime to beat this upper bound. The contraction may have many internal witnesses that cancel. A deterministic SAT machine deciding an ordinary existential formula has no corresponding parity operation. The result supplies neither a RAM algorithm of this cost nor an unrestricted RAM lower bound.

## One shared characteristic polynomial

Put $D(z)=\det(I-zA)$ and $\chi(x)=x^dD(1/x)$. Both have binary coefficients and $D(0)=1$. We first build the exact graph of all $d+1$ coefficients of $D$ jointly. For a proposed vector $c$, in characteristic two the graph value is
$$
                 \prod_{j=0}^d\left(1+c_j+[z^j]D(z)\right).
$$
At each factor choose the constant, the proposed bit, or a single monomial term of the determinant of the indicated degree. A determinant choice is a permutation on the $d$ columns with one label for each row. A matrix label requests the input entry $A_{ih}$ at that permutation position and contributes one to the degree. An identity label requires $i=h$ and contributes zero to the degree. Require exactly $j$ matrix labels. Distinct choices at a diagonal entry are counted even when the matrix entry is one. Permutation signs disappear in characteristic two.

Verify the permutation in each chosen factor by sorting its $d$ column indices. The other branches have one fixed dummy assignment, including every unused selector and address. Across all factors there are at most $d(d+1)$ requests to the same external table $A$. Initialize one record per external cell and verify the requests by a joint sort and equality of consecutive replies with the same address. The claimed value at every active request is then uniquely determined. A deterministic sorting circuit and its copy gates have one internal extension per input. The result costs $\widetilde O(d^2)$ sites. It has value zero on every wrong coefficient vector and one on the right vector. Subsequent networks can condition on these internal bits and sum over them. The bits and table records are copied with explicitly charged bounded-degree trees. [Batched lookup and exact graph conventions](matching-characteristic-networks-for-binary-products.md)

## Reducing an arbitrarily long exponent

For each $1\le j\le d$ compute
$$g_j=\deg\gcd(\chi,x^{2^j}-x).$$
The indicated polynomial need only be kept modulo $\chi$. Start at $x$ and square the previous residue modulo $\chi$ once per $j$. Every monic irreducible over $\mathbb F_2$ of degree $e$ divides $x^{2^j}-x$ exactly when $e\mid j$. Its roots are simple there. Thus
$$
 h_j=g_j-\sum_{e\mid j,\ e<j}h_e
$$
is $j$ times the number of distinct degree-$j$ irreducibles of $\chi$. This also works when $\chi$ has repeated factors or a zero root. Form the positive integer
$$M=2^{\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil}\prod_{j:h_j>0}(2^j-1).$$
Its binary length is $O(d)$ because $\sum_{h_j>0}j\le d$. Each nonzero root of $\chi$ has multiplicative order dividing the odd part of $M$. The power of two in $M$ then gives each such root multiplicity at least $d$ in $x^M-1$. All zero roots with multiplicity are covered by $x^d$. It follows that $\chi\mid x^d(x^M-1)$ and Cayley–Hamilton gives $A^{d+M}=A^d$. Replace $k\ge d$ by $k'=d+((k-d)\bmod M)$; use $k'=k$ otherwise. This single $O(d)$-bit exponent works for all queries. [Rabin on finite-field polynomials](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0209024)

The calculation fits $\widetilde O(d^2+b)$ bit gates. Polynomial multiplication over $\mathbb F_2$ has quasilinear bit complexity. A reduction modulo a known monic polynomial can also be verified by unique quotient and remainder with degree bounds and one multiplication. To verify a monic gcd $G$ of $p=\chi$ and a given residue $v$, require $p=Ga$, $v=Gc$ and polynomials $U,V$ with $Uc+1=Va$, $\deg U<\deg a$. If $a=1$ set $U=0,V=1$. Otherwise coprimality of $a,c$ gives exactly one inverse $U$ modulo $a$ and then exactly one $V$. This verifies each gcd with $O(d)$ coefficient bits and a fixed number of polynomial products and monic or degree checks. Allocate $d$ such checks. Computing all degree totals, constructing the product for $M$ with its growing $O(d)$-bit accumulator, and checking a unique integer quotient and remainder for the reduction of $k$ cost $\widetilde O(d^2+b)$ as well. Standard fast multiplication on tapes can be compiled to bounded-fan-in circuits at logarithmic overhead. [Harvey, van der Hoeven and Lecerf on finite-field polynomial multiplication](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3361) · [Harvey and van der Hoeven on integer multiplication](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2021.193.2.4) · [Pippenger and Fischer on tape simulation](https://research.ibm.com/publications/relations-among-complexity-measures)

## Exactly how many exponents must a summary distinguish?

Suppose all information about $k$ available later must pass through one deterministic label, and after receiving the label the decoder must answer any entry query. If the encoder has to choose the label before seeing $A$, the exact minimum number of labels for unbounded exponents is
$$
       d+\Pi_d,\qquad
       \Pi_d=2^{\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}\operatorname{lcm}_{1\le j\le d}(2^j-1).
$$
The universal annihilator argument above, with multiplicity bound $2^{\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}\ge d$, gives the upper bound. A single size-$d$ nilpotent Jordan block distinguishes all $d$ early powers from one another and from every later power. On invertible matrices any collision between two exponents forces their difference to be a multiple of $\Pi_d$. Indeed a size-$d$ unipotent block has order exactly $2^{\lceil\log_2 d\rceil}$ by successive squaring. For every $j\le d$, multiplication by a generator of $\mathbb F_{2^j}^{\times}$ on a $j$-dimensional block padded with an identity forces $2^j-1$ to divide the difference. Distinct powers differ in some selectable entry. This is the classical finite general linear group exponent applied to a label that precedes the input matrix. [Karolinsky and Seliutin on finite linear-group exponents and cyclotomic factors](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/journals/JIS/VOL23/Karolinsky/kar2.pdf)

In fact the same lower bound holds if the only requested entry is $(1,1)$ and the matrix can contain at most $5d$ ones. The nilpotent or unipotent blocks, and primitive companion blocks padded by an identity, all use at most $2d$ ones. If their difference of powers $E$ has a diagonal one, move it to $(1,1)$ by a simultaneous permutation. Otherwise take $E_{ij}=1$ and conjugate by $S=I+e_{ji}=S^{-1}$; then $(SES)_{ii}=E_{ii}+E_{ij}=1$. This conjugation changes one row and one column of $A$ and adds at most $2d$ ones. Each pair of exponents can thus be distinguished by a single fixed diagonal query on a sparse matrix. Even a public catalog of $O(d^3)$ sparse matrices suffices: choose one primitive block per degree, the two Jordan examples, the exposing indices and the transposition sending the resulting diagonal to position one. Bob can specify his catalogued matrix in $O(\log(d+2))$ bits. If the matrix is already known to the encoder and the only subsequent output is that fixed bit, two labels suffice.

For these sparse inputs there is also an ordinary proper SAT encoding of length $\widetilde O(d^2+b)$ for the fixed diagonal query. Compute the first $2d$ scalar Krylov terms $e_1^{\mathsf T}A^te_1$ directly by sparse matrix–vector multiplication in $\widetilde O(d^2)$ bit work, using at most $5d$ input edges per step and sorting the requests by index. The scalar sequence has recurrence order at most $d$ by Cayley–Hamilton. Berlekamp–Massey on those $2d$ terms returns a monic recurrence of order at most $d$ that extends the entire sequence, in $O(d^2)$ field operations: a fitted recurrence of order at most $d$ has error sequence satisfying the true recurrence, with at least $d$ initial zero errors. The degree-zero case is identically zero. The period calculation and modular exponentiation above apply to the recovered recurrence with its degree in place of $d$. Unroll the arithmetic and the checks of the canonical quotient and gcd witnesses into a Boolean verifier. It has at most one accepting witness for the claimed bit. For Berlekamp–Massey, updating a degree-$d$ polynomial by a variable shift can use a barrel shifter of $O(d\log d)$ gates. Put fixed input bits for the exponent and sparse matrix in disjoint regions of the formula, represented by pins. Enforce all gate equivalences and the claimed output bit by proper ternaries using three pinned false anchors. The uniquely determined gate values make the formula satisfiable exactly on the required output. An initial segment can hold the exponent pins and the other input can follow; a random-access solver can inspect the matrix first. This gives a one-way communication consequence on ordinary formulas, where the required prefix summary has quadratic size in dimension. [Wiedemann on the scalar Krylov sequence and minimal recurrences](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1986.1057137) · [Massey, *Shift-register synthesis and BCH decoding*](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1969.1054260)

The universal labels take $\Theta(d^2)$ bits in the worst case. To see the lower estimate, $\operatorname{lcm}_{j\le d}(2^j-1)=\prod_{j\le d}\Phi_j(2)$ for the cyclotomic polynomials $\Phi_j$. For an odd prime $p$, let $e$ be the order of $2$ modulo $p$. The valuation of $2^{et}-1$ is $v_p(2^e-1)+v_p(t)$, and the other exponents have valuation zero. Factoring $2^j-1$ by divisors of $j$ shows that $\Phi_e(2)$ contributes $v_p(2^e-1)$ and $\Phi_{ep^h}(2)$ contributes one for each $h\ge1$, giving precisely the maximum valuation for the lcm. The cyclotomic product formula gives $\log_2\Phi_j(2)=\varphi(j)+O(1)$ uniformly because $\sum_{h\ge1}|\log_2(1-2^{-h})|<\infty$. Finally $\sum_{j\le d}\varphi(j)=\Theta(d^2)$: among ordered pairs from $[d]^2$, the proportion sharing a prime factor is at most $\sum_{n\ge2}1/n^2<1$.

If both encoder and decoder know $A$, the exact maximum, over matrices of size $d\ge2$, is only $2^d-1$ labels. The invariant-factor decomposition supplies a vector $v$ with annihilator equal to the minimal polynomial of $A$. Thus equality of any two orbit vectors $A^iv=A^jv$ implies equality of the two matrix powers, by applying the minimal polynomial divisibility to $x^i-x^j$. An orbit avoiding zero has at most $2^d-1$ elements. If this orbit reaches zero, then $A$ is nilpotent and there are at most $d+1\le 2^d-1$ powers. A primitive degree-$d$ companion matrix attains the maximum. For $d=1$ the zero matrix needs two labels. With a $b$-bit horizon, replace the corresponding number of labels by its minimum with $2^b$, since powers repeat periodically after the initial segment. These label counts measure information across a cut. They do not require the encoder to compute its optimal labels efficiently. The preceding constructive period already attains $O(d)$ bits after learning the matrix.

## Degree weights shared by every adaptive query

Let $w_n=[z^n](1/D(z))$, with $w_n=0$ for $n<0$. Compute the first $2d$ coefficients from $D(z)\sum_n w_nz^n=1$ in $O(d^2)$ binary operations. The sequence obeys the monic recurrence $\chi$ at every starting position. The desired weights are
$$
                    W_j=w_{k'-j},\qquad 0\le j<d.
$$
For $k'<d$ these are already available. Otherwise compute $r(x)=x^{k'-d+1}\bmod\chi(x)$ by repeated squaring. This takes $O(d)$ polynomial multiplications and reductions of degree $O(d)$ since $k'$ has $O(d)$ bits. If $r(x)=\sum_{h<d}r_hx^h$, the recurrence gives
$$W_j=\sum_{h<d}r_hw_{d-1-j+h}.$$
This takes $O(d^2)$ further bit operations. Thus even computing the entire window of $d$ coefficients fits the preparation budget. Fast extraction of an isolated term of a rational series is classical. [Bostan and Mori, *A Simple and Fast Algorithm for Computing the N-th Term of a Linearly Recurrent Sequence*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08822)

For the cofactor $C_{ij}(z)=\operatorname{adj}(I-zA)_{ij}$, the resolvent identity gives
$$ (A^k)_{ij}=[z^{k'}]\frac{C_{ij}(z)}{D(z)}
                   =\sum_{h<d} W_h [z^h]C_{ij}(z).$$
A single cofactor term is a bijection of rows other than $j$ onto columns other than $i$, with the same two labels as above. Count its actual degree $h$ and return the weight $W_h$ along with its selected entries. This needs at most $d-1$ matrix lookups. Invalid row, column or inactive codes contribute zero or are fixed uniquely as appropriate; the empty minor at $d=1$ has one empty term. For a bilinear measurement also sum over $i,j$ with weights $u_i v_j$. Accessing those two query-vector entries can use a binary multiplexer of size $O(d)$ per vector.

Finally guess an answer bit $y_t$ for each successive query. Run the controller on those bits to generate its claimed path. Multiply the factors $1+y_t+\text{queried value}$ by giving each position its independent constant, answer-bit or weighted cofactor choice. Conditioned on the full bit transcript the adaptive addresses are fixed, and the product is its exact validity indicator. Sequential determinism gives exactly one valid bit transcript for every external input. A single batch checks every chosen matrix entry against $A$, even though the controller depends on the answers. Registers, index wires and replies from the shared $W$ table require $\widetilde O(qd)$ additional sites; a direct $d$-bit multiplexer for each chosen $W_h$ already fits. This proves the bound.

Neiger, Pernet and Villard compute maximal Krylov bases, Frobenius forms with transformations, and matrix powers at essentially the arithmetic matrix-multiplication exponent. The volume bound here counts sites in a network that sums over binary internal wires. [Neiger, Pernet and Villard, *Computing Krylov iterates in the time of matrix multiplication*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07345)
