# Parallel proof attempts of 27 November, and why each stopped

On 27 November, two rounds of parallel attempts tried routes to an unrestricted RAM lower bound for explicit SAT. Each attempt had about one hour and wrote a note. **None bridges the gap; the linear, quadratic and cubic questions remain open.** The results published as separate notes, listed below, were each checked by an independent referee and revised. This note records the other attempts, with the exact reason each stopped, so that later work does not repeat them. None of the attempts recorded here was refereed: their lemmas are attempt history, not verified theorems.

## Attempts recorded here

**Importing a k-clique resolution bound (dead).** The source cited for an n^{Ω(k)} general-resolution lower bound on unary k-clique formulas, Atserias, arXiv:2601.12503, was withdrawn by its author on 26 January 2026 ("the main claim Theorem 1 remains unproved"); a May 2026 paper, arXiv:2605.10941, still calls that bound a long-standing open problem. For this encoding at fixed k, n^{Ω(k)} bounds are proved only for regular and tree-like resolution, on random graphs. Resolution bounds never bind an arbitrary RAM: Tseitin formulas on expanders need exponential resolution size, yet Gaussian elimination over GF(2) decides them in polynomial time. An O(N^D) SAT algorithm gives fixed-k clique in n^{2D+o(1)} time, since the clique encoding has Θ(k²n² log n) bits, so a RAM exponent growing with k would be as strong as P ≠ NP. [Withdrawn paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12503) · [Open problem statement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10941)

**Word-priced rung (dead as a bridge).** Pricing time in words of the input gives the rung W, nondeterministic versus deterministic word-linear time in the journal's RAM model. The milestone implies W by padding, so W is a weaker target, and, as with NLIN versus DLIN, the move removes no barrier. A manuscript proof collapses O(1) alternation blocks losslessly at word price within the journal's n³ cap, so a fixed alternating program with O(1) blocks that decides the diagonal language in o(t) word steps on good extensions would give W, though not the milestone. Against the journal's alternation criterion, the only difference is that the choices made before a change of player are counted in words instead of bits. The speedup itself is still missing: snapshots and faithful access logs are Θ(t) words, and a proof of it must not relativize. [Alternation criterion](alternating-native-speedup.md)

**Compiler counting (partial).** A proper ASCII DIMACS 3-CNF file of M bits has at most (log₂10/8 + o(1))·M/log₂M distinct clauses, matching the journal's bound for occurring variables. By the variable bound, a locally indexed compiler for all of the native language U with output length o(s log s) would decide U deterministically in time 2^{o(s)}, so the count excludes such a compiler only if nondeterministic linear RAM time lies outside deterministic time 2^{o(s)}, a hypothesis that itself implies the target. ETH would force only output length above εs, and statements under ETH are vacuous in the journal, which takes the October preprint "The Exponential Time Hypothesis is False" as a pinned external theorem; the variable-count Weak ETH forces only s^{1/3−δ}, both infinitely often. For the native milestone the criterion's proof compiles only the deterministic diagonal source, which the count does not obstruct; for the SAT target it also compiles a nondeterministic verifier. [Compiler criterion](compact-compilers-and-linear-sat-time.md) · [ETH preprint](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)

**Alternation-depth ladder (partial).** The padding step holds once the block budget is measured at the padded length; the top rung is then all of alternating linear time, and the step relativizes. Williams's Corollary 4.2 (see the QBF entry) gives, as stated, no gain and no round count for the unit-cost log-word RAM. On the journal's stacked grate runs, R rounds gain at most O(R log² R). Such caps never help the ladder, which needs the reverse: an R-round strategy on every access graph whose gain tends to infinity. Valiant's depth reduction (used from memory) supplies one only for R = T^{1−o(1)}, and the game on the RAM itself, with O(1) words per move, is also missing. [Bounded-round pebbling](mixed-tape-pebble-frontier.md)

**Hamming closest pair as an iterated list predicate (dead).** The simulation-threshold and technique-ceiling notes already cover it: however many projections are combined, the list-distinctness technique certifies at most O(N log N) for closest pair, below the N log^{3+δ} N that its Θ(N log N)-bit DIMACS projection would need. The one addition extends the ceiling from permutations to wirings that feed one input bit to several tests: one Beneš topology followed by a chain of r small vertices, with tables chosen per pattern, realizes every equality pattern exactly (checked for all 15 patterns at r = 4 and all 4,140 at r = 8). Compressing embeddings and techniques that are not routing-based stay outside the cap. [Simulation threshold](simulating-a-ram-step-by-exact-networks.md) · [Technique ceiling](the-list-distinctness-technique-cannot-certify-more-than-n-log-n.md)

**QBF rung (partial).** Williams's 2008 lower bound for succinct QBF, and the alternating speedup behind it (his Corollary 4.2), cover multitape and random-access Turing machines and log-cost RAMs. Translating the unit-cost RAM into log cost loses the log t factor that the speedup gains, so DTIME_RAM[t] ⊆ ATIME_RAM[o(t)] is not in the paper and remains unproved. The paper's footnote 1 puts traditionally encoded QBF in alternating time O(N/log N). Transferred to the journal's RAM (a sketch), this excludes any compiler built without the QBF hypothesis that encodes an alternating source at clock h into o(h log h) bits with constant-time packed reads, though not one built from the assumed solver. An unconditional o(t)-bit encoding of the diagonal language would put it in bit-priced alternating time o(t/log t), beyond what Dymond–Tompa-type games give. Settling claimed last writers by challenge costs T−1 placements on one family (exact for T = 3 to 14). The attempt's space conditions are no obstruction: the space-route note already fits RAM time t in about t^{4/5} bits, using the unrefereed two-fifths-power preprint. [Williams 2008](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2008/076/)

**Homomorphic segment digests (dead).** The per-run form of the proposed lemma, that a run with more free-load value tuples than digest values admits a cheat at every challenge, is false: on an all-zero toy run, a one-sided digest independent of the input admits no cheat for any prime from 211 to 2^20 (proved, and checked at 19 primes). Theorem 3 of the fractional-separation note is an earlier counterexample of the same kind, and the family form is the journal's Lean-checked common-collision lemma. For Z_p-valued digests and independent loads, the per-run threshold valid for every digest is the Cauchy–Davenport bound, attained by the toy digest; it is linear in the number of free loads and vacuous at the field sizes fingerprint checks need. The live question is whether every run of the diagonal machine admits a program-specific equality digest of O(1) words, as Theorem 3 provides for an OR program. [Common collision](separate-fingerprint-witnesses.md) · [Equality digests on an OR program](fractional-separation-of-native-memory-histories.md)

**Distinctness of pairwise XORs (dead as a mechanism).** The predicate asks that the n² words a_i ⊕ b_j be pairwise distinct, on N = 2nw input bits. A linear-image form of the list theorem, volume m^{1−o(1)} for any m records that are fixed linear forms of the input, is false: the n² concatenations (a_i, b_j) are such records, yet their distinctness has exact networks of volume O(nw log n). The list theorem's selector step survives the substitution when the difference set B⊕B of the second list is a subspace (the converse was checked only at small size), and then the n² records collapse to n cosets. Since the function has only N exterior bits to route, bounds through restrictions or constant-copy gadgets stay under the O(N log N) ceiling, below the ω(N log³ N) the route needs. Collision counts modulo p have exact networks of volume O(pN), so a superlinear bound must use the exact zero test. The escapes left are a compressing gadget and a technique that uses B⊕B not being a subspace. A follow-up showed that the batch bound for the counting tensor U*_ℓ of the Hamiltonian-cycle preprint, the only polynomial lower bound cited in the list theorem's section of the matrix-multiplication preprint, gives nothing for SAT. U*_ℓ counts a condition whose witnesses have ℓ^{100} bits, and in a characteristic admitting ports any 0/1 condition on ℓ + m bits, decided in T RAM steps, whose weighted count is U*_ℓ satisfies T log²T + ℓ + m ≥ c·ℓ^{1+δ}. So the bound is at most quasi-linear in the size of any NP condition that counts U*_ℓ: length, not the non-Boolean values, is the obstruction. This sharpens the reason given in the technique-ceiling note, that U*_ℓ is not a 0/1 predicate in NP: modulo p it is a count of an NP condition, but only of one whose length is at least about ℓ^{1+δ}, which is what keeps the bound from reaching SAT. [Technique ceiling](the-list-distinctness-technique-cannot-certify-more-than-n-log-n.md) · [Hamiltonian-cycle preprint](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Circuit-lower-bounds-for-Hamiltonian-cycle-and-the-permanent-November-24-2026)

**Access-log entropy (partial; retired as a bridge).** The price is the shortest code, for a fixed clocked decoder, of the work-memory store log sorted by address. Adjacency in the sorted log gives an ∃∀ certificate of a deterministic run whose first message is that code plus O(Tw/B) bits, with replays of B steps (checked at finite size on two 4-step machines). Through the native band method, every linear-time solver of U then has code length at least εN, for decoder clocks up to N^{1/3}, on inputs of each verdict at every large length. For SAT at polylog decoder time such a band already follows from the snapshot-description note; for U it is a routine new instance of the band method, incomparable with the space band. Given that band, normalizing a linear-time solver to code length o(N) is equivalent to the native milestone itself, and the certificate gives no speedup unless the worst-case code length is o(T), which is not known even for tape runs. [Snapshot descriptions](solver-snapshot-descriptions.md) · [Native band method](native-unary-clock-band.md)

**Games for linear recursion schemes (dead).** Under the fixed-offset reading, every stratum of the proposed induction is a linear-time machine with O(1) words of memory, so Ajtai's theorem (read from its abstract) excludes an explicit NLIN problem from all strata at once. It also excludes element distinctness, which is in DLIN, so the induction fails the easy-language test. Where DLIN is characterized, Grandjean–Schwentick need only one recursively defined function (again from the abstract), so an induction on the number of functions has no steps. Counting input reads cannot exclude any problem from DLIN, since a DLIN machine can copy its input, so a proof must charge steps that do not read the input; the missing statement is DLIN ≠ NLIN itself. A hierarchy over data-dependent lookbacks inside single-function schemes remains open here, since the Grandjean–Schwentick full text was not available. [Ajtai, ECCC TR98-077](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/eccc-reports/1998/TR98-077/index.html) · [Grandjean–Schwentick](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539799360240)

**One-pass compression and magnification (dead; a barrier).** Whether an N-bit string has an s-bit description, each bit evaluated in E steps, is decided in one pass that keeps O(s + E + log N) bits between blocks, in time O(k²N(s+1)) if the native language U has a kN-time solver, and with an extra factor log⁶(s + E + log N) if SAT has a linear-time solver: the McKay–Murray–Williams streaming algorithm with its oracle replaced by the hypothesis. The unconditional bound ω(N(s+1)) that the route needs is false for sparse F₂ polynomials, juntas and low-rank matrices. For every description language it fails for one-pass machines with an oracle on O(s + E + log N)-bit queries, which, when s and E are O(log N), is one extra opcode on O(1) words: the locality barrier. It would also have to bound computation rather than space, since s + O(log N) retained bits always suffice. For circuit and KT-type description languages the bound is open; any proof of it would put an explicit Σ₂ problem outside deterministic linear time, which implies the binary-choice native milestone and for KT-type languages is equivalent to it. [McKay–Murray–Williams](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/MCSP-MKTP-stoc19.pdf) · [Locality barrier](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08297)

**Linear space in subexponential time (no new lemma).** The transfer is already in the journal: under linear SAT, the language of a fixed nondeterministic machine with O(N) work bits is decided in deterministic RAM time 2^{O(√(N log N))}; quasi-linear SAT changes only the constant, which the counting note makes explicit. The missing step, a linear-space language outside time 2^{O(√(n log n))}, would itself prove P ≠ PSPACE, and it fails relative to TQBF and in the journal's multilinear-clock world. Closing it through the space hierarchy would need RAM time t in space o(log²t/log log t), which would put P inside SPACE(o(log²n)); the space-route note's simulation uses about t^{4/5} bits. [Linear quantifiers](linear-quantifiers.md) · [Counting under quasi-linear SAT](counting-sat-under-a-quasi-linear-sat-solver.md)

## What the routes were missing

The alternation and certificate routes (the word-priced rung, the alternation-depth ladder, the QBF rung, segment digests, access-log entropy, and compiler counting for the diagonal source) stopped at one missing statement: that every deterministic RAM run of t steps, with word-valued stores at fresh addresses, has a locally checkable certificate, or an alternating simulation, of o(t) bits or words. No attempt supplied it, and the barrier note lists what a proof of it must get past. The other uniform routes reduced to an open statement at least as hard as the one they aimed at, or met a known barrier: DLIN ≠ NLIN itself for recursion schemes, a step that implies P ≠ PSPACE for linear space, and the locality barrier for one-pass compression. Resolution bounds do not bind a RAM at all. The nonuniform route needs an exact-network SAT bound above N log² N, beyond the list-distinctness technique, which stops at O(N log N) for restriction-type embeddings.
