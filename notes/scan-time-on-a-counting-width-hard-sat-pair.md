# A scan-time SAT solver on both label orbits of a counting-width hard pair

There is an explicit infinite family of pairs of proper 3-CNFs on $3u$ variables and $32u$ clauses. One member is satisfiable and the other is unsatisfiable. On their unordered signed incidence structures no counting-logic sentence with fewer than $c u$ variables separates them, for an absolute $c>0$ and all sufficiently large family sizes. Consequently symmetric Boolean threshold circuits that separate their full domain-label orbits require $2^{\Omega(u)}$ gates. A **single globally correct deterministic SAT program** decides every ordinary sparse DIMACS encoding in both orbits in $O(u)$ word-RAM steps, allowing arbitrary variable names permuted within the dense range, clause order and literal order. Here the encoded bit length is $N=\Theta(u\log u)$ and the word width is $\Theta(\log N)$. The circuit receives unordered incidence relations, while the RAM receives an ordered list of signed variable identifiers.

The pair specializes the local XOR construction of Atserias and Dawar to Tseitin equations on cubic expanders. Their published approximation-gap construction uses a more general system $Ax=b$. Its global parity need not decide consistency. The exponential symmetric circuit conclusion below uses the established counting-width transfer of Atserias, Dawar and Ochremiak. [Local XOR lift, §3.2, Lemma 2](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~atserias/papers/definable-inapproximability/definable-inapproximability.pdf#page=9) · [Counting width and symmetric threshold circuits, §§2–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.07825)

## The pair and the counting game

Let $H$ be a simple cubic graph on $u$ vertices, with edge boundary $|\partial U|\ge h|U|$ for $|U|\le u/2$, for a constant $h>0$. An edge carries a variable $x_e$. At each vertex $v$ require that the three incident edge bits have XOR $b_v$. Compare zero charge with a single charge one. In any connected graph the image of its binary incidence matrix consists of the even-total vertex vectors. Every edge contributes twice to the total. Conversely, set non-tree edges to zero and process a spanning tree from the leaves, assigning each parent edge to satisfy the current vertex. Even total guarantees the root equation.

Here is a way to fix explicit cubic graphs if desired. Start with the symmetric eight-neighbor Margulis–Gabber–Galil graph on $\mathbb Z_s^2$; its second normalized eigenvalue is at most $5\sqrt2/8$. Make it lazy with eight more identity labels. The bipartite double cover is a loop-free 16-regular multigraph with second normalized eigenvalue at most $(1+5\sqrt2/8)/2<1$: its spectrum consists of both signs of the lazy walk's nonnegative eigenvalues. Thus its edge expansion $h_0>0$ is bounded below uniformly. Replace each base point by a 16-cycle of its labeled ports and join matched ports across the base edges. The result is simple and cubic even where the base had parallel edges. To check expansion, a cut $S$ of size at most half has $M$ mixed clouds and $F$ full clouds, with $|S|\le16F+16M$. It has at least $2M$ internal cycle boundary edges. Also $F$ is at most half the base order, and at least $h_0F-16M$ external edges leave $S$. These two bounds imply $|\partial S|=\Omega(|S|)$ by separating $M\ge\epsilon |S|$ from its complement for any sufficiently small fixed $\epsilon>0$. This gives $u=32s^2$. [Margulis–Gabber–Galil graph and spectral bound](https://isa-afp.org/browser_info/current/AFP/Expander_Graphs/Expander_Graphs_MGG.html)

For odd charge, the base system is locally satisfiable with linearly many pebbles. After deleting any set $P$ of fewer than $hu/2$ edges there is a unique component larger than $u/2$. Otherwise summing the expansion inequality over components would give $hu\le2|P|$. Duplicator maintains a full edge assignment whose only failed equation is at a vertex of this giant component, agreeing with all pebbled answers. The initialization is possible by correcting the charge at any chosen giant vertex and using the incidence-image calculation. If a pebble is lifted, the old giant is contained in the new one. When Spoiler asks for the bit of an edge $e$, first consider the giant after deleting $P\cup\{e\}$; it is contained in the giant of $H-P$. Move the defect to it by toggling the edges of a path in $H-P$ and only then return the resulting bit of $e$. This preserves all old answers. The defect cannot be a fully pebbled equation because its component has at least two vertices and hence an unpebbled incident edge there. The variable-only ternary-relation instance therefore has a winning existential pebble strategy for every $k<hu/2$.

Double each $x_e$ into $x_e^0,x_e^1$. Give each vertex's incident edges a fixed local listing $e_1,e_2,e_3$, the same on both sides. Include all eight equations
$$
 x_{e_1}^{a_1}+x_{e_2}^{a_2}+x_{e_3}^{a_3}
       = b_v+a_1+a_2+a_3 \qquad(a\in\mathbb F_2^3).
$$
A solution sets $x_e^1=1+x_e^0$ by subtracting two rows differing only at that edge. Conversely any solution of the base system extends this way. Atserias–Dawar's Lemma 2 upgrades the existential strategy to a bijective pebble strategy between the two doubled systems: after removing a pebble, compute a permissible response bit $f(e)$ for every possible next edge from the same residual position. Duplicator offers the permutation swapping the two copies above $e$ by $f(e)$. Whichever side Spoiler plays on, this same involution works and the response for the actually selected edge has a compatible continuation. Previously occupied fibers use their fixed old bit. The local incidence listing stays respected. Hence the two ternary-relation structures are $C^{\Omega(u)}$-equivalent in the sense that they agree on all sentences up to a fixed linear variable budget. [Atserias–Dawar, Lemma 2 and its proof](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~atserias/papers/definable-inapproximability/definable-inapproximability.pdf#page=9)

Replace every parity equation by the four proper ternaries forbidding the assignments of opposite parity. No two distinct base vertices share two edges, so the output has $8u$ distinct variable triples, each with four distinct clauses. In the signed incidence structure a clause point can be represented by its ordered triple and one of four fixed pattern tags. The variable points are a separate copy. The interpretation has fixed tuple dimension and needs no domain order. It loses only a constant factor in counting width, including for size-dependent counting sentences. Equivalently one can lift a tuple by successively pebbling its bounded number of coordinates, with previous tuples retained, assembling a bijection on tuples from the conditional bijections on their next coordinates. Consequently the proper 3-CNF pair retains linear counting width. The small symmetric-circuit to bounded-counting-variable transfer applies to any separator of these two label orbits: that conversion is independent of what the invariant circuit outputs elsewhere. With domain size $35u$, its bounds exclude $2^{o(u)}$ gates and give $2^{\Omega(u)}$ by the quantitative estimate in §5.2. [Parity-to-clause transfer](https://www.cs.upc.edu/~atserias/papers/definable-inapproximability/definable-inapproximability.pdf) · [Symmetric LP, circuit and counting-width bounds](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.07825)

## Recovering the equations from an arbitrarily relabeled file

The program starts by parsing a candidate formula. Every check below must succeed before using the fast answer. On a failed check use a complete general SAT decider with the usual handling of malformed inputs.

1. Sort the literals within each clause by variable, carrying their signs. Group clauses by the unsigned triple. Require three distinct variables and exactly the four distinct forbidden sign patterns of one parity; duplicate identical clauses can be discarded. The group is exactly one equation with right side opposite to the forbidden parity.
2. Make three records per resulting scope, one per unordered pair of its variables. Group by pair. On the target inputs every occurring pair belongs to exactly two scopes. Require this and connect those two scopes. The eight scopes from one base vertex form the binary three-cube under this adjacency. Scopes from different base vertices share at most one physical variable. Hence the connected components recover the vertex blocks.
3. In each block require eight scopes on exactly six names, partitioned into three disjoint pairs of mates. The mate of a name is the other name with which it never co-occurs inside this block. Check that the eight scopes are exactly the eight transversals; these are constant-size tests. Require that each name has the same mate wherever it appears and that each mate pair belongs to exactly two distinct blocks. These incidences define an undirected graph with blocks as vertices and mate pairs as edges.
4. Choose the smaller internal variable index in each pair as its representative. At a block let $c_v$ be the right side of the scope containing its three representatives. For every scope of that block, check that its right side is $c_v$ plus the parity of the number of nonrepresentatives used. These checks establish that the entire formula equals the doubled system on the recovered graph. It is satisfiable exactly when the XOR of the $c_v$ in each connected component is zero. Selecting the other representative of an edge adds one at each endpoint and leaves this answer invariant.

More generally, the same scope components unpack the doubled equations of any 3-uniform hypergraph in which two base rows share at most one variable. The residual right side is changed by a variable orientation, and solving that residual system can still require other work. Here each recovered variable belongs to precisely two rows, which is what makes the graph parity test exact even if the recovered graph is disconnected. Expansion, girth and the original charge choice need never be recognized by the program. Thus the globally correct program handles both target orbits through the same checked computation.

In canonical dense DIMACS on this family, the number of literal fields is $96u$, variable identifiers are at most $3u$, and the header has $O(\log u)$ digits. All names occur. This gives the asserted $\Theta(u\log u)$ bit length in every ordering. For a direct packed parser, use fixed-width groups of at most $\max(1,\lfloor\log_2 N/32\rfloor)$ consecutive bytes. Tabulate, for every possible group, its separator positions, validity, and decimal values and lengths of its token pieces. There are $O(N^{1/4})$ group patterns and the table costs $O(N^{1/4}\operatorname{polylog}N)=O(u)$ to build. Each $O(\log u)$-digit name spans only constantly many groups, combined using precomputed powers of ten; tabulated piece lists are traversed once per group and token. Header counts, range, signs and clause endings are checked. Before allocating buckets the fast path requires word-sized counts and an identifier bound at most a fixed multiple of the number of records; on overflow or a failed check it falls back. This costs $O(N/\log N+u)=O(u)$ native steps. Alternatively the journal's general packed normalizer returns the distinct clause set in this time. The remaining tuple keys have constantly many components in an $O(u)$ range, so stable counting-sort passes, including bucket initialization, cost $O(u)$ in total. The two component traversals use adjacency lists of total size $O(u)$ and mark each vertex once. No general dictionary, graph canonization or hidden graph isomorphism test is used. ## A linear-bit-time implementation whose arithmetic and addresses have constant local support

A stronger test for the RAM lower-bound strategy uses the sparse, padded incidence serialization. It has a public header, public anchor clauses, and one domain row at each label $i\in[D]$, where $D=35u$. A clause row contains its three variable identifiers. A variable row contains a fixed true filler. The padded rows in either orbit have fixed length $\Theta(\log u)$ bits. A support at a labeled input fixes names pointwise and must preserve the specified **numerical** observation under every remaining relabeling of that same structure. [Serialization and local support](name-support-of-a-native-ram-word.md)

**Theorem.** There is one globally correct deterministic SAT RAM taking $O(u\log u)=O(N)$ time on every labeling of both structures, such that at each genuine instruction on those inputs the program counter, branch condition, effective addresses, and results of arithmetic and logical instructions all have local supports of bounded size. Only a fixed number of token words and temporary registers can carry values with larger local support at any one time. Their payloads are single bits, and their only intermediate uses are loads, copies and stores. The numerical verdict is invariant within each orbit. This is a different implementation from the faster component traversal above. It addresses the proposed inference from a name-dependent transfer to a name-dependent calculation. [Necessity of a name-dependent delivered value](scatter-and-productive-steps-of-a-sat-ram.md)

**Validation at fixed positions.** On a candidate file validate the header, public section, row widths, domains, signs and padding. Preserve one storage slot per domain label throughout. No step computes how many clause rows preceded the current row. A fixed number of operations per digit in a fixed-width field suffices for this version. For every lifted variable reserve 32 occurrence slots. A lifted variable appears at two base vertices, in four scopes at each, and in four clauses per scope. Scan the rows in label order, appending the three incidences to the relevant lists. Reject the fast path on overflow. A variable's running lane count at any point is determined by that variable and all its incident clause labels. Fixing these names fixes their positions relative to the current row, including whether the lane is still empty. Fixing the other endpoints of these same clauses also fixes all fields stored there. On the target orbits these are constant-size sets, including for partially filled lists.

Recover the scopes, their sign patterns, mates, and the three pairs at each quotient vertex using bounded incidence neighborhoods. Concretely, the occurrence list for one member of a given triple suffices to enumerate all clauses with that triple. For a co-occurring pair the two possible third names must be mates. Check the mate relation is consistent and fixed-point-free. Collect the lists at a mate pair to recover its incident blocks, and check each claimed block has exactly three pairs, all eight scopes and the prescribed 32 clauses. Check the affine sign pattern and that each pair has exactly two distinct end blocks. Require every non-dummy row to occur in its verified block and every variable row to belong to a verified pair. Use the least clause label in a block as its representative and the least variable label in a pair as its orientation. No global renumbering is needed. On valid inputs a constant-radius neighborhood with bounded degree suffices to perform or certify each check, including a comparison for the local minimum. For other inputs decline the fast path as soon as a required bounded list or check fails. A complete SAT algorithm handles the declined file. These checks certify the reconstructed equations irrespective of the verdict.

Loops use public bounds and padded substeps. In particular a dummy row and a clause row occupy equal time. Conditional updates can use a bounded-time branch with equal path lengths or write to dedicated dummy slots. Dummy slots can receive public zeros; temporary indices and counters are reset or overwritten according to the fixed schedule. Keep validation success flags alone across rows; they stay true on the hard orbits. Per-row Boolean results, offsets, incidence entries, and numerical addresses have supports bounded by the union of constantly many bounded neighborhoods of the current labels. This remains true if a register still holds the last public iteration's temporary, since there are a fixed number of registers and every per-row temporary is reset. These pointwise support bounds do not require a canonical global ordering of quotient vertices.

**Certifying the even answer.** An even total charge alone could hide two odd connected components. Choose the root block incident to domain label 1. For a clause point use its own block. For a variable point use the smaller representative of its two incident blocks. Execute $C\lceil\log_2(D+2)\rceil$ synchronous breadth-first rounds with a fixed sufficiently large $C$. Each round scans the whole domain and writes a fresh bit array, one update for each representative and zero for dummy slots. The update is the OR of the old bit and the old bits of the three neighbors. Only the root starts marked. A cubic graph of edge expansion $h$ has balls growing by a factor at least $1+h/3$ up to half the graph. Two balls larger than half must meet. Thus the public number of rounds covers the diameter of every target graph. Check every block was reached, otherwise fall back.

After round $j$ a represented node is marked exactly if its **intrinsic graph distance** from the root is at most $j$. To support that bit, fix the bounded labeled neighborhoods selecting the root and the target representative. These choices determine corresponding vertices under the induced graph isomorphism. All intervening path labels may vary. The same reasoning supports partially computed updates by additionally fixing the constantly many neighbor representatives whose bits have been accessed. Within a round the fixed destination array prefix holds new fields, and the rest holds earlier-round fields or zeros. The selection between these descriptions is public at a fixed time and address. This is why the iteration can range over a graph of unbounded size while every field and every arithmetic result in the reachability computation has constant local support. The final running check stays successful everywhere in both orbits.

**Carrying parity.** Scan the domain in its public order. At a block representative let $p_i$ be its locally determined baseline charge. Else let $p_i=0$. Initialize two token slots with the bits 0 and 1. At position $i$, for each $j=0,1$, copy the old token at address $j\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}p_i$ into slot $j$ of a second bank. Then exchange bank roles on the public clock. The token in slot $j$ equals $j$ XOR the prefix parity. The only selection arithmetic acts on $j,p_i$. It never takes a carried bit as operand. The public syntax, local checks and connectedness check certify that the final token in slot zero is the total charge of the one component. Return YES iff this token is zero. On either orbit that final test has empty local support. The linear-bit-time routine uses $O(u)$ work words, most of which hold locally supported graph data. A constant bound on simultaneously held high-support values allows linearly many distinct times at which a high-support bit crosses a bus.

The algebra of two-bank routing, the walk interpretation of synchronous reachability and its invariance under relabeling are checked in Lean. The executable local decoder compares reconstructed answers with PicoSAT, and tests disconnection, corruption and an insufficient round budget. Its Python runtime and control flow do not certify native instruction timing or supports; those guarantees use the padded RAM schedule above. ## How often can a large-support value appear?

On these same hard orbits there is also an $O(N)$ implementation with the following simultaneous bounds. For every threshold $s$ above a fixed constant, only $O(u/s)$ actually issued scalar or transfer records on a run have minimum local support greater than $s$ (zero when $s>D$). Here one record contains only a fixed number of observed fields. Keep the locally supported validation and reachability stages, but finish with a public balanced binary XOR tree over the $D$ slot charges and zero padding to the next power of two. Each leaf bit has a support of size at most a fixed $a$, which can depend on the labeling. A node over $m$ leaves has a support of at most $am$ by taking the union of leaf supports. Its evaluation takes a bounded number of actual steps at public addresses. Those steps observe only its operands, the result, and fixed-schedule quantities. Use a fixed iteration schedule with cleared temporary registers and direct stores. At level $j$ there are $O(u/2^j)$ nodes over $2^j$ leaves. Summing over levels with $a2^j$ greater than a constant multiple of $s$ gives the bound, with a change of constants for records containing several fields. If $s\le D$ the constant number of instructions at the final output fits the same estimate. Only issued observations are counted, so leaving an untouched partial sum in an array produces no extra records. This tree uses arithmetic on some high-support bits. The token implementation instead keeps all arithmetic locally supported.

In particular a superlinear-time argument cannot follow solely from a claim that *all* linear-bit-time solvers on these orbits need unbounded arithmetic supports, or that they have more than order $u/s$ observed values of support above $s$ on one run. Each of these candidate lemmas has a linear-bit-time counterexample. The unrestricted SAT time targets remain open.
