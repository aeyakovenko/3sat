# Common time-bounded complexity jumps throughout a Hamming ball

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Assume linear deterministic time for explicit SAT on the unrestricted logarithmic-word RAM. Fix any finite list of efficient native universal prefix interpreters. At **every sufficiently large output length** one can compute a single Hamming ball on which every word's shortest timely description falls by a linear number of bits on every listed interpreter between the same two deadlines. The center needs only a logarithmic-length program at the later deadline. A second construction gives a Martin-Löf random series supported on words that cross simultaneously on the entire list. A scalar randomness statement for each interpreter separately would not ensure common witnesses. Algorithmic rate-distortion theory studies minimum description length within a distortion ball. The additional constraints here are the two close exponential clocks and one shared ball on actual machines. [Vereshchagin and Vitányi](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0411014)

## A ball at every length

Let $U_1,\ldots,U_k$ be the fixed interpreters, $m$ the output length, $R=\lfloor m/16\rfloor$, and $V_m=\sum_{j=0}^R\binom mj$ the common ball volume. Take

$$T=2^{3m},\qquad t=\max(1,\lfloor T/m^a\rfloor),\qquad \ell=m-\lceil\log_2(kV_m)\rceil.$$

For one fixed sufficiently large $a$, call a center $y$ good if no machine prints a word at distance at most $R$ from $y$ by $t$ on a name of length less than $\ell$. A prefix-free machine has at most $2^{\ell-1}$ such halting names when $\ell\ge1$. Each can spoil at most $V_m$ centers, so at least $2^{m-1}$ centers are good. The same proof works for any fixed rational distortion fraction with $R$ defined accordingly.

Given a prefix of the center, ask if a good center extends it. Guess the center, universally select an interpreter and short name, and check the bounded native computation and distance. One can also universally exclude a valid producing transcript at that name; chronological and address-order consistency of the single guessed transcript are deterministic local checks. The fixed-block predicate has a uniform encoding of size $t\operatorname{poly}(m)$. Linear SAT eliminates the fixed blocks with polylogarithmic overhead. Polynomially many prefix decisions recover the lexicographically first good center within $T$ on each listed interpreter if $a$ absorbs the fixed encoding and native compilation exponents. The procedure treats $a$ as data in clock arithmetic; for fixed $a$ and large $m$ its overhead exponent can be chosen independently of $a$. Equivalently fix a single large $a_0$ for the construction. Its output then satisfies the lower bound at every earlier cutoff corresponding to $a\ge a_0$. [Bounded native computations](near-clock-credits.md) · [Fixed-block elimination](linear-quantifiers.md)

Supply $m$ in self-delimiting binary. Appending $\lceil\log_2 V_m\rceil$ raw bits suffices to specify a displacement of weight at most $R$; its weight and lexicographic position can be unranked using the binomial counts in polynomial time. Each machine can compile this same center computation followed by the displacement, leaving room for its polynomial finalization in the choice of $a_0$. On the resulting ball, simultaneously for every $z$ and every $i$,

$$K^{U_i}_{t}(z)\ge\ell,\qquad K^{U_i}_T(z)\le\lceil\log_2 V_m\rceil+O(\log m).$$

In particular $\log_2 V_m\le mH_2(1/16)$ and $1-2H_2(1/16)>0.32$, yielding a drop of at least $0.32m$ bits for all large $m$. The same center computation with no displacement has a timely description of $O(\log m)$ bits. The unrestricted number of early names is essential. Simulating all those names individually at a polynomial cost per native step only proves a corresponding claim with a larger exponential separation of clocks.

The every-length part also works at polynomial clocks. For example take $t=m^2$. The center-prefix query above never lists the $2^m$ possible centers. It uses one $m$-bit candidate and one bounded native transcript. The native words, instruction encoding and addresses now cost only $\operatorname{polylog}(m)$ bits per simulated step, so one prefix query costs $m^2\operatorname{polylog}(m)$ under linear SAT. The whole center and any specified displacement can be printed by $m^3\operatorname{polylog}(m)$ on each fixed interpreter; binomial unranking can use a table of $O(m^2)$ binomial coefficients with $O(m)$ bits each and Pascal additions. Thus the same simultaneous $0.32m$-bit decrease holds between two polynomial clocks. The following randomness argument uses a larger explicit list and the exponential clock choice above.

## A random sum supported on joint crossings

Keep the same ball size and set $L(m)=\lfloor m/4\rfloor$. There are fixed nonnegative integers $e_1,\ldots,e_k$ and infinitely many good centers of lengths $m=2^r$ satisfying

$$K^{U_i}_T(y)=L(m)-e_i\quad (1\le i\le k),\qquad K(y)\ge L(m)-O(1),$$

where $K$ is ordinary prefix complexity on a fixed reference. To construct these centers use the paired halting indices $r=r(p,t_p)$, with $w_r=2^{-|p|}\ge\delta\mathbf m(r)$ on infinitely many indices. Here $\mathbf m$ is the universal lower semicomputable semimeasure and $t_p\le r$. After the prefix program $p$ read $L(m)-|p|-c$ rank bits, with one fixed $c$ large enough to pay every compiler on the finite list, including its own specification. Select the good center of that rank. The space of good centers suffices by the preceding count. For exact prefix counts guess a sorted list of distinct good centers, universally choose an entry, and apply the previous bounded test. This costs $(m2^m+t)\operatorname{poly}(m)$ with fixed blocks. Binary search over the count and then prefix unranking fit the later deadline with sufficiently large common $a_0$. Every interpreter has a timely program of length at most $L(m)$ for each selected center. [Paired halting indices and word ranks](varying-description-savings.md)

The Kraft weight of shortest ordinary names at length $2^r$ is a lower semicomputable summable function of $r$ and hence at most $C\mathbf m(r)$. At a tight index, among the $2^{L(m)-|p|-c}$ distinct selected centers, a fraction at most $(C/\delta)2^{c-h}$ can have $K(y)\le L(m)-h$. Choose $h$ fixed and large. The others also have ordinary complexity $L(m)-O(1)$ on each of the finitely many interpreters, by fixed compiler constants. A single vector of timely offsets repeats at unbounded lengths by the finite pigeonhole principle.

For such a vector, sum over **all** good $m=2^r$ centers with those timely offsets the quantity

$$\min_{1\le i\le k}\big(2^{-K^{U_i}_T(y)}-2^{-K^{U_i}_{t}(y)}\big),\qquad 2^{-\infty}=0.$$

Each term is computable with no time requirement and the sum is bounded by Kraft on the first interpreter. On the infinitely many displayed witnesses, $\ell>L(m)$ and the minimum is at least $2^{-L(m)-1}$, a fixed multiple of $2^{-K(y)}$. The computable-series term criterion therefore makes the sum Martin-Löf random. The same vector and witnesses work for all fixed $a\ge a_0$, since goodness is preserved and the increments only increase. [Bienvenu and Shen, Theorem 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5028)

## Testing the covering bound on the actual machines

There is a sharp constraint on the lexicographic search in the analogous radius-one experiment. Take $m=2^r-1$ at the paired tight indices and $R=1$, so $V_m=2^r$ and $\ell=m-r-\lceil\log_2 k\rceil$. At each sufficiently late such index the first good center has exactly $|p|+O(1)$ initial zero bits. This statement is unconditional at either the polynomial early clock $m^2$ or the exponential early clocks above.

Indeed the total Kraft weight on all panel members of all names producing length $m$ is $O(w_r)$ on these indices, by lower semicomputability and disjoint lengths. The number of early outputs witnessed by names shorter than $\ell$ is at most $2^\ell$ times that weight. Their radius-one balls cover at most $O(2^m w_r)$ centers. Thus the first good center has at least $|p|-O(1)$ initial zeroes. For the other inequality fix one panel member. A fixed decoder reads $p$, computes $r$, sets $q=|p|+C$, and supplies $q$ leading zeroes, followed by a binary Hamming code of block length $2^{r-1}-1$ and arbitrary remaining coordinates. In the Hamming block place the free bits in the non-check positions and set its $r-1$ check bits to give zero syndrome. Every block is within distance one of a codeword because the check-matrix columns are all the nonzero $(r-1)$-bit strings. The raw data length is $m-q-(r-1)$ and the full name length is $m-r+1-C+O(1)+|\operatorname{gamma}(C)|<\ell$ for a sufficiently large fixed $C$. Computing and writing the result takes $m\operatorname{polylog}(m)$ time. Hence every center with $q$ leading zeroes has a genuinely early neighbor. These matching bounds exhibit the cost of identifying the length when trying to use perfect Hamming codes to cover the constructed centers. Lean 4.19 checks the syndrome identity and single-coordinate correction; the Kraft, clock, and joint randomness arguments are manuscript proofs. [Lean verification](../lean/CommonDistortion.lean)

A direct exclusion test would be to exhibit a fixed finite list of efficient interpreters such that, for arbitrarily large $a$, their early short outputs come within $m/16$ of every later logarithmic-description center on one of the machines at infinitely many lengths. The volume bound leaves many uncovered centers on purely counting grounds. The issue is whether any such center can be found by the later deadline, or whether all common late descriptions can be approximated by an early name within this length budget. I found no panel giving that exclusion. The unrestricted SAT lower bound remains open.
