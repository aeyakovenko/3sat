# Short templates for canonical bits force long refutations on many pins

Fix any sound polynomial-time checker $Q(F,\pi)$ for refutations of ordinary Boolean formulas. One example is a fixed encoding of Extended Frege. There is an infinitely-often canonical Arthur–Merlin table $g$ on $V=2^b$ addresses with the following property. If an ordinary SAT template of total description length $l=o(V)$ predicts $g$ with fixed advantage $\nu>0$, then at least $(\nu-o(1))V$ addresses are **correctly predicted zero**, and none of their formulas has a $Q$-refutation of length at most $2^{b^2}$. This conclusion is unconditional. It permits a different refutation on each address, of arbitrary computational cost to find. The table is selected for the fixed checker. The hypotheses assert neither the existence of such a template nor efficient generation of the hard pin indices.

For a CNF of physical length $N\le V$, the bound $2^{b^2}$ is at least $N^{\log_2 N}$. Thus a compact template accurate on these tables would itself give a superpolynomial length lower bound for the chosen proof system. Include the earlier uniform-binomial density test for deterministic descriptions with the larger interpreter cutoff $M$. Then a compact ordinary template with this advantage would also rule out SAT running in $2^{o((\log N)^2)}$ time on a logarithmic-word RAM: constructing an individual pinned input and simulating that clock on tapes takes $2^{o(b^2)}<M$ steps when $N\le V$. The stored template and pin map still occupy $o(V)$ bits. A linear-time lower bound could instead be obtained by an argument that bypasses these tables. Proof systems with efficiently provable disjointness of NP pairs were studied by Beyersdorff. The theorem here counts correctly witnessed pin values on canonical strings. Its priority is unverified. ## Choosing the property and its clock

At source length $M$, let $b=\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor\log_2 M\rfloor}\rfloor$ and $V=2^b$. The first $V$ bits of the candidate are its proposed table $g$. A description of length $l\le V$ includes its public template and pin conventions, or more generally a fixed-interpreter program that outputs a formula $F_i$ at each $b$-bit address $i$ in at most $M$ deterministic steps. Default timeouts and malformed formulas to one fixed valid formula. This includes nonuniform ordinary hardwired templates with individually coded sign locations, using their physical length plus $O(b\log V)$ bits of metadata when $N\le V$. It also allows generated formulas longer than the table when their common recipe is short. The cutoff and universal interpreter are fixed parts of the dense property.

Put $a$ equal to the number of addresses with either of these witnesses for the proposed table bit. If $g_i=1$, exhibit a satisfying assignment to $F_i$. If $g_i=0$, exhibit a $Q$-refutation of length at most $M$ of $F_i$. Each address contributes at most once. Soundness of $Q$ makes these two labels disjoint for each fixed index and description. Require
$$
 a\ \le\ V/2+\sqrt{2V(l+b+3)}
$$
for every description of length $l\le V$. This is a dense coNP property in $M$. Its violation has a certificate naming a description, distinct addresses, and the indicated local witnesses, whose aggregate length is polynomial in $M$. A SAT witness only needs to specify the values of variables occurring in the generated formula, whose physical length is at most $O(M)$; alternatively use one value per occurrence and check name consistency. Check the strict threshold using integers. For each fixed description the labels available at all addresses are fixed independently of the candidate. At a uniform candidate, $a$ is a sum of independent fair Bernoulli variables on the singly labeled addresses, with zero at missing addresses. It is stochastically dominated by $\mathrm{Binomial}(V,1/2)$. The probability of violation is at most $\exp[-4(l+b+3)]$. At length $l$ there are at most $2^l$ descriptions. Summing the tail bounds gives density greater than $1/2$ for all large $M$; accept all small lengths. Other dense hard-table conditions with exponentially small violation probability can be included by the same union bound.

Apply Chen, Li and Liang, Theorem 1.3, with their constant parameter $k=4$. Infinitely many $M$ yield a canonical $M$-bit string meeting the property. We have $M\ge 2^{b^2}$ and $\log_2 M<(b+1)^2+1$. The construction advice occupies $2^{O(\sqrt b)}$ bits. Supplying $M$ itself costs $O(b^2)$ further bits. The canonical generator still runs in $2^{b^{O(1)}}$ time, and the prefixes appear at unbounded distinct $b$. Its bit protocols can be normalized and amplified on shared outer coins as in the earlier construction. Use the smaller $V$ only as the number of private addresses; the source construction still acts on length $M$. [Chen, Li and Liang, Theorem 1.3 and proof of Theorem 1.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/182/revision/1/download/) · [Conversion to pinned formulas](semantic-public-tapes.md)

For the specified template, let $t$ be the number of correctly predicted bits. Every correct positive address contributes to $a$ via a SAT witness. A correct negative address fails to contribute exactly when its formula has no short refutation. Therefore the number of such hard correct negatives is $t-a$. Agreement $t\ge (1/2+\nu)V$ gives
$$
 t-a\ \ge\ \nu V-\sqrt{2V(l+b+3)}.
$$
If the template is exact, nearly one half of the table is hard in this sense. The constant all-positive and all-negative descriptions (using a fixed contradiction with a $Q$-proof if there is one) give the usual balance, or enforce balance itself by adding the direct binomial condition. More generally no balance assumption is needed for the displayed lower bound. This result is a disjunction between template accuracy/size and proof length. An upper bound on SAT decision time does not produce short proofs in a preselected system such as Extended Frege.

## Public-coin refutations with a different reply to each challenge

A randomized checker need not decide a total inference language. Fix a public-coin checker that reads a formula, Arthur's coins, and Merlin's response. For each budget $B$ and $|F|\le B$, use a public padded randomness length $r=r(B)=B^{O(1)}$ and a response cap $B$. A fixed polynomial time bound includes all computations. Define $p_B(F)$ as the probability on fresh coins that **some** response within the cap is accepted. Soundness requires $p_B(F)\le 1/3$ for each satisfiable $F$. On unsatisfiable formulas the values may lie anywhere in $[0,1]$. The target resource is now $p_B(F)\ge 2/3$ rather than a deterministic refutation. The canonical family can exclude robust short refutations on the same number of correct negative pins, using one common auxiliary table in the dense property.

Here choose $b=\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor\log_2 M\rfloor/K}\rfloor$ for a sufficiently large fixed integer $K$ depending on the checker's polynomial clock, and put $B=2^{b^2}$, $V=2^b$. These sizes can equivalently be selected by integer comparisons. Choose $h=\lceil 100(B+r+1)\rceil$. After the first $V$ bits, reserve $hr$ candidate bits for $h$ coin strings $R_j$. The dimensions fit since $hr=B^{O(1)}$ and $M\ge B^K$. Descriptions for this part generate formulas within $B$ deterministic steps, adjusting fixed interpreter overheads or rejecting outputs longer than $B$. Ordinary templates of length at most $V$ fit. The advice estimate is still $2^{O(\sqrt b)}$. The deterministic proof conditions above may be included on these same scales.

A provisional negative label for $F$ asks for a common $r$-bit shift $\tau$ and separate Merlin responses which pass at least half of the $h$ checks on $R_j\oplus\tau$. If $p_B(F)\ge2/3$, averaging the empirical score over all shifts gives $p_B(F)$ for **every** $R$, so some shift supplies the provisional label. For a fixed satisfiable $F$ and fixed shift, the chance on uniform $R$ of a provisional success with this shift is at most $e^{-h/18}$ by Hoeffding and the bound $p_B(F)\le1/3$. This already allows fresh, adversarial responses to every sampled challenge. A union bound over at most $2^{B+1}$ possible formulas and $2^r$ shifts leaves probability $o(1)$ that any satisfiable formula acquires a negative label.

Impose two conditions on the candidate. Its public coin sample must give no provisional negative to **any satisfiable** $F$ of length at most $B$, and the earlier cap on correctly witnessed addresses must hold using these provisional negatives. A failure of either has an NP certificate in $M$: for the first exhibit $F$, a satisfying assignment, a shift and the required responses; for the second use the matching address certificates. Condition on a suitable coin sample. Its accept sets are fixed independently of the random first $V$ bits, and the positive and negative labels are disjoint. The same binomial tail proves density of the intersection. On the selected canonical prefix, at least $\nu V-\sqrt{2V(l+b+3)}$ correct negatives have $p_B(F)<2/3$. No gap convention for the remaining unsatisfiable inputs is used. A shift or Merlin response may vary with the pin. The large coin table is used in selecting the canonical string, and is never granted to the short template generator. This argument uses the same elementary translation averaging as the [self-referential shifted-check construction](shifted-proof-challenges.md).

## Allowing certified exceptions to duality

A common proof of mutual exclusion permits the same calculation for two arbitrary nondeterministic SAT templates $F_0,F_1$. Fix a sound polynomial-time certificate checker for assertions that the number of addresses where both templates accept is at most $e$. Its input consists of the two described templates at the scale, $e$, and a certificate of length at most $M$. For $e=0$ one may use a propositional proof of the unsatisfiability of their conjunction with a shared address and separate witness variables. For other $e$ a checker can, for instance, verify an explicit exceptional list and a refutation outside that list. In this paragraph the template descriptions, addresses and verified certificate assertions have fixed effective syntax and polynomial checking time in $M$. Write $l$ for the combined description length.

Choose the canonical prefix also to require, for every certified bound and every pair, that the number $a'$ of addresses admitting the candidate's own label satisfy
$$
 a'\ \le\ (V+e)/2+\sqrt{2V(l+2b+4)}.
$$
Again a violation has an NP certificate. For a pair whose actual ambiguity is $e_*$, the random number of correct witnessed labels has distribution $e_*+\mathrm{Binomial}(j,1/2)$, where $j\le V-e_*$ is the number of singly labeled addresses. Its expectation is at most $(V+e_*)/2$. Hoeffding's bound and the same description count suffice, even after summing over $0\le e\le V$. The potentially long ambiguity certificate and the per-address SAT witnesses incur **no description-length penalty in the tail**. All certificates for the same bound and pair refer to precisely the same accept sets. An untrusted assertion of disjointness cannot be substituted into the coNP density test. Two always-accepting branches supply every desired label on every string.

The existing output-explicit canonical protocol may use a larger formula or far longer common random tape than the $o(V)$ description budget; the bitwise generator time bound can also exceed $M$. No short predictor for the resulting tables follows from the protocol. A candidate allowing only a different short positive certificate at each pin still needs sound opposite labels, or it incurs the refutation lower bound above.
