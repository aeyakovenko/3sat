# Near-linear circuit size for register histories of scan-time SAT computations

There are explicit satisfiable and unsatisfiable 3-CNF families on which essentially all word orders force $\Omega(N/\log N)$ gates to return the actual controller and registers of any timely correct solver, given just the time index. **One fixed correct solver decides all these orders in $O(N/\log N)$ word steps.** Its register history on the hard orders requires $\Theta(N/\log N)$ gates. The result is unconditional. It supplies a counterexample to inferring a superlinear running time from even this strong per-input history lower bound.

Fix any polynomial cutoff $T(N)=\lceil C N^a\rceil$, any fixed finite bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit basis including AND, OR and NOT, and the aligned read-only load/store RAM of the word-permutation construction. Use a fixed number of registers and a fixed-size instruction record. Each input load places the full returned word in a register. A monitor is sound for sign $b$ if it signals $b$ only when the physical 3-CNF has that verdict. The register table records the controller through its signal and idles thereafter up to $T$. A circuit is specific to this history, takes only a time index, and receives no copy of the formula as a free input.

## Forcing every word to be read

Choose any sufficiently large integer $\ell$, $B=\lfloor 2^\ell/(16\ell)\rfloor$ and the explicit layout with $4B+9$ clauses and word width $12\ell$. Each of the $B$ movable words contains one complete logical clause, an identifying tautology, a filler tautology and identical guard fragments. The total bit length satisfies $N=12\ell B+O(\ell)$ and $\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil=\ell$. A word order $\pi$ is any permutation of the $B$ distinct source tags. Use the same layout and the same order for signs zero and one. [Packing proof](word-permutation-positive-histories.md#making-a-physical-word-movable)

For the positive family use the unit clause $x_1$ in every movable word. For the negative family use the $B$ clauses of the path contradiction
$$x_1,\quad (\neg x_i\lor x_{i+1})\ (1\le i\le B-2),\quad \neg x_{B-1}.$$
Repeat literals to give width three. In the positive family replacing just one logical clause by the opposite unit produces a contradiction when $B\ge2$. In the negative family replacing any one logical clause by a tautology makes the conjunction satisfiable. Removing the first or last unit allows a constant assignment. Removing an implication allows ones through its source and zeros thereafter. These modifications remain valid formulas of the same length, and every changed bit belongs to the one aligned word.

If a deterministic sound sign monitor signals without loading some movable word, this modified input gives it precisely the same run and signal. Induction on time proves this even for adaptive indirect addressing and unlimited work storage. Thus **every one of the $B$ word positions occurs in its observed input loads before a sound signal**.

Given the fixed program and the actual register table through the signal, enumerate the executed input loads. The address is known from the pre-instruction registers and the returned word from the post-instruction registers. Recover the tag at each destination. This reconstructs the entire permutation, so distinct timely signaling orders yield distinct register tables for a given monitor and sign. No efficiency bound on the reconstruction is needed.

## Counting and matching the gate bound

There are $B!$ orders and $\log_2(B!)=(1/12+o(1))N$. A topologically ordered circuit with at most $s$ gates, $O(\log N)$ time-index input wires and $O(\log N)$ output wires can be specified using
$$ O\big((s+\log N)\log(s+\log N)\big) $$
bits. Specify the type and predecessor indices of each gate and the source of each output wire. This is the classical circuit-counting argument applied to the recovered order. [Shannon, *The Synthesis of Two-Terminal Switching Circuits*](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1949.tb03624.x)

In particular there are constants $\eta,\lambda,c>0$ such that at every sufficiently large scale, all except a $2^{-cN}$ fraction of the permutations have the following simultaneous property. For both signs and all sound programs with at most $\lambda N$ bits of code, **if a signal occurs by $T$, its register table requires more than $\eta N/\log N$ gates**. For fixed program, sign and circuit, injectivity permits at most one order to violate the bound with that circuit. Sum over circuits, both signs and the fewer than $2^{\lambda N+1}$ program strings. Choose $\lambda$ and $\eta$ small enough that the logarithm of the numerator is below $N/12$ by a fixed positive fraction of $N$. Every fixed solver is eventually included. The result concerns the uniform distribution over orders of these two explicit clause lists.

A hard order can also be found without deciding which programs are sound. Enumerate short (program, circuit) descriptions of the above sizes. Evaluate each circuit on the polynomially many time indices, decoding the purported loads from the stated code. A consistent, complete list of tagged reads determines at most one permutation even if the remaining claimed instructions are fictitious. Use a dummy outcome for an invalid list. Include both signs in this enumeration. Its range contains every order excluded by the previous count. For any fixed $\delta>0$, choose $\lambda,\eta$ small enough that the number $M$ of descriptions is at most $2^{\delta N/2}$ and $2M<B!$ eventually. Use fixed-length padded description indices, increasing $M$ to a power of two within a constant factor. In an initial interval of $2M$ factorial ranks, find an empty rank by bisecting the interval. At each step count descriptions hitting each half and retain a half with fewer hits than its size. Recompute by enumeration at each level. This takes $O(M\operatorname{poly}(N))\le2^{\delta N}$ bit time and $O(N)$ working bits for descriptors, table evaluation, seen tags, the permutation, its rank, and the counters. Outputting the resulting formula fits the same bounds. The lower-bound constant may depend on $\delta$. Arithmetic on a rank with $O(N)$ bits can be carried out in polynomial time and linear space.

A fixed solver tests these two families by one scan. Compute the guard phase and tag position, check the syntax and the fixed prefix and suffix. For each tagged word, check its index range, detect repetitions with a directly addressed bit or word table, and keep a flag for whether every word matches the positive template and another for the negative template. Both words are generated from their source tag in constantly many operations. After $B$ distinct tags all slots have appeared. Otherwise fall back to exhaustive SAT decision. The scan costs $O(B)$ native instructions on both entire families, including header processing and initialization. Its lower bound of $B$ input loads is attained to a constant factor.

For completeness, any particular register stream of $O(B)$ steps on this RAM has a time-index circuit of $O(B)$ gates, even if the padded clock is polynomially larger. Extend its meaningful table to a power-of-two height $H=O(B)$, using idle entries. Let $b=2^r$ fall between $(\log_2 H)/4$ and $(\log_2 H)/2$. Precompute all Boolean functions of the low $r$ bits by enumerating their $2^b$ truth tables and OR-ing the chosen low-address indicators. This costs $O(b2^b)$ gates. Generate the $H/b$ high-address indicators in $O(H/b)$ gates by extending binary prefixes in a tree. For each of the $O(\log N)$ output bits, select one precomputed low-address function per high address and combine its masked values, costing $O(H/b)$ gates. A test of the higher time-index bits supplies the final idle controller outside the table. The total is $O(B)$ when $H$ is padded to $\Theta(B)$. Thus the bound on the scan solver is sharp, even though its elapsed time is sublinear in encoded bit length. [Finite checks on the two clause families](../audits/word_permutation_history_audit.py)

## Predicting only the first read of each word

The lower bound also tolerates errors on the actual discovery events. Sample a uniform permutation, fix its sign and a deterministic sound monitor, and ask for the tag just before its first load of each new movable position. The predictor may know the complete previous transcript, including all previously loaded tags, the current registers, address and time. It may use unbounded computation on those data. For a possibly incomplete monitor, extend the experiment after its cutoff by exposing any unread positions in increasing order. Every timely signal has already exposed all positions.

After any such history, conditional on the remaining $j$ tags, the chosen fresh position has a uniform tag from that set. A predictor gains nothing by guessing an already revealed tag. For one fixed total predictor always guessing an unused tag, the conditional probability of a hit is $1/j$, independent of the preceding outcomes. If $K$ counts hits, for integer $B\ge2$ this gives
$$
 \mathbf E[B^K]=\prod_{j=1}^B\left(1+\frac{B-1}{j}\right)
 =\binom{2B-1}{B}\le4^B.
$$
Thus the probability of $r$ or more hits is at most $4^B B^{-r}$. This is the complete-feedback card-guessing law applied to adaptively discovered physical words. [Diaconis and Graham](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/1981-diaconis.pdf) · [Adaptive moment in Lean](../lean/AdaptiveDiscoveryMoment.lean)

A family of at most $2^u$ predictors, from which one may be selected after seeing the actual order, has probability at most $2^{u+2B-r\log_2 B}$ of reaching $r$ hits. In particular predicting a fixed fraction $\alpha B$ of the true tags on a constant fraction of orders needs at least $\alpha B\log_2 B-O(B)$ advice bits for a fixed predictor algorithm. Listing the first $\lceil\alpha B\rceil$ tags of the realized discovery order achieves this many correct predictions with at most $\lceil\alpha B\rceil\lceil\log_2 B\rceil$ bits. This leading rate holds even when the solver chooses the addresses adaptively.

For a time-index circuit, predict the load result from its output at the true next time, using the actual destination register. Treat an invalid tag or one already seen as a miss, or replace it by any unused guess for the upper bound. Every correctly reported post-load register row on a discovery is then a hit. For each fixed $\alpha>0$, union over circuits of $\eta_\alpha N/\log N$ gates, programs of at most $\lambda_\alpha N$ code bits and both signs. Choosing the two constants sufficiently small gives an exceptional fraction $2^{-\Omega_\alpha(N)}$. On all remaining orders each such circuit gets **fewer than $\alpha B$ full discovery rows right** on every timely run. On the scan solver these errors occupy a constant fraction of its entire trajectory. This proof tests the newly obtained input information, which explains why the large error count is attainable at scan time.

The reconstruction depends on observing actual immutable-input load results. A history evaluator with direct queries to the formula gets these words for free.
