# Agreement at every rational rate leaves room for different near-deadline behavior

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** There are two efficient universal prefix machines with the same exact input-to-output map and the following properties. For every positive rational $q$, their shortest description lengths by time $2^{\lfloor qm\rfloor}$ agree on every word of every sufficiently large length $m$. At every rational rate in $(3,4)$ their sums of description-weight improvements in the last polynomial factor of the clock can nevertheless differ at every odd saving. On the first machine every such sum is Martin-Löf random. On the second each has finite support. A related difference in the actual shortest programs holds at almost every fixed real rate in $(3,4)$. The construction is unconditional. The threshold length for agreement may depend on the rational rate.

Put $T_x(m)=2^{\lfloor xm\rfloor}$, $t_{x,a}(m)=\max(1,\lfloor T_x(m)/m^a\rfloor)$, and use the exact-saving columns of the [integer-rate construction](programmable-clock-spectrum.md). Fix $B\ge2$ and an enumerable family $W_{e,d}$, setting $S_e=\{d\ge1:B\mid d\text{ or }W_{e,d}\text{ is infinite}\}$. The constructed machines $U_e$ have the following properties.

- At each rational $x\in(3,4)$ and simultaneously at almost every real $x\in(3,4)$, every $d\in S_e$ has infinitely many words with $K^{U_e}(y)=K^{U_e}_{T_x}(y)=m-d$ for which the first program of that length finishes in $[cT_x,T_x]$, with one constant $c>0$.
- At each such rational and almost every such real, for every $d\notin S_e$ and every integer $a\ge1$ only finitely many words of saving $d$ give a positive improvement from $t_{x,a}$ to $T_x$.
- The full-measure conclusion concerns words at the indicated deadlines. At each rational rate the terms are computable, so the first assertion also gives a Martin-Löf random exact-saving sum for every fixed $a\ge1$. The example in the opening takes $B=2$ with one everywhere-infinite and one everywhere-empty enumeration.

## Where to put the earlier release

Keep the common generic residue branches behind the same long prefix. On the exceptional branch a name $q$ specifies $d$ and one of two kinds of target, either a dyadic interval $I$ with closure in $(3,4)$, or a reduced rational $x_0\in(3,4)$. The name $p$ runs on the condition consisting of this datum and $k=|q|$. Use the same injective record code, $m=2^r$, $h=1+k+|p|$ and $\ell=m-d-h$ free bits. Choose $r$ also at least the dyadic depth or the rational denominator, as appropriate. The output is $0^{d+h}$ followed by all $\ell$ payload bits. Selection uses the same test on $W_{e,d}$ by the runtime of $p$. All branches release the same output eventually for every $e$.

At length $m$ form a partition of $[3,4]$ by all rationals there of denominator at most $r$, including the endpoints. There are $O(r^2)$ cut points, consecutive ones are at least $1/r^2$ apart, and the partition can be generated and sorted in time polynomial in $m$. To request release at an exponent $E$, perform the binary-tree delay from the integer-rate construction, with depth $\lfloor E\rfloor$ minus a fixed constant. Including preprocessing, the completion time at large lengths lies in $[c_1 2^{\lfloor E\rfloor},2^{\lfloor E\rfloor}]$ uniformly for a constant $c_1>0$. All exponents used here exceed $2m$ eventually.

For an interval target of depth $b$, split $I$ into cells of width $1/m$. The first $r-b$ bits of the payload choose the cell with left endpoint $z$ and the remaining bits distinguish its outputs. Every cell has the same number of payloads. Let $u$ be the midpoint of the coarse partition interval containing $z$, using the interval on the right at an exact cut. Request release as follows.

- For a selected tuple whose $z$ is farther than $r^2/m$ from every coarse cut, use exponent $zm$.
- For every other interval payload, use exponent $um$.

For a rational target, the designated $x_0$ is itself a cut. Request release at exponent $x_0m$ if selected and $x_0m-\lceil\sqrt m\rceil$ otherwise. Since $m/r^2$ eventually dominates $\sqrt m$, both completions fall strictly after the deadline at the preceding cut, and by the deadline at $x_0$. For interval targets both completions fall strictly inside the same pair of coarse deadlines: midpoint exponents have a margin of at least $m/(2r^2)$, and selected fine exponents used directly have a margin of $r^2$. These bounds absorb floors, setup, and the fixed log delay constant. Every fixed rational in $[3,4]$ eventually occurs among the cuts. Outside this rate interval all exceptional names in either variant have the same timely status for large lengths. The generic part is identical. This proves the claimed pointwise agreement of all large-length outer complexities on every fixed rational cut; in fact, the timely status of **each individual name** agrees there.

## Tight and finite columns

For a true $d$ and one fixed target, choose $q$ shortest and use arbitrarily late conditional runtime records $p$. As in the integer-rate proof, $K(m)\ge k+|p|-O(1)$ uniformly, all sufficiently late records are selected, and increasing the common generic header by a constant makes at most a quarter of the exceptional outputs have a competing generic name of length at most $m-d$. The other outputs have just their own exceptional name at that length and no shorter one. For the fixed rational target $x_0$ these clean outputs give the required constant-factor witnesses directly.

For a fixed interval target, at least three quarters of its $1/m$ cells contain a clean output because the payload counts are equal. At most $O(r^4)$ cells in all of $[3,4]$ have their left endpoint within $r^2/m$ of a coarse cut. This is a vanishing fraction of the $m|I|$ cells for fixed $I$. At each sufficiently late record at least half of $I$ therefore consists of cells with a clean output released at exponent $zm$. Throughout one such cell $T_x$ differs from $2^{zm}$ by at most a factor of two. Taking the limsup over the record lengths, at least half of $I$ has infinitely many witnesses. This holds with the same fraction for every dyadic $I$ with closure in $(3,4)$. If the complement had positive measure it would have a dyadic density point there, contradicting the half-interval estimate. Intersect the resulting full-measure sets over $d\in S_e$.

For a false $d$, a timely shortest name in its column must be exceptional because $B\nmid d$. Only finitely many exceptional tuples of this $d$ are selected, even allowing the target to vary, since $k+|p|\le |W_{e,d}|$ for every selected one. At length $2^r$ all other possible publication exponents belong to an $O(r^2)$-element list: the coarse midpoints times $m$ and the rational cut points times $m$ minus $\lceil\sqrt m\rceil$. The bounded log timing error enlarges the list to intervals of constant width. For fixed $a$, a positive term requires some listed time to lie in $(t_{x,a},T_x]$. The set of rates where this can happen at that length has measure $O((1+a)r^3 2^{-r})$. The sum over $r$ is finite. By the first Borel–Cantelli lemma almost every rate meets only finitely many of these events, simultaneously for all integer $a\ge1$. At a given rational rate the same conclusion holds directly: it eventually is a cut, midpoints stay at least $m/(2r^2)$ away in the exponent, other cuts are at least $m/r^2$ away before the $\sqrt m$ shift, and its own shifted time precedes the early deadline. This proves the finite-support assertion.

An exact rational-arithmetic experiment checks both ends of each coarse interval at six finite values of $r$ from 18 through 64. It tests the delayed exponents and counts how many fine cells the rational-boundary margin excludes. The asymptotic and measure assertions above use the manuscript proof. [Finite experiment](../audits/rational_clock_experiment.py)

The finite exceptional support can address every odd saving on one machine, but the even savings still meet the [bounded-gap condition implied by linear SAT](exact-saving-blocks.md). The construction gives no unrestricted SAT time lower bound. Results on randomness of a single time-bounded complexity sum and on logical depth provide related precedents; priority for this particular simultaneous timing construction is unestablished.
