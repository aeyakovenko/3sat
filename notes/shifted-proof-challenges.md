# Fresh challenges at each timely rejection

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Can a solver recover the bits of a proof of its own rejection approximately, with advice specialized to the single rejected formula? A shared translation of the verifier coins makes this question testable on every rejected member of the self-referential family. With a constant-query proximity verifier the errors also have a public, efficiently samplable distribution that is the same for every formula at its scale. [Underlying pair language, compiler and sampling bound](exact-sampled-verdicts.md)

## A threshold on every rejected instance

Use the pair language, bounded simulation and locally generated 3-CNF in the covering construction of the linked note. For clarity, at scale $k$ the monitor has $H=2^k$ steps, the decoder has a shared advice string $z$ of $s=2^{\lfloor k/4\rfloor}$ bits, and the public raw array $R$ consists of $d=s k^C$ challenges of $b=k^2$ bits each. Write $K=db$. Every decoded proof coordinate is a deterministic function of its address, $z$, $R$ and the actual physical formula, computed in $k^{O(1)}$ time from fresh scratch. Finite program text and any fixed polynomial clock are chosen before constructing the family. The true implicit inputs to the pair language at the chosen explicit monitor program are exactly the arrays on which the monitored SAT solver signals rejection within its clock. The clock covers an $O(N^e\operatorname{polylog} N)$ solver at encoded formula length $N=s k^{O(1)}$ for each fixed $e<4$.

Choose a PCPP with perfect completeness and fresh soundness $\sigma=1/4$ outside its proximity radius. On the fixed physical input $R$, let $v_{R,z}(r)$ indicate whether its verifier accepts coins $r$ against the proof decoded from $z$. Modify the ordinary formula to guess a second string $\tau$ of $b$ bits. Accept if the empirical score of $v_{R,z}(R_i\mathbin\oplus\tau)$ is at least $\theta=1/2$. All rows use the same $\tau$. Define the decoded proof on the original, fixed formula and raw input. Its oracle receives no guessed assignment to the shift variables. The raw array is never translated as data, and row ordinals stay private. An extra $O(db)$ Boolean gates suffice for the translations before the fixed checker. The new template is what the monitored solver receives.

For each fixed $R,z$, even if $R$ is adversarial,

$$
 2^{-b}\sum_{\tau}\frac1d\sum_{i=1}^d v_{R,z}(R_i\oplus\tau)
 = 2^{-b}\sum_r v_{R,z}(r) = p_z(R).
$$

Each row sees every coin string exactly once. Thus on **every** timely rejection $R$ of a correct solver, every $z$ has $p_z(R)<1/2$. Indeed a decoder with $p_z(R)\ge 1/2$ would supply the advice for a satisfying assignment by choosing a shift at least as good as its mean. The inequality is pointwise even on the exceptional arrays of the sampling theorem. For comparison, on a constant batch all unshifted tests can fail while fresh acceptance is $1-2^{-b}$. For that batch any preselected family of shifts whose cardinality is at most $2^{b-1}$ also misses some Boolean predicate of density at least $1/2$. The quantification over all shifts costs only $b$ witness bits. Translation averaging is classical. [Lautemann](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3)

The same physical modification still produces many negative inputs under a hypothetical fast solver. For fixed $z,\tau$ apply the ordinal-blind moment inequality to $w^R(r)=v_{R,z}(r\oplus\tau)$. Its fresh mean is $p_z(R)$ and its probe bound is that of the original checker. Union over $2^{s+b}$ pairs. Taking $C$ sufficiently large gives empirical error less than $1/6$ for every pair except on at most $2^{-k}$ of the arrays. Denote the timely rejection arrays by $A$, the unsatisfiable timeouts by $B$, and the proximity fraction by $\rho$. Outside the Hamming radius $\lfloor\rho K\rfloor$ of $A$, every decoded proof has fresh acceptance at most $1/4$. Hence every sampling-good array there yields an unsatisfiable formula. Such arrays belong to $B$. (With an integer boundary convention one may use the ceiling radius.) Consequently, with $V(K,j)=\sum_{i\le j}\binom Ki$,

$$
 |B|+|A| V(K,\lceil\rho K\rceil)\ \ge\ (1-2^{-k})2^K.
$$

Using the small-radius verifier of Theorem 2.5 gives the earlier near-full entropy exponent for $A$ when $B$ is empty. Alternatively, Theorem 2.6 with fixed $\epsilon=1/4$ and any fixed $0<\rho<1/2$ gives a bound of $Q=O(1)$ on the **total** number of binary oracle queries, $O(k)$ coin bits and $k^{O(1)}$ verification time. This gives $|A|\ge 2^{(1-H_2(\rho)-o(1))K}$ when $B$ is empty, where $H_2$ is binary entropy. Query constants can depend on $\rho$. Both verifiers fit the same asymptotic physical clocks. One fixes the verifier first. [Ben-Sasson, Goldreich, Harsha, Sudan and Vadhan, Theorems 2.5 and 2.6](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/2005/bghsv2-full.pdf)

## One public distribution for all the proof tables

Choose the constant-query verifier. There is a single distribution $\mu_k$ on proof addresses at scale $k$ such that, for **every** $R\in A$, **every** perfectly accepting proof $\pi$ for this true timely rejection and **every** candidate decoder header $z$,

$$
 \Pr_{j\sim\mu_k}[D^{R}_z(j)\ne\pi(j)]\ \ge\ \eta,
 \qquad \eta=\frac{1-\theta}{Q2^Q}.
$$

Generate fresh verifier coins and $Q$ independent fair answers. Run the verifier, supplying the next fair answer for *every* oracle query, including queries to the raw implicit input. Independently choose one of the $Q$ query slots uniformly. Output its address if it is a proof query, otherwise output a fixed default proof address. A uniform slot can be drawn exactly with expected constant time or by rounding $Q$ up to a power of two and padding. This sampler uses $k^{O(1)}$ time, and depends on the fixed public program and the lengths only. In particular it has no access to $R$ or $\pi$.

To prove the claim, fix $R,\pi,z$. The honest verifier always accepts. For more than $1-\theta$ of the fresh coins the verifier with the decoded proof rejects. On each such coin string some proof answer on the honest path must differ, by deterministic transcript induction with the raw input fixed. Conditional on these coins, the guessed answer tape agrees with the entire honest trajectory with probability at least $2^{-Q}$, even if some addresses repeat. An independent uniform slot hits a differing proof query with probability at least $1/Q$. This proves the bound simultaneously in the sense of one common distribution. Simulation with guessed replies also bounds the support by $2^{r+Q}Q+1$, where $r=O(k)$ is the number of actual verifier coins. It does not require uniform sampling from the inverse image of an address.

## Predicting parity on publicly sampled addresses

Fix $a>0$ and a deterministic, polynomial-in-$k$ time predictor for the parity of $t$ proof bits with arbitrary postselected advice and physical-input access as above. The compiler can choose its original single-address decoder to evaluate a majority of suitably restricted copies of this predictor. For $t=O_{a,Q}(\log k)$ there are fixed constants $C'$ and $k_0$ such that, for every $k\ge k_0$, every timely rejection, every perfect proof, and every predictor advice of length at most $s/k^{C'}$, success on $t$ independent addresses from $\mu_k$ is at most $1/2+k^{-a}$. The predictor sees the addresses, the physical formula and the raw array. It receives no private random coins used inside the address sampler. The construction is made for the stated predictor or a fixed clocked universal interpreter. The fixed PCPP for the universal pair language precedes the choice of the predictor wrapper. The result applies to that new family of physical formulas.

Here are the finite hardness-amplification details. Interpret signs as $\pm1$ and let $f$ be the true sign at an address. Allow as weak functions each restriction of the predictor in which $t-1$ input addresses, their position, its advice and an output sign are fixed arbitrarily. This class is closed under negation. Its members need the predictor header plus $k^{O(1)}$ overhead for addresses. Make the single-address decoder from the preceding argument a majority of $L$ such independently advised functions. For $\gamma=k^{-a}$ choose $L=O(\gamma^{-2}\log(4/\eta))$ odd, large enough for the following test. All $L$ headers fit in $s$ by choosing $C'$ large enough, and the wrapper is polynomial-time per address.

There exists a distribution $\nu\le 2\mu_k/\eta$ pointwise such that $|\mathbb E_\nu[f g]|\le\gamma$ for every weak $g$. Otherwise, by sign symmetry and finite minimax, a mixture of weak functions has expected correlation strictly greater than $\gamma$ against every such distribution. On all but $\eta/2$ of the $\mu_k$ mass its pointwise mean margin must exceed $\gamma/2$, since otherwise the conditional distribution on the low-margin set is an admissible $\nu$. A majority of $L$ independent samples from the mixture has expected total error less than $\eta$ by Hoeffding's inequality. Some such majority contradicts the address bound.

Write $\mu_k=(\eta/2)\nu+(1-\eta/2)\nu_0$. In a product draw, condition on the component choices. Whenever a $\nu$ coordinate exists, fix all other addresses and use the first such coordinate. The predictor's correlation with parity has absolute value at most $\gamma$, by the restriction property and the constant sign contributed by the other coordinates. The probability of having no such coordinate is $(1-\eta/2)^t$. Choose $t$ so that this is at most $\gamma$. Correlation is then at most $2\gamma$, which proves the stated success bound. This is the hard-core proof of the XOR lemma applied to an explicit distribution arising from the verifier. [Impagliazzo](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1995.492584) · [Goldreich, Nisan and Wigderson](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~/oded/pp_pseudo.html)

## Attempt to get the decoder from SAT search

The candidate implication would assign every true timely rejection one proof with a specialized $s$-bit description accepted on half the fresh challenges. This weaker hypothesis already suffices for a contradiction at a fast clock. If it held, there would be no arrays in $A$, and the covering bound would force unsatisfiable timeouts. Ordinary self-reduction on the explicit conjunction of all proof checks can fix a lexicographically first perfect proof coordinate by coordinate. Its later decisions depend on the already fixed prefix, and the construction gives no $k^{O(1)}$-time way to obtain a queried bit from $s$ shared bits. Even one call to a hypothetical $O(N)$ SAT solver on a large prefix instance has only a linear upper bound in that instance's entire length. Thus this route leaves the required fresh-acceptance implication unproved.

## Allowing a separate nondeterministic hint on every lookup

A proof-coordinate decoder need not find the bit in polynomial time itself. Fix a polynomial-time certificate checker taking the usual address, short header and physical inputs, a claimed bit, and a fresh certificate of $k^{O(1)}$ bits. Assume only **single-valuedness**: for any fixed raw array, header and address, certificates for opposite bits cannot both pass. A bit may have many certificates. An address may have none. A fresh PCPP test counts as certifiably accepted if it has an accepting execution with a valid certificate at every proof lookup. The shift construction and covering theorem still hold, with the bound $p_z(R)<1/2$ at every timely rejection now applying to this certifiable fresh acceptance probability. The formula guesses the short header and shift once, and a separate set of bounded certificates for every row; there is no cost of finding them. Any fixed collection of the certifiably accepting rows can be witnessed simultaneously.

The sampling estimate requires care because nondeterministic hints could reveal the ordinal of a row in the raw batch. Bound by $h_i$ the number of distinct raw symbols read to validate bit $i\in\{0,1\}$, including generation of physical formula words. Fix an address and header. Each accepting transcript defines a cylinder specifying the observed symbols. The two finite families of accepting cylinders for opposite bits are pairwise incompatible across families by the single-valuedness assumption on *all* raw arrays. There is a deterministic function extending every certified answer, computable with at most $h_0h_1$ raw probes. Its local computation can be unbounded.

Indeed, repeat at most $h_1$ times. If no zero-cylinder is compatible with observations, return one. Otherwise select a compatible zero-cylinder by a fixed rule, query all its symbols, and return zero if it matches. Against every still-compatible one-cylinder, each failed round probes at least one of its previously unqueried positions. The selected zero-cylinder and that one-cylinder specify different values at some common position, which could not have been previously read while both were compatible. Hence every one-cylinder still compatible after $h_1$ rounds has all its entries read. Return one if such a cylinder exists and zero otherwise. These rules are correct also when one family is empty. This is the certificate-complexity argument with an arbitrary completion on uncertified inputs. The disjointness of accepting cylinders is stronger than a promise allowing contradictory certificates on raw arrays outside a selected promise. [Certificate complexity and decision trees](https://doi.org/10.1137/0220062)

Use this completion separately at every proof address in the **analysis** of the far inputs. For fixed header it always induces one genuine proof oracle on any fixed raw array. Its bounded-probe fresh acceptance is therefore small on far inputs, and the ordinal-blind moment theorem applies to the completed verifier regardless of the time needed to select a compatible cylinder. Every physically certified accepting run follows the completed verifier, so the same empirical upper bound applies to the physical formula. On a timely rejection, apply translation averaging directly to the certifiably accepting coin strings. Thus the pointwise fresh upper bound concerns the fraction of coins on which all needed certificates actually exist. At a rejection the arbitrarily completed oracle can still pass every fresh test while certifying none. A contradiction would require honest local certificates on a threshold fraction of the coins. The proof uses no assertion that empirical acceptance estimates the fresh certification domain. The public-address conclusion similarly charges an absent certificate or an incorrect answer on an honest query. This removes the need for an efficient deterministic per-bit proof generator. A successful attack would suffice with locally checkable, mutually consistent certificates of the right bit on enough verifier runs. Constructing these for unrestricted SAT solver rejections is open here.

Under the **linear SAT hypothesis**, one can also screen guessed headers whose exclusivity is semantic. The defect for $z$ asserts existence of an address, two opposite acceptable certificates, and a single raw array on which both pass. Guess the two short validation runs and their raw replies. Enforcing equality at repeated raw addresses suffices for one joint array to exist, including for adaptive runs. A bounded checker with access to the $s$ header bits thus has an ordinary defect CNF of length $s k^{O(1)}$. Simulate the hypothesized correct near-linear solver on this defect once per outer formula, and allow the chosen header only if its defect is rejected within the specified $s k^{O(1)}$ clock. The extra deterministic bounded simulation is encoded once with length $s k^{O(1)}$. Its own input-word generator uses the guessed header and the fixed checker template; the defect quantifies an independent raw array. The outer shape depends only on the dimensions, including on headers it will exclude. Correctness of the solver excludes every defective header, and the fast time hypothesis admits every defect-free one at a sufficiently large polynomial clock. The far-array union bound can consequently range over the globally exclusive headers of this scale. This variant permits searching for individually valid headers in a larger syntactic format. The honest header must still validate enough true proof accesses.

## A linear decision problem with hard approximate proofs

A generic implication from linear-time decision and short PCP checks to approximate local proofs has a concrete counterexample. Take an implicit array $P$ of $M=2^m$ words of $m$ bits. The language of permutation arrays has an $O(M)$-word-step decider using a bitmap and unrestricted work memory. A verifier requests the $m$ proof bits giving the preimage of a random range value $y$ and tests $P[\mathrm{preimage}]=y$. The genuine inverse passes every test. If the array is farther than relative bit distance $\rho$ from every permutation, at least $\rho M$ values are missing, since replacing one duplicate for each missing value repairs the array at a cost of at most $m$ bit changes per edit. A fixed number of independent repetitions gives soundness $1/4$ for any fixed $\rho>0$. Both verification time and number of bit queries are polynomial in $m$.

For a random permutation, consider any fixed forward-probe predictor of an inverse word with $s$ postselected advice bits and at most $q$ array reads in total across its output bits. Choose $j$ distinct random targets independent of the permutation. Run the predictor at each target with one fixed advice, and inspect the proposed preimage to validate its answer. Pad the combined distinct forward probes to $Q'=j(q+1)\le M$. Condition on the targets. For a uniform permutation the values at these successively exposed positions form a uniform sample without replacement. Total success implies that all $j$ specified targets occur among those $Q'$ values, of probability at most $(Q'/M)^j$. On a permutation with success fraction at least $\epsilon$, all targets succeed with probability at least $(\epsilon/2)^j$ for $j\le\epsilon M/2$. Consequently

$$
 \Pr_P[\exists z:\text{ inversion success at least }\epsilon]
 \le 2^s\left(\frac{2j(q+1)}{\epsilon M}\right)^j.
$$

Taking $j=\lfloor\epsilon M/[8(q+1)]\rfloor$ bounds the probability by $2^{s-2j}$ whenever $j\ge1$. For fixed $\epsilon=1/2$, if $(s+1)(q+1)=o(M)$ it tends to zero. This simple batch argument lies in the classical permutation-inversion-with-advice setting. Even a separately optimized proof description for each input fails on most true permutation inputs for this verifier. It remains possible to exploit the structure of the particular verifier for RAM rejections, or to replace it. [De, Trevisan and Tulsiani](https://www.ias.edu/sites/default/files/math/De_Trevisan_Tulsiani.pdf)

The obstruction also admits **constant binary query complexity**. Let $E$ be a systematic binary linear code encoding an $m$-bit word in $L=O(m^2)$ bits with relative distance $c\ge1/4$. One explicit choice represents the bits as coefficients of a polynomial of degree less than $m$ over a binary field of size $4m\le u<8m$. For each field point use the binary inner products of the evaluation with every field element, and append the $m$ systematic bits. Distinct polynomials disagree on more than three quarters of points, and distinct evaluation symbols disagree on half their inner products. The $u^2+m$ total coordinates have the claimed distance. Encode each value of the forward permutation separately. The new decision problem, on $K=ML$ implicit bits, asks whether the blocks encode a permutation. It still has an $O(K)$ deterministic RAM decider with unrestricted memory. Generate the $m$ generator codewords in $\operatorname{poly}(m)$ time; generate all $M$ codewords from them in $O(ML)$ word operations using $E(2^i+j)=E(2^i)\oplus E(j)$ for $0\le j<2^i$, representing integers by their bits. Compare each block with the codeword selected by its systematic bits and check a bitmap of selected values. This bound counts constructing the entire lookup table.

Interpret the first $K$ proposed proof bits as an array $Z$. Run the constant-query verifier from Theorem 2.6 on the virtual implicit input $X\Vert Z$ for the relation of pairs $(E(p),E(p^{-1}))$ of encoded permutations and inverses, using the rest of the proposed proof as its PCPP oracle. Here $E$ acts blockwise. The relation is in deterministic polynomial time with explicit parameter $m$. Use constant proximity $\delta<c/24$ and soundness $1/4$. Correct inputs have a perfect proof. Inputs $X$ farther than $2\delta$ from the coded permutation language give virtual inputs farther than $\delta$ from this graph for every $Z$. The new verifier makes constantly many binary queries overall and takes $\operatorname{poly}(m)$ time. [BGHSV, Theorem 2.6](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/2005/bghsv2-full.pdf)

On the genuine input $X=E(p)$, any proof with fresh acceptance above $1/4$ makes $(X,Z)$ at most $2\delta ML$ bit edits from $(E(p'),E(p'^{-1}))$ for some permutation $p'$. Then $p$ differs from $p'$ on at most $2\delta M/c$ words, and their inverses differ on exactly the same number. Decoding each block of $Z$ to a nearest codeword makes at most $4\delta M/c$ errors against $p'^{-1}$, since an incorrectly decoded block differs from its intended codeword on at least $cL/2$ bits. Reading the $L$ candidate bits at a target therefore gives the true inverse on more than half the targets. The inversion lower bound allows unlimited computation between reads, so enumerating codewords for this last decoding suffices. Each raw coded-bit probe can be served by one probe to the unencoded permutation. If the proposed proof-bit decoder uses at most $q$ raw probes per coordinate, the forward inversion algorithm uses at most $Lq$ probes and the same advice. Thus whenever $(s+1)(Lq+1)=o(M)$, almost all genuine inputs lack **even one** such description accepted on more than a quarter of fresh challenges. The bound also covers the single-valued nondeterministic local validators above with $q=h_0h_1$ raw probes for their completions, because certifiable acceptance implies acceptance of the completed proof and the inversion bound allows unlimited local computation. This verifier has a fast decision problem and exactly the constant-query, perfect-completeness and public sampling parameters used above. An argument for the RAM rejection verifier has to use more than these parameters.

The same distinction can be witnessed at an actual SAT rejection if the verifier may be chosen adversarially. Use formulas with a fixed pair of contradictory unit clauses at the front (or duplicated literals in width three) and a suffix containing the raw bits at tautological pins. A globally correct solver can reject upon recognizing this prefix in a constant number of packed word reads for a fixed header layout, with a general SAT fallback. To impose a proof obligation on *all* raw strings, decode each of their $M$ blocks to a nearest codeword, with fixed tie-breaking. For each range value require the encoded least index with that decoded value, defaulting to zero when missing. This defines a total auxiliary relation, decidable in polynomial time even by exhaustive nearest-word search. Include the relation and the timely rejection claim in the virtual-input PCPP. At a genuinely encoded random permutation, proximity within $2\delta ML$ bits can change at most $4\delta M/c$ decoded raw words. Altering $u$ words can affect at most $2u$ range values' canonical replies, namely the old and new values in the altered positions. Including errors in decoding $Z$ gives at most $12\delta M/c$ wrong inverse answers. Taking $\delta<c/24$ forces inversion on more than half the range whenever fresh acceptance exceeds $1/4$. This example chooses a verifier with a redundant obligation. It establishes a limit on an argument meant to work uniformly over verifiers, while leaving room to exploit the actual universal-language verifier used in the diagonal.

The pointwise translation, common distribution and parity conclusions concern proof readers of actual timely rejections. The unrestricted SAT time goals, including linear time, are open here. Priority of this particular application is unverified.
