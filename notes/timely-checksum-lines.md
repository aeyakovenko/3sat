# Recovering an isolated proof from timely exact checksum decisions

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Suppose the two SAT formulas for a checksum of one isolated proof have complementary verdicts. If an exact solver finishes on either pin, its answer gives a correct checksum bit. Erasing both answers when both runs time out leaves a partial system of linear equations. A local reconstruction from density $\theta$ of these equations works over a binary extension field of size $O((D+1)/\theta)$, where $D$ is the total degree of the grid extension. This gives a time tail for both pins simultaneously. Its application to SAT uses the conditional random-tape witness bound. [Random-tape witness theorem](random-tape-decision-witnesses.md)

### Exact erasures on affine lines

Let $F=\mathbb F_{2^s}$, $Q=|F|$, and fix its binary coordinates. Let $P:F^w\to F$ be a polynomial of total degree at most $D$, with $w\ge1$. At $(z,t)\in F^w\times F$, an oracle returns the binary inner product $\langle t,P(z)\rangle$ or an erasure. Its answers and its erasures are deterministic. Let $E_z$ be the set of nonerased masks. Suppose $\mathbb E_z |E_z|/Q\ge\theta>0$ and $Q\ge512(D+1)/\theta$.

There exist an anchor $a\in F^w$ and its correct value $P(a)$ such that a deterministic oracle algorithm with these parameters agrees with $P$ except on at most $1/128$ of the domain. With additional advice of $O(ws\log(2+v))$ bits, a deterministic algorithm returns $P$ on all of any designated $v$ points. Time and oracle calls per evaluation are polynomial in $Q,w,\log(2+v)$. The parameter choices can depend on the entire partial oracle and message.

On an affine line $L$, choose a coordinate $u\in F$ along the line. Query all $Q$ masks at each of its $Q$ points and impose the returned equations on the binary coefficients of a univariate polynomial $p(u)$ of degree at most $D$. Evaluation at a known $u$ is binary linear in these $(D+1)s$ bits. The resulting affine solution space $S_L$ contains the true restriction. Changing the affine coordinate preserves the solution functions on $L$. Write $\theta_L$ for the average of $|E_z|/Q$ over $L$. Then

$$
\theta_L\ \le\ \frac{1}{|S_L|}+
  \left(1-\frac{1}{|S_L|}\right)\frac{D}{Q}.
$$

Indeed, all candidate values at $z$ lie in one coset of the annihilator of the span of $E_z$. This coset has size $Q/2^{\operatorname{rank}E_z}$. For independent uniform candidates, the probability of equal values at $z$ is at least $2^{\operatorname{rank}E_z}/Q\ge |E_z|/Q$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Averaging gives the lower bound. Equal polynomials collide everywhere, and distinct members collide at at most $D$ line points. This gives the upper bound. Related global bounds for Reed–Solomon codes concatenated with Hadamard codes are classical. [Guruswami and Sudan](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/2000/concat-full.pdf)

Let $\mu=\mathbb E_z |E_z|/Q$. For a uniform affine line $L$, $\mathbb E\theta_L=\mu$ and $\operatorname{Var}(\theta_L)\le\mu/Q$. To see this, give the line a uniform affine parametrization. Values at two distinct parameters are sampled at a uniform ordered pair of distinct domain points, so their covariance is nonpositive. Each summand has variance at most $\mu$. Therefore $\Pr[\theta_L<\theta/2]\le4/(Q\theta)$. On every remaining line $|S_L|\le4/\theta$.

Choose an anchor $a$ and target $x$ independently uniformly. If $x=a$, return the advised value. Conditioned on $x\ne a$, the line through them is uniform, and on each such line $a$ is uniform and $x$ is uniform among its other points. Against the true restriction, the union of root sets of all false candidates on a good line has size at most $4D/\theta$. Thus with probability at least $1-4(D+1)/(Q\theta)$, pinning $p(a)=P(a)$ leaves only the true restriction or the target equals the anchor. Here the lower bound counts success from good lines and from $x=a$. The decoder uses Gaussian elimination and returns the unique polynomial's value when the system has a unique solution. It returns zero on an inconsistent or ambiguous system. Averaging gives a fixed correct anchor and pin with error at most $1/128$. Random anchors and line self-correction also occur in the Sudan–Trevisan–Vadhan reconstruction. [Sudan, Trevisan and Vadhan, Theorems 29 and 32](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/1999/stv-journ.pdf)

For any requested point $y$, draw a uniform $d\in F^w$ and query this deterministic approximation at $y+ud$ for all $u\in F$. For $u\ne0$, the point is uniform, so the expected number of errors is at most $1+(Q-1)/128$. With probability greater than $2/3$, there are at most $e=\lfloor Q/4\rfloor$ errors by Markov's inequality. This argument allows $d=0$. Decode the true degree-$D$ restriction and read its value at zero. For completeness, given replies $h_u$, solve $B(u)=A(u)h_u$ for monic $A$ of degree $e$ and $\deg B\le D+e$. With at most $e$ errors one can take an error locator multiplied up to degree $e$. Every solution has $B=A p$ as the difference vanishes at $Q-e>D+e$ points. On failure to divide with degree at most $D$ or to solve, output zero. Repeat for $O(\log(2+v))$ independent directions and take a majority, with a fixed default if none. A union bound gives one common direction list correct on the designated points. Arbitrary false advice still defines a total deterministic reader. In the PCP application soundness applies to every table such a reader produces.

### Consequence for time on the two pins

Fix the short input, public hash tape $R$ and level of the isolated proof construction, and condition on a unique surviving proof $\pi$. Put its bits on a specified grid $A^w\subset F^w$, $|A|=a$, padding unused entries with zeros. Its tensor Lagrange interpolant $P$ has individual degrees $<a$ and total degree $D=w(a-1)$. For each $(z,t,c)$ use an ordinary SAT formula requiring a surviving proof with $\langle t,P(z)\rangle=c$. The circuit computes one value by tensor interpolation, costing $a^w\operatorname{poly}(r)$ when $Q=\operatorname{poly}(r)$, $r=\log_2 H$. A static bounded computation encoding supplies each requested formula word from the short input, hash tape and checksum parameters in $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ time. Its input records contain grid or verifier parameters, the tape and the checksum request. This permits a reader to simulate the two fresh solver invocations without explicitly constructing the whole formula on each call. [Local formula-word construction](random-tape-decision-witnesses.md)

Run both pins with a fixed cutoff $q$ and with one shared advice string if the reader is advised. Whenever a timely answer is available it determines the inner product, since precisely one pin is satisfiable. For an exact solver the returned bit is necessarily correct. The erasure lemma applies if either pin finishes by $q$ on at least a $\theta$ fraction of uniform $(z,t)$. It yields a reader for all $v$ proof coordinates from that same $R$, with $b+\operatorname{poly}(r)$ advice bits and $(q+1)\operatorname{poly}(r)$ time per bit if the original advice length was $b$. Only the advice producing exact timely answers on the isolated positive instance is needed for completeness.

Fix $d\in\{1,2,3\}$ and the reference SAT-time assumption of the random-tape theorem. For any fixed $k>0$ take $\theta=r^{-k}$ (or use integer polynomial bounds with fixed rational parameters). On the infinitely many hard short inputs from that theorem, at its isolating level, with conditional probability $1-O(r^2 2^{-r})$ over the tape given isolation, no eligible exact evaluator can have either pin timely on a $\theta$ fraction of the pairs. Here the evaluator is fixed, budgets are dyadic, advice is shared, and $(b+r+1)(q+1)\le H^{1/d}/r^C$ with a sufficiently enlarged fixed $C$. A fixed universal evaluator can charge the program to advice. This follows by applying the original theorem to the fixed decoder, whose multiplicative losses are polynomial in $r$. Thus on more than $1-r^{-k}$ of the uniform pairs both the satisfiable and the unsatisfiable formula take longer than $q$. The time measured here is the time on each actual encoded formula. The conclusion permits a linear solver on formulas of length $H\operatorname{poly}(r)$.

For $H\le v\le H\operatorname{poly}(r)$, choose $a=2^{\lceil A_0\log_2 r\rceil}$, $w=\lceil\log_a v\rceil$, and the next power of two $Q\ge512(D+1)r^k$. Then $v\le a^w<av$. The uniform checksum and pin take $(w+1)\log_2 Q+1$ bits. Dividing by $\log_2 v$ gives at most $1+(k+1)/A_0+o(1)$, so any fixed overhead factor $1+\delta$ is available by taking $A_0>(k+1)/\delta$. The long public tape and short input are shared separately and charged as in the original theorem.

### A limit of recovery from exact checksum answers

Take any even $a=|A|\ge2$, $a\le Q$. In each grid coordinate choose $a/2$ elements of $A$. Prescribe zero message bits at every grid point with any chosen coordinate and leave the other $(a/2)^w$ bits independent. Every extension vanishes on each selected full $F$-hyperplane, by uniqueness of grid interpolation in the other coordinates. An oracle returning zero on all masks on the union of these hyperplanes and erasing elsewhere is identical for every message in the family. Its nonerased density is

$$
1-\left(1-\frac{a}{2Q}\right)^w
   =\Theta\!\left(\min\{1,D/Q\}\right).
$$

For the estimate, $1-e^{-wa/(2Q)}$ bounds the fraction below, $\min(1,wa/(2Q))$ bounds it above, and $D=w(a-1)$. Distinguishing all messages by a fixed reader requires at least $(a/2)^w$ advice bits in the worst case, even with unlimited queries to this common oracle. Indeed, $2^{(a/2)^w}$ distinct outputs must be selected. In a variable-length convention there are still fewer than this many strings shorter than $(a/2)^w$. When $a$ grows as a fixed power of $r$, the remaining message length is $v^{1-o(1)}$ on full grids $v=a^w$ of the sizes above. This is an information bound for the checksum oracle. A general SAT machine can also inspect the formula and public input. The local-line proof combines known erasure list decoding and polynomial self-correction. Priority of the precise transfer to two actual SAT runs has not been established.

An exhaustive check on $\mathbb F_4$ tested the line collision inequality and the ambiguous-anchor bound for every one of the $65{,}536$ erasure patterns on a degree-one line. The collision inequality attained equality with multiple candidates on 28 patterns. Over $\mathbb F_8$, 16 distinct binary grid messages shared the same forced answers at 28 of 64 field points.
