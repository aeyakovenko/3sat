# How many challenges buy a shorter parity certificate?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Consider a verdict verifier with an initial public certificate of $s$ bits, one of $M$ possible challenges, and an arbitrarily long reply chosen after the challenge. Its deterministic final check may adaptively inspect $w$ bits of a $k$-bit input. For a prescribed parity, the optimal $s+1$ for sound and complete verification on the Boolean cube is, up to absolute constant factors,

$$
\max\!\left\{1,\frac{k/w}{\log_2(2+Mw/k)}\right\}, \qquad Mw\ge k.
$$

If $Mw<k$, soundness prevents acceptance of even one input. Here $1\le w\le k$ and $M\ge1$. In particular, with the minimum feasible number of challenges $M=\lceil k/w\rceil$, a public certificate needs $\Omega(k/w)-1$ bits. With $M$ exponential in $k/w$, zero public bits suffice. The first statement is a lower bound on proof length conditional on a *single fixed verifier* being sound on the whole cube and complete for its chosen sign.

## The accepting set of one public certificate

Fix the verdict, public certificate and challenge. For each final reply, every accepting path gives a conjunction of at most $w$ literals: another input matching those literals follows the same path. Take the disjunction over all accepting paths and all replies. There are finitely many distinct conjunctions on $k$ input bits even if the reply alphabet is infinite. The result is a width-$w$ DNF. Let $A$ be the intersection of the $M$ challenge DNFs for the fixed public certificate. Soundness places $A$ inside a single parity class. Tests may compute arbitrarily between probes. Each challenge and reply domain is independent of the hidden bits, with any input-dependent eligibility checks included in the verifier's probes.

For $x\in A$, choose one accepting term per challenge. Every coordinate occurs among these terms. Flipping an absent coordinate would preserve all the terms and reverse parity. This gives $Mw\ge k$ from a single accepting input. Lean checks the pivotal-bit covering implication for arbitrary challenge and reply types. [Finite coverage argument](../lean/LiveValueReplay.lean)

We next bound the full-cube density $\mu(A)$. There is an absolute constant $c>0$ for which

$$
\mu(A)\le 2^{-c(k/w)/\log_2(2+Mw/k)}. \tag{1}
$$

Håstad's bottom-fan-in parity correlation theorem directly gives the denominator $1+\log_2 M$ here. Its gate count excludes the bottom conjunctions and the one-sided correlation has magnitude $2\mu(A)$. To account for the ratio $Mw/k$ we use the sequence switching lemma of Impagliazzo, Matthews and Paturi (IMP), together with the isolated-solution bound of Paturi, Pudlák and Zane (PPZ). The fixed certificate can specify completely different test DNFs from the other certificates. [Håstad, Theorem 3.1](https://www.csc.kth.se/~johanh/paritycorr.pdf) · [IMP, Lemma 1.4 and Section 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1107.3127) · [PPZ, Lemma 2](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/PaturiPudlakZane_1999_cjtcs.pdf)

**Proof of (1).** Write $R=k/w$. It suffices to treat $R\ge200$, since $\mu(A)\le1/2$ handles smaller $R$ after decreasing $c$. Set $K=\lfloor R/100\rfloor$, $p=K/k$, and draw the uniform restriction $\rho$ with exactly $K$ live coordinates and independent fair values on the others. Order the challenge DNFs and their terms as in the canonical decision trees of IMP. Use an integer cutoff $B\ge1$.

For each accepted completion of $\rho$, visit the challenges in order. Inspect the length of its path in the current canonical tree for this challenge. If the length exceeds $B$, call the challenge long and fix all bits of the path. Otherwise leave every bit free and proceed to the next challenge. This classification is an analysis of a full completion. It charges no speculative probes to the verifier.

Group accepted completions by the subset $T$ of long challenges and by their path in the canonical sequence tree for $T$ alone. This is precisely the same path because only long challenges fix variables. Suppose its length is $j$. Each member of $T$ contributes at least $B+1$ fresh probes. For each skipped challenge we know the partial restriction at its original visit from this sequence path. Truncate its tree there at depth $B$, assigning rejection to any unfinished node. The inputs accepted by this truncated tree form a width-$B$ CNF (one clause per rejecting leaf). Impose all these CNFs on the $K-j$ remaining variables. Discard a path if any long challenge rejected. Completions in our group satisfy the resulting CNF. Conversely, every model of it passes every original challenge: the long paths were fixed, and the short trees only accept after genuine acceptance. In particular, the CNF's models are isolated, including after restricting to the remaining coordinates. PPZ bounds the fraction of remaining completions by $2^{-(K-j)/B}$, with the same inequality when $K=j$.

For fixed $T\ne\varnothing$, the number of eligible sequence leaves of length $j$ is at most $2^j$. For such a leaf to exist the canonical tree on the fixed subsequence must have a path of length at least $j$ to which every member contributes. The IMP extended switching lemma bounds the probability of that event over $\rho$ by $(13pw)^j$, since $p\le1/13$. A given leaf has conditional mass $2^{-j}$ before imposing the short-challenge CNFs. For $T=\varnothing$ there is just the empty path, with mass one before those constraints. Averaging over restrictions preserves uniform input measure. With $\alpha=13pw\,2^{1/B}\le0.26$, summing the bounds gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu(A)&\le 2^{-K/B}\sum_{T\subseteq[M]}\sum_{j\ge B|T|}\alpha^j\\
&=2^{-K/B}\,(1+\alpha^B)^M/(1-\alpha).
\end{aligned}
$$

Take $B=\lceil8\log_2(2+M/K)\rceil+8$. Then $M/K\le2^{B/8}$, $B\ge16$, and $\log_2(1+\alpha^B)^M\le 2M2^{-B}\le K/(8B)$. The prefactor $1/(1-\alpha)<2$ can be absorbed into the exponent for $K/B\ge4$. For $K/B<4$ use $\mu(A)\le1/2$. Finally $K\ge R/200$ and $B=O(\log_2(2+M/R))$, proving (1). The parameters $w$ and $B$ play different roles: $w$ bounds an original accepting term and $B$ bounds a skipped canonical path after restriction.

If $s=0$, the minimum challenge count is between $\lceil2^{k/w-1}\rceil$ and $2^{\lceil k/w\rceil-1}$, with equality of the two endpoints whenever $w$ divides $k$. Every challenge then has to pass *every* input of the claimed parity. Each challenge's failing set is given by a width-$w$ CNF confined to the opposite parity, so PPZ bounds its density by $2^{-k/w}$. Together these sets must cover half the cube. For the upper bound, split the coordinates into $\lceil k/w\rceil$ packets and challenge each vector of packet parities whose total XOR is wrong. A reply catches one incorrect packet.

## Exact structure with one probe and the minimum number of challenges

For $w=1$ and $M=k$, the optimal public length is **exactly $\lceil k/2\rceil-1$ bits**. In fact the accepting set of each fixed public certificate has a simple structure. Start from any accepted vertex $a$. There are pairwise disjoint nonempty even sets of coordinates such that all passing vertices are obtained by flipping any union of these sets in $a$.

To prove this, each width-one test DNF is a single clause (possibly constant). In the CNF of $k$ clauses, flipping each of the $k$ coordinates of $a$ must falsify a clause. A non-tautological clause can witness this for at most one coordinate, its unique true literal. Thus each variable $i$ has its own distinct clause, every clause is accounted for, and duplicates of a literal can be removed. Use variables $y_i$ indicating a flip from $a$. The clause for $i$ has the form $\neg y_i\lor\bigvee_{j\in D_i} y_j$ with $i\notin D_i$. In the directed graph with edges $i\to j$ for $j\in D_i$, a set of flips passes precisely when each of its vertices has a successor in the set.

The support of a directed cycle passes. If an exterior vertex had an edge into that cycle, adding the vertex would give an adjacent passing support, contradicting parity soundness. Hence any two cycle supports that meet are equal: traversing one that partly overlaps the other would give an edge entering from outside. Starting at any vertex of a passing support, successive chosen edges eventually reach a cycle. The prohibition on entering that cycle shows the initial vertex already belongs to it. Thus every passing support is a union of disjoint cycle supports, and every such union passes. All the cycles have even size because the empty support passes and the verdict fixes parity. This graph argument uses only the elementary successor condition.

There are at most $\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ cycle supports, giving at most $2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$ passing vertices per proof. Covering $2^{k-1}$ vertices gives the claimed length lower bound. For equality pair consecutive coordinates, leaving one singleton if $k$ is odd. Supply all group parities except the last, which the claimed verdict determines. Each pair parity takes two clauses (one per forbidden assignment), and the singleton parity takes one. These use exactly $k$ challenges and can certify either sign. An exhaustive check over CNFs with up to four variables tests the full structural claim. [Finite experiment](../audits/alternating_parity_threshold.py)

## Matching construction and SAT applications

Covering every input of one sign requires total density at least $1/2$. Summing (1) over at most $2^s$ public certificates gives $s+1\ge cR/\log_2(2+M/R)$. Also $s+1\ge1$.

For the upper bound let $r=\lceil k/w\rceil\le M$. Use $r$ consecutive packets of at most $w$ bits. Put $t=\min\{r,\lfloor\log_2(1+M/r)\rfloor\}\ge1$ and group the packets consecutively in groups of at most $t$. Supply the prefix parities at all internal group boundaries. The endpoints are the empty prefix and the claimed total parity. A challenge chooses a group of $d\le t$ packets and proposes any of the $2^{d-1}$ vectors of packet parities whose XOR disagrees with that group's claimed parity. The last reply selects a packet where the proposed parity disagrees with the actual parity, verified in at most $w$ bit probes. The challenge indices can have a fixed domain, encoding the first $d-1$ parities and inferring the last from the public certificate. The number of challenges is at most $r2^{t-1}\le M$. Every correct claim has honest prefixes. If the verdict is wrong, some group claim is wrong for every possible public string, and the group's true packet parities defeat every reply. This uses $\lceil r/t\rceil-1$ public bits. Since $r=\Theta(R)$ and $t=\Theta(\min\{r,\log_2(2+M/r)\})$, this matches the lower bound over the full feasible range.

The parity-chain 3-CNFs have $k=\Theta(N/\log N)$ independent charges for encoded bit length $N$. A packed input-word probe depends on at most a fixed $C$ charges. A global SAT or UNSAT proof checker restricted to this family therefore satisfies (1) with $w=\min(k,Cq)$ for $q\ge1$ packed probes. The upper bound concerns checking the promised cube. A globally sound checker also has to recognize or verify the layout when invoked on arbitrary formulas. [Encoding and earlier equal-budget bound](parity-chain-verdict-checks.md)

There is also a common set of difficult inputs for any fixed finite menu of $J$ sound verifiers, including both verdict signs. Cap their resources by $s,M,w$, padding unused challenges with duplicates. Their union certifies at most $J2^{s-cR/\log_2(2+M/R)}$ of the full cube. For any specified $\ell$ coordinates with $0\le\ell\le k$ and $\ell+s+\log_2 J<cR/\log_2(2+M/R)$, fix all other coordinates uniformly. The expected number of certified vertices on this $\ell$-cube is less than one. Some fixing therefore leaves every vertex uncertified by the menu, and both signs appear whenever $\ell\ge1$. The same argument applies, conditional on the repository's deterministic near-linear SAT hypothesis, inside the signed layout on which every actual run of a fixed solver has the stated cumulative read-description cost. The resulting subcube inherits that cost on every vertex and every stated horizon. [Signed layout theorem](live-value-replay.md)

For example, asking for $s,\log_2 M,q\le N^{1/3-\varepsilon}$ on every rejecting input fails on the elementary parity chain even with unlimited final replies. If $q$ is bounded and $M=\Theta(k)$, every complete such verifier requires $s=\Omega(k)$. These results compare local proof budgets, including in the regime where both sides admit a direct linear scan. The derivation supplies no conversion from a quadratic, cubic, or linear SAT decider into a verifier below these budgets.

The earlier repository calculation charged all three messages together. The formula above separates the challenge count, removes the final message length, and distinguishes $M=\Theta(k)$ from $M=k^2$ when $w$ is constant. For $w=1$, the fixed-certificate function is an ordinary CNF with $M$ clauses, and the density scale already follows from the depth-two case of IMP Theorem 1.3. For general $w$ the proof specializes the short/long path partition from IMP and the PPZ encoding. Existential CNF encodings of parity are a related restricted case. Priority for this exact protocol characterization is unverified.
