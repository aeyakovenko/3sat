# How many noisy forecasts can a SAT certificate eliminate?

Let $x_J$ be the last $L=\lfloor\log_2 J\rfloor$ output bits of the [affine priority program](affine-priority-window.md), each realized as the answer to an explicit pinned 3-CNF. Write $U=4+\lfloor\log_2(1+\lfloor\log_2(J+16)\rfloor)\rfloor$ and $s=\lfloor U/2\rfloor$. The construction proves $|x_J|\le s$ at every stage and $|x_J|=o(U)$, where $|x|$ denotes Hamming weight. All logarithms below are base two.

Under an unrestricted deterministic $O(n^k)$ SAT algorithm for $k=1,2,3$, every fixed sound verifier that certifies a list containing $x_J$ within $2^{\lfloor J/k\rfloor}/(J+1)^B$ steps must allow at least $2^{\eta U^2}$ list entries at every sufficiently late success. Here $B$ is sufficiently large and $\eta>0$ can depend on the verifier and solver. If each listed word may instead be within $h$ errors of $x_J$, the same conclusion holds for $h=o(U)$ on the successful stages. The extension allows one good prediction among arbitrary other predictions. At a given success the conclusion is about a single list whose coverage is certified. A list that happens to contain the true answers at an unmarked stage requires further evidence. There is an unconditional randomized predictor with exact-word success probability $2^{-o(U^2)}$ on the actual windows, and the convergence of this normalized log loss has only moduli that compute the halting problem, including one computable from it.

## Which coordinate patterns must a certified list cover?

A list certificate consists of a guessed finite list of $L$-bit centers and integer radii between zero and $L$, together with a run of the fixed verifier. Soundness means that every accepting run supplies a list for which at least one ball contains $x_J$. The verifier may have no successes on some stages. Charge the time to produce or read the entire list and check its format in addition to the verification time. With explicit word records all this takes a fixed polynomial in $J$ times the original run and record length. Equivalently a fixed decoder may return the $m$ entries from a checked public record. Charge the **sum** of its costs on all $m$ indices and the verifier's work. If each entry costs $t$, $mt$ is available as an upper bound. A succinct decoder with only a per-entry budget need not give this aggregate guarantee. A fixed additional polynomial factor in $J$ throughout the composition is paid by increasing $B$.

Guess a subset $R$ of $r$ distinct window positions with $1\le r\le s$ and consider the codimension-$r$ flat requiring all those bits to be one. Check whether some word of weight at most $s$ in any listed ball could contain $R$ in its support. If no such word exists then the true window misses this flat. The nondeterministic verifier of the flat runs the list verifier on the same guessed record and checks **every** ball against the supplied coordinate flat. It can verify this condition with a single scan. The preceding affine-window theorem applied to this fixed composed verifier gives a constant $\alpha>0$ such that, on every sufficiently late timely success of the list verifier, every $r$-subset must be realizable for every $1\le r\le\lfloor\alpha U\rfloor$. For example choose $\alpha$ strictly below both the affine theorem's exclusion constant and $1/4$. The verifier does not need $\alpha$ to be computable as a function of the solver or of its program text. It accepts arbitrary valid $R$ in the stated range $r\le s$.

For exact candidates discard words heavier than $s$. Each remaining word covers at most $\binom{s}{r}$ of the $\binom Lr$ required subsets. Thus any timely certified list of $m$ candidates at such a stage satisfies
$$
m\ \ge\ \frac{\binom Lr}{\binom sr}\ \ge\ (L/s)^r.
$$
The second inequality compares the $r$ factors $(L-i)/(s-i)$ to $L/s$. Taking $r=\lfloor\alpha U\rfloor$ and using $\log(L/s)=(1+o(1))U$ proves the exponent $\Omega(U^2)$. The counting step is the elementary covering-design bound. The small weights forced by this particular computation improve the exponent from counting unrestricted words by a factor of order $U$. [Gordon, Kuperberg and Patashnik on covering designs](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9502238)

For a center of weight $w$, write $b=|R\setminus\operatorname{supp}(c)|$. To obtain a light word containing $R$, add these $b$ positions and remove $\max(0,w+b-s)$ ones outside $R$. There are enough removable ones because $r\le s$. Every other solution has at least this many total edits. The minimum distance is therefore $b+\max(0,w+b-s)$. With radius $h$, exactly the $b$ satisfying $b\le h$ and $w+2b\le s+h$ qualify. The exact number of $r$-subsets covered by this ball equals
$$
A(L,s,r,w,h)=\sum_{\substack{0\le b\le r,\ b\le h\\w+2b\le s+h}}
\binom{L-w}{b}\binom{w}{r-b},
$$
where an impossible binomial coefficient is zero. For radii $h_i$ and weights $w_i$, any timely certified cover must satisfy $\sum_i A(L,s,r,w_i,h_i)\ge\binom Lr$ in the full preceding range of $r$. This is a necessary condition. Overlaps make a volume test alone insufficient to establish coverage.

For a common upper radius $h<r$, discard all centers with $w>s+h$. A uniformly sampled ordered $r$-tuple of distinct positions can be covered only if at least $r-h$ of its slots lie in the center. For any particular set of $r-h$ slots this has probability at most $(w/L)^{r-h}$ by sampling without replacement. The union bound gives
$$
m \ge \binom rh^{-1}\bigg(\frac{L}{s+h}\bigg)^{r-h}.
$$
This yields the same $\Omega(U^2)$ log-list bound whenever $h=o(U)$. More generally the inequality gives a tradeoff between the Hamming error allowed and the number of predictions. With $r=\lfloor\alpha U\rfloor$ and $h\le r$, it implies $h/U+(\log m)/U^2\ge\alpha-o(1)$, using $\log\binom rh\le r$ and $\log(L/(s+h))=(1+o(1))U$. The case $h>r$ already satisfies this last bound. Thus certificates reaching both $h=o(U)$ and $\log m=o(U^2)$ infinitely often would exclude the chosen unrestricted SAT clock.

For each fixed $\delta>0$, there also exists a deterministic polynomial-in-$J$ procedure that outputs a true list of log-size at most $\delta U^2$ at every sufficiently late stage. Hardwire a starting threshold beyond which $|x_J|\le\delta' U$ for a sufficiently small fixed rational $\delta'>0$. List all supports of size at most $\lfloor\delta' U\rfloor$ thereafter. Their number is at most $(\lfloor\delta' U\rfloor+1)L^{\lfloor\delta' U\rfloor}$, and $2^L\le J$ permits direct enumeration in polynomial time. The threshold proving soundness can vary non-effectively with $\delta$. This order of quantifiers accounts for the verifier dependence of $\eta$.

## A probability law that guesses the actual window

At large $J$ take $s\le L$. Choose an integer uniformly from $0,\ldots,s$, then a uniformly random subset of that size. Its indicator is the predicted window. Both draws can use rejection sampling and unranking with binomial coefficients in expected polynomial time in $J$. The probability of any word and its ranking within the weight class are exactly computable in deterministic polynomial time. Early stages may use $\min(s,L)$. If the actual weight is $H$ then the exact success probability is
$$
p_J=\big((s+1)\binom LH\big)^{-1}.
$$
For $1\le H\le s$, the product bound $(L/H)^H\le\binom LH\le L^H$ gives
$$
\frac{H\log(L/s)}{U^2}
\le \frac{-\log p_J}{U^2}
\le \frac{H\log L+\log(s+1)}{U^2}.
$$
The inequalities also hold at $H=0$. Since $H=o(U)$, the normalized log loss tends to zero. This one law, with no hardwired cutoffs, assigns $2^{-o(U^2)}$ mass to the exact actual word. Ranking by weight and subset index is classical enumerative coding. [Cover, *Enumerative source encoding*](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/transIT/0073cove.pdf)

On any fixed infinite polynomial-time recognizable endpoint set, **every** modulus of the convergence of this normalized log loss computes the halting problem, and the halting problem computes a modulus. Indeed $\log(L/s)\ge U/2$ eventually, so a modulus for the loss gives one for $H/U$ after shifting the requested precision by one and taking the maximum with a fixed initial threshold. The [theorem on moduli for the true sparse window](affine-priority-window.md#the-information-in-a-genuine-modulus) applies. Conversely a late counterexample at precision $2^{-t}$ is computably enumerable. Compute the genuine window and compare the integers $((s+1)\binom LH)^{2^t}$ and $2^{U^2}$. A halting oracle can search for a cutoff with no later counterexample on the given set.

For any exactly polynomial-time evaluable distribution $q_J$ on the $L$-bit words and nonnegative integer $a$, the set $\{x:q_J(x)\ge2^{-a}\}$ has at most $2^a$ members. Enumerate all $2^L\le J$ words, assuming the threshold comparison at the reported $a$ also takes polynomial time in $J$ (it suffices here to restrict $a\le U^2$). A timely sound certificate of $q_J(x_J)\ge2^{-a}$ with $a=o(U^2)$ on infinitely many stages would give a forbidden list under the fast-SAT hypothesis. The displayed sampler attains the asymptotic likelihood. The missing step is certifying a numerical lower bound on that likelihood at individual stages.

### Every efficiently evaluable model with vanishing log loss has this obstruction

Fix an infinite polynomial-in-$J$ recognizable set of endpoints $S$ and a probability or subprobability law $q_J$ on the window words. Assume its values are computable rationals and the comparisons $q_J(y)\ge2^{-a}$ for integers $0\le a\le U^2$ take polynomial time in $J$, uniformly in $y,a$. Assign infinite log loss when $q_J(x_J)=0$. If $(-\log q_J(x_J))/U^2$ tends to zero on $S$, then every modulus computes the halting problem and the halting problem computes a modulus. This applies to models that inspect the construction when choosing their probabilities. The hard part is a lower bound for arbitrary fixed models, since their masses need not be determined by Hamming weight.

Given a machine $P$, give a predictor its description and the fixed tests for $S,q$. As in the original construction its index $e$ in the native enumeration is computable from $P$, and the trial receives that same rank. Write $d=4^{e+1}$, $r_J=\lfloor U_J/(2d)\rfloor$ and $a_J=\lfloor U_J^2/(16d)\rfloor$. The predictor considers only endpoints with $U_J\ge\max(32,4d)$ and $J\ge256$. At all such endpoints $r_J\ge U_J/(4d)$. Also $L+2\ge2^{U_J-4}$, by the definition of $U_J$ and $\lfloor\log(J+16)\rfloor\le L+1$. Thus $L\ge2^{U_J-5}$ and $\log(L/s)\ge U_J-5-\log U_J\ge U_J/2$. There are at most $2^{a_J}$ words of mass at least $2^{-a_J}$. The number needed for these words, after discarding weights above $s$, to cover all $r_J$-sets is at least $(L/s)^{r_J}\ge2^{U_J^2/(8d)}>2^{a_J}$. Select the lexicographically first $r_J$-set $R_J$ missed by every such light word, using coordinates in increasing order from $J-L+1$ to $J$. This selection depends on $J$ and the rank, independently of the trial stage. Enumerating all candidate subsets and all words takes polynomial time in $J$ because each universe has at most $2^L\le J$ elements. Handle an excessive rank or a malformed early record by remaining silent.

At stage $i$ first simulate $P$ for at most $i$ Turing steps. Only after seeing a halt, try $J\in[i,2i]\cap S$ in increasing order, subject to the displayed cutoffs. Report the first $R_J$ whose earliest coordinate is $i$ and whose size meets the rank quota $\lfloor U_i/d\rfloor$. The report asks for ones at exactly those positions as its affine constraints, leaving other bits free. Every sufficiently large $J\in S$ supplies an eligible $i=\min R_J\ge J-L+1$. In particular $J\le2i$ and $U_J\le U_{2i}\le2U_i$, so $r_J\le\lfloor U_i/d\rfloor$. At fixed rank, all sufficiently late trials fit their allotted native time. Only finitely many higher-priority starts can interrupt. After $P$ halts this rank therefore completes some reservation, possibly before higher-priority interference ends or else after it ends. On completion all positions in its selected $R_J$ actually have value one. If the final pivot precedes $J$, later output cannot undo these assigned bits. The genuine window at $J$ also has weight at most $s$. By the defining choice of $R_J$ its probability is strictly below $2^{-a_J}$, including the possibility of zero probability.

A modulus for the loss at precision $1/(64d)$ now bounds the halting time of $P$. More precisely, simulate $P$ through the larger of this threshold and a computable initial stage $G(e)$ with $J\ge256$ and $U_J\ge\max(32,4d)$ for $J\ge G(e)$. The precision is dyadic since $d$ is a power of four. If $P$ halted later, no reservation of its predictor could already have started. The subsequent completed reservation supplies $J$ beyond both thresholds and loss exceeding $a_J/U_J^2\ge1/(32d)$. The floor inequality follows from $U_J^2/(16d)\ge2$ here. This contradicts the modulus. The simulator never needs an effective cutoff for higher-priority interference. For the reverse reduction one can search with a halting oracle for the first cutoff with no later violation in $S$. At $q_J(x_J)=0$ there is a violation of every finite threshold. Otherwise raise the rational to the integer power $2^t$ and compare to $2^{-U_J^2}$ to test for loss strictly greater than $2^{-t}U_J^2$. This last search is unbounded in running time and uses only computability of the rational values.

Under $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ the same modulus conclusion holds for the output law of **any** fixed polynomial-in-$J$ worst-case time sampler using boundedly many fair random bits. Pad its coin tape to a fixed polynomial bound in $J$. Exact output probabilities are then computable rationals. They need not be efficiently evaluable. Present a unary clock parameter $1^J$ to the following test. For $a\le U^2$ put $\tau=2^{-a}$. A comparison between $q_J(y)\ge\tau$ and $q_J(y)\le\tau/2$ has additive gap $\tau/2$. Use $O(\tau^{-2})$ independent trials and compare the fraction producing $y$ to $3\tau/4$. With a sufficiently large absolute constant, the variance bound gives error less than $1/3$ on both promised sides. Since $U=O(\log\log J)$, even $2^{2U^2}=J^{o(1)}$, and the coin and time costs of the test are polynomial in $J$. Lautemann's amplification and translate-cover argument gives one fixed $\Sigma_2^{\mathsf P}$ predicate that accepts on the high side and rejects on the low side. The argument depends on the density bounds only on promised inputs, so on intermediate probabilities it simply defines a fixed arbitrary choice. Under $\mathsf P=\mathsf{NP}$ this single predicate has a deterministic polynomial-time decider. The resulting marked set contains every word of mass at least $\tau$ and has fewer than $2^{a+1}$ members because marked words have mass greater than $\tau/2$. In the preceding rank construction use these marks wherever the exact heavy-word test was used. The volume gap still suffices since $2^{a_J+1}<2^{U_J^2/(8d)}$ at the explicit cutoffs. A missed light word therefore has mass strictly less than $\tau$. The same halt-triggered spike and computable-rational oracle converse follow. In particular a bounded-coin, polynomial-in-$J$ sampler with a computable modulus of $o(U^2)$ true log loss on an infinite polynomial-time recognizable endpoint set would imply $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$. Resource-bounded prediction under log loss is classical; here the parameter is the modulus of convergence on windows of one explicit computation. ## Entire windows can agree with zero on almost all dyadic stages

Even a predictor returning the zero word without running the scheduler forecasts all $\lfloor\log J\rfloor$ answers at once at almost every endpoint $J=2^m$, with an effective bound on the errors. A nonzero bit in the window came from a reservation starting at $i$ with horizon at most $2i$. Then $i\le2^m$ and $2^m-m+1\le2i$. For all sufficiently large $m$, this implies $2^m\le3i$, so the endpoint lies in $[i,3i]$. At most two powers of two lie in this interval. The construction has at most $\sqrt{U_{2^M}}$ starts through $2^M$. Thus at most $2\sqrt{U_{2^M}}+O(1)=O(\sqrt{\log(M+2)})$ among the first $M$ dyadic windows disagree with zero. The bound also applies to the number of incorrect predictions of the parity of the entire window on this sequence. Nevertheless every polynomial-in-$J$ recognizable infinite subset of dyadic endpoints has infinitely many with odd total parity. Otherwise, after an initial threshold a polynomial-time tester could certify an even window parity everywhere on that set, contradicting the deterministic affine obstruction. A symmetric argument gives infinitely many even windows on the same set. A global exception count has not identified an infinite selectable sequence of correct parities.

The role of coding in these arguments is to quantify how much simultaneous uncertainty a verifier may certify about actual SAT answers. No verifier meeting the separation criterion has been constructed. Priority for the stated quantitative applications to these priority windows is unestablished.
