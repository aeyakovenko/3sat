# Positive SAT histories under every permutation of the movable input words

Fix $k\in\{2,3\}$ and suppose ordinary explicit SAT has a deterministic $O(N^k)$-time algorithm in the packed model, where $N$ counts input bits. At a computable increasing sequence of positive source lengths there is an orbit of $B!=2^{\Theta(N)}$ different satisfiable 3-CNFs, $B=\Theta(N/\log N)$. Every permutation of the $B$ tagged physical input words is allowed. For every fixed sound affirmative monitor and every fixed clock multiplier, at every sufficiently late selected length, **every** order on which the monitor signals within $C N^k$ has the following properties.

* A circuit given just time, returning the actual controller and registers, has size at least $N^{1/k^2}/\operatorname{polylog}N$.
* If the circuit also reports the preceding writer of each work-memory load, it needs at least $N^{1/k}/\operatorname{polylog}N$ gates. Every satisfying assignment given by an ordinary circuit of its variable index needs the same lower bound.

Yet each positive orbit contains orders on which one fixed sound verifier accepts in $O(N/\log N)$ word steps and the assignment is accessible with one query to the physical input. Adding exhaustive search when verification fails gives a correct total solver with that same time on the selected orders. These statements test the proposal that high circuit complexity of a genuine execution would by itself force a superlinear running time.

## Making a physical word movable

Use read-only input with aligned word loads of width $w=12\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil$, a zero-filled last word, and a fixed register load/store instruction set. A formula starts with two self-delimiting integers for its variable bound and clause count, with a header of logarithmic length and polylogarithmic decoding time. Each literal is one sign and a fixed-width binary variable number. Use indices starting at one and the bit length of the declared bound as the number-field width. Unused variable numbers are permitted. These are choices within the journal's explicit packed encoding. An ISA that exposes only single-bit answers may be instrumented with its aligned backing-word loads. That changes which history is being measured.

Start with the unary hierarchy formula with $m$ clause records and largest variable slot $v_0=O(m)$, both available from its length parameter with polylogarithmic work. Take $\ell$ to be the **least** integer at least four with $2^\ell\ge128(m+v_0+1)\ell$. Set $V=2^{\ell-1}-1$, $B=\lfloor 3\cdot 2^\ell/(48\ell)\rfloor$, and use $4B+9$ clauses. A literal then occupies $\ell$ bits, each clause occupies $d=3\ell$ bits, and the body has $12\ell B+27\ell$ bits. Including the header gives $N=3\cdot2^\ell/4+O(\ell)$. Thus $\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil=\ell$ for all large sources and $w=4d$. Also $B\ge m$, $B\ge 2(v_0+1)$, and $B=\Theta(m)$. A fixed guard tautology can include $V$ as a variable, so the declared maximum occurs. If the header has $h$ bits, the first aligned word boundary at or after it is $b=w\lceil h/w\rceil$. This boundary cuts clause $j_0=\lfloor(b-h)/d\rfloor\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ at phase $r=(b-h)\bmod d$, numbering clauses from zero. Place the identical guard clause at $j_0+4i$ for $0\le i\le B$. Between consecutive guards put three entire clauses in this order:

1. Source clause $i$ if $i<m$, otherwise a fixed tautology.
2. A tautology with the unique tag variable $2^{\ell-2}+i$, repeated with both signs.
3. A fixed filler tautology.

Fill the prefix and suffix outside these groups with tautologies. Each of the $B$ aligned words starting at $b$ contains the final $d-r$ guard bits, three complete clauses and the first $r$ bits of the next guard. This includes the aligned case $r=0$. Permuting those words merely reorders the three-clause groups. Their tags make all $B!$ strings distinct. From a word purported to have come from slot $i$, extract its tag at a fixed offset, check its range and compare the whole word to the source word generated from $i$. This takes $\operatorname{polylog}N$ steps from the unary length parameter. The bits before and after the movable interval are predetermined.

## Two observed loads certify failure of a common order

For each load to a movable word position $p$ in a proposed history, decode the claimed returned word. Failed authentication is a single local defect. An authenticated load gives a pair $(p,i)$. All such observations belong to one permutation exactly when repeated positions give the same source and distinct positions never give the same source. Necessity follows from bijectivity. For sufficiency the observations specify an injective map on the subset read, and the unused destinations and unused sources have equal cardinalities. Match those arbitrarily. Fixed inputs and padding must also agree with their prescribed answers. A disagreement takes one load or two to exhibit, regardless of how many loads there were or how adaptively the machine selected their addresses. [Finite permutation extension](../lean/WordReadPermutation.lean)

The hierarchy verifier guesses a small circuit for a timely affirmative history. For the input part of its defect test it chooses at most two times, simulates the register circuit there and at their successors, and checks the rules above. Once the independent work-memory and controller tests pass, the completed permutation makes the entire deterministic history genuine by induction. Thus a single guessed history on even one order would give a valid hierarchy witness. With the last writers supplied, one SAT call checks all defects. Without writers, a second nested SAT call also decides whether there was an intervening store. The costs are $(s+1)^k\operatorname{polylog}N$ and $(s+1)^{k^2}\operatorname{polylog}N$ respectively for a circuit of $s$ gates. A circuit assignment to any reordered formula restricts directly to the original variable slots. Include all three tests for this family in the finite menu before choosing the common subsequence. The positive unary hierarchy supplies the contradiction and the dovetailed selection works for the combined menu. [Proof of the exponents and computable selection](common-positive-histories.md)

## An order that reveals a satisfying assignment

Fix any satisfying assignment $a$ of the source. At destinations $2v,2v+1$ for $0\le v<\lfloor B/2\rfloor$, put source tags $2v,2v+1$ in ascending order for bit zero and descending order for bit one. Extend $a$ arbitrarily to unused low indices. Every original variable has such a pair. Leave any unpaired word at the end. The low bit of the **source index** extracted from destination $2v$ now gives $a(v)$, including after subtraction of the tag base. Use zero for indices outside the paired range. All remaining clauses are tautologies. Given $N$ one can recover $\ell,B,d$ and $h=N-(4B+9)d$ by arithmetic, so selecting the assignment word entails just one physical query. The construction requires the existence of the assignment and supplies no search algorithm for finding this order from an arbitrary formula.

One fixed monitor parses the header, checks the applicable layout and bounds, and defines that assignment function for every apparent input of the right shape. Give failed or out-of-range tag decodings a deterministic default. It scans every physical clause and checks its literal fields and its truth under that one function. Each clause takes constantly many packed reads, including random reads for the assignment bits. Hence a signal is sound even on strings with arbitrary forged tags. Header work is polylogarithmic and the scan costs $O(N/\log N)$ on these encodings. Fixing just the encoded parity at $v_0$ designated destinations leaves at least $B!/2^{O(B)}$ permutations when $v_0\le B/2$. The parity pattern on all destinations is uniform among the $\binom B{\lfloor B/2\rfloor}$ feasible patterns. Any required pattern on these $v_0$ destinations admits a completion since both parity classes have at least $v_0$ members.

On a successful run the monitor can have a fixed instruction count per clause, using masks for bounds and clearing temporary registers between clauses. An input-aware evaluator for its registers at time $t$ then needs only the header metadata, the current clause and its assignment queries. Earlier iterations are known to have passed. The metadata fits $O(\log N)$ bits, and even the header phase can be replayed in $\operatorname{polylog}N$ time. Thus an indexed register circuit deprived of the formula may be large on exactly the same physical input on which a decoder consulting the formula is cheap. The proof leaves the worst-case time exclusions for unrestricted solvers open. Historical priority for the simultaneous bounds on this orbit is unverified.
