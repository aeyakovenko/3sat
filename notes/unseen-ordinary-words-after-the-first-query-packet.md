# Processing sensitive ordinary words after the first query packet

Fix the aligned deterministic RAM with writable input, bounded-arity register operations, separate LOAD and STORE instructions, fixed terminal verdicts, and byte-compatible word width \(w=C\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil\). Here \(N\) is the encoded bit length. The constant \(C\) is sufficiently large to hold a public clause with three decimal names in one word. Use the suffix charge \(c+m+D\) from the [native processing theorem](processing-after-the-database-packets-of-a-sat-file.md). Thus \(c\) counts numerical operations and comparisons with a marked operand, \(m\) counts stores with a marked operand, and \(D\) counts distinct addresses and payloads used by the other suffix stores. LOADs alone cost zero. A direct read of a fresh query packet at an unmarked address is free to use. The charge does apply to uses of still-unread ordinary packets.

**Theorem.** For each sufficiently large packet count \(s\), let \(q\) correct SAT programs share this RAM model. Fix a quota \(r\ge0\) with \(q(r+1)\le s\). There is a single pair of proper 3-CNFs differing in one physical bit. One is satisfiable and the other unsatisfiable. In each program, cut its execution at its **first read of any query packet**, including the tag. Before this reply it has already read at least \(r\) distinct ordinary packets. On each of the two files, each program subsequently has
\[
 c+m+D \;\ge\; \Omega(s-qr-q)-O(1).
\]
The constants depend only on instruction arity. No bound on the time, storage, or contents of the private prefix is required. The \(q\) cut times and first query addresses can differ. In particular, for \(q\le s/4\), take \(r=\lfloor s/(4q)\rfloor\). The lower bound is \(\Omega(s)=\Omega(N/\log N)\) subsequent charged processing for all the programs on both files.

## A raw-memory replay lemma

Let \(U\) be a set of ordinary word addresses at one cut. Vary their initial contents while holding the controller and the rest of cut memory fixed. None is among the query holes \(H\). Suppose the fixed run would change verdict on modifying any one of \(b\) distinct addresses of \(U\), one at a time, in this comparison class. Let \(K\) be the number of distinct word values actually present in \(U\) on this run. For a model constant \(A\),
\[
 b\le A(c+m)+D+K+O(1).
\]
The alternative words on the modified inputs can lie outside the original set of \(K\) values.

To prove this, give a simulator everything outside \(U\) at the cut, including the controller, the complete set \(U\), the query layout, and the actual contents of \(H\). Its oracle can read one word of \(U\) at a chosen address. It uses the same causal replay as in the [first-hole pointer proof](processing-after-the-database-packets-of-a-sat-file.md#a-static-index-of-the-cut-independent-of-the-query-replies), with a different accounting for the unknown words.

Let \(f\) be the known cut memory with every address of \(U\) temporarily zeroed. The simulator can compute any orbit in \(f\) and its first visit to \(U\) without reading the oracle. Save suffix writes with their actual addresses, payloads and execution times. Resolve the operands of a suffix store immediately. Keep a marked LOAD result as a deferred chain of load times beginning at the numerical address of its first load. A further indirect load appends a time to the chain. Register copies can share it. On an unmarked address, a current suffix write or original query packet supplies its known word directly. Every other direct load may be deferred. Resolve a marked operand when a numerical operation, branch, or STORE uses it. All other arithmetic and control can be executed on known register values.

During a resolution start at its numerical origin. At each recorded load time use the latest earlier suffix write to the current address if one exists. This resets the origin to the stored payload. Otherwise, at an address of \(U\), read its original word and use that word as the new origin. Otherwise follow \(f\). Keep every queried original word in a cache. Between successive resets the path is a segment of an orbit of \(f\), **ending by or at its first encounter with \(U\)**. In particular the unresolved address, when one is genuinely queried, can only be the first point of \(U\) on the orbit of that segment's origin. An overriding write at that very point resets the origin first. This argument covers repeated earlier writes, zero payloads and self-loops.

There are at most a constant times \(c+m\) initial chain origins from the operands resolved at charged instructions. There are at most \(m+D\) distinct reset payloads from all suffix stores, and at most \(K\) distinct reset origins resulting from original raw oracle replies on this run. Each numerical origin identifies at most one first address in \(U\). Thus the stated bound counts all **distinct original word probes**, even if the native computation follows extremely long chains or unboundedly repeats an unmarked write. If an individually sensitive original word escaped this list, the deterministic replay would have the same transcript and verdict under that word's modification. This proves the lemma. The reasoning applies to the cut machine without granting it any new operations or preprocessing its unknown raw words. ## The ordinary SAT files

Pin three variables numbered 1, 2 and 3 to false with the seven proper clauses that forbid their seven other joint assignments. Use names 12 and 13 for complementary aliases of the tag. The two public clauses with third literal 1 enforce exactly one of the aliases. Allocate one bit variable \(v_j\) per physical packet position. A public chain of binary XOR gates computes the parity of these \(s\) variables, and two public clauses using the false pin 1 equate that parity to alias 12. Each XOR gate uses the four proper triples forbidding its four incorrect input/output triples.

Each slot takes two aligned words. Its private first word begins with one complete clause. For an ordinary slot this is the literal text `-1 2 3 0` and a newline. For a query slot use `2 -1 3 0` and a newline. Both clauses are true, and their physical words have different spellings. For the tag use `12 1 2 0` or `13 1 2 0` and a newline. After this middle clause put spaces up to the last byte of the private word, which is either a space or a minus sign. The public second word starts immediately with the decimal digits of \(v_j\), then ` 1 2 0`, spaces, and a newline. The final byte of the private word therefore chooses the sign of \(v_j\) in a unit constraint padded to three distinct names. Fix that byte to a minus on the tag. Use the same padding for all private words of a given kind. A tag swap changes one bit in ASCII 2 versus 3 and nothing else.

This is a fully specified parity test on actual DIMACS words. There is exactly one tag in each promised file. In particular, if all slot values are zero, changing any single ordinary sign reverses the verdict for either tag spelling. All zero-valued ordinary packets have **one common raw word value**, independent of the site. Their site-specific names are entirely outside that word.

The header and each wholly public clause may be padded with spaces immediately before its newline to fill one word. With the consecutive names used by the checker the file takes exactly \(6s+8\) native words. For each large \(s\) choose the least integer \(e\ge2\) with \(C(6s+8)e+2\le2^e\). Its minimality gives \(2^{e-1}<C(6s+8)e+2\), so \(N=C(6s+8)e\) indeed uses native width \(Ce\). Every public clause fits for a sufficiently large fixed \(C\).

## One first query for each machine on the same file

First simulate each program until it has read \(r\) distinct private slots, providing the same zero-valued ordinary packet at every previously unseen site. Give each program its own initial writable memory, registers and writes. Let \(V\) be the union of the private positions so exposed, of size at most \(qr\). For each program continue through \(V\) until its first request for a slot outside \(V\); stop before returning that word and call its position \(h_i\). Put \(H=\{h_i:1\le i\le q\}\). Choose any one element of \(H\) as the tag. Make all other members zero-valued query packets and all remaining positions zero-valued ordinary packets.

These simulations reach their cut and replay on the same two actual files. On a prefix seeing only ordinary packets, halting or running forever without another first read would have the same behavior on two opposite valid tag completions. First overwriting an unread slot is also impossible. Put the tag at exactly that slot, complete all others with ordinary zeros, and compare its two spellings. The overwrite would erase their only difference. This argument applies separately to each machine on its actual observed prefix. Thus its first visit to \(H\) occurs at \(h_i\). Supply this first reply as known at the cut if the marking convention grants it.

Use \(U=[s]\setminus(V\cup H)\), referring to aligned private-word addresses. Every machine reaches the cut with these ordinary words unread and unmodified. Every one of them is individually sensitive on each tag file, and modifying one preserves the entire prefix and the query layout. Their actual contents use a single raw value. Apply the replay lemma with \(b=|U|\ge s-qr-q\) and \(K=1\).

## Clock and endpoint

The physical family has a promised evaluator using one word read and constant work per slot to update parity and record the tag. On a conventional word RAM with shifts and Boolean word operations, a globally correct program can also recognize the whole scaffold in \(O(s)\) word steps on the family. Read the header, check \(6s+8\) equals the physical word count, and compare its prescribed public clauses and all private words to their canonical spellings. For the decimal names, tabulate right-aligned ASCII spellings and lengths in increasing numerical order with a decimal odometer. Across \(O(s)\) consecutive names the total number of changed digits is \(O(s)\). Constantly many shifts, masks and concatenations assemble each public word from these tables. Require exactly one allowed tag and return the computed verdict. A mismatch transfers to a complete SAT procedure on the original input. This gives a matching \(O(s)\) total native clock on the hard family.

Here the contribution of the remaining ordinary packets can be read directly from the proof. For a single program, setting \(r=s-u-1\) leaves \(u\) unread ordinary packets at the tag and gives a lower bound proportional to \(u\). Once all ordinary packets have been read, their parity fits in one controller bit. A solver which has validated each old packet can combine that bit with the later query parity using unmarked arithmetic. The result at a full-disclosure cut is a separate question for other SAT families.
