# Sharp separator sizes at intermediate clique-minor orders

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Let $T$ be the number of executed instructions and $h$ the largest clique-minor order in the undirected native graph of chronological edges and true last-write edges. One fixed linear-time logarithmic-word RAM, returning a constant on every input, has the following property. For every fixed $1<c<3/2$ there are arbitrarily large all-zero inputs of length $n$ with $T=\Theta(n)$, $h=\Theta(T^{c-1})$, and minimum balanced separator size
$$
 \Theta(h\sqrt T)=\Theta(T^{c-1/2}).
$$
A balanced separator here leaves each component with at most $3T/4$ instructions. The transfers have a unique positive source, so the statement also holds for the [choice of any valid same-value anchor](slab-product-tradeoff.md). All stored values are one. A policy allowed to predict the values can thus discard all the transfer edges.

At the endpoint of the present certificate recurrence, a near-linear run can have $Th$ of order $n^c$ with this intermediate value of $h$. Uniform improvement of the *number* of separator records by a polynomial factor at this profile already fails for actual RAM traces. This also specifies which aspects of the certificate the example leaves open. Its records have short descriptions, and the final verdict is immediate. The graph construction uses the classical sharpness examples for genus separators, based on expanders with grid neighborhoods. The additional checks below realize the intermediate parameter ranges in the native chronological model on a simple input. [Gilbert, Hutchinson and Tarjan](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/703aaace-92f1-462c-9ce8-d2e4255f6735) · [Conroy, Le and Spalding-Jamieson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00663)

## A core with grid neighborhoods

For a power of two $q$ take the eight-port expander on $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^2$. Its four forward maps send $(x,y)$ respectively to $(x+1,y)$, $(x,y+1)$, $(x+y,y)$ and $(x,y+x)$. The other ports are their inverses. Keep edge identities even at parallel edges and loops. Write $m=q^2$. There is an absolute $\gamma>0$ such that every set $U$ of at most $m/2$ vertices has at least $\gamma|U|$ outside neighbors. The fixed degree converts the edge expansion theorem to this version. All port and inverse-port calculations take constantly many word operations. [Lee's proof for these four maps](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.6296)

Give each core vertex a square grid with side $l=2s+2$, where $s$ is a power of two. Excluding the corners, each of the four sides has $2s$ available cells. Split its cells into two consecutive groups of $s$, giving one group per port. For each old edge join the two groups by $s$ parallel strands with distinct endpoints. Choose the reverse boundary order at the opposite port, so these strands fit in one untwisted ribbon. Each grid has constant maximum degree including its strands. All grids and ribbons embed in an orientable surface of genus $O(m)$. One can thicken $m$ disjoint disks and attach one oriented band for each of the $4m$ old edges, then cap every boundary circle. This also handles loops. A grid is drawn inside its disk with the eight port groups in cyclic order on its boundary.

Let $X$ be a set of removed grid cells. A chamber with fewer than $\delta s$ removed cells, for fixed small $\delta>0$, has fewer than $\delta s$ damaged rows and fewer than $\delta s$ damaged columns. Its entirely intact rows and columns form a connected set. Every intact row meets every intact column, and the union omits fewer than $(\delta s)^2$ of the $l^2$ cells. At least $(1-\delta)s$ cells in each port group belong to this set, using the intact columns on a horizontal side and intact rows on a vertical side. If both endpoints of a core edge have such chambers, at least $(1-2\delta)s>0$ of the strands join the two sets.

If $|X|<\rho\delta ms$, fewer than $\rho m$ chambers are bad. For $\rho$ small compared with $\gamma$, the good core has a component of size greater than $m/2$. Otherwise a union of good components has between $m/4$ and $m/2$ vertices (take one in that range or accumulate smaller ones). All its outside neighbors are bad, violating expansion. Outside that giant component, the remaining good vertices together number at most $\rho m/\gamma$ by the same expansion inequality. Hence a single surviving component contains at least
$$
 (1-\rho-\rho/\gamma)m\big(l^2-(\delta s)^2\big)
$$
grid cells. Taking $\delta,\rho$ sufficiently small makes this any desired fixed fraction below one of all grid cells.

## Actual read and write slots

Traverse the chambers in lexicographic order, and each chamber in the usual serpentine Hamiltonian order of its grid cells. Treat each cell as a contiguous phase. The serpentine edges come from chronology. For every other grid edge and every strand allocate one unique work address. Its first endpoint stores one, and its second endpoint loads and discards the word. The addresses can be encoded by the edge type, chamber or labeled forward core edge, and the corresponding offset. The endpoints and their ranks in the chronological traversal are computed from a fixed number of coordinates, using only words of $O(\log T)$ bits. Send and receive events are selected independently of the returned data. Keep the loop variables and all temporary calculations in a fixed number of registers. Unused access slots idle. No other instruction needs a writable-memory access. The true last-write graph now has exactly the specified transfer edges plus the chronological path, with constant-length phases.

Here is a detail important for the upper bound on $h$. In the embedded grid graph add the chronological edge from the last cell of one chamber to the first of the next one. At most $m-1$ extra handles suffice, since each such edge may have its own handle. At each cell, the two incident chronological half-edges delimit the two sides of its phase path in a little disk. On one side order the transfer endpoints from entrance to exit along the boundary arc. On the other use the opposite cyclic ordering, also from entrance to exit. Interleave these two lists preserving each of their orders. The ports can then be attached to distinct successive native instruction slots without a crossing in the disk. The start and end can use short dummy path extensions. The cyclic order at a cell and the two chronological half-edges are given by a finite set of grid, port and endpoint cases. At the new handles use predetermined free wedges. Thus this ordering can be implemented by the same fixed program. Dummy arithmetic or idling along a phase subdivides its path. The resulting native graph embeds in genus $O(m)$, rather than paying one handle per cell. Euler's inequality for an embedded simple clique gives $h=O(\sqrt m+1)$ by minor monotonicity of genus.

For the lower bound on native separators, mark every grid cell whose phase contains a removed instruction. There are at most as many marked cells as removed instructions. An intact connected union of grid cells lifts to a connected union of whole native phases because every surviving grid edge is realized by a true transfer or a chronological edge. Phase lengths can all be padded to one fixed constant, including loop bookkeeping. Alternatively choose the uncovered cell fraction smaller than the inverse of ten times the largest phase length. The initial setup and terminal instructions form $o(T)$ extra vertices. The giant-component estimate then proves that every balanced native separator has size $\Omega(ms)$. Since $T=\Theta(ms^2)$, the uniform $O((h+1)\sqrt T)$ separator theorem also gives $h=\Omega(\sqrt m)$ as $m$ grows. This proves matching bounds for both parameters. ## Encoding all the profiles with a fixed unary program

The input bits may all be zero. As usual the RAM has its input length. Take $q=2^a$, $s=2^b$ for integers $a,b\ge1$. Use length $n=2^{2(a+b)}+a$. The largest power of two at most $n$ recovers $2^{2(a+b)}$. The difference recovers $a$, hence also $b$. Computing these parameters in registers takes $O(\log n)$ time, and ineligible lengths can return the same constant immediately. The core needs no input table and no expander search. Since $l=2s+2$, the RAM runs in $\Theta(ms^2)=\Theta(n)$ time on eligible lengths. For each growing integer $k$ take $a=\lfloor2(c-1)k\rfloor$ and $b=k-a$. This yields the indicated powers within constant factors.

At smaller component caps the same example can be repeated. Take $z$ copies of one gadget, with disjoint cell addresses and only one chronological bridge between adjacent gadgets. Let each copy have $T_0$ instructions and take any cap $b_0$ between $T_0/3$ and $T_0/2$. Splitting the native timeline into components of size at most $b_0$ costs $\Omega(zms)=\Omega((h+1)T/\sqrt{b_0})$ deleted instructions, because in each copy the uncut giant argument applies independently. A clique minor of order at least three cannot straddle a bridge except for removable parts of individual branch sets, so the clique-minor order of the chain is the maximum for one copy. Repeating a fixed number or a length-encoded number of copies uses ordinary register loops. For powers of two $z=2^p$ all three parameters can be encoded in a unary length $n=2^{p+2(a+b)}+D$, where a fixed binary pairing encodes $a,b$ in $D=\operatorname{poly}(p+a+b)$ and $p$ is recovered from the leading bit. This demonstrates sharpness for each chosen scale, without requiring one graph simultaneously to attain the bound at every scale.
