# Robust directed reachability on two writable planar tapes

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** There is a fixed deterministic Turing machine with two writable two-dimensional tapes and a sequential read-only instruction tape, and an infinite family of inputs on which its chronological cell-access graph has the following property. For some constants $c_0,c_1>0$, deleting any $c_0 T$ step vertices leaves at least $c_1T^2$ distinct ordered pairs connected by directed paths, where $T$ is the number of steps. In particular no $o(T)$ deleted vertices can leave every vertex with $o(T)$ ancestors. Each plane has a single head taking nearest-neighbor moves or staying still. Time and consecutive visits to the same work cell supply forward arcs.

Paul, Pippenger, Szemerédi and Trotter discussed the possibility of linear-size grates on two-dimensional tapes. They distinguished grates from the linear-size two-dimensional superconcentrators already known. Klawe previously amplified robust paths by replacing vertices with sets and edges with expanders. The construction below uses that principle, with a bounded-degree outer grate and lane permutations that two planar heads can traverse in linear time. Priority for the tape realization has not been established. [PPST, §§2, 5](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf) · [Klawe, *Shallow Grates*](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1992/TR-92-03.pdf)

The follow-up construction implements the transfers with a single writable line alongside the plane. A matching positive result gives sublinear segregators when all auxiliary lines together visit $o(\sqrt T)$ cells in $T$ steps. [Mixed-tape threshold and bounded-round pebble game](mixed-tape-pebble-frontier.md)

## Amplifying an outer grate

Use Schnitger's family of acyclic graphs with $s$ inputs, $s$ outputs, $O(s)$ vertices and bounded indegree $\Delta\ge2$. Some $c,\eta>0$ work at every family size. After deletion of at most $cs$ vertices, at least $\eta s^2$ input-output pairs remain connected. Set $q=\lceil\sqrt s\rceil$ and $m=q^2$. Make a copy of the lane set $[m]$ at every vertex. Along each outer edge put the same bipartite regular graph on its two lane sets. [Schnitger, *On depth-reduction and grates*](https://doi.org/10.1109/SFCS.1983.38) · [Blocki and Cinkoske, §3.1, formulation with bounded indegree](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10200741)

Identify the lanes with $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^2$. Take a fixed power of the lazy Margulis–Gabber–Galil walk. Its transition matrix $P$ is doubly stochastic, symmetric, and has norm at most $\lambda$ on the mean-zero subspace, for any prescribed constant $\lambda>0$ by choosing the power large enough. Every edge label is a permutation $x\mapsto Ax+a\pmod q$ with fixed integer coefficients, since the original generators are affine shears, their inverses and identity. The degree and coefficients are independent of $s$. Count edges with multiplicity. [Gabber and Galil](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4) · [Formalized affine family and spectral estimate](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Expander_Graphs.html)

Fix $B>\Delta^3$, $\delta=1/100$, and choose $\lambda$ with $\lambda\sqrt B<1/4$ and $2\lambda^2/(1/4-2\delta)^2<1$. Suppose at most $c\delta sm$ lanes are deleted. Discard outer vertices with more than $\delta m$ lost lanes. At most $cs$ vertices are discarded, so the outer graph still connects at least $\eta s^2$ input-output pairs.

Consider any surviving outer path of length $h$. At its last vertex use all surviving lanes. Working backward, at each vertex retain surviving lanes that send at least $3/4$ of their edges to the next retained set. Every retained set has size at least $(1-2\delta)m$. Indeed, if the next excluded set $E$ has proportion $u\le2\delta$, the mean-square deviation of $P1_E$ from $u$ is at most $\lambda^2 u(1-u)$. The fraction of lanes with more than $1/4$ of their edges going to $E$ is at most $2\delta\lambda^2/(1/4-2\delta)^2<\delta$. Physical deletions at this vertex exclude at most another $\delta m$ lanes.

From any retained set $S$ of reachable lanes in a layer, take its neighbors $Y$ in the next retained set. The spectral estimate gives
$$
\tfrac34 |S|\le \langle 1_S,P1_Y\rangle
\le |S||Y|/m+\lambda\sqrt{|S||Y|},
$$
using the unnormalized inner product. If $|Y|<m/4$ and $|Y|<B|S|$, the right side is less than $|S|/2$. Thus $|Y|\ge\min(m/4,B|S|)$. Once $h\ge H=\lceil\log_B m\rceil$, every retained starting lane reaches at least $m/4$ final lanes. The retained sets may be chosen separately for each path.

At most $s\sum_{i=0}^{H-1}\Delta^i=O(s m^{\log_B\Delta})=o(s^2)$ outer input-output pairs have a path of length less than $H$, by counting possible predecessors at each output. All other connected pairs have a sufficiently long surviving path. Distinct outer pairs give disjoint sets of lifted pairs of terminals. The lifted graph therefore retains $\Omega(s^2m^2)$ reachable ordered pairs after the stated deletions.

## Realizing the directed lanes on two planes

On each worktape allocate a disjoint $q\times q$ tile to each outer vertex. Arrange tiles in a nearly square array of diameter $O(q\sqrt s)=O(m)$. Denote the two copies of lane $i$ at outer vertex $v$ by $A(v,i),B(v,i)$. In topological order of $v$, visit $A(v,i)$ and $B(v,i)$ simultaneously once for each $i$, marking this time as $H(v,i)$. Next, for each outer edge $v\to w$ and each directed permutation $F$ of the lane walk, visit $A(v,i)$ and $B(w,F(i))$ simultaneously for every $i$. Call this meeting time $E$. Separate the passes by at least one time step.

A pass costs $O(m)$ nearest-neighbor steps. The first head scans its tile in row order. Within each row the coordinates of the desired second-head locations advance by fixed integer increments modulo $q$. For an increment $a$, there are $O(|a|+1)$ wraparounds over the row, each costing $O(q)$ moves, and the ordinary steps together cost $O((|a|+1)q)$. Moving between rows costs $O(q)$, and moving between passes costs $O(m)$. Heads may wait for each other. There are $O(s)$ vertices and $O(s)$ outer edges, with a fixed number of permutations per edge. Thus $T=O(sm)$, while the distinct hub times imply $T=\Omega(sm)$. One finite controller follows a stream of direction pairs, reading one symbol per step and reading and rewriting each scanned work cell. The existence claim permits an input specifying the trajectory. It requires neither advice to the machine nor a fast construction of the outer grate on a blank input.

A deleted step spoils the abstract lanes corresponding to the positions of its two work heads, at most two lanes total. This includes incidental visits while crossing tiles or waiting. If lanes $(v,i)$ and $(w,F(i))$ are unspoiled, the chain of consecutive visits to $A(v,i)$ connects $H(v,i)$ to $E$. The chain of consecutive visits to $B(w,F(i))$ connects $E$ to $H(w,F(i))$. Topological order gives the required time directions. Neither chain contains a deleted event. Consequently every surviving lifted path gives a directed path between the corresponding distinct hub times. Taking $c_0$ sufficiently small and using $T=\Theta(sm)$ proves the claim. The terminal events are designated vertices and need not be sources or sinks of the full chronological graph. Lean checks the loss-free chains on the two cells, their concatenation at the transfer time, and the induced path length. An independent two-head simulator tested the transfer after 4,082 deletion sets on small layouts using only same-cell edges. ## Consequence for temporal contractions

For any partition into $K=\Theta(T/b)$ consecutive time intervals of size at most $b=o(T)$, contract each interval to one vertex and retain an arc for each original arc between different intervals. Deleting $O(c_0T/b)$ intervals with a sufficiently small constant removes at most $c_0T$ native events. A surviving native path maps to a directed path through the surviving interval vertices. At most $Tb=o(T^2)$ ordered pairs lie within a single interval, and each pair of intervals represents at most $b^2$ pairs of events. Thus $\Omega(K^2)$ directed pairs survive in the contraction. The argument applies simultaneously to every such partition of the same trajectory.

This obstruction concerns the physical chronological cell graph and its temporal contractions. A simulation choosing a different tape representation or retaining only data relevant to the answer may produce a different graph. The fixed controller can ignore every writable symbol when deciding what to do. Robustness of this graph therefore supplies no lower bound on the running time of an equivalent decision algorithm or of a SAT solver.

## Exact geometric block graphs

There is a separate realization for blocks that are coarsened **in space and in time**. Take any one of the two-head schedules above and let $p$ be its number of slots after adding an initial setup slot and enough idle slots to make $p$ odd. Replace each logical cell by a physical square of side $b=p^2=2h+1$, and each slot by $b$ microsteps. During an epoch, all read/write events on a given tape stay in its one indicated square. Only the last move may enter the next square. For a crossing, choose facing side midpoints in the old and new squares. For a stay, choose a side midpoint in the current square as its next start position. Between entry and exit midpoints one can move first to the center and then to the exit, in exactly $h+h=b-1$ steps. Spend the last step crossing the boundary or staying. The setup epoch moves each head from the physical origin to the designated first midpoint, staying inside its first block. The two heads can execute these schedules simultaneously.

The resulting run has $f=p^3$ microsteps and square blocks of side $f^{2/3}=b$. The graph on its $p=f^{1/3}$ epochs has an edge for consecutive visits to a spatial block on either tape. After restricting to the original epochs these edges reproduce every logical consecutive-visit chain. The bounded setup/padding cannot affect the asymptotic robustness. An unrolled finite-alphabet input describes the two directions at every microstep. This proof concerns the graph of a legal block-respecting trajectory. It does not assume that the transformation of some pre-existing decision algorithm to block form will choose this trajectory.

The argument also works when a two-dimensional block is required to have **volume** $b$, so that the number of its symbols fits the epoch budget. Keep $b=p^2$ microsteps per epoch and choose squares of side $p$. The two midpoint-to-center legs now take $p-1$ steps in all. Insert $b-p$ stationary steps before the final crossing or stay. The block names and all arcs of the epoch graph are unchanged. More generally the same padding works for any odd side at most the epoch length. With an unrolled command input each work head and the sequential input head obey the block convention on these trajectories. This is a counterexample to a graph theorem quantified over all such trajectories. Whether a particular normal-form simulation can always avoid them is a distinct question.

For the side-$b$ version one can also use an input of length $O(b+p)$ while keeping exactly two writable planar heads. Allow an additional $O(p)$ preparatory epochs and use their inclusion when choosing the final odd $p$ and $b=p^2$. During the first, explicitly scripted $b$-step setup epoch, put both heads at the west midpoint of the origin square and mark on tape $B$ the two endpoints of a vertical length-$h$ timer from that midpoint to the origin. Next, perform the intended macro trajectory on tape $A$ only, using the oscillation of $B$ between these points to time the two length-$h$ moves per epoch. Mark on $A$ the center and the entry and exit midpoints of each square on that trajectory. In another $O(p)$ epochs, return $A$ to the initial square and its west midpoint while $B$ continues to time the moves and ends at its west midpoint. In the real run, the marks on $A$ time both heads. On the segment from an entry midpoint to its center and from there to an exit midpoint, no other center or side midpoint lies strictly inside that segment. Both length-$h$ phases consequently end at the correct time, even for a macro stay. One symbol of macro directions per epoch suffices after the initial setup. The additional epochs precede the hard suffix, which still occupies a constant fraction of all epochs. This gives the stated input length on the selected graph-realizing runs.

A [stronger terminal-pair analysis](two-tape-robust-terminals.md) gives a single large set of inputs and a single large set of outputs with all pairs connected on its construction.

## A fixed machine enforcing block discipline on every input

For the volume-$b$ variant, two further timer tapes give a uniform construction that enforces the block sizes on **every** command string. The two payload planes still carry the whole grate. Take a string of $n$ simultaneous head commands, put $s=n+1$, $p=2s$, $f(n)=p^3$ and $b=p^2$. This is a time-constructible upper clock satisfying $f(n)\ge n\log^* n$ for large $n$. Give every planar tape squares of side $p$, hence volume $b$. Use a read-only input with positions zero and $s$ occupied by endmarkers. It fits in the first input block. The following setup and all timer moves stay within the first square on their respective work tapes. Finitely many small sizes may halt immediately.

A fast timer head oscillates from coordinate 0 to $s$ and back, one step per microstep, with exact period $p$. During its first outward scan it follows the input head, so it learns where to mark the right endpoint when the input head reads the endmarker. Mark the origin, the two endpoints' interior neighbors and the far endpoint for later turns and warnings. The near neighbor at $s-1$ can be marked on the first return. The input head returns to zero during that same cycle. A slow timer head initially makes the same trip, except that on the final microstep of the first cycle it stays at 1. Its position at time $p$ is consequently the first position after the start of a new outward sweep. From then on it moves one unit on this same marked segment only at the final microstep of each fast cycle. The fast head signals that step at position 1 on its return. Thus the slow head increments once per completed cycle and has period $p$ cycles. A fast end-of-cycle signal when the slow head is at 1 on its return is exactly the last microstep of an epoch of length $b$. Both directions and both heads' turns use finite control. Initially the special setup suppresses the slow timer's ordinary advance until time $p$.

Choose the center coordinate $(s,s)$ in each square $\{0,\ldots,2s-1\}^2$. During the first fast cycle the two payload heads can go from the origin corner to this center, using $s$ moves in each coordinate. They wait there until the setup epoch ends and the input head advances to the first command. During a payload epoch each head goes from its entry point to the center and then to the exit point specified by its command. A crossing uses a face at coordinate 0 or $2s-1$ with the other coordinate equal to $s$. Each inward or outward leg therefore takes $s$ or $s-1$ moves; an entry at the center takes none. Use the two halves of the first fast cycle for these legs, suppressing one move for a short leg. On a stay command use the center as the exit. Wait at the exit until the epoch's last microstep, when each requested crossing is made and the input advances. The timers mark the halves of the fast cycle and determine the epoch boundary independently of the command values.

After all commands, halt and stutter for unused slots of $f(n)$. There are $p$ total epochs and $n$ command epochs, so the hard command schedule occupies a fixed positive fraction. The two timer tapes add no work-block edges between nonconsecutive epochs, since each visits just its one initial block. This realizes the same robust directed subgraph on the selected inputs of one fixed machine that is block-respecting on every input at this clock. All four worktapes can be planar with one head each. It proves a negative answer for a method requiring small segregators for all such multitape graphs. It does not exclude choosing a different simulation of a language.
