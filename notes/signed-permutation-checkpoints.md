# One global permutation at a RAM checkpoint, and the cost of a second

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A RAM checkpoint may assert that the entire memory, after a change of coordinates, is an even permutation. No inspection of fewer than all but one of the entries of an unknown permutation can generally certify its sign. Nevertheless, a short RAM block violating such an assertion always has a sparse counterexample. Two simultaneous assertions of this same type suffice to destroy that property. For those, testing one load can require reading an entire independent parity input.

## A sparse counterexample for one assertion

Use a word RAM with address and value set $D=\{0,\ldots,2^s-1\}$, with $s=O(\log N)$ on the polynomial clocks in question. A smaller address field can be embedded in a full word bank with unused addresses. Memory initially contains zero throughout. For a fixed input, time and allowed controller, a row gives a clocked, locally evaluable map $T_i:D\to D$ for each address $i$. Every map must be bijective and satisfy $T_i(0)=i$. The row asserts that $P(i)=T_i(M[i])$ is a permutation of a prescribed sign. It may also fix explicitly listed entries of $P$. Both the normalizers and the sign can depend on the controller. A row need not store a permutation, its inverse, or the actual memory. The row description and its list of pins are charged. The other allowed rows are an exact zero-default sparse memory with a specified controller, and a terminal controller test.

**Window theorem.** Consider a defective interval between two well-formed permutation rows. Fix the controller and a violating native execution. Let $q$ be the number of distinct addresses accessed in it or pinned by either boundary at its corresponding controller. There is a violating execution with the same trace of native instructions, read replies and writes whose starting memory has at most $2q+4$ nonzero words.

Freeze the initial native values at that union $S$ of addresses, including initial values at write-only addresses. Old normalized values on $S$ give a partial injection. Every full permutation of the required old sign extending this injection specifies a unique starting memory, reproduces the trace by determinism, and satisfies the old pins. The final controller and final normalized values on $S$ are fixed. On every untouched address $i$, the final normalized value is $h_i(P(i))$ with $h_i=T^{\mathrm{end}}_i\circ(T^{\mathrm{start}}_i)^{-1}$. This inverse is used only in the existence proof.

A feasible partial injection from $k$ positions has a prescribed-sign completion with support of size at most $\max(2k,k+2)$. If $a$ images are outside the domain of the partial injection, complete it to a permutation on the union of the domain and image, of size $k+a$, fixing everything else. If its sign needs changing, swap the images at two unpinned positions. Use the available $a$ unpinned positions in the union first. If fewer than two exist in the entire address set, the completion was unique and already had the feasible sign. This bounds the support by $k+\max(a,2)$. Calibration and injectivity make the support of $P$ exactly the set of nonzero native words. [Constructive sparse extension in Lean](../lean/SparsePermutationCompletion.lean)

If some extension produces a repeated final value, freeze also the original words at the two colliding addresses. The completion bound with $k\le q+2$ preserves the collision within the claimed support. Otherwise every extension produces a permutation. Any two old extensions of the given sign differ by an even permutation of the unpinned positions. This permutation is a product of 3-cycles on those positions. Each generating move changes exactly three old values and hence exactly three final values, since the individual $h_i$ are injective and the block leaves these positions untouched. Two permutations differing in exactly three places also differ by a 3-cycle, so their signs agree. Consequently every old extension gives the same final sign. Any feasible sparse extension reproduces whichever controller, pin or sign defect the original had. This covers small free sets too, since their fixed-sign extension can be unique.

There is additional rigidity with four or more free positions. Any two distinct unused values at two distinct free positions extend to the prescribed sign by swapping two remaining values if necessary. In a collision-free family, each $h_i$ maps the unused values bijectively onto the complement of the fixed final values. For two different free rows $i,j$ the values $h_i(v)$ and $h_j(w)$ must differ whenever $v\ne w$, by the two-position extension. Surjectivity onto the common palette now forces $h_i(v)=h_j(v)$ for every unused $v$. In particular if address $v$ itself is free, this value is $h_v(v)=v$. A counterexample with three free positions occurs on $\mathbb Z_3$, $h_i(v)=2i-v$ carries every even permutation $i\mapsto i+a$ to the even permutation $i\mapsto i-a$. The preservation of sign used in the window theorem still holds. ## Checking the chart and charging its cost

The existential test guesses a sparse initial state and the starting controller. It checks the old row, replays one block, and tests the end row. If a sparse state is zero outside a list $A$, its normalized table is identity off $A$. Sorting its images on $A$ tests whether they consist of exactly $A$, and counting cycles there gives its sign. After the replay the list grows by at most the number of writes. Validate all pins and controller conditions. A malformed coordinate system itself has a short existential witness, either $T_i(0)\ne i$ or two distinct inputs with the same image under $T_i$. This check can select any allowed controller of any submitted row. Equal finite domain and codomain make injectivity sufficient. Use clocked total evaluators and validate the directory and field widths. Even local maps whose individual signs are expensive can be used.

An exact starting row can be replayed from its charged sparse table. If the ending row specifies an exact state, disagreement has a controller or a single-address witness. Freezing one extra initial word preserves the latter under the sparse completion lemma. A pure terminal controller test needs no extra address. Therefore, for maximum block width $b$ and total transmitted row data $z$, all defects have a sound existential check in $(1+b+z)\operatorname{polylog}N$ native steps. This covers adaptively addressed reads, writes of zero, and arbitrary data on a dishonest certificate. A chart without defects propagates the real initial state through to the specified signal.

For a deterministic RAM that signals UNSAT soundly and eventually on every negative input, let $E$ be the first signal time. Minimize $K=1+E+\sum_{0<t<E}|z_t|$ over complete one-step inductive proofs of that signal in this row format. The worst-case $K$ on negative inputs is outside $O(N^c)$ for every fixed $c<(1+\sqrt5)/2$. A proof always exists using exact sparse rows. To check the transfer, if $K=O(N^c)$ choose a shortest row in each full window of $b$ internal times. Their total payload including the directory is $O((1+K/b)\operatorname{polylog}N)$, and adjacent cuts are at distance $O(b+1)$. Inductivity survives discarding other rows. For a bounded nondeterministic computation of time $R$ on $m$ public words let $H=\max(2,R,m)$. As in the cumulative calculation, a nonacceptance time bound $(R^e+m^d+1)H^{o(1)}$ initially holds at $e=d=c\ge1$ by materializing the SAT formula and running the hypothesized recognizer. With $b=H^{cd/(e+d)+o(1)}$ the single defect test, including the chart as public data, gives the update $e'=cde/(e+d)$. Its iterates fall below one if $d(c-1)<1$. The complementary time diagonal yields the stated exclusion. Smaller $c$ are covered by increasing the exponent. The unrestricted SAT time question would need an independent upper bound on the proof cost. In particular, **if** SAT has a fixed near-linear deterministic reference solver, then on the two formulas at each good seed of the common construction, every fixed near-linear correct solver requires $K\ge N^2/\operatorname{polylog}N$ for its respective verdict in this format. The sign certifier guesses the shorter-window chart with width $\lceil\sqrt{K}\rceil$, reduces its existential defect test to SAT and uses the reference to exclude a defect. Apart from the polylogarithmic public seed and fixed log powers the cost is $O(1+\sqrt K)$. A smaller chart would beat the common offer deadline $N/\operatorname{polylog}N$. ## Two simultaneous permutation assertions can encode an entire parity input

Let $x$ have even length $k\ge2$. Layer $j$ of a graph has vertices $(j,0),(j,1)$, for $0\le j\le k$. In layer $j<k$ put edges from $(j,b)$ to $(j+1,b\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}x_j)$. Add a closing edge $e_*$ from $(k,0)$ to $(0,0)$. Without it the undirected graph consists of two disjoint paths. The closing edge lies on a cycle exactly when $\bigoplus_j x_j=0$. That cycle has odd length $k+1$.

Use distinct word names for the edges and vertices, plus two spare words, and round the bank size up to a power of two. Set $P(i)=i\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}M[i]$. Set $Q(i)=h_i(P(i))$, where on an edge row $i$ the relabeling $h_i$ swaps the two endpoint words of its edge, and on every other row it is identity. Both normalizers are calibrated because row names for edges differ from vertex names. They take a constant number of word operations and at most one packed input probe. The old assertion is that **both** $P$ and $Q$ are even permutations. The genuine zero start satisfies both for every $x$. A fixed block makes one writable load and signals a defect exactly if $P(e_*)=(k,0)$.

In any permissible state, call an edge active if its two tables disagree there. Direct it from its $P$ value to its $Q$ value. Each value occurs once in each full permutation. Cancel all equal entries to obtain indegree equal to outdegree at every vertex, with both at most one. The active edges therefore form vertex-disjoint directed cycles. Triggering the load forces the closing edge to be active. Thus it requires even input parity and activity at all $k+1$ edges of its cycle. Each active edge row has nonzero native memory.

Conversely, for even input parity orient the cycle along the two endpoints of the designated closing edge as prescribed. For each cycle edge $e$, swap its row name with the word for its tail in the identity permutation $P$. These disjoint swaps yield $P(e)$ equal to its tail. An extra swap of the two spare words makes $P$ even if necessary. Now $Q$ differs from $P$ by a cycle of length $k+1$ on vertex outputs. It too is even. This realizes an actual defect. Consequently the existence of a defect in this single-load block equals even parity on $k$ independent public bits. On every successful branch of a sound complete nondeterministic tester for this family, every input bit must be covered by a read. Flipping an uncovered bit preserves all replies along the same branch and reverses the correct answer. A packed RAM needs $\Omega(k/\log k)$ public probes. This lower bound covers arbitrary certificates for the test, including concise descriptions of dense states. It applies to these locally supplied rows without length-dependent input-specific preprocessing. It excludes a polylogarithmic generic closure test for two simultaneous assertions even when the actual initial memory is zero. It gives no SAT time exclusion and leaves open formats that carry extra, charged information across the cuts.

The public-bit probe bound alone permits preprocessing of a short seed for a virtual input. A second construction makes the two-word-table test NEXP-complete even with a succinct input. A single old permutation row with explicit pins behaves differently for the very same load. Append its required value at the queried address to the pins after applying the local normalizer. Check injectivity, and check the unique completion’s sign if at most one address is free. Otherwise either sign extends the request. This costs polynomial time in the description and pin list. The two-row reduction preserves the fact that zero memory meets the starting assertions. Both can therefore be established honestly from the genuine zero state in a preceding idle step. The hardness concerns propagation from their full intersection. [Succinct rainbow paths and the two-assertion reduction](rainbow-permutation-defects.md)

## How large a locally decodable violating memory has to be

The succinct construction gives a conditional consequence for all actual witnesses to this one-load test. Fix $d\in\{1,2,3\}$ and suppose deterministic SAT takes $O(n^d\operatorname{polylog}n)$ time on explicit $n$-bit inputs. For some fixed $K_d$ there are arbitrarily large affirmative instances of the two-permutation test, on a bank of $D$ words with a $\operatorname{poly}(\log D)$-bit public description, for which **every** compatible memory triggering the specified load has address-to-word circuit description length greater than
$$
 D^{1/d}/(\log D)^{K_d}.
$$
The result even applies if both signs are discarded and the two normalized tables need only be permutations. It concerns the allowed counterfactual memories of this test. The computer's genuine initialized state is zero throughout the preceding idle step.

To see the cost, guess a topologically ordered Boolean circuit of description length $b$ for the native starting word at an address, with the short parameters available. Evaluate it at the designated address. One ordinary existential CNF detects a collision in either normalized table by guessing two unequal addresses, evaluating the circuit at both, and comparing either pair of outputs. The formula and its generation take $(1+b+\operatorname{poly}(\log D))\operatorname{polylog}D$ bits and steps, absorbing the local row circuits. Run the reference solver on it and accept the proposed memory if the closing word is right and no collision exists. Equal finite domain and codomain make both tables permutations. On the constructed family *every* unsigned witness implies a rainbow path, and a path gives even witnesses. Ignoring the signs here makes the test sound even for malicious proposed circuits. Thus small circuits on all affirmative instances would give a nondeterministic decision bound of $(1+b+\operatorname{poly}(\log D))^d\operatorname{polylog}D$ with no separate cost for reading $D$ native words.

For the fixed-log conclusion, choose a language in $\mathrm{NTIME}(2^m)$ outside $\mathrm{NTIME}(2^m/m^2)$ on multitape machines by the nondeterministic time hierarchy. Use the near-linear regular layout in the succinct reduction and pad by identity rows to $2^m\le D\le 2^m m^A$ for a fixed $A$. The polynomial-time reduction from the hierarchy language has description size $m^{O(1)}$. The logarithmic overhead for checking a word-RAM transcript on nondeterministic tapes also has a fixed exponent. Choose $K_d$ larger than the combined fixed losses divided by $d$, with slack for $m^2$. If all sufficiently large affirmative instances on this reduction had a circuit within the displayed budget, the clocked verifier would decide the language within $O(2^m/m^2)$. Finitely many short lengths can be hardwired. This proves the existential lower bound on the minimum description, without presuming that a near-minimal circuit can be found.
