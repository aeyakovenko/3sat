# How close to pairwise independence can 3-SAT witnesses be?

Let a proper 3-CNF have three distinct variables in each of its $m$ clauses. For a probability distribution on its satisfying assignments define the pair bias as the maximum of $|\mathbb E(-1)^{x_i+x_j}|$ over distinct occurring variables. Require each occurring variable to be unbiased. The distribution is a promise on positive files. An algorithm receives just the formula and must also reject every unsatisfiable file.

* For every fixed $c$, pair bias at most $c/m$ gives a deterministic polynomial-time promise algorithm. Strictly below $2/(3m)$, solving one linear system suffices. The constant is sharp for that single-system guarantee.
* For every fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$, the promise with pair bias at most $m^{-1+\varepsilon}$ is NP-hard. In fact, for every fixed positive bias bound, there is a deterministic linear-time, linear-bit-length reduction from arbitrary proper 3-SAT. This preserves each of the proposed linear, quadratic and cubic RAM deadlines at fixed bias.
* The promise asks for the existence of one distribution on satisfying assignments. The reduction constructs the formula without being given an assignment.

Balanced pairwise independence on the satisfying inputs of a single predicate has been used in approximation resistance. Robust satisfiability asks which partial assignments extend. The promise here requires one global probability distribution on actual solutions and bounds its numerical bias on every variable pair, including all auxiliary variables. Priority for the quantitative bounds here is unestablished. [Austrin and Mossel](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.2300) · [Abramsky, Gottlob and Kolaitis on robust CSP](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2013/73)

A formula with at most two satisfying assignments and at least two occurring variables cannot achieve pair bias below one with unbiased singletons. Two support points would have to be complementary, equiprobable assignments, so each variable pair has constant XOR. The existing signed one-sort network handles that small-count promise by a different identity. [Decision at two solutions](one-sort-for-bounded-ambiguity-sat.md)

## The deterministic algorithm at inverse clause count

In a satisfied clause write $u,v,w\in\{-1,+1\}$ for the signs of the three literal truth bits, with false represented by $+1$. The indicator of all three being false vanishes. Expanding $(1+u)(1+v)(1+w)=0$ gives
$$
 2[\text{even number of true literals}]=1+uvw=-(u+v+w+uv+uw+vw).
$$
Call a clause with even truth parity a defect. If all singleton means vanish and pair bias is at most $\delta$, its defect probability is at most $3\delta/2$. By summing over clauses, some satisfying assignment has at most $\lfloor3m\delta/2\rfloor$ defects. It suffices to bound the pairs co-occurring in clauses for this direction.

Set $Ax=b$ to be the system over the two-element field requiring **odd** truth parity in every clause, taking the literal signs into account. For every set $D$ of at most $k=\lfloor3m\delta/2\rfloor$ rows, solve $Ax=b+1_D$. For each exceptional row choose one of its three literals to be true and add that linear pin. There are at most $3^{|D|}$ choices. A solution satisfies the original formula, and the search finds any satisfying assignment with at most $k$ defects. This proves the polynomial bound for fixed $c$ without a distribution oracle. At $k=0$ every solution of the single system is already a witness.

There is a small improvement at odd clause counts. If $Ax=b$ is inconsistent, some rows have zero coefficient sum and right-hand sum one. A row basis and one additional row suffice, so the certificate uses at most $v+1$ rows on $v$ variables. Its size must be even, since each selected row has exactly three coefficients and every column has even selected degree. Apply the same expectation argument to this subsystem, in which every assignment has at least one defect. Its size $s$ satisfies $\delta\ge 2/(3s)$, in particular $\delta\ge 2/(3(m-1))$ for odd $m\ge3$.

For exact pairwise independence there is also a recognition criterion. The odd-parity system must be consistent, and its row space must contain no nonzero vector of weight one or two. Necessity follows because the distribution is supported on the affine solution set, and such a vector fixes a singleton or pair character there. Sufficiency follows by taking the uniform distribution on the affine solution set.

## Up to two defects within one elimination cost

For bias strictly below $2/m$, at most two defects suffice. After one elimination, all $O(m^2)$ candidates can be checked in $O(m^2)$ additional RAM steps. Here are details to account for what each check reads.

Compute an invertible row transformation $E$ making $EA$ reduced echelon, and put $h=Eb$. The zero rows of $EA$ determine consistency. On those rows, let $s$ be the restriction of $h$, and $s_i$ the restriction of column $i$ of $E$. With two defects $i,j$, consistency is exactly equality of $s_i$ and $s+s_j$. Intern all these bit strings, the zero string and $s$ in a binary trie in $O(m^2)$ steps in total. Empty and singleton sets use the same test with missing columns zero.

For a consistent right-hand side, set the free coordinates to zero to obtain a particular solution. Its value at a requested pivot coordinate is a bit of $h$ plus at most two bits of $E$, all addressed by row and column. For each variable also precompute the vector expressing its homogeneous coordinate as a linear form in the free coordinates and intern these vectors in a second trie. Pinning one literal adds one equation on those free coordinates. At most two such equations are inconsistent exactly when one has zero left side and right side one, or when two identical left sides have unequal right sides. The tests use their precomputed identifiers. Each candidate needs at most nine choices of pins. Free vectors of length zero and a system of full row rank are also covered.

There are at most $3m$ occurring variables. Elementary packed elimination on the rows of $[A\mid I]$, pivoting only within $A$, costs $O(m^3/\log(m+2)+m^2)$ word steps. One can instead compute the transformation by fast deterministic echelon reduction, using for example Strassen multiplication to obtain $O(m^{\log_2 7})$ field operations. Initial parsing and interning add $O(N)$ steps for an $N$-bit DIMACS file. Thus this promise has a genuinely subcubic deterministic RAM algorithm. The two-defect preprocessing computes a decision bit. A satisfying assignment, when requested, can be reconstructed from a successful candidate's reduced equations. [Jeannerod, Pernet and Storjohann, reduced echelon transformations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.5717) · [Independent two-defect solver and exhaustive checks](../audits/two_parity_defects_audit.py)

## A sharp example from a prism

The smallest example uses three variables and two clauses, $(x\lor y\lor z)$ and $(\neg x\lor\neg y\lor\neg z)$. One proper clause always has an odd-parity witness. The two clauses require opposite parities, yet have six common satisfying assignments. Under their uniform distribution the variables are unbiased and every pair has correlation $-1/3=-2/(3m)$. This is the first failure of the single-system algorithm, even with unlimited time to solve that system.

Take the prism on two cycles of the same odd length $r\ge5$, with a rung at each position. It has $m=2r$ vertices and $3r$ edges. Put one bit on each edge. Each vertex contributes a three-literal clause. Choose opposite literal signs at the two ends of each edge. Equivalently, orient each edge and require at least one incoming edge per vertex. For example, orient both rim cycles cyclically. The total number of true incidences is $3r$, odd, whereas odd truth parity at all $2r$ vertices would give even total. Consequently every satisfying assignment has at least one defect.

For each chosen vertex $d$, flip its parity equation. The adjusted system is consistent. A connected graph's incidence matrix over the two-element field has image the even-weight subspace, as is seen by solving along a spanning tree from its leaves. Choose $d$ uniformly among the vertices, then sample an orientation uniformly from its adjusted solution space conditional on the clause at $d$ being true. All samples satisfy every clause and have precisely the one defect.

To compute the biases, note that the prism has no nonempty edge cuts of size one or two, and its only three-edge cuts are single-vertex stars. Indeed, a vertex subset has two cyclic membership strings. Its cut size is the sum of their transition counts and their Hamming distance. Two nonconstant strings contribute at least four transitions. Two distinct constant strings differ on every rung. If just one is nonconstant, size at most three forces Hamming distance one, giving a vertex or its complement.

The cut space is the row space of the incidence matrix, hence exactly the characters constant on the affine solution set. At the defect vertex the three literal bits before conditioning are uniform on the four even patterns because within that triple the full star is the only nonempty constant character. Conditioning away $000$ leaves the other three equally likely. The three singleton characters and the three pair characters on that triple all have mean $-1/3$. A character anywhere else has conditional mean zero unless, modulo the cut space, it is zero or one of the three singleton characters at the defect. This follows by averaging on the fibers of the projection to that triple.

For a particular edge, only defects at its two ends contribute to its mean. The opposite endpoint signs cancel. For a pair of edges meeting at $v$, let $g$ be the third edge there. Only defects at $v$ and at the far end of $g$ contribute, by the cut classification. At $v$ their literal pair mean is $-1/3$. With the defect at the other end, odd parity at $v$ makes the pair character minus the character of $g$ at $v$, and the two endpoint signs on $g$ are opposite. This gives $-1/3$ again. Disjoint edges cannot join one more edge to make a cut, so their mean product is zero. The resulting pair bias is exactly $2/(3m)$. Taking $g$ disjoint equal-size prisms with independent distributions gives minimum defect count $g$ and bias exactly $2g/(3m)$, now writing $m$ for the total clause count. These give equality in the expectation argument at every integer defect budget. Starting from a single prism, add one clause on three fresh variables and give them an independent uniform odd-parity assignment. This preserves the obstruction and pair bias. The resulting odd clause counts attain the sharper bound $2/(3(m-1))$. [Exhaustion of all 32,768 pentagonal-prism orientations](../audits/prism_pair_bias_audit.py)

## Encoding arbitrary 3-SAT with small pair bias

Fix $t\ge1$ and an input proper 3-CNF $F$ on occurring variables. Make $t$ blocks. Each has one logical activation bit $a$ and one logical bit per source variable. Represent each logical bit by the XOR of three fresh physical bits, called its shares. Require the XOR of all logical activation bits to be one. In each block include the following logical tests for each source clause $C$.

* If $C$ has an odd number of negated literals, require $C$.
* If it has an even number, require $\neg a\lor C$.

When $a=1$, these are exactly the source tests. When $a=0$ and all source bits are zero, every test passes. More precisely, the unique forbidden **logical** tuple for each test has odd Hamming weight. For the first type its weight is the number of negated source literals. For the second add one for activation. It is this parity property that will preserve small correlations after the conversion to proper ternary clauses.

For a test on $q=3$ or $4$ different triples of shares, enumerate the $4^q$ raw patterns $p$ with that forbidden decoded tuple. For each, include a width-$s$ clause excluding $p$, where $s=3q$. Place the bits by share index, with the first shares of all $q$ groups followed by their second and third shares. Write $l_j=x_j\mathbin{\oplus}p_j$ for the truth bit of the literal asserting that raw coordinate $j$ differs from $p_j$.

Use fresh state bits $y_0,\ldots,y_{s-1}$ for **each** wide clause and write $y_s=1\mathbin{\oplus}y_0$ as a literal, without adding a coordinate. Enforce
$$
 (l_j\lor\neg y_j\lor y_{j+1})\quad\land\quad
 (l_j\lor y_j\lor\neg y_{j+1})\qquad(0\le j<s).
$$
A false literal forces equal adjacent states. A true literal allows either transition. The complemented closing state requires an odd total number of flips. Thus an extension exists exactly when some $l_j$ is true. To enforce the global activation parity, take the $3t$ activation shares in the same round-robin order in a separate cycle. Use exact XOR links $y_{j+1}=y_j\oplus x_j$, each expressed by the four proper clauses forbidding its four incorrect triples. The same complemented closure enforces odd parity. Every physical clause now has three distinct variables, including at $t=1$. An active block gives a source model. Conversely any source model can be placed in one active block with all other blocks inactive.

## One distribution works for every physical pair

Given a source model, choose one uniformly random block $d$ to be active, put that model there, and set all logical bits outside it to zero. Conditional on these logical values, choose the three shares of each group independently across groups, uniformly on its four encodings. Every share is unbiased and every pair of distinct shares is independent, including two from the same group.

In the global cycle use the prescribed XOR increments. In each inactive wide-clause cycle choose increments $y_j\oplus y_{j+1}=l_j$. The raw selected shares have even total parity (all decoded bits zero), while the forbidden pattern has odd total parity. The increments consequently have odd parity, as the closure requires. In active wide-clause cycles flip at the first true literal only. In **every** cycle choose the initial state by a fresh independent fair bit. Each state is thus unbiased even conditional on all the raw shares. A state paired with a share, or states belonging to different cycles, have zero pair bias.

Two different states of one inactive cycle have a difference given by the XOR of the literals in a nonempty proper interval of the round-robin order. Its cyclic indicator has exactly two transitions. It cannot be a union of full triples, because a nonconstant union-of-triples indicator repeats the same $q$-bit word three times and has at least six. A proper nonempty subset of a triple of uniform fixed-parity shares has fair XOR. Independence across groups makes the interval parity fair. The same proof applies to the global cycle conditional on any selected block, including when $t=1$. Only two states of the *same active wide-clause cycle* can therefore be correlated. A given physical cycle belongs to the chosen block with probability $1/t$, proving absolute pair bias at most $1/t$. In joint-probability terms each pair atom differs from $1/4$ by at most $1/(4t)$.

The order $1/t$ is necessary for this particular construction. On three source variables forbid the seven triples other than $001$. The unique witness differs in two coordinates from $010$, whose excluding clause is unguarded. In any distribution on satisfying outputs, some block is active with probability at least $1/t$, since every assignment has an active block. Take any one wide clause in that block excluding a raw encoding of $010$. When the block is active the XOR of its nine literal truth bits is even.

Let $d_j$ be its state increment, including the complemented closing link. Every satisfying assignment has $d_j\le l_j$ and an odd number of increments. Thus $\sum_j(l_j-d_j)\ge1$ on the even-parity event and is always nonnegative. With unbiased physical variables each literal is fair, giving
$$
 \sum_{j=0}^8 \mathbb E[(-1)^{d_j}]
 = 2\,\mathbb E\sum_{j=0}^8(l_j-d_j)\ \ge\ 2/t .
$$
Each increment character is a signed product of two distinct state characters. Thus every distribution with unbiased singleton bits on this output has pair bias at least $2/(9t)$. Altering the distribution alone cannot improve the order of the guarantee.

## An exact quantity that survives adding another block

For a satisfiable source, the minimum number of physical parity defects among **all** satisfying assignments of the output is
$$
    2\min_{x\models F}
         \sum_{\substack{C\in F\\ C\text{ has two true literals at }x}}4^{q(C)},
    \qquad q(C)=3+[\text{$C$ has an even number of negations}].
$$
In particular going from $t$ to $t+1$ blocks leaves this optimum exactly unchanged. Relative to the source's unweighted minimum it increases by a factor between 128 and 512 when that minimum is positive.

Indeed, in a complemented conditional cycle the allowed increment $d_j$ equals zero if $l_j=0$. The two physical clauses at a link both have odd truth parity when $d_j=l_j$. If $l_j=1$ and $d_j=0$, **both** have even truth parity. A nonzero truth vector of odd weight thus costs zero, by flipping on every true coordinate. A nonzero vector of even weight costs exactly two at optimum, by omitting just one flip. Fresh state bits make these choices independent across cycles conditional on the raw shares. On an active block the XOR of the raw truth bits in each wide clause is the XOR of the three original literal truth bits. This follows by grouping the three shares of each logical bit; the guarded activation coordinate agrees with its forbidden value. All $4^{q(C)}$ cycles therefore have the same parity. The global exact-XOR links have zero physical defects on every legal transition. At least one block is active, so its cost gives the lower bound. Place a minimizing source model in one block and zeros elsewhere to attain it.

The seven-clause source with unique witness $001$ has minimum exactly 384 in the output. Its three clauses forbidding $010,100,111$ have two true literals at the witness and are unguarded. Appending $h$ disjoint such source blocks to any positive $h$-clause formula forces at least $384h$ output defects in the resulting construction, independent of $t$. The extension preserves satisfiability and has linear encoded size on deduplicated inputs with dense new names. Thus a source carrying arbitrary SAT decisions can sit far from the single parity system. A solver may simply ignore disjoint, visibly satisfiable components.

## Uniform sampling over all solutions gives a different answer

The witness distribution used in the reduction can be distinguished from uniform sampling on all solutions by looking at just two adjacent cycle states. Under the latter distribution their absolute correlation is at least $1/2-4^{-q}$ in every width-$3q$ conditional cycle, regardless of $t$. Each individual bit is still unbiased.

For a nonzero raw truth vector of weight $u$, a wide clause has exactly $2^u$ extensions. Choose an odd subset of its true coordinates as increments, and choose either initial state. For one passing decoded test and any fixed share encoding $x$, the exponent in the product of the extension counts over all its forbidden patterns is $\sum_p |x\oplus p|=3q\,4^q/2$, since exactly half of those patterns contain a one at each coordinate. Thus conditional on passing decoded values, all raw encodings of **all** logical bits have equal total multiplicity, even when several tests share a raw coordinate. The global XOR cycle contributes two extensions. Given the raw bits, states in a wide cycle use a uniform odd subset and an independent fair seed.

At link $j$, a false literal gives increment zero. If just that literal is true the increment is one. If it and some other literal are true, the increment is fair by toggling the two corresponding increments. Hence its sign expectation is $1/2-\Pr[l=e_j]$. Conditional on the decoded values the raw scope is uniform on $4^q$ words, at most one of which gives $l=e_j$. The increment character is the product of the adjacent state signs up to the fixed closing sign. This gives the claimed lower bound and explains exactly where the use of a chosen witness distribution enters the circuit and RAM reductions.

## Output size and what follows for the RAM clock

Write $h\ge1$ for the number of distinct source clauses after deleting duplicates. At most $3h$ variable names occur. Each unguarded test contributes $2\cdot9\cdot4^3=1152$ ternary clauses and each guarded one at most $2\cdot12\cdot4^4=6144$. The activation cycle adds $12t$ clauses. Thus the output has $M\le6156th$ clauses, $O(th)$ variables and $O(th\log(th+2))$ bits with dense decimal identifiers. An empty source formula can be replaced by a fixed satisfiable proper clause before this construction.

For a fixed $0<\varepsilon<1$, choose an integer exponent $a>(1-\varepsilon)/\varepsilon$ and $t=\lceil B(h+1)^a\rceil$ with sufficiently large fixed integer $B$. Then $1/t\le M^{-1+\varepsilon}$, since $t^\varepsilon\ge (6156h)^{1-\varepsilon}$. The output and construction time are polynomial. The strict slack in $a$ also absorbs the logarithm if the bias is measured against the encoded number of bits. Thus arbitrary 3-SAT reduces to the promised decision problem at any fixed exponent short of inverse size.

For fixed $t$, the cost is linear even on the original $N$-bit DIMACS files. Intern the occurring decimal names by a digit trie (ignore leading zeros when permitted). Sort each signed triple of identifiers, counting-sort the clause records by their three coordinates, and discard duplicates. All keys are $O(N)$. The original strings contain representative encodings of $h$ distinct clauses in disjoint positions. Over a fixed alphabet these require $\Omega(h\log(h+2))$ bits in total by counting short strings. Dense output names and their printed lengths therefore fit in $O_t(N)$, including the cost of emitting them. Unused declared variables do not enter the decision. The RAM uses logarithmic words with a fixed coefficient sufficient to address the polynomial workspace of the algorithm being simulated. In particular the echelon algorithm can address its quadratic storage.

For any fixed $\delta>0$, a deterministic timed procedure accepting all these positive promised files by $O(N^k)$ and accepting no unsatisfiable file by that time would decide ordinary proper 3-SAT in $O(N^k)$, for $k=1,2,3$. Compile with $t\ge1/\delta$, simulate to its deadline, otherwise reject. This uses a constant $t$. The polynomial-size reduction for vanishing bias has a different time overhead. Thus a hard-positive exhaustion can be moved to a fixed arbitrarily small pair bias without changing the proposed clock. Neither construction proves that any correct unrestricted solver misses such a clock. The verdict for bias $f(m)/m$ with $f(m)\to\infty$ subpolynomially is also left open by this argument.

## Superlinear circuits under a vanishing pair-bias promise

Let $\sigma>0$ be an exponent from the preprint's list-distinctness theorem over $\mathbb F_2$. For any fixed $0<a<\sigma$, circuits that distinguish every unsatisfiable file from every positive file admitting unbiased singletons and pair bias at most $(\log_2 N)^{-a}$ need
$$
                 \Omega\bigl(N(\log N)^{\sigma-a}\bigr)
$$
bounded-fan-in Boolean gates at every sufficiently large byte-aligned DIMACS length $N$. This holds for files with dense identifiers and three distinct variables per clause. In particular $a=\sigma/2$ gives the same positive exponent in the bias and gate bounds. The new step is preserving the list's bit projection while making the global witness distribution nearly pairwise independent. The quantitative gate lower bound comes from the imported list theorem. [Upstream Theorem 2.2](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/build/obstruction.tex#L40-L79) · [Earlier SAT projection and circuit-to-network proof](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md)

For width parameter $w$, the input consists of $2^w$ records of $2w$ bits. Verifying distinctness takes $\Omega(2^w w^{1+\sigma})$ Boolean gates by the cited transfer. Its projection to proper SAT detects a duplicate and has $H=\Theta(2^w)$ physical clauses. Every source sign, token boundary and clause position is fixed by $w$. The only varying digits are individual copied list bits in positive decimal identifiers. Each source identifier from $1$ to the public $V\le3H$ occurs on every list. Apply the construction above to the physical clauses in their fixed order, retaining duplicates. Each expanded test and the indexing of its private variables now have data-independent positions.

Set $t=\lceil(3w)^a\rceil$. For each source identifier $x$ give its $3t$ raw shares names $Kx+j$, for $0\le j<3t$ and $K=10^{\lceil\log_{10}(3t)\rceil}$. In ASCII this appends fixed digits to the *original* decimal name. All activated-test signs and raw-pattern signs are public; each occurrence of a data-dependent digit is still a copy of the same original list bit. Other variables receive fixed numbers, and the header is fixed. Density is possible without any extra clauses. There are at most $K(V+1)\le120tH$ holes below the largest marked identifier, whereas the wide-clause cycles already have at least $9\cdot4^3tH=576tH$ private states. Assign the first states to the public holes and assign the remaining fresh variables consecutive higher numbers. Every raw share and every state occurs on every file. The public count and maximum are $O(t2^w)$.

The byte-aligned output length $L_w$ is $\Theta(2^w t w)$. The upper bound counts all physical clauses and identifier digits. For the lower bound there are $\Omega(2^w t)$ distinct occurring state names, whose decimal strings alone require $\Omega(2^w t\log(2^w t))$ bits. Adjacent such lengths have bounded ratio. For any sufficiently large byte-aligned target $N$ take the last $w$ with $L_w\le N$ and append public spaces before a newline. Then $N=\Theta(2^w t w)$ and $\log_2 N=w+O(\log w)<3w$. The promised bias on every positive image is at most $1/t\le(\log_2 N)^{-a}$. Every other image is unsatisfiable. A circuit for the promise therefore gives a circuit for distinctness by substituting input bits and constants for the projected pins and negating the verdict. Dividing the list bound by $t=\Theta(w^a)$ relative to output length gives the claim. [Decimal projection audit](../audits/pair_bias_projection_audit.py)

For a rational choice of $a$ strictly between zero and $\sigma/2$, one globally correct RAM program handles this same circuit-hard family in $O(N)$ time on either verdict. Generate the projected file as a template whose varying low digit bits are labelled by their source coordinates. The comparison with the input recovers each list bit at its first appearance and checks all its repetitions. On a match, two counting-sort passes on the two $w$-bit record halves decide distinctness in $O(2^w)$ steps. Fall back to a complete SAT procedure on any mismatch. Every source bit has at least one occurrence. Agreement with the entire template therefore certifies an actual list projection.

To select the width for the padded family, compute the template lengths for candidates with $2^w t_w w\le C N$, using the lower bound on $L_w$ and a sufficiently large fixed $C$. Their total unpadded length is $O(N)$ by geometric growth. Choose the last candidate with $L_w\le N$. Tables of fresh identifiers, holes and decimal strings can be generated in time linear in the corresponding template length by indexing consecutive integers and printing their digits. Each source-dependent field is an unchanged original name plus a fixed suffix, so its labelled digits can be written within that budget. Computing $t_w$ exactly for rational $a$ needs only integer power comparisons on polylogarithmic-sized numbers. Generate and compare the padded file only for the selected width. This gives actual scan-time controls for the new witness-distribution promise as well as for the original lists.
