# Forecasts from arbitrarily rare events on the balanced SAT family

Consider the [balanced, public-label family of ordinary 3-CNFs](almost-everywhere-hard-sat-forecasts.md), with its original labels, advice and formula generator. Fix its positive integer parameter $A$. Assume a deterministic logarithmic-word RAM solves explicit SAT in $O(N^k)$ steps, where $k=1,2,3$, with arbitrary workspace. Put $e_1=1$, $e_2=8$, $e_3=18$. For every fixed predictor there is a constant $B$ such that at each sufficiently large stage $j$, with $M=2^j$, its accuracy satisfies
$$
   \left|\Pr[\text{prediction equals the SAT verdict}]-\tfrac12\right|
       \le (j+1)^{-A}
$$
at the clock $T=\lfloor 2^{\lfloor j/e_k\rfloor}/(j+1)^B\rfloor$. This has scale $N_j^{1/e_k}/(\log N_j)^{O(1)}$ in encoded bits. The same bound holds for every common advice of up to $\lfloor\log_2(j+1)\rfloor$ bits. The label is drawn uniformly **first**, then the prediction uses the following distribution on that label. Thus rare events at one label do not downweight it relative to other labels.

At a label $y$, allow two sets $S_{y,0},S_{y,1}$ of primary strings of the same bounded length $r$. Predict one with probability
$$
     p_y=\frac{|S_{y,1}|}{|S_{y,0}|+|S_{y,1}|},
$$
using $p_y=1/2$ if both sets are empty. Membership in either set has a fixed nondeterministic $T$-step native verifier given the formula, public label, stage and advice. Each distinct primary string counts once for its color, independent of how many auxiliary witnesses work. The primary and auxiliary bit lengths must be at most $T(j+1)^{O(1)}$, with uniform computable bounds and a fixed polynomial overhead for reading or writing the packed strings. In particular a randomized word RAM can consume fresh independent random bits at every instruction and keep a run according to its private tape and output. Conditioning its answer on keeping the run is covered even if only one full tape survives. An indexed independent private coin array can instead be exposed at first visits, remembering replies on repeats. Padding unused fresh coins then gives the same output law. All timeouts and keep tests belong to the stated clock.

Thus finding an above-threshold forecast infinitely often at the stated budget contradicts the relevant SAT hypothesis. In particular, having to produce an accepted sample by rejection sampling can be dispensed with. No above-threshold sampler at these budgets is known here. Unconditionally, the previous theorem treats deterministic machines and short random tapes.

## One reporter row for each advice string

For each of the $2Q$ public labels ($Q=2^h$ in the balanced construction), compute an approximation $p'_y\in[0,1]$ with additive error at most $O((j+1)^{-A-2})$. The counting algorithms below run in $T^{e_k}(j+1)^{O(1)}$ per label. The reporter uses the fixed hypothesized SAT program, which may be hardwired. It generates formula words locally from the label while verifying runs, before applying the SAT simulations. With $Q$, the number of advice strings, and $(j+1)$ all polynomially related, the total time fits the native reporter budget $M/(j+1)$ for a sufficiently large fixed $B$. All tested predicates have fixed code. Their polynomial overhead exponents can be chosen independently of the cutoff exponent $B$ by providing the numerical cap as stage data. The forecast is computed without the hidden Walsh slope.

To output just one binary table per advice, round these fractions simultaneously against all possible slopes. For each slope $a$ let $s_{a,y}$ be $1$ when that slope predicts a true SAT verdict at $y$ and $-1$ otherwise. Include the negatives of all these sign rows as well. There are $2Q$ rows and $L=2Q$ coordinates. Each row has initial weight one. Use a positive dyadic $\lambda\le1/2$ within a constant factor of $1/\sqrt L$. At coordinate $y$, for a candidate bit $z$ update each row weight by the positive factor $1+\lambda s_{a,y}(z-p'_y)$. The weighted average of the two possible new total weights, with bit-one probability $p'_y$, equals the old total. Choose an endpoint at which that total does not increase. Each final weight is at most $2Q$. The inequality $\ln(1+u)\ge u-u^2$ for $|u|\le1/2$ then yields
$$
      \left|\sum_y s_{a,y}(z_y-p'_y)\right|
        \le \ln(2Q)/\lambda+L\lambda
$$
for every slope by applying it to both signs. Dividing by $L$ bounds precisely the change in expected **accuracy**. Rounding the probability estimates first to a common polynomial-size dyadic grid adds at most its mesh to this error. Products of $L$ weight factors have a common denominator with polynomially many bits in $j$, so ordinary integer arithmetic implements every decision within $\mathrm{poly}_A(j)$ time. This is an elementary conditional-expectation rounding argument. The rounded table fits the existing reporter limit with every original advice string present.

The native portfolio's actual bound before simplifying constants is $\tfrac12\sqrt{(j+1)(2^{b+1}-1)/Q}$, where $Q=2^{b+(2A+2)\lceil\log_2(j+2)\rceil}$. This is $O(j^{-A-1/2})$. Both the probability error and the rounding term $O(\log Q/\sqrt Q)$ are $o(j^{-A})$. This leaves the claimed threshold eventually. The argument controls the mean conditional accuracy even if the signs of correlations differ between stages.

## Estimating a conditional probability on tiny sets

For numerator count $a$ and denominator count $c$ use respectively the color-one set and the disjoint union of the two tagged sets. If $a$ or $c$ vanishes, one existential test handles it. Otherwise take $m=\lceil(j+1)^{A+4}\rceil$ independent copies of the primary strings. The two product sets have cardinalities $a^m,c^m$. Membership remains nondeterministic by guessing a verifying extension for each copy. The new primary length $H$ and membership cost are at most $T(j+1)^{O_A(1)}$. Formula generation from a supplied label costs a fixed power of $j$ per visited word. A logarithmic count estimate with additive error $O(\log(H+2))=O(j)$ on each product count gives
$$
   \left|\frac{d_a-d_c}{m}-\log_2(a/c)\right|=O(j/m).
$$
Consequently $2^{(d_a-d_c)/m}$, clamped to one, approximates the conditional probability to additive error $O(j/m)$. Exponentially small answers can be set to zero as soon as the displayed estimate is below the chosen grid mesh. For remaining answers $|d_a-d_c|=O(m\log j)$ after handling positive exponents by clamping. Comparing each dyadic grid point raised to the $m$th power to $2^{d_a-d_c}$ requires only polynomially many bits in $j$. The large individual logarithms of the counts are stored in binary.

For $k=1$ apply the [projected approximate-count algorithm under linear SAT](near-native-counting.md) to each product set. It gives a constant additive error in the logarithm at cost $(H+T m)(j+1)^{O(1)}$, including a fixed number of quantified blocks for membership and the local generator overhead. That algorithm implements random hashing and amplification by a span of translated seeds. Its answer in a gap of the hash test can be chosen arbitrarily. Only its guaranteed two extremes are used. [Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1137/0214060) · [Viola](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2005/137/)

For $k=2,3$, the following two-block version trades a quadratic increase in the matrix time for one fewer SAT slowdown. It is the isolating-hash test of Sipser and Stockmeyer with its bit lengths charged. Given a nonempty product set $S\subseteq\{0,1\}^H$, choose $u=H+1$ binary Toeplitz maps to $d$ bits. Ask if every $x\in S$ is isolated by at least one chosen map, meaning no different $y\in S$ gives that map the same value. If $|S|\le2^{d-1}$, each fixed $x$ fails isolation under a fresh random map with probability at most $(|S|-1)2^{-d}\le1/2$. The Toeplitz collision bound follows by exposing the successive new diagonals against a nonzero difference string. With $u$ independent maps, a union bound over at most $2^H$ points leaves positive success probability. In the other direction each map isolates at most $2^d$ points. Thus a successful test implies $|S|\le u2^d$. For $d=0$ take the unique constant map. At $d=H$ the identity is available. Extra output rows preserve every established isolation, so existence is monotone in $d$. The least successful $d$ is found with $O(\log(H+2))$ tests and lies between $\log_2 |S|-\log_2 u$ and $\log_2 |S|+2$. [Stockmeyer, *On Approximation Algorithms for #P*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0214060)

The first block guesses the $u$ maps. A counterexample consists of $x\in S$, one $y_i\in S$ different from $x$ for each map, their verifying witnesses, and equal map values for each pair. A universal block rules out all such counterexamples. The deterministic matrix checks memberships and collisions in $uT(j+1)^{O_A(1)}$ time. Evaluate each full Toeplitz image by binary convolution. For example embed the two bit vectors as radix-$2^s$ digit strings with $s=\lceil\log_2(2H+2)\rceil$. Fast integer multiplication produces all ordinary convolution coefficients without carries between digits. Take the parity of the appropriate digits. An $O(H\,\mathrm{polylog}(H+2))$ bit-time multiplication bound suffices. Both quantifier lengths fit the same $T^2(j+1)^{O(1)}$ cap. A near-linear bit-length Cook reduction eliminates the innermost block using one SAT solver call and complementation. Eliminating the outer block the same way costs at most $T^{2k^2}(j+1)^{O(1)}$ in total. The fixed address widths and the cost of generating ordinary clauses are absorbed in those polynomial-in-$j$ factors. [RAM-to-3-CNF compiler](projected-parity-holes.md#a-parsimonious-ordinary-3-cnf-near-the-native-clock) · [Integer multiplication](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2021/193-2/p04)

There is a stronger clock for sparsely supported postselection. If both counts are at most $K$ for a known cap, find each *exactly* by binary search on whether $s$ lexicographically increasing primary strings have verifying extensions, for $s\le K+1$. One bounded existential block writes the sorted strings, guesses their witnesses, and checks all of them. With a computable $K\le M^{O(1)}$, the reporter fits if $((K+1)(T+1))^k\le M/(j+1)^B$ for a sufficiently large fixed $B$, with the cap itself available in $\mathrm{poly}(j)$ time. Even if more than $K$ strings exist on other labels, assigning a fair coin at such labels gives the same bound on the resulting forecast: the $(K+1)$st test detects this case without assuming global sparsity. In particular, for a polynomial-in-$j$ number of surviving primary strings, clocks of order $M^{1/k}/\mathrm{poly}(j)$ suffice. Auxiliary accepting witnesses may have arbitrary multiplicities.

One can also exploit a bound $K$ between polynomial and exponential. For $S$ a product of $m$ tagged factors with at most $2K$ members apiece, $u=1+\lceil m\log_2(2K)\rceil$ independent maps already give the union bound. Use this when it decreases $H+1$. The two-block cost becomes $((T+1)(1+\log_2(2K)))^{k^2}(j+1)^{O_A(1)}$. This interpolation assumes the cap holds on the tested sets, or an independent mechanism supplies that fact. Inferring the support size by first listing $K+1$ strings may exceed this clock.

## Testing short proofs with unequal counts

One candidate predictor samples a color and a proposed short proof history, retains verifiable histories, and guesses the color of the surviving one. If the two colors have $W_0(y),W_1(y)$ different valid primary histories, its centered accuracy at the true slope $a_j$ is
$$
   \frac1{2L}\sum_y s_{a_j,y}\,\frac{W_1(y)-W_0(y)}{W_1(y)+W_0(y)},
$$
taking an empty fraction as zero. Thus either the candidate time bound fails or the signed sum of these *local count imbalances* is small under the SAT hypothesis. Requiring each retained proof to be sound is unnecessary. A keyed proof shared by both colors counts independently for each tagged pair. Common multiplicative padding of both counts at a label leaves the expression unchanged, even if the padding varies across labels.

A direct experiment modeled the full transcript as a uniformly guessed truth table and retained it when all local constraints checked out. For the parsimonious formula one correct color survives and the other has count zero, giving perfect prediction. This verifier pays for the full witness and its global checks. Removing the terminal constraint from both colors gives identical counts on every label and zero advantage. A proof of the unrestricted lower bound along this route would need imbalance with a verifier inside the stated clocks, also on the negative formulas. Membership in a large pair of sets by itself says nothing about its sign. Priority for the specialized forecast implication has not been established.

## An unconditional limit on locally verified survivor counts

Fix a genuine table $h$, a selector of support size $w$, and $t<w$ native clauses true at $h$. Fix one true supporting literal per retained clause at its value in $h$. The remaining subcube satisfies every retained clause and has at least $v=w-t$ free selected coordinates. Fix additional coordinates if needed so exactly $v$ free selected bits remain. On this subcube the target checksum is their parity up to a fixed sign. Comparisons between two checksum answers use the support of the XOR of their selectors in place of $w$.

Suppose each accepted tagged primary string on each proposed table has a positive witness such that its verification inspects at most $u\le v$ of these free bits. Inspections can be adaptive, all positions and masks are public, and one may choose any one verifying witness per accepted string. This counts distinct bit positions exposed, including the bits in any packed table words read. No upper bound on the number of accepting witnesses or primary strings is needed beyond finiteness. The two tagged sets determine the prediction with the empty-set fair fallback as before.

Under a uniform fill of the subcube the absolute prediction advantage is at most $u/v$. To see this, write $S(x)$ for the surviving tagged primary strings and let $\ell(x,i)$ be the fraction of $S(x)$ lost when free bit $i$ flips; use zero for an empty $S(x)$. For each survivor, flipping any bit outside its chosen certificate preserves the entire verification path. Hence $\mathbb E_i\ell(x,i)\le u/v$ at each $x$. For two nonempty sets the total variation between their uniform laws equals one minus their intersection size divided by the larger set size. The change in probability of predicting one along an edge $(x,x')$ is therefore at most $\ell(x,i)+\ell(x',i)$. This inequality still holds if just one side is empty, since the other side then loses every survivor. Both empty sides give identical predictions. If $s(x)$ is the signed parity, it changes sign on every edge. Thus
$$
     |\mathbb E_x s(x)(p_x-1/2)|
       =\tfrac12|\mathbb E_{x,i}s(x)(p_x-p_{x'})|
       \le u/v .
$$
The left side is the absolute accuracy advantage. This argument applies before conditioning on correctness at the real table and requires the stated local certificate property across the subcube.

Pointwise accuracy $p>1/2$ on every fill satisfies the stronger bound $p\le v/(2v-u)$. Choose a table $x$ with a maximal number $D$ of surviving tagged strings. It has at least $pD$ survivors of its true color. Every neighbor has at most $(1-p)D$ survivors of that color, as parity changes. Summing the survivals of one chosen certificate per true-color string over the $v$ neighbors gives $(v-u)pD\le v(1-p)D$. Pointwise reliability above one half ensures $D>0$.

This pointwise bound is attained whenever $b=v-u>0$ divides $v$. Partition the free coordinates into $g=v/b$ blocks and let $a$ be their parity vector. A primary string $z\in\{0,1\}^g$ has color $1\oplus\mathrm{parity}(z)$ after correcting for the known sign. It survives if its Hamming distance from $a$ is at most one. A witness names an exceptional block, and verification checks the other $g-1$ block parities using exactly $u$ free bits. Of the $g+1$ different surviving strings precisely the $g$ at distance one predict correctly. The center may have $g$ different witnesses and counts once. Success is $g/(g+1)=v/(2v-u)$ on each fill. For $u=v$ direct parity attains one. Cade studies ordinary classical postselected decision trees. Blais and Tan prove sharp width bounds for a single DNF approximating parity. Here membership in every retained tag can itself be nondeterministic, and the normalization can vary across inputs. [Cade](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10010) · [Blais and Tan, Theorems 6–7](https://doi.org/10.1137/14097402X)

For the [typical interior selectors](local-clauses-and-checksum-symbols.md), $w$ is a positive fraction of the full native table, so $t=o(w)$ retained clauses and $u=o(w)$ exposed table bits cannot give a uniform positive constant advantage on every valid completion. The genuine trajectory imposes all its remaining clauses. No such uniform-completion assumption is available for an unrestricted solver.
