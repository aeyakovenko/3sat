# Finite reflection on the fixed-cap native program

Fix $k\in\{1,2,3\}$. There exist a sound finitely axiomatized arithmetic theory $A$ with a fixed full first-order proof checker, and a fixed native program $p$, with the following properties. Let $J_h$ say that some site of this very program stays quiet through its $2^h-1$ step horizon. Let $L_h$ be the length in bits of the shortest $A$-proof of $J_h$. Use the finite-alphabet Hilbert presentation specified below and set $b=h^4$.

* Unconditionally, $J_h$ is true and $L_h\le Cb$ for all sufficiently large $h$, for some constant $C$.
* If ordinary SAT has a deterministic $O(N^k)$ logarithmic-word RAM solver, then $L_h>b$ for all sufficiently large $h$.
* Consequently, an $A$-proof of length **at most $b$** for infinitely many $h$ would exclude that clock. The finite-reflection construction supplies a constant-factor upper bound. It supplies no constant $C\le1$ or infinite subsequence crossing the cap.

All three statements concern one checker, program, proof alphabet and bit measure. This program is a fixed-cap variant of the earlier joint-proof program. In particular, every way to latch here enforces the same literal proof-length cap. The earlier program's unconditional search can materialize longer proofs. The upper bound here applies to the new program text. [Earlier construction and native model](joint-proofs-for-a-block-of-quiet-runs.md)

## A single syntactic bridge for all finite theories

Choose a finite-alphabet first-order Hilbert calculus of the form in §1 of Pudlák's paper. Use his binary numerals of length $O(1+\log(n+1))$, finite axiom schemata for the fixed logical language, and proofs written as sequences of formulas. Use the ordinary presentation **without the auxiliary rule C**, with unrestricted proof depth. Syntactic checking is polynomial time. For example, the verifier can try all earlier tuples of premises for each rule of its fixed finite arity and check substitutions and variable conditions directly. Finite nonlogical axioms are supplied by a fixed code $\gamma$. Pudlák notes explicitly on p. 309 that his upper bound holds also without rule C. His sharper lower bound uses a particular C-rule and is not invoked here. [Pudlák, *Improved bounds to the length of proofs of finitistic consistency statements*, pp. 309–312](https://users.math.cas.cz/~pudlak/improved.pdf#page=1)

For every code $\gamma$ of a finite list of axioms in this language, let $P(\gamma)$ be a uniformly generated program. The unknown prospective solver is selected by the fixed $k$-clock learner. At width $h$, the program uses its prescribed self-code seed and constructs one ordinary proper 3-CNF $F$. Its satisfying assignments certify syntactic $\gamma$-proofs of $J(P(\gamma),h)$ having literal length at most $b$. A marker specifies the actual length of a padded proof. A circuit checks the literal string and its last formula; the bounded computation or circuit is converted to clauses. For each fixed $\gamma$, both construction time and explicit file length are polynomial in $h$, since the proof-length cap and the proof checker are polynomial. The query refers to the actual seed and width.

A common preamble asks the selected solver about $F$. On a YES it uses ordinary SAT self-reduction to compute one candidate value for every variable in a fixed order, imposing the earlier choices. For example, it tries zero first, retaining zero on YES and one otherwise. Pins and variable ordering are identical at all sites. A site latches only after its one original clause passes on this common vector. Index every clause, send spare sites to a tautology, and disable latching on capacity or time failure. The number of clauses and variables is polynomial in $h$ for this fixed theory, so a fixed number of $h$-bit site words suffices at all large widths. Small widths can stay quiet. The construction may omit the exhaustive prephase entirely. It uses no decision problem besides explicitly encoded SAT.

If all sites latch, the vector satisfies all clauses of $F$. This yields an actual syntactically valid proof of the stated sentence within the cap, whether or not the learner supplied correct answers. This inference holds even when $\gamma$ is an inconsistent theory. Fix one arithmetic sentence $E$ expressing this syntactic implication for **all** finite theory codes and widths:
$$
 \forall \gamma,h\quad
 \neg J(P(\gamma),h)\ \Longrightarrow
    \exists \pi\ \bigl[|\pi|\le h^4\ \land\
       \mathsf{Prf}_{\gamma}(\pi,\ulcorner J(P(\gamma),h)\urcorner)\bigr].
$$
Here the quotation abbreviates the primitive recursive construction of the closed instance with binary numerals. The constructor, correctness of the proof-checker's Boolean simulation, and the semantics of an actual native latch give truth of $E$ in standard arithmetic. This is a uniform assertion about extraction of strings from satisfying assignments. It contains no axiom asserting that those strings prove true statements.

Take $A$ to consist of a fixed finite axiomatization of $I\Sigma_1$ and the one true sentence $E$. One may include fixed elementary definitions of the syntax and machine primitives in the finite language. Use the corresponding full syntactic checker. The sentence $E$ and the constructor $P$ were fixed before choosing $A$. If $\gamma_A$ is the axiom-list code, set $p=P(\gamma_A)$. Soundness of $A$ now rules out all sites latching, since a proof extracted on that assumption asserts that one remains quiet. Within $A$, specializing $E$ yields the syntactic implication $\neg J_h\to\mathsf{Prov}_{A,\le b}(J_h)$. Codes of $A$ and $p$ are fixed constants, and verifying $P(\gamma_A)=p$ is a fixed finite computation. Specializing the width and calculating $h^4$ costs polynomial in $\log(h+2)$ proof symbols. Choosing the bridge as a stated axiom keeps this estimate independent of an unperformed formal verification of the native compiler in bare $I\Sigma_1$. [Pudlák, §1 and binary numeral arithmetic in Lemma 2.3](https://users.math.cas.cz/~pudlak/improved.pdf#page=4)

Under the fast SAT hypothesis the learner eventually selects a correct clock. Every query then has polynomial size in $h$. Polynomially many fresh calls, pin construction, and local checking all fit below the exponential native horizon and storage capacity. If a proof of length at most $b$ existed, $F$ would have a model and self-reduction would find one. All sites would latch. This proves the strict lower comparison $L_h>b$ under the clock assumption, including for proofs of high descriptive complexity.

## The same checker accepts a proof of length at most a constant times the cap

We use the proof of Pudlák's Theorem 3.7. Its published conclusion concerns the fixed sentence $0=1$. We give the extra specialization needed for the changing closed sentence $J_h$. All constants below can depend on the fixed theory $A$, formula template, coding and calculus.

1. If there is a proof of $J_h$ of length at most $b$, there is a shortest one. It has quantifier depth $m=O(1+\sqrt b)$, since the logical depth of $J_h$ is bounded independently of $h$. Lemma 3.2 shows that a shortest proof containing depth at least $2d$ of a sentence of depth at most $d$ has length $\Omega(d^2)$. Its proof shortens a derivation by replacing unused deep subformulas. This argument applies to the ordinary Hilbert rules as well when C is omitted. The proof of Theorem 3.7 explicitly notes that the shortening arguments are elementary and formalizable in the base theory. Fix a sufficiently large integer constant and take $m$ to be that constant times $1+h^2$. This avoids any square-root operation in the closed numerical instantiation. [Pudlák, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2](https://users.math.cas.cz/~pudlak/improved.pdf#page=16)

2. For that rank there is a partial satisfaction formula $\mathsf{Sat}_m(e,y)$ of length $O(m)$. A proof of length $O(m^2)$ gives its compositional truth clauses and shows that logical axioms and inference rules preserve it for formulas within the rank. The finitely many nonlogical axioms, including $E$, can be disquoted at an additional cost $O_A(m^2)$. In particular $A$ may use $E$ as an axiom when proving its disquotation. Let $\alpha(s)$ say that the lines of any rank-$m$ proof of length at most $s$ satisfy this partial predicate under all assignments. The base case and the progress implication for $\alpha$ have proofs of total length $O_A(m^2)$ as in Theorem 3.7. Lemma 3.6 yields the bounded instance at $s=b$ using $O_A(m\log^2(b+2))$ more symbols. This is finite iteration for a specified numeral. Uniform induction for arbitrary $\alpha$ in the weak base is unnecessary. [Pudlák, Lemmas 3.4–3.6 and proof of Theorem 3.7](https://users.math.cas.cz/~pudlak/improved.pdf#page=18)

3. The constant in Lemma 3.5(a) is stated for a **fixed sentence**, so it cannot simply be invoked with a new unexamined constant for each $h$. Write the end formula as the fixed open template $\theta(x)=J(p,x)$ applied to the numeral for $h$. For each of the finitely many subformulas of this template, apply the compositional clauses with arbitrary parameter value and assignment. In the atomic cases the fixed syntactic evaluation lemma for a binary numeral identifies its denotation with the parameter. In more detail, the elementary identity $\mathsf{Val}(\mathsf{numeral}(u),y)=u$ follows by binary induction in $I\Sigma_1$ with the fixed coding. Substitution under a quantifier uses a fresh bound-variable convention and the assignment updated at that variable. Substituting a closed numeral cannot capture a variable. Each Boolean or quantifier step then uses the Tarski clauses already proved, once per logical node of the fixed template, leaving the numeral code as a parameter. This gives disquotation of $\theta(\bar h)$ with cost $O_\theta(m^2+\mathrm{poly}(\log(h+2)))$. Equivalently the proof can first disquote the fixed syntax template with the code-producing function $\mathsf{numeral}(u)$ and then instantiate $u=h$. The numeral identity and the finitely many elementary identities for forming those template codes can also be installed at the start as fixed true syntactic axioms. They do not assert the truth of any proof conclusion.

4. The resulting actual $A$-derivation of $\mathsf{Prov}_{A,\le b}(J_h)\to J_h$ uses at most
$$
 O_A\!\left(m^2+m\log^2(b+2)+\mathrm{poly}(\log(h+2))\right)=O_A(b)
$$
symbols. Combine it with the specialized syntactic extraction implication by classical propositional logic to obtain $J_h$. The checker accepts this proof, including all its unrestricted intermediate formulas, for the original fixed $p=P(\gamma_A)$. A simpler polynomial upper works without the shortest-proof argument by setting $m=b+O(1)$, since no proof line has more quantifiers than the entire proof has symbols. This gives $O_A(b^2)$ and the same substantive distinction between a polynomial upper bound and passing the prescribed cap.

The bit measure for the cap is also the proof alphabet used in the upper bound. A translation with polynomial blowup into another formal system preserves a polynomial upper bound but need not preserve $O(b)$ or the comparison with $b$. The fixed-cap proof uses the ordinary Hilbert presentation throughout. No numerical leading constant smaller than one follows from the partial truth or shortest-proof estimates. Measuring the generated proof, weakening its conclusion, or transferring it to a different verifier does not establish $L_h\le b$ on infinitely many widths. Bounded self-reference of this kind already appears for finite Gödel sentences in Pudlák's proof of Lemma 2.1. Priority of the native construction is unestablished. [Pudlák, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 3.7](https://users.math.cas.cz/~pudlak/improved.pdf)

There is an unconditional calibration of the constant gap. In the same arithmetic presentation, choose a parameterized finite Gödel sentence $D(x)$ with the provable equivalence that $D(x)$ says its numerical instance has no $A$-proof of length at most $x$. If a proof of $D(\bar b)$ had length at most $b$, soundness of $A$ would make $D(\bar b)$ true and the equivalence would preclude that very proof. Thus its shortest proof exceeds $b$ for every $b$. The other direction of the fixed point gives $\neg D(\bar b)\to\mathsf{Prov}_{A,\le b}(D(\bar b))$. The same finite-reflection construction proves $D(\bar b)$ in $O_A(b)$ bits. Hence the strict lower and constant-factor upper coexist for every sufficiently large cap in a classical arithmetic example. To cross the cap for the native $J_h$ requires information about its particular computation beyond the syntactic extraction implication. [Pudlák's parameterized diagonal sentence in Lemma 2.1](https://users.math.cas.cz/~pudlak/improved.pdf#page=5)
