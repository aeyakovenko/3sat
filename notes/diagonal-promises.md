# Can symmetry compress a time-hierarchy computation into a short SAT query?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** The candidate uses the binary input as the cardinality of an unordered domain. Existentially chosen relations on this domain can describe a large computation. If a satisfying expansion has many interchangeable pieces, a SAT query could name one Boolean variable per orbit of relation entries. The obstacle below concerns even templates chosen after inspecting the input, with correctness required only on a designated set of squares. Finite spectra as a representation of computation go back to Jones and Selman. [Jones and Selman](https://doi.org/10.2307/2272354)

## Which witnesses can be copied?

A template is a relational first-order sentence on a pure set of size N. The relation interpretations are existential witnesses. There is no built-in order, arithmetic or input predicate. In a block witness, preserve a core of c individually named slots. The remaining slots form rⱼ copies of an aⱼ-element block for each type j. Permuting whole copies of any one type, with the internal slots aligned, preserves every relation; different types have independent permutations. Relations can link different copies. Write B=c+Σⱼaⱼ. Multiplicities may be enormous while B stays small.

For a fixed vocabulary of maximum arity k, a relation tuple is described by its type and slot at each coordinate and the equality pattern of copy indices within each type. There are at most Bᵏ times the kth Bell number such descriptions for k-ary tuples. This explains the proposed space saving. Grounding a q-variable verifier also costs for orbits of its variable assignments, formula length, and the binary multiplicities. For fixed bounds on arity and variables these are polynomial in B, formula length and log N. One can guess the sizes, types, multiplicities and relation-orbit tables, check Σⱼrⱼaⱼ+c=N by binary arithmetic, and evaluate the sentence on tuple patterns bottom up. A certified upper bound for B on YES inputs, together with the construction and full query cost, would be needed to get a saving in a hierarchy argument. [Related hierarchy setup](sparse-lengths.md)

Allow at most q≥1 first-order variable symbols, with reuse. Allow ordinary quantifiers, counting thresholds up to H≥1, and modular counting with moduli dividing a common P≥1. **Copying lemma.** If a type has r≥q+H−1 copies, then adding z≥0 copies preserves truth whenever P divides z. Ordinary first-order logic takes H=P=1. This works for unrestricted quantifier depth. The back-and-forth step for ordinary quantification is the finite-variable pebble argument. [Immerman](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/book/ch6.pdf)

For a proof, extend the relations by matching the slot names and equality pattern of the copy indices. A previously unrealizable pattern at a relation of high arity may be filled arbitrarily and consistently by orbit. An atom evaluated on at most q variables always has a realizable old pattern. Induct on subformulas with corresponding free tuples. When binding one variable, at most q−1 copies of the enlarged type are in use. For each slot in the type, choices in an already named copy match exactly. The fresh-copy choices form one orbit for that slot, all satisfying the subformula or all failing it by induction. If f copies were named, their number has grown from r−f to r+z−f. Other types and the core contribute the same counts. Thus total satisfying counts agree modulo P. If the count grew and a threshold of at most H is tested, the old count was already at least r−f≥H. Ordinary existence follows as the case of threshold one.

## A diagonal test allowing input-dependent menus

Take t≥1 template labels shared within a group of n-bit roots u. The templates themselves can depend arbitrarily, even nonuniformly, on n and on the group. The input to the selector is the binary integer u². Let E(u) be any subset of the labels which it enables there. Acceptance requires an enabled template with a satisfying expansion. Soundness must cover every expansion on a promised NO. The same proof works with an admissibility condition closed under the indicated cloning. An external comparison of the current multiplicities with an input-dependent table need not have that closure.

Fix a maximum block footprint A≥1. Pick Q=tP·A(A+1) consecutive distinct roots, all with the same binary root and square lengths. For each enabled label i, each 1≤a≤A, and each residue class of u² modulo aP, mark the **last** root with that class which enables i, when such a root exists. Declare the marked roots NO and the others YES. At most tΣₐaP=Q/2 roots are marked, so at least half are YES. This bound can be reduced by counting only attainable square residues. There is no promise outside the group.

Suppose u is YES and admits a block witness of footprint B≤A for an enabled label i. Assume u²>(q+H−2)A+A, which also covers a witness consisting mostly of individually named slots. Some type has r≥q+H−1 copies: if every r were smaller, the domain would have at most c+(q+H−2)Σaⱼ≤(q+H−1)A slots. Let a be the size of this type. The last root v in the class of u² modulo aP enabling label i is marked, and v>u because u is unmarked. Then z=(v²−u²)/a is a positive multiple of P. Copying exactly z blocks yields a satisfying expansion on the promised NO input v², for the same enabled label. This contradicts soundness. Consequently every YES either has no accepting expansion or needs footprint greater than A. The construction permits an arbitrary number of simultaneously enabled labels and requires soundness only at the marked roots.

For an effective diagonal, use a self-delimiting selector program code e after a fixed leading 100 in each n-bit root. Take the first Q roots in that prefix interval when it has room. Their squares all have bit length 2n−1. On a proposed input, integer square root and prefix parsing identify its group. Simulate its clocked selector on those Q squares. For each eligible label and size a, store the last root using the key (i,a,u² mod aP). Reading off the stored roots determines the answer. On a multitape machine, sort the (key,root) records, extract the last root per key, and check the list of marked roots. This uses Q(U+tA) poly(n) time with U the charged cost per simulated selector, including production of its menu. Here Q, t, P and A have O(n)-bit encodings and the bounds are uniformly constructible within the stated time. The specification uses sequential sorting so no assumption about addressing exponentially many cells with O(log n)-bit words is required. Reject malformed or off-group inputs. Timeout and malformed selector replies receive a specified empty menu.

For example take H=P=1, t polynomial, A=2^⌊n/4⌋, and a uniform selector clock of 2^⌊n/4⌋ steps with polynomial simulation overhead. The group fits at every sufficiently large n for each fixed e. The diagonal language is decidable in 2^(3n/4) poly(n) time on tapes. On the slice for each candidate selector it excludes small block witnesses for any choice of the t template sentences with qA below the domain size. The bound concerns this restricted representation. Neither the spectrum theorem nor a polynomial SAT algorithm says that a computation has an accepting expansion of this form.

## Try a more symmetric representation of squares

On [h]² guess relations for sharing a row or a column, plus a diagonal predicate. A three-variable sentence can require that row and column are equivalence relations, every row meets every column, any pair sharing both is equal, and each row and each column contains exactly one diagonal element. These conditions enforce a square cardinality, and every positive square has such a witness. Simultaneously relabeling both coordinates by a permutation of [h] preserves the standard witness. For fixed arity its relations have a constant number of tuple orbits, illustrating how coupling two sets of copies escapes the preceding block representation.

Try this action on mixed YES and NO squares. Allow a satisfying expansion invariant under every simultaneous permutation fixing s selected base coordinates pointwise. For ordinary q-variable logic, whenever h−s≥2q the expansion transfers from [h]² to every larger [v]². A tuple of q grid elements uses at most 2q coordinates. Copy its equality pattern and its coincidences with selected coordinates. Upon rebinding one grid element, at most 2(q−1) coordinates remain in use, leaving room for both coordinates of the newly selected pair. Induction on the formula gives the transfer. As above, higher-arity tuples never reached by an atom can receive arbitrary invariant values. The standard row, column and diagonal relations extend with exactly the same patterns.

Mark the last occurrence of each enabled label in a group of 2t roots and let all unmarked roots be YES. At least half are YES. Correctness on the marked NO squares forces every accepting grid witness on a YES square to have s>h−2q for every support of the stated kind. The stabilizer then has at least s² singleton orbits on grid elements. For q=o(h), a method allocating one slot per unary orbit of this stabilizer thus already uses Ω(h²) slots. This counts such a method's slots, not the bits required by every possible circuit for the relations. A succinct arithmetic predicate may answer many distinct orbits with one program.

The promise and selector construction above was derived for this journal. Priority has not been established. The step needed for the SAT question is an efficient simulation on a genuine hierarchy language using representations whose availability follows from the hypothetical quadratic or cubic SAT algorithm. These counterexamples identify two specific compression assumptions that require a different justification.
