# Affine hull of a partial implication cycle

For every $r\ge2$ and every prescribed $t\in\{0,1\}^{3r}$, there is an explicit proper 3-CNF with unique model $t$ such that the models of **every** subset of at most $2r+2$ clauses affinely span $\mathbb F_2^{3r}$. Consequently, no such subset implies even one nonconstant affine equation. This holds for subsets chosen after reading the entire file.

**Construction.** Put $m=3r$, index variables cyclically modulo $m$, and first take $t=0$. For each $i$ write the two clauses
$$
 (\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1}\vee x_{i+2}),\qquad
 (\neg x_i\vee x_{i+1}\vee\neg x_{i+2}).
$$
Together they express $x_i\Rightarrow x_{i+1}$. Add $(\neg x_0\vee\neg x_r\vee\neg x_{2r})$. Every clause uses three distinct variables. The implications force all $m$ values to agree, and the last clause excludes the all-one assignment. Replacing $x_i$ by $x_i\oplus t_i$ throughout just changes literal signs. Translation preserves affine dimension and moves the unique model to $t$.

**Proof of the local claim.** Fix a subset of at most $2r$ original clauses. Replace each selected padded implication clause by its underlying implication. This strengthens the selected formula, so it suffices to give affinely spanning models after the replacement. The selected directed edges form disjoint paths and isolated vertices because fewer than $m$ edges occur. For each vertex $v$ take the assignment that is one exactly at the vertices reachable from $v$, including $v$. Every selected implication holds.

If the three-anchor clause was selected, at most $2r-1$ selected edges remain. A directed path visiting all three anchors needs at least $2r$ edges, since successive anchors have directed separation $r$. Thus each reachability assignment satisfies the anchor clause too. The zero assignment also satisfies the subset. In any topological vertex order, the matrix whose row $v$ is the reachability assignment from $v$ is upper triangular with ones on the diagonal. The rows are linearly independent, and together with zero they give the required affine hull.

**Exact physical threshold.** Write $A_i,B_i$ for the positive- and negative-padding clauses, respectively. The fewest original clauses implying any nonconstant affine equation is $2r+3$. For an upper bound take $A_0,\ldots,A_{2r-1}$, the two clauses $B_{r-1},B_{2r-1}$, and the anchor clause. Starting from a true $x_0$, the $A_i$ permit a forward walk of true vertices with step one or two. At the predecessors of $r$ and $2r$ the added $B_i$ force a step of one, so neither anchor can be skipped. This contradicts the anchor clause and proves $x_0=0$.

For the lower bound first take $r\ge3$. Without the anchor clause, $2r+2<m$ edges leave a path forest. With it there are at most $2r+1$ implication clauses. The preceding reachability proof works unless the selected edges contain an arc $I$ of $2r$ successive edges from an anchor $a$ to $a+2r$. There is at most one such arc, since the union of any two different anchor-starting arcs is the entire cycle. At most one selected edge lies outside $I$. Use cyclic indices below.

If the edge entering $a$ is absent, every reachability assignment except that from $a$ satisfies the anchor clause and vanishes at $a$. The remaining $m-1$ independent rows span the hyperplane $x_a=0$. At least one of $j=a+r,a+2r$ has an incomplete pair at $j-1$, since completing both would already take $2r+2$ implication clauses. If $A_{j-1}$ is missing, the assignment true exactly on the forward interval $a,\ldots,j-1$ works. It violates only $A_{j-1}$ among all the implication clauses. If $B_{j-1}$ is missing, the all-one assignment except at $j$ works. Both satisfy the anchor clause and have $x_a=1$.

If the entering edge is present, precisely $2r+1$ edges occur with one clause each. Every reachability row except those from $a-1,a$ satisfies the anchor and vanishes at both coordinates. Set $j=a+r$. If $A_{j-1}$ is missing, intervals ending at $j-1$ and starting at $a$ or $a-1$ give the respective projections $(0,1),(1,1)$ on $(a-1,a)$. If $B_{j-1}$ is missing, take all ones except $j$ and optionally also $a-1$. The extra zero can create an outgoing implication failure only at $a-2$, an unselected edge. These two projections are independent. Together with the other $m-2$ rows and zero they restore the full affine hull.

For $r=2$ the same anchor argument works. Without the anchor there is one extra case of six singly represented edges covering the cycle. If every $B_i$ is missing, all ones with any single bit flipped is legal. Otherwise choose $i$ with $A_i$ missing. Intervals ending at $i$ of lengths $1,\ldots,m-2$ are legal and span every coordinate vector except $e_{i+1},e_{i+2}$. The all-one model gives their sum modulo this span. If $B_{i+1}$ is missing, flipping bit $i+2$ in the all-one model supplies $e_{i+2}$ by subtraction. Otherwise $A_{i+1}$ is missing and the singleton $e_{i+1}$ itself is legal. This completes the threshold proof.

**A successor increasing the threshold by exactly one.** Allow three unequal directed gaps between successive anchors. Assume each gap is at least two and the largest gap $g$ is at least three. Put $d=m-g$. The exact minimum physical support is $d+3$. For the upper bound choose an arc through all three anchors of length $d$, omitting a largest gap. Include its $d$ clauses $A_i$, both negative-padding clauses immediately before its second and third anchors, and the anchor clause. The same forward walk forces the first anchor false.

For the lower bound, without the anchor, at most $d+2<m$ selected edges leave a path forest. With the anchor, at most $d+1$ implication clauses remain. If any forward reachability set covers all three anchors, the selected edges include a complete arc from the first anchor reached through the third. Such an arc omits exactly one of the three gaps. Two different complete arcs would omit disjoint gaps, so their union would contain every cycle edge, more than $d+1$. The unique contained arc has length $d$ or $d+1$. If the edge entering its first anchor is absent, at most one of its edge buckets can be doubled, and the missing-sign construction above restores the single missing dimension. This includes the borderline case of a longer arc of length $d+1$, where every bucket is single. If the entering edge is present, the arc has length exactly $d$, and the entering edge and all arc edges are single. The same two models restore the two missing dimensions. In both interval constructions every omitted gap has at least two edges, so the sign at the end of the interval is as claimed.

For $r\ge3$, the gaps $(r,r,r)$ give minimum $2r+3$. Gaps $(r,r+1,r+1)$ give $2r+4$, and $(r+1,r+1,r+1)$ give $2r+5$. Repetition realizes every successive integer threshold starting at nine.

The files may also be nested. Append each new cycle to the retained satisfiable formula using fresh names. For any selected clause subset its model set is the product of the old and fresh model sets. Fix one satisfying old assignment. If an affine equation with a nonzero fresh coefficient held on that product, its restriction would be a nonconstant affine equation on the fresh models. Thus fewer than $d+3$ total clauses cannot infer a relation involving a fresh bit. The displayed $d+3$ fresh clauses attain the bound. Old relations keep their original witnesses, and every old full model has a unique extension to the newly added cycle.

**An executed-step consequence.** Consider the deterministic SAT algorithm that collects all affine equations implied by any subset of at most $s$ original clauses, solves the collected linear system, then tests its solutions against the original CNF one at a time in increasing binary order. This is a total correct algorithm, implementable by exhaustive local truth tables and Gaussian elimination. With $s\le 2r+2$ the collected system on this family leaves every seed available. Choose $t=1^m$. The algorithm executes exactly $2^m$ seed trials before its SAT answer. This count holds for any permutation of the bit positions. With ordinary decimal DIMACS names $1,\ldots,m$, the $2m+1$ clauses use $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ encoded bits, giving $2^{\Omega(N/\log N)}$ actual trials.

The lower bound concerns this enumerator's fallback. A solver that resolves the opposite padding literals can recognize the global implication cycle directly. Guessed assignments can also expose consequences unavailable before branching. Global implication-graph algorithms are classical. [Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan (1979)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(79)90002-4)

The finite audit checks actual DIMACS clauses and computes affine dimensions from their truth tables at $m=6,9$. It checks every subset of size $2r$ for both extreme target words. Independently, for every nonzero coefficient mask it computes a minimum hitting set of original clauses excluding all assignments of odd masked parity at the zero target. The exact minima are seven and nine clauses, attained by nine and three coefficient masks respectively. [Reproducible audit](../audits/partial_cycle_affine_hull_audit.py)
