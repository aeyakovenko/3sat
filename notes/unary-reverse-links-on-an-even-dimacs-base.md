# Writing the old cursor back before a unary update

Consider the continuing cycle
$$
 x\gets M[p],\qquad M[x]\gets p,\qquad p\gets f(x).
$$
Here $f$ is one numerical instruction of the [five-slot ISA](five-instruction-loops-on-dimacs-sat.md), using the raw word $x$ once or twice and any fixed-on-input operand. It does not use the old cursor as a numerical operand. Branches may test registers and exit at fixed positions in the cycle. **This schedule cannot decide ordinary DIMACS SAT with any running time.** The ordering matters. The corresponding schedule writing $f(x)$ in place of the old $p$ has a [different no-clock proof](adaptive-five-slot-prefixes-without-a-clock.md).

Use the published [even-word DIMACS base, property 1](five-instruction-loops-on-dimacs-sat.md). At arbitrarily large lengths it has a bounded original word palette, including default zero, consisting entirely of even words. It has $\Theta(N/w)$ consecutive full words each sensitive to a well-formed edit confined to that word. The forcing clauses fix three variables true, a repeated proper three-literal clause accepts that assignment, and changing the three signs of one occurrence in a full word falsifies the formula. Its layout puts every minus sign at an odd byte offset, all word boundaries at even byte offsets, and even ASCII characters at the low byte of each word, including the header. The bounded alphabet follows from the fixed repeat period and the constant number of header words. Since the edit changes the verdict, a correct halting base execution must observe each such original word before any overwrite.

All ordinary mutable registers start at zero, or can receive the public byte-aligned bit length and word width, both even. In the actual five-slot case the LOAD, STORE and ALU are all on the continuing cycle. An initial partial turn before the first regular LOAD may contain the STORE followed by the ALU, the ALU alone, or neither. In particular any preliminary STORE writes an initial even cursor value. Let $M_0$ be the memory just before that first LOAD, $H$ its image and $h=|H|$. A bounded number of exceptions changes $h$ by a bounded amount, and for this partial turn every word in $H$ remains even. If $f$ maps even values to even, the initial cursor $p_0$ is even too. Prologue or exit observations account for at most $O_P(1)$ originals outside the regular turns.

For every regular-time prefix, **a cell $a$ outside $H$ still holds $M_0[a]$**. Indeed a step reading $x=M[p]$ and assigning $M[x]\gets p$ can delete an undirected memory edge, but the new arrow only reverses the edge $p$ just followed. Thus every current undirected edge was present initially. An address outside $H$ has just one possible initial neighbor, its own value $M_0[a]$. Its current outgoing edge must reach that same neighbor. This invariant was proved previously, and the Lean theorem covers every sequence of addresses, including adaptive choices. If the parity of $f(x)$ is fixed for even $x$, consider the two possible parities.

* If the output on even $x$ is even, then all memory and $p_0$ start even, the old cursor is written back, and induction keeps every cursor and every word even. None of the sensitive odd addresses can be visited in the regular turns.
* If the output on even $x$ is odd, $f(H)$ and $H$ are disjoint. On the first regular read outside $H$ the invariant gives $x=M_0[p]\in H$. Consequently $p'=f(x)\in f(H)$ is outside $H$, and the same reasoning applies forever. Before that first read the cursor belonged to $H$. Thus the regular reads use at most $2h+1$ distinct addresses.

Modular addition, either subtraction, multiplication, moves and every bitwise truth table have the required parity property with fixed operand roles, even when both inputs are $x$. In particular this covers $p'=x+d$ for any fixed odd immediate. A comparison or fixed overwrite has constant-size image and hence allows only a constant number of distinct future cursors.

For a shift or rotation with $x$ supplying only the shift count, the image has $O(w)$ words because the data operand is fixed. If $x$ supplies the data operand, possibly also the count, each forward orbit of $f$ contains $O(w)$ different points. Repeated rotations stay among the rotations of the starting word; shifts in a fixed direction compose until zero or sign saturation, even when the individual effective counts vary. Every $x$ returned is an initial word in $H$ or an earlier cursor written by the STORE. Induction therefore puts every regular cursor in the union of the $f$-orbits from $H\cup\{p_0\}$. These shifts and rotations can only reach $O((h+1)w)$ distinct addresses.

The excluded alternatives cover the listed one-instruction unary forms. In each, the run misses some sensitive original because $h=O_P(1)$ and $N/w$ grows faster than $w$. The conclusion is confined to the old-cursor reverse write followed by a unary update. It is independent of the settling-time argument for a public translation scan.
