# Exact finite obstructions for games with affine projections

Here is a sufficient test for a fast certification of the actual priority sign that does not assume a probability gap. Each Alice question $x$ has an answer $u_x\in\mathbb F_2^a$. For each Bob question $y$ there is one common response bit $b_y$. Perfect acceptance means $b_y=\pi_{xy}(u_x)$ for every pair, where each $\pi_{xy}$ is an affine function of $u_x$. The question sets may be exponentially large and all pairs must be eligible. Coordinates of longer Bob messages can be treated as separate columns when winning requires equality of the entire projected message. A local description of the affine functions suffices; no uniformity in the choice of incompatible rows or columns is required.

For any $x$ the possible complete Bob strings form an affine subspace $U_x$ of dimension at most $a$. The game is perfect exactly when $\bigcap_x U_x$ is nonempty. For a finite imperfect game, take an inclusion-minimal incompatible set of $m$ rows. Then $2\le m\le a+2$. Keeping these rows, **at most**

$$D(m,a)=m-1+m(a-m+2)$$

individual Bob coordinates suffice to certify imperfection. Every value of this bound is attained, even with ordinary binary answers on Bob's side and every row of dimension exactly $a$. Thus the largest possible coordinate requirement is $\lfloor(a+3)^2/4\rfloor-1$. This maximum concerns selecting an incompatible panel, not the computational cost of deciding its value. ## The dimension of a minimal witness

For each selected $i$, take $p_i$ in every $U_h$ except $U_i$. These witnesses are affinely independent. In a nontrivial affine dependence, a point with nonzero coefficient would be an affine combination of the other points. They all belong to its omitted subspace, a contradiction. Their affine hull $S$ consequently has dimension $m-1$. Each $U_i$ already contains the dimension-$(m-2)$ facet spanned by the other witnesses. This proves the bound on $m$. Extending $S$ to contain $U_i$ costs at most $\dim U_i-(m-2)$ new independent directions. The affine hull of the union therefore has dimension at most $D(m,a)$, with the sharper sum using the individual row dimensions available as well.

On any affine space of dimension $D$, at most $D$ of the original coordinate functionals suffice for injective restriction. Choose a basis from the rows of a generator matrix for its translation space. If the projected subspaces at these coordinates had a point in common, preimages chosen in the different $U_i$ would have identical restrictions and hence be equal. Every row intersection and every projection used here refers to the same global columns. The small row count alone is the elementary affine form of a Helly bound; tracking the minimum coordinate restriction requires the hull-of-union argument. [Formal sublemmas](../lean/AffineGamePanels.lean)

For sharpness, take the affine simplex $p_1=0,p_2,\ldots,p_m$ in $\mathbb F_2^{m-1}$ where the remaining $p_i$ are the unit vectors. Give each row its own disjoint space $T_i$ of dimension $a-m+2$. Let $U_i$ consist of the opposite facet plus $T_i$. The facets have empty common intersection since all barycentric coordinates cannot simultaneously be zero and sum to one. Projection onto the simplex directions shows that the private spaces preserve this incompatibility. Omitting row $i$ gives the common point $p_i$.

Use the following **basis of directions** for the ambient $D(m,a)$-dimensional space. Include $p_i-p_1$ for $i>1$. For each private basis vector $v\in T_i$ include $p_i-p_{s(i)}+v$, with $s(i)\ne i$. These vectors form a basis since after quotienting by the simplex directions the remaining vectors are precisely the private basis. Use as Bob's questions the coefficient in this basis, i.e. the *dual* coordinate functionals. Each basis direction joins $p_i$, which satisfies all other rows, to a point satisfying $U_i$. For the first kind take $p_1$ in $U_i$, and for the private direction take $p_{s(i)}+v$ in $U_i$. Deleting that coordinate identifies the two points and makes the whole panel feasible. The $m=2,a=0$ example consists of the two different singleton answers on one column. Parameterize each $U_i$ by an $a$-bit answer to realize this example as a game. Historical priority of the sharp two-parameter bound is unverified.

## Cost on the original target

Suppose a uniform indexed game of this form has a perfect strategy exactly when a named rank wins the [native priority stage](disjoint-winner-certificates.md). On a loss, after selecting the small row and column lists above, introduce $ma$ unknown answer bits. Compare each row to an anchor row at a selected coordinate. These are affine equations in the $ma$ bits, and their system is inconsistent. There is an inconsistent subsystem of at most $ma+1$ comparisons whose sum is $0=1$: take a minimal dependence with inconsistent constants, or a basis of the augmented rows representing $(0,\ldots,0,1)$. A nondeterministic verifier can guess just this list (including its row and column names), generate the coefficients, sort and cancel identical variable terms and check the final constant parity. A perfect strategy satisfies every comparison, so this test has no false positives. In particular there is no inner SAT call for testing the panel, and the verifier need not list all $mD$ pairs. No time for computing the witness family from scratch is being assumed.

If each affine coordinate lists its nonzero coefficients and constant in at most $g$ word operations, inclusive of its output, and a name or pair of names costs at most $u$ to read and validate, the work is $O((a+2)^2(g+u+1)\operatorname{polylog} M)$ for polynomially bounded indices at native scale $M$. With only a promised affine black-box evaluator, determining the whole row can instead cost $a+1$ calls on zero and unit inputs. Inserting the first cost in the actual two-rank test gives a sufficient condition: under an $O(n^k)$ SAT hypothesis a game family sound and complete on all named ranks cannot have this cost $O(M^{1/k-\epsilon})$ on the two designated late losing pins with the required coverage. Uniform public caps and setup are charged as in that test. For example, for locally listed constant-size rows and polylogarithmic names, the sufficient answer-dimension threshold is $a=O(M^{1/(2k)-\epsilon})$. This is an upper bound required of a reduction, not an assertion that the priority computation already has it. ## Testing the proposed affine conversion on an actual 3-CNF family

A bitwise AND instruction is a concrete obstacle for a faithful affine encoding of the private computation. For $t$ independent bit pairs take the ordinary 3-CNF with clauses $(\neg x_i\lor\neg y_i\lor z_i)$, $(x_i\lor\neg z_i)$ and $(y_i\lor\neg z_i)$ per pair. Its $4^t$ assignments are exactly the graph of the coordinatewise products. Binary clauses can repeat a literal. Any representation that projects a union of affine solution spaces onto **exactly all** these assignments needs at least $2^t$ different affine spaces. This holds with arbitrary existential auxiliary bits: projecting an affine space gives an affine space. For one triple the four graph points contain no affine plane. Therefore each triple projection of a contained flat has dimension at most one, and the whole flat has dimension at most $t$ by projection into their product. It covers at most $2^t$ assignments. Equality is attained by fixing the whole $x$-word, after which all products are affine in $y$.

The same bound applies if the uniform distribution on the projected points of each nonempty flat has correctness strictly greater than $3/4$. Parameterize it by independent uniform bits. A violated relation $z_i+x_i y_i$ restricts to a multilinear polynomial of degree at most two. If it is nonzero, it is one on at least a quarter of the assignments: for a degree-two monomial fix all other variables; its coefficient remains one, hence the four-point restriction is nonzero. Nonconstant linear polynomials are one on half the assignments. Consequently such a flat is already entirely correct. Covering only a fraction $1-\eta$ of the valid points still requires at least $(1-\eta)2^t$ flats. Strictness matters: fixing $z=0$ on one triple attains exactly $3/4$ correctness.

This pins down the cost of choosing modes when the conversion promises faithful witness projection even for independent native AND gates. A decision-only reduction can answer this always-satisfiable family immediately. A semantic construction for the canonical false output could also exploit restrictions on which operands are reachable. The projection calculation rules out using the truth table of the general instruction as an affine gadget with only polynomially many modes; the decision problem itself remains open.
