# Can local certificates hand a computation from one region to another?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** An accepting computation might pass between several regions that are easy to describe, with no one region preserved by every step. If their union is preserved and excludes rejection, an ordinary SAT call can test a more general certificate without knowing those regions. The test pays for possible failures of induction. Their worst-case number has an exact bound even on a safe initialized computation.

## One query for inductive covers

Let x be the specified initialized state of a terminating computation. Fix m observable Boolean coordinates of a state. The allowed transition relation contains every step from x on the actual run. A predicate Bad includes every rejecting terminal accessible on that run. Both tests must be sound for the use in question. A safe inductive set contains x, includes the successor of every allowed transition from the set, and excludes Bad. Existence of such a set proves acceptance. A cube is a conjunction of signed coordinate tests. An inductive union of at most w cubes has a CNF description with clauses of width at most w. Distribute the terms, selecting one literal from each cube per resulting clause. Empty or inconsistent terms simplify directly. Transitions can go from one cube to another. For example, cycle through 00, 01, 10, with 11 a separate bad sink. Their union is certified by ¬a ∨ ¬b. Every single cube or affine space containing the initialized cycle includes the bad point.

For a finite library of proposed clauses, begin with the clauses true at x and delete a clause whenever an allowed transition satisfies all survivors at its source and violates that clause at its destination. Every inductive sub-conjunction containing x remains among the survivors. At a fixed point the surviving conjunction is itself inductive. A failure certificate can end earlier as soon as a bad state satisfies every survivor. Thus a list of at most the library size in counterexample steps followed by one bad state certifies that no safe inductive sub-conjunction exists. Each step can delete all clauses it violates. For succinct finite transitions, polynomially many explicit predicates and polynomial-time local tests, failure is in NP. Existence is already coNP-hard with constant features, identity transition, and a bad-state test given by an arbitrary Boolean formula of the state bits. The only representable set containing x then contains every state. This is the candidate-deletion principle of Houdini. With all width-w clauses, success certifies an inductive CNF, which may cover more cases than the initial union-of-cubes proposal. [Flanagan, Joshi and Leino](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0020-0190(00)00196-4) · [Flanagan and Leino](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-45251-6_29) · [Feldman and Shoham on inference under reachability conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07451)

## Shortest failure transcripts in the worst case

Assume 1≤w≤m. Keep the list E of inserted feature vectors, beginning with the vector at x. The conjunction of all width-at-most-w clauses true throughout E admits a vector v exactly when, on every set of w coordinates, its restriction agrees with that of some earlier vector in E. A missing pattern on fewer than w coordinates can be extended to w using v's own bits. A failure transcript requests the vector of a locally valid source, verifies it is admitted, and inserts its actual successor's vector. At the end it requests an admitted bad state. Sources of successive steps may be completely different actual states. Inductivity makes the reasoning sound at these jumps. When no new insertion is possible, the admitted set is inductive, proving completeness.

Write B=Σ_{j=0}ʷ binom(m,j). A complete search for a failure needs at most B−1 transitions. Delete insertions that expose no new pattern, since they preserve every later admission test. Write each point as a row of all squarefree monomials of degree at most w evaluated at that point over the binary field. For each remaining insertion, an absent signed pattern now occurs. Its indicator polynomial has degree at most w, vanishes on every earlier row, and equals one on the fresh row. This separates that row from the earlier linear span. The initial row has a one in the constant coordinate. Counting dimensions proves the bound. The argument also works with the rank of the monomial evaluations on the feasible feature vectors when an upper bound for that rank is independently justified. The polynomial-dimension method has precedent in constraint sparsification. [Jansen and Pieterse](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03233)

For every 1≤w<m the number B−1 is attained on a safe computation. Order all B vectors of weight at most w by nondecreasing weight with any fixed ordering of ties. The transition advances down this list, fixes the last point, and fixes every higher-weight vector. Start at zero and take the all-ones vector as the single bad state. The run is safe. For an unvisited low-weight vector v, the ones on its support form a pattern of size at most w that no earlier listed point has. A fixed high-weight source can produce no new pattern once it is admitted. Inductively a transcript that has reached only an initial segment can gain at most its next listed low-weight point in a single step. The all-ones state becomes admissible only after every w-element support has appeared with all its bits one, including the support of the last point. At that point each required all-ones pattern has appeared. Therefore every failure transcript needs B−1 transitions, achieved by the actual initialized prefix followed by a bad-state request. Lexicographic order within each weight permits a polynomial-size successor circuit by the ordinary next-combination algorithm.

There is also an exact cover-size change. Any cube excluding all ones must require at least one zero bit, after empty cubes have been discarded. From each of at most w cubes select one such coordinate. The point whose ones occupy those selected positions belongs to the low-weight orbit and escapes every selected cube. In the other direction, the disjunction of w+1 distinct negative unit tests contains the entire initialized orbit and excludes all ones. Every point outside the low-weight list is fixed, so that union is inductive. This establishes a representation bound on this test family even though the safety answer is immediate from the transition definition.

## What the one SAT call costs

A chronological list alternates insertions and requests, beginning with the initialization and ending with the bad request. Put each source request before the corresponding successor insertion. For each of the binom(m,w) w-subsets sort its projected rows by pattern and event time. The first event in every occurring pattern must be an insertion. This verifies all past-agreement tests together. For a fixed program let U bound the cost in word operations of any one observable, transition and local guard or bad test. With a public input of n bits and W-bit simulated words, a conservative bound on the ordinary explicit SAT length is

    Õ((n + B[m(U+1) + w binom(m,w)]) poly(W)).

The formula guesses a list with at most B−1 transitions and its length, and validates active rows only. The initial row is computed from the genuine initialization. For each guessed source, all m coordinates and the guard must see the same preexisting memory. Sort the queried reads and writes by source, address and time, allowing one arbitrary initial word per touched work address. Separate the scratch of each observer from the observed machine. Share the step's writes with all observations of the successor, and check immutable input reads against the common input. Consistency on touched cells extends to a full memory by filling other cells arbitrarily. A final bad row gets its own common memory. This charges for every observation of the unrestricted RAM without guessing all previously occupied cells. If the supplied allowed relation has a different local verifier, include its entire cost. The word-operation and sorting computations can be reduced quasilinearly in their bit cost to SAT. [RAM simulation and reduction](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

At width two there is a shorter direct verifier. There are O(m²) events. Group them in chronological blocks of m. For each block put the truth bits of its 2m signed literals into a literal-by-event matrix. The product with its transpose counts pair cooccurrences in that block. Compute over a power-of-two modulus exceeding m so a positive count cannot become zero. Record the first nonempty block for each pair, skipping any pair of incompatible literals and handling unit patterns by pairing a literal with itself. For each pair that occurs, scan just its first block for the earliest event and check its type. The O(m) block products use Õ(m^(1+log₂7)) bit operations by Strassen's algorithm. Searching for the first block and scanning inside it cost O(m³) up to word overhead. Thus the SAT length at width two can be bounded by Õ((n+m³(U+1)+m^(1+log₂7)) poly(W)). This saving concerns *verifying the guessed failure transcript*. A hypothetical SAT time O(Nᵈ) is applied to the entire resulting bit length N. [Strassen](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02165411)

## Applying the exponent and testing the representation hypothesis

Use a fixed deterministic multitape decider for a language in time O(2ⁿ) outside time O(2^(n/2)). Suppose polynomial-time uniform programs generate the observables and local tests on each input, with W polynomial in n. For instance, at width two take m≤2^(n/48) and U≤2^(n/64), allowing polynomial factors. The bound above has exponent at most max(3/48+1/64,(1+log₂7)/48)<1/12 per input bit up to o(1). Under cubic SAT the one query decides existence of the inductive width-two conjunction. A quadratic-time-overhead multitape simulation of that RAM algorithm still fits strictly below 2^(n/2). It suffices for the generated observations to admit a safe inductive conjunction on all accepting inputs. The actual rejecting run prevents one on every rejecting input. Equivalently, under cubic SAT any such sound uniform generator misses infinitely many accepting inputs, since finitely many exceptions can be tabulated. A two-cube cover with transitions between cubes would imply the required conjunction. The existence of an appropriate cover or conjunction for this hierarchy computation remains unknown. [Hennie and Stearns](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/tss/files/papers/HennieStearns66.pdf)

One cannot remove m from the clock solely because the clauses have width one. Give a succinct deterministic transition on k-bit states a coordinate pᵢ(s)=[s≠i] for each k-bit index i, all evaluable by a fixed linear-size comparison. Every set of states is expressible by a conjunction of these positive unit tests, by excluding each state outside the set. Existence of a safe inductive conjunction is now exactly nonreachability of the bad state from the initial state. This is PSPACE-complete for succinct transition circuits. A polynomial-space machine can iterate the transition up to 2ᵏ times, and the transition circuit of any polynomial-space decider can map its chosen halting outcome to a canonical bad absorbing state. Complementing the simulated outcome gives either polarity of the reduction. Short indices and single-literal obligations leave the cost of discovering their conjunction unbounded by the index length alone. The conclusions here do not impose a running-time lower bound on ordinary SAT.

[The Lean proof](../lean/GuardedCovers.lean) verifies distribution of the cubes into clauses, inheritance of every allowed-pattern conjunction across an admitted request, exclusion of every safe such conjunction by a failure transcript, and extraction of one from a safe saturated transcript. The dimension bound, its matching construction, the local RAM encoding and the quantitative hierarchy argument are manuscript proofs.
