# Recovering a whole table from short programs for its polynomial evaluations

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** The single-interpreter numeric test is equivalent to the following assertion quantified over *all* positive integers $a,b$. For each fixed pair, if an ordinary sampler $S$ publishes $y$ by $t=2^q$ with probability $p\ge(q+2)^{-a}$ and $|y|\le(q+2)^b$, a deterministic prefix program of at most $\lceil-\log_2p\rceil+O(1)$ bits prints the entire $y$ by $t(q+2)^{O(1)}$. The earlier [numeric equivalence](sampling-at-a-numeric-length.md) gave this inference only through output length $q$. Linear SAT already implies the longer-output assertion by [projected counting](near-clock-coding.md). Here the implication from the short-word test uses **no SAT assumption**. Thus a counterexample seeking to rule out linear SAT can use a whole truth table of polynomial size in $q$ and still force a failure on an ordinary binary integer at high mass. No such truth table is presently known.

## Evaluations that fit into the short-word test

Assume the short-word assertion from the numeric equivalence, on one efficient literal reference $V$. Its constants may depend on the source and the fixed probability exponent. The converse follows by restricting the extended assertion to $|y|\le q$. We only need to consider lengths $m=|y|>q$. In particular a qualifying probability is at least $(m+2)^{-a}$.

Set $A=a+2$, $D_m=(m+2)^A$. Choose the first prime $P\ge 32(m+1)D_m$, separately for each $m$. Bertrand's postulate and trial division give a uniform construction in $\mathrm{poly}_a(m+2)$ steps. Work over $\mathbb F_P$. Encode the bits of $y$ as the coefficients, in order, of $f_y(X)$, of degree less than $m$ (zero coefficients may be leading). Different words of the same length have polynomials agreeing at no more than $m-1$ field positions.

One fixed source runs $S$ to publication. Given its word of length $m$, draw $\lceil\log_2 P\rceil$ fresh fair bits. If the resulting integer $i<P$, publish the self-delimiting tuple $(m,i,f_y(i))$; otherwise abandon the run. Each $i$ gets at least $p/(2P)$ mass. The tuple has $O_a(\log(m+2))$ bits, and evaluation takes $\mathrm{poly}_a(m+2)$ steps in addition to the original computation. For $m\le(q+2)^b$ the tuple is short at a dyadic deadline $2^{q+O(\log(q+2))}$, even after compilation. Its mass is inverse-polynomial in the logarithm of that deadline. By the short-word assertion all its field positions have timely deterministic names of length at most $\log_2(P/p)+O(1)$. The constants and deadline are uniform over the positions. The deduction does not require the names to be related to one another.

## An output measure obtained from the names

Let $\mu(m,i,v)$ be the total exact-prefix probability of all $V$ names that ever publish the specified valid tuple. It is a subprobability over all lengths, positions and values. For each $m$-bit string set
$$
 s_y=P\min_{i\in\mathbb F_P}\mu(m,i,f_y(i)).
$$
Keep the words with $s_y\ge1/D_m$. Across *all* lengths these scores sum to at most two.

For a fixed length, any collection of at most $2D_m+1$ retained words gives each word at least $P-(2D_m)(m-1)>P/2$ positions at which its value differs from every other word in the collection. At those positions it claims mass at least $s_y/P$ per cell. Claims to the same cell cannot come from two words. If there were $2D_m+1$ retained words, they would claim total mass greater than one. Therefore at most $2D_m$ exist. Apply the same argument to the entire retained set, now charging at least $s_y/2$ for each word to its own cells. The charges across lengths are also disjoint. This proves the global bound. A count of candidates separately at each deadline would not by itself ensure that their names form a global prefix code.

## Efficient recovery and assignment of prefix names

At epoch $j\ge1$, simulate all exact candidate $V$ names of length at most $B\lceil\log_2(j+2)\rceil$ through $2^j$ native steps. Choose the fixed constant $B$ large enough for the tuple names above. The accumulated tuple masses $\mu_j$ approximate $\mu$ from below and increase with $j$. This computation, including checking exact consumption, parsing outputs, accumulating dyadic weights and resetting the simulated memory, costs $2^j\mathrm{poly}(j+2)$. The name cap bounds all denominators by a polynomial in $j+2$.

For each $1\le m\le(j+2)^b$ and integer $d\ge0$ with $2^d\le D_m$, mark field cells with $P\mu_j(m,i,v)\ge2^{-d}$. There are at most $P2^d\le PD_m$ marked cells. We need all binary-coefficient polynomials of degree less than $m$ whose graphs are entirely marked. Here the field, list size and all polynomial degrees are polynomial in $j+2$.

Construct a nonzero bivariate polynomial $Q(X,Y)$ over $\mathbb F_P$ vanishing on every marked cell. Use monomials $X^uY^v$ with $0\le u<\lfloor P/2\rfloor$ and $0\le v\le\lfloor P/(2(m+1))\rfloor$. There are more than $PD_m$ such monomials by the choice of $P$, so homogeneous Gaussian elimination succeeds. Each monomial has $u+(m-1)v<P$. Consequently a fully marked $f$ has $Q(X,f(X))=0$ identically: the substituted polynomial has degree less than $P$ and vanishes at all $P$ field elements.

For completeness the low-degree candidates can be found deterministically by successive coefficient extraction. Divide $Q$ by its largest common power of $X$ and let $h(Y)=Q(0,Y)\ne0$. Try each root $c$ of $h$ as constant coefficient (only $0,1$ are needed). Replace $Q$ by $Q(X,c+XY)$, divide out its common power of $X$, and repeat. The substitution preserves nonzeroness. If $c$ has multiplicity $e$ in $h$, the coefficient of $Y^e$ after substitution has lowest $X$-exponent exactly $e$. All coefficients of $Y^k$ are divisible by $X^k$. Hence the degree of the new constant slice is at most $e$. At a node with constant slice $h$, both the number of children and the sum of their constant-slice degrees are at most $\deg h$, by distinctness of the roots and the multiplicity bound, respectively. This bounds the next level using the current level even when a child has degree budget zero. There are at most $\deg_Y Q$ nodes at each positive depth, including last-level leaves with degree budget zero. After $m$ levels check each surviving binary word against all the marks. The $X$-degree grows by at most $m\deg_Y Q$ before normalization, so this entire calculation takes polynomial time in $j+2$. This is the interpolation and polynomial-root method from Reed–Solomon list decoding. [Sudan’s interpolation method](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcom.1997.0439) · [Roth and Ruckenstein’s coefficient extraction](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/efficient-decoding-of-reed-solomon-codes-beyond-half-the-minimum-/)

At the first qualifying epoch for $(y,d)$, issue an interval request of width $2^{-d-3}$. Process epochs, lengths, depths and recovered words in fixed order. A given word's widths sum to at most $s_y/4$ by a geometric sum, so total width is at most one half. Place disjoint intervals consecutively and use an aligned dyadic cell of half the requested width inside each one. A decoder reproduces the scans. At each candidate cell it compares the program prefix to the cell's address, caching previously read bits and reading only until a mismatch or a full match. Earlier disjoint cells cannot cause overreading on a later assigned name. The cell costs $d+4$ bits plus a fixed compiler header. Scans, interval arithmetic and remembered first qualifications take $2^j\mathrm{poly}(j+2)$ time through epoch $j$. The depths are $O(\log(j+2))$ and all dyadic endpoints have $O(\log(j+2))$ bits.

For an original qualifying $y$, at epoch $j=q+O(\log(q+2))$ the timely names make $P\mu_j(m,i,f_y(i))\ge c p$ at every field position with some fixed $c>0$. Choose $d=\lceil-\log_2(cp)\rceil$ after decreasing $c$ to at most one. For sufficiently large $m>q$ this depth satisfies $2^d\le D_m$, since $p\ge(m+2)^{-a}$. All remaining bounded lengths can be handled by constants. This proves the stated length and time bounds, without providing $q,m,p$ to the output program.

The mechanism uses a number of tuple names and an enumeration cost polynomial in the explicit output length. For lengths approaching $2^q$, the tuple masses can fall below the high-probability hypothesis and the enumeration no longer fits the nearby clock. The construction supplies neither a deterministic full-output code in that regime nor a word that violates the numeric test.
