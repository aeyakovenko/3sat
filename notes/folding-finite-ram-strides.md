# A RAM walking by finitely many word strides on a fixed-dimensional tape

**Theorem.** A deterministic SAT solver has a single head for its unrestricted writable word memory. At length $N$ let $Q=2^w$ with $w=\Theta(\log N)$. Suppose a list of $d$ residues $g_1=1,g_2,\ldots,g_d$ can be computed in $(\log N)^{O(1)}$ time from the length and random-access input. The integer $d$ is fixed. Between successive memory accesses the head address changes by

$$y' = \sigma y + \sum_{j=1}^d h_jg_j\pmod Q,\qquad \sigma\in\{-1,1\},\qquad \sum_j |h_j|\le(\log N)^{O(1)}.$$

The controller can choose the change from the data, branch arbitrarily, and write any word at the head. It may read the input separately at arbitrary addresses and use a fixed number of word registers. The first head position is public or computable at setup, or is determined at the first probe and chosen as the coordinate origin then. Include 1 as one extra generator when necessary. For this model and each fixed $d$, there is an $\epsilon_d>0$ for which ordinary encoded SAT has no $O(N^{1+\epsilon_d})$ solver. The restriction concerns distances between accesses in the cyclic group generated by the listed strides. A single huge stride, a stride computed from the input, and modular wraparound are all allowed. The exponent depends on $d$.

## Coordinates on the address space

Define $\phi:\mathbb Z^d\to\mathbb Z/Q$ by $\phi(z)=\sum_j z_jg_j$. A basis of its kernel has columns $Qe_1$ and $e_j-g_je_1$ for $j>1$. Indeed these columns lie in the kernel, have index $Q$, and $\phi$ is onto. Reduce this integer basis by LLL to obtain columns $b_i$ of a matrix $B$ with

$$\prod_i\|b_i\|_2\le D_d |\det B|,\qquad D_d=2^{d(d-1)/4}.$$

Writing $\lambda_i$ for row $i$ of $B^{-1}$ gives $\|b_i\|_2\|\lambda_i\|_2\le D_d$. The norm of the cofactor row is the $(d-1)$-volume spanned by the other columns, at most their product of lengths. LLL takes polynomial time in the basis description length. Here the input has $O_d(w)$ bits. [Lenstra, Lenstra and Lovász, *Factoring polynomials with rational coefficients*, §1](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~lenstrahw/PUBLICATIONS/1982f/art.pdf)

Set $q_i=4d\|b_i\|_1$. For an address $y$, take any lift $z$ with $\phi(z)=y$, for instance $z=ye_1$. The cyclic coordinate is

$$t_i(y)=\left\lfloor q_i\{\lambda_i z\}\right\rfloor\in\mathbb Z/q_i,$$

where braces give the fractional part. Changing the lift adds an integer to each $\lambda_i z$. The tuple $t(y)$ uniquely identifies the address. If two lifts $z,z'$ have the same tuple, choose $v\in\mathbb Z^d$ by subtracting the two vectors of integer parts of $B^{-1}z$ and $B^{-1}z'$. The components of $B^{-1}(z-z')-v$ have absolute values less than $1/q_i$. It follows that

$$\|z-z'-Bv\|_1 < \sum_i \|b_i\|_1/q_i=1/4.$$

The left side is the norm of an integer vector, so $z-z'=Bv$ and $\phi(z)=\phi(z')$.

One generator step can be lifted to $e_j$. Before rounding, the $i$th scaled coordinate changes by $q_i(\lambda_i)_j$ modulo $q_i$, whose absolute representative is at most $4d\sqrt d\,D_d$. Floors add at most one to this cyclic distance. Under negation, $t_i(-y)$ differs from $-t_i(y)$ by at most one cyclic step since $\lfloor -u\rfloor=-\lceil u\rceil$. Fold the cyclic coordinate to $f_i(y)=\min(t_i(y),q_i-t_i(y))$. This distance from zero is 1-Lipschitz for cyclic distance. A permitted access moves each $f_i$ by $O_d(1+\sum |h_j|)$. One sign per coordinate records which preimage is present, with fixed choices at ties. Thus $(f(y),\text{signs})$ uniquely identifies the actual word. The simulator computes both tuples directly from the true addresses. It has no need to find a short coefficient vector $h$.

## A single physical head

Embeddings of general flat tori with distortion bounded in terms of dimension have an extensive prior literature. The integer bins above supply the features used here, namely exact cell identity, a constant number of tape tracks, bounded reflection cost and uniform address computation. Priority of the resulting RAM simulation is undetermined. [Haviv and Regev, *The Euclidean Distortion of Flat Tori*](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/papers/embeddingtori.pdf)

Use the folded coordinates for the $d$ tape axes. Place the bits of each guest word and a dirty flag consecutively along the first axis by scaling that coordinate by $w+1$. The at most $2^d$ sign choices are separate tracks in the fixed tape alphabet. Blank flags represent initial RAM contents. A first read uses the appropriate packed input word or zero outside the input; a write sets the dirty flag and stores the new bits on that track. Return to the flag after an access. Translating by the initial folded tuple lets the tape head start at the origin. If nonnegative tape axes are required, fold each translated coordinate at zero and add its sign to the tracks. This adds no dimension. One head reaches the next address in $(\log N)^{O(1)}$ elementary tape moves and reads or writes a word in $O(w)$ more moves.

All coordinates are computed exactly. Since $|\det B|=Q$, each fractional part on the lift $ye_1$ uses a numerator reduced modulo $Q$. At fixed $d$, LLL, cofactors, products, divisions and floor calculations use polynomially many bits and operations in $w$. Keep the basis, the simulated registers and temporary integers in a polylogarithmic read-write auxiliary store. Both preprocessing and individual steps fit the claimed polylogarithmic time overhead. In particular no initialization of an entire address range is required.

Williams's $\mathrm{DTIME}_d$ model in §7 permits a random-access read-only input, $n^{o(1)}$ bits of read-write random-access store and one sequential two-way $d$-dimensional tape. Theorem 7.5 excludes $O(n^c)$ SAT algorithms there for $c<r_d$, with $r_d>1$ for each fixed $d$. Indeed its displayed polynomial has value $-4d^2-3d-1$ at $x=1$. A hypothetical $O(N^a)$ solver yields time $n^{a+o(1)}$ in that model. When encodings differ, generate the virtual ordinary DIMACS input using the locally indexed quasilinear-size SAT reduction, charging polylogarithmic time per requested word. The stride precomputation can make its polylogarithmically many virtual input queries this way as well. Choose $1<a<c<r_d$ and set $\epsilon_d=a-1$. [Williams, §7 and Theorem 7.5](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf) · [Local explicit DIMACS conversion](packed-band-transfer.md)

For example, the additive marker RAM uses only a uniformly computable bounded palette of input words on the aligned virtual files, its fixed marker and the public initial register values. Taking these as generators gives a tape simulation regardless of their byte patterns. The earlier mixed-radix fold uses the byte patterns to construct its particular axes. For general RAM code no fixed $d$ is known to meet the displayed access condition on hard SAT inputs. A finite number of instructions by itself gives no bound on the values of successive indirect-address differences. ## Total travel on unrestricted executions

Let $P$ now be **any** correct RAM solver, fix any admissible procedure for its list $G$ of $d$ strides, and enumerate its writable-memory probe addresses $y_1,\ldots,y_m$. Define

$$\ell_G(y,y')=\min\left\{\sum_j|h_j|:\; y'=\sigma y+\sum_j h_jg_j\pmod Q,\quad \sigma=\pm1\right\}.$$

The minimum is finite because $1\in G$. The proof bounds the physical travel between successive probes by $O_d(w(1+\ell_G))$. The simulator computes the folded endpoints and goes straight between them along the axes, even when $\ell_G$ is large. It can carry this out without computing $\ell_G$. Thus for every $1\le a<r_d$, every $K$ and every length threshold there is a formula above that threshold on which

$$T_P + \sum_{i<m}\ell_G(y_i,y_{i+1}) > K N^a.$$

Otherwise its time on the hybrid tape would be $n^{a+o(1)}$, contradicting Williams as above. In particular, a hypothetical linear solver must incur this large cumulative travel for each fixed-size computable stride list. On an ordinary RAM a single probe can traverse an arbitrarily large distance in this metric. A bound on the sum leaves its native running time unresolved.

A short RAM trace can force large travel even if the stride list is selected after the trace. Alternate reads at address zero with reads at $m$ distinct other addresses. For any $d$ strides, at most $(2R+1)^d$ distinct addresses have a representation of cost at most $R$ from zero, since the coefficients are integers between $-R$ and $R$. Choose $R$ of order $m^{1/d}$ small enough that this covers at most half the addresses. The trace then has total travel $\Omega_d(m^{1+1/d})$ while a fixed RAM loop produces it in $O(m)$ probes and register operations. This tests the inference from large measured travel to a large native clock, including a stride list optimized for the trace.
