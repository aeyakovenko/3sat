# Decoding checksum parameters from corrupted selector clauses

In the [balanced checksum SAT family](sat-on-random-checksums.md), print one selector pin for every position of the full $2^d$-entry table, including its prescribed zero padding. Give the decoder the public stage and fixed clause layout, but no field-parameter header. At a chosen binary address it reads the sign of the corresponding selector pin. Suppose these signs form a fixed word at relative Hamming distance at most $1/256$ from the true selector. Other clause corruptions that prevent these indexed reads, a changing adversary answering fresh probes, and errors in random positions of the entire physical file are different access models. The object recovered here is the selector, up to parameters giving the same selector. The hidden checksum of the native computation still requires its table values.

For typical descriptors in the original random checksum distribution, the public parameters can be recovered in randomized polynomial time even under this adversarial corruption, up to the simultaneous Frobenius ambiguity. The decoder learns the additive labels of the corrupted rows by a minimum-cost matching computation. A shorter argument first gives a quasipolynomial bound, and the final construction gives a simpler polynomial decoder for a modified layout.

Let $K=\mathbb F_q$, $q=2^s\ge4$. Write $r_i=p_i/(1-p_i)$ at interior coordinates and $A=\lambda\prod_i(1-p_i)$. A nonzero mask and interior point give the selector
$$
                  c(x)=\operatorname{Tr}\!\left(A\prod_{i:x_i=1}r_i\right)
$$
with $A\ne0$, $r_i\notin\{0,1\}$. This uses the trace convention for the binary mask. The public change of basis to ordinary binary dot products is invertible. All running times below include field arithmetic, indexing and physical word access. The parameter sizes in the checksum construction are $d=\Theta(j)$ and $q=\operatorname{poly}(j)$.

## A uniform decoder for the existing random parameters

Fix the first $b=\lceil s+2\log_2(d+1)+12\rceil$ coordinates as a block, for sufficiently large stages where $b<d$. For a typical true tuple, the distribution of $H(u)=\prod_{i\le b:u_i=1} r_i$ for uniform $u\in\{0,1\}^b$ is within $1/32$ of uniform on $K^*$. Indeed, for independent ratios uniform on $K^*\setminus\{1\}$, each nontrivial multiplicative Fourier coefficient has expected squared magnitude at most $2^{-b}$. Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz give $\mathbb E\|\mathcal L_u(H)-U_{K^*}\|_{\rm TV}^2\le(q-2)2^{-b}/4$; Markov bounds failure by $256q2^{-b}=O((d+1)^{-2})$. This expectation is over the sampled ratios, with the total variation computed after fixing them. Also require the previously established full-selector distance of at least $1/64$ from every inequivalent parameter tuple. Both properties hold together with probability tending to one under the original parameter distribution. [Full-selector distance and product-distribution proof](distance-between-checksum-queries.md)

Given the true block ratios, consider the length-$2^b$ binary words $C_z(u)=\operatorname{Tr}(zH(u))$, $z\in K$. Two distinct words differ on at least $15/32$ of the block addresses: a nonzero field linear functional is one on at least half of $K^*$, and the distribution of $H$ changes this probability by at most $1/32$. For an outside address $y$, the verified model of the row is $C_{z(y)}$ with $z(y)=A\prod_{i>b:y_i=1}r_i$. Read the entire observed row and choose a field label minimizing Hamming distance, breaking ties deterministically. If its fraction of corrupted signs is at most $1/5$, this returns the actual $z(y)$ because $2/5<15/32$. The average row error is at most $1/256$. Fewer than $5/256$ of all outside rows have error above $1/5$.

For each outside coordinate $i$, sample uniformly a pair $y,y+e_i$ with $y_i=0$. With probability at least $1-10/256$ both decoded rows are correct. Indeed the sum of their two bad marginal probabilities is twice the unconditional bad-row probability. Their nonzero label quotient is $r_i$. Repeat independently $O(\log((d+1)/\eta))$ times per coordinate and take a majority, aiming for total decoder failure probability $\eta\in(0,1/2)$. Record an invalid quotient as a failed vote. With the recovered ratios, use fresh uniform rows to vote for $A=z(y)/\prod_{i>b:y_i=1}r_i$. This vote is correct on at least $1-5/256$ of rows when the preceding ratios are correct. The repeated trials' concentration and a union bound prove success probability $1-O(\eta)$ with the true block. Obtain $p_i=r_i/(1+r_i)$, then $\lambda=A/\prod_i(1-p_i)$.

Enumerate the at most $q^b$ possible block tuples of interior ratios and run the same procedure for each, rejecting invalid outputs. The random addresses above can be fixed in advance and shared across all guesses; only the true guess needs the majority argument. Test proposed full tuples on an **independent** set of $O((d+1)\log q+\log(1/\eta))$ uniformly drawn single addresses. Accept an empirical fraction of errors at most $1/128$. The true descriptor has error at most $1/256$ on the observed oracle, and each inequivalent descriptor has error at least $3/256$. The bounded-variable concentration inequality and a union bound over $q^{d+1}$ possible descriptors give simultaneous correctness with probability $1-O(\eta)$ with a sufficiently large absolute sample constant. This also covers candidates depending on the first-phase samples. At a good stage every retained tuple lies in the same Frobenius orbit. Use a fixed selection among them, and allow explicit failure if none are retained. For $d\le b$, exhaustively test all $q^{d+1}$ descriptors on validation samples under the same distance promise.

Raw selector queries cost $O(2^b(d+1)\log((d+1)/\eta)+(d+1)\log q+\log(1/\eta))$. Row decoding can enumerate all $q$ words at cost $O(q2^b)\operatorname{poly}(d,s)$ per row and per block guess. For inverse-polynomial $\eta$ the total RAM work is $2^{O(s(s+\log(d+1)))}\operatorname{poly}(d,q)=2^{O((\log j)^2)}$. The stored data fit this same budget, and the enumeration can stream guesses, using polynomial space in $j$ for fixed inverse-polynomial failure. Field arithmetic and constructing $K$ cost polynomial time in $q,d$. All physical row addresses take $O(d+s)$ bits plus the public layout's polynomial-in-$j$ offset computation. This algorithm does not use a SAT solver. Giving it just one of the independent random $r_i$ saves one guessed field element in this argument; the next algorithm avoids all block guesses.

## Polynomial time on the original random distribution

The enumeration of unknown block parameters can be eliminated. For $q\ge2^{16}$, $d=\Theta(j)$ and the stated $q=\operatorname{poly}(j)$ with $q/d\to\infty$, the following randomized algorithm uses $\operatorname{poly}(q,d,\log(1/\eta))$ time and physical word probes. With probability $1-o(1)$ over the original field parameters, **for every fixed error pattern of relative weight at most $1/256$**, its probability of recovering a simultaneous Frobenius conjugate of the genuine parameters is at least $1-\eta$. It needs no field element as advice. The polynomial bound concerns asymptotically sufficiently large stages. For a machine with the fixed $O(j)$-bit native address convention take inverse-polynomial $\eta$; the general confidence bound uses words sufficient to address the stated polynomial-size arrays. It may reject outside its promise.

Use the same short block of $b$ coordinates, $L=2^b=\operatorname{poly}(d,q)$, and unknown length-$L$ binary linear code $\mathcal C=\{C_z:z\in K\}$. Its relative distance is at least $15/32$, so it contains exactly $q$ words. Put $g=q-1$ and $\tau=10^{-4}$. In the outside block require every nonzero label to have probability in $[(1-\tau)/g,(1+\tau)/g]$, both unconditionally and after fixing any one outside address bit. This stronger mixing condition is typical. Apply the same squared total variation bound to every remaining set of at least $d-b-1$ ratios and use Markov with threshold $\tau/(2g)$. A union bound gives failure at most $O(dq^3 2^{-(d-b-1)}/\tau^2)$ conditional on interior parameters. Such total variation bounds imply the stated pointwise bound; normalization by a nonzero scalar preserves it. This also applies to the distribution of $z(y)$, where $y$ is the outside address. Its support is all of $K^*$.

**1. Obtain distances to an unknown linear code.** Use relative Hamming distance $d_H$ on the $L$-bit strings. For probability distributions use $W_1$, the minimum expected $d_H$ under a coupling. If $U$ is uniform on $\mathcal C$, and $U+w$ denotes translation by a trial string $w$, then
$$
                   W_1(U,U+w)=d_H(w,\mathcal C).
$$
Every pair of elements from the two cosets has distance at least the right side; translating each element by $w+c_0$ for a closest $c_0\in\mathcal C$ attains it. For a distribution $D$ within $\kappa$ of $U$ in $W_1$, translation invariance and the triangle inequality give
$$
       \left|W_1(D,D+w)-d_H(w,\mathcal C)\right|\le2\kappa
$$
simultaneously for all $w$.

Construct $D$ as the empirical distribution of $N$ observed rows at independently uniform outside addresses, reading all $L$ selector bits of each row. Coupling to the clean row at the same address costs at most $1/256+10^{-4}$ with failure probability $O(\eta)$ by concentration of independent bounded row-error fractions. The empirical frequencies of the **clean** labels differ from their population frequencies by at most $10^{-4}/q$ at every label with the same confidence, with $N=Cq^2\log(q/\eta)$ for a sufficiently large absolute constant $C$. These labels are needed only in the proof. Their population law is within $\tau+1/q$ in total variation of uniform on all $K$, allowing for the missing zero. Total variation bounds transportation distance in a space of diameter one. Thus $W_1(D,U)\le\kappa=0.005$ with probability $1-O(\eta)$. For any trial $w$, compute $W_1(D,D+w)$ as the minimum-cost matching of the $N$ stored strings to their $N$ translates, with integer Hamming costs and final division by $NL$. The finite assignment formulation handles repeated strings as separate copies and equals the fractional transport optimum by integrality of bipartite assignment. All distances and comparisons can use integers. [Edmonds and Karp on assignment algorithms](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/matching/Edmonds-Karp-network-flow.pdf)

**2. Recover the addition table of the latent labels.** Generate $O(q\log(q/\eta))$ independent pairs of fresh observed rows and use their bitwise XORs as candidates $w$. For each $z\in K$, the probability that the two genuine labels sum to $z$ is at least $(q-2)(1-\tau)^2/g^2>0.99/q$. Conditional on this sum, the probability of any first outside address increases by a factor at most
$$
               \frac{(1+\tau)g}{(q-2)(1-\tau)^2}<1.01;
$$
the same holds for the second address. Indeed the independent partner has either a prescribed nonzero label or a missing zero label. Hence the expected sum of the rows' relative errors conditioned on this label sum is at most $2.02/256$. The conditional probability that $d_H(w,C_z)\le0.025$ exceeds $0.6$ by Markov. Independent repetition and a union bound therefore give such a candidate for every $z$ with probability $1-O(\eta)$.

Keep the candidates with $W_1(D,D+w)\le0.036$. On the first phase's event every candidate within $0.025$ is kept and every kept candidate is within $0.046$ of a true codeword. Two kept candidates belonging to the same codeword differ by at most $0.092$, and ones belonging to different codewords differ by at least $15/32-0.092$. Cluster at distance $0.15$ and pick one representative per cluster. With the stated confidence there are exactly $q$ representatives, each within $0.046$ of a different true codeword. Given two representatives, the nearest representative to their bitwise XOR is the one for the sum of their underlying labels, since its distance is at most $3(0.046)<(15/32)/2$. This constructs the exact addition table, without identifying any individual field element in advance. The zero representative and a binary basis can be found by table lookup and successive doubling of the spanned set. This assigns a distinct $s$-bit coordinate vector to each representative. Under the successful events its relation to the original field labels is a fixed invertible $\mathbb F_2$-linear map, denoted by $U_0:K\to\mathbb F_2^s$.

**3. Recover multiplication by the outside ratios.** Decode a fresh full row to its nearest representative. When its error is at most $0.17$, that representative is correct because $0.17+0.046<(15/32)/2$. At most $(1/256)/0.17$ of the outside addresses exceed this error. For each outside coordinate $i$, sample fresh uniform pairs with the $i$th bit fixed first to zero and then one. At most $2/(256\cdot0.17)<0.046$ of these pairs give an incorrect label at either end. The genuine first label is pointwise nearly uniform on $K^*$ by the single-bit conditioning promise. In the learned coordinates the true pair is $(v,M_i v)$, where $M_i=U_0 (z\mapsto r_i z)U_0^{-1}$ is binary linear. For each output bit compare all $q$ binary linear forms on the decoded first labels with the decoded second bit. The correct form has error at most $0.046$; every false form disagrees on at least $(1-\tau)q/(2g)-0.046>0.45$ of pairs. With $O(\log(qds/\eta))$ independent trials per coordinate, their empirical errors choose all the true matrix rows simultaneously with probability $1-O(\eta)$. This comparison can enumerate the forms because the field cardinality is polynomial in $j$.

Generate the multiplicative semigroup of the $M_i$, starting with the identity and closing under multiplication by the generators. On good data it has exactly $g$ elements: every product acts by field multiplication, and the pointwise lower bound ensures the subset products of the original outside ratios reach every nonzero field element, up to the fixed scalar $A$. Enumerate their orders to find an element $J$ of order $g$. Enumerate its powers to write each $M_i=J^{e_i}$. Compute the first monic dependence of $I,J,J^2,\ldots,J^s$ as binary matrices by Gaussian elimination. Its polynomial $f$ has degree $s$ and is the minimal polynomial of the primitive field element acting by $J$; indeed multiplication realizes an injective algebra embedding of $K$ in the matrices. Evaluate $f$ on all of the public field $K$ to find a root $a$. Every root is a Frobenius conjugate of that primitive element. Hence $a^{e_i}$ recovers every outside ratio up to **one common** field automorphism $\sigma$. This argument needs neither a discrete-log oracle nor a supplied generator: the group has polynomially many elements. Cap the semigroup at $g$, reject failed group, basis or root searches, and bound all loops by their stated limits even on malformed samples.

**4. Recover the remaining parameters.** The outside ratios are now known up to $\sigma$. Use them as the known block in the first algorithm. For an inside-block address $u$, the true row as $y$ varies has scalar $\sigma(AH(u))$ in the recovered representation because the absolute trace is invariant under Frobenius. To decode this row, try all $q$ scalars against the observed bits at fresh independent random outside addresses. The corresponding true scalar words have relative distance at least $(1-\tau)q/(2g)>0.49$. Whenever this row's actual error is at most $1/5$, the genuine scalar has a margin at least $0.49-2/5$ over any incorrect one in expected error. A logarithmic number $O(\log(qd\log((d+1)/\eta)/\eta))$ of fresh samples per attempted row suffices, now without reading the exponentially long outside block. Take repeated random opposite-bit pairs on the small block to decode its coordinate quotients, and then scalar votes for $\sigma(A)$ as in the first algorithm, using fresh samples in each phase. The overall error rate over this transposed array is still $1/256$, so at most $5/256$ of inside-block rows have error larger than $1/5$. This completes recovery of all $\sigma(p_i)$ and $\sigma(\lambda)$, after rescaling the failure budgets by an absolute constant.

All estimates condition only on successful prior phases and use fresh independent addresses as indicated. The oracle's errors may be coordinated across all probes; the argument uses their fixed total weight, including when an address repeats. Computing all proposal-to-distribution matchings, the finite code addition table, the binary matrices and their powers takes polynomial time in $q,d,\log(1/\eta)$; in particular the matching subroutine uses $O(N^3)$ operations after its cost matrix is formed. All algorithmic tables have polynomial size in these parameters, and costs such as $NL$ require only logarithmically many bits in their magnitudes. At inverse-polynomial failure in $j$, the running time and the number of physical formula probes are both polynomial in $j$. Removing a query header therefore does not, even with this selector-bit noise, conceal the public parameters at typical stages. The actual base-table parity remains undetermined.

## A polynomial-time variant preserving the genuine SAT verdicts

There is a different, still near-native physical encoding where the block is known. Append $s$ binary index bits and **repeat** the original table across all their settings. Each copy is forced to equal its source original table variable; the originally padded zeros stay zero. Use disjoint variables for the deterministic checksum suffix. For every choice of the original field point, summing the interpolation basis over all new coordinates multiplies its field symbol by one, so the genuine SAT verdict and number of full satisfying assignments are unchanged. This adds a factor $q=\operatorname{poly}(j)$ in table slots and a polynomial-in-$j$ factor in encoded size and local-generation time.

Fix a primitive $g\in K^*$ and prescribe the new ratios $r_i=g^{2^i}$ for $0\le i<s$, equivalently $p_i=r_i/(1+r_i)$. None equals one since the odd group order $q-1>1$ is coprime to every $2^i$. The $q$ block products are $g^n$ for $0\le n\le q-1$, yielding every nonzero field element once and the unit twice. Every pair of scalar words therefore differs on at least $q/2$ of these $q$ positions. The primitive element can be public, found by a fixed deterministic enumeration of field elements and exact order tests. Alternatively knowing the genuine $g=r_0$ suffices for this correlated block. Apply the row-decoder and quotient votes for this *single* block. For every original interior tuple with nonzero mask, under the same corruption limit this takes $\operatorname{poly}(j,\log(1/\eta))$ RAM steps with success $1-O(\eta)$. No descriptor search or validation against descriptors with a different block is necessary here. A nonzero original mask and an interior original point occur with probability at least $1-(2d+1)/q$ for the random checksums.

The distinction is in what a decoder of the public clause signs learns. The decoders recover a description of the measurement applied to the base variables. Computing the result of that measurement is the SAT decision under study. The polynomial variant changes the distribution of public selector parameters in a specified extended presentation, while computing exactly the old hidden checksum.

The finite audit recovers 23 actual selector ratios up to a common Frobenius conjugation over $\mathbb F_{128}$ under a fixed error pattern, tests 137 transport scores and verifies all 16,384 additions of the recovered labels. It stratifies a small empirical sample by its clean label to calibrate the matching step, so it illustrates the finite algorithmic identities rather than testing the large-$q$ sampling theorem at its stated constants. Lean checks the coset matching lower bound, a translation attaining it, and the uniqueness of the recovered prototype sum in a translation-invariant binary group metric.
