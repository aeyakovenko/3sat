# An atomless clock and the price of preserving a missing representative

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A computable change to the prefix machine really can force every limiting first-production measure to be atomless on $[3,4]$. The construction also identifies the failure in applying the linear-SAT transfer to this modified source. A second version repairs the missing-representative issue but incurs a precise increase in the prefix-code cost for producing one word. ## Staggering the times at which an output can be published

Run an ordinary universal stream machine as the body. When it finishes with an $m$-bit word $y$, hold that candidate until the next permitted publication step. For $j=\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor$, set $L=2^j$ and let $u(y)\in[0,L)$ be the integer given by its last $j$ bits. The permitted steps are $2^n$ with $n\equiv u(y)\pmod L$. For definiteness, if the body is ready by time $t$, take the least such power at least $t+m+2$. The body consumes no further input. The event can be defined by a clocked publication instruction, or by keeping the candidate pending and testing the indicated global timestamps. This gives a computable prefix-free source. It retains unbounded universality of function values. All start-up conventions at small lengths can be fixed arbitrarily.

On the $m$-bit target words beginning with $j$ zeroes the residues are exactly uniform when $m\ge2j$. For any interval of normalized log-rates of length $\delta$, there are at most $\delta m+2$ integer exponents in it. Thus at most $(\delta m+2)/L\le2\delta+4/m$ of the target words can **ever** be published there, regardless of how many programs they have. The feedback measure normalized by the phase cost $P_r$ charges total target mass at most two. Its mass on every interval of length $\delta$ is bounded by $4\delta+o(1)$ as the phase grows. The open-set inequality for weak convergence gives the same estimate for open intervals at any weak limit. There are no atoms. This part makes no SAT assumption.

Fix a rational $\alpha\in[3,4]$ and a constant $a>0$, write $D=2^{\lfloor\alpha m\rfloor}$, and consider the microscopic interval $(\lfloor D/m^a\rfloor,D]$. In fact **no target word first appears in that interval at any sufficiently large $m$**. A word that could appear there must have one of $O(a\log m+1)$ permitted residues. To see the clock-independent coding directly, take a fixed integer $A>a+2$ and list the residues of exponents $\lfloor\alpha m\rfloor-A(j+1)$ through $\lfloor\alpha m\rfloor$. This easily computed interval includes all possibilities. A decoder reads a self-delimiting code for $j$, $j$ bits for $m-2^j$, $m-2j$ free output bits, and an index into this list. It prepends the $j$ zeroes and appends the chosen residue. Including the compiler, its name has length

$$
  O(\log(j+2))+j+(m-2j)+O(\log(j+2))=m-j+O(\log(j+2))<m .
$$

The body finishes in $\operatorname{poly}(m)$ time. Publication occurs by $2^{L+O(\log m)}$, eventually well before $D/m^a$. Hence even genuine permanent holes avoid the affordable set of words with the required timing residue. This holds across all lengths, not only the selected phase lengths.

A body that must first finish at or after $D$ and has residue $(\lfloor\alpha m\rfloor-1)\bmod L$ cannot publish before $2^{L-1}D$. More generally the worst-case multiplicative price of this gate is of order $2^L$. Restricting $L$ to $O(\log m)$ restores polynomial gate overhead, but a logarithmic time interval of sufficiently large constant width then includes every residue. Choosing $\log m\ll L\ll m$ can make the fixed microscopic interval sparse while still costing a superpolynomial factor in $m$. The near-clock decoder relies on a polynomial factor in $m$ between its counting deadline and its publication deadline. These facts concern the gate and its exact output times. They do not give a lower bound for arbitrary compilation of untimed functions.

Backdating a virtual timestamp moves the expense to the cutoff test. More generally, suppose an unbounded nondecreasing rounding on physical completion times for a fixed $y$ uses only its permitted stamps. At a jump from virtual stamp $A$ to $B\ge2^L A$ between physical times $t-1$ and $t$, the worst forward inflation is at least $B/t$, and the worst inverse inflation is at least $(t-1)/A$. The latter is charged when a direct bounded-transcript verifier must include every physical run eligible by virtual stamp $A$. Their product is at least $2^L(1-1/t)$. Thus even nearest-stamp rounding cannot keep both costs polynomial in $m$ with $L\gg\log m$. A faster verifier of the long physical run would require a separate argument.

## Trying to keep a genuine omission at every timing residue

Modify the body protocol. First a prefix program selects $(m,x)$ with $|x|=m-j$. Only after that selection does the machine consume $j$ more literal bits for $u$, and output $x\|u$. The core never sees the suffix. Add a final publication opportunity at $B(m)=2^{20m}$ for every residue. For example, count a core as ready for the deadline only if it left the same $j+O(m)$-step allowance to copy its suffix, independently of the suffix value. All literals take the same copying time. A core ready by that time covers all its suffixes, and otherwise covers none by $B(m)$. For the same $x$, every literal has exactly the same total name length. Thus full-cutoff omissions are unions of whole fibers. The extra publication opportunity at rate 20 leaves the interval estimate on $[3,4]$ unchanged.

In a good block, at least one of the prescribed lengths has a missing target. Its whole fiber is missing, including a representative whose residue has a gate in the desired interval. That one word would be enough to contradict a dispatcher capable of covering *all* requests of the thin admissible list. The price of its name is the obstruction. For a core fixed before reading the literal suffix, a valid short name must reach the end of the core field in at most $m-j-1$ bits. Distinct selected pairs $(m,x)$ require different prefix-free core fields. On a fixed length, $n$ such fields therefore cost at least

$$
       n\,2^{-(m-j-1)}=2L\, n\,2^{-m}
$$

in input-cylinder measure. If one needs $k$ acceptable residues per core, those $nk$ target words have normalized weight $nk2^{-m}$. The core-field lower bound is $2L/k$ times that weight. The factor two comes from strict shortness. The factor $L/k$ exactly cancels the thinning from retaining $k$ out of $L$ timing residues. A standard dispatch with core depth $m-j-1$ attains the individual cell cost. The promise of an admissible missing representative did not supply the information bits needed to select a core independently of its raw suffix.

This identifies two different, actual failure modes. With an unrestricted body, the timing-admissible targets have short early producers. With compulsory literal copying, permanent omissions retain the desired residues, but the shared dispatcher must charge for cores. Neither source currently gives a contradiction for SAT.
