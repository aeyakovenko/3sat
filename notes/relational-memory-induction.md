# Proving equal verdicts of two RAM runs from a relation on their memories

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** I tested whether two long computations can be certified to agree without producing an accepting history for either. A relation can correlate all their memory cells through shared hidden bits. When each cell contributes affine equations on those bits, a failure of induction has a witness involving at most one more equation than there are hidden bits. This gives an exact fixed-alternation test even when the memories are exponentially larger than the test. On the [sparse diagonal language](coded-diagonal.md), a linear SAT algorithm would imply that proofs of this kind can connect only a bounded number of independent pairs of inputs. Finding sufficiently many connections for one implementation of that language would rule out linear SAT.

Affine program invariants and finite-instantiation methods for array properties have established precedents. Here the machine transition can use ordinary nonlinear word operations. Linearity is imposed on the hidden variables inside the supplied relation. Priority for the particular rank-sensitive verification bound is unestablished. [Karr, *Affine Relationships Among Variables of a Program*](https://www-apr.lip6.fr/~mine/enseignement/mpri/attic/2014-2015/exos/karr.pdf) · [Bradley, Manna and Sipma, *What’s Decidable About Arrays?*](https://theory.stanford.edu/~arbrad/papers/arrays.pdf)

## What the certificate says

Fix a deterministic RAM implementation, two public inputs and a time bound. Pad early halts with absorbing steps, and compare the runs at the same clock up to the terminal deadline. The entire bounded machine has a fixed number of registers and an array of words indexed by a finite address space. The clock, registers and addresses have a specified bit bound. Any extra register carrying the verdict belongs to the public register state. Read-only input bits can be inspected using the public input. The certificate may choose its programs separately for every input pair. Use $\rho$ for the two register states and the common clock.

At each $\rho$, pair an address $a$ of the first memory with address $\pi_\rho(a)$ of the second by a supplied permutation with a supplied inverse. Both may depend on $\rho$. The certificate has one tag $z\in\mathbb F_2^g$. For register constraints and for each quadruple $(\rho,a,u,v)$ it specifies a system of affine equations on $z$. Here $u,v$ are the words at the paired addresses. The equations may depend arbitrarily on these word values. Use a fixed-length local index: it selects one equation, with unused indices returning $0=0$. The output is the entire $g$-bit coefficient row and its right side. Different addresses may supply different numbers of effective equations.

A pair of states is related if **there exists one common tag satisfying all register and cell equations**. To certify the two verdicts agree, require that the initial pair is related, every related pair before the deadline steps to a related pair, and every terminal related pair has equal verdicts. The equations at the successor may be satisfied by a fresh tag depending on the entire new state. No program for finding that tag is required.

## The size of a failed step

A finite inconsistent affine system on $g$ variables contains an inconsistent subsystem of at most $g+1$ rows. Choose a maximal independent family of coefficient vectors. The corresponding equations are consistent, and every other coefficient vector has a unique expansion in their basis. If all the corresponding right sides match their expansions, the full system is consistent. Otherwise the mismatching equation and its basis terms give the required subsystem. This includes $g=0$ and a zero row with right side one. The $g$ equations $z_i=0$ together with $\sum_i z_i=1$ show sharpness for the number of witness rows.

First validate the supplied permutation and inverse on all register states and addresses by bounded universal challenges. For a proposed failure of step closure, guess the pre-register pair, its tag $z$, and the finite memory events of one step on each side. Also guess at most $g+1$ register or address-indexed equations at the successor, along with the locations, word values and local indices needed to evaluate them. Determine the post-registers from the proposed steps and pair post-addresses by the actual post-permutation. Pull all needed old-word accesses back to pre-address pairs using the pre-permutation and its inverse. Require that repeated guesses for an address agree, that both steps read the guessed old memory with the proper ordering of writes, and that the guessed post words follow from the writes. Sort these sparse records or compare them directly.

For each mentioned pre-address pair guess both words and check **every** local equation on the pre-tag. Check the pre-register equations also. On the remaining addresses require
$$
\forall a\ \exists u,v\ \forall j\quad
  \text{the pre-equation at }(\rho,a,u,v,j)\text{ holds for }z.
$$
One may check this on all addresses, since the already prescribed pairs pass. The alignments form disjoint pairs, so the remaining choices extend simultaneously to two full pre-memories. Finally test the displayed post-rows for inconsistency by elimination. If all checks pass, a real pair of related configurations makes a step to an unrelated pair. Conversely every failing step supplies such a certificate by the affine subsystem bound. This establishes exactness, including register-dependent changes of alignment. Requiring only the touched pre-addresses to pass without the extension check could report a failure from an impossible pre-state.

Testing the initial condition uses one tag and universal equation indices, evaluating the actual initial memory words directly. A violation of the terminal condition uses the same pre-tag feasibility and extension checks and discordant verdict registers. All tests have a fixed number of Boolean quantifier blocks. Initial and terminal conditions therefore also admit the [fixed-block simulation](linear-quantifiers.md) under linear SAT.

For an explicit cost, allow $g$, template length, each local evaluation time and local-index length all at most $S$, with words of $O(\log S)$ bits in the source RAM. Bound the template evaluators by a public clock, using fixed instruction sets with quasilinear simulation overhead. Computing up to $S+1$ full rows, guessing their indices, sorting sparse address records and checking all selected pre-fibers (universally challenge an index and a selected address) costs at most $S^2\,\mathrm{polylog}(S+2)$ per branch. Gaussian elimination costs $O((S+1)^3\mathrm{polylog}(S+2))$ in bit operations. This bound includes writing the selected rows. A bound $S$ on the binary length of each local index allows up to $2^S$ local equations; no enumeration is used. Existentially choosing the entire template, followed by the indicated fixed-block checks for its validity, still has a fixed number of blocks.

## A rank bound that is part of the relation

The coefficient rows can also be given **implicitly** with $2^b$ columns. A local evaluator now takes one $b$-bit column name and returns the coefficient there; another evaluation gives the right side. For a pair of register states and memories form the full coefficient matrix $A$ and right side $v$ of all register and cell equations. Define relatedness to mean
$$
\operatorname{rank}(A)\le R\quad\text{and}\quad A z=v\text{ has a solution}.
$$
The size restriction concerns the rank on pairs of states that the relation admits. Other pairs may have unbounded rank. A particular step preserving this relation can change both the row space and the solution. The test uses no supplied representation of that change. I use finite binary matrix rank here; all row and column index domains are finite even when the matrix is enormous.

For a proposed **related pre-state**, guess $r\le R$ anchor rows and columns with nonsingular $r$-square minor, caching the pre-words of the anchor cells. Certify the minor by supplying its entries and inverse. Universally check any entry against its evaluator and any entry of the product against the identity. For each further pre-row require that there are $r$ coefficients whose combination of the anchored rows equals that row on every column **and** on its right side. Equivalently the anchor columns determine the unique candidate combination, which must then work everywhere. For $r=0$ the row and right side must both vanish. The anchors plus these tests suffice: they force rank $r$ and the independent anchor equations always have a common solution for any anchor right sides. Conversely a related pair of memories has such a certificate by choosing a full-rank minor and using consistency.

Unmentioned pre-addresses still need a simultaneous extension. After guessing the anchors, all their word values are pinned, along with the sparse pre-accesses of the step and post challenges. Validate each pinned pair and all register constraints. For the other addresses the branch quantifiers have the order
$$
\forall a\;\exists u,v\;\forall \text{ local row }j\;\exists \lambda\in\mathbb F_2^r\;\forall \text{ column }k .
$$
Check the row identity and its right side against the anchored rows. The register state, anchors and their values stay fixed across all these tests. An unmentioned address now interacts only with its own paired address, so its choices extend just as in the explicit-tag proof. This is the reason to include the anchor *rows* as well as the anchor columns.

There are two ways to witness an unrelated successor. A nonsingular $(R+1)$-minor witnesses excessive rank using the same matrix-entry test. Otherwise inconsistency has a witness of at most $R+1$ rows: give a combination with XOR of right sides one and universally challenge a column to check that the XOR of its coefficients is zero. Every failure has one of the two certificates. Neither check reads the full coefficient vector. Incorporate all the required post-address pairs in the same pre-access log as the actual step, and perform the unchanged pre-extension test. For the initial condition check anchored rows on the actual initial memories. Terminal disagreement uses anchored feasibility and the discordant registers.

Take $R,b$, local-row index lengths, template length and bounded evaluator time at most $S$. The branch clock is $O((S+1)^2\mathrm{polylog}(S+2))$. Lists of anchor/challenge descriptions, column names, two explicit minors and inverses take $O(S^2)$ bits. A single chosen matrix entry needs one coefficient query. A single checked product entry costs $O(S)$ bit operations up to addressing costs. For a universally challenged column of a reconstruction or inconsistency identity, at most $R+1$ evaluators are executed, at cost $O((S+1)^2)$ up to logarithms. Sparse access sorting, checks of repeated address pairs and the remaining register operations fit this same budget. Large local evaluations may use their own charged scratch. Comparison of all cells, rows or columns uses constant alternation depth. This permits $2^S$ implicit feature coordinates even when an explicit tag of that length exceeds the running time of the original computation.

## An implication on an actual sparse diagonal

In the [linear-claim diagonal](coded-diagonal.md) use all pair queries $e_i+e_j$ for distinct length-$n$ inputs, with either proposed right side. For each $n$ its length-$2^n$ table $J_n$ has at most $n$ ones, since at most $n$ independent equations are imposed and the lexicographically first solution has independent columns at its one coordinates. Fix any deterministic single-exponential time RAM implementation of this particular language with $O(n)$-bit addresses, a common explicit single-exponential deadline and a simple initialization. The original single-exponential multitape construction has such a RAM implementation. The transition, clock arithmetic and initial-memory lookup calculations cost $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ with this presentation.

Let $S=2^{4n}$ and use the implicit rank-bounded construction. Join two inputs by an edge when there exists a valid certificate of the kind above with budget $S$ comparing them on this implementation. The calculation charges $2^{8n+o(n)}$ target RAM time under linear SAT, including elimination of the fixed quantifier blocks. A multitape scan of the list of all RAM writes gives a quadratic simulation up to word overhead, hence $2^{16n+o(n)}$ tape time. This fits the diagonal's $2^{20n}$ simulated-step clock. The deterministic program for deciding claims is fixed after the hypothesized solver and implementation are fixed. It proposes just the zero equations for the accepted pairs. All are sound.

The diagonal therefore bounds the rank of the edge normals by a constant at all sufficiently large $n$. In a component of $k$ vertices these normals span dimension $k-1$: tree edges are independent by the leaf test and every pair in the component is their path sum. Distinct components have disjoint coordinates. Consequently all but $O(1)$ inputs are isolated, even though all but $n$ inputs have the same verdict. This applies separately to every fixed implementation meeting the stated clock conventions. A construction of certificates that connects unboundedly many vertices on an infinite sequence of lengths for just one of them would refute unrestricted linear-time SAT. Merely computing an initial segment of each run and explicitly comparing its configurations is among the weaker possibilities.

## How many hidden bits can a single verdict need?

Consider two ordinary linear-time routines on a common $m$-bit input $x$. At a synchronized checkpoint one stores $x$ in $m$ cells. The other stores its parity in a distinguished cell and can leave the remaining cells zero. Erase auxiliary registers or synchronize them to fixed values; discard the read-only input and any auxiliary history from the comparison interface at the checkpoint. The first routine's suffix returns the parity of its stored cells, and the second routine's suffix returns its distinguished cell. The routines genuinely agree for every $x$.

Any sufficient checkpoint relation covering all these input pairs and guaranteeing equal verdicts for its members must cover those $2^m$ left words. For a fixed tag its memory constraint is a product of allowed paired-cell sets. For every left position except the partner of the distinguished right cell, the left bit is fixed in a nonempty sound product. Indeed if that bit can vary, replace its paired-cell choice while holding all other pairs fixed. Left parity changes and the distinguished right bit does not. Thus one tag covers at most two left words. At least $m-1$ tag bits are necessary. The lower bound allows a different permutation and arbitrary local tests for each tag. Conversely take one tag bit for each left cell other than the distinguished cell's partner. Pin those bits to their left values and in the remaining pair require that their sum equals the XOR of the two local bits. These are affine constraints. The exact cost in this interface is $m-1$ tag bits. Holding an arbitrary original input available for every local test, choosing a different certificate per $x$, or exposing a running parity register at this checkpoint changes the problem. The example rules out an automatic tag bound for a uniform relation over genuine executions.

For the implicit-coordinate version, even arbitrarily many feature coordinates cannot reduce the required rank below $m-1$ when the address alignment is fixed at the synchronized registers. An unrelated state has a certificate consisting of at most $R+1$ register or paired-cell rows, by the excessive-rank and inconsistency tests. Choose a false assignment with all undistinguished right cells zero. Flipping any one left bit makes it a checkpoint pair that the certificate must cover, with exactly this fixed right array. If fewer than $m$ aligned pairs are inspected, flipping an uninspected left bit preserves the entire certificate of unrelatedness. This is impossible. Thus $R+1\ge m$, and the explicit affine construction attains equality. The argument also covers arbitrary higher-dimensional feature spaces and relation templates with unboundedly many equations per cell.

For the pair diagonal, a tempting implementation first constructs its sparse table using work independent of the requested argument and reads that argument at the end. During most of this prefix the two work memories can indeed be coupled by equality. At the lookup step the claimed relation must still exclude tables on which the two specified answers differ. Even a possible table with just one exceptional bit can defeat a fixed pair. I have no sufficiently small sound invariants for the actual remaining table values.
