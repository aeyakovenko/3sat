# Common RAM checkpoints from overlapping input views

Fix any deterministic SAT solver on the journal's logarithmic-word RAM. There are ordinary fixed-layout 3-CNFs with three input banks $x,y,z$ of $m$ independent literal signs each and encoded length $N=\Theta(m\log(m+2))$. For either verdict, an exponential bound controls the probability of compressing *arbitrary hindsight partitions of the solver's genuine run*. The number and lengths of the phases are unrestricted. A phase may designate any one bank after seeing the entire run. Across phases charge a distinct sign of the chosen banks once, regardless of repeated reads.

Suppose that each of three fixed decoders, given any two of the banks, the size, the proposed verdict and a shared $h$-bit description, can recover the **same ordered list of all internal checkpoint configurations**. The decoder missing $x$ can compute on all of $y,z$, and likewise cyclically. Their local computation time and the length of their outputs can be unlimited. They do not receive cut positions or phase lengths. Let $q$ be the total number of distinct bank-tagged signs accessed in the bank selected for their phase, after taking a union across all phases. For a uniform input the probability of having the proposed verdict and admitting such a description and partition with charge at most $q$ is at most
$$
  2^h \left(\sum_{i=0}^{\min(q,3m)}\binom{3m}{i}2^i\right)
       (3/4)^{m/4}.                                                  \tag{1}
$$
For unspecified description lengths through $h$, replace $2^h$ by $2^{h+1}-1$. In particular, for sufficiently small absolute $c>0$, all but an exponentially small fraction of inputs of either verdict require $h>cm$ or $q>cm$. This holds simultaneously over partitions and bank choices even if every native instruction is its own phase. The hard inputs may depend on the three decoders and the solver. The same bound also holds when the three decoders supply private, unordered fragments of the execution and only their combined endpoint parity is required to agree.

## Formula and discrepancy

Pin $3m$ variables to the three banks. Use gate equivalences to compute the AND of the three bits at each coordinate and the XOR of all $m$ ANDs. Require the final parity bit to be one. Two-input AND and XOR gates have constant-size 3-CNF equivalences. Duplicate literals to pad short clauses. On each accepted pinning all auxiliary bits are unique. Use ordinary fixed-width binary literals and a regular consecutive layout. Separate variable pin signs by a large enough constant number of fixed tautologies on already occurring variables that each native formula word depends on at most one private bit. The required constant depends on the fixed word width coefficient. The formula length and all other bits of the layout are public. Count a private sign as accessed whenever its dependent formula word is read. The predicate is the three-party generalized inner product
$$
 g(x,y,z)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}x_i y_i z_i .
$$

For any three real functions $A(y,z), B(x,z), C(x,y)$ taking values in $[-1,1]$, one has $|\mathbb E ABC (-1)^g|\le\Delta$ for $\Delta=(3/4)^{m/4}$. The case of three indicators gives the usual cylinder-intersection bound. For completeness, apply Cauchy–Schwarz with $y,z$ fixed to eliminate the test missing $x$ and introduce independent $x,x'$. Apply it again with $x,x',z$ fixed to eliminate both copies of the test missing $y$ and introduce $y,y'$. The remaining test factors are independent of $z$ and bounded by one. The inner average over $z$ vanishes unless $(x_i\oplus x'_i)(y_i\oplus y'_i)=0$ at every coordinate. This event has probability $(3/4)^m$. Taking fourth roots proves the bound. This is the classical Babai–Nisan–Szegedy discrepancy argument. The earlier journal use charged a bounded number of prescribed views or communicated the route. ## Detecting an uncovered phase without being told its owner

Fix a candidate header and a global dictionary of at most $q$ bank-tagged sign locations with their values. Every party checks the dictionary entries for both of its visible banks. Each party computes its proposed list of internal configurations from its own view and the header. Use a canonical finite bit encoding including list boundaries, registers and memory. Zero words at unused addresses need only be encoded canonically. Computation and workspace of these tests are free. A public finite horizon can be chosen to dominate every native run on the finite family at this size. The counting argument need not know how to compute it efficiently.

Each party tests every consecutive pair on its own list, including the prescribed initial state and a final goal of halting with the stated verdict. The last test needs no putative final memory. Start from the supplied state and replay the deterministic machine until the goal for this phase is met. The party reads both visible banks and uses the dictionary for every listed sign of its omitted bank. Keep track of which visible banks have supplied any **unlisted** sign. If replay requests an unlisted sign of the omitted bank, stop before reading its value. Reject if both visible banks have already supplied unlisted signs. Otherwise this party makes no objection to this phase. Also reject on malformed state encodings, on halting at an incorrect goal that can no longer be reached, or on reaching the horizon without the requested goal during a fully visible replay. Test for reaching the goal before these terminal cases. This convention permits an empty phase. All objections are computable from two banks.

If the three lists agree and the tests pass, the dictionary is truthful on every bank. In the actual execution from an agreed phase start, consider the first unlisted access to each of the three banks before the next goal or the horizon. If all three occur, the party missing the **last** of the three first accesses can simulate all the way to its request. It has seen unlisted signs from both other banks and rejects. If at most two banks occur, a party missing an untouched bank can run all the way to the goal, a fatal halt, or the horizon. Passing therefore implies that the actual path from the agreed start reaches the agreed goal. Concatenation of these genuine paths from the initial state proves the verdict. Conversely, for a genuine proposed segmentation choose the dictionary as the union of the exceptional addresses and their true values. Every phase omits all unlisted signs in at least one bank. The completed replays are genuine, no third first access occurs, and all parties pass. Reaching a state earlier than the designated cut only shortens the required replay. No party needs to learn which bank is the designated one, or how many steps the chosen phase lasts. ## Checking agreement of arbitrarily long decoded lists

For fixed header and dictionary, let $L_b$ be the list computed by the party missing bank $b$ and $a_b$ its local pass indicator. The set where all local checks pass **and all lists agree** is monochromatic, by the replay proof. Agreement of long private outputs usually costs communication. In a discrepancy bound it can be imposed exactly with *zero loss*. Draw two independent fair sign tables $U,V$ on the finite range of list encodings. Then
$$
 {\bf1}_{L_1=L_2=L_3}
  =\mathbb E_{U,V}\big[ U(L_1)\big(U(L_2)V(L_2)\big)V(L_3)\big].
$$
Each random expectation tests one equality by orthogonality. In a mismatch, flipping the sign at exactly one unequal label pairs terms with opposite values. For fixed tables multiply each of the three local factors by $a_b$. The factors are bounded in absolute value by one and each still sees only two banks. The signed discrepancy proof therefore bounds their correlation by $\Delta$. Averaging over signs shows that the mass of this monochromatic set is at most $\Delta$. No collision probability, communication cost, or bound on the lists' length enters. The union over headers and dictionaries proves (1). Each sign has probability $(1\pm(3/4)^m)/2$, so conditioning on either sign changes only the constant for large $m$. For $q\le cm$ the dictionary count is at most $2^{3m H_2(c/3)+cm}$, where $H_2$ is binary entropy. Taking $c$ small enough gives the claimed exponential decay.

The same reasoning permits arbitrarily many prescribed equalities between pairs of local reports. Give each equality its own independent sign table and attach its two factors to the corresponding parties. Even a graph of overlapping equalities or many equalities at one party yields one local factor of absolute value at most one. For a reconciliation constraint $v_1+v_2+v_3=0$ in a finite abelian group, average a uniform group character at all three local values. Character orthogonality gives the exact indicator and the complex-valued Cauchy–Schwarz proof uses conjugation when copying factors. This extension applies when a passing, reconciled report genuinely proves the verdict. Unchecked additive shares of arbitrary RAM states alone give no such replay.

The argument allows the dictionary itself to be reconstructed as part of the common list by any two-bank decoder. If the three proposed dictionaries and boundary lists agree on a genuine input, all the tests work with these dictionaries and the single hash covers the entire tuple. No separate address charge then enters the bound. Thus a long dictionary whose positions and values are reproducible from *each* pair of banks can be used without paying for its length. Arbitrary hindsight exceptions use the explicit binomial accounting in (1).

## Decoding private, unordered fragments of the computation

There is a stronger certificate to which (1) applies with the same constants. Each party need only produce its *own* list of pairs of configurations. The three lists may have different lengths, endpoints and orders. They can be computed independently from the party's two banks, the header and the candidate dictionary. A pair claims that there is a finite native path from its first endpoint to its second. Include a distinguished sink $\dagger$ for the specified verdict. A path to a halting state with this verdict may continue by one formal edge to $\dagger$. Each party verifies its own pairs by genuine replay, rejecting on any unknown sign from its missing bank, on timeout or on a malformed path. A chosen finite public horizon only needs to cover honest segments.

For every party record a bit vector $d_b$ indexed by *all* canonical state identifiers and the sink. Toggle the bit for each endpoint of each of its verified pairs, regardless of orientation. The sole common consistency condition is
$$
  d_1\oplus d_2\oplus d_3=e_{\rm initial}\oplus e_{\dagger}.
$$
Individual boundary states can remain invisible to an entire party. Reversals of recorded orientations, disconnected extra cycles and duplicate fragments do not affect this reconciliation after the local forward paths have been validated.

To see soundness for the actual fixed input, mark all configurations whose future deterministic execution halts with the proposed verdict, and mark $\dagger$. Both endpoints of every genuine macroedge have equal marks. Thus the parity of marked coordinates in each $d_b$ is zero. The syndrome forces the initial state to have the same mark as $\dagger$, which proves the real verdict. Determinism matters here. In a nondeterministic graph with edges $s\to v$, $w\to v$, $w\to\dagger$, the three endpoint pairs have syndrome $e_s\oplus e_\dagger$ while $s$ cannot reach the sink.

For a uniform independent sign at each state identifier, let $\chi(d)$ be the product of the signs at the positions where $d$ is one. The expectation of $\chi(d_1)\chi(d_2)\chi(d_3)\chi(e_{\rm initial}\oplus e_\dagger)$ is exactly the syndrome indicator. Multiply each private factor by its own validation bit. Every factor remains bounded by one on two banks, so the monochromatic passing mass per header and dictionary is at most $\Delta$, as before. No sorting of private fragments or index-by-index agreement is needed.

Any genuine partition with independently recoverable lists of its assigned fragments supplies such a certificate. With the common lists in the original statement one can even construct the private fragments locally. A party attempts each phase from its decoded start up to the first visit of its next endpoint. If it can finish without an unlisted read from its own missing bank, it knows the full set of banks used outside the dictionary on that path. Emit the pair precisely when this party's omitted bank is the least unused bank. Whenever an honest phase has an untouched bank, exactly one party emits it. Consecutive pairs cancel their common endpoints. The last-first-access argument gives an alternate exact check for the common-list format.

The character average is a counting tool with unrestricted local computation. Computing the endpoint syndrome of a succinctly described list inside a fast SAT reduction would require an additional parity or enumeration argument. The probability estimate does not supply one. ## A fast solver that attains the resource scale

One globally correct solver recognizes the packed family, scans all public gadget slots, evaluates the parity from the pins on a successful match and otherwise invokes a complete SAT procedure. Rendering each expected fixed-template clause and checking it takes a constant number of word operations with sequential gadget numbering. Its time on the family is $O(m)$ with constants depending on the packed ISA. With one phase and a dictionary containing all $m$ signs of one bank, both the shared header and the list of internal cuts can be empty. In the other direction $3m$ shared bits suffice to supply all private data. Each decoder can then enumerate the true states after every single input read, so every phase has an untouched bank and $q=0$. These upper bounds explain why a linear elapsed-time contradiction cannot follow from (1) alone. For the unrestricted problem one still needs to control the *simultaneous recoverability* of genuine checkpoints and the union of exceptional addresses on genuinely slow computations. The discrepancy theorem is classical. Priority of its application with privately chosen boundaries, independent list reconstructions and globally deduplicated exceptions is unverified.
