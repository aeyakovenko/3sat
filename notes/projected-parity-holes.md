# Counting exceptions in a projected witness set for the actual sign

Put $M=2^j$. A fixed verifier takes a primary word $x$ of $v$ bits and secondary data $y$. Let $W_j$ consist of the distinct primary words having at least one accepting secondary witness. Membership uses one existential block and a deterministic branch of clock $T$, including all witness lengths. Its only external input is $j$ and fixed locally computable stage data. Suppose
$$
 |W_j|\equiv 1\pmod 2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b_j=c
$$
for a fixed color of the [actual priority program](priority-pinned-sat.md). On an odd stage we can certify this parity even for exponentially large $W_j$ by learning its local constraints and counting their exceptions. A corresponding nonuniform bound covers small arithmetic approximations on much longer primary words.

## Learning the local constraints

Fix an arity $d\ge2$. For every pattern on at most $d$ named coordinates, ask if some $x\in W_j$ extends it. This is one ordinary existential query, including the secondary witness. For each infeasible pattern add the clause forbidding it. The resulting width-$d$ CNF $H$ contains $W_j$ and is its smallest width-$d$ CNF superset. Indeed, if a clause of a width-$d$ CNF containing $W_j$ is violated, its falsifying pattern never extends to $W_j$. The construction uses $O_d((v+1)^d)$ tests, including the empty pattern. It also handles a library of arbitrary relations on at most $d$ coordinates, since each failed tuple is one forbidden pattern. All $v$ primary variables are counted, including unconstrained ones.

A hole is a model of $H$ outside $W_j$. To test if there are at least $r$ holes, existentially guess $r$ distinct, lexicographically sorted primary strings. Check ordering, then use **one universal block**. Its challenge names a row and a proposed secondary witness. Reject if the row fails $H$ or if the witness verifies membership of that row in $W_j$. Out-of-range row challenges pass. The acceptance condition is exactly the requested threshold. It charges $rv$ bits to the table and one branch clock $T$ to the inner test. It does not require $r$ separate time-$T$ computations in its matrix.

Take a constructible cap $B$ and assume deterministic $O(n^k)$ SAT for $k=1,2,3$. Test threshold $B+1$, abstaining if it passes. Otherwise binary search the lower thresholds through $B$ to obtain the exact hole count $h$. For $v=\operatorname{poly}(j)$, $T,B\le M^{O(1)}$, the aggregate time to learn $H$ and find $h$ is at most
$$
 \big(T+(B+1)v+\operatorname{poly}(j)\big)^{k^2}\operatorname{poly}(j).
$$
The two [ordinary bounded-run SAT eliminations](packed-band-transfer.md) charge the complete already-guessed table, all local computations and the $O(\log(B+2))$ threshold calls. A fixed SAT program suffices. If a hole instead has a positive short membership proof for $x\notin W_j$ of verification time $T_-$, one may guess and check $r$ of these proofs in a single existential query; the alternative hole-count cost is $((B+1)(v+T_-)+\operatorname{poly}(j))^k\operatorname{poly}(j)$ including checking $H$ (which has polynomial-in-$j$ size). Learning $H$ still uses the positive verifier.

Counting $H$ now gives $|W_j|=\#H-h$. The October preprint gives a deterministic exact width-$d$ CNF counter on $v$ variables and $m$ clauses with time $\operatorname{poly}_d(v+m)2^{O_d(v\log\log v/\sqrt{\log v})}$. For $m\le Dv$ with fixed $D$, its exponential factor is $2^{O_{d,D}(v/\sqrt{\log v})}$. Thus the last counting step takes $o(M)$ for $v\le a j\sqrt{\log j}/\log\log j$, or for $v\le a j\sqrt{\log j}$ in the fixed-density case, with a sufficiently small positive constant $a$. A conventional tape algorithm for the external counter runs on the target RAM with polynomial-in-$j$ addressing overhead in these ranges. The cited Lean development for that external result records an extra $\log\log v$ in its exponent. The sharper rates here take the manuscript theorem as input. [Exact counting paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf) · [Formal scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/lean/docs/378.md)

For fixed density it is enough that $H$ has an equivalent width-$d$ description with at most $Dv$ clauses. The learned list may be much longer. Guess the smaller description and universally compare its answer to $H$ on every input, then recover a valid description by prefix search. This query has length and branch time polynomial in $j$. A polynomial-time SAT solver makes the fixed alternation search polynomial in $j$. If the size test fails, abstain or use the general counting range.

In either range, take $T+(B+1)v\le M^{1/k^2}/(j+1)^C$ for sufficiently large fixed $C$. If there were infinitely many odd stages with at most $B$ holes, the procedure would signal color $c$ soundly in $o(M)$ on those stages, contradicting the native lower bound. Hence all sufficiently late odd stages have **more than $B$ distinct spurious primary words**, each matching the local views of true witnesses on every set of at most $d$ coordinates. If parity distinguishes both colors in both directions, use two detectors to obtain the statement on every sufficiently late stage. The single-existential alternative gives the same conclusion when its separate learning and counting times are $o(M)$. For example, at $B=\operatorname{poly}(j)$ it admits $T,T_-\le M^{1/k}/(j+1)^C$ with a sufficiently large fixed $C$.

## A long primary word with a low-degree approximation

There is a second exact count that needs no external counting theorem. A Boolean predicate represented over $\mathbb F_2$ by an arithmetic circuit of syntactic degree less than $v$ has an even number of true inputs on the $v$-cube. Each monomial misses a variable, so its sum over the cube vanishes modulo two. Constants, input bits, addition and multiplication suffice. A verifier can compute a syntactic degree bound from the gate list using maximum for addition and sum for multiplication, saturating at $v$. This covers any supplied representation meeting the degree bound, including a nonuniform one at a particular stage.

Let $S$ bound the bit length of an approximating circuit description. Suppose it computes $A$ with $|A^{-1}(1)\mathbin\triangle W_j|\le B$. There is a three-block proof of the true parity. Guess the circuit and the distinct sorted exception list. Check degree, list length and the claimed parity of that length. Write $g(x)=A(x)\oplus 1[x\text{ occurs in the list}]$. Universally choose $x$ and a proposed secondary witness $y_0$; existentially choose $y_1$. If $g(x)=1$ require that $y_1$ accepts. If $g(x)=0$ require that $y_0$ fails. This enforces $g=1_{W_j}$ at every primary word. The exception list has the same parity as $W_j$. With deterministic primary membership the last existential block is unnecessary. All the work in one branch is at most $(S+(B+1)v+T+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ for parameters at most $M^{O(1)}$, allowing direct scans of the list and a topologically indexed circuit evaluator.

In particular $S+(B+1)v+T\le M^{1/k^3}/(j+1)^C$ is impossible for any such approximation at sufficiently late **odd** stages under $O(n^k)$ SAT. For deterministic primary membership the threshold improves to $M^{1/k^2}/(j+1)^C$. For $k=1$ this permits $v$ and $B$ exponential in $j$ as long as their product fits. A parity-encoding verifier that admits sufficiently small low-degree approximations on infinitely many odd genuine stages would exclude the associated unrestricted SAT clock. This method requires a syntactic certificate for the degree bound and prices the actual exceptions.

This has a nonuniform arithmetic-circuit consequence even for **one-point repairs**. Every Boolean function $f$ has a unique multilinear polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$. For existence sum, over its true inputs $a$, the indicator polynomials $\prod_{i:a_i=1}x_i\prod_{i:a_i=0}(1+x_i)$. For uniqueness, split a multilinear polynomial as $p_0+x_v p_1$ and evaluate on $x_v=0,1$, inducting on the dimension. Each indicator has coefficient one on the full monomial $x_1\cdots x_v$. The degree is below $v$ exactly for an even-weight function. Therefore, on every odd stage, flipping the value of $1_{W_j}$ at **any** chosen point produces a canonical multilinear polynomial of degree below $v$. All these one-point approximations exist. The preceding lower bound forces large *descriptions* of their low-degree circuits.

The lower bound also applies to ordinary division-free arithmetic circuits computing each canonical repair as a **formal polynomial**, allowing a factor $O(v^2)$ in the number of gates. Given a $G$-gate circuit over $\mathbb F_2$, maintain at every gate its homogeneous components of degrees $0$ through $v-1$. Addition works componentwise and multiplication uses truncated convolution. Each output component of index $i$ has syntactic degree at most $i$, even if the original circuit had larger intermediate degrees. Since the final formal degree is below $v$, adding the retained components gives the exact output with $O((G+1)v^2)$ gates and syntactic degree below $v$. Binary gate descriptions introduce the usual logarithmic index factor. For $v=\operatorname{poly}(j)$ and $G\le M^{O(1)}$ the conversion costs only a polynomial-in-$j$ factor. Merely computing the same Boolean function on zero-one inputs need not give its formal polynomial by this truncation.

## Two dense supports realized by ordinary formulas

Here is an unconditional construction for the real sign that exposes the cost of membership. Take $v\ge3$ large enough to record the final winning rank (with a default code) and $j$, with zero padding. Read that $v$-bit string $s_j$ from the genuine deterministic computation, using an unambiguous fixed layout. Remove $s_j$ from the full primary cube at every stage. On color $c$ remove just this point; on the other color also remove its bitwise antipode. Since $v\ge3$, the hole count is respectively one or two and determines the color. Every proper coordinate projection of the remaining set is full: each nontrivial subcube contains an edge, while the two antipodes are nonadjacent. Both the positive set and its complement are nonempty on both colors.

The deterministic bounded-run formula of the [signed family](priority-pinned-sat.md) can expose the final award's rank with extra registers and its time with a counter, preserving its $O(M)$ simulation clock. Add fresh primary variables and deterministic comparators to the terminal record, and enforce the two exclusions just described, the second controlled by the terminal sign. From $j$ the resulting ordinary 3-CNF is locally accessible for polynomial-in-$j$ lengths $v$. Projecting away the auxiliary variables gives exactly the dense sets above regardless of routing multiplicities in the original tableau. The genuine membership algorithm replays through the terminal record. Proving the existence of a sufficiently cheaper **positive** projected membership verifier remains open. The single hole on the odd stages then suffices for the two-block detector.

The complement check cannot be obtained generically by a short sequence of positive intersection queries. Set a baseline cube with a known single missing point $s$. Query any subsets of the primary cube adaptively, asking whether they hit the remaining witness set. Along the baseline execution, protect one witness in each query answered YES. A query answered NO stays empty upon deleting more points. There are $2^v-1-v$ candidates at distance at least two from $s$. If the number of queries is smaller, some candidate $u$ is unprotected. Deleting $u$ gives the same entire transcript and changes the parity. Both possible support sets still have every proper coordinate projection full. For randomized procedures taking at most $q$ queries, a uniformly random additional hole from those candidates changes the transcript with probability at most $q/(2^v-1-v)$, by fixing the coins on the baseline and then coupling. The two promised families in this query argument allow the second hole anywhere at distance at least two. The explicit priority example uses the stricter antipode choice. The query lower bound concerns black-box access to the witness set, not inspection of a particular projecting formula.

## Long primary words with fast local positive verification

There is a second construction in which **positive verification takes $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time with a read-only, random-access primary input**. Start with the [closed Boolean circuit](signed-proof-time-and-storage.md) computing the genuine $b_j$, with $m=M^{O(1)}$ gates, locally indexed in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. Lower its fixed bounded-arity gates to AND, OR, NOT and constants with regular padding. Let $s$ be the unique complete gate table. A primary word consists of a tag $t$ and $m$ bits $y$. Normalize the supplied table to $z=y$ for $t=0$ and $z=\neg y$ for $t=1$. Accept the primary if any normalized gate equation is false, or if $t=1$ and the normalized output equals $c$. A nondeterministic checker names a bad gate and reads its proposed value and predecessor values; the sign alternative needs the tag and the output bit. It needs at most four primary bit probes including the tag. The only hole for $b_j=c$ is $(0,s)$. On the other color there is the additional hole $(1,\neg s)$, its precise antipode. The constant-one circuit has degree zero and differs from the resulting support on at most two primaries on **every** stage.

The holes even form a width-three CNF without auxiliary variables in this gate-table format. On the raw values use $\operatorname{maj}(a,b,t)$ for an AND gate and $\operatorname{maj}(a,b,\neg t)$ for an OR gate. Majority is self-dual, so either tag enforces the normalized gate equation. Enforce $o=\operatorname{maj}(a,b,h)$ by three clauses making any two true inputs imply $o$ and three making any two false inputs imply $\neg o$. Negation remains negation; encode raw constant zero as $t$ and one as $\neg t$. End with $\neg t\lor[y_{\mathrm{out}}=c]$. Induction on gates leaves the two complementary raw tables before the last clause, which removes exactly the second table on color $c$. Thus a locally indexed width-three DNF realizes the positive predicate by listing failed clauses. This construction uses the known circuit topology and never asks for $b_j$ during clause generation.

There is a shorter primary word for the same fast positive test. Normalize the native priority run through $R=C M$ fixed steps, with a fixed number of ordered memory micro-operations per step and a canonical idle continuation after halting. Store the chronological controller states, ports, read replies and writes, with all unused fields fixed to zero. Every enabled access to writable memory also records the word just before and after it. The CPU check enforces the transition with the real zero input, exact addresses and enables, the before-word as the read reply, and the prescribed after-word for reads or writes. For reads the word is preserved. There are $O(M)$ slots and $O(j)$ bits per slot. Separate logical memory banks form part of the address. A write's old value is explicitly determined by the memory check below even when the instruction ignores it.

Append two inverse arrays relating chronological positions to memory-sorted positions and vice versa, one position in each array for every slot including disabled slots. Require range, inverse consistency, and strictly increasing keys in the sorted order. Use the key (enabled/disabled class, bank, address, chronological position) with canonical zero address fields for disabled slots. Positions break all ties. At the start of each enabled address group require a zero before-word. Otherwise require its before-word to equal the preceding after-word in that group. Explicit input accesses are checked directly, with no writable entry for the read-only bank. All initial, final, padding, format and CPU conditions are part of the test. To find a violation, guess an array position or a row condition, then read a constant number of complete records through the inverse arrays in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. Starting states of adjacent chronological rows must agree with the preceding computed result. In chronological order, the memory equality for each address forces the actual prior word, so the CPU check forces the correct transition. This gives the unique chronological table. Its unique keyed order forces both arrays. Conversely the real run fills all fields as required. The resulting table has $O(Mj)$ bits. Normalizing its raw bits under the tag and using its terminal sign gives the same one-or-two antipodal holes. The short local check uses indirect reads of the provided primary; the width-three gate claim above applies to the gate-table format.

The time to *guess* a primary word of $O(Mj)$ bits in an ordinary bounded-run SAT reduction still includes writing it. A prescribed slot can be inspected cheaply once that word exists. A succinct name would change the bound. Suppose some circuit of description length $S$ accesses the genuine complete table at scale $j$. Guess the circuit, its sign and the appropriate bounded format, then let a universal challenge choose a row condition. Evaluate the circuit on the required bit positions (including indirectly obtained addresses) and check the condition. Check the terminal output also. A false color fails some challenge by uniqueness. A true table name requires no computation to find it in advance. These two blocks can be eliminated under $O(n^k)$ SAT in time $(S+\operatorname{poly}(j))^{k^2}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ for $S\le M^{O(1)}$. Consequently every sufficiently late genuine table needs circuit description length above $M^{1/k^2}/(j+1)^C$ for sufficiently large fixed $C$ under that SAT hypothesis. Two detectors cover the two colors. The positive construction has a very small exception *count* and cheap local tests; the remaining resource is access to the actual exception.

## A parsimonious ordinary 3-CNF near the native clock

For an explicit formula, deterministic sorting avoids unconstrained choices of routing switches. Guess only the chronological row fields just described, including enabled bits and before/after values, in a fixed $O(Mj)$-bit layout. A fixed Boolean verification circuit checks the CPU conditions, copies each enabled/address/time key and corresponding data into a Batcher sorting network, then checks adjacent output records and group starts. Pad the network to a power of two with fixed dummy records after the real records. Comparisons carry all fields; compare-exchanges are deterministic Boolean operations, with a fixed tie convention. Zero every unused controller, port, operand and padding field, including disabled records and the idle tail; every guessed enabled before/after bit participates in the CPU or sorted-memory checks. Declare exactly the actual input bits and implemented gate variables, with contiguous names and no unmentioned variables. All dummy entries are fixed. There are $O(M\log^2 M)$ compare-exchanges, each using $O(j)$ binary gates on records of $O(j)$ bits. Local instruction checks use $O(M\operatorname{poly}(j))$ gates. The circuit therefore has $O(M\operatorname{poly}(j))$ gates. A regular layout by layer, row, subgate and bit gives gate identifiers and their local wiring in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time (the bitonic network pairs wire indices by XOR with a stage-dependent power of two). This use of a sorting network is classical. [Batcher's sorting networks](https://doi.org/10.1145/1468075.1468121)

Give each internal gate its ordinary width-three equivalence clauses and assert that the verification succeeds. The accepting chronology is unique and each gate on that chronology has one legal value. Thus **every variable** has a unique value in the satisfiable formula. A final unit pin on the terminal verdict gives a pair of ordinary 3-CNFs of $M\operatorname{poly}(j)$ encoded bits with zero or one complete satisfying assignment; exactly one pin has a solution. Repetition pads short clauses to width three without introducing free variables. On either pin, an always-correct solver promised this family still needs $\Omega_D(M/\operatorname{poly}(j))$ time at every sufficiently late scale, even with the scale and pin supplied: simulate it on locally generated formula words, losing at most a fixed polynomial in $j$, then apply the native sign bound. The bounds on both the compiled length and generation overhead give an $\Omega_D(n_j/\operatorname{polylog}n_j)$ promise lower bound. The exact logarithmic loss is not optimized here.

Self-dualizing this verifier instead gives an ordinary near-native-size instance with exactly one or two **complete** satisfying assignments. Normalize the free chronological input bits by the tag as well as the computed gate bits. Require the raw verifier output to equal $\neg t$ (normalized output one) and add the same terminal tag/sign clause on the raw chronological verdict. The two valid unpinned full assignments are complements because exactly one normalized input passes and each Boolean gate is deterministic. The ordinary decision problem on this one-versus-two family always answers satisfiable. The two separate verdict pins above have opposite SAT decisions. These constructions do not yield any of the unrestricted time exclusions.
