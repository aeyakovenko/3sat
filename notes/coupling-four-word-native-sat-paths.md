# Coupling accepting paths at an eight-step RAM deadline

The 16-bit sign-plane SAT catalog has four physical input words. A correct native RAM needs at least eight steps on 5488 and can decide the entire catalog in nine. Every one input, with either verdict, can be recognized with four loads and four literal comparisons. An attempt to prove a nine-step worst case must therefore constrain which short paths coexist in one program. ## A criterion using the number of comparisons

Consider the 64 satisfiable files $S=\{(p,q,15-p,15-q):4\le p,q\le11\}$ with equal weights. All four words on each of these files are individually pivotal. A correct accepting path therefore requires at least four fresh original loads. In an eight-step run it has at most four comparisons.

For any deterministic fixed program the comparison-bit strings at its reachable terminals form a prefix code. Between comparisons the next instruction pointer is determined by the previous control path. This uses fixed labels, binary branches and a terminal verdict fixed by its label. Noncomparison instructions may compute different words on different inputs. They do not select different instruction pointers. If every sound accepting path with $c$ comparisons in at most eight instructions contained at most $64/2^c$ elements of $S$, the accepting leaves would cover strictly less than 64. Indeed summing their support bounds gives at most 64 times their Kraft sum. Including one reachable rejecting leaf gives a total Kraft sum at most one, with a strictly positive contribution from rejection. The 64 positive files must all be accepted, a contradiction. This criterion is sufficient. Checking each positive separately cannot establish it.

## Four and three comparisons

For four comparisons the four remaining instructions are fresh loads on any accepted file of $S$. All words on $S$ are high (at least four). The input addresses are $0,1,2,3$. An original data word cannot address a fresh input cell on these files. Thus all reads on this one path must be direct literal reads. Write the low band as $4,5,6,7$ and the high band as $8,9,10,11$. For a file in $S$, fix which member of each complementary pair belongs to the low band. This gives four orientations.

Suppose two files of the same orientation satisfy all the raw comparisons on an accepting path. Form two hybrids by exchanging the high-band coordinates between these files. Every comparison persists. Between two variable columns in different bands their order, signed or unsigned, and their inequality are fixed. Two columns from the same band come from the same file. A comparison with a literal depends on a single column. If the original files differ, choose a bit where one of their positive-mask words differs. Its negative mask is its bitwise complement. In one of the hybrids the two mate bits are both one. That hybrid is unsatisfiable and follows the accepting path. Consequently at most one accepted member per orientation is possible, giving the required bound $4$.

For three comparisons, four fresh loads leave one surplus instruction. With one extra load and no ALU or store, every register equals a fixed column or literal throughout the set of all-high files. A raw input used as an address reads zero. With one store and exactly four loads, every load is still fresh on an accepted member of $S$. Inductively its address must be a literal low cell; a previous store with a high raw address misses it on all-high files and a previous store to that fixed cell would destroy freshness. These cases are covered by the same orientation argument. Moves and fixed jumps can be omitted. The remaining case has four loads, one native ALU operation and three comparisons.

For this last case move the comparisons to the end of the one path. With read-only data on this path, move all its direct loads to the start. If there is an indirect fresh load on an accepted member of $S$, its address must be the ALU result. The four loads are distinct and fresh, so just one direct input coordinate is omitted and the ALU result must equal its index on every accepted member of $S$. On negative files an indirect load reads the actual word, including zero at a scratch address. Enumerate all 20 opcodes with inputs from available words and any of the 16 literal values. For each operation, enumerate three guards with either outcome and equality, signed or unsigned order in either direction. Equal register tables on the finite input suite may share one representative. A guard triple must accept nine or more positive lanes and zero negative lanes to disprove the required cap on that suite.

The finite test uses all 64 positives and 3,328 distinct negative examples, including 00FF, every single-bit conflict obtained by modifying a complementary positive, and every overlapping replacement of one complementary pair with the other pair unchanged. The audit exhausts 5,138 distinct register tables across the five addressing layouts and finds no such triple. This gives the bound $8$. It is attained by loading all four words and testing $P\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}M=15$, $Q=4$, $N=11$. This is a sound path for the full 65,536-file catalog. ## A two-comparison subcase

Suppose the survival predicate factors as $g(X)h(\bar X)$ for a partition of the four input coordinates. If this partition splits a complementary mate pair $(P,M)$ or $(Q,N)$, the value of that pair must be identical on all accepted files of $S$. Otherwise exchanging the two groups of coordinates between two files puts a conflict in one hybrid, while both factors still pass. At most eight positives can survive if a pair is split, and at most one if both are split. This part permits arbitrary predicates in the two factors.

The only nontrivial unsplit partition groups $(P,M)$ separately from $(Q,N)$. Suppose each factor is computed by at most one native arithmetic operation and one comparison, with direct access to its two input words and unrestricted literals. If the path accepts even one member of $S$, both local predicates are nonempty. Each must reject all overlapping two-word inputs: otherwise that overlap can be paired with an accepted local input from the other factor. At least one factor must also reject $(0,15)$ because 00FF is unsatisfiable. The 256-input pair census shows that every allowed factor rejecting all overlaps and $(0,15)$ rejects all eight high complementary pairs as well. The enumeration uses 607 distinct four-bit ALU tables over the two data words and literals. For each it tests comparison to both input words and every literal in both directions and with either branch outcome. Raw comparisons are included through degenerate ALU operations. Thus this factored two-guard strategy cannot accept any member of $S$.

The criterion for a nine-step bound still requires all one-comparison paths to cover at most 32 positives and all two-comparison paths to cover at most 16. Two guards may have overlapping dependencies or use indirect loads and writable memory. The factor argument does not establish those cases. The results here concern a single native word size and 16 encoded input bits.
