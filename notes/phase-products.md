# Can approximate jump circuits speed up a clocked computation?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** Consider a public k-bit clock and a register with a fixed finite alphabet. An explicit Boolean circuit H takes a phase as input and selects a register update. The phase advances modulo P=2ᵏ. A full period applies H(0), …, H(P−1) in order. This separates the cost of describing a schedule from the cost of composing it. The register can stay constant in size even when the composition problem is PSPACE-complete. A positive result below allows incorrect local composition identities, provided the number of incorrect nodes is small enough to enumerate.

## Repairing sparse errors in the composition tree

Treat an update as its action on every possible register value. These finite tables compose associatively. At level i the aligned blocks have length 2ⁱ and indices 0≤j<2^(k−i). Guess circuits Aᵢ(j) for the products. Count a leaf as defective when A₀(j) differs from H(j); count an internal node when Aᵢ(j) differs from applying Aᵢ₋₁(2j) followed by Aᵢ₋₁(2j+1). These tests compare the claimed values, so a node whose true product is wrong may nevertheless pass its local test. Use a fixed valid encoding of the finite tables, or check validity at every output being compared.

Let s bound the size of H plus the total size of all candidate circuits, counting inputs and gates. The defect predicate on a (level,index) pair is a Boolean circuit of size Õ(s+k²), including range checks. One can instantiate all k+1 guarded level comparisons, using constantly many copies of each Aᵢ; the polynomial in k pays for the variable index fields. Its word-RAM construction and explicit Tseitin CNF bit encoding have the same bound up to logarithms. Choose lexicographic order on valid node codes; each has O(k+1) bits.

Given a deterministic O(mᵈ) ordinary SAT algorithm on m encoded bits, find the first defect strictly above a stored lower bound. Ask first whether one exists; if it does, recover its binary representation by prefix queries imposing the same lower bound. Repeat, stopping at the first empty suffix or upon finding E+1 defects if E is the allowed budget. Every query uses just one lower bound and one prefix. There are O((E+1)(k+1)) queries of size Õ(s+k²+1), regardless of the number of earlier answers.

With at most E defects, mark every defect and every ancestor of a defect. Outside this set the entire subtree is locally consistent, hence A gives its exact product by induction. Recompute values of marked nodes bottom up, querying A on unmarked children and H at marked leaves. At most (k+1)E nodes need repair. One can sort the marked (level,index) keys and join with the child keys per level; all classical list-processing work is bounded by O((E+1)poly(k+1)) plus the corresponding circuit evaluations and logarithmic factors. If E=0 the root claim is already exact. This proof works for noncommuting updates.

When the summary and defect list can both be guessed, **batching the checks saves one power of the query size per defect**. Guess a sorted list of at most E distinct valid node codes. Extra names are harmless because recomputing a clean node from exact children preserves its true product. Put S=s+k²+1, let w=O(k+1) be the node-address length, and take g=max(1,⌊S/w⌋). Partition the guessed list into consecutive groups of g entries with a possible short last group. For each group form a disjoint address interval; use the preceding group's last entry as its exclusive lower endpoint and this group's last entry as its inclusive upper endpoint, extending the first and last intervals to cover both ends of the domain. With an empty list use one interval covering the domain.

For each interval make one SAT query for a genuine defect inside it whose code differs from every listed entry in its group. One positive answer rejects this guess. Every query has size Õ(S+gw)=Õ(S) and their number is O(1+Ew/S). The negative answers certify that every defective node was listed. Mark the ancestors of the list and apply the exact repair above. For d≥2, evaluating circuits on O((E+1)(k+1)) nodes and the address-list processing fit within the following bound up to logarithmic factors and polynomials in k. Both claimed answer bits now have nondeterministic recognizers of time

    Õ(poly(k+1) · (S^d + E S^(d−1))),

plus construction of the true schedule. Soundness holds on every guess. Completeness requires just one summary family with the specified size and defect cap. For example when k is logarithmic in an original clock T, S≤T^β and E≤T^γ, cubic SAT gives a polynomial saving over T whenever 3β<1 and γ+2β<1, with fixed margins and affordable uniform setup. If k can be constant, the same bounds use k+1. A tape implementation of the nondeterministic recognizer can guess and check a consistent sorted RAM history with polylogarithmic overhead where that simulation is needed. To contradict a hierarchy theorem one still needs a source genuinely separated from the resulting nondeterministic clock.

Nonuniformly, store the corrected product for every marked node in its level's circuit. A list of at most (k+1)E exceptional addresses of O(k+1) bits, with constant-size corrected values, adds O((k+1)²E) gates in total to the Aᵢ. Test for each address and use the original value elsewhere. This converts a polynomial-size family with polynomially many local defects into a polynomial-size *exact* family. For fine-grained estimates explicit repair and circuit patching have different costs: guessing and batching pays roughly E S^(d−1) to verify completeness of the defect list, while submitting the entire larger exact circuit to SAT puts its size inside the exponent. ## One bad phase can corrupt every endpoint

Let the register be a bit. Compare the reversible maps

    F(a,b) = (a+1 mod P, b),
    G(a,b) = (a+1 mod P, b XOR [a=0]).

Use the true step F and supply all jump circuits of G. They obey every squaring identity exactly. Their base disagrees with the true step on 1/P of all states. For t≤P a uniform initial phase encounters zero in its first t steps with probability exactly t/P; on this event the two final register bits disagree. At t=P every endpoint is wrong. The false jumps themselves are small circuits: a modular interval-membership test detects the single toggle. The factor t in the elementary union bound for transported local discrepancies is therefore sharp, even for uniform starting states, permutations and a one-bit observation. A forward-evaluation sampling procedure making q distinct phase probes finds a uniformly hidden toggle with probability at most q/P. SAT can certify that an explicitly described toggle exists, which is the resource the repair algorithm uses.

## Which schedules have small summaries?

For a bit register with only identity, set-zero and set-one instructions, find the greatest time in the interval which has a nonidentity instruction. A SAT query tests whether the prefix-constrained time of such a reset exists, so O(k+1) adaptive queries identify the last reset or prove there was none. The update at that time, or the initial bit when there was no reset, determines the result. This permits efficient composition on this restricted schedule.

Allow identity and flip instead. Given any Boolean circuit h(t), let H(t) flip precisely at its accepting inputs. The result on initial zero after a period is the parity of the number of satisfying inputs. A short circuit B for cumulative parity could certify this with one local identity. Require B(0)=0 and, over the cyclic phase space, use the proposed answer b in

    B(t+1 mod P) = B(t) XOR h(t) XOR ([t=P−1] AND b).

XOR over t proves b must be the true parity, and the true prefix-parity function supplies a solution. If SAT is in P, the assertion that **every** circuit has such a B of a single polynomial size bound is equivalent to ⊕P=P. In the forward direction guess B and check that the violation circuit is unsatisfiable; P=NP then decides existence. In the reverse direction the prefix parity for an explicit circuit and threshold is in ⊕P, so a polynomial-time algorithm unrolls to small circuits with h hardwired. This is a precise obstruction to replacing the original trace by an assumed easy-to-verify one-bit parity accumulator. It gives no unconditional direct lower bound on SAT.

With five register values, even *permutations* selected by H suffice for PSPACE-completeness of the endpoint problem. This is an instance of the established characterization by polynomial-time bit reductions to word problems of fixed finite nonsolvable groups. The construction uses the same commutator underlying Barrington's bounded-width theorem. [Hertrampf, Lautemann, Schwentick, Vollmer and Wagner](https://doi.org/10.1109/SCT.1993.336526) · [Barrington](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~barring/publications/bwbp.pdf) · [Bartholdi et al., Lemma 25](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2020.29)

To check the input-description cost, expand a quantified Boolean formula down its assignment tree, replacing quantifiers by binary OR or AND. Include its propositional matrix below, rewriting OR by AND and NOT with constant depth overhead. The depth D is polynomial in the description length. A path to a leaf and its truth value can be computed in polynomial time from its choices, since each quantifier records one assigned bit. Given a requested 5-cycle π, the word for a subformula has product π if true and the identity otherwise. For an AND gate choose 5-cycles α,β whose commutator is π, and concatenate the two child words and their inverses in commutator order. Such cycles exist for one 5-cycle by Barrington's calculation and for every 5-cycle by conjugation in S₅. A NOT gate uses its child's inverse word and a fixed π. Leaves give one constant permutation. Pad with identity instructions to blocks of exactly 4ᵈ positions at available depth d, using one child block for the inverse and one padded constant block for π at a NOT. Any requested instruction is obtained by following its base-four address down at most D levels, reversing the address and inverting letters where required. Thus a polynomial-size circuit constructed uniformly from the formula describes the whole word, even though its length is 4ᴰ. Choose the register action consistently with word multiplication and take an initial point moved by π. The endpoint test is the original quantified formula. Conversely a polynomial-space machine iterates the schedule with a binary counter.

Consequently, under SAT∈P, a common polynomial bound for exact aligned-product summary circuits for **all** five-state permutation schedules is equivalent to P=PSPACE. The same holds if polynomially many local defects are allowed, by patching. For one direction the guessed summary family with polynomially many universally checkable identities yields a polynomial-hierarchy endpoint test, hence a polynomial-time test under the hypothesis. For the converse, the indexed aligned-block product is computable in polynomial space from its explicit schedule and index, so P=PSPACE supplies the claimed small circuits by hardwiring the schedule and level. This equivalence concerns polynomial bounds in the *encoded schedule size*. It supplies neither a separation of these classes nor a denial of sublinear-in-the-period certificates on a particular source.

For the unrestricted SAT question, the unresolved construction is a **uniformly cheap representation of the designated hierarchy computation** by schedules whose summary size and number of defects meet the displayed repair bound. Constant register width, invertibility and high average one-step agreement each leave that construction open.
