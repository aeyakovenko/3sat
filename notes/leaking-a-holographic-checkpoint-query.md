# A midpoint fingerprint revealed before the suffix commits

Holographic interactive proofs can reduce an output assertion about a supplied circuit input to a claimed value of its multilinear extension at a random point. The verifier may generate that point from its coins independently of the prover's messages. This invites an optimization for verifying a RAM execution in two halves. Ask the prefix to certify the chosen evaluation of its terminal bank at the same time as the suffix certifies its own answer. A single prover helping the prefix might then learn the point before fixing the suffix's alleged starting bank. The timing alone permits a false output in this construction. [Berger, Goyal, Hong and Kalai, Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.19075v1#S3.SS3.SSS1) · [Alternating cost of statistical proofs](statistical-proof-alternation-cost.md)

Use a zero-initialized bank of \(b\) native words containing elements of a finite field \(F\) that fit the word width. The prefix can leave the bank untouched. The suffix tests each word for a nonzero value and takes the OR. The real answer is zero, on a fixed input of constant information content. In the proposed verification rule the prefix answers \(k\) field-linear functionals of its genuine bank. The suffix may use any proposed bank agreeing with those answers and is otherwise checked soundly for that proposed initial bank. Multilinear-extension evaluations are field-linear functionals of the table values, including when several Boolean variables are encoded per point.

If all \(k<b\) probes are revealed first, choose a nonzero vector in their common kernel. The alleged bank passes every comparison and its genuinely executed suffix returns one. The calculation works for every list of evaluations, including lists containing actual cell addresses. If up to \(h\) fresh random bits remain hidden after the alleged bank is chosen, and for each possible completion at most \(k\) fixed functionals are tested, the joint rank is at most \(k2^h\). The same bank passes all continuations whenever \(k2^h<b\). Suffix communication used to choose later functionals would be a different test; here the list is fixed by the coins and the earlier public information. For Boolean-only bank entries and probes over \(\mathbb F_{2^w}\), a sufficient inequality is \(kw2^h<b\).

This hidden-randomness order is attainable for a supplied bank. Interpret the \(b\) field words as coefficients of a polynomial of degree below \(b\). One secret uniformly random challenge in a field of size between \(2b\) and \(O(b)\), chosen independently of the already fixed false coefficients, detects a nonzero difference with probability above one half. Sizes that are powers of two suffice. But if a *single prior message* depending only on an arbitrary genuine bank must let the verifier recover its correct response to every hidden challenge, that message distinguishes all \(|F|^b\) banks. The evaluations span rank \(b\); with fixed-width messages, at least \(b\log_2|F|\) bits are needed. This is an exact reconstruction requirement with no later access to the bank.

The counterexample rules out revealing linear midpoint queries early as a generic composition rule. Comparing the halves sequentially leaves a challenge unknown until the suffix commits, but pays for the two proofs in series. A verifier exploiting the actual prefix code can immediately recognize its all-zero bank. On locally compiled hierarchy inputs of original length \(u\), the reachable midpoint banks have at most \(2^u\) possibilities at fixed length, so counting arbitrary banks also cannot lower-bound the time to generate their values from the short input.
