# Individual degree two, unique traces, and SAT

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** An explicit representative of the exact-cycle predicate has polynomial circuit size and individual degree two over every field. The corresponding existence claim for fully encoded SAT would have a Boolean parallel-complexity consequence. A deterministic SAT computation supplies a small degree-two verifier with auxiliary bits. Summing out those bits can already be hard for a linear-time decision problem.

## A determinant gives the degree-two representative

Let cₘ indicate that the input matrix itself consists of one directed Hamiltonian cycle, for m≥2. Fix a root r. Set

    Yᵢⱼ = Xᵢⱼ ∏_{l≠j}(1−Xᵢₗ),         Rᵣ = Σⱼ Yᵣⱼ,
    Cⱼ = Σᵢ Xᵢⱼ ∏_{k≠i}(1−Xₖⱼ).

On a Boolean row, Yᵢⱼ selects its sole one, if any. Cⱼ tests for exactly one entry in column j. Let Tᵣ(Y) be the sum of the weights of spanning trees oriented towards r, with each weight the product of its tree arcs. It is the determinant of the root-deleted out-Laplacian, with diagonal entries Σ_{j≠i}Yᵢⱼ and off-diagonal entries −Yᵢⱼ. This polynomial has a division-free circuit of polynomial size over any field. [Chaiken, directed matrix-tree theorem](https://doi.org/10.1137/0603033) · [Berkowitz, determinant circuits](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(84)90018-8)

Consider G=Rᵣ Tᵣ(Y) ∏ⱼ Cⱼ. Each tree uses one outgoing arc from every nonroot row and none from the root. Consequently Rᵣ Tᵣ(Y) is multilinear in the original X entries. The product of the Cⱼ is also multilinear because their column sets are disjoint. Their product has individual degree at most two.

If a nonroot row fails its test, every outgoing weight in that row is zero, hence every tree weight vanishes. Rᵣ and the column factors test all remaining requirements for a permutation matrix. On a permutation matrix every nonroot vertex has a forced outgoing arc. These arcs form a rooted spanning tree exactly when all vertices belong to the root's permutation cycle. In that case there is exactly one such tree, so G equals cₘ even in positive characteristic. An integer check compared the determinant with the successor-orbit test on every Boolean matrix of orders two and three, on every permutation matrix of orders four and five, and on 150 sampled matrices of each of those last two orders.

The unique representative of individual degree one already has a hard formal HCₘ homogeneous component. Thus allowing exponent two suffices for this particular Boolean function. Functional circuit complexity with bounded individual degree has earlier been studied in depth-restricted circuit models. [Canonical-extension proof](sat-extension.md) · [Forbes, Kumar and Saptharishi](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2016.33)

## What degree two for ordinary SAT would imply

Fix the binary CNF encoding with a separate sign bit per literal occurrence and a fixed polynomial-time malformed-input convention. Suppose the length-N SAT truth function has a polynomial-size arithmetic representative of individual degree at most two over a fixed finite field. Every nonuniform polynomial-size nondeterministic Boolean circuit family then has such representatives. For a size-s verifier on q input bits and w guess bits, encode gate consistency and a true output as a fixed CNF. Pin each of the q primary inputs to its own unit clause, leaving its sign as the only live encoding bit. Every guess bit stays existential. The length is O((s+q+w+1) log(s+q+w+2)) with conventional binary variable names. Substitution of constants and a distinct xᵢ or 1−xᵢ for each live sign preserves the individual-degree limit. Width-three padding is described in the earlier encoding argument. [Sign-bit projection](sat-extension.md)

In particular NP/poly would have polynomial-size Boolean circuits of depth O(log² n). Indeed each proposed arithmetic output has total degree at most 2N. The Valiant–Skyum–Berkowitz–Rackoff parallelization theorem gives a polynomial-size depth-O(log² N) arithmetic circuit even if intermediate gates of the original circuit had large degree. Each operation of the fixed finite field has a constant-size Boolean implementation, and the input projection has constant depth. The conclusion is nonuniform. Hrubeš discussed the corresponding parallel-complexity consequence for multilinear extensions. [Valiant et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/0212043) · [Hrubeš, introduction](https://users.math.cas.cz/~hrubes/PDFs/Mult.pdf)

A quadratic or cubic SAT algorithm would give P=NP and hence P/poly=NP/poly. Under either clock, existence of small individual-degree-two representatives for SAT is therefore equivalent to their existence for every P/poly predicate family. One direction uses the projection. For the converse SAT itself is then in P/poly. Direct Boolean arithmetization gives small arithmetic circuits with uncontrolled degrees.

## Can the unique computation of a solver remove the auxiliary bits?

For any deterministic fan-in-two Boolean circuit C there is a polynomial R(X,B,Z)=A·D with the following properties. X contains the primary input bits, B is the claimed output, and Z contains one auxiliary bit for each gate port. A and D are each multilinear and have circuits of size linear in the circuit description. R is multilinear in (X,B) and has individual degree at most two in Z. For every Boolean X there is exactly one joint assignment (B,Z) making R equal to one. Its B is C(X). All other Boolean joint assignments give zero.

To construct A, multiply the multilinear truth-table indicators of the gate relations, on disjoint sets of gate ports. For D, take one equality indicator per wire signal connecting all its ports, including the primary bit or claimed output when applicable. On distinct bits u₁,…,uⱼ, the equality indicator is ∏ᵢuᵢ+∏ᵢ(1−uᵢ). The different equality factors have disjoint variable sets. An unused input needs no constraint. Evaluating the Boolean circuit in forward order proves existence and uniqueness, even when a gate feeds both ports of its successor. This is the usual normalization by equality constraints in a factor graph. [Forney and Vontobel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0316)

The formal polynomial Σ_{z∈{0,1}^t} R(X,1,z) is the canonical multilinear extension of C. It is multilinear in X by construction and agrees at every Boolean input by uniqueness. Taking C to be a polynomial-size circuit for cₘ proves that no polynomial-size exact elimination bound can hold for all such deterministic traces. The obstruction persists when the entire computation has a unique trace on both yes and no inputs and all trace bits participate in just two multilinear factors. The published HC lower bound and homogeneous-component extraction give the contradiction. A hypothetical fast SAT solver also has a polynomial-size Boolean simulation on logarithmic-word RAMs and hence has this trace representation. [Exact-cycle component](sat-extension.md) · [Formal HC lower bound](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00954v1)

There is an exact positive elimination criterion. If the formal verifier polynomial is multilinear in all t summed variables, its Boolean sum costs polynomial overhead over any field. In characteristic different from two the sum equals 2ᵗ times its value with all summed variables set to 1/2. In characteristic two it is the coefficient of uᵗ after replacing every summed variable by u, accessible by truncated coefficient arithmetic. If only b of the t variables violate multilinearity, enumeration of those b bits yields a size bound O(2ᵇ(s+1)(t+1)²) for a size-s verifier. Determinism supplies uniqueness on the cube. Port equalities and gate tests both depending on the same witness bit account for the extra power in the formal polynomial. [Mahajan, Saurabh and Tavenas, Theorem 1](https://www.imsc.res.in/~meena/papers/mVNP.pdf)

## The hypothetical SAT algorithm can recover circuits if they exist

Over the two-element field a constructive issue can be resolved. Assume SAT∈P, and fix a polynomial bound s=Nᵇ large enough to cover representatives at every sufficiently large length. If the representatives of individual degree at most two exist, a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm on unary input 1ᴺ can construct one. Finitely many smaller lengths may be hardwired.

For a candidate of size ≤s, an incorrect Boolean value has a polynomially checkable witness using the assumed SAT decider. An excessive individual degree also has a short witness. Work in an extension E of order 2^{s+3}. Fix three distinct elements there. For a candidate circuit computing G and an index i, let Jᵢ interpolate the three substitutions for Xᵢ by a quadratic in Xᵢ, with all other variables formal. Then G−Jᵢ is nonzero exactly when the individual degree exceeds two. Its total degree is at most 2ˢ+2, below |E|, so a nonzero difference has a nonzero evaluation somewhere in Eᴺ. A certificate gives i, an irreducible degree-(s+3) modulus defining E, and that evaluation point. Check irreducibility, perform the four circuit evaluations and use the three Lagrange coefficients. All witness and arithmetic costs are polynomial in s+N. [Rabin, finite-field irreducibility test](https://doi.org/10.1137/0209024)

Existence of a good completion of a circuit-description prefix is thus a Σ₂ᴾ predicate on the unary length and the prefix. SAT∈P collapses this class to P. Bitwise search in a fixed padded circuit-description format constructs the representative. This gives polynomial-time preprocessing if the algebraic existence premise holds. Its exponent need not match the hypothesized SAT clock.

The remaining implication would have to derive the existence premise, or use a different invariant of the computation. Reading each physical input position only once does not supply degree control. A logarithmic-word RAM can copy its input into workspace in O(N) time and redirect all subsequent input accesses to that copy. This preserves both proposed SAT time bounds and permits arbitrary reuse of the copied data. The determinant construction, trace obstruction and synthesis argument leave the unrestricted SAT time bound unresolved. Priority for these combinations has not been established.
