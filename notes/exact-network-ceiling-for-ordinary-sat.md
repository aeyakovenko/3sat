# A quadratic exact tensor-network upper bound for ordinary CNF-SAT

**Theorem.** For every fixed finite field \(F\) and every input length \(N\) in bits, ASCII DIMACS CNF-SAT has a nonuniform binary-wire tensor network over \(F\) of volume \(O_F(N^2)\), even with unbounded clause width. Its scalar contraction is exactly zero or one on **every** input. Internal indices range over two values, local tables have arity at most three, and explicit equality tensors pay for copying. The construction allows arbitrarily many models, arbitrary decimal identifier lengths, unused declared variables, repeated clauses and reordered literals. A syntax check returns zero on malformed files. The constants and the network can depend on \(F\). We first prove the bound for 3-SAT, including the usual fixed-width binary clause lists with identifier width charged in the input length. For unbounded-width ASCII CNF we give a parsimonious reduction to an \(O(N)\)-bit proper 3-CNF.

The probabilistic ingredient has substantial prior art. Naik, Regan and Sivakumar give a quasilinear-time constant-success reduction to parity, using monomial evaluation over a binary extension field and an inner Hadamard code. Dell, Kabanets, van Melkebeek and Watanabe describe OR amplification and fixing randomness for all inputs of one length. Here the prime-field version, caching input-dependent routing, and linear per-trial accounting give the explicit quadratic volume. This is an upper bound. The infimum of exponents for ordinary SAT networks remains undetermined. The journal's lower bound \(\Omega(N(\log N)^\sigma)\) comes from its reduction to the November 24 preprint's list theorem. Neither bound supplies a superlinear RAM-time lower bound. [Naik, Regan and Sivakumar, §3 and Theorem 3.1](https://cse.buffalo.edu/~regan/papers/pdf/NRS95.pdf) · [Dell et al., §1.3](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2011/151/revision/1/download/) · [List theorem and DIMACS transfer](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md)

## Shared preprocessing and the number of distinct clauses

Use the actual file length as the storage parameter. Check the header, signed decimal literal syntax, variable range, clause terminators, at most three literals per clause, and the number of body clauses against the declared count **before deduplication**. Whitespace and comment lines are discarded as lexical tokens. Leading zero digits of decimal names can be removed. The unsigned zero terminator and the admissibility of particular spellings of zero can be set according to the chosen syntax. A proper-clause convention can additionally reject repeated names or require exactly three literals. Empty clauses and an empty conjunction have their usual meanings.

Sort the literals within each clause, retaining multiplicities, and delete duplicate clause tuples. Keep tautological clauses, so every originally occurring name is represented. If \(m\) distinct clauses remain, then

\[
          m\log(m+2)=O(N).
\]

Choose one physical representative for each retained tuple. Spell each representative by concatenating its original signed literal tokens with a one-byte separator and a terminal byte. These strings are distinct, since equal spellings give equal clause tuples. Their total length is \(O(N)\), because their literal tokens occupy disjoint positions and their number is bounded by the number of original delimiters. Over a fixed alphabet, at least half of \(m\) distinct strings have length \(\Omega(\log m)\) for sufficiently large \(m\). Thus both the retained clause capacity and, up to a constant, the retained occurrence capacity can be set in advance to \(O(1+N/\log(N+2))\). [Related distinct-prefix count](sat-time-from-model-counts-of-clause-prefixes.md)

Here are explicit preprocessing bounds for long or noncanonical names. A bounded-fanin Boolean circuit of \(O(N\log^4(N+2))\) gates suffices. Parallel prefix scans mark token boundaries, line comments and signs, and propagate clause numbers, digit-run lengths and first nonzero-digit positions. The token and line-level syntax conditions between numeric fields are finite-state checks. Prefix and suffix scans on \(O(\log N)\)-bit positions and counters fit in this budget. Compute name ranks by doubling. Starting at every digit position, rank its first digit, using zero as a special end symbol ordered before any real digit. At level \(a+1\), use the pair of level-\(a\) ranks beginning at positions \(i\) and \(i+2^a\), substituting the all-end rank for the second if the digit run ended before that offset. Nondigits have the all-end rank. A sort of these \(O(\log N)\)-bit pairs, adjacent comparisons, a prefix sum and restoration of the old positions assign lexicographic ranks of the \(2^{a+1}\)-symbol prefixes padded with the end symbol. Initially digit values use ranks 1 through 10. Maintain zero for the all-end rank throughout. After \(O(\log N)\) levels every digit suffix is ranked. Looking up the rank at the first nonzero digit of each token is one batched sort-and-broadcast of position-keyed records. For all-zero tokens use a single canonical numeric zero. Normalized length followed by digit rank gives numeric order, so even a header count much wider than a machine word can be compared with the occurring names. For a declared clause count, saturating a decimal prefix at \(N+1\) or comparing its normalized digits with the actual count suffices. No table is indexed by the numerical identifier.

Gather the bounded-width clause tuples by token position and clause number. Canonicalize, sort, mark first occurrences and compact marked rows by sorting. The resulting array has a predetermined padded capacity. The shared circuit also computes the swap decisions of a sorting network routing the remaining literal occurrences by name, and the equality flags of consecutive sorted names. A second sort stably compacts the first records of the name groups into rank order. Break ties by original position when needed. All its swap decisions and all dummy, sign and group flags depend only on the original input. Each preprocessing sort of \(O(N)\) records can use \(O(N\log^2 N)\) comparator positions with \(O(\log N)\) gates each. The final two shared payload routes use AKS with \(O(L\log(L+2))\) comparator positions, where \(L=O(1+N/\log(N+2))\) is the padded occurrence capacity. [Ajtai, Komlós and Szemerédi](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808726)

## One prime-field scalar in linear volume

Let \(p=\operatorname{char}(F)\), and do all scalar arithmetic in its prime subfield. Draw a mathematical extension \(K=\mathbb F_Q\), where \(Q\) is the least power of \(p\) such that \(Q-1\ge2\max(1,L)\). Then \(Q=O_p(L+1)\). The local network tables will use only the prime field. For each assignment on the occurring variables, form the bit vector \(a\) in increasing normalized name order and pad missing coordinates to zero. For a fixed instance define

\[
             P(Y)=\sum_{a\models\varphi}\prod_{i:a_i=1} Y_i .
\]

Every model has a distinct monomial with coefficient one. If the instance is satisfiable, \(P\) is nonzero and has total degree at most \(L\). Choose \(y_i\) independently and uniformly from \(K^\times\); choose an independent uniform \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear functional \(\ell:K\to\mathbb F_p\). Polynomial identity testing over the sampling set \(K^\times\) gives \(\Pr[P(y)\ne0]\ge1-L/(Q-1)\ge1/2\). For any fixed nonzero value, the functional is nonzero with probability \(1-1/p\). Thus the scalar \(z=\ell(P(y))\) detects every satisfiable instance with probability at least \(1/4\), and is always zero on unsatisfiable instances. The binary special case is the unblocked monomial and inner-code construction of Naik, Regan and Sivakumar. [Naik et al., §3.2](https://cse.buffalo.edu/~regan/papers/pdf/NRS95.pdf)

For one trial, guess one Boolean value per retained occurrence. Require zero at every dummy. Test each active clause and route the guesses through the first shared set of controlled swaps. Check equality within each group of equal names. The consistent patterns that pass all tests are in bijection with satisfying assignments on the occurring names, including the empty assignment when there are no names. Route the first values of the groups through the second shared set of controlled swaps and zero the trailing coordinates. This gives the compact vector \(a\) without any numerical-name lookup in the trial. Checking clauses and dummies and moving a single payload through both routes costs \(O(L\log(L+2))\) bounded-fanin Boolean gates, including the needed copies of shared controls.

Choose a generator \(g\) of the cyclic group \(K^\times\), and hardwire \(y_i=g^{e_i}\). The fingerprint weight of a single model can be computed within the same gate bound, using

\[
          s=\sum_i a_i e_i \pmod{Q-1},\qquad w(a)=\ell(g^s).
\]

Adding a selected constant to the residue and conditionally subtracting \(Q-1\) costs \(O(\log Q)\) bit gates per position. A hardwired truth table for \(s\mapsto\ell(g^s)\) uses \(O_p(Q)\) binary multiplexers: use a complete tree on the binary address and pad unused entries. This step uses the *exponent* of each nonzero weight. There is no need for a separate multiplication circuit over the extension field per occurrence. For fixed \(p\), an output table converts the computed base-\(p\) residue into its scalar weight at constant cost. Invalid guess patterns receive weight zero.

Summing over the guesses gives \(z\), since \(\ell\) is linear. Boolean gate and equality tensors have a unique internal evaluation once the external input and the guesses are given. Any fixed-arity output table can be implemented with a constant number of trivalent tables, for example by computing its weight bits and summing over a constant-size selector of a bit with its place value. Thus each trial adds \(O_p(L\log(L+2))=O_p(N+1)\) to the volume. Shared preprocessing is deterministic and receives only one consistent evaluation, independent of the number of trials. [Binary-wire volume and copying convention](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md) · [Routing independent model columns](one-sort-for-bounded-ambiguity-sat.md)

## A version that isolates a unique minimum model

The same linear trial volume can be obtained from the isolation lemma, with neither Toeplitz multiplication nor an individual network for every possible isolating weight. Set \(L\ge1\), choose independent integer weights \(w_i\) uniformly from \(\{1,\ldots,2L\}\), and let \(s(a)=\sum_i w_i a_i\). With probability at least \(1/2\), a nonempty model family has a unique minimum-weight member. Indeed, after fixing the other weights, the best model omitting position \(i\) can tie the best model containing \(i\) at at most one value of \(w_i\), if both kinds exist. Distinct minima differ somewhere, so a union bound proves the claim. [Mulmuley, Vazirani and Vazirani](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579206)

On this event the univariate polynomial \(H(X)=\sum_{a\models\varphi}X^{s(a)}\) has lowest nonzero term of coefficient one over every prime field. Its degree is at most \(2L^2\). Take the least power \(Q\) of \(p\) with \(Q-1\ge4L^2\), sample \(y\in\mathbb F_Q^\times\) uniformly and use a random base-field linear functional as before. Conditional on isolation, the evaluation is nonzero with probability at least \(1/2\), and projection then preserves nonzeroness with probability at least \(1/2\). The unconditional detection probability is at least \(1/8\). The network sums a weighted contribution from all models. The unique minimum proves this sum is a nonzero polynomial without requiring that the final accepting configurations themselves be unique.

There are quadratically many possible exponents, but a quadratic lookup per trial can be avoided. Compute \(s(a)\) with \(O(L\log(L+2))\) bit gates. Write \(s=s_0+B s_1\), where \(B\) is the least power of two greater than \(2L\); each digit is less than \(B\). Two hardwired lookup tables give the binary field-coordinate vectors of \(y^{s_0}\) and \((y^B)^{s_1}\) in \(O_p(L\log(L+2))\) gates. Multiply those two field elements and project the result. In a fixed polynomial basis there are \(O_p(\log(L+2))\) base-\(p\) digits. Schoolbook polynomial multiplication and reduction by a fixed irreducible polynomial cost \(O_p(\log^2(L+2))\) bit gates. The bounded-degree field operations have deterministic internal evaluations. Thus summing the final scalar weights computes \(\ell(H(y))\) in the same asymptotic per-trial volume. Taking \(6(N+1)\) trials instead of \(3(N+1)\) makes \(2^N(7/8)^{6(N+1)}<1\); the exact OR construction below applies unchanged.

## Simultaneous fixing and exact output

Take \(r=3(N+1)\) independent trials. The probability that *any* satisfiable length-\(N\) input is missed is at most \(2^N(3/4)^r<1\). Fix one successful list at this length. Fermat's identity gives the exact scalar decision

\[
              1-\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-z_j^{p-1}).
\]

There is no free copying of a computed scalar here. Use \(p-1\) copies of the trial witnesses for each \(z_j^{p-1}\). Realize each difference \(1-Z\) by an extra summed Boolean tag. On the constant branch force every unused guess to zero and output weight \(1\); on the other branch give the computed product weight \(-1\) times its value. Use the same construction for the final difference, pinning every unused inner tag and guess on its constant branch. These tests, conditional weights and copies have size linear in the component volumes. A final input-validity bit zeros the output on malformed inputs. Over \(\mathbb F_2\) the network is a nondeterministic circuit, and its accepting count on a well-formed input is \(1+\prod_j(1+c_j)\), where \(c_j\) is the number of models of weight one in trial \(j\); this is the parity-OR count, so the network gives no unique-witness verifier ([unique-witness verifiers](unique-witness-verifier-size-of-sat-and-unsat.md)). The construction costs \(O_p(N\log^4(N+2)+N^2)=O_p((N+1)^2)\). On non-byte-aligned ASCII input lengths the reject convention allows a constant zero network with linear cost for terminating unused open wires. The good trial lists are length-specific. No efficient uniform way of finding them is asserted.

Increasing \(Q\) would reduce the probability that an extension-field evaluation vanishes. Recovering all \(d=[K:\mathbb F_p]\) coordinates and testing their joint nonzeroness has its own cost. For arbitrary vectors in \(\mathbb F_p^d\), the unique polynomial of individual degree at most \(p-1\) for this test is \(1-\prod_{i=1}^d(1-X_i^{p-1})\), of degree \(d(p-1)\). Reducing any polynomial modulo \(X_i^p-X_i\) cannot increase total degree. Thus any scalar expression whose terms use at most \(a\) linear forms in the vector needs \(a\ge d(p-1)\) in this black-box setting. Simply trading \(N\) scalar trials for \(N/\log N\) whole-extension trials does not by itself improve the exponent. This argument does not lower-bound networks that exploit formula syntax in other ways.

The finite audit enumerates all 256 possible model sets on three bits, all nonzero sampling tuples and all coordinate functionals in \(\mathbb F_8\) and \(\mathbb F_9\). It checks the exponent-based lookup against multiplication, the probability bounds, and an exact fixed-list decision over each prime subfield. It also exhausts the two-bit unique-minimum construction over \(\mathbb F_{32}\) and \(\mathbb F_{27}\), including both tables of the exponent split. ## Unbounded clause width at the same physical bit length

**Encoding lemma.** There is a bounded-fanin Boolean preprocessing circuit of size \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}(N+2))\) taking an \(N\)-bit DIMACS CNF to a proper ASCII 3-CNF of a single length \(O(N+1)\) depending only on \(N\). Every model on the *occurring* source names extends uniquely after renaming; every target model arises this way. Unused declared source names are irrelevant to SAT and are omitted from this model correspondence. Malformed input maps to an unsatisfiable file. The preprocessing uses only deterministic gates.

Let \(B=\max(1,\lfloor\log_{256}(N+2)/3\rfloor)\) bytes. Spell a body literal as its original signed decimal token followed by one separator character. The total length of all these words is \(O(N)\); lexical whitespace, comments and zero terminators carry no assignment information. Short decimally spelled names that denote the same number, including long tokens with leading zeros, must refer to the same target variable. Reserve variables for all positive canonical decimal names of at most \(B\) digits and pin every absent one false. Assign consecutive indices to the remaining occurring numeric names, obtained by the long-name ranking circuit above. There are \(O(N/B)\) of the latter since one representative per name occupies more than \(B\) digits. A literal whose *spelling* exceeds \(B\) is still mapped to a reserved short name if its *value* has at most \(B\) canonical digits.

Build a shared dictionary of all nonempty concatenations \(D\) of complete, valid literal words whose total byte length is at most \(B\). Their number, including all aliases and orders, is at most \(\sum_{j=1}^B256^j=O(1+N^{1/3})\). Give \(D\) an OR value. Removing its last word gives a shorter dictionary entry; a binary gate equivalence combines the preceding value with the signed literal of the last word. For a one-word entry equate its output to that literal. The spelling of its variable fits the reserved short-name range. These \(O(1+N^{1/3})\) gates give unique values even to dictionary entries that never occur. For a clause with total spelling length at most \(B\), mark its dictionary entry as required; a unit forces each marked value true just once, however many physical clauses had that spelling. Treat an empty clause separately as a fixed contradiction.

Each remaining clause has more than \(B\) spelling bytes, so there are \(O(N/B)\) of them. Inside these clauses, take each word longer than \(B\) as a single signed variable. In every intervening run of short words, greedily pack maximal groups of total length at most \(B\); each group contributes its one dictionary value. If successive packs in a run have sizes \(s_i,s_{i+1}\), then \(s_i+s_{i+1}>B\), since the first word of the second did not fit in the first. The total number of short packs is at most twice the total short-word length divided by \(B\), plus one per run. The number of runs is bounded by the number of long words plus the number of long clauses. Both are \(O(N/B)\). Thus all long clauses together refer to \(O(N/B)\) terms, even when long and short identifiers alternate. Fold each such clause with binary OR equivalences and require its result true. [Lean proof of the packing inequality, summed over all runs](../lean/ClausePackBounds.lean)

Use the usual gate clauses on fresh outputs. Inside a generated clause erase duplicate literals and discard tautologies, which handles repeated or opposite inputs to an OR gate. Pin three special false anchors by the seven proper clauses excluding every other anchor tuple. Pad short generated clauses with different false anchors on distinct variables; an empty generated clause becomes the disjunction of the three false anchors. For every occurring source variable \(x\), include \((x\lor\neg f_0\lor f_1)\) with distinct false anchors \(f_0,f_1\). This guarantees its harmless appearance even when its source occurrences were all tautological. This yields \(O(1+N/B)\) clauses and variable slots, since the dictionary, all reserved small names, and their pins cost \(O(1+N^{1/3})\). Fresh variables are determined by the original model. Pin any unused reserved target slots to zero. Use bound-dependent fixed-width **space-padded** decimal fields and a predetermined number of clause slots. Fill inactive slots with a clause already entailed by the anchor pins; allocate sufficient room for the pins of unused variables. Every output name fits \(O(\log(N+2))\) bits and every row has constant width in names. The serialized output therefore has \(O(N+1)\) bits. A promised proper format with three distinct variables per clause is preserved, including on the fixed contradiction branch.

For the circuit bound, validate syntax and numerical ranges as in the 3-SAT preprocessing, omitting the width-three rejection. At each token's last byte retain its sign, normalized-name rank, length clipped at \(B+1\), and, for short tokens, its spelling copied from the preceding \(B\) byte positions. The normalized-name rank is transferred from its first significant digit as above. Mark the occurring reserved short names and the dictionary entries for short clauses by sorting records against the fixed catalog. A first scan, resetting at a clause terminator, can accumulate each clause's clipped spelling length and its string while it still fits. Propagate the small/large flag back to the clause's literal records by a reverse scan. For large clauses a second scan carries the current partial pack of at most \(B\) bytes, its length, the current OR wire, and the next fresh wire number. A long word can flush the pending pack and emit its own term at the same slot. A clause terminator flushes the last pack and emits the final requirement. Each original byte emits at most a constant number of candidate gate or clause records. The dictionary index can be the base-256 spelling, with a leading length marker or an offset by the number of shorter strings. Gaps for invalid entries still use \(O(1+N^{1/3})\) slots. Every scan transition, including a variable shift of a \(B\)-byte pack and comparison of wire indices, takes \(\operatorname{polylog}(N+2)\) gates by direct bit operations. Stable compaction of the candidate records by sorting, generation of the pins, and conversion of the \(O(1+N/B)\) retained rows to fixed-width decimal fields all fit the claimed circuit size. This implementation permits sequential depth.

Composing this single deterministic preprocessing circuit with the proper 3-SAT network adds its gate and copying volume once. The exact network on the output costs \(O_F(N^2)\). The forced dummy variables and uniquely determined gate values also preserve the ordinary model count on occurring source names.

An audit instantiates the dictionary on demand, serializes 1,377 proper outputs, and checks the exact zero-or-one number of extensions for 5,241 fixed source assignments. The cases include leading-zero aliases, long decimal identifiers, tautologies, repeated clauses and empty clauses. [Dictionary reduction audit](../audits/cnf_width_dictionary_audit.py)

**Uniform RAM cost and the three deadlines.** A single logarithmic-word RAM constructs the physical output in \(O(N+1)\) native steps. Parse the input in a constant number of byte scans, retaining source token boundaries, signs and normalized lengths. Compare a normalized name against the declared maximum first by length, then lexicographically only in the equal-length case. The latter scan costs at most the length of the token being tested, even for a huge header. Saturate the declared clause count at \(N+1\) and compare with the terminator count. Intern normalized name digits in fixed-alphabet tries with a constant-size child array per node. Each token is scanned only a constant number of times. Allocate dense identifiers for occurring long names at their first encounter. Construct the bounded catalog by enumerating its at most \(O(1+N^{1/3})\) byte strings and parsing each in \(\operatorname{poly}(B+1)\) steps, which costs \(O(N+1)\) altogether. The length threshold \(B\) can be found by repeated constant-base multiplication or doubling.

During each clause scan, decide the pack boundaries using the stored token lengths before appending a token. Read each packed spelling into the catalog trie one byte at a time, resetting at each flush. Thus the work is linear in the total input spelling length, including long runs of tiny literals. Mark required short entries by a single array access, and save only the \(O(1+N/B)\) long-clause terms and gate records. Integer indices and pointers for the tries fit logarithmic words. Precompute the physical decimal spellings of all \(K=O(1+N/B)\) output indices with a decimal counter and fixed-width fields in \(O(K\log(N+2))=O(N+1)\) byte operations. The final pass copies those fields into the predetermined clause slots, again in \(O(N+1)\) steps. This works with a packed input and output by extracting and storing bytes, and needs no comparison sort of the source tokens. Linear-size transformations of propositional formulas in literal-occurrence measures are classical. Here the output-bit accounting pays for spelling every emitted decimal index, and the RAM time includes producing those bytes. Priority for this precise encoding and bound is unestablished. [Bubeck and Kleine Büning, 2009](https://www.ub-net.de/cms/fileadmin/upb/doc/bubeck-3cnf-transform-ipl-2009.pdf)

Consequently, for each \(k\in\{1,2,3\}\), **ordinary unbounded-width DIMACS CNF-SAT has a deterministic \(O(N^k)\) unrestricted-workspace RAM solver if and only if proper DIMACS 3-SAT has one.** One direction applies the constructed reduction and runs the 3-SAT solver on its \(O(N+1)\)-bit output. The reverse applies the general solver to the width-three sublanguage, checking proper syntax in linear time if malformed files must return zero. Distinctness among the at most three variable names in a clause is checked using their interned indices. In the ordinary RAM class the composed program can use a sufficiently large fixed logarithmic word-width constant to address its work arrays and emulate the solver at the output's word width. The equivalence transfers each deadline, including the linear one; a separation at any of them still requires a lower bound.

## Corollary for tree-formula SAT

Let an ordinary Boolean formula be a **tree** spelled in delimited prefix order over a fixed finite basis of nullary, unary and binary gates. Each occurrence of a variable carries its decimal name. There is a uniform \(O(N+1)\)-step RAM reduction to a proper 3-CNF of one length \(O(N+1)\) per source length, preserving the exact model count on occurring names. Consequently the same \(O_F(N^2)\) exact tensor-network ceiling holds for this syntax. For the standard basis containing AND, OR and NOT, all three RAM deadlines are equivalent to the corresponding proper 3-SAT deadlines: the reverse reduction writes the conjunction of clauses as a tree, using constant additional operator tokens per literal occurrence and copying each actual name once.

Ganardi, Hucke, Jeż, Lohrey and Noeth previously constructed small tree grammars in linear time and compressed arithmetic formulas into circuits, with their size bound parameterized by the number of distinct variables. Here the accounting charges the actual bytes of each name occurrence and of every wire name written to proper DIMACS. Priority for this encoded-bit corollary is unestablished. [Ganardi et al., *Constructing small tree grammars and small circuits for formulas*](https://ii.uni.wroc.pl/~aje/prace/JCSS2017.pdf)

Parse the input with a stack, intern normalized variable names, and store the canonical prefix token stream in one byte array. Each standard spelling includes its token boundaries. For a node \(u\), let \(W(u)\) be the number of these bytes in its whole subtree. The subtree's boundaries and \(W(u)\) are found in a postorder pass. In particular, a variable occurrence is charged for its complete name spelling and an operator for its own fixed-size token. The root weight is \(O(N+1)\). Use a cutoff \(B=\Theta(\log(N+2))\) small enough that the catalog of all strings of length at most \(B+c\) over the fixed alphabet has size \(O(1+N^{1/3})\), where \(c\) covers a special hole token. For every valid small expression make a circuit computing it on the common short-name variables. For every valid expression with one distinguished hole, make two circuits, replacing the hole by zero and one, respectively. Straight parsing costs \(O(B+1)\) gates and \(\operatorname{poly}(B+1)\) generation time per catalog string. Their combined gate count is \(O(1+N/B)\) and their total generation time is \(O(N+1)\).

Call a node large when \(W(u)>B\). If the root is small, one catalog lookup suffices. Otherwise large nodes form a rooted tree. Its leaves have disjoint original subtrees of more than \(B\) bytes, so there are \(O(N/B)\) such leaves, and at most that many binary branching nodes. At a large leaf, a long variable token is resolved to its interned variable. An operator there acts on at most two small catalog values. At a binary branching node, apply its original gate to its two large child values.

It remains to process paths of nodes with exactly one large child. For such a node \(u\) with large child \(v\), the local text weight \(d(u)=W(u)-W(v)\) is the operator token plus at most one small sibling. It is at most \(B+O(1)\), and these local contributions are disjoint. Handle each \(d(u)>B\) by one original gate using the small sibling's catalog value if present. There are \(O(N/B)\) such exceptions. On every remaining path run, greedily group consecutive nodes until adding the next local weight would exceed \(B\). Consecutive groups have combined local weight greater than \(B\). The number of runs is \(O(1+N/B)\), since the large tree has that many leaves and branching nodes and each exception splits at most one run. Hence the total number of groups is \(O(1+N/B)\).

A group represents a one-hole context: replace the subtree of the last large child by the hole. Its text length is at most \(B+c\). Its two catalog outputs, together with the actual child bit, compute the result with one Boolean multiplexer, implemented by a constant number of bounded-fanin gates. The context text consists of two slices of the canonical token array with the hole between them. Looking up all groups in a trie visits \(O(N+1)\) bytes in total. The individually requested small siblings and small children at the remaining \(O(1+N/B)\) interfaces cost \(O(N+1)\) bytes of lookup even if each has size \(B\). The parse tree can be traversed in linear time to label the groups and resolve the resulting gates in dependency order. Altogether the circuit has \(O(1+N/B)\) gates. Reserve or intern its source names as in the CNF construction, pin absent reserved names, preserve all occurring variables, constrain all gate outputs by equivalences, and require the root to be true. The same anchor and padding rules yield proper clauses and an \(O(N+1)\)-bit physical output in \(O(N+1)\) RAM steps. A failed parse takes the common contradiction branch.

For the network composition, one deterministic RAM checker on a source \(X\) and candidate output \(Y\) runs the entire reduction and compares **all** output bytes with \(Y\) in \(O(N+1)\) steps. The parsimonious bounded-run encoding gives a binary-wire network of \(O(N\operatorname{polylog}(N+2))\) volume with exactly one accepting internal evaluation precisely for the correct output. Connect its candidate-output wires to the proper 3-SAT network and sum over \(Y\). Exactly the actual output contributes. This uses one checked run for the whole string and gives the claimed volume. Priority for the tree formulation with these physical-bit and RAM-time bounds is unestablished. [Bounded deterministic RAM computation with unique internal extensions](locally-indexed-formulas-for-the-delayed-forecast.md)

## The optimal number of fixed-field measurements of the model set

A separate lower bound identifies the cost of the amplification step in a specific model. The input to a measurement is a set of satisfying assignments on a known ordered variable space. A query chooses **any** function $f$ from that space to a fixed finite field $F=\mathbb F_{p^e}$ and returns $\sum_{a\text{ satisfies}} f(a)$. The next query may depend on every previous field answer. The final decision may use arbitrary computation. In this model the only input-dependent information supplied is the sequence of field answers. At every sufficiently large byte-aligned bit length $N$, for ordinary proper DIMACS files the exact worst-case number of queries is $\Theta_F(N)$. The upper follows from the fixed fingerprint trials, on the canonical occurring-name ranks with unused positions assigned zero. Equivalently one can use independent field weights on the entire common name universe, which is finite at each length, when query-description size is free. The lower below already uses the very same occurring variables and fixed header on all files of a given size. It does not lower-bound general network volume by $N^2$: a network can read the formula, share gates across trials, or use checks depending on its syntax.

Here is a length-linear encoding realizing *every* subset of a fixed set of full assignments. Take $G=2^h$ groups of $b=\max(1,\lfloor h/3\rfloor)$ bits, and $q=Gb$. Introduce one-hot variables $R_g$ for the chosen group and $L_j$ for the offset. Force exactly one in each family in $O(G+b)$ clauses with canonical prefix-OR gates: fix the starting prefix false and final prefix true, use gate equivalences, and forbid a true next bit when the preceding prefix is already true. All auxiliary gates have unique values. For each $b$-bit string $v$ introduce one variable $Y_v$. On each choice $L_j$, require $Y_v=v_j$ by a binary implication. These $b2^b$ fixed implications determine the whole library. Use three shared false anchors, pinned by the seven proper clauses excluding the other anchor assignments. Short clauses can now be padded to three distinct variables with false anchors; a preexisting literal of an anchor must be padded with different anchors.

To encode a desired subset $I$ of the $q$ positions, let $v_g$ be its indicator word in group $g$. For each group append just one clause
$$
                     \neg R_g\lor Y_{v_g}\lor f_0 ,
$$
where $f_0$ is false. The full models are exactly the assignments $u_{g,j}$ with $(g,j)\in I$. Every bit of $u_{g,j}$, including all anchors, gates and library values, depends only on $(g,j)$, irrespective of $I$. Occurring names are fixed because the common prefix uses every name. Padded fields of width $O(h)$ bytes make the varying files equal-length with canonical decimal names. The number of clauses is $O(G+b2^b)=O(G)$; the $G$ data clauses use $\Theta(Gh)$ bits in the fixed-width layout. Hence $N=\Theta(q)$. Dyadic scales have bounded length ratios; add common legal whitespace to cover every sufficiently large byte length. This packing matters: spending one named disabling clause per position would lose a logarithm.

Follow an adaptive tester on the empty model set. Its $s$ answers are zero. For each fixed $u_i$ record its $s$ query weights. Choose an $\mathbb F_p$ basis of $F$; this gives $es$ prime-field scalar coordinates. If $q>es(p-1)$ there is a nonempty $I$ whose column sum vanishes, so the same transcript occurs on a satisfiable formula. In fact there are at least $2^{q-es(p-1)}-1$ missed positive members whenever the exponent is positive. For a direct proof let $x_i$ be the mask bits and let $L_j(x)$ be the $es$ homogeneous linear coordinate sums. Over $\mathbb F_p$ the polynomial
$$
                P(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{es}(1-L_j(x)^{p-1})
$$
equals one exactly when all answers vanish. It takes value one on the all-zero input. After multilinearization its degree is at most $d=es(p-1)$. A highest-degree nonzero monomial of degree $d'\le d$ retains its nonzero coefficient under every fixing of the other $q-d'$ Boolean variables. Uniqueness of the multilinear representation then ensures a nonzero point for each fixing. This gives at least $2^{q-d'}$ such masks. The ordinary zero-sum existence statement is a classical elementary abelian case of the Davenport constant; Lean independently verifies it for all primes via Chevalley–Warning. [Lean proof](../upstream-checks/ModelSumMaskLower.lean) · [Olson, *A Combinatorial Problem on Finite Abelian Groups, I*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-314X(69)90021-3)

On this mask family the exact number is $\lceil q/(e(p-1))\rceil$. Divide the positions into batches of at most $e(p-1)$, each further divided into at most $e$ groups of at most $p-1$. Weight each model in a group by the associated basis element. A group contributes its ordinary integer size among the present models, strictly less than $p$. A batch sum is nonzero exactly when it contains a present model. This also explains the characteristic dependence: over the rationals a single unrestricted counting query detects nonemptiness. Turning an unknown rational count into an exact zero-or-one scalar using only the permitted network contractions is a distinct operation.
