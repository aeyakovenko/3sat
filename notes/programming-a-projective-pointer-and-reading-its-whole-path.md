# Programmable pointer motion and the cost of observing the path

A logarithmic-word RAM can update a pair of words by a different determinant-one matrix at every step. For all initial projective points at once, the bank of final reads after $T$ such updates has a Boolean circuit of size $O(v(1+k)^{1-\delta}+T(1+k)^2)$, where $v=3\cdot2^{k-1}$, for an absolute $\delta>0$. The matrices are ordinary input bits and may fail to commute. The circuit for the final action uses the finite cyclic-shift construction. For a different native law, $j\gets \pm j+a_t$, the XOR of **every** intermediate read on every initial address has an exact projected circuit using $O_\epsilon(2^k(1+k)^{1/2+\epsilon}+T(1+k)^2)$ gates. The latter bound imports the binary convolution theorem. A reversible data-dependent branch already realizes permutations outside the entire projective group. ## A different matrix at each native step

Put $M=2^k$ and $R=\mathbb Z/M$. A projective point is a pair $(x,y)$ with at least one odd entry, up to simultaneous multiplication by an odd unit. If $y$ is odd its unique representative is $[j:1]$ for $j\in R$. Otherwise its unique representative is $[1:u]$ with $u$ even. Thus the two charts have $M$ and $M/2$ data sites. Each matrix acts by the usual two multiply-adds per coordinate. A native program can keep the unnormalized pair throughout. If an actual indexed read is needed, normalize the odd coordinate by modular inversion, implementable by iterated Newton doubling in $O(\log(k+1))$ native operations. The claim about $T$ pointer advances charges a constant number of native word operations per advance and the one final normalization per trajectory.

First consider the upper shear $U_s$ acting by $[x:y]\mapsto[x+sy:y]$. Its action on the first chart is ordinary cyclic shift by $s$. On the second chart, $u$ goes to $u/(1+su)$, and zero is fixed. On a shell $u=2^h z$ with $1\le h<k$ and $z$ odd modulo $2^{k-h}$, the new inverse odd coordinate is
\[
               z'^{-1}=z^{-1}+2^h s\pmod{2^{k-h}} .
\]
The shell is fixed if $2h\ge k$. Otherwise partition by the bottom $h$ bits of $z^{-1}$, and in each such part use the remaining $k-2h$ bits of $z^{-1}$ as the site label. This is an ordinary cycle of length $2^{k-2h}$ shifted by $s$. These cycles partition the bank. Apply the cyclic shifter on each of them, for total $O(v(1+k)^{1-\delta})$ Boolean gates. All site relabelings are public wires. Swapping the two coordinates is a fixed permutation of the projective points, so the same bound holds for lower shears $L_s$. Uppers and lowers need not commute even after passage to the projective action. For instance $U_1 L_1$ and $L_1 U_1$ send $[1:0]$ to different points for $k\ge2$.

For a determinant-one matrix $G=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ with odd $a$ put $w(q)=U_qL_{-q^{-1}}U_q$. Direct matrix multiplication gives
\[
 G=L_{ca^{-1}}\,w(a)w(-1)\,U_{a^{-1}b},\qquad
 w(q)=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\-q^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If $a$ is even, $c$ is odd, so $w(1)G$ has odd top-left entry. Apply the preceding formula and undo this public pivot. The pivot bit controls a constant number of multiplexers per site. Inverting an odd word in ordinary Boolean gates costs $\operatorname{poly}(k)$ by Newton doubling, absorbed by the bank term. Multiply the $T$ matrices in chronological order once, at $O(T(1+k)^2)$ Boolean cost by schoolbook word multiplication, then do this bounded number of bank transforms. This proves the stated final-read circuit. One may check the determinant promise with the same order of gates. The construction specifies public modular-inverse wiring on the data sites and makes no low-degree claim about the physical addresses of those wires.

## Observing all intermediate addresses

Now use a single native word modulo $M$. At time $t$ let $j\gets e_tj+a_t$, with $e_t\in\{-1,1\}$, and XOR the bit presently indexed in a shared immutable bank $X$ into a private accumulator. The desired output is its final value from *each* starting address. Prefixes act by $j\mapsto s_tj+b_t$, where $s_0=1,b_0=0$ and $(s_t,b_t)=(e_ts_{t-1},e_tb_{t-1}+a_t)$. Both prefix components cost $O(k+1)$ Boolean gates per step. Form two bit histograms aggregating occurrences by parity,
\[
    H_s[b]=\bigoplus_{t:\ (s_t,b_t)=(s,b)}1 .
\]
Their existential verification uses one zero-initialized bank of $2M$ bits. For each prefix read a bit at $(s_t,b_t)$ and immediately write its complement there. The end bank must equal the two histograms. The sparse read/write checker gives $O(M+T(1+k)^2)$ gates, including every untouched location. If $2T>2M$, break the events into consecutive chunks of at most $2M$ with shared intermediate banks. The extra full-bank cost is $O(T)$. [Sparse chronological access certificates](sparse-batch-lookup-with-position-certificates.md)

For $s=\pm1$ let $C_s[j]=\bigoplus_b H_s[b]X[j+b]$. This is cyclic convolution over $\mathbb F_2$ with the indices of $H_s$ negated. Reduce an ordinary product modulo $z^M-1$ by adding its two coefficient halves. The answer is $C_+[j]\oplus C_-[-j]$ for every $j$. This proves the all-reads bound. The histogram bits are uniquely determined even if the routing witnesses used to certify them have multiple choices. Fixed input/output bits may be pinned. With $T\le M$, the projected proper 3-CNF has $O_\epsilon((M(1+k)^{1/2+\epsilon}+T(1+k)^2)(1+k))$ actual named bits by the usual bounded-fan-in translation. This proof gives circuit size. It does not claim a fast physical printer for unrestricted full-width convolution.

More generally take a public group $K$ of $h\le k+1$ odd word multipliers. The update is $j\gets u_tj+a_t$ with $u_t\in K$. Compute the actual prefix multipliers and intercepts using word multiplication in $O(T(1+k)^2)$ gates. Guess the ordinal in $K$ of each prefix multiplier and verify it by an $O(h(1+k))$-gate lookup into the public table. Pad the ordinal address space to the next power of two and require the unused final histograms to be zero. Collect a histogram and perform one cyclic convolution for each slope. A known unit $s\in K$ sends the lane $j$ to the public output wire $C_s[sj]$. Thus the projected bound is $O_\epsilon(hM(1+k)^{1/2+\epsilon}+T(1+k)^2)$. Even $h$ may grow below the saving. For example the powers of $5^{2^{k-2-d}}$ modulo $2^k$ form a subgroup of order $2^d$ for $0\le d\le k-2$. A full group of affine motions with any nonidentity such unit is nonabelian.

## One reversible conditional update escapes the projective law

Let $k\ge3$ and mark the first-chart points $[0:1]$ and $[M/2:1]$ in a read-only bank, leaving every other point unmarked. Read the bit at the current point, apply $U_{M/2}$ exactly when it equals one, and otherwise stay put. This single native conditional rule interchanges the two marked points and fixes all the rest. It cannot arise from *any* invertible projective matrix, of any determinant. A matrix fixing $[1:0]$ has $c=0$ and acts on the first chart by an invertible scalar affine map. Fixing $[1:1]$ and $[2:1]$ forces this affine map to be the identity because the difference of the arguments is a unit. The image of $[0:1]$ gives the contradiction. Conditionally applying one fixed shear can therefore leave the matrix-summary class while preserving reversibility. On normalized natural addresses the mark lookup is one native read, and the first-chart update is addition of $M/2$. The same program, on banks marking arbitrary unions of pairs $\{j,j+M/2\}$ in the first chart, realizes $2^{M/2}$ distinct reversible transports.

Even when every step is a genuine shared matrix, its product alone loses intermediate observations. The two streams $(U_1,U_{-1})$ and $(I,I)$ have identical products. Starting at $[0:1]$, they give different XORs of the two successive reads whenever $X_{[1:1]}\ne X_{[0:1]}$. The histogram proof identifies precisely the additional data it uses for the scalar affine stream.

## A branch-preserving lower bound on an actual unary-hierarchy guest

Consider a fixed nondeterministic guest on unary input of length $n$. Choose $k=\lfloor\log_2(n^2/(1+\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil))\rfloor$ for all large $n$ and $m=2^k$. Guess $m$ data bits $Z_j$, storing them in dense addresses. Then guess $m$ words of which the bottom $k$ bits are $a_1,\ldots,a_m$. Accumulate $\bigoplus_{i=1}^m Z_{a_i}$ and use that bit as the verdict. The guest is a single fixed program operating in $O(n+m+\operatorname{polylog}n)=o(n^2)$ native steps, even if determining the unary length costs a scan. The word width is ample to hold $n^2$ and the disjoint stores. One may calculate $k,m$ by comparisons and doubling without a division opcode. For example choose the largest power of two $m$ with $m(1+\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil)\le n^2$.

Every one of the $mk$ low address bits is essential to this exact **branch** predicate. To test coordinate $b$ of word $a_i$, keep all other addresses fixed, change just that bit, and set the two selected data entries differently. Contributions from every other address cancel in the comparison of these two executions. A fan-in-two Boolean circuit with one output depending on $s$ input bits has at least $s-1$ gates, by counting edges in its connected backward cone. Thus the named source guest has an $\Omega(mk)=\Omega(n^2)$ circuit cost for any compilation preserving its bitwise branch predicate. On every sufficiently large interior length assigned to this guest by the fixed quadratic unary hierarchy machine, its interpreter budget permits this whole guest. Its endpoint rule and the checker for the specific diagonal opponent have distinct semantics. [Fixed unary hierarchy and simulation budget](a-fixed-unary-ram-source-for-physical-sat-compilers.md)

The same $\Omega(mk)$ order of magnitude holds for a bounded-fan-in *projected* checker with a single output and freely quantified auxiliary bits, provided every branch address bit remains an independently pinnable external input. Each essential input must occur in the output cone even after existential projection. Equivalently a bounded-width conjunction describing the branch relation must mention every essential address bit, so it needs $\Omega(mk)$ literal occurrences. The actual guest has an accepting choice at every length. A compiler preserving only the existential verdict may erase this entire guest computation. The special packed-verifier opponent used in the hierarchy diagonalization has only $O(t/\log t)$ genuinely guessed assignment bits; the $\Omega(t)$ bound just proved relies on an opponent exposing $\Theta(t)$ independent address bits. A bound on its existential verdict, or a fast reduction for that special opponent, requires another argument.
