# What nearby SAT decisions reveal about a proof of rejection

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** The conjectured extraction would use a fast solver on related inputs to predict proof bits of one of its actual rejections. There is an exact obstruction for local perturbations of the guarded self-referential formula. A simpler, unconditionally easy SAT family also excludes much larger conditional completions. On that second family a different transformation does recover a genuinely nonlocal bit. [Guarded formula, code and monitor](guarded-code-rejections.md)

## An entire neighborhood of the unmodified guarded formula

Use all the notation of the guarded construction. Write $n_0=2bd$ for its number of raw bits, $h$ for its per-row raw-symbol probe cap, and $\mathcal D_+$ for the law with $X$ a uniform degree-$<u$ codeword and $Y$ an independent uniform array. By increasing the fixed batch exponent as there, for some $c>0$ and all sufficiently large scales,

$$
 \sup_{e\in\{0,1\}^{n_0}}\Pr_{R\sim\mathcal D_+}[F(R\oplus e)\text{ is satisfiable}]
 \le 2^{-c d/(h+1)}.
$$

In fact, translating by any fixed $e$ preserves exact $u$-wise uniformity of the raw symbols. The XOR of the two translated halves is still uniform because the entire $Y$ was independent uniform. These are exactly the two properties of $\mathcal D_+$ used to transfer the empirical moments and bound the code guard. The negative reference law $\mathcal D_-$ and its distance from the actual timely-rejection fiber are unchanged. The solver still receives the physical formula for the *edited* raw input. This is the same formula family, with the same solver program, proof system and compiler as before.

Let $t=\lfloor c_1 d/[(h+1)\log_2(2+b(h+1))]\rfloor$ for a sufficiently small constant $c_1>0$. The number of masks of Hamming weight at most $t$ is at most $(\mathrm e n_0/t)^t$ when $t\ge1$, with the bound one at $t=0$. Its base-two logarithm is at most $c d/[2(h+1)]$ for sufficiently small $c_1$. Hence except on at most $2^{-\Omega(d/(h+1))}$ of the positive centers, **every** raw perturbation of at most $t$ bits gives an unsatisfiable ordinary formula. A preselected collection of at most $2^{cd/[2(h+1)]}$ arbitrary XOR masks has the same simultaneous property. All probabilities are over the center; a perturbation within its good neighborhood can subsequently be selected after inspecting it.

Fix a stateless proof-bit decoder with shared advice and the raw access budget of the verifier-independent estimate. Grant it arbitrary adaptive, uncharged calls for the genuine SAT verdict at raw points within distance $t$ of the center. On a good center, replacing every answer by the constant UNSAT reproduces every decoder execution, regardless of the number of calls. This holds for repeated steps whose endpoints stay in the ball. The stubbed decoder is a well-defined proof oracle on *every* raw input and inherits the direct raw-probe bound. The verifier-independent moment inequality therefore bounds its fresh success on $\mathcal D_+$. Add the probability of a bad center. If the solver meets the proposed fast clock, these centers are genuine timely rejections. Thus an adaptive neighborhood verdict oracle does not supply the missing local proof bits. Native stopping times can be included when testing a theorem about **every** fast solver: from a hypothetical solver with a fixed constructible near-linear worst-case clock, use a fixed-rate bounded simulation that returns the computed answer at an input-length-dependent clock, then construct the monitor for that padded solver. All the counterfactual formulas at fixed dimensions have the same encoded length. Arbitrary memory events of the native executions can contain additional data and are outside this verdict-and-time assertion.

A finite raw-probe transcript can control even edits of unbounded weight. Fix an extractor with at most $p$ bits of read-only auxiliary parameters, $a$ advice bits, $v$ direct raw-symbol reads, and at most $J$ verdict calls per proof-coordinate run. If its edited inputs are explicitly specified XOR masks chosen from those data, its constant-UNSAT execution ranges over at most $J2^{p+a+bv}$ masks (allow one padding symbol per unused reply, or pad the reads). Whenever $p+a+bv+\log_2(J+1)\le cd/[2(h+1)]$, all these calls return UNSAT on a common set of centers of probability $1-2^{-\Omega(d/(h+1))}$. This count charges any random seed in the parameters when simultaneous validity over seeds is desired. A symbolic mask whose individual output entries obtain additional uncharged reads of the center is a different oracle.

## A correct solver fast on every conditional completion

For a comparator with an actual unconditional fast solver, let $d=2^b$, take $\mathbb F=\mathrm{GF}(2^b)$, and $u=d/4$ for $b\ge2$. Let $C\subseteq\mathbb F^d$ be the evaluation table on *all* field points of every polynomial of degree less than $u$. Each raw input is an arbitrary $db$-bit string, interpreted in the polynomial basis. Fix as field presentation the first degree-$b$ monic irreducible in a fixed order. Trying all $2^b=d$ candidates and using Frobenius powering and gcd tests takes $d\operatorname{poly}(b)$ work. A deterministic full decision tests $X\in C$ by interpolating the first $u$ positions and checking evaluation at every point by product and remainder trees. Its clock is $d\operatorname{poly}(b)$. [Multipoint evaluation and interpolation](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/Papers/fast-modular-transforms.pdf) · [Fast polynomial multiplication](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/91/CaKa91.pdf)

Make an ordinary fixed-layout 3-CNF $G_b(X)$ for that deterministic computation accepting $X\notin C$, with each raw bit pinned by a literal sign. All other words of the syntax depend only on the dimensions. A correct general SAT solver can recognize this format, recover and validate the pins, and compute the native answer by the interpolation test. To make recognition *linear in the full encoded length*, use a fixed public dimension header, generate and compare the active template in $d\operatorname{poly}(b)$ steps, and append a canonical string of tautological padding clauses long enough to dominate that bound. Check this simple suffix by scanning it with its prescribed literal names. Thus $N=d\operatorname{poly}(b)$, the full validation and decision fit $O(N)$, and all other inputs take a complete SAT fallback. The solver can use a fixed-rate padded decision clock on recognized inputs. A universal monitor with $k=4b$ can generate this syntax for $S$ and signal its rejection within $2^{4b}$ steps: the implicit length $db$ passes the universe's lower-size guard and all simulation costs are $d\operatorname{poly}(b)$. Its timely rejection fiber is exactly $C$. This ordinary comparator has its own monitor; its formula encodes code nonmembership, and differs from the self-referential guarded formula in the first part. [Bounded-RAM formula compiler](packed-band-transfer.md)

The solver on these templates can also expose an address and instruction schedule fixed by the dimensions, apart from the final Boolean answer. For a direct algorithm take the flag of additive subspaces $V_j$ spanned by the first $j$ binary basis vectors, and $s_j(T)=\prod_{v\in V_j}(T-v)$. Starting at $s_0(T)=T$, adjoining basis vector $w$ gives $s_{j+1}=s_j^2+s_j(w)s_j$. Induction shows that $s_j$ is additive and constant on each coset of $V_j$. The polynomials $B_i=\prod_j s_j^{i_j}$ for the binary digits of $i$ are monic with degrees $i$, hence form a triangular basis. At a coset of dimension $j+1$, write $P=A+s_jB$ in this basis. Interpolating its two child cosets yields the coefficient vectors of $A+c_0B$ and $A+c_1B$ with fixed distinct $c_0,c_1$. Scalar inversion of their fixed difference and componentwise linear operations recover $A,B$. The recursion takes $O(d\log d)$ field operations on a predetermined schedule; the coset constants can be generated in $d\operatorname{poly}(b)$ steps from the recurrence. Code membership is zero testing the upper coefficients with one fixed scan. Fixed-length field operations can use bit masks in place of value-dependent branches and tables. For valid templates the syntax check accumulates differences from fixed bits, allowing each pin sign, and reads all inputs on a fixed schedule. Thus the native timing, branch and address sequence beyond its returned verdict can be simulated from the dimensions alone on this family. Values loaded by those instructions can contain nonlocal information. This algorithm uses the established binary additive FFT basis. [Lin, Chung and Han](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.3458)

Consider *any* proximity verifier for this latter monitor with fixed fresh soundness $0<\sigma<\theta<1$ beyond relative bit distance $1/16$. Give a proof-bit reader, in addition to its postselected $a$-bit header and ordinary raw reads, uncharged access to this actual native solver on conditional completions. At each call the reader specifies a subset $T$ of whole field-symbol positions, replacement values $v_i$ on $T$, and arbitrary known offsets $e_i$ outside $T$. The counterfactual table has values $v_i$ on $T$ and $X_i+e_i$ elsewhere. All parameters may be chosen adaptively from the advice, ordinary observations, query address, and previous answers. Computing large vectors from those observations may take unlimited work. New center reads used to define a vector count as direct reads. This class includes further whole-symbol overwrites and translations of previously transformed inputs, with their net masks combined. Let $H$ count all direct raw-symbol reads in one fresh verifier invocation and $J$ all such native calls, including those made by its proof-bit reader. Use a stateless deterministic proof-bit procedure inducing a single proof table for fixed center and advice.

For a true center $X\in C$ each such native query needs at most an equality test against specified values on a known subset. If $|T^c|\ge u$, a codeword agreeing with $e$ there is determined by those data. If it does not exist, the counterfactual table cannot be a codeword. If it exists, call it $E$. The table is a codeword exactly when $X_i=v_i-E_i$ for every $i\in T$, since the difference of two codewords belongs to $C$. If $|T^c|<u$, then $|T|\ge u$. Interpolate $v$ there, checking consistency. If that gives a codeword $W$, require $X_i=W_i-e_i$ on $T^c$. This also includes $T=\varnothing$ and $T=[d]$. In particular a pure translation of a codeword returns a verdict determined by its offset alone.

**Aggregate bound.** Put $\ell=\lfloor u/[4(H+J+1)]\rfloor$, with $\ell\ge1$. In addition to an exponentially small term in $ub$, the probability over a uniform true codeword that some header yields fresh acceptance at least $\theta$ is bounded by $2^a(\sigma/\theta)^\ell$. In particular this probability tends to zero exponentially in $d/(H+J+1)$ for $a=o(d/(H+J+1))$ and $d/(H+J+1)\to\infty$.

For fixed header, implement the equality tests by reading until the first mismatch. On arbitrary raw inputs this rule gives a deterministic, possibly fictitious, answer to every native query; it matches the actual answer on every codeword by the interpolation argument. It always induces some genuine proof table, so its fresh score on a far input is at most $\sigma$. Simulate $\ell$ independent fresh checks with this evaluator. Cache the raw symbols across checks for accounting only. At most $\ell H$ new symbols come from direct reads and at most $\ell J$ can be the failing comparisons that stop their equality tests. Every other new comparison must match its value specified before the read. Until $u$ different positions have been exposed, each next new value is uniform in $\mathbb F$ conditional on the entire preceding transcript, both for a uniform codeword and for an independent uniform raw table. If the process reaches $u$ new probes, at least $u-\ell(H+J)\ge 3u/4$ of its first $u$ new replies must be successful prescribed matches. Union over their positions (padding a stopped run with arbitrary distinct reads for this estimate). The chance of reaching the cap on either law is at most $2^u d^{-3u/4}$. On runs that stop short of the cap, the entire transcript has identical probability on the two laws.

An independent uniform raw table is within relative binary distance $1/16$ of $C$ with probability at most $\mu=2^{bu+H_2(1/16)bd-bd}$. On the remaining inputs far soundness applies to the simulated proof. Writing $p_z$ for the actual fresh probability at a true center yields

$$
 \Pr_{X\leftarrow C}[\exists z:\ p_z(X)\ge\theta]
 \le 2^a\theta^{-\ell}\big(\sigma^\ell+\mu+2^u d^{-3u/4}\big).
$$

Thus this obstruction applies to a solver taking $O(N)$ on every input to the conditional-completion oracle, including completions at arbitrary distance. It applies to any later fixed choice of the proximity verifier. It counts whole-symbol overwrites; a symbolic change that computes a new output value from several center coordinates need not be an overwrite or an offset known to the reader.

## A different transformed query actually recovers a nonlocal bit

Write $X(\alpha)=P(\alpha)$ with $P(T)=\sum_{j<u}p_jT^j$, and let $\operatorname{Tr}:\mathbb F\to\mathbb F_2$ be the absolute trace. Fix $\beta\ne0$. Construct the virtual raw table

$$
 B(\alpha)=\alpha\sum_{r=0}^{b-1}\beta^{2^r} X(\alpha^{2^{-r}})^{2^r} .
$$

Here $2^{-r}$ denotes inverse Frobenius on the field, computed by the $(b-r)\bmod b$ iterate of squaring. Each entry needs at most $b$ reads of $X$ and $\operatorname{poly}(b)$ local work. Expanding $P$ gives $B(\alpha)=\alpha\sum_{j<u}\operatorname{Tr}(\beta p_j)\alpha^j$. Its degree is at most $u<d$. It represents a codeword of $C$ precisely when $\operatorname{Tr}(\beta p_{u-1})=0$, by uniqueness of polynomial evaluation below degree $d$. Consequently a single genuine decision of $G_b(B)$ recovers this trace bit. For uniform $P$, the leading coefficient remains uniform even after $u-1$ adaptive ordinary symbol probes: the affine space of polynomials with the prescribed values ranges over every leading coefficient. Thus this query computes a bit whose conditional distribution at that query depth was still fair. Choosing a dual basis recovers the full coefficient. After higher coefficients are already known and subtracted, the factor $\alpha^{u-j}$ performs the corresponding test on coefficient $j$; this sequential algorithm uses up to $bu$ full decisions for the complete polynomial.

The cross-coordinate substitution in $B$ requires data-dependent reads by the virtual instance generator that the overwrite and translation models exclude. A full call to the fast native solver still costs up to $O(N)$ before accounting for virtual accesses. No method here amortizes those whole-instance calls across enough proof addresses to attain a polylogarithmic cost per lookup and a short common header on the guarded SAT instance.
