# Average-case lookup hardness in deterministic output-linear time

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Suppose SAT has a deterministic $O(n^k)$ algorithm on the specified packed RAM, where $k\in\{1,2,3\}$. Fix $0<\epsilon<1/k^2$. A deterministic RAM on an all-zero length-$B$ input, $B=2^m$, can print a binary string of length $B$ in $O(B)$ word steps such that, at every sufficiently large arity $m$, every Boolean circuit of size at most $B^{1/k^2-\epsilon}$ errs on more than $1/1000$ of the indices. For $k=1$ this gives a deterministically constructed, constant-error hard table at every exponent strictly below one. The machine's word width is $\Theta(\log B)$. This is a consequence of the time hypothesis, not an unconditional Boolean circuit lower bound.

The construction starts with an explicit hard table that can be written at packed-output speed under the same hypothesis. A local correction algorithm lets us recover any of its bits from an approximate evaluator for the encoded table. The time spent on a query is charged. [Exact hard table and proof](output-linear-hard-tables.md) · [Sudan, Trevisan and Vadhan on reconstruction from encoded functions](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2000.1730)

## Outer polynomial and binary inner code

Put $\alpha=1/k^2$. Choose a sufficiently large fixed integer $r$ with $r\alpha>5$ and $(r+1)\epsilon>3+\alpha$. Begin with arities $m=(r+1)b$. Let $Q=2^b$, use the field $\mathbb F_Q$, take $d=Q/2^{\lceil\log_2(16r)\rceil}$, and use any fixed efficiently indexed set $S$ of $d$ field elements. For sufficiently large $b$, $D=r(d-1)<Q/16$. The message is the exact hard binary table of length $M=d^r$. Interpret its bits as field elements on $S^r$ and take the unique interpolation $P$ of individual degrees less than $d$. The output at $(u,z)\in\mathbb F_Q^r\times\mathbb F_Q$ is the binary inner product between the coefficient vectors of $P(u)$ and $z$ in a fixed binary basis. The total output length is $B=Q^{r+1}$.

Use the quotient by the first irreducible binary polynomial of degree $b$. Trial division by every monic polynomial of degree at most $b/2$ finds it within $O(Q^2\operatorname{poly}(b))$ time. Precompute the field arithmetic and binary inner-product tables, and the $d$ Lagrange weights at every field point. Elementary polynomial arithmetic, even a cubic-in-$Q$ weight calculation with polylogarithmic overhead, fits $O(B)$. Extend one grid dimension at a time by evaluating its $d$-term interpolation dot products, using $O_r(Q^r d)$ field operations in total. Output all inner products in $O(B)$ word steps using the tables. Thus the entire encoding and the source construction fit the claimed bulk cost.

## Local correction

Consider an evaluator with advice length $s$ and time $q$ per encoded bit that disagrees with the output on at most $B/1000$ positions. At a field point $u$, query all $Q$ binary positions and choose the symbol with the nearest binary word. Two distinct inner-code symbols differ on half their positions. Thus whenever the block error is strictly below $1/4$ its symbol decodes correctly. At most a fraction $4/1000$ of the field points can decode incorrectly. Ties and malformed answers have fixed outcomes.

For each message coordinate $x\in S^r$, take a uniformly random direction $v\in\mathbb F_Q^r$ and consider the line $x+tv$ for $t\in\mathbb F_Q$. For every $t\ne0$, this point is uniform, so the expected number of bad symbols on the line is at most $1+4Q/1000$. Markov's inequality makes the chance of more than $\lfloor Q/4\rfloor$ bad symbols less than $1/8$ for large $Q$. The true line polynomial has degree at most $D$. It is uniquely recoverable under this error cap because $D+2\lfloor Q/4\rfloor<Q$.

For completeness, solve the linear equations for a monic error polynomial $E$ of degree $a=\lfloor Q/4\rfloor$ and a numerator $J$ of degree $D+a$ with $J(t)=E(t)y_t$, where $y_t$ are the decoded line symbols. On a good line a solution exists by including all erroneous positions among the roots of $E$. For *every* solution, $J-E(P|_{\mathrm{line}})$ vanishes at at least $Q-a>D+a$ points and hence is zero as a polynomial. Divide to obtain the value at $0$; failed checks on a bad line may return a fixed fallback. This uses $O(Q^3)$ field operations with ordinary Gaussian elimination. A finite check covers 768 line recoveries over the field of 16 elements, including corruption at the queried point. [Reproducible line test](../audits/local_table_correction_experiment.py)

For any fixed $x$ a majority of $O(\log M)$ independent directions succeeds except with probability less than $1/(2M)$ by a binomial tail bound. A union bound fixes **one list of directions** serving all $x$. Put that list, the approximate evaluator's advice, and the field polynomial into one recovery description. Its size is $s+O_r((\log B)^2)$. The online work, without storing any original hard-table bits, is at most
$$
 [Q^2(q+1)+Q^4]\operatorname{polylog} B .
$$
Here there are $Q^2$ bit evaluations per direction, brute nearest-symbol search costs $O(Q^3)$ arithmetic operations per direction, and all field computations can use polynomial-in-$b$ word operations. Separate read-only views and fresh scratch implement repeated decoder invocations.

If $s+q\le B^{\alpha-\epsilon}\operatorname{polylog}B$, the recovery cost and advice together are below $M^\alpha/\operatorname{polylog} M$ by a strict power margin with the chosen $r$. This contradicts the exact table bound. A circuit of the stated size gives such an evaluator. For other arities use $b=\lfloor m/(r+1)\rfloor$ and repeat the table on the remaining most significant bits. Some fixing of those bits preserves an approximator's average accuracy on the original table. Constant-factor padding in $B$ is absorbed in the strict margins above.

## Matrix rigidity and the remaining time question

For $k=1$, view an even-arity output as an $l\times l$ binary matrix, $l=2^{m/2}$. For every fixed $0<\sigma<1$, choose $2\epsilon<\sigma$. A rank-$R$ matrix over $\mathbb F_2$ factors as an $l\times R$ matrix times an $R\times l$ matrix. An indexed-bit circuit can store both factors and select the specified row and column by binary multiplexers, taking $O(lR+R+\log l)$ gates including the inner product. For $R\le l^{1-\sigma}$ this circuit falls below the excluded size. Consequently reducing the rank to $l^{1-\sigma}$ requires changing more than $l^2/1000$ entries. The matrix can be written in $O(l^2)$ word operations. Using the low-bias version of the table instead gives $(1/2-l^{-\delta'})l^2$ required edits for some fixed $\delta'>0$ depending on $\sigma$, in $O(l^2/\log l)$ generation time. In both cases use a slack in the bit-circuit exponent to pay for selecting the factor rows and columns. [Low-bias table and local list reconstruction](biased-hard-table-lookup.md) This argument establishes the claim at all sufficiently large power-of-two dimensions. Prior rigid-matrix constructions use other computational assumptions and have different ranks and distances. [Alman and Chen](https://chen-lijie.github.io/documents/FOCS_2019_rigidmatrix.pdf)

A putative universal theorem that output-linear generation forces easy approximate bit access would thus exclude the corresponding SAT running time. None of the known memory or chronology arguments proves that universal theorem. The construction also does not produce the lexicographically first matrix satisfying a separate rigidity specification. The distinction matters for arguments whose contradiction relies on that particular choice. [Lexicographic-selection problem](fixed-program-parity.md)
