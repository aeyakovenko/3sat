# A linear-size quadratic circuit at a SAT solver's half-read cut

Fix $0<\eta<1/4$ and a packed-word RAM with a specified $\Theta(\log N)$-bit width. For every sufficiently large $m$ there are $N=\Theta_\eta(m\log m)$-bit ordinary 3-CNFs with $2m$ independently pinned data bits. At any fixed good public graph all these variants have the same layout. Every correct deterministic solver reads at least $(2-\eta)m-O(1)$ distinct data pins on every variant. At its first cut after reading $m$ distinct pins, write $S$ for their locations and $C$ for its resumable configuration. For every fixed pair $(S_0,C_0)$, under uniform data,
$$
 \Pr[(S,C)=(S_0,C_0)\ \text{and the suffix rereads at most }
          \lfloor(1/2-\eta)m\rfloor\ \text{distinct pins in }S]
       \le \exp(-\Omega(\eta^2m)).
$$
The hidden constant is absolute for large $m$ depending on $\eta$. In particular on an event of constant probability with this few rereads, the conditional min-entropy of $(S,C)$ is $\Omega_\eta(N/\log N)$ bits. The set of locations itself is counted. This scale has the same order as the total number of data bits. With a prescribed balanced partition there is also a three-way bound below that charges actual work-memory reads. All cuts use a load/store ISA with at most one physical input-word load per native access slot.

The public graph has $O_\eta(m)$ edges. A random sparse graph has the required properties with probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(m))$ for a sufficiently large constant depending on $\eta$. The CNFs contain the ordinary, explicit gate connections. Efficient uniform selection of one deterministic good graph at every length is unnecessary to the result and is not asserted. The polynomial-table construction gives the larger range of simultaneous two-sided probe caps. ## Every bisection of the same sparse graph

Let $G$ have $2m$ labeled vertices and independent edge probability $b=\Delta/m\le1/2$, for $\Delta$ a large constant, and set $d=\lceil3m/\sqrt\Delta\rceil$. At a balanced split the $m$-by-$m$ cross adjacency matrix $M$ has independent Bernoulli entries. A random row belongs to a given subspace of codimension at least $d$ with probability at most $(1-b)^d$. Indeed condition on its entries in an information set for the subspace. The remaining entries are either completely prescribed or no extension exists, and each specified bit costs a factor at most $1-b$. For rank at most $m-d$, some selection of $m-d$ rows spans all the remaining rows. Given the spanning candidates each of the other $d$ rows is independent. A union bound over row sets and over at most $2^{2m}$ bisections gives
$$
 \Pr[\exists\, M:\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}M\le m-d]
  \le 2^{3m}(1-b)^{d^2}\le 2^{3m}e^{-9m}.
$$
This is the Bernoulli subspace estimate and spanning-row argument of Lee, Lee and Oum applied to square matrices. [Lee, Lee and Oum, Proposition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10120245/1/rankwidth.pdf)

The probability of an independent set of size $\lceil\eta m\rceil$ is at most $2^{2m}(1-b)^{\binom{\lceil\eta m\rceil}2}=\exp(-\Omega(m))$ when $\Delta\ge36/\eta^2$. A binomial tail also bounds $|E(G)|\le4\Delta m$ with the same probability. Fix a graph satisfying all three conclusions. Take the Boolean polynomial $Q(u)=\sum_{vw\in E(G)}u_vu_w$ over $\mathbb F_2$. On fixing any coordinates, a surviving free edge retains its coefficient and the restricted function is nonconstant by uniqueness of the Boolean multilinear representation. Thus on any monochromatic subcube the free vertices form an independent set. A deterministic halting path fixes all data coordinates it read, leaving every uninspected bit free, which proves the every-input bound.

Form one AND gate per edge and compute the XOR of those gates by a chain of equivalences. Pin the $2m$ inputs and the requested output sign. The satisfiable pin has a unique extension to all gates. Isolate each data pin from other data pins by a fixed constant number of records so that a physical word meets at most one data bit for the specified machine width. Use copies of fixed clauses in the spacing, introducing no free variables. There are $O_\eta(m)$ clauses with $\Theta(\log m)$-bit names. A globally correct solver can recognize this layout, collect the input pins and evaluate and check each successive gate and its source names in constant word operations, using a general SAT fallback otherwise. It takes $O_\eta(m)$ steps on these inputs. Both truth pins have the same lower/upper order in elapsed time.

## Exact one-sided spectral norm

Fix any bisection and write the sign of $Q(x,y)$ as $g(x)h(y)(-1)^{x^\top My}$, where the first factors contain the within-side edges. Let $U=\operatorname{im}_{\mathbb F_2}M$ have dimension $k$. Let $B_p$ be the Hamming ball of radius $p$ in $\mathbb F_2^m$ and $L=2^m$. With a prefix independent of $y$ the matrix of the spectral cut test has rows $x$, columns $(y,J)$, $J\in B_p$, and entries $f(x,y)(-1)^{J\cdot x}$. Its normalization is exactly
$$
 \frac{\|K\|^2}{L^2}
   =\max_{v\in\mathbb F_2^m}\frac{|(v+U)\cap B_p|}{2^k}
   \le 2^{-k}\sum_{i=0}^{\min(k,p)}\binom ki.                         \tag{1}
$$
Remove the separable row and column signs. Each column is a Walsh character with index $My+J$, of Euclidean length $\sqrt L$. All distinct indices are orthogonal. A frequency $v$ occurs $2^{m-k}|(v+U)\cap B_p|$ times. This proves the identity. Projection on $k$ pivot coordinates sends uniform points of any affine coset of $U$ to uniform $k$-bit strings. A string of weight at most $p$ projects to weight at most $p$, proving the inequality. For a coordinate subspace the upper bound is attained by the zero coset.

Choose $\Delta\ge 36/\eta^2$ so every cut has $k\ge(1-\eta)m$ at large $m$. At $p=\lfloor(1/2-\eta)m\rfloor$ the gap $k/2-p$ is at least $\eta m/2$. A binomial tail in (1) proves $\|K\|/L\le\exp(-\eta^2m/4)$. For an actual cut cell $(S_0,C_0)$ use $x=S_0$, $y=S_0^c$. For fixed $x$, all $y$ or no $y$ reach this cell, since before it the solver has read only $x$ and public layout. Start the suffix at $C_0$, giving it all $y$ and stopping on more than $p$ distinct $x$ reads. For each $y$ its signed answer or abstention has degree at most $p$ in $x$ by expanding decision-tree leaves. The normalized correlation bound gives the displayed probability, since the assertion is correct whenever both the cell and suffix tests succeed. The bound with no probe from either side also shows the two signs are balanced up to exponentially small error.

Allowing at most $r$ queried bits of $y$ in the prefix adds Hamming characters $I\in B_r$ indexing row blocks. Each such block is the one-sided matrix times a diagonal matrix of column signs. The normalized norm is at most $\sqrt{|B_r|}\exp(-\eta^2 m/4)$ by stacking. Thus for any fixed $\eta$, some constant $\rho_\eta>0$ allows $r=\lfloor\rho_\eta m\rfloor$ and $p=\lfloor(1/2-\eta)m\rfloor$ with exponentially small acceptance probability for any fixed sound suffix description in the two-sided test. For example choose $H_2(\rho_\eta)\ln2<\eta^2/4$.

This also applies when the suffix can inspect genuine work memory adaptively. For a fixed balanced partition, on all but $\exp(-\Omega_\eta(m))$ of the data strings, at any cut within a prescribed polynomial native clock, the prefix exceeded $r$ right reads, the suffix exceeded $p$ left reads, or the suffix needs $\Omega_\eta(m/\log N)=\Omega_\eta(N/\log^2 N)$ distinct work addresses before its first overwrite to each. Indeed expanding the capped suffix in word characters of the incoming work array multiplies the correlation bound by at most $\sqrt{\sum_{i\le t}\binom{U_0}i(2^w-1)^i}$ for $t$ incoming addresses in a universe of $U_0$ words of $w$ bits. The logarithm of this factor is $O(t\log N+\log(t+1))$ on the fixed RAM. Choose a small constant in $t$ and take the polynomial union over times and register states. The addresses may carry initially zero words. This fixed-partition statement permits a different exceptional set for each partition. ## Charging the state alone when the read order varies

For a reachable half-read configuration $C_0$ define $P$ as the union of pins read up to that cut over *all* data inputs reaching $C_0$, and $R$ as the union of all subsequent pin reads on all such inputs. Time, if observable, belongs to the resumable state. Set $J=P\cap R$. Write $B$ for the complement of $P$ in the $2m$ coordinates, $A=P\setminus J$, and $M_{A,B}$ for this rectangular adjacency submatrix. Then for uniform data and the fixed public graph,
$$
 \Pr[C=C_0]\le 2^{-\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb F_2}(M_{A,B})}
       \le 2^{-m+d+\alpha(G)+2|J|}.
$$
Here $\alpha(G)$ is the maximum independent-set size, and an exponent above zero gives only the trivial probability bound. This version charges neither an address directory for $S$ nor a public choice of the split.

The event $C=C_0$ is a union of reaching prefix cylinders, each depending only on pins in $P$. Hence its truth depends only on $P$. Fix the values on $J$. For any two assignments to $A$ which reach the state, and every fixed assignment to $B$, both continuations start in the same state. They see identical values on $R\subseteq B\cup J$ and can read no other data pin on these runs. Their outputs coincide. For the quadratic verdict, subtracting the two restrictions as polynomials in the free variables $B$ gives linear part $(x_A+x'_A)^\top M_{A,B}$. Equality on every assignment to $B$ forces this row vector to vanish. The permitted $A$-assignments therefore lie in one coset of the left kernel, proving the first inequality after averaging over $J$. For the second, take one reaching prefix with actual $m$-set $S$. All coordinates of $A\setminus S$ are unread on that input both before and after the cut, so they form an independent set. Therefore $m\le|P|\le m+|J|+\alpha(G)$. Use as the left side of a balanced split any $m$ coordinates in $P$. Retain only its rows outside $J$ and delete the $|P|-m$ columns belonging to the rest of $P$. Rank drops by at most $|J|+|P|-m$, yielding the bound.

If every genuine run makes zero post-cut rereads of its own $S$, then $J$ is empty at *every* reachable state. Fix a reaching prefix and any reaching suffix that would query one of this prefix's pins. Up to the first such query, the suffix reads data only outside the prefix. Combine the values fixing the prefix with those observations. Determinism realizes the suffix from the same $C_0$ through the offending query, contradicting the assumed property on this hybrid data input. Taking the union over prefixes proves disjointness. Thus for every such solver the state $C$ alone has min-entropy at least $m-d-\alpha(G)\ge(1-2\eta)m-O(1)$ bits at the half-read cut. The statement also bounds the mass of any state with small $|J|$, even when other states have unrestricted overlap. A bound on the number of rereads along one selected input does not by itself bound the union defining $J$.

## All parities on one specified split

One can specialize the two-lane CNFs to encode arbitrary right selector pins $y_j$ and left lanes $a_i=(0,x_i)$ with the identity permutation. The sign is inner product $(-1)^{x\cdot y}$ up to fixed pins. On the more general source-order layout the register-only solver can read a diverse selector pin $y_{\pi(i)}$ directly at each source record. An interface that withholds those bits until the cut has a measurable cost. [Source-order scan and the original matching](single-cut-transfer-graphs.md)

Suppose no right input bits are available until the cut. Afterward an $s$-bit message from the left and at most $p$ adaptive probes of left bits must compute the exact inner product for **every** $y$. The sharp redundancy bound up to $O(\log(m+2))$ bits is
$$
 m-\log_2|B_p|\ \le s_{\min}\ \le m-\log_2|B_p|+O(\log(m+2)).
$$
For each message fiber $F$ and each $y$, the Walsh character on $F$ agrees with a real multilinear polynomial of degree at most $p$. The full set of characters restricted to $F$ spans all real functions on $F$, by Walsh inversion after extending a function by zero. Hence $|F|\le |B_p|$ by dimension even for nonlinear messages and adaptive probes. Sum over the at most $2^s$ messages. For the upper bound take a linear code $D$ with $D+B_p=\mathbb F_2^m$. Starting at zero, if a fraction $u$ of points is still uncovered, adding a suitable generator reduces that fraction to at most $u^2$ by averaging over translates. After $s$ generators the uncovered count is at most $2^m\exp(-2^s|B_p|/2^m)$. Thus at most $m-\log_2|B_p|+O(\log(m+2))$ generators suffice, also using the trivial full-dimensional code if this exceeds $m$. Send each generator's parity on $x$. Given $y$, choose $z\in D$ with $|y+z|\le p$. Use the message to compute $z\cdot x$ and probe the remaining positions. This is a small-message, unlimited-local-computation bound. [Systematic linear data structures and rigidity](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2020.35) · [Linear covering construction](lex-factor-responses.md)

For the two-sided test on this specified split there is also an exact reduction to the concentration of the Walsh transform. The lifted sign entry is $(-1)^{x\cdot y+I\cdot y+x\cdot J}$. Change row labels to $(x+I,I)$ and column labels to $(y+J,J)$. The exponent becomes $(x+I)\cdot(y+J)+I\cdot J$. Consequently $\|K\|/2^m=\|P_{B_r}H^{\otimes m}P_{B_p}\|$, where $H=2^{-1/2}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ is the normalized single-bit Walsh matrix. The uncertainty threshold for Hamming balls is known. For completeness, write $D_s=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{-s})$. Weighting the two balls gives an upper bound $e^{sr+tp}\|D_s H D_t\|^m$ for $s,t\ge0$. Put $E=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$. The squared two-dimensional norm for tilts $s=\lambda u,t=\lambda v$ has expansion $1-2\lambda\lambda_{\min}(uE+vHEH)+O(\lambda^2)$ as $\lambda\downarrow0$. This follows by multiplying the matrix by its transpose and bounding the operator-norm remainder. The smaller eigenvalue is $(u+v-\sqrt{u^2+v^2})/2$. Hence at fixed $\alpha=r/m,\beta=p/m<1/2$, choosing $(u,v)=(1-2\alpha,1-2\beta)$ gives exponential decay whenever $(1-2\alpha)^2+(1-2\beta)^2>1$. The elementary tensor calculation here only proves the indicated decay regime. Partitions separating pairs in a different way can have zero cross-rank for this inner-product example. [Polyanskiy and Samorodnitsky on the uncertainty threshold](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.07491)
