# A free game on columns and lines of verifier seeds

A locally computable PCP with $N=2^r$ equiprobable tests, at most $\ell$ distinct proof bits per test, perfect completeness and soundness $1-\delta$ gives a two-prover free game. After duplicating each test once if $r$ is odd, write $N=Q^2$. Each answer has at most $\ell Q$ bits. A suitable fixed slope gives independent questions of exactly $\log_2 Q$ bits each and soundness at most $1-\delta^2/(24\ell^2)$ for $\ell\ge1$. A slope that works for all strategies exists without any degree bound on the proof addresses. Under polynomial-time SAT it is deterministically findable in time polynomial in the description, the single-test evaluation time and $r$. Without that hypothesis a private random slope on the right still gives a constant fraction of the stated gap. Square-root communication for 3SAT via birthday repetition is established. The construction here addresses sparse randomness, local enumeration and unbounded proof-address degrees together; priority for the particular transformation is unestablished. [Aaronson, Impagliazzo and Moshkovitz](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/ammm.pdf) · [Manurangsi and Raghavendra](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.02986)

Identify the seeds with points $(x,y)\in\mathbb F_Q^2$. On a fixed public slope $a$, ask the left prover for the local answers to *all* tests in one uniform vertical column $I$. Ask the right prover for one value for every distinct proof address queried by the tests on one independent uniform line $J=\{(x,ax+c):x\in\mathbb F_Q\}$. Require every test in $I$ to accept and compare all common addresses. The left prover may answer an address differently in different tests; the right prover must provide a single answer for each address in its line. Enumerating two lists of $Q$ tests, sorting their address occurrences and scanning equal-key runs suffices. This avoids enumerating pairs of tests or computing inverse neighborhoods. Binary field representations of the required degree have deterministic polynomial-time construction. [Shoup](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

## A good direction for every strategy

Write $A(s)$ for the distinct proof addresses at test $s$ and $d_z$ for the number of tests mentioning $z$. For the proof only, define

$$
 K(s,v)=\sum_{z\in A(s)\cap A(v)}1/d_z,\qquad
 C_{IJ}=\sum_{s\in J,\,v\in I}K(s,v).
$$

The symmetric matrix $K$ has row and column sums at most $\ell$, including the diagonal. Put $f_I(s)=\sum_{v\in I}K(s,v)$. Then $f_I(s)\le\ell$, $\sum_s f_I(s)\le\ell Q$ and $\sum_{I,s} f_I(s)\le\ell N$. Two distinct seeds share a right bucket for at most one of the $Q$ slopes. Expanding squares therefore gives

$$
 \mathbb E_a\sum_{I,J}C_{IJ}^2
 \le \sum_{I,s} f_I(s)^2 + Q^{-1}\sum_I\left(\sum_s f_I(s)\right)^2
 \le 2\ell^2 N .
$$

Fix a slope achieving this bound. A deterministic left strategy provides one assignment per test, since the columns partition the seeds. If at least $\delta N/2$ local assignments fail their tests, at least a $\delta/2$ fraction of columns always reject. Otherwise round every address to its majority among the left occurrences. This global proof fails at least $\delta N$ tests, so there are at least $\delta N/2$ minority occurrences. At an address appearing $d$ times, with label counts $d_0,d_1$, the ordered pairs of opposite labels have total weight $2d_0d_1/d\ge\min(d_0,d_1)$.

For each conflicting triple $(s,v,z)$, send its weight $1/d_z$ to a rejecting pair of questions. Use the unique right line $J$ through $s$. The right answer for $z$ conflicts with at least one of its two left occurrences. Use the left column of a conflicting endpoint. Charges for a conflict at $s$ sum to at most $\ell$ in a given cell $(I,J)$ since $|I\cap J|=1$. Charges at $v$ sum to at most $C_{IJ}$. If $D$ question pairs reject, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

$$
 (\delta N/2)^2 \le D\sum_{I,J}(\ell+C_{IJ})^2 \le 6D\ell^2N .
$$

There are $N$ question pairs. This proves the claimed gap; convexity covers randomized strategies. At least half the slopes have energy at most $4\ell^2N$, giving gap at least $\delta^2/(80\ell^2)$ when the right question privately includes a uniform slope. Neither prover needs the proof-address degrees.

One can use an existentially specified slope when certifying a negative PCP instance. Every slope has value one on positive instances. Under $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$ the fixed direction can also be computed. Use deterministic constant-factor approximate counting with a fixed level of the polynomial hierarchy on the short description of the test map. Approximate each positive degree $d_z$ within a factor two, round to the nearest padded power of two, and clamp to $[1,N]$, obtaining $h_z$ with $d_z/4\le h_z\le4d_z$. Replacing each $d_z$ by $h_z$ changes the energy by a factor at most 16 in either direction. To estimate the new energy, expand its squares over ordered seed pairs $s,t$ on a common line, $v,w$ in a common column, and addresses $z\in A(s)\cap A(v)$, $z'\in A(t)\cap A(w)$. Append two integers in $[0,N)$ and require them to be below $N/h_z,N/h_{z'}$, respectively. The number of resulting witnesses divided by $N^2$ is exactly the modified energy. A second constant-factor count gives a deterministic estimate within a factor 32 of the original. Accept slopes estimated below $65\ell^2 N$. Some slope passes, and any passing slope has energy $O(\ell^2N)$. A prefix-existence search finds one in polynomial time on the short input under the hypothesis. The weaker constant in the resulting gap is absolute. [Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1137/0214060)

**Fewer coins for larger answers.** With a fixed good slope, merge each consecutive group of $L$ column identifiers and each group of $L$ intercept identifiers, for a power of two $L\le Q$. The left answer covers all tests in its columns. The right answer gives consistent values for the union of addresses in its lines. Any coarse strategy induces a fine strategy by restricting its answers to the desired single column or line. An accepting coarse question pair implies acceptance of all $L^2$ constituent fine pairs. The constant gap therefore persists, and sorting the two combined lists checks all constraints in $LQ\operatorname{poly}(r)$ time for a constant-arity, locally computable PCP. No quadratic loop over the constituent questions is needed. At $L=2^{\lfloor(\alpha-1/2)r\rfloor}$, $1/2\le\alpha\le1$, the total verifier randomness is $2(1-\alpha)r+O(1)$ bits and the answer lengths are $O(\ell N^\alpha)$. Selecting the slope remains a separate cost as described above.

## Instantiating the pinned source

For a specified color, use a language of implicit inputs of length $M=2^j$ whose bits are ignored. Its length-$M$ slice is either the whole cube or empty according to the priority bit $b_j$. A nondeterministic multitape verifier for the fixed $O(M)$-time native computation guesses a chronological transcript, verifies each CPU step and sorts memory events on tapes by address and time to check each read. Its time is $M\operatorname{polylog} M$ including word operations and copying counters. Reject nonpowers of two. A proof of proximity consequently gives exact soundness for the requested color, since the negative slice is empty. Theorem 2.6 of Ben-Sasson et al., with a fixed sufficiently small positive exponent, gives $r=j+o(j)$ random bits, constant query complexity and a $\operatorname{poly}(j)$-time verifier. The implicit input answers are just zero; duplicate or unused proof queries can be removed. If queries are adaptive, unfold the constant-depth constant-alphabet query tree before defining $A(s)$. [Ben-Sasson et al., Theorem 2.6](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/polylogPCP.pdf) · [Pinned source](priority-pinned-sat.md)

The resulting local game uses $M^{1/2+o(1)}$ verifier time and communication, with $j+o(j)$ random bits for a fixed good direction or $3j/2+o(j)$ for the randomized direction. A general left strategy still lists order $N$ local replies across its $Q$ possible questions. These parameters do not establish a sub-$M$ deterministic computation or an unrestricted SAT lower bound.

## Two obstructions to simpler tests

**A predetermined column–row partition.** At each off-diagonal seed $(x,y)$ ask for one bit at address $\{x,y\}$ and require it to equal $[x<y]$, in any fixed total order on the field. Diagonal tests always accept. Every proof fails $(N-Q)/2$ checks. On a horizontal line of fixed $y$, the right prover can agree with all occurrences on that line. Cross-checks then fail only against column $y$, so soundness gap is at most $1/Q$. For a nonzero slope the map $x\mapsto ax+c$ is a permutation. Its nonloop edges are directed cycles. Except for the identity map it has at most one fixed point. Every right answer must disagree with a vertex cover of this undirected conflict graph, hence with at least $(Q-1)/2$ columns on such lines. This example distinguishes a guaranteed intersection from a guaranteed contradiction.

**The raw final-register tableau.** For each cut $t<H$ take the wrong-pin spliced assignment. Keep the actual row-$t$ input, memory event, and private transition wires. Following the cut, use the idle halted output and reroute the memory records. Every clause outside the row bundle $B_t$ is satisfied. The real local answers within $B_t$ differ from this assignment only at variables in the next controller $U_{t+1}$. A clause-side prover can answer individually from the real local labeling on $B_t$ and the spliced assignment elsewhere. A variable-side prover always uses the spliced assignment. For independent, arbitrarily structured challenge sets, rejection requires hitting $B_t$ on the left and $U_{t+1}$ on the right. If the expected numbers of distinct such bundles touched on each side are $u,v$, the respective marginal probabilities $p_t,q_t$ satisfy $\sum_t\sqrt{p_tq_t}\le\sqrt{uv}$. Some fixed cut therefore gives rejection probability at most $uv/H^2$. Even a left challenge requiring one consistent assignment to its whole set of clauses has gap at most $u/H$ by using the common spliced assignment whenever $B_t$ is missed. This concerns raw local incidence checks; the PCP transformation changes those checks.
