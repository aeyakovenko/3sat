# Certifying output coverage while a shared computation stays hidden

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A slow preparation may choose all the lower-degree coefficients of a map on the unread program suffix. A single public coefficient can prove that every output occurs even if the verifier never obtains the preparation's result. Several packets of programs can also give a joint coverage certificate when each packet separately could miss the target. Both arguments need a sound description of what the preparation can change.

## A coefficient that survives preparation and faults

Fix a packet of equally long program names. Split the free suffix into a face index and $s$ active bits $x$. On each of $L$ disjoint faces suppose the promised suffix evaluator outputs a public injective encoding of the $r$ Boolean values

$$F_i(x)=H_i(x)+Q_i(x),\qquad 1\le i\le r.$$

Use arithmetic over $\mathbb F_2$. Each $H_i$ is a public homogeneous multilinear polynomial of degree $d_i\ge1$, and $Q_i$ has degree strictly less than $d_i$. The latter coefficients may be arbitrary functions of the shared preparation and the face. Even preparation that depends on the active bits is allowed if the **composed** error $F_i-H_i$ has that degree bound. Assume $\sum_i d_i=s$ and the coefficient of $x_1\cdots x_s$ in the ordinary product $\prod_i H_i$ is one. Each encoded label then has at least $L$ preimages in the promised evaluator.

For a label $y$, form its fiber indicator $J_y=\prod_i(1+y_i+F_i)$. The sum of a monomial over the Boolean cube vanishes in $\mathbb F_2$ unless it uses every variable. Since $\deg J_y\le s$, only the squarefree monomial of degree $s$ can contribute. Its coefficient comes entirely from $\prod_i H_i$. Thus each face has an **odd** number of preimages of every label. For sparse $H_i$ the coefficient is the parity of the number of ways to pick one monomial from each polynomial so that their supports partition the active variables. For example, one disjoint monomial block per output suffices. This is an application of the finite-grid coefficient formula. [Schauz, *Algebraically Solvable Problems*](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/734)

If at most $e$ named rows can disagree with the promised map, including timeouts, at most $\lfloor e/L\rfloor$ of its $2^r$ labels can disappear. The nominal fibers are disjoint and erasing any one costs at least $L$ disagreements. The estimate is sharp up to the number of labels, by taking the identity on the active bits, repeating it on each face, and suppressing all copies of selected labels. No probability or independence assumption on the errors is needed. For an actual program implementing one of these maps, a syntactically bounded common preparation can be arbitrarily slow within its cutoff. A separate clock argument must guarantee completion on all but the charged rows.

## Joint parity checks when no individual row is guaranteed

Fix a target and $N$ named rows. Each legal setting $K$ of the shared preparation defines a Boolean vector $v_K$ marking its producing rows. Assume the distinct vectors form a family of size $H\ge1$. The target is guaranteed exactly when none is zero. In that case there are $t\le\lfloor\log_2(H+1)\rfloor$ public binary masks such that **every legal setting has odd hit count on at least one mask**. The appropriate mask may depend on $K$. On the current nonempty family of $h$ distinct nonzero vectors, the average number left undetected by a uniform random mask is $h/2$. The zero mask misses all $h$, so some mask leaves at most $\lfloor(h-1)/2\rfloor$. Repeating proves the bound. It is attained for the $2^t-1$ nonzero vectors in a $t$-dimensional subspace, since fewer masks have a common nonzero kernel there. This is the critical number of the represented binary matroid. For example, the three patterns $10,01,11$ force coverage jointly but admit no single always-odd mask. [Nomoto and van der Pol on the critical number](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1511229)

One can also describe the tests with a short field element. If rows are numbered $0$ to $N-1$, form $P_K(X)=\sum_j(v_K)_jX^j$. Over a binary field of order greater than $H(N-1)$, a single evaluation point makes every nonzero $P_K$ evaluate nonzero. Indeed each has at most $N-1$ roots. For $N\ge2$ a field of degree $\lceil\log_2(HN)\rceil$ suffices, and a modulus describing this field and the point together take $O(\log(HN))$ bits. For $N=1$ use the identity mask. Evaluating the field fingerprint checks a collection of binary parity conditions jointly. This is a certificate of existence for a common point. Checking its guarantee still needs access to the legal family or to an identity such as the coefficient computation above.

## Application to the first omitted word

For the native auxiliary interpreter with $M=2^n$, $B=M^{20}$, let $v$ be its first omitted word. Suppose packets and their error bounds have a uniform, sound fixed-block verifier of size and construction time $T\operatorname{poly}(n)$. The verifier must justify how the promised evaluator describes the physical rows by $B$. Certify a target as produced when a packet with $e<L$ includes it in its output encoding, or when a batch of actual rows admits verified masks with the joint property above. Under linear SAT, $T\le B/n^a$ for sufficiently large fixed $a$ lets a decoder query the unique certified membership table within $B$. Guess the $M$ membership bits, universally select an entry, and enforce its fixed-block definition or its negation according to the guessed bit. On the guessed table compute the trailing-window scores and ranks in $M^2\operatorname{poly}(n)$ steps as in the trailing-interval lemma. Asking for a particular bit of the point of specified rank adds one deterministic check to this quantified test. The $n$ queries needed to print that point cost $(M^2+T)\operatorname{poly}(n)$ under linear SAT. The decoder does not have to recover the $M$ table entries one at a time. The certified set excludes $v$. Consequently under linear SAT there must be a constant $c>0$ such that every sufficiently large trailing interval $[v-k,v)$ contains at least $ck$ true predecessors outside this set, with an additive constant allowed for the discrete endpoint. This is the existing trailing-interval lemma applied to a concrete sufficient algebraic certificate. A template whose simple default key is legal already covers each of its key-independent guaranteed labels at that default setting. The missing step is to certify this algebraic structure or an adequate joint parity condition for enough genuine programs whose common preparation and control flow are unrestricted. I found no such certificate. The finite check enumerates 1,024 overlapping-quadratic maps with hidden affine perturbations and all 127 nonempty families of nonzero vectors in three dimensions.
