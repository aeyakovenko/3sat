# Certifying a satisfying assignment by differences along descending paths

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** In a not-all-equal (NAE) clause it suffices to certify that two specified literals differ. This gives an alternative to supplying a small circuit for all the bits of an accepting history. The certificate presents equations between unknown bits and checks that the equations have a common solution. The equations can determine values that would be PSPACE-hard to recover from the presentation. One additional comparison can also make the feasibility question itself PSPACE-complete.

## A certificate for succinct NAE-3SAT

Let a circuit enumerate the three signed variable names in a clause given its binary index. An occurrence (v,s) has value A(v) XOR s. Variable names occupy designated vertices of a larger finite domain of binary integers. A candidate circuit specifies directed edges v→u labeled by a bit b, interpreted as A(v)=b XOR A(u). It may use additional vertices. Require that every edge go to a strictly smaller integer. A walk's label is the XOR of its edge labels.

Require the following conditions.

* For each pair v→u and v→w with labels a and b, the certificate joins u and w by directed walks to a common vertex. The two walk labels, after including a and b respectively, must agree.
* For each clause, select two different occurrence positions (v,s),(w,t). Join v and w by walks to a common vertex. The XOR of their two walk labels must be 1 XOR s XOR t. An empty walk is allowed. Distinct positions may contain the same variable.

**Soundness.** A descending walk can always continue to some sink. At each vertex all walks ending at a sink have the same total label. Prove this by induction on the vertex number. Given two different first edges, join their targets using the first condition, and extend the join to a sink. The induction hypotheses at the two targets compare each original suffix with its joined extension. The common tail contributes the same bit on both sides. At a sink the empty walk has label zero. Assign A(v) the resulting sink-walk bit. Every edge equation holds, hence every selected literal pair differs. This is a labeled form of the local confluence argument for terminating rewriting systems. Applying ordinary local confluence to states (vertex, accumulated bit) is another proof. [Newman](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968867) · [DifferenceWitness.lean](../lean/DifferenceWitness.lean)

**One explicit SAT call, even for long joins.** Guess circuits for the edge predicate, pair selection, and the joins. A join circuit receives the fork or clause index and a binary position in a bounded walk. At each position it reports the two current vertices and one bit of *relative* accumulated difference. Its step report supplies the two labels and whether either walk pauses. A pause preserves its vertex and contributes zero. Pad to a fixed common length with binary-length index fields large enough to cover the candidate walks. Check the initial vertices, the final common vertex and an initial difference of a XOR b for forks, or 1 XOR s XOR t for clauses. At each step the difference must change by the XOR of the two step labels. The final difference must be zero. These checks require no circuit for either individual prefix sum. Deleting pauses leaves exactly the walks used in the soundness proof. Check the name domain at every endpoint and the edge predicate on every unpaused step. A wrong step, endpoint, selected position, or descending-order assertion has an existentially indexed error. Compare endpoint indices before incrementing so wraparound cannot create an unchecked transition.

Let S bound the total gates plus interface widths of the clause enumerator and certificate circuits, including the walk counters. Descriptions in topological order and the explicit Tseitin formula for any error take Õ(S) bits. Under deterministic SAT time O(mᵈ), a nondeterministic verifier guesses the descriptions and accepts on an UNSAT answer in U+Õ(Sᵈ) time, where U is the charged cost of generating the clause enumerator and parameters. The exponent is in the encoded SAT input length. An ordinary size-s witness is a special case: create a fresh least vertex and join each variable to it by one edge labeled with its witness bit; pick any unequal pair using the witness circuit. Names can be shifted by one to reserve the root. Zero-length and one-step joins suffice. The certificate may also work when these bit circuits are unavailable.

There is a second option when the graph has short joins but no known circuit for selecting them. For a specified fork or clause, the existence of appropriate explicit walks of at most k steps is an NP test with a SAT formula of Õ((k+1)S) bits, charging all graph tests. An outer existential search for a fork or clause that fails this test can use the deterministic SAT algorithm internally and a second quasilinear RAM-to-SAT compilation. The resulting verification cost is U+Õ(((k+1)S)^(d²)) for constructible polynomial-clock parameters. This method needs the short-walk premise on YES instances, never an assumption on the size of a shortest contradiction on NO inputs.

**Hierarchy implication.** Use a nondeterministic tape language with clock T=2ⁿ outside time T/n². The local reduction of Jahanjou, Miles and Viola yields a small circuit enumerating its T poly(n) clauses. Replace each ordinary clause (l₁,l₂,l₃) by NAE(l₁,l₂,y) and NAE(¬y,l₃,z), with one new private y and a common fresh z. Complementing all bits preserves NAE, so z can be set to zero for the proof of equisatisfiability. The enumerator only adds index decoding and names of O(log T) bits. For any fixed constructible α<1/d with positive α, size-T^α difference certificates on every sufficiently long YES input would contradict the hierarchy under the SAT hypothesis. Verification uses a single SAT call and the nondeterministic RAM-to-tape simulation by transcript sorting. Soundness on NO inputs is unconditional. This identifies a sufficient mathematical premise about the particular hierarchy reduction. Its availability has not been proved. [Local reductions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3171) · [Hierarchy and simulation](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf) · [Schaefer's framework for satisfiability](https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350)

## Remove the need to index even the joining walks

Instead guess a Boolean relation J(v,w,c) and a progress circuit. The intended obligation is A(v) XOR A(w)=c. Require J(u,w,a XOR b) for the two targets of every fork, and J(v,w,1 XOR s XOR t) for the chosen pair in each clause. For every triple in J, require one of the following.

1. Its vertices are equal and c=0.
2. The progress circuit chooses one edge or a pause on each side. At least one side descends. If the new vertices are u,t and step labels are a,b, require J(u,t,c XOR a XOR b).

The second alternative may also be used when the vertices coincide. The sum of the two vertex numbers decreases strictly in that alternative. Induction on this sum gives walks to a common vertex with the promised total difference for *every* triple in J. The fork and clause proofs then apply exactly as before. Verify these local implications by searching for a single existential error. The SAT input contains only a constant number of copies of the circuits and the index arithmetic, so the U+Õ(Sᵈ) clock still applies without a run-time or space bound on evaluating a vertex's actual bit. In particular it no longer requires a circuit for the jth pair of vertices of a long path. Lean verifies this step in `progress_joins`.

If outgoing edges are given by a fixed number of explicit parent functions with enable bits, the progress circuit is optional: test the disjunction over all pairs of enabled moves and pauses in the local error circuit. For a general succinct edge predicate without an enumerator, finding a progressing successor for a specified triple is an NP test on binary vertex names. A deterministic SAT solver supplies the search in polynomial time when such successors exist. Alternatively checking whether any triple lacks a successor uses the two nested SAT calls above with no walk-length factor. The quantitative difference matters for the targeted clocks. Small J remains an additional hypothesis.

## Test on a pair of long traces

Take two disjoint chains with N edges ending at the same root, with identical label yᵢ on the ith edge down either chain. For each parent edge impose its XOR equation by a repeated-literal NAE clause. To impose A(v) XOR A(u)=b use NAE(A(v), A(u) XOR (1 XOR b), A(u) XOR (1 XOR b)). Add a clause forcing the two top values to agree. The descending graph needs only its parent edges. Its fork joins are trivial. Each edge clause has a one-step join, with one side pausing. For the extra clause move down the two chains together, using a zero relative accumulator at every time. Binary counters give both vertex names. The size is polynomial in log N and the description of the label circuit, without evaluating parity(y).

If y is an arbitrary truth table accessed as individual bits, any deterministic computation of the top-to-root value difference needs all N probes in the worst case. Each local check of the proposed joins uses O(1) such probes. The entire formula always has a satisfying assignment; this tests a *method for certifying* long runs with common data. It also illustrates how a non-tree consistency condition can be discharged when an individual walk's running state is difficult. [Earlier grid construction](parity-fillings.md)

A stronger example changes even the sequence of vertex names. At a positive counter t, make both copies of (t,c) point on their own track to (t−1,F(c)), with bit g(t,c). At t=0 let both tracks point to the same root with equal prescribed labels for equal c. F and g can be arbitrary small circuits. The cross-track J relates corresponding configurations at relative bit zero; its successor moves both sides using the same F. Include equality obligations and direct single-edge obligations for the parent clauses, progressing the latter by moving just the child. The union of these cases is a small circuit. Hence equality between the two top values can be certified for every initial configuration using only one-step circuits, without fast access to iterates of F. Computing F iterated an exponentially large, binary-specified number of times can simulate a polynomial-space machine, as in the next construction. This is a certificate of equality between two computations, so their common answer need never be evaluated.

## Synthesize a comparison relation from a bounded observation window

For two deterministic step circuits F₀,F₁ with observable bits g₀,g₁, let Jₖ(c₀,c₁) say that the next k observed bits match, starting with the current observation. Assume k≥1. The graph at a paired positive countdown emits these bits and applies the two step circuits; the two terminal edges lead to one root with the same fixed label. There is a direct test for whether the guessed window works on *all* state pairs:

    Jₖ(c₀,c₁) implies g₀(F₀ᵏ(c₀)) = g₁(F₁ᵏ(c₁)).

Unroll k+1 ordinary steps on each side and ask SAT for a counterexample. Also check that the two requested starting states pass Jₖ. If both tests pass, equality for the next k observations implies equality for the next shifted k observations. Induction gives equality throughout any common countdown, so the full paths have zero relative difference. This constructs the progress relation from the original circuit descriptions. A hypothetical O(mᵈ) solver costs Õ(((k+1)s)ᵈ) for size-s step/observation circuits with the state widths charged. The truth of the window implication is checked, not assumed from a small number of samples. A long trajectory and large number of distinct states are both permitted.

For example, if an observed bitstream in each implementation is generated by a k-bit shift register that outputs its leading bit, the initial k observations expose the logical register. When both implementations have the same feedback rule on that register, the next bit agrees on every agreeing window, even if their physical state encodings differ. A common feedback rule need never be recovered separately by the test. Conversely take two copies of a countdown machine emitting a single one just at expiry and then only zeros. For every k shorter than its available delay, two states can agree on k zeros and then differ. The universal window test fails, although equal starting configurations in the two copies still have the small equality relation from the previous example. The remaining opportunity is a relation valid only on the configurations needed by the hierarchy reduction and cheap enough to check inductively.

## How difficult is extracting an ordinary assignment?

Already for a succinct *tree* of mandatory XOR equations, the following assertion is equivalent to PSPACE ⊆ P/poly: every such polynomially presented family with a decreasing parent function and polynomial-bit vertex names has a polynomial-size circuit for some satisfying assignment, with a common polynomial bound in the presentation length. Arbitrary inputs use a fixed explicit circuit encoding. Every such forest has small difference certificates using its given edges, independent of the answer to this extraction question.

For the upper direction, iterating the parent from a given vertex and accumulating one parity bit takes polynomial space. Stop at a root, choosing its value as zero. A single PSPACE predicate takes the graph description and initial vertex, so a nonuniform circuit for it can be specialized to a graph. The same argument works for a general valid descending presentation by searching all smaller vertex names and labels for the first available edge in polynomial space. The joins ensure the resulting answer is independent of the choices.

Conversely fix any deterministic polynomial-space decider. At input length n encode its configurations in k=poly(n) bits, allowing room for the input itself and absorbing halting outcomes. Extend its transition to a total polynomial-size circuit F on k-bit strings, with a fixed choice on malformed encodings. There are at most 2ᵏ distinct configurations. Use vertices (t,c) for every configuration and 0≤t≤2^(k+1), plus a least root r. For t>0 set the parent to (t−1,F(c)) and label the edge zero. At t=0 point to r with the bit indicating whether c is accepting. Ordering by time then configuration gives strict descent. All vertices connect to r and each nonroot has one parent, so the undirected graph is a tree. Its potentially exponential family of clauses is indexed by circuits of size poly(n). For every input x of length n, the XOR between its designated top vertex (2^(k+1),init(x)) and r equals the machine's answer. A polynomial-size satisfying assignment circuit yields a polynomial-size decision circuit for this language, regardless of the unknown root bit. This proves the implication even for a tree and repeated-literal NAE clauses. Succinct graphs as a means of representing long computations are classical. [Galperin and Wigderson](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/ABSTRACT/gw84.pdf)

If SAT∈P, the universal extraction assertion in fact implies P=PSPACE. For each of the constructed formula presentations, a candidate assignment circuit can be checked by SAT for a violated clause. With a common polynomial size cap, existence of a good completion of a circuit prefix is then in NP=P. Search for a full circuit and evaluate it on the two designated indices. Conversely P=PSPACE supplies the uniform parent-walk computation. Thus the gap between a local certificate and an evaluator for the underlying tape bits can be stated precisely without assuming a circuit lower bound.

## Attack the last unresolved crossing condition

To the tree for a given x add just one repeated-literal clause demanding XOR one between the top and r. The new instance is satisfiable exactly when the polynomial-space machine accepts x. With the tree given by its parent and label circuits and both vertices specified, this one-extra-equation problem belongs to PSPACE by following the two walks. It is therefore PSPACE-complete already on the single-root promise. All the other edges have mandatory labels and need no assignment circuit. In a descending graph with a single sink, existence of a potential implies compatible fork joins of some length: follow both targets to that sink. For multiple sinks the join test is sufficient and can fail on consistent labels. Even a single additional comparison can contain the entire decision problem. Counting the number of such comparisons cannot establish their cheap verification. The synchronized-chain example explains why it can still matter *which* two walks are compared.

In the ordinary-clause gadget above there is a concrete way to reduce the number of comparisons involving the anchor z. Fix a satisfying original assignment and z=0. If the three original literal values are mixed, choose y equal to l₃. The first NAE triple is mixed, and in the second triple ¬y and l₃ differ, so its selected pair can avoid z. If all three originals are true, y must be zero in the first triple, and both other literals in the second triple are true; any opposing pair there uses z. Thus the minimum number of selected second-triple pairs using z for this fixed assignment is exactly the number of original clauses with three true literals. This is a statement about the selected comparisons; it leaves their circuit descriptions and path costs open.

A proof for the unrestricted SAT clock needs a hierarchy verifier whose satisfiable outputs have sufficiently small, uniformly indexable differences and joins, or a different way to exploit their absence. The parent-tree completeness example prevents inferring those joins just from sparsity or local syntax. The simulation and extraction results are manuscript arguments; Lean checks existence of the potential from descending edges and compatible forks. The [finite check](../audits/difference_experiment.py) exhausts 4,096 descending signed graphs on four vertices, including parallel edges of opposite sign. All 241 graphs passing the join check had a potential; for all 2,835 single-sink graphs the two tests agreed. All eight literal patterns passed the anchor-count check. Priority of this application has not been established.
