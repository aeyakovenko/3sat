# Solving cubic parity files with few changed vertices

Consider an explicit loopless cubic multigraph with one Boolean variable per edge. Originally each vertex contributes four proper 3-clauses on its three incident edges, excluding the four triples of one parity. At a changed vertex a single literal of one of those four clauses has its sign flipped. All other clauses stay in place. Write $d$ for the number of changed vertices, $n$ for the encoded bit length, and $\nu$ for the matching number defined below.

A deterministic logarithmic-word RAM decides these inputs in
$$
 O\big(n+(d+1)2^{d-\nu}\big)
$$
executed steps. The algorithm takes linear time whenever $(d+1)2^{d-\nu}=O(n)$, in particular up to $d=\log_2 n-\log_2\log_2 n+O(1)$. It also produces an equivalent ordinary proper 3-CNF with $O(d+1)$ clauses, dense variable names, and $O((d+1)\log(d+2))$ encoded bits in $O(n)$ time. Both statements allow the support graph, the original parities and the locations of the changes to depend on the input.

## Recovering the graph and the original parities

The names in each source clause are distinct. Canonicalize their order and read the signs of its three literals as the unique forbidden triple. Require four distinct forbidden triples within each putative group of four clauses. Four triples of the same parity describe an unchanged vertex. Otherwise, after a permitted change, three distinct forbidden triples have one parity and one has the other. The missing triple $b$ of the majority parity was previously forbidden. The newly forbidden triple $a$ must differ from $b$ in exactly one coordinate $j$. This checks the promise and identifies both the original parity and $j$ uniquely. The original *allowed* parity is opposite the forbidden majority.

For grouped files check equal supports within each block and exactly two incidences for every edge variable. Parallel edges are allowed. Self-loops would repeat a variable at a vertex and are excluded by proper clauses. To accept undistinguished clause order as well, intern variable-name strings with a fixed-alphabet trie and assign consecutive machine-word identifiers as they are encountered. Canonical sorting within a triple takes constant time. Three stable counting-sort passes on the triples group equal supports in $O(n)$ time. A support bucket must contain four or eight clauses. An eight-clause bucket represents two vertices sharing all three edges, hence an isolated component. If recognizing the promised class, check the constant number of splits into two groups of four for at least one valid split. Count the three edge names twice in the global incidence check. On this isolated component test all eight assignments directly, independent of which valid split was chosen. The algorithm can discard such solved pairs before counting $d$ and computing its matching. The same preprocessing works if the groups are supplied and their vertices form such a pair. For the stated bound, discarded defects can be included in $d$ and $\nu$, since deleting an isolated component removes at most one matching edge per two removed vertices.

This parsing charges time to the actual ASCII bits, including unusually long decimal names and whitespace. Equivalent spellings with leading zeros can first be normalized if the source accepts them. Dense binary fields can also be interned by scanning their bits. The standard RAM has $\Theta(\log n)$-bit words and enough addresses for $O(n)$ words of scratch. Declared but unused DIMACS names need no entries.

## Removing every unchanged vertex

Fix the other two edge values at a changed vertex. Before the edit the original XOR uniquely determined the bit on edge $j$. Switching the parity-forbidden triple $b$ for its neighbor $a$ toggles this output on exactly one of the four possible input pairs. The resulting Boolean function on the other two edges has truth-table weight one or three, hence is an AND with possibly complemented inputs and output. Fixing either input gives a unary Boolean function of the other. All four unary functions are affine over $\mathbb F_2$.

Delete the $d$ changed vertices from the graph for the moment. In every connected component $K$ of unchanged vertices the internal edges can be eliminated, leaving just one condition
$$
 \bigoplus_{e\in\partial K} x_e=\bigoplus_{v\in K} b_v ,
$$
where $b_v$ is the original allowed charge and the boundary edges have exactly one endpoint in $K$. Summing the vertex equations gives necessity. For sufficiency, subtract the prescribed boundary values at their endpoints, assign zero to internal edges outside a spanning tree, and eliminate leaves. The root equation is precisely the parity condition displayed above. A closed component decides immediately by its charge. This is the usual component-parity criterion for Tseitin systems. [Itsykson, Riazanov and Smirnov, Lemma 2.12](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol236-sat2022/LIPIcs.SAT.2022.6/LIPIcs.SAT.2022.6.pdf)

After a linear-time traversal, keep the changed factors and the conditions for components with a nonempty boundary. There are at most $3d$ remaining edge variables, at most $3d$ component conditions, total boundary length at most $3d$, and $d$ local factors of arity three. Edges directly between changed vertices remain common variables at the two factors.

## The number of distinct inputs to fix

Form a multigraph on the changed vertices. Include an edge precisely when it joins two changed vertices and is one of the two inputs at both ends. Its maximum degree is two. Traversing its paths and cycles finds a maximum matching of size $\nu$ in $O(d)$ steps, including a two-edge cycle of parallel edges. Each matched edge supplies the pinned input to both endpoints. Choose one input at each unmatched vertex. In total $t=d-\nu$ distinct edge variables suffice. This is the smallest number for the strategy of choosing at least one designated input per gate. Indeed any choices serving two gates at once pair disjoint vertices after each gate designates just one chosen input. Those pairs are a matching.

Enumerate all assignments to these $t$ variables by depth-first branching. No counter with $2^t$ possible values is required. For each assignment substitute every fixed occurrence, including other gates' outputs. At a gate the output now equals a constant or equals the other input up to negation. Include its resulting single affine equation and the boundary parity conditions. Dropping fixed variables and adding their contributions to right-hand sides preserves the total incidence bound $O(d)$. Each free edge variable occurs in at most two remaining equations. A constant gate may drop a formerly incident input, giving a variable with only one occurrence.

Make a graph of equation rows. Draw an ordinary edge for a twice-occurring free variable and a dangling edge at its row for a once-occurring variable. A connected component with no dangling edge is feasible if and only if its right-hand sides sum to zero modulo two, by the same spanning-tree proof. In a component with a dangling edge one may set all other dangling edges to zero and choose this edge to balance the total charge. This also handles isolated rows and rows with no remaining variables. Each branch therefore costs $O(d+1)$. It accepts exactly when the global constraints admit an extension.

The [finite check](../audits/few_cubic_edits_audit.py) compares the contracted decision algorithm with complete edge-assignment enumeration on 1,000 generated cubic networks, including parallel edges and disconnected components. It independently enumerates matchings and choices of pinned inputs to check the exponent.

## Linear-time compression to native 3-SAT

The contracted system before pins already gives a kernel. Retain the four original proper clauses at each changed factor. For each boundary condition encode its XOR by a chain of binary XOR gates with distinct fresh output variables, a direct equation for length one or two, and the required final charge. A gate $z=x\oplus y$ uses the four proper clauses excluding the wrong parity. Short one- or two-literal clauses can be padded with the usual two or four sign choices on globally reserved fresh names. A boundary list has no repeated edge variable. Each new name is distinct from the inputs it accompanies. The chain introduces $O(1+k)$ variables and clauses for boundary length $k$; summing gives $O(d+1)$. Use fixed proper yes and no formulas for constant outcomes. Number the names actually used consecutively. Writing their decimal strings gives $O((d+1)\log(d+2))$ bits.

This kernel takes $O(n)$ RAM steps to compute and write on the explicit source format. Indeed the $3d$ changed-port occurrences contain at least $3d/2$ different edge names. Distinct decimal identifiers have total digit length $\Omega(d\log(d+2))$ for growing $d$, and each occurs in the original file. The contracted edge names can be replaced by dense indices during traversal, and all decimal output names may be tabulated successively with a decimal counter in time proportional to the output size.

Consequently, suppose a deterministic $O(n)$-time transformation of **every** proper 3-SAT instance of length $n$ to an equivalent file of this form guarantees $d\log(d+2)=o(n)$ uniformly over the source instances. Apply the kernel afterward to obtain proper 3-SAT again. For all sufficiently large lengths this composition produces at most $n/2$ bits in $O(n)$ time. Iterating and adding the geometric time costs, then solving the bounded remaining lengths directly, gives a deterministic $O(n)$-time algorithm for ordinary proper 3-SAT. The parity supports may encode source-specific information; computing the compression from the actual file has already charged for reading them. A reduction promising merely $O(n)$ residual bits gives no strict size decrease. This implication identifies a quantitative limit of reducing general SAT to a sublinear number of changes, and supplies no unconditional lower bound on unrestricted SAT time.
