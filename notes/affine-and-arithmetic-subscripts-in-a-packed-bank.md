# Hidden affine maps and native arithmetic subscripts of a packed bit bank

Let \(v=2^k\). The \(v\) data bits \(X_i\) are independent, and the parameters determining the addresses are also inputs. Fix \(0<\epsilon<1/2\) and put \(\alpha=1/2+\epsilon\). The November finite-Fourier preprint gives \(O_\epsilon(v(1+k)^\alpha)\) linear gates for the binary-index Walsh transform over \(\mathbb F_5\), and the same count of arithmetic gates for length-\(v\) formal polynomial multiplication over \(\mathbb F_2\). We use its upper bounds. [Finite Fourier exponent, Theorem 1 and polynomial convolution corollary](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/build/intro.tex)

1. For a hidden binary \(k\times k\) matrix \(A\) of any rank and hidden vector \(s\), the projected relation \(Y_i=X_{Ai+s}\) has a proper ordinary 3-CNF of \(O_\epsilon(v(1+k)^{1+\alpha})\) physical bits. A packed RAM prints the formula in \(O_\epsilon(v(1+k)^\alpha)\) steps. Each raw bit is locally computable in \(\operatorname{poly}_\epsilon(k+1)\) time, with extension degree \(O_\epsilon(1+\log(k+1))\) in its binary physical address on each of polynomially many public intervals.
2. For a hidden scalar \(s\), the functions \(X_{i+s},X_{s i}\) (native modular arithmetic), and \(X_{\lfloor i/s\rfloor},X_{i\bmod s}\) for \(s>0\) have deterministic Boolean circuits of \(O_\epsilon(v(1+k)^\alpha)\) gates. For every fixed degree \(D\) the same holds for **\(X_{P(i)\bmod v}\)** with independently hidden native coefficients of the entire integer polynomial \(P\) of degree at most \(D\). This includes addresses computed by multiplying two different live affine expressions in \(i\). Gate-size bounds alone cost another \(O(k+1)\) physical bits per gate under ordinary DIMACS names.
3. Independently of strong uniformity of the full polynomial-convolution circuit, a fixed-degree integer-polynomial gather has locally generated ordinary proper 3-CNFs of \(o(v(1+k)^2)\) bits with the same \(O(\log(k+2))\) piecewise raw degree. The compiler is detailed below. For a separately verified, slower-than-Fourier cyclic shift exponent, only an explicit finite algebraic rank calculation is needed. [Finite cyclic shift construction](mixing-bits-for-an-unknown-cyclic-shift.md)

These banks have an independent input value at every possible address. Clauses pinning that data or computing the controls elsewhere must also be counted when used. Historical priority for the combinations here is unestablished.

## A hidden binary matrix, including a singular one

Write \(H_{i,j}=(-1)^{i\cdot j}\) over \(\mathbb F_5\), so \(H^2=vI\). For symmetric \(B\) let \(H_B(i,j)=(-1)^{i^\top B j}\). Orthogonality gives
\[
        (v^{-1}H_B H x)_i=x_{B i},
\]
including when \(B\) has a kernel. Define an integer modulo four and a nonzero field value by
\[
 q_B(i)=\sum_j B_{jj}i_j+2\!\!\sum_{j<l}B_{jl}i_ji_l\pmod4,\qquad K(i)=2^{q_B(i)}\pmod5 .
\]
Expansion at the binary sum \(i+j\), and \(2^2=-1\bmod5\), give \(K(i+j)/(K(i)K(j))=(-1)^{i^\top Bj}\). Hence \(H_B\) is a diagonal \(K^{-1}\), XOR convolution by \(K\), and a second diagonal \(K^{-1}\). XOR convolution is two forward Walsh transforms, pointwise multiplication and one inverse Walsh transform. Finite-field multiplication and inversion on the four permitted nonzero values take a constant number of Boolean gates. To compute the \(v\) phases with hidden coefficients, for highest set bit \(j\) and lower mask \(a<2^j\) use
\[
 q_B(2^j+a)=q_B(a)+B_{jj}+2\bigoplus_{l<j}B_{jl}a_l .
\]
For each \(j\), its whole parity list costs \(O(2^j)\) XOR gates by the same highest-bit recursion. The total phase preparation is \(O(v)\).

Every square \(A\) over \(\mathbb F_2\) has a factorization \(A=B C\) into two symmetric matrices. Indeed there is an invertible symmetric \(J\) with \(JA=A^\top J\). Then \(B=J^{-1}\), \(C=JA\). To see the underlying form directly on a cyclic companion block \(\mathbb F_2[z]/(p)\), pair \(u,w\) by the coefficient of \(z^{\deg p-1}\) in \(uw\bmod p\). For nonzero \(u\) of degree \(l\), pairing against \(z^{\deg p-1-l}\) is nonzero. Multiplication by \(z\) is self-adjoint. Take orthogonal sums over the rational canonical blocks and change basis. The projected checker simply guesses symmetric \(B,C\) and verifies their product against the actual \(A\) with \(O(k^3)\) gates. Do the \(B\)-gather followed by the \(C\)-gather. A hidden additive binary offset can be applied to the input table first with a Walsh-diagonal-Walsh shift. Thus all auxiliary factorizations passing the checks give exactly the requested values. [Taussky and Zassenhaus, Theorem 1](https://msp.org/pjm/1959/9-3/pjm-v9-n3-p25-p.pdf)

For the physical statement use the dyadically batched uniform Walsh templates. The extra phase wires are indexed by fixed parity-row \(j\), highest set bit of the varying submask, and its remaining low bits. All varying operand references append, set, or remove fixed bits; they are affine on the slab. One copy of the polynomially many factor-verification gates costs polynomial setup. Fixed-width digit names, fresh operand copies, proper truth-table clauses, aligned slabs and the shared decimal-expansion table give the bounds in item 1. [Local shift template and packed printing proof](locally-encoding-an-unknown-xor-shift.md)

There is also a block version. Every invertible even-dimensional binary matrix is in the special linear group and is a product of at most six alternating block unitriangular matrices. A block shear \((u,z)\mapsto(u+Lz,z)\) is one independently controlled XOR shift per \(z\)-slice. All slice controls may be precomputed in \(2^{k/2}\operatorname{poly}(k)\) gates. Fixed-width uniform slices also permit \((u,z)\mapsto(u+f(z),z)\) for an arbitrary \(f\) supplied at this cost. This gives hidden nonlinear shears without asserting the six-factor theorem for nonlinear maps. [Urschel, block unitriangular factorization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.00485)

## Modular address arithmetic

A cyclic shift of a length-\(v\) bit table by a hidden integer is polynomial convolution against a one-hot monomial in \(\mathbb F_2[z]/(z^v-1)\), using its negative exponent for the forward pullback convention. A binary prefix tree decodes the monomial in \(O(v)\) gates and a cyclic fold costs \(O(v)\). Any modular rotation/reflection of indices by a public amount is wiring.

For odd \(c\), partition the nonzero indices by their 2-adic valuations. On the shell \(i=2^{k-r}u\), \(u\) runs over odd units modulo \(2^r\). For \(r\ge3\), write each such unit uniquely as \((-1)^e 5^j\), \(0\le j<2^{r-2}\). The usual formula \(v_2(5^{2^l}-1)=l+2\) follows by repeated squaring. The powers of five give exactly the units congruent to 1 modulo 4. Thus multiplication by \(c\) is a sign swap and a cyclic shift of the exponent. Compute the exponent controls for \(c\) in \(\operatorname{poly}(k)\) gates: first pick its sign from its class modulo four, then at stage \(l\) test whether appending exponent bit \(l\) is required to match modulo \(2^{l+3}\), using public repeated squares. Smaller shells reduce this control modulo their periods. The cases \(r=1,2\) have fixed size. The total shift cost is \(O_\epsilon(\sum_{r=1}^k2^r(1+r)^\alpha)\). Relabeling the data for all shells uses only public wires. This relabeling, on its own, does not give the physical-degree assertion of item 1.

For \(s=2^h c\ne0\), split off its trailing zeros with polynomially many control-only gates. Gather by \(c\) and then by \(i\mapsto2^h i\bmod v\). Implement a hidden left or right bit shift on the **address** by composing the \(O(\log(k+1))\) fixed powers controlled by the bits of \(h\); each stage is a choice of two public wires at every lane. The cost is \(O(v\log(k+1))\). A zero multiplier just repeats \(X_0\). Coordinatewise AND, OR, XOR by a shared word, and logical or sign-extending shifts with shared amount can also use the affine-address theorem or these controlled fixed stages.

For positive division let \(q_*=\lfloor(v-1)/s\rfloor\), \(h=v_2(s)\), \(c=s/2^h\). The inverse odd scalar can be computed modulo \(v\) by bit lifting. Put
\[
 j(i)=((i\mathbin{\mathrm{div}}2^h)c^{-1})\bmod 2^{k-h}.
\]
This gather is a fixed composition of the preceding ones: truncating to the low \(k-h\) bits is a left shift by \(h\) followed by an unsigned right shift by \(h\), both on addresses. The true multiples of \(s\) in range are exactly \(i\) for which the low \(h\) bits vanish and \(j(i)\le q_*\). Indeed then \(i=sj(i)\bmod v\) with \(0\le sj(i)<v\). Precompute the unary table of \([j\le q_*]\) with \(O(v)\) gates and gather it together with \(X_j\); a vanishing low-bit condition on public \(i\) only compares the shared \(h\) to its fixed trailing-zero count. Thus no fresh \(k\)-bit comparator per output is required.

Make an array of impulses carrying \(X_{j(i)}\) at these multiples and zero elsewhere. Its ordinary truncated convolution with the indicator of \([0,s)\) gives \(X_{\lfloor i/s\rfloor}\) with exactly one contribution in each output. Conversely convolve the bare impulses of value one with the initial portion \(X_i[i<s]\) to get \(X_{i\bmod s}\), also with exactly one contribution. Here the mask of \([0,s)\) is obtained in linear gate count by the same unary decoding. A zero divisor can select a separately specified zero, identity or maximum-address convention at \(O(v)\) extra gates. The estimates include the control computation. [Other operand order, including the reverse quotient and remainder](reverse-native-quotient-and-remainder-tables.md)

## All fixed-degree integer polynomials by one recursive call

Take \(m=\lceil k/2\rceil\), write \(i=a+2^m u\) with \(0\le a<2^m\), and split the output address into its low and high parts. The binomial expansion gives
\[
 P(a+2^m u)\equiv P(a)+2^m P'(a)u\pmod{2^k}.
\]
The low part is \(P(a)\bmod 2^m\). Apply that same smaller polynomial gather on the low coordinates of every fixed high slice of \(X\). The resulting array is \(Z[h,a]=X[2^m h+(P(a)\bmod 2^m)]\). For each \(a\) separately, pull its high column back by the hidden modular affine map
\[
                 u\ \longmapsto\ \lfloor P(a)/2^m\rfloor+P'(a)u
                       \pmod{2^{k-m}} .
\]
This is a cyclic additive shift and a native multiplication, allowing zero and nonunits. The \(2^m\) control pairs cost only \(2^m\operatorname{poly}_D(k)\) Boolean gates at this level. There is one *kind* of recursive operation on the low part and a direct nonrecursive transform on the high part. If \(L(k)\) is total cost divided by \(2^k\), then \(L(k)\le L(\lceil k/2\rceil)+O_{\epsilon,D}((1+k)^\alpha)\), after enlarging the fixed base range. The geometric series proves item 2. Arbitrary compositions of a fixed number of these full gathers also have the same size. A general second independently computed table used as an address operand is outside this argument.

## Strong local generation without full-size convolution wiring

For large \(k\) choose a digit width \(b=\max(1,\lfloor\tfrac14\log_2\log_2(k+4)\rfloor)\) and radix \(B=2^b\). For \(j\ge1\), a digit at bit \(t=jb\) in an index with lower prefix \(a<2^t\) has address update
\[
       d\ \longmapsto\ \lfloor P(a)/2^t\rfloor + P'(a)d\pmod B .
\]
Higher index bits do not affect this digit; use nonnegative coefficient representatives modulo \(2^k\) to compute its residue. The bottom digit has just the separate map \(d\mapsto P(d)\bmod B\). As maps of indices update higher digits before lower ones to use the original low prefix. As operations on data tables perform their pullbacks in the reverse order. A top digit shorter than \(b\) would leave too many prefixes for the following estimate. Instead for \(k\ge2b\) give the **bottom** digit between \(b\) and \(2b-1\) bits, and use \(b\)-bit digits everywhere above it. The first upper digit then meets the divisibility \(2t\ge t+b\). All digits are of width at least \(b\).

For each upper digit prepare all its needed control pairs in \(O_D(k2^t+\operatorname{poly}_D(k))\) gates. To do this with no slowly growing hidden factor, tabulate \(P(a)\) and \(P'(a)\) at consecutive integers modulo \(2^k\) by forward differences. Their lists of at most \(D+1\) differences at \(a=0\) cost polynomial initialization using hidden coefficients. The update is parallel addition of each difference to its successor in the usual difference triangle, namely \(\Delta^jP(a+1)=\Delta^jP(a)+\Delta^{j+1}P(a)\). Each is a \(k\)-bit addition of linear Boolean size. The total preparation over digit positions is \(O_D(kv/B+\operatorname{poly}_D(k))\). For the bottom digit, repeat one local verifier across all higher indices. To keep its size subcritical even when its width varies, use the **polynomial circuit of item 2 at this little width**, of size \(O_{\epsilon,D}(B_0(1+\log B_0)^\alpha)\) for its actual radix \(B_0< B^2\). The upper digits use little modular-affine circuits of the same size per bank with their two shared controls.

It suffices to find these bounded-width templates deterministically. Exhaust ordinary two-input Boolean circuits up to an elementary \(O_D(2^r\operatorname{poly}_D(r))\) bound for inner width \(r\) and compare complete truth tables, for the common multioutput map (a circuit must give **all** outputs correctly on each setting of the data and controls). Directly evaluating \(P(d)\) or an affine control for each little public \(d\) and using switches is one existence bound of polynomially more than \(2^r\) size via a standard sorting network on \(O(2^r)\) records of \(O(r)\) bits. Alternatively enumerate in increasing size until the first success; item 2 bounds the search length. Time including the exhaustive data assignments is at most \(2^{O_{\epsilon,D}(2^r\operatorname{poly}_D(r))}\). Even for the bottom digit of width \(<2b\), the stated choice of \(b\) keeps the search \(\operatorname{poly}_{\epsilon,D}(k+1)\). Hidden polynomial coefficients need only their bottom \(r\) bits in that kernel. A total of
\[
 O_{\epsilon,D}\!\left(v\left(1+\frac{k}{(1+b)^{1-\alpha}}+\frac{k}{2^b}\right)+\operatorname{poly}_{\epsilon,D}(k+1)\right)
\]
gates suffice. The unbounded saving survives this choice of \(b\).

Each of the polynomially many little kernel roles uses a fixed gate tag and arrangement of high/low spectator bits, with arbitrary public constants only at its own small bit positions. The typed forward-difference table updates refer to adjacent \(a\)'s and a fixed bit position within a \(k\)-bit adder. Split a transition with \(a>0\) by the position of its least set bit, so \(a\) and \(a-1\) are affine on its varying lane bits. The \(O(1)\) carry wires of each bit position in ripple addition use separate roles. Initial differences have only polynomially many gates with individual descriptions. Fixed literal copies, canonical decimal names, and sorted aligned dyadic slabs then give \(O(\log(k+2))\) physical raw degree, polynomial local bit indexing and packed generation linear in the number of gates plus polynomial setup. This proves item 3. Using the deterministic polynomial-in-list-length printer for the fixed binary field, one may instead take \(b=\lfloor c\log_2(k+2)\rfloor\) for any fixed \(c>0\). Generating each little modular-affine or fixed-degree polynomial circuit costs \(2^{O_{D,\epsilon}(b)}=\operatorname{poly}(k+1)\), including the explicit unit-shell permutations and the recursive layout. This gives \(O_{D,\epsilon}(vk/(\log(k+2))^{1-\alpha})\) gates at the same piecewise degree and local access cost. [Fixed-field gate-list printer and its dependencies](printing-finite-field-convolution-kernels-for-native-addresses.md)

A prescribed \(o(v/k)\) sparse list of arbitrary exceptional reads can be added while retaining \(o(vk)\) gates. [Sparse read/write compiler](sparse-batch-lookup-with-position-certificates.md)

For the fixed unary hierarchy source at clock \(t=\Theta(vk)\), the generated local polynomial-bank relations take \(o(t)\) native time to print and \(o(t)\) nondeterministic packed time to check. Thus an exact physical reduction to only a bounded number of such relations with interfaces of \(o(t)\) total preparation and verification cost cannot exist on every length of that source. ## A finite binary-address construction independent of the Fourier exponent

A direct rank calculation also gives a qualitative exponent \(\theta<1\) for hidden XOR shifts. This changes the data field and the address labels in the finite cyclic-shift construction, whose label schedule, batching and accounting we use. With \(h=22,\ v_0=\binom h3=1540\), put address vectors \(t_T=1_T\) in \(\mathbb F_2^h\), with the standard dot product. Each has norm one. Over the **data field** \(\mathbb F_3\), use the center matrix \(M_{S,T}=(|S\cap T|-1)/2\). It factors through \(h+1\) center coordinates by incidence and a constant coordinate. Its diagonal is one. Off the diagonal every nonzero coefficient occurs on an even intersection of size zero or two. Those two distinct address lines are orthogonal over \(\mathbb F_2\). Use a distinct side auxiliary for each such ordered pair, weighted so its net matrix is \(I-M\). [Finite network and complete tensor label schedule](mixing-bits-for-an-unknown-cyclic-shift.md)

In the eight-update local computation, subtract the old side and center sums from the target, add the source contributions into the auxiliaries, add the new sums to the target, and subtract the contributions from the auxiliaries. The result adds precisely the source into the target and restores the auxiliaries. The inverse update in the middle of the three-coordinate construction subtracts. The final physical bank pair is \((-Y,X)\), so one constant sign correction gives a swap. All pointwise data gates have constant Boolean cost. The address spaces and projections now live directly in binary characteristic: tensor line factors are nondegenerate, and each nesting or orthogonal-pair decomposition has the same dimensions. An orthogonal complement is only required to be nondegenerate; it need not have an orthonormal basis.

With \(m=h^3\), the sum \(S\) of binary edge ranks and the number \(W\) of independent scalar data roles satisfy \(Wm-S=2v_0^3-6v_0^2(h+1)h=104350400>0\). A paired endpoint difference is \(I\), including the case \(I-2P=I\) in the address field. On \(m\) digits of \(f\) bits each, a rank-one edge difference \(u v^{\mathsf T}\) induces a binary vector shift of length \(2^f\), shared across its fibers, after a public binary change of basis. All components of its hidden parameter \(v^{\mathsf T}s\) are XORs of the input digit vectors. A rank-\(r\) difference factors into \(r\) such terms. Fiber batches of power-of-two size give the same normalized recurrence with multiplier \(<m\). No carries occur. There is a fixed \(\theta<1\) and an explicit \(O(2^k(1+k)^\theta)\)-gate Boolean circuit. Constants may be large. The direct construction prints its full list in \(2^{O(k)}\) deterministic time by enumerating the fixed-template batches and their public binary coordinate permutations.

This also gives a qualitative bound for arbitrary hidden binary affine maps without using the Fourier exponent. Factor a square matrix as \(P\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)Q\) with \(P,Q\) invertible. Guess the factors and verify their invertibility and product over \(\mathbb F_2\) in polynomially many gates. Pad an odd dimension by one coordinate of identity and duplicate the input bank at that coordinate. For each invertible even-dimensional factor, guess the six block shears and verify their product. On each half slice a shear is one unknown XOR shifter as above. The padded rank projection with hidden \(r\) can use \(O(v)\) gates: for each fixed \(i\), its truncated index is the ancestor of \(i\) at depth \(r\) in a binary trie ordered low bits first. Start at depth zero, and propagate a single selected input bit down each edge, replacing at depth \(r\) by the input labeled with that prefix and zeros elsewhere, otherwise retaining the parent. The \(r=0\) and full-rank endpoints follow the same rule. Thus the projected affine relation uses \(O(v(1+k)^\theta)\) gates with an elementary binary-data construction and the cited six-factor theorem. Bounded fan-in Boolean gates and the ordinary dimension doubling cover all ranks.

## Hidden finite-field cubics

For a fixed public polynomial basis of \(\mathbb F_{2^k}\), an unknown \(P(x)=a x^3+b x^2+c x+d\) has a full-bank projected checker with the same gate count and physical profile as item 1. The case \(a=0\) is binary-affine, since squaring is binary-linear. Otherwise let \(h=b/a\), \(\eta=c/a+h^2\), and choose \(g=1\) if \(\eta=0\), \(g=\sqrt{\eta}\) otherwise. In characteristic two,
\[
 P(x)=P(h)+ag^3\left(y^3+[\eta\ne0]y\right),\qquad y=(x+h)/g.
\]
Inverting or taking a square root of a nonzero shared field parameter and computing its binary multiplication matrix takes \(\operatorname{poly}(k+1)\) Boolean gates. The two outer and inner field-affine actions are hidden binary affine maps. The middle is a public cube or a public cube-plus-input map, selected by one bit. Both functions have binary coordinate degree at most two in the public lane address. Composition here means full bank gathers, and remains exact for collisions of images. A growing-degree representation of \(P\) is outside this argument.

To print all references \(i\mapsto i^3\) in linear packed time, traverse the binary prefix tree of the varying lane. Toggling basis bit \(e_j\) changes the cube by \(i^2 e_j+i e_j^2+e_j^3\). For each \(j\) split the binary-linear part into lookups on two half-words, using \(O(2^{\lceil k/2\rceil}\operatorname{poly}(k))=o(2^k)\) setup. The update then costs constant word work. The fixed quadratic degree adds only a constant to the offset-indicator bound. The field modulus can be supplied publicly, or constructed uniformly in deterministic polynomial time in this fixed small characteristic. This covers arbitrary hidden cubic coefficients despite the demonstrated expense of covering a cubic store schedule by low-rank affine pieces.
