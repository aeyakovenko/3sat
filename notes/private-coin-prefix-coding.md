# A private-coin prefix program for each frequently sampled word

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Let $S$ be an ordinary sampler and suppose it prints $y$ by $t=2^q$ with probability $p>0$, where $|y|\le q$. There is a fixed randomized decoder and a program of at most $\lceil-\log_2 p\rceil+O_S(1)$ bits that prints $y$ and consumes **exactly** those program bits with probability at least $0.99$. Its successful time bound is
$$
 t\,p^{-3}\,\mathrm{poly}(q+\log(1/p)+2).
$$
The input program supplies neither the sampling deadline nor the probability. The decoder has an independent, private stream of fair bits and one sequential program input with no end-of-input signal. The program is chosen independently of the private coins. For $p\ge(q+2)^{-a}$ with fixed $a$, its time is $t\,\mathrm{poly}_a(q+2)$. This is an unconditional reduction of the linear SAT attack to derandomizing short programs that already have a single high-probability answer.

Resource-bounded probabilistic coding and the use of encoded cumulative probabilities are established subjects. Lu and Oliveira derive probabilistic representations from samplers, and Hirahara, Lu and Nanashima treat optimal coding for randomized time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity under different parameters. Here the running time can grow polynomially with $1/p$, and the source publishes at most one word across all deadlines. The exact-prefix, constant-bit and near-deadline combination is the property being tested. Priority is unestablished. [Lu and Oliveira](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2021.94) · [Hirahara, Lu and Nanashima](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12744)

## One ordered distribution

Use the [unconditional numeric sampler construction](sampling-at-a-numeric-length.md). For a fixed sampler $W$, integer constant $c\ge1$ and fixed projection, some $n\le(t/p)\,\mathrm{poly}(q+\log(1/p)+2)$ projects to $y$ and has
$$
 \mu_n=\Pr[W\text{ publishes }\mathrm{bin}(n)\text{ by }cn]\ge c_0p
$$
for a constant $c_0>0$. Use one sufficiently large $c$ for all targets. The positive integer output uses canonical binary encoding. Put $F(0)=0$ and $F(v)=\sum_{1\le u\le v}\mu_u$. The time inside each summand is its own $cu$. The sum over all $u$ is at most one since one run publishes at most once.

At parameters $j,d\ge1$ estimate **all** $F(v)$ for $0\le v\le2^j$ simultaneously. Set $\varepsilon=2^{-d}$. Use $M=\lceil C4^d(j+d+1)\rceil$ independent runs of $W$ through $c2^j$. Credit only a canonical integer $u\in[1,2^j]$ that meets its individual deadline $cu$. The empirical prefix proportions $E(v)$ have means exactly $F(v)$. A Bernoulli tail bound and a union bound over $2^j+1$ endpoints give
$$
 \Pr[\max_v|E(v)-F(v)|>2^{-d}]\le 2(2^j+1)e^{-2M4^{-d}}.
$$
Taking a fixed large $C$ makes the sum over all $j,d$ smaller than $0.01$. The bounded simulation can reject oversized outputs by their length. Counting and sorting, or an array histogram and prefix summation, give the whole table in $2^{j+2d}\,\mathrm{poly}(j+d+2)$ native time. Processing parameter pairs by increasing $j+2d$ costs at most $2^h\mathrm{poly}(h+2)$ through score $h$. Couple all stages to disjoint private random bits, including stages the run never reaches. From here on use the event that every table is accurate.

## Consuming the right number of program bits

For the target atom let $\mu=\mu_n$, $l=F(n-1)$, $r=F(n)$, $z=(l+r)/2$. Put $b=\lceil-\log_2\mu\rceil+5$ and use the $b$-bit dyadic cell $I$ containing $z$, with half-open cells. Its width is greater than $\mu/64$ and at most $\mu/32$. The decoder reads a three-bit header for $b\bmod 8$ and then that many bits of cell address. In subsequent steps it either reads another eight bits, waits for the next table, or publishes. The successful name is the header followed by the $b$ actual address bits. Its definition is deterministic even at dyadic midpoints.

For the current cell $J=[L,R)$ and table, test the following.

* If $E(v-1)+\varepsilon\le L$ and $R\le E(v)-\varepsilon$ for some $1\le v\le2^j$, finish by projecting $v$.
* If $L<E(v)-\varepsilon$ and $E(v)+\varepsilon<R$ for some $0\le v\le2^j$, read eight more program bits and continue on the subcell in the same table.
* Otherwise advance to the next table.

On an accurate table a successful first test certifies inclusion in the true atom. A successful second test certifies a true boundary strictly inside the current cell. Along the designated input, every cell visited contains $z$ before halting. Every proper ancestor that can be tested has width at least $256|I|>4\mu$. Such a cell cannot be certified inside any atom, since $z$ is interior to the $n$-atom and that atom is too small. The final cell is interior to that atom, so the second test can never read beyond the chosen name.

By the table with $j=\max(1,\lceil\log_2 n\rceil)$ and $d=\lceil-\log_2\mu\rceil+5$, every remaining proper ancestor triggers the read. Indeed either $z-L$ or $R-z$ is at least $2\mu$. On the side with room, the corresponding endpoint $l$ or $r$ is at distance at least $\mu/2$ from both edges of $J$. Its empirical error cannot defeat the strict test. In the final cell both endpoints of $I$ lie at distance at least $\mu/2-|I|$ from the atom edges, so its first test passes. This proves exact consumption and publication simultaneously even if earlier empirical decisions differ across good random tapes. Along the successful name there are only $O(b+1)$ extra cell refinements. Their table scans fit within the polynomial factor. The time through the designated stage is $n\mu^{-2}\mathrm{poly}(\log(n+2)+\log(1/\mu)+2)$. The projection removes the leading bit of $\mathrm{bin}(n)$ and inverts a length-lexicographic rank. Substitution of the numeric sampler bounds gives the claimed clock. The probability of a longer run or different consumption on the remaining private random tapes is at most $0.01$.

For fixed $W$ the accounting is stronger on a finite batch. One event of probability at least $0.99$ makes **all** these chosen names decode correctly when all runs use the same random tables. Private coins can realize this simultaneous distribution by consuming the bits for the tables in fixed score order and streaming a predetermined number of fresh bits per bounded trial, even on early publication. Moreover for any randomized stream decoder, the set of programs that consume exactly themselves and halt with probability greater than $1/2$ is prefix-free. An input and its strict extension cannot both have that property: drive both executions by identical random bits and arbitrary identical program bits past the extension. The two exact-consumption events are disjoint outcomes of the same run. [Finite geometry checks in Lean](../lean/MidpointProgram.lean)

## Finding a program from the target word

The name can itself be found by a randomized encoder given $(q,y)$ and the fixed source. Its coins are independent of the decoder's. With probability at least $0.99$ it outputs a valid name within $t p^{-3}\mathrm{poly}(q+\log(1/p)+2)$ steps. Constants can be chosen for this success probability throughout the following estimates.

First run independent trials of $S$ through $t$ until a fixed large number of successes for $y$. By the mean and variance of the geometric waiting time, with the required constant confidence this takes $O(1/p)$ trials and supplies a factor-two estimate. Use a conservative dyadic lower estimate $u$ with $p/8\le u\le p$ on this event. Compute a public upper bound $N$ of order $(t/u)\mathrm{poly}(q+\log(1/u)+2)$ large enough for the numeric construction at any such $u$. Choose a fixed known lower constant $c_0$ from that construction. At least one $n_*\le N$ projects to $y$ and has $\mu_{n_*}\ge c_0p$.

Use $O(u^{-1}\log(N+2))$ bounded trials of $W$ through $cN$. Count only the integer outcomes satisfying their own time cutoffs. With probability arbitrarily close to one, $n_*$ clears the empirical threshold $c_0u/2$, and no integer of mass less than $c_0u/4$ clears it. Apply multiplicative tail bounds at those two boundary probabilities and take a union bound over at most $N$ integers. Select a qualifying integer $n$ projecting to $y$. If none is found, the procedure may return a failure symbol. On success $n\le N$ and $\mu_n\ge c_0u/4$.

Use $O(u^{-2})$ new trials through $cn$ to estimate $F(n-1)$ and $F(n)$ within $c_0u/1000$ by an additive tail bound. Let $\widehat z$ be the midpoint of these estimates and $\widehat\mu$ their difference. Return the cell containing $\widehat z$ of the largest dyadic width at most $\widehat\mu/28$, with the same three-bit residue header. On success $|\widehat z-z|\le\mu_n/100$ and $|\widehat\mu-\mu_n|\le\mu_n/100$. This width lies strictly between $\mu_n/58$ and $\mu_n/27$. Every proper testable ancestor again has width greater than $4\mu_n$ and contains $\widehat z$. The endpoint on its wider side has clearance at least $\mu_n/2-\mu_n/100$ against either edge. The final cell has clearance greater than $\mu_n/3$ inside its atom. Thus the same decoder consumes exactly the returned name and gives $y$ on its good event regardless of how the encoder's estimates were rounded. Scanning the integer coordinates or sorting the samples, three groups of simulations, and fixed interpreter overhead all fit $Nu^{-2}\mathrm{poly}(q+\log(1/u)+2)$ time. The per-word success statement also accommodates rare estimation failures with a larger running time.

## What derandomization would have to preserve

The high-mass numeric test in the [sampling argument](sampling-at-a-numeric-length.md) is equivalent to the following statement about one universal randomized prefix decoder, or to the version for all fixed randomized prefix decoders. For every fixed $a$ and randomized decoder $R$, a program $w$ of length at most $a\log_2(q+2)$ that produces $|y|\le q$ with exact consumption by $2^q$ with probability at least $2/3$ admits a deterministic replacement of length $|w|+O_{R,a}(1)$ and time $2^q(q+2)^{O_{R,a}(1)}$.

In one direction drive $R$ with fair program bits and independent private coins, interleaving the two from fresh fair bits on demand. The resulting ordinary source produces $y$ with probability at least $(2/3)2^{-|w|}$ by the native simulation clock, which is $2^q\mathrm{poly}(q+2)$ after literal compilation. Apply high-mass hard-deadline coding. Conversely the unconditional representation above has length at most $a\log_2(q+2)+O(1)$ at such masses and runs in $2^q\mathrm{poly}_a(q+2)$. Round its time upward to a dyadic clock and apply replacement, absorbing the fixed simulation exponents in the original clock. An efficient randomized prefix interpreter simulates each fixed decoder by a constant prefix and a fixed polylogarithmic overhead. This gives the single-interpreter version. A separating randomized program family would have to defeat **every** deterministic replacement within the stated bit budget. Failure to derandomize with the original program text held fixed is insufficient.

Lu, Oliveira and Santhanam's Theorem 14 supplies infinitely-often pseudodeterministic generators with one advice bit, seed length $m^\epsilon$, polynomial evaluation time and security against a chosen $m^c$ clock. Guessing the advice bit costs a constant factor of probability, but selecting a particular seed costs $2^{-m^\epsilon}$. At polynomial evaluation time the high-mass test permits only $O(\log\log m)$ bits of probability cost. A uniform inputless source also pays to specify the length. Padding its time until the seed becomes admissible gives a replacement clock far beyond the stated security. Their theorem supplies no matching generator for this test. A promise-BPTIME hierarchy separates a smaller bounded-error running-time class from a larger one; here the proposed deterministic replacement is allowed *more* time than the originating randomized computation. Neither source gives the required separation. [Lu, Oliveira and Santhanam, Theorem 14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.08539) · [He, promise-BPTIME hierarchy](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/004/)

## A deterministic test on one bounded nondecreasing function

A high-mass counterexample would also give an ordinary sampler $W$ and a fixed integer $k>0$ such that its **single scalar function** $F(v)$ above cannot be approximated pointwise to error $(2+\log_2(v+2))^{-k}$ by any deterministic $v\,\mathrm{polylog}(v+2)$ time algorithm. The argument permits estimates that oscillate with $v$. The function is computable (each summand can be evaluated by finite enumeration), nondecreasing and bounded in $[0,1]$. Randomized sampling to its own index gives the requested additive precision for every $v$ within that same time class with probability $0.99$. The deterministic task can specify dyadic fixed-point outputs with $O_k(\log\log(v+4))$ bits after the binary point, increasing precision by a constant number of bits for rounding.

For the reduction use the numeric sampler for the given high-mass exponent $a$ that also preserves high mass relative to its new integer $n$. It gives $\mu_n\ge c p$, $\mu_n\ge (2+\log_2 n)^{-a-2}$ for sufficiently large $n$ (absorbing constants), and $n\le t\,\mathrm{poly}_a(q+2)$. Take $k=a+4$. Suppose the claimed deterministic approximator supplies $E(v)$ for this sampler and precision. At epoch $j\ge2$ query only indices in the block
$$
 u_0=2^{j-1}-1\ \le\ v\ \le\ 2^j .
$$
All these estimates have error at most $\varepsilon_j=C_k(j+2)^{-k}$ for a fixed sufficiently large integer $C_k$. Cover the first two integers with a fixed initial block and exact hardwired values. At the target epoch $j=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ the entire pair $n-1,n$ lies inside the block and, at sufficiently large $n$, $\varepsilon_j<\mu_n/1000$.

For each tested cell $[L,R)$ set $s=L+4\varepsilon_j$. If $E(u_0)\ge s$, take $v=u_0$. Otherwise if $E(2^j)<s$, advance to the next epoch. In the remaining case binary-search the bracket by halving it, keeping its left endpoint below $s$ and its right endpoint at or above $s$ in the *estimated* values. This finds adjacent endpoints $v-1,v$ with signs in that order even if $E$ is nonmonotone. Test the conservative interior-boundary condition on $E(v)$ and, if $v>u_0$, the atom condition on $E(v-1),E(v)$. An inconclusive test advances the epoch. These tests are sound by their error margins.

On a proper target-name ancestor at the target epoch, there is an index $w$ in the block with $L+8\varepsilon_j\le F(w)\le R-8\varepsilon_j$ by the midpoint argument. The block cannot be skipped since $F(2^j)\ge F(w)$. The chosen $v$ satisfies $v\le w$: otherwise $E(v-1)<s$ would be incompatible with $F(v-1)\ge F(w)$. For $v=u_0$ the inequality holds directly. Thus $E(v)\ge s$ and $E(v)\le F(w)+\varepsilon_j$ certify a boundary strictly inside the cell. In the final cell all indices $v<n$ have estimates below $s$ and all $v\ge n$ have estimates above it, by the atom's margins and $|I|>\mu_n/64>4\varepsilon_j$. Binary search finds $v=n$ and both atom tests pass.

This implements the deterministic decoder with $O(j+1)$ calls per cell check at epoch $j$. The number of cell refinements is $O(\log(1/\mu_n)+1)$ on the target name. Work through that epoch is bounded by $n\,\mathrm{polylog}(n+2)$, since the dyadic clocks sum geometrically and the names are short at their new numeric lengths. Its length is at most $-\log_2 p+O(1)$. This proves the pointwise approximation consequence by contraposition. Controlling the variation of $F$ gives no algorithm for estimating its values at every required coordinate.
