# A hidden pair of signed integer entries for the permanent

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A finite distribution on square integer matrices has public candidate permanent zero. A coupled YES and NO instance differs in at most two fixed-width integer slots. At cubic field-operation time in the encoding length and the stated polynomial bound on test degrees, constant distinguishing advantage requires $\Omega(m^2)$ digitally probed pairs. An ordinary RAM can locate it by scanning signs. The matrix order in this construction is $m^2+m$. ## Integer matrices on both sides

Start with the complete bipartite graph on $m$ rows and $m$ columns, $m\ge 2$. Write $s=m^2$. For each edge $e=(i,j)$, introduce one private row $r_e$ and one private column $c_e$. In the enlarged matrix, put $U_e$ at $(i,c_e)$, $1$ at $(r_e,j)$, and $V_e$ at $(r_e,c_e)$. All other entries are zero. A perfect matching uses the two outside edges of this path or the middle edge. The original edges used in the first way form a perfect matching $M$ of the original graph, and each such $M$ extends uniquely. Thus the enlarged permanent is

$$
H(U,V)=\sum_M\prod_{e\in M} U_e\prod_{e\notin M} V_e .
$$

For a NO instance, draw every $U_e,V_e$ independently from the integers $1,\ldots,B$, with $B\ge2$. Then $H>0$. Draw a uniform hidden edge $J$, independently of the matrix, and an independent uniform integer $t\in[1,B]$. Write $H=a U_J+b V_J$, where $a,b$ use only the other pairs. Both are positive on this grid because the complete graph has matchings using $J$ and matchings avoiding $J$. Replacing the two entries by $(-t b,t a)$ gives a YES instance. Every magnitude is at most $m! B^s$. Use fixed, public slot widths of $1+\lceil\log_2(m!B^s+1)\rceil$ bits for a sign and the magnitude, including the zero and one slots. Dimensions, layout, total length and candidate coincide on both sides.

## A hard factor after numerical probes

Allow a tree arbitrary complex field operations and equality tests on the entries. It may inspect digits of at most $q\le s/4$ distinct original edge pairs in a path, in adaptive order. In particular a bound of $q$ on individual inspected matrix cells suffices. The two slots of a pair may be revealed together at the first such inspection. Digit operations on the resulting transcript and inspections of fixed zero, one and candidate slots cost no field operations. Computed field registers admit only algebraic operations and equality tests. Charge at most $T$ such instructions per syntactic path, and require every actual division on a promised input to be defined. Let $D_i$ bound the degree of each cross-multiplied equality numerator at field instruction $i$, after pins and before the hidden-edge substitution.

Fix a prefix with the observed pairs pinned to positive grid values. Let $F$ denote the specialized $H$. In a factorization over the complex numbers, total degrees in each free variable pair add. Since $F$ has degree one in each free pair, exactly one irreducible factor depends on that pair, with multiplicity one. For two incident free edges $e,f$, both $U_e$ and $U_f$ occur in $F$, by positivity of the pins. No monomial uses both, since two incident edges cannot be in a matching. If the corresponding factors were distinct, the coefficient of $U_e U_f$, viewed as a polynomial in the other variables, would be a product of nonzero polynomials. Hence all pairs in a connected component of the graph of free edges belong to the same irreducible factor.

Take $k=\lfloor s/[2(m+q)]\rfloor$ and assume $k\ge1$. There are at most $2q/m\le m/2$ rows with at most $m/2$ free edges. Every two rows of free degree greater than $m/2$ have a common free column, so all these rows lie in one component. The $k\le m/2$ rows with the fewest pins are in this component. Together they have at most $kq/m$ pins, leaving at least $m-kq/m\ge k$ columns free in every one of the selected rows.

Let $G$ be the irreducible factor of $F$ containing this free square. Fix all remaining free pairs outside the square to $(1,1)$. Every other irreducible factor becomes a nonzero scalar, since it is independent of the square and $F$ evaluated with all free variables one is positive. In the square put $V_e=1$ and $U_e=\lambda Z_e$. The coefficient of $\lambda^k$ in $F$ is a positive scalar times $\operatorname{per}_k(Z)$. In fact all matchings contributing to this coefficient match the chosen rows to the chosen columns, and each has the same outside sum over complementary matchings, including the weights of unused edges. A border program for $G$ thus gives a border program for $\operatorname{per}_k$, with a polynomial factor in $m$ overhead. Use interpolation at $k+1$ distinct complex scalars after substitution. Taking the linear combination commutes with taking the border limit, even when the approximating programs have larger degree.

A larger minor survives a constant fraction of exposed pairs. Suppose $q\le m^2/16$, $m\ge16$, and let $h=\lfloor\sqrt m/4\rfloor$. Call a row or column high if it has at most $m/4$ pins. At least $3m/4$ rows and $3m/4$ columns are high. Choose $h$ high rows and $h$ high columns as terminals. For each of their $h^2$ pairs, construct a three-edge path in the free graph with its own internal row and column. First choose a high internal column adjacent to the source terminal row. Then choose an internal row adjacent to both this column and the target terminal column. For either choice there are at least $m/2$ candidates before excluding terminals and previously allocated vertices, and at most $h+h^2<m/2$ have been reserved in each class. All the selected edges meet the component of high-free-degree rows identified above.

On this path graph choose $U_e=\lambda Z_{ij}$ on the first edge of path $(i,j)$ and $U_e=\lambda$ on the two other edges. Set all free $V_e$ and all other free $U_e$ to one, retaining the actual pins. Write $d=h+h^2$ for the number of selected rows, equal to the number of selected columns. At degree $d$ in $\lambda$ a matching uses a perfect matching of the path graph. All remaining matched edges belong to the complement on the unselected vertices. Every pinned edge incident to a selected vertex is unused, contributing its fixed positive $V_e$. Consequently the degree-$d$ coefficient is a positive scalar times the permanent of the path graph with weights $Z_{ij},1,1$. The matching bijection of the first section makes this $c\,\operatorname{per}_h(Z)$ with $c>0$. All the selected variables belong to $G$, so numerical interpolation at $d+1$ scalars recovers this minor from a border program for $G$. For $q\le m^2/16$ the hardness estimate below therefore holds also with $h$ in place of $k$.

For any unprobed $J$ in this component, write $F=aU_J+bV_J$ with its current pins. Over the fraction field of all other free pairs, the substitution $(U_J,V_J)=(-tb,ta)$ parametrizes the entire line $F=0$. Its kernel in the two-variable polynomial ring over that field is $(aU_J+bV_J)$. In the full polynomial ring the primitive part of this linear form is precisely $G$, up to a nonzero scalar, because the other factors are independent of the pair. Gauss's lemma implies that any nonzero numerator $E$ vanishing identically after substitution is divisible by $G$. The numerator of one equality on a length-$T$ program uses $O(T+1)$ division-free operations. The general characteristic-zero factor theorem gives a border program for $G$ of size $\operatorname{poly}(m)(T+1)$, since its degree is at most $s$. The square extraction then gives the same bound for the all-entry border complexity of $\operatorname{per}_k$. ## Advantage, precision and scope

Follow the virtual path that uses the NO grid for all digital probes and declares a field equality true exactly when its numerator is the zero polynomial after the current pins. This path is independent of $J,t$. For its final probe set, a bad pivot is a pinned pair or a free edge in a row with at most $m/2$ free edges. In each such row there are at most as many free edges as pinned ones. Hence at most $2q$ pivots are bad, and their probability is at most $2q/s$. Every other pivot stays unprobed in a row of degree greater than $m/2$ at every earlier prefix, so it always belongs to the hard component.

For each field instruction, condition only on its virtual prefix and a fixed currently unprobed $J$ in a row of high free degree. All other free pair coordinates and $t$ are still independent uniform grid integers. The NO evaluation of an ambient-nonzero numerator of degree $D_i$ has zero probability at most $D_i/B$. Each of $a,b$ has degree at most $s-1$. After substituting $(-tb,ta)$ the degree is at most $sD_i$, and the resulting polynomial is nonzero unless the preceding factor-complexity alternative holds. Its zero probability is therefore at most $sD_i/B$. Ambient identities hold on both runs. When an actual execution reaches this prefix on its virtual path, its divisors there are defined, so the numerators suffice to decide a first equality divergence. The final-pivot bad event is charged separately. The per-instruction estimates use only prefix information.

For a single tree, including an input-independent randomized choice of tree, the worst-case YES acceptance guarantee minus the worst-case NO acceptance bound consequently satisfies

$$
\underline S(\operatorname{per}_k)\le \operatorname{poly}(m)(T+1)
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\Delta\le \frac{2q}{m^2}+\frac{(m^2+1)\sum_{i=1}^T D_i}{B}.
$$

For example, take $q\le m^2/16$, $D_i\le m^u$ for a fixed $u\ge0$, and $B$ the smallest power of two at least $m^{u+24}$. Then $h=\Omega(\sqrt m)$ and the dense binary encoding has length $L=O(m^6\log m)$. At $T=O(L^3)$ the imported permanent border lower bound rules out the first alternative for $h$ for large $m$. The second gives $\Delta\le 2q/m^2+o(1)$. In particular $q=o(m^2)$ forces vanishing advantage, and any fixed positive gap requires $\Omega(m^2)$ digitally inspected pairs. Repeated squaring may violate the degree bound. Across these two distributions, inspecting signs at $q$ uniformly distinct $U$-locations detects the changed cell with probability exactly $q/m^2$. The example establishes a resource tradeoff for the finite algebraic-and-digital model. A reduction transferring it to unrestricted Boolean SAT is still missing.
