# How short would a RAM-to-DIMACS compiler have to be?

Let $U$ be bounded nondeterministic word-RAM acceptance with a unary clock, where one instruction can guess a full word. All sizes count encoded bits. Suppose a decision-preserving compiler for $U$ produces proper ASCII DIMACS 3-CNF on every input of length $s$. Require a uniform local bit indexer: obtain the output length and any requested bit in $\mathrm{polylog}(s+2)$ time with random reads of the source. The compiler always emits a well-formed formula with canonical decimal names. There is no clause-access hypothesis or bound on an individual name's length.

**A compiler of length $o(s\log s)$ with this local access would exclude deterministic $O(N)$ SAT time on the unrestricted word RAM.** Its physical construction time is unrestricted. More generally, output length $O(s\log^\beta(s+2))$ excludes $O(N\log^a(N+2))$ SAT time whenever $a,\beta\ge0$ and $\beta+a<1$. The proof repeatedly applies the short-assignment check to its own bounded native acceptance problem. The existing quasilinear reductions suffice for the final, smaller query. No compiler below the stated threshold is known here. [Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas, *Time-Space Lower Bounds for Satisfiability*](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf) · [Native model and deterministic diagonal](native-unary-clock-band.md)

## Reading a short assignment on a virtual formula

Fix the deterministic self-input diagonal $J$ that simulates the opponent named in its input $x$ for at most $n^2$ opponent steps and inverts its verdict before timeout, with $n=|x|$. The universal interpreter uses at most $T=Cn^2$ real steps. No deterministic $o(n^2)$-time RAM decides $J$ on all sufficiently long inputs. Its bounded native file $y$ has length $\Theta(T)$, and a query to its input or unary clock is answered from $x$ and known endpoints. Let $F$ be the compiler output on $y$, of bit length $M$. The verifier will generate selected raw formula bytes from $x$.

There is an assignment string of length $O(1+M/\log(2+M))$ for this file. Take $h=\max(1,\lfloor(\log_{10}(M+2))/4\rfloor)$. Use a bit indexed by the numerical value for names of at most $h$ decimal digits. For every longer name occurrence use the bit indexed by the starting byte position divided by $h$, rounded down, in a second table. Two distinct starts of long occurrences cannot lie in the same block of $h$ positions. The first table has $O(10^h)$ bits and the second has $O(1+M/h)$. On a proper formula, an assignment passes exactly when every clause is satisfied and every pair of equal long names has equal occurrence bits. Thus either a falsified clause or a conflicting pair witnesses a failure. Checking a selected clause or pair uses at most three assignment bits. In the dense case, $v$ occurring names take $\Omega(v\log(2+v))$ encoded bits, and the $v$-bit assignment is indexed directly by name. The bound on occurring names and short assignments has been used previously for multitape SAT verification. [*Effective Guessing Has Unlikely Consequences*, Lemmas 19 and 21](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-023-10119-x)

If clause ordinals already give the signs and $O(\log s)$-digit names and their starting byte offsets in polylogarithmic time, these tests need no extra index. Under mere bitwise indexing, supply the following additional existential data, still totaling $O(1+M/\log(2+M))$ bits.

* Divide the raw byte string into blocks of $\Theta(\log^2(2+M))$ bytes, with synthetic terminal whitespace. At each block boundary store a scanner state of $O(\log(2+M))$ bits. It records the current header/comment/whitespace/token mode, a current literal's sign and starting offset, whether the token denotes zero, and the last three completed signed literal intervals. The scanner skips comments and the header, reports a literal interval on ending a nonzero numeral, and reports the three intervals of a clause on ending the zero terminator. Properness guarantees exactly three literals per clause. Require boundary zero to contain the prescribed initial record; a mismatching initial record is itself a failure. A challenge names a block, reads its two boundary states and checks the deterministic update through its raw bytes. With all these tests passing, induction determines every state. A proposed byte at which a literal or clause ends is tested by scanning from its preceding boundary. This handles arbitrarily long whitespace, comments and names.
* Guess integers $p\ge2$ and $a<p$ of $O(\log(2+M))$ bits and supply prefix hash values at the same boundaries. Require $H(0)=0$ in the initial record and update $H(i+1)=(aH(i)+b_i)\bmod p$ for each byte $b_i$. A block challenge verifies these updates. The hash of an arbitrary literal's digit interval $[l,r)$ is $H(r)-a^{r-l}H(l)$ modulo $p$; recover each endpoint by scanning a single block and compute the power by squaring. Reject unequal assignment bits whenever two long names have equal lengths and equal hashes. Identical names always hash equally for any admissible $p,a$. For completeness choose a prime larger than $\max(256,(M+2)^4)$ and within twice this bound. Each pair of distinct equal-length names gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $M$ over that field, with at most $M$ forbidden bases. Fewer than $M^2$ pairs occur, so a base avoiding all collisions exists. The verifier need only enforce the integer ranges; primality serves the existence proof.

Each table record has $O(\log(2+M))$ bits. A single challenge reads only constantly many records and assignment bits: either check a block, check a clause at a proposed byte, or compare literals ending at two proposed bytes. Range and registration checks rule out spurious bad events. The original formula bytes required by these tests are generated from $x$ in $\mathrm{polylog}(T+2)$ time per byte.

Pad the entire witness to $W=\Theta(n+1+M/\log(2+M))$ bits. For fixed $x,z$ with $z$ that witness, the existence of a failure has a proper explicit SAT instance of length and construction time
$$
       O(W+n\,\mathrm{polylog}(T+2)).
$$
Guess challenge indices and implement the deterministic polylogarithmic calculations on $O(\log T)$-bit words. A chronological simulation can scan its own polylogarithmically many earlier writes at each load. Multiplexing the $n$ source bits at each read of $x$ adds $n\,\mathrm{polylog}(T+2)$ even after the gates' names are serialized. The checked bits and records of $z$ use separate decimal-mask tables. For a bit lookup split the table into chunks of $b=\Theta(\log W)$ bits with $b$ a power of two and a sufficiently small coefficient. Each chunk contributes one clause selecting a library name with its $b$ data digits. A library tabulates the reply for a proposed low address and bit using $O(b2^b)$ fixed clauses; each high-address decoder takes a number of gates linear in the number of chunks. To read an entire aligned record, arrange the data by bit position within its records and look up one bit in each of its $O(\log W)$ position tables at the common record ordinal. Their combined data length is the original table length; padding the final chunk of each table adds only $\mathrm{polylog}(T+2)$. All library overheads together are $O(2^b\mathrm{polylog}(T+2))=o(W)$ for large $n$ since $W\ge n$ and $T=\Theta(n^2)$. Names cost $O(\log T)=O(\log W)$ digits, and all chunk clauses and decoder gates cost $O(W)$ encoded bits and word steps. Separate copies for a fixed number of reads retain this bound, even with adaptive read addresses. ## Repeating the reduction on its own verifier

Write a native instance as $(P,x',1^t)$ and keep $(P,x',t)$ physically, with the clock in binary. Its original encoding of length $s$ can be read locally in constant time with fixed-width bookkeeping, including reads at field boundaries. Suppose a fixed SAT machine runs in $O(N\log^a(N+2))$ steps. A fixed nondeterministic program $V$ receives $(P,x',t)$ as payload. It computes the compiler's output length $M$, guesses the assignment, physically constructs the failure test and runs the SAT machine. It accepts precisely if the machine rejects that test. All calls use the actual assignment bits. With $L=\lceil\log_2(s+2)\rceil$ and $u$ the new payload length, a sufficient new step cap is
$$
 h=K\big((u+1)L^b+(1+M/\log(2+M))L^a\big).
$$
Round up using integer logarithms and integer arithmetic; for the strict power trade choose a slightly larger rational $a$ if needed. The constants $K,b$ are fixed independently of the iteration. If the payload is shorter than $n$, it can be padded to $n$. Throughout the proof $\log s=O(\log n)$, so the local compiler and the SAT machine operate on at most a fixed number of guest limbs. The usual fixed-depth sparse simulation implements their addressing. The decimal lookup can likewise be padded to at least $u$ bits. This gives the equivalence of $(P,x',1^t)$ and $(V,(P,x',t),1^h)$ for the truth of their bounded native acceptance predicates.

Start from $(J,x,1^{Cn^2})$ and iterate this single transformation while the current clock exceeds $n^{3/2}$. The new payload contains the literal previous program and payload and the binary previous clock, never the guessed assignment or a unary clock. After the first iteration the program is always the same $V$. Each round increases the physical payload by only $O(\log n)$ bits, including prefix-length fields. The controller can copy the entire current payload to form its successor. Local source reads access its packed bytes directly, regardless of how many earlier records they contain.

For $a=0$ and $M=o(s\log s)$ uniformly, $M/\log(2+M)=o(s)$: split at $M=\sqrt s$. For the power trade the corresponding term times $L^a$ is $O(sL^{\beta+a-1})=o(s)$. During the first $O(\log n)$ rounds the payload has $n+O(\log^2 n)$ bits. As long as $t>n^{3/2}$, its additive contribution to $h$ is $o(t)$ and $s=(1+o(1))t$. Consequently every such round halves the clock for all sufficiently large $n$, uniformly in the round number. Induction up to $\lceil 2\log_2(n+2)\rceil$ rounds therefore suffices. The controller obtains each actual $M$ from the local indexer and the displayed cap. It never needs a convergence rate for the little-$o$ assumption.

The final native instance has $O(n^{3/2})$ physical bits including its unary clock. The usual fixed polylogarithmic-overhead RAM-to-SAT compiler and the hypothesized SAT solver decide it in $O(n^{3/2}\mathrm{polylog}(n+2))$ time. All the controller's copies and indexing operations together take $O(n\,\mathrm{polylog}(n+2))$. The equivalences would thus decide $J$ in $o(n^2)$ time. [Existing bounded-run compiler](native-linear-time-and-locally-uniform-circuit-depth.md) · [Native width and sparse-address implementation](native-unary-clock-band.md)

Under the separate hypothesis that $U$ itself has a deterministic linear-time solver, the same length exclusion follows in one iteration. A native challenge with $x,z$ physically present guesses a faulty index block, a falsified clause, or an inconsistent pair and checks it in polylogarithmic time. Run the native solver on that challenge inside an existential verifier, then use it once more on the verifier and its unary clock. Both calls fit in $O(W+n+\mathrm{polylog}(T+2))=o(T)$ if $M=o(s\log s)$.

## An unconditional lower bound for compilers that preserve counts

For a positive integer $K=2^e q$ with $q$ odd, the minimum bit length of a proper ASCII DIMACS 3-CNF having exactly $K$ models on the full set of declared variables is
$$
  \Theta\big(1+\log(q+1)\log(2+\log(q+1))+\log(e+2)\big).
$$
If $v$ variables occur out of $V$ declared, then $K=2^{V-v}K'$ with $K'\le 2^v$. Thus $q\le 2^v$. The distinct decimal identifiers alone occupy $\Omega(v\log(2+v))$ bits, and $2^V\ge K$ forces the header to advertise at least $e$ variables. Conversely, a binary comparator against the constant $q$ uses $O(1+\log(q+1))$ gates and guesses a binary integer in $[0,q)$. Its ordinary gate equivalences have unique internal extensions and occupy $O(1+\log(q+1)\log(2+\log(q+1)))$ bits. Use distinct false pins to pad to proper ternary clauses, forced by seven clauses forbidding all other assignments of three pins. Number all occurring names densely. Declaring $e$ further variables increases the header by at most $O(1+\log(e+2)+\log(2+\log(q+1)))$ bits and multiplies the model count by $2^e$.

Now fix a native program on a payload of $m$ bits. In $O(m)$ steps it guesses $m$ independent $w=\Theta(\log m)$-bit words, accumulates their bitwise OR and accepts exactly if it is nonzero. A sufficiently long unary clock gives native length $s=\Theta(m)$ and exactly $2^{mw}-1$ accepting branches. This is odd. If the output model count is **any positive integer multiple** of the accepting branch count, its odd part is at least $2^{mw}-1$. The output therefore has $\Omega(s\log^2 s)$ bits. This includes parsimonious compilers, even with arbitrarily many unused declared names. The bound itself is independent of all time assumptions. The decision on this example is constant for $m\ge1$, so the remaining compiler question concerns how far one can compress while preserving only existence.
