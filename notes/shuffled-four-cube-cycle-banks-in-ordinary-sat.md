# Five missing labels admit a linear SAT algorithm, and six encode arbitrary proper 3-SAT

Fix a Hamiltonian cycle of the four-dimensional Boolean cube on four distinct variable names. Each edge has three fixed bits. Make its label the proper three-literal clause that is false exactly at its two endpoints. Take a subset of the sixteen labels as one **bank**. A file is the conjunction of any number of banks, on overlapping names, with arbitrary Hamiltonian cycles, coordinate permutations, complements, literal orders and physical clause order. The ordinary DIMACS file carries no bank partition. Clause occurrences may repeat.

* If each bank omits at most five labels, a fixed deterministic RAM decides the file in time $O(N)$ for encoded bit length $N$, with no annotations. It infers unary and binary clauses by six passes over the named triples. Every original clause contains one of the inferred clauses.
* If each bank omits exactly six labels, there is a parsimonious deterministic $O(N)$-time reduction from arbitrary proper 3-SAT to this format. The reduction uses one fixed reflected cycle and one fixed omission pattern in every bank. Each individual bank has precisely three satisfying assignments on its four names.
* If at most $e$ banks omit six labels and all others omit at most five, an algorithm finds an equivalent formula with a strong backdoor of at most $e$ variables into 2-SAT. The input omits the bank partition and $e$. Its time is $O(N(1+\lceil e/w\rceil)+2^e\operatorname{poly}(1+e))$ on a standard word RAM of width $w=\Theta(\log(N+2))$. This includes linear time for $e\le(1-\epsilon)\log_2 N$ with fixed $\epsilon>0$.

These are a sharp threshold for this family of encoded instances and an equivalence to ordinary SAT on the upper side. The unrestricted superlinear SAT running-time question remains open. Historical priority for this family and the numerical threshold is unestablished. Schaefer's Boolean constraint dichotomy supplies the classical context for bijunctive and exact-one relations. Moore and Robson establish NP-hardness even for a restricted monotone exact-one input. The direct reduction below also preserves assignments and encoded linear length. [Schaefer](https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/lieber/courses/csg260/f06/materials/papers/max-sat/p216-schaefer.pdf) · [Moore and Robson](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0003039)

## Six rounds with no bank names

Aggregate the truth tables of clauses with the same sorted triple $S$ of names by intersection, obtaining $T_S\subseteq\{0,1\}^S$. Start with an empty set $B$ of forbidden unary and binary assignments. In each synchronous round, form the rows of each $T_S$ obeying every ban in the previous $B$ whose names lie in $S$. For every singleton or pair $P\subseteq S$, add to the next $B$ each assignment on $P$ missing from the surviving rows. A forbidden assignment denotes the complementary unary or binary clause. Every addition is a logical consequence of the input. Write $L_S(B)$ for the rows on $S$ obeying its local bans.

The finite lemma is stronger than equality of solution sets. On an individual bank with at most five missing labels, after six rounds one has $L_S(B)\subseteq T_S$ on every occurring triple. A false row of a three-clause specifies all its named bits, so this inclusion says an actual inferred clause is a subset of the original clause. Across overlapping banks, all initial tables on a given bank's names are contained in that bank's isolated tables. An induction on rounds shows that global bans contain every ban inferred from any isolated bank. This proves direct subsumption also on the shuffled file. The resulting binary formula is equivalent on every assignment. Linear-time 2-SAT finishes the decision. [Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/a-linear-time-algorithm-for-testing-the-truth-of-certain-quantifie/)

The finite lemma has two independent representations. Both start a simple cube path at 0, try all four unused-neighbor steps until all 16 vertices occur, and close only when the end neighbors 0. There are 2,688 oriented cycles beginning at 0. Coordinate permutations, bit complements, rotation and reversal leave nine representatives. In their lexicographic order after rooting and orienting, the first row below counts undirected cycles and the second gives the largest number of rounds needed for actual subsumption.

| Orbit | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Size | 192 | 192 | 96 | 48 | 384 | 192 | 48 | 96 | 96 |
| Rounds | 5 | 6 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 |

There are $\sum_{i=0}^5\binom{16}i=6885$ masks per representative. In orbit 2, omitting the zero-based edge indices $2,5,6,10,11$ or $3,4,8,9,12$ requires six rounds of this update rule. On the reflected cycle $g_i=i\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}\lfloor i/2\rfloor$, omitting edges $0,7$ leaves an unsatisfiable bank whose first-round binary clauses still admit $0000$.

For an independent bitset recurrence, store assignments as the 16 bits of a mask. Let $T_j$ be the table on the three coordinates other than $j$, cylindrical in coordinate $j$. Write $E_iR$ for the result of existentially forgetting bit $i$. Starting with all $L_i$ full, take $R_j=T_j\cap L_j$ on each occurring scope. In the next $L_i$, intersect the old $L_i$ with $E_iR_j$ for each $j\ne i$, and with $\bigcap_{a\ne i}E_aR_i$ for its own occurring scope. Skip absent scopes as senders. These are exactly the projections onto every pair usable in the receiving scope. Unary bans are automatically included by the corresponding pair projections. Lean checks that every simple closed traversal is represented and that the six-round bitset inclusion holds for all listed representatives and masks using compiled finite decision. The Python verifier uses explicit lists of forbidden pairs and singletons. [Lean finite statements](../lean/GrayCycleBankClosure.lean) · [Independent exhaustive and physical audit](../audits/gray_cycle_bank_audit.py)

For the native running time, parse decimal variable tokens using a digit trie and assign dense addresses to active names. Ignore leading zeros in the numerical names if accepted by the parser. One need never allocate the numerical size of the DIMACS header. Sorting fixed-arity records on dense names takes a constant number of stable counting-sort passes. Generate a record for each unary/pair projection and an occurrence record for each recipient triple using that projection; join by sorted scope, broadcast the constant truth table to its occurrences, then collect updates at the triples. Each triple has only six unary/pair scopes. Across six rounds this takes $O(N)$ word operations, including parsing. A globally correct SAT program may delete all subsumed clauses and use a complete solver on the remaining formula, so its linear behavior on the promise requires no recognition oracle.

## Six missing edges and a four-atom clause compiler

Number the reflected Gray cycle vertices by $g_0,\ldots,g_{15}$ and its edges $i=(g_i,g_{i+1\bmod16})$. Omit edges $2,3,6,7,14,15$. A vertex survives exactly when both incident edges were omitted. The only surviving vertices are $g_3=2,g_7=4,g_{15}=8$. Use bit zero as a new private variable $p$ and bits one through three as $x,y,z$. The ten surviving clauses enforce $p=0$ and $E(x,y,z)$, where $E$ means exactly one true. Each edge fixes three distinct coordinates, so every printed clause is proper.

First express a source proper formula in positive $E$ atoms. Introduce four distinct variables $t,f,g,h$ and the three atoms $E(t,f,g),E(t,f,h),E(t,g,h)$. Subtracting the equations gives $f=g=h$ and then $t+2f=1$, fixing every anchor. Give each active source variable a positive and negative wire, related by $E(x,\bar x,f)$. For any signed source literal $l$, its wire $L$ equals its value and its other twin equals $1-L$. For each source clause with wire values $X,Y,L$, introduce fresh variables $z,a,b,c,d$ and the four atoms

$$
 E(z,X,a),\qquad E(z,Y,b),\qquad E(a,b,c),\qquad E(z,1-L,d).
$$

From the first two equations, $a=1-X-z$ and $b=1-Y-z$. The third requires $a+b\le1$, forcing $z=(1-X)(1-Y)$, with $a,b,c$ then unique. The last permits precisely $z\le L$ and determines $d=L-z$. Thus the input clause holds exactly when it has a completion, and that completion is unique. Replace every atom by its ten-clause cycle bank with a distinct private fourth coordinate fixed to zero. All three atom arguments are distinct names. Arbitrarily shuffling these clauses preserves the same correspondence.

Here is a bit-length bound including inactive header variables. Gather and deduplicate the signed source triples by a digit trie and fixed-arity radix sorting. For $m$ distinct source clauses there are $O(m+1)$ active wires and $O(m+1)$ output clauses. Give the active output variables dense consecutive IDs. Since at most exponentially many distinct bounded-alphabet strings have a given bounded length, spelling out $m$ distinct clauses in the input uses $\Omega(m\log(m+1))$ bits for $m\ge2$. Sorting the literal order and canonicalizing the names cannot increase the minimum sum of their lengths. The output therefore costs $O(N)$ bits. If the source declared $D$ variables of which $v$ occur, set the output header to $D-v+v'$ where $v'$ is the number of active output variables. The two files have the same number of unconstrained inactive coordinates, so even counting assignments to every declared variable is parsimonious. Computing the new possibly long decimal header takes $O(N)$ digit operations, using the absolute header difference $O(m+1)$ and one elementary addition/subtraction. To print all dense names, precompute their decimal spellings in total $O((m+1)\log(m+2))$ operations by appending digits to shorter prefixes; copying the printed names has the same bound.

The equality relation on four names can be supplied by the same reflected cycle. Omit edges $0,3,6,9,10,15$; precisely the vertices $g_0=0$ and $g_{10}=15$ survive. Omitting just $0,9,10,15$ gives the same relation with four omissions. Give each positive exact-one atom private copies of its three arguments. For each old wire, force all its copies equal along a path of equality banks: one copy per bank, one connector to each predecessor or successor, and fresh names on unused arms. A wire used just once needs no equality bank. Each connector occurs in precisely two banks, and each padding bit is uniquely fixed by its bank. Every pair of banks has at most one name in common, every name belongs to at most two banks, and the exact-one banks have pairwise disjoint scopes. The linear bound and parsimonious correspondence survive. An arbitrary proper input can therefore use either two fixed six-omission masks or a single six-omission mask coupled through four-omission equality banks.

Both attachment bounds also hold with **one fixed six-omission mask**. Omit $0,1,3,4,7,8$. The surviving raw vertices are $1,6,12$. Complementing bit two (counting from zero) yields the normalized rows $5,2,8$, so renaming the axes gives
$$D(x,x',y,z)=[x=x']\land E(x,y,z).$$
A private $x'$ realizes an $E$ atom with unique extension. A pin with external port $p$ has three banks
$$
D(p,a,b,c),\qquad D(\neg a,\neg d,u,v),\qquad D(\neg d,\neg b,s,t).
$$
The duplicate coordinates imply $p=a=d=b$. Then $E(p,p,c)$ forces $p=0,c=1$, and $u,v,s,t$ are all zero. The three banks meet pairwise on single distinct names. With a fresh copy of this pin, $D(x,x',\neg y,p)$ enforces $x=x'=y$ with a unique extension. Use these three-port equalities to link each list of atom ports by a path, padding unused arms with fresh, uniquely determined names. Only signs and coordinate permutations vary. Thus even a family with at most two bank attachments per name, at most one common name per pair of banks, and a single six-omission mask retains the encoded linear-time equivalence to arbitrary proper SAT.

## Finding a small exceptional part

For a set $R$ of four-bit assignments, let $H(R)$ be the satisfying assignments of every unary and binary clause true on $R$. A second finite lemma gives $|H(R)\setminus R|\le1$ for every bank with at most six omissions. In the same 16-bit representation, $H(R)=\bigcap_{i<j}E_i E_j R$. The Lean check exhausts all masks up to size six. Among exactly-six masks, whenever the difference is nonempty there are three nonconstant coordinate columns modulo complementation. Counting all 1,344 undirected cycles, the numbers with $|R|=3,4,5$ are $103680,43392,3456$, respectively. The Python check also scans **all 65,536** omission masks on the reflected cycle. The first nonbijunctive ones have six omissions, and there are 112 at that level.

In fact **each individual nonbijunctive six-omission pattern** suffices for the parsimonious reduction, allowing coordinate permutations and literal complements. Keep one representative name from each of the three nonconstant coordinate classes. The fourth bit is fixed or uniquely determined as a signed copy of a kept bit. The same unary and binary identifications hold on $H(R)$. Its unique missing row projects to a point outside the projected relation $T$; complement the three selected axes to move this point to $000$. Every pair of zero bits has a witness in $T$, so all three weight-one rows are present. If $111$ also belonged, those four rows would realize all four patterns on every pair, making the projected binary hull the full cube. Binary constraints involving the fourth bit become unary or binary on substituting its fixed value or signed copy. Thus there would be seven rows in $T$, contrary to $|T|=|R|\le5$. Also at least one of the three weight-two rows is absent, since adding all three to the weight-one rows would require six allowed rows. Intersect $T$ with its two cyclic coordinate rotations. The result is exactly the three weight-one rows. Use three private fourth names, one per bank, guaranteeing a unique completion. The audit tests this three-bank construction for every nonbijunctive six-mask on every representative.

Run the six blind rounds on the full file and delete syntactically subsumed clauses. Let $S$ be the names appearing in the remaining ternary clauses. Every remaining clause occurs in an exceptional bank, so $|S|\le 4e$ and there are at most $10e$ remaining clause occurrences under the stated six-omission promise. For each four-subset $W\subseteq S$, collect all **original** clauses on its names and compute the unary and binary consequences of their conjunction on sixteen rows. Add these consequences to $B$. Finding the original clauses takes a single grouping pass on the input, followed by $\operatorname{poly}(e)$ array work on the dense core. Every new constraint is sound.

If $B$ is contradictory, reject. Otherwise existentially project it onto $S$ to get a 2-CNF $B_S$. Condense the implication graph to a DAG and propagate bitsets of reachable marked literals (the two polarities of each name in $S$) in reverse topological order. Conflicts among forced literals give unary bans, and paths $a\leadsto\neg b$ give binary bans. To justify sufficiency, a partial assignment on $S$ extends to a model of satisfiable $B$ exactly when its forced implication closures avoid a complementary pair. If literals $a,b$ imply $u,\neg u$, then contraposition gives $a\leadsto u\leadsto\neg b$; allow $a=b$ for a forced unary ban. A consistent implication closure extends by the usual 2-SAT construction. Keep both signed literals as vertices even for an unconstrained marked name. In particular, a path $a\leadsto\neg a$ bans $a$ regardless of any second selected value. The whole projection costs $O((N+\operatorname{poly}(e))(1+\lceil e/w\rceil))$ word operations. Later 2-SAT calls use this projected formula of size $O((1+e)^2)$.

Associate to each surviving clause $C$ its underlying triple and its *violation event*, the models of $B_S$ falsifying $C$. Discard empty events and identify equal events. These tests need only $\operatorname{poly}(e)$ time: a violation fixes three bits, and a counterexample to inclusion of events requires those three bits fixed and one literal of the second clause true. Form a bipartite graph whose vertices on one side are underlying triples, on the other the distinct nonempty events. Each remaining clause gives an incidence.

Every exceptional bank contributes incidences coverable by one vertex. If its initially surviving clauses have at most one underlying triple, choose that triple. If it has two different triples, their union contains all four bank names, so one of the four-subsets processed above was the entire bank scope. The strengthened $B$ therefore entails its full $H(R)$. Every original bank clause holds on every row in $R$. If a clause has a nonempty violation event under $B_S$, it must be violated at the unique row of $H(R)\setminus R$. Its event is precisely the set of all $B_S$-models with that row on the bank's four names. This event is the same for every surviving nonredundant clause of that bank.

Consequently the graph has a vertex cover of size at most $e$. A bipartite matching and its alternating reachability find such a cover in polynomial time on the core. For each selected triple retain all residual clauses on it. For each selected event retain one representative clause. Over $B_S$ the retained conjunction is equivalent to all residual clauses, since every omitted clause either has its event represented or shares a selected triple. Choose a single name from each selected triple and one name from each selected event's representative. The resulting at most $e$ names meet every retained ternary clause, so every fixing of these names leaves only 2-SAT. Exhaust the $2^e$ fixings on the core. To recover an active model, retain a successful full core assignment, then run ordinary 2-SAT once on the full $B$ with those marked values pinned. Every resulting extension satisfies the original formula by subsumption and event equivalence. Inactive header variables can be assigned zero implicitly; explicitly listing them can take longer than the decision time. This is a strong backdoor for the equivalent formula produced by the algorithm. The algorithm uses only trusted consequences and equivalences even on files outside the promised family. [Backdoors to typical-case complexity](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2003/167)
