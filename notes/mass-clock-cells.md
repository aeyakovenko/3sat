# How often must the program probability of one word multiply?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Suppose explicit SAT takes deterministic linear time on the unrestricted logarithmic-word RAM. On every fixed efficient native literal prefix-universal interpreter, some $m$-bit word gains a factor of at least $m^\gamma$ in its actual cumulative program probability in each of $\Omega(m/\log m)$ separate grid cells, for all sufficiently large $m$ and some fixed $\gamma>0$. The cells concern one word and each cell spans only a polynomial time factor. Such words carry a positive fraction of the eventual probability conditioned on length, at every large length. The same conclusion holds for probability conditioned on timely publication at almost every grid point in a terminal part of the window. Counting arbitrarily small positive mass gains would lose this distinction.

## Local bounds on the size and frequency of gains

Use $b=\lceil\log_2(m+2)\rceil$, $T_j=2^{3m+jb}$, $0\le j\le N=\lfloor m/b\rfloor$. For a fixed $m$-bit $y$ write $K_j=K^V_{T_j}(y)$ and $L_j=-\log_2 P^V_{T_j}(y)$, where $P$ sums the full fair-bit mass of all timely exact names. Both profiles are finite throughout this grid for large $m$ because of literal printing. We have $L_j\le K_j$ unconditionally. The [near-clock coding theorem](near-clock-coding.md) under linear SAT gives $K_{j+C}\le L_j+D$ for fixed nonnegative integers $C,D$, whenever both indices exist. Enlarge $D$ to absorb rounding. For $F=K,L$, put $\delta F_j=F_j-F_{j+1}$ and $H_F(h)=\#\{j:\delta F_j\ge h\}$.

For any $j\ge C$, $\delta K_j\le L_{j-C}-L_{j+1}+D$. For $j+1+C\le N$, $\delta L_j\le K_j-K_{j+1+C}+D$. If the left side is at least $h>D$, at least one of the $C+1$ cells in the right-hand neighborhood drops by $(h-D)/(C+1)$ or more. A given receiving cell belongs to at most $C+1$ neighborhoods. Thus, in both directions,

$$ H_F(h)\le C+(C+1)H_G\!\left(\frac{h-D}{C+1}\right),\qquad \{F,G\}=\{K,L\}. $$

This applies inside any subinterval by counting its first or last $C$ cells as boundary exceptions. There is no upper bound on the size of a single drop in the proof. If $L$ is rounded up to integer bits, the [plateau comparison](occupied-clock-cells.md) also compares the counts of occupied cells at threshold one, after adjusting the fixed additive slack.

The [pattern construction](successive-description-times.md) gives a word for each of a $1-o(1)$ fraction of $k$-bit patterns, $k=\Theta(m/b)$. Each selected word drops by at least $W=12b$ bits of $K$ in each of $k$ disjoint blocks of $A$ cells for fixed $A$. Indeed its allowed bands at the adjacent endpoints have a gap of at least $W$. Hence $H_K(W/A)\ge k$. The local bound gives $H_L(\gamma\log_2 m)=\Omega(m/\log m)$ for some fixed $\gamma>0$. At each of these cells the new probability is at least $m^\gamma$ times the starting probability. The endpoints and constant may depend on the machine.

## A fraction of all eventual length-conditioned probability

Write $Q_t(m)=\sum_{|y|=m}P^V_t(y)$, and let $G_t(m)$ be the sum on just the selected words $y_P$ for good $P$. Their first $k$ bits are $P$, so they are distinct. One fixed stream machine simulates an arbitrary $V$-name $p$, takes the length $m$ of its output, then reads precisely $k(m)$ bits $P$ and runs the selector on $(m,P)$. Every $p$ producing any $m$-bit word, even long after the clocks in the history, supplies mass to the same $y_P$ for each good $P$. The exact compiled names for the distinct pairs $(p,P)$ have a fixed prefix and are disjoint. Their fair-bit weights imply, for every large $m$,

$$ P^V_\infty(y_P)\ge c_V 2^{-k}Q_\infty(m)\quad\text{for every good }P,\qquad G_\infty(m)\ge c'_V Q_\infty(m). $$

The aggregate $G$ itself has synchronized gains. Every good pattern has $K_{A(i-1)}-K_{Ai}\ge W$ at each of the same $k$ block endpoints. Thus $L_{A(i-1)-C}-L_{Ai}\ge W-D$ for each word whenever the first index exists. Taking every $2+\lceil C/A\rceil$-th block gives $\Theta(m/\log m)$ disjoint enlarged windows. Across each, $G$ multiplies by at least $2^{W-D}$ by summing the pointwise mass inequalities. The ratios of $Q$ across all these windows have product at most $1/Q_{T_0}(m)=\mathrm{poly}(m)$ by monotonicity. Only $O_V(1)$ windows can therefore multiply $Q$ by more than $m^5$. For all the others even the conditional fraction $G/Q$ multiplies by at least $m^6$ for large $m$. At the initial clock it is at most $2^{-\Omega(m)}$ by multiplying the disjoint gains for $G$ and using the same lower bound on $Q_{T_0}(m)$.

The selector from $(m,P)$ finishes by $2^{(4-\epsilon)m}$ for fixed $\epsilon>0$, after reducing $\epsilon$ to absorb compilation. For $2^{(4-\epsilon)m}\le t\le2^{4m}$, a $p$ that finishes by $t$ contributes by $t\,m^{O(1)}$; this accounts for measuring output length and both native simulations. Thus there is a fixed $C'$ such that $G_{T_j}(m)\ge c'_V Q_{T_{j-C'}}(m)$ whenever the earlier index is in this terminal range. There are $\Theta(m/\log m)$ such $j$. Among them, only $O_V(\log m)$ can satisfy $Q_{T_j}(m)>2Q_{T_{j-C'}}(m)$. Indeed the sum of their log ratios is at most $C'\log_2(1/Q_{T_0}(m))=O_V(\log m)$, by telescoping and by the fast $O(\log m)$-bit name for $0^m$. At every remaining $j$, the selected words consequently carry a fixed positive fraction of the length-$m$ mass already published by $T_j$. This gives an all-length test with a set of possible terminal clocks. A prescribed individual clock can belong to the exceptional set.

For an integer $a\ge 0$, there are infinitely many dyadic blocks $[2^{s-1},2^s)$ in which a fraction $1-O(2^{-a})$ of the integers $m$ have an efficiently executable prefix name of length at most $K(m)+a+O(1)$ on a fixed reference, as proved in the [terminal-dictionary calculation](occupied-clock-cells.md). At every such $m$, the final selector for each good pattern $P$ can read this name followed by precisely $k$ bits for $P$. It publishes its word $y_P$ by $2^{4m}$ with name length at most $k+K(m)+a+O_V(1)$. The $y_P$ are distinct because their first $k$ bits are $P$.

Their guaranteed masses therefore sum to at least $c_V 2^{-K(m)-a}$ by the precise endpoint $2^{4m}$. Since $Q_\infty(m)$ is a lower semicomputable semimeasure of $m$, it is at most $c'_V 2^{-K(m)}$. At these lengths $G_{2^{4m}}(m)\ge c''_V 2^{-a}Q_\infty(m)$, comparing probability already published at the fixed endpoint to all eventual probability. ## Why exact positive increments are insufficient

Even unconditionally the word $0^m$ has a strictly positive mass increase in $\Omega_V(m/\log m)$ cells at every large $m$. A fixed native machine reads an integer $m$ and a unary delay $1^u0$, then prints $0^m$. For $u\gg m$, a literally compiled name must read all $u$ delay bits and publish within $O_V(u(\log u)^d)$ for one fixed $d$. Choose $u=2^{3m+Ajb}+1$ with $A$ large relative to $d$. This supplies positive mass strictly after the start and by the end of each of $\Theta(m/\log m)$ disjoint $A$-cell blocks. On the high-density lengths above, however, the same word already has $K_0\le K(m)+a+O_V(1)$ because the length name can print the zero word before $T_0$. Its unbounded complexity is at least $K(m)-O_V(1)$. Hence it has at most $a+O_V(1)$ genuine improvements in shortest length throughout the grid, despite all those strictly positive probability increments.
