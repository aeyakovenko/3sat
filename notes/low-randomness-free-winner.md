# Two independent PCP questions with sublogarithmic randomness for the actual priority bit

Let $M=2^j$. Either sign of the [priority computation](priority-pinned-sat.md) has a uniform, perfectly complete, constant-gap cooperative two-prover game using $O(\sqrt j)$ random bits, with total verifier work and communication $M/2^{\Omega(\sqrt j)}$. The two questions are independent. There is also a uniform tradeoff: for any fixed $1/2<\alpha<1$, work and communication are $M^{\alpha+o(1)}$ with at most $(2(1-\alpha)+o(1))j$ fair bits. The verifier uses the actual signed computation on its short scale metadata. These bounds make the cost of preserving the two provers' separate information material for the SAT question.

## An overlap distribution with controlled conditional entropy

Start with a uniformly generated perfectly complete PCP using $r$ fair coins, a fixed set of at most $\ell$ queried bit addresses per seed, and soundness $1-\delta$. Write $N=2^r$. Repeated addresses within a seed receive the same bit. Pad a short query list with fresh dummy addresses unique to that seed, ignored by the predicate, to make all lists have $\ell$ distinct entries. Only nonempty constant $\ell$ is needed. The proof may have highly nonuniform address frequencies.

Partition the seeds into left blocks $I$ of $2^k$ consecutive indices. For an address $x$ let $d_x$ count the seeds querying it. To couple two uniform seeds, choose $v$ uniformly, choose a uniform address $x\in Q(v)$, then choose $s$ uniformly among the $d_x$ seeds containing $x$. The transition probabilities are

$$P(v,s)=\sum_{x\in Q(v)\cap Q(s)}\frac1{\ell d_x}.$$

This matrix is symmetric and each row sums to one, so each column also sums to one. Conditional on $v\in I$, the distribution of $s$ has each atom at most $2^{-k}\sum_v P(v,s)=2^{-k}$. Two distributions will matter for each block: the seed uniform in $I$, and its image under $P$. Both have min-entropy at least $k$. None of the verifier's operations requires finding all seeds querying an address. [Geometric version of the overlap argument](affine-line-proof-game.md)

Let $h_d:\{0,1\}^r\to\{0,1\}^m$ be a family of balanced maps with an efficient algorithm to enumerate any one fiber; $d$ is sampled uniformly. Alice receives a uniformly chosen left block $I$ and reports one local answer for each test in it. Bob independently receives $(d,y)$ with uniform $y$ and reports one proof bit per distinct address used in $h_d^{-1}(y)$. The verifier checks every left test, and compares all occurrences of the addresses appearing on both sides. To implement this, enumerate the two collections of tests and sort their address lists, retaining occurrence labels. The honest proof wins on every pair. No tables for other blocks or for inverse address neighborhoods are supplied.

For a fixed $d$, write $D_0(d)$ for the average over left blocks of the total variation distance of $h_d(s)$ from uniform for $s$ drawn uniformly in that block. Write $D_1(d)$ for the same quantity for $s$ obtained by the overlap transition from a uniform seed in that block. If $p_d$ is the probability of rejection on independently uniform $(I,y)$, every pair of strategies on a false instance satisfies

$$
 p_d\ \ge\ \frac{\delta}{4\ell}-\frac{D_0(d)+D_1(d)}2 . \tag{1}
$$

Indeed, a fixed left strategy labels every seed occurrence, since left blocks partition the seeds. If a fraction at least $\delta/2$ of its seed tests fail locally, at least that fraction of left blocks contain a failure. Otherwise round each proof address to its majority bit across all occurrences. The resulting global proof fails on at least $\delta N$ seeds. At least $\delta N/2$ occurrences must differ from their majority, since each failing rounded test that passed locally contains such an occurrence. Under the overlap coupling, the probability that the two occurrence labels at its sampled address disagree is at least $\delta/(2\ell)$: at one address with counts $n_0,n_1$ the contribution is $2n_0n_1/((n_0+n_1)\ell N)\ge\min(n_0,n_1)/(\ell N)$. On a disagreement consider Bob's one answer for that address in the bucket containing $s$. At least one of the pairs $(I(v),h_d(s))$, $(I(s),h_d(s))$ rejects. Their rejection probabilities are at most $p_d+D_1(d)$ and $p_d+D_0(d)$ respectively. Summing proves (1). The two distance terms depend only on the query incidences. Even allowing strategy choices to depend on $d$ preserves the pointwise bound. Lean checks the shared-answer conflict, the global proof obtained from perfect independent strategies, weighted coverage of disagreements and the final arithmetic implication. The entropy calculation and the application of the PCP theorem are manuscript arguments. [Formal overlap lemmas](../lean/FreeGameOverlap.lean)

## An elementary sampler in the sublogarithmic-coin regime

Take $k=r-h$ and $m=h$. For a desired constant $\varepsilon>0$, take $L$ a power of two between $C h/\varepsilon^2$ and $2C h/\varepsilon^2$ for an absolute constant $C$. When $Lh\le r$, choose $L$ disjoint chunks of $h$ bits in the seed. $d$ selects one chunk and $h_d(s)$ is its value. Each fiber and its complementary coordinates are explicit. If $X$ is any min-entropy $r-h$ source, its Shannon entropy is at least $r-h$ bits. Entropy subadditivity over the disjoint chunks and unused coordinates gives

$$\sum_{d=1}^L(h-H(h_d(X)))\ \le\ r-H(X)\ \le h.$$

Pinsker's inequality with base-two entropy and Cauchy–Schwarz give average total variation at most $\sqrt{(\ln 2)h/(2L)}\le\varepsilon$. This applies separately to both sources for each $I$. Take $\varepsilon\le\delta/(16\ell)$. Averaging (1) gives a constant rejection probability. The questions use exactly $2h+\log_2 L$ random bits and each enumerates $2^{r-h}$ tests. One can take $h=\lfloor c\sqrt r\rfloor$ with a sufficiently small fixed positive $c$. The calculation only uses disjoint coordinate blocks and works for arbitrary PCP address patterns. ## A tunable power saving

For fixed $1/2<\alpha<1$, use $k=\lceil\alpha r\rceil$ and $m=r-k$. A linear strong seeded extractor at min-entropy $k$ has $m$ output bits, seed length $d_0=O_\alpha(\log^2 r)$ and any fixed constant error $\varepsilon$. Truncate the output of a construction with sufficient fixed output fraction, if necessary. For each fixed seed this map is linear in the $r$ input bits. The fraction of extractor seeds with rank below $m$ is at most $2\varepsilon$, by testing the extractor on a uniform $r$-bit string: for every such seed its output is at distance at least $1/2$ from uniform. At those seeds replace the matrix by the first $m$ coordinate projections. This preserves strong extraction with error at most $3\varepsilon$ by coupling and ensures every fiber has exactly $2^{r-m}$ elements. Evaluate the extractor on basis vectors to construct its matrix, then solve the linear system and enumerate the fiber by its nullspace basis. The extra setup and the work per enumerated point are polynomial in $r$. [Linear strong extractors, Theorem 10](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol353-approx-random2025/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2025.33/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2025.33.pdf)

By strong extraction, $\mathbb E_d D_i(d)\le3\varepsilon$ for both $i=0,1$. Choosing $3\varepsilon\le\delta/(16\ell)$ preserves constant soundness gap. Alice's block and Bob's fiber each contain $2^k$ seeds. Their independent questions use $(r-k)+m+d_0=2(1-\alpha)r+O_\alpha(\log^2 r+1)$ random bits up to rounding. The cost of the local PCP and of address sorting must be added to each enumerated seed. This result concerns uniform free games; standard birthday repetition and dense game approximation are prior work. The particular small-randomness tradeoff and its priority are unverified in the literature. [Aaronson, Impagliazzo and Moshkovitz](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.6848) · [Manurangsi and Raghavendra](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2017.78)

## Native application and the unresolved resource

Apply the local PCP theorem to the language containing every string of length $2^j$ exactly when the specified priority sign is correct. A nondeterministic multitape machine checks a guessed $O(M)$-step controller and memory log by chronological CPU checks, sorting the memory records and checking reads against previous values. Its time is bounded by $T=M\operatorname{poly}(j)$. Use the proximity verifier on the virtual zero string. A false length has no member at that length. With a fixed small constant parameter, Theorem 2.6 gives constant query count, perfect completeness, constant gap, $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ local work and a randomness cap $r=j+O(j^{1/3})$ for large $j$. Unfold the constant-depth adaptive query tree, if necessary, and pad unused coins. Use a nondecreasing bound on $T$ for other input lengths. The choice $h=\Theta(\sqrt r)$ dominates both the $O(j^{1/3})$ overhead and the additional polynomial-in-$j$ work per seed. This proves the two resource statements at the start, for the actual bit at every sufficiently large scale. The small constants and the fixed verifier are independent of the scale. [Ben-Sasson et al., Definitions 2.1–2.4 and Theorem 2.6](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/polylogPCP.pdf) · [Native application with explicit oracle conventions](affine-line-proof-game.md)

At the elementary parameters there are $2^h$ Alice blocks and her prescribed message has $\ell 2^{r-h}$ bits. Listing her response on every question costs $\ell 2^r$ bits before including Bob. Giving one prover both questions also changes the consistency requirement: it can now choose new Alice labels for each bucket. Hence few coins and sublinear work in this genuine protocol do not yet give a single-prover verification or an ordinary SAT contradiction. The missing improvement would have to preserve the relevant strategies implicitly. A compiler capable of decoding **all** binary codewords of a $K$-bit message from all $B$ blocks with $a$ bits per block satisfies $Ba\ge K$ by injectivity. Two such fixed partitions require $a_1a_2\ge K^2/(B_1B_2)$. This explains a limitation of block interpolation arguments that must serve arbitrary messages. The actual winner's canonical proof may have additional structure.

## Testing the change to one prover on the same priority predicate

Fix one Alice block $I$, and now tell Bob which block it is. Fix an Alice reply passing its own tests. Let $D$ be the set of addresses for which two local tests in that reply supplied different bits. An informed Bob can answer a bucket exactly when none of its addresses is in $D$: use the common bit on every constrained address, and extend the rest arbitrarily. Since the right questions cover every address used by the block, this proves

$$
 \exists A_I\ \forall (d,y)\ \exists B_{I,d,y}\ [\text{accept}]
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \text{one partial proof satisfies every original PCP test in }I.
$$

In particular, both sides have perfect informed strategies on a false input if every left block separately has a satisfying assignment. Soundness against this leakage therefore asks for an unsatisfiable block of the *PCP* checks. One can test a named block by generating its constant-arity test circuit and running SAT on its conjunction, with one shared Boolean variable per proof address. At the elementary parameters guessing the block and compiling this test, even after a fixed number of quasilinear SAT simulations under a linear-SAT hypothesis, takes $o(M)$ since $h=\Omega(\sqrt j)$. Thus a PCP for each sign with some unsatisfiable block on every false claim, and all blocks feasible on true claims, really would exclude a linear-time SAT algorithm by deciding the hard sign. The established PCP gap certifies only the original information order. For time $n^k$ the two existential searches with an inner complementary test can cost the $k^2$ power of the block budget. Smaller left blocks would be needed for this route at $k=2,3$. [Native sign lower bound](priority-pinned-sat.md)

There is a simple uniform experiment using the actual priority predicate. Augment its PCP with one common proof bit selecting an alternative verification mode. In the ordinary mode run the original test. In the new mode read one bit at address $s$ with the first bit of its left-block index removed, in a separate proof bank. Require its value to equal the removed bit. Every global proof in this mode succeeds on exactly half of the uniform seeds. Thus the modified PCP keeps perfect completeness and a fixed soundness gap for the **same signs at every scale**; a mode bit and a tagged fallback address only add a constant number of queries. Any seed padding needed to unfold its query tree can preserve the same public index layout. Yet on every false claim the left prover can pass all its local tests in the new mode by using its block's first bit throughout. If Bob learns that one bit, he matches the mode and returns that bit at every fallback address. The two then pass every question pair. The independently sampled game still has a constant soundness gap by (1). This is a uniform counterexample to moving even that one bit across the prover boundary based solely on the established resources and perfect completeness. A verifier exploiting the code can recognize and discard this added mode. The information-order equivalence above remains a concrete test for the unmodified PCP.
