# One nearly perfect packing is hard for polynomial calculus over every field

There are simple cubic exact-cover instances with $q$ indexed triples and $q$ points, for unbounded $q$, for which all of the following hold at once.

* A supplied disjoint packing leaves three points uncovered, and a supplied signed cover has one entry $-1$ and all other entries Boolean. There are explicit satisfiable cyclic lifts of every degree above one.
* The family comes from a parsimonious reduction in which every perfect cover, whenever one exists, differs from the packing along a single simple alternating path.
* On the infeasible members, polynomial calculus (PC) refutations require degree $\Omega(q)$ and total monomial size $2^{\Omega(q)}$ **over every field on the same instances**. Real sum-of-squares refutations also require degree $\Omega(q)$. Here the input size in the explicit two-permutation format is $N=\Theta(q\log q)$ bits.

These are proof-complexity lower bounds for the specified equations. A deterministic RAM is free to decide whether a repair exists by other means. The hard source systems here themselves have a linear-word-time decision by adding charges on each connected component.

## A source family independent of the field

Fix sufficiently expanding graphs of a constant even degree $r$ on $t\equiv1\pmod6$ vertices for arbitrarily large $t$. Such simple graphs exist on all sufficiently large admissible sizes by the two-sided eigenvalue bound for random regular graphs. For fixed large even $r$, Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the neighbor-count vector gives $|N(S)|/|S|\ge1/(|S|/t+(\lambda/r)^2(1-|S|/t))$, with $\lambda<r$ bounding the absolute nontrivial eigenvalues. This supplies both expansion conditions below. [Bordenave, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04482) For $p=2$ take the ordinary Tseitin instance with every vertex charge one and a Boolean edge bit. For $p=3$ take the Boolean indicator version on the two orientations of each edge, with opposite directed values summing to zero and outgoing values at a vertex summing to one modulo three. Both are inconsistent by totaling charges. Their bounded-width CNFs, denoted $G_2,G_3$, each have $O(t)$ variables and clauses. The lower bounds used below are those of Buss, Grigoriev, Impagliazzo and Pitassi (BGIP), Theorems 10 and 15, Lemmas 1–3 and Corollary 21. For any field of characteristic different from two the ordinary Boolean mod-two instance requires PC degree $\Omega(t)$, including characteristic zero by $Y_e=1-2X_e$ in Theorem 10. In characteristic two use the mod-three instance and Corollary 21. Its proof allows extension fields when a primitive cubic root is needed. The constants can be chosen uniformly here since only $p=2,3$ and fixed $r$ are used. [BGIP, *Linear Gaps Between Degrees for the Polynomial Calculus Modulo Distinct Primes*](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/lineargaps/journalfinal.pdf)

The root-of-unity transfer can also be checked directly. In characteristic two extend scalars to contain a primitive cubic root $\omega$. In BGIP's system with $Y_e^3=1$ replace each charge indicator $X_{e,a}$ by $\frac13\sum_{j=0}^{2}(\omega^{-a}Y_e)^j$. This is one precisely when $Y_e=\omega^a$ on the three roots. A local clause residual that fails on a root tuple must violate an associated vertex, edge or domain condition there. Modulo the edge root axioms, interpolate on the bounded Cartesian grid of roots: on each offending tuple divide by one nonzero violated equation and multiply by that tuple's point indicator. The sum expresses the local residual in constant degree. One-hot axioms hold identically on the grid; multilinear reduction here means reducing each exponent modulo the monic $Y_e^3-1$. This translates any low-degree Boolean proof over the original field into a low-degree proof of the root-of-unity system over the extension, contradicting BGIP Theorem 15. For $p=2$ in all other characteristics use $X_e=(1-Y_e)/2$ and the same two-point interpolation. This argument makes the direction of the reduction explicit.

Form one disjunction by giving each clause of $G_2$ an extra literal $\neg s$ and each clause of $G_3$ an extra literal $s$, on disjoint variables. No Cartesian product of clause lists is required. Replace each gated clause by its own constant-size definitional chain of OR gates and require its output true. Pad short clauses if desired. The result $F$ has $O(t)$ ternary clauses. In either choice of field, fix $s$ to activate the hard branch and set variables of the inactive branch to constants. Every new gate can be given its honest value as a Boolean function of the boundedly many bits of its one clause. Every gate relation then holds identically; failure of a final assertion on the active side is exactly failure of its source clause. This explains why a field chosen after the particular instance is known still encounters a degree lower bound.

## Local substitution into the physical packing

Apply the [path augmentation compiler](path-augmentations-with-three-uncovered-points.md) to $F$. The number of proper indexed triples and rows is $q=\Theta(t)$. The equations use a variable $x_j$ per column, Boolean axioms $x_j^2-x_j=0$, and, for each row with columns $a,b,c$, the following four equations:
$$
              x_a+x_b+x_c-1=0,\qquad
              x_a x_b=x_a x_c=x_b x_c=0 .
$$
In characteristic two the three pair equations are essential: the row-sum equation by itself would accept three selected columns. The four equations enforce the same exact-one predicate over every field.

For **every** assignment to the ternary source take its variable tracks, and use the first true clause option, using the first option as the default for an entirely false clause. Turn on its arc columns and terminal, turn on the old column of a color if neither of its vertices is visited, and set the forced filler columns to their fixed values. This assignment to packing columns is defined even when the path repeats a color. Each arc decision depends on just one variable choice or the bounded number of bits in one clause stage; unconditional junctions separate the stages. A paired color links one test and its blocker for a literal in that very clause. Its old-column indicator also depends on just the bits in that clause. In a false clause the default option has one test and collides with its blocker. This produces exactly one extra unit at each of the three rows of that color. Other rows have sum one. These facts are established by the arc conservation calculation in the path proof, independently of any PC semantics.

Consequently a substituted row equation on the Boolean cube either vanishes identically or can fail only if one identified source clause is false. The same holds for each pair equation: a nonzero pair requires at least two ones at its row. Each row uses only a bounded number of original bits. Composition with the disjunction restriction and the gate values preserves this property. A possible failure belongs to one active original clause. For an OR gate relation there is no failure; for a final assertion the original clause directly gives it. All substituted variables are total Boolean functions on that cube. Their unique multilinear interpolation polynomials have bounded degree over *any* field; use the products of bits and their complements as point indicators.

Here is the degree bookkeeping. If such a substituted axiom $g$ can be nonzero only when a clause violation indicator $f$ is one, then $g=gf$ modulo Boolean axioms. Derive $gf$ from $f$ by monomial multiplications and addition, and reduce powers by the Boolean axioms. Both polynomials involve only a constant number of bits and have bounded degree. An identically vanishing axiom follows from Boolean axioms alone the same way. Replacing a target variable in each PC line by its interpolation polynomial changes a degree-$D$ refutation into one of degree $O(D+1)$. To simulate multiplication by a substituted polynomial, derive its monomial multiples separately; the degree at such a step is bounded by the previous substituted degree plus the constant substitution degree. BGIP's lower bound gives $D=\Omega(t)$. The size–degree theorem for bounded-degree axioms on $q$ Boolean variables then gives at least $2^{\Omega(q)}$ monomials in the proof. The count is over the entire derivation. [Impagliazzo, Pudlák and Sgall, §5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/1997/042/)

For the real sum-of-squares bound choose the mod-two branch. Grigoriev's degree bound holds on sign variables and transfers by the affine change from bits to signs. A substituted equality that is zero on the locally satisfying sign patterns is a bounded-degree multiple of the original vertex parity residual modulo $Y_e^2=1$: interpolate the ratio at violating patterns (the residual there is nonzero) and set the multiplier to zero elsewhere. Squares stay squares under substitution. This transfers a low-degree static real refutation as well. The [code-weight argument](code-weight-and-sum-of-squares-for-one-negative-covers.md) gives the corresponding two-unit coset distance promise. [Grigoriev, §3, Corollary 1](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~grigorev/pub/square_journal.pdf)

The PC bound for deriving $1$ also extends to any nonzero unital commutative coefficient ring by mapping the entire purported derivation to a residue field. This uses the unit as the contradiction; a definition that accepts any nonzero constant over a ring would require a separate argument.

## Adding local tables

One may add extension bits for arbitrary truth tables on at most $k$ *original packing variables* each. Use Boolean axioms and the usual defining graph-of-a-function clauses. Substituting the truth function for its new bit replaces an extension by a degree-$O(k)$ source polynomial. Each of its defining equations vanishes identically on the relevant Boolean cube, with a Boolean-axiom derivation of degree $O(k+1)$: a defining-clause indicator is a product of at most $k+1$ literals, with only one extension bit. Simulating a degree-$D$ proof this way gives degree $O((k+1)(D+1))$ at the hard source. Thus $D+1=\Omega(q/(k+1))$, irrespective of the number of supplied local functions. For $k=O(\log q)$ and $O(q)$ extension variables the size–degree theorem still gives size $\exp(\Omega(q/\log^2 q))$. This argument does not allow an extension to address a previously computed table at an arbitrary data-dependent location or depend recursively on other extension outputs. The distinction matters for simulation of a word RAM. For prior PC lower bounds with local extension variables see [Impagliazzo, Mouli and Pitassi, CCC 2023](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol264-ccc2023/LIPIcs.CCC.2023.7/LIPIcs.CCC.2023.7.pdf).

The source lower bounds and local degree transformations are manuscript arguments. The [path audit](../audits/path_augmentation_audit.py) tests the local residual identity on 601 assignments. The nearby Lean weight theorem verifies the integer count for the code interpretation. It does not formalize the field-uniform refutation theorem.
