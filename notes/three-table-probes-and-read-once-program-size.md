# Three table probes and the size of a read-once program

Let \(d=2^w\), \(w\ge1\). The input consists of three \(d\)-square Boolean tables and three \(w\)-bit selectors. The decision bit is
$$
                f_d(A,B,Z,i,k,j)= A_{ik}\land B_{kj}\land Z_{ji}.
$$
There is a subcube of proper ASCII DIMACS files of \(N=\Theta(d^2)\) bits on which this is exactly the SAT verdict. Every table or selector bit controls one distinct physical bit. The other bits are fixed, and every positive file has exactly one model. With the selectors in hand, three table probes suffice. [Physical encoding and arithmetic exponent of this same verdict](three-lookups-and-the-multilinear-sat-verdict.md)

| Representation on the subcube | SAT | UNSAT |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deterministic syntactic read-once branching program | \(\Theta(d^3)\) | \(\Theta(d^3)\) |
| Nondeterministic syntactic read-once branching program | \(\Theta(d^3)\) | \(\Theta(d^2)\) |
| Decomposable negation normal form (DNNF) | \(\Theta(d^3)\) | \(\Theta(d^2)\) |
| General bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit | \(\Theta(d^2)\) | \(\Theta(d^2)\) |

Thus the cubic entries are \(\Theta(N^{3/2})\) on this particular subcube. The argument holds independently of the matrix multiplication exponent. Its ingredients include the classical monotone arithmetic lower bound for the triangle polynomial. The tripartite triangle support is also the colorful subgraph polynomial for \(K_3\) in the treewidth theorem of Komarath, Pandey and Rahul. The bounds for unrestricted SAT files in these read-once models are larger. [Schnorr's monotone circuit bound](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0304397576900839) · [Jukna on the triangle polynomial](https://web.vu.lt/mif/s.jukna/ftp/counting-web.pdf) · [Komarath, Pandey and Rahul, Theorem 16](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2022.83) · [Read-once size of full SAT and UNSAT](read-once-size-of-sat-and-unsat.md)

Programs are nonuniform directed acyclic graphs with binary queries. A nondeterministic program may also have choice nodes and accepts if a consistent accepting path exists. Syntactic read-once means that no variable is queried twice on any source-to-sink path. DNNFs have AND and OR gates and constants or literals as leaves. The input-variable sets of different children of an AND gate must be disjoint. OR children can overlap and can accept the same assignment. Sharing is unrestricted in both models. Size counts nodes and wires, equivalently nodes after binarizing fan-in at gates or fan-out at nondeterministic choice nodes. Deterministic queries have two outgoing arcs. [Darwiche on DNNF](https://doi.org/10.1145/502090.502091) · [Beame, Li, Roy and Suciu on read-once programs and knowledge compilation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.6815)

## Extracting the tripartite triangles

Take any DNNF for \(f_d\). Interpret it as an arithmetic circuit over the nonnegative integers. Give each positive table literal its own variable, each negative table literal weight one, and every selector literal weight one. The Boolean constants retain their values. Replace OR by addition and AND by multiplication. A parse tree chooses one child at each OR and includes both children at each binary AND, unrolling shared gates on their uses.

Every nonzero parse tree of the original circuit fixes a consistent set of literals. Decomposability proves this by induction at each AND, and also prohibits any repeated variable within one parse tree. Every total assignment extending the selected literals accepts. Fix an arbitrary consistent choice of selectors and set all table bits outside its positive literals to zero. The addressed triple must then belong to the positive literals. In particular their number is at least three. If it is exactly three, the resulting monomial is a triangle \(A_{ik}B_{kj}Z_{ji}\).

Conversely, select any \(i,k,j\) and set exactly those three table bits to one. The true assignment has an accepting parse tree. Its positive table literals are a subset of those three bits and must contain an addressed triple. They give that very monomial. The homogeneous component of degree three therefore has support consisting of all \(d^3\) tripartite triangles. Coefficients can exceed one because several parse trees can certify the same assignment. Nonnegative arithmetic preserves their support. Taking homogeneous components of degrees zero through three at every gate increases size by a constant factor. Use addition coordinatewise and, for a product, sum the products of components whose degrees add to the requested degree.

Consider one of these homogeneous circuits after removing zero gates and gates with no productive path to the degree-three output. For any productive degree-two gate, choose one route from that gate to the output, and choose one nonzero monomial at each sibling of a multiplication along the route. The product of the chosen siblings is a scalar times a single edge variable \(e\), by the degree difference. Hence **every** degree-two monomial at the gate must complete to a triangle together with this same \(e\). To check this even with sharing, unfold the circuit to a parse tree along the chosen route and hold the sibling choices fixed. Each choice of a monomial at the original gate still contributes, since no term cancels.

For fixed \(e=A_{ik}\), the allowable pairs are \(B_{kj},Z_{ji}\) as \(j\) varies. They form a matching on edge-variable names. The other two types of \(e\) have the same property. At a productive product of two linear forms the Cartesian product of their supports must consist entirely of these allowed pairs. Fix a variable from one support. It has just one partner, forcing the other support to be a singleton. Reversing the roles forces the first support to be a singleton as well. Thus every productive multiplication of degrees one and one creates a single pair. Such a pair determines its completing triangle uniquely.

Every degree-three output monomial has a parse in the homogenized circuit that multiplies two degree-one expressions somewhere. This follows by following a branch of positive degree until the first binary product combines positive degrees, or directly by pruning constant factors from the three-leaf multiplicative parse. A pair produced this way can be used in only its one completing triangle. The \(d^3\) output monomials therefore require at least \(d^3\) such gates in the homogenized circuit. This gives the \(\Omega(d^3)\) lower bound for the original DNNF. The proof uses the support and applies regardless of the triangle coefficients. Representations of joins by union and product circuits are a related database model. [Berkholz and Vinall-Smeeth on factorised join representations](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICDT.2026.11)

## Programs and the complementary verdict

A syntactic read-once query \(x\) with children \(G_0,G_1\) gives the decomposable disjunction \((\neg x\land G_0)\lor(x\land G_1)\). No suffix from either child can query \(x\) again. A nondeterministic choice gives an OR of its successors. Make one shared gate construction per program node. This proves the same lower bound for both program models. A deterministic upper bound first decodes all selectors in a binary tree and then tests the three named bits at each leaf. It has \(O(d^3)\) nodes. Swapping the two sinks preserves deterministic size for the complementary verdict.

For nondeterministic UNSAT, choose which of the three entries is zero, read just the two relevant selectors and test that table entry. Each of the three branches costs \(O(d^2)\) nodes and is read-once. Its DNNF conversion has the same order. Every table variable affects the function on some input, so all \(3d^2\) names must be queried or used as literals in these representations. General Boolean circuits for either sign use three ordinary multiplexer trees of total size \(O(d^2)\). Binary fan-in and the same essential variables give the matching ordinary circuit lower bound.

A randomized read-once program can check any **two** selected entries with \(O(d^2)\) nodes. The two edges share an index. Read and retain this common selector, read the first other selector and test its entry, then discard that other selector and read the last one for the second lookup. Choose the omitted edge uniformly among the three. This program accepts every positive input, and on a negative input it can accept only when all its zero entries lie in the omitted edge, giving error at most \(1/3\). This precise perfect-completeness bound uses a uniform ternary choice. With finitely many fair binary choices, omit the three edges in proportions \(3/8,3/8,2/8\) to obtain perfect completeness and error at most \(3/8\). To obtain the customary two-sided error bound \(1/3\) with fair bits, accept a successful pair with probability \(7/8\). Its acceptance probability is \(7/8\) on positive inputs and at most \(21/64\) on negative inputs. Bounded error still requires all data bits to occur somewhere, hence \(\Theta(d^2)\) size is sharp. In the opposite direction, perfect soundness and positive acceptance probability on every positive input force \(\Omega(d^3)\) size. Keeping just the positive-probability coin arcs gives a correct nondeterministic read-once program.

## Treewidth and pathwidth for a fixed graph of lookups

Let \(H\) be a fixed finite connected simple graph with at least one edge. Give each vertex \(v\) its own public selector \(s_v\in[d]\) and each edge \(uv\) its own independent table \(X^{uv}\), with a fixed order of its two endpoints. Accept if every \(X^{uv}_{s_u,s_v}\) is one. One physical selector pin per selector bit and one copy of the chunk library per edge give the same proper unique-or-zero DIMACS construction of length \(N=\Theta_H(d^2)\). With the selector values given, the decision requires exactly \(|E(H)|\) table probes in the worst case. On the all-one input, every addressed bit must be inspected. The exact positive representation sizes, up to constants depending on \(H\), are

| Representation | Size |
| --- | --- |
| DNNF | \(\Theta_H(d^{\operatorname{tw}(H)+1})\) |
| Nondeterministic or deterministic syntactic read-once branching program | \(\Theta_H(d^{\operatorname{pw}(H)+1})\) |

Komarath, Pandey and Rahul obtained the treewidth and pathwidth exponents for monotone arithmetic circuits and algebraic branching programs from parse decompositions of the colorful subgraph polynomial. The corresponding exponents here follow from their support argument. For a DNNF, use the literal weights above. For a read-once program, sum accepting-path weights, using the queried variable on a positive table arc and weight one on the other arcs. In both cases keep the component containing one variable from every edge table and no others. This costs a constant factor for fixed \(H\): at each gate or path state track the subset of edge types, discarding products or steps that repeat a type. For products create one gate per disjoint partition of the subset. Every retained term is a colorful copy, since its consistent certificate must still accept when just its positive bits are one. Conversely each of the \(d^{|V(H)|}\) vertex labelings gives an accepting sparse input and hence a retained term. Every vertex has an incident edge, so the labelings give distinct monomials. Two certificates for the same copy can increase its coefficient. The following counting argument allows any positive multiplicities. [Komarath, Pandey and Rahul, Definition 11 and Theorems 16–17](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol229-icalp2022/LIPIcs.ICALP.2022.83/LIPIcs.ICALP.2022.83.pdf) · [Full path-decomposition argument](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.04778)

For the circuit, choose a parse of a retained monomial, suppress sum choices and constant factors, and put the endpoints of each edge at its input leaf. At an internal node put every vertex whose incident edge leaves occur in two or more of the three parts (left child, right child, outside). For each vertex the bags containing it form the minimal subtree connecting its incident leaves. The leaf bags cover every edge. This is a tree decomposition, so some bag has at least \(\operatorname{tw}(H)+1\) vertices. The gate at such a bag fixes the labels of all its bag vertices throughout every productive parse that uses it. At a leaf this follows from its table indices. At a product the two child type sets are fixed. A vertex shared by the children must receive the same label across all parses through the gate, because one may combine either left child parse with either right child parse. For a vertex shared with the outside, one may replace the entire subparse at this gate in any fixed outer context. A disagreement in either exchange would give a forbidden output monomial. Thus a gate with a large bag covers at most \(d^{|V(H)|-\operatorname{tw}(H)-1}\) distinct labelings. Counting gives the DNNF lower bound.

For a program, order the edges of \(H\) by the positive data arcs on an accepting path of the retained component. At each such arc put every vertex whose interval from first to last incident edge contains that position. The bags form a path decomposition. The arc fixes its own edge and labels. Every other vertex of its bag occurs in both the prefix and suffix. The prefix edge-type set is recorded in the state, and combining any prefix at that arc with any suffix must yield a valid monomial. This forces one common label on every bag vertex across paths using the arc. Some arc on each path therefore covers at most \(d^{|V(H)|-\operatorname{pw}(H)-1}\) labelings. The same count gives the program lower bound.

For the DNNF upper bound, root a minimum-width tree decomposition. Assign each vertex to one bag containing it and each edge to one bag containing both endpoints. For every bag labeling, take the AND of its assigned selector equalities, its assigned data bits at the given labels, and its child messages for the prescribed intersection labels. To send a message to the parent, OR over the bag labelings extending the parent intersection. Every selector bank and every edge table has one owner, so the AND children have disjoint input variables. Agreement on the intersections gives one global labeling, which the selector equalities pin. All equality tests for the \(d\) values of one binary selector together use \(O(d)\) decomposable gates by sharing prefixes. The total size is \(O_H(d^{\operatorname{tw}(H)+1})\). For the deterministic program upper bound, walk a path decomposition that introduces and forgets one vertex at a time. At introduction read its selector by a binary tree, at each edge's assigned bag check its addressed entry once, and at forgetting merge states with identical remaining labels. Introducing a vertex costs \(O(d)\) nodes per preceding bag assignment, which is \(O(d^{\operatorname{pw}(H)+1})\) in total at that stage. No selector or edge table needs a second query to the same bit.

For example \(H=K_7\) has treewidth and pathwidth six. Its \(21\) indexed table probes give a SAT cube of length \(\Theta(d^2)\) on which both positive sizes are \(\Theta(d^7)=\Theta(N^{7/2})\). A general bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit for the same input still has size \(\Theta_H(d^2)\) by multiplexing. Nondeterministic read-once programs and DNNFs for the complementary verdict also have size \(\Theta_H(d^2)\): choose an edge and test its one selected entry for zero. These are costs of representing all input-dependent choices. Each deterministic positive path needs only \(O_H(\log d)\) bit queries.

## The price of rereading selected bits

Designate at most \(t\) selector bits, with \(0\le t\le w\), that may be reread without a bound. Require every other input bit to be queried at most once on each syntactic path. For the positive verdict the optimal program size, when the choice of those \(t\) bits is part of the design, is
$$
                  \Theta\!\left(\max\{d^2,d^3/2^t\}\right)
$$
in both branching program models. The lower bound holds for **every** fixed designation of that size. The upper bound designates \(t\) bits of one selector. A choice of budget depending on the actual path is a different model.

Indeed, first read all designated bits in a binary decision tree. At each leaf put a copy of the program with the designated tests replaced by their now-known successors. The new program is syntactic read-once and has size \(O(2^t(s+1))\) when the given size is \(s\). Thus \(s=\Omega(d^3/2^t)\), in addition to essential-variable cost. The same proof gives this lower bound for Boolean negation normal forms in which every intersection between AND-children's variable sets lies in the fixed designation. Conditioning makes each leaf circuit a DNNF, and putting the branches back by a binary decision tree preserves decomposability. The program upper bound below converts back to this relaxed DNNF too, since a repeated selector is the only possible overlap in a literal-guarded query.

For the upper bound take the high \(t\) bits of \(i\). Retain its low \(w-t\) bits throughout. Read \(k\) and the high bits of \(i\), test \(A_{ik}\), and discard the high bits. Read \(j\) and test \(B_{kj}\), retaining the low part of \(i\) and then discarding \(k\). Finally reread the high bits of \(i\) and test \(Z_{ji}\). Halt with zero as soon as a tested data bit is zero. The first lookup uses \(O(d^2)\) nodes. For each of \(d/2^t\) low values and \(d\) choices of \(k\), the middle lookup uses an \(O(d)\)-node tree on \(j\). Share a final high-bit tree across all successful prefixes with the same low part and \(j\). The final stage uses \(O((d/2^t)d\,2^t)=O(d^2)\) nodes. All tests outside the designation occur only once, and a designated bit is tested at most twice. With \(t=w\), rereading one selector reduces program size to \(\Theta(d^2)\). The same designated-bit tradeoff applies on the negative side for deterministic programs by swapping sinks.

Fixing all public DIMACS positions and renaming each remaining input bit passes any promised physical representation to the raw function with no increase in size. The displayed upper bounds pass back to the cube by directly naming its varying positions. The comparison charges the size of the program graph; the length of a path on these inputs is \(O(\log d)\). The counts give no superlinear time lower bound for a general RAM.

At all four sizes it covers every designation size \(0,\ldots,w\) in the stated construction. It checks the read restrictions across every syntactic path using unions of descendant query sets, and verifies the matching and unique-completion properties of all edge pairs lying in triangles.
