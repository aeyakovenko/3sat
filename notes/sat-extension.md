# What the VP–VNP separation implies for SAT encodings

**Research journal, 2026-11-23.** Anthropic's November 23 preprint proves a lower bound of m^{c(log₂ m)^{1/3}} gates for the formal Hamiltonian cycle polynomial HCₘ over every field, and for the permanent outside characteristic two. The constant c is absolute and the threshold for m may depend on the characteristic. Its published Lean statement uses exact equality of multivariate polynomials for a circuit with arbitrary field constants and binary addition and multiplication. We inspected the statement and the successful proof-checking job at the release commit. That job built the proof and ran the repository's comparator. Our checks here use that published result. [Paper, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00954v1) · [Formal statement](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L39-L47) · [Verification job](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/actions/runs/45272623486/job/138036167794)

## A previously conditional obstruction now applies

Hrubeš's Theorem 1 shows over any field that the canonical multilinear extensions of an explicit family of monotone 2-CNFs are VNP-hard in the sense of his reduction. Consequently VP ≠ VNP rules out polynomial-size formal arithmetic circuits for all those extensions. The Boolean test, given the assignment, just checks a list of two-literal clauses. This answers a question in our [field-evaluation attempt](algebraic-residuals.md): a polynomial-time Boolean comparison alone cannot guarantee a small formal arithmetic circuit for its exact extension. The SAT simulation in that note only needs evaluations on bit encodings of points of one specified finite field. A Boolean program for those evaluations would still suffice there. [Hrubeš, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2](https://users.math.cas.cz/~hrubes/PDFs/Mult.pdf#page=3)

## A quantitative example and a restriction of the SAT truth table

Fix a field K. Let cₘ(a) test whether the edges present in an m-by-m Boolean adjacency matrix themselves form one directed Hamiltonian cycle, with m≥2. Scan the matrix to find the unique successor of each vertex and check indegrees. Follow successors from vertex one to verify a single orbit of length m. This takes O(m²) word operations. The canonical multilinear extension, as a formal polynomial, is

    Lₘ(X) = Σ_H  ∏_{e∈H} Xₑ  ∏_{e∉H} (1−Xₑ),

with H ranging over the directed cycles visiting every labeled vertex once. Each indicator product matches its own adjacency matrix on the Boolean cube and vanishes on every other one. Its degree-m homogeneous component is just ∏_{e∈H}Xₑ. Thus the degree-m component of Lₘ is HCₘ, also in characteristic two. From any size-s arithmetic circuit for Lₘ, retain at each gate the components of degrees zero through m. Addition is componentwise and multiplication uses truncated convolution. This gives a circuit for HCₘ of size O((s+1)(m+1)²), when input gates are counted. Dividing the published bound by that polynomial overhead still leaves m^{Ω((log m)^{1/3})}. The same extraction over Laurent series preserves the constant coefficient of each output coefficient when a circuit approximates Lₘ in the preprint's border sense. This deduction uses standard homogeneous-component extraction. [Hrubeš, Lemma 4](https://users.math.cas.cz/~hrubes/PDFs/Mult.pdf#page=4) · [Anthropic, exact and approximate lower bounds](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/11b6c7e3c927df2658f513b6b68ea7f8929c62c5/vp-vnp/Statement.lean#L39-L60)

Now let Sₙ be the canonical multilinear extension over K of the SAT decision bit on n-bit strings. Use an explicit binary CNF encoding in which each literal occurrence has its own sign bit and changing that bit preserves length and well-formedness. Choose any fixed convention on malformed strings. There exists a length Nₘ, polynomial in m and at least m², with the following restriction.

1. Build a polynomial-size Boolean circuit for cₘ on variables aₑ. Encode its gates and the requirement that the output be true as a fixed CNF. For a fixed assignment to the aₑ this CNF has an extension to its gate variables exactly when cₘ(a)=1.
2. Append one unit clause per aₑ. Its sign is positive for desired input bit one and negative for zero. Under the convention that sign bit one means negative, the bit in the encoding is 1−xₑ. Freeze every other encoding bit. For width exactly three with distinct variables, replace a unit by a clause with its literal and two fixed-false padding variables. A padding variable p can be forced false by four clauses (¬p ∨ ±u ∨ ±v) using all four sign pairs and fresh distinct u,v. Short fixed gate clauses can be padded the same way.
3. All assignments to the unfrozen bits describe valid formulas. On the Boolean cube, SAT on this restriction equals cₘ. Each xₑ occurs in exactly one encoding position. Substituting constants and xₑ or 1−xₑ into S_{Nₘ} therefore preserves multilinearity. Uniqueness of multilinear interpolation gives the formal identity with Lₘ over K. A reduction that copies one live bit into several positions would require a separate multilinearization step.

It follows that **the canonical multilinear extension of this SAT encoding requires N^{Ω((log N)^{1/3})} arithmetic gates at infinitely many encoded lengths N**. The change of parameter uses m²≤Nₘ≤mᴬ for a fixed integer exponent A. The restriction increases circuit size by at most O(m²) gates for the sign complements. Every length Nₘ in the lower bound already has a restriction on which the Boolean decision takes O(m²) word operations when given the adjacency bits. Even ruling out worst-case O(n) time for deciding SAT on a deterministic word RAM with unrestricted workspace remains open, where n counts the encoded input bits. Computing the exact multilinear extension is an additional requirement. Known SAT time–space lower bounds impose a simultaneous restriction on workspace. [Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas](https://doi.org/10.1145/1101821.1101822)

[SignRestriction.lean](../lean/SignRestriction.lean) verifies the semantics of pinning a verifier's input and the identity for restricting Shannon interpolation to a face of the cube, including complemented coordinates. The CNF encoding, homogeneous-component extraction and size estimate above are manuscript arguments. The projection is a corollary of the published circuit lower bound. We have not established research priority for this particular SAT formulation.

## Where direct transfer fails

Section 2.5 of the preprint identifies two places where the proof inspects the internal polynomial of a putative circuit. One extracts terms linear in each variable block, the other extracts low-degree terms. A Boolean arithmetization can add any multiple of xᵢ²−xᵢ. Such an addition preserves every answer on the cube while changing both components. The paper's substitutions into HC preserve Boolean inputs. Obtaining the requisite bounds for a representative computed by a fast Boolean solver is the unresolved step. Even for the easy test cₘ above, the coefficient of any specified degree-m squarefree monomial in Lₘ can be found by checking one proposed cycle, while computing the full formal degree-m part requires superpolynomially many arithmetic gates. [Preprint, §2.5](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00954v1#page=10)
