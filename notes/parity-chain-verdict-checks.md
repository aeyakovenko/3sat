# Short rejection proofs checked by local input queries

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Consider a sound UNSAT proof format that takes one shared $s$-bit description and then tests it against every challenge. May all rejecting inputs of a fast SAT solver have such proofs if each challenge reads at most $q$ packed input words? Even on an explicit family of parity constraints, the necessary and attainable tradeoff is
$$
  (s+1)q=\Omega(N/\log N)
$$
for encoded formula length $N$ and $q\ge1$. The inequality is independent of the solver, its workspace and the number of challenges. This applies even when the proof claims only the rejection verdict.

## A uniform family with one charge per constraint

For $x\in\{0,1\}^n$, use variables $u_0,\ldots,u_n$. Require $u_0=u_n=0$ and impose $u_i=u_{i-1}\mathbin{\oplus}x_i$ for $1\le i\le n$. At charge zero use the two clauses for equality. At charge one use $(u_{i-1}\lor u_i)$ and $(\neg u_{i-1}\lor\neg u_i)$. Duplicate a literal to make each binary clause a triple and repeat the unit in the endpoint clauses. Propagation from $u_0=0$ forces $u_n$ to equal the XOR of the charges, so the formula $F_x$ is unsatisfiable precisely for odd parity.

Give each clause three fixed-width signed binary variable indices, using $\Theta(\log(n+2))$ bits per index. Every $x$ at the same $n$ has the same layout, header and length $N=\Theta(n\log(n+2))$. Only signs in the two clauses for $i$ depend on $x_i$. A packed read of $O(\log N)$ consecutive bits intersects a constant number $C$ of charge records, including unaligned reads. Its value therefore depends on at most $C$ charge bits. The formula can be written by a fixed program in $O(N)$ time. Given a packed-word index, the intersected records can also be located arithmetically from $n$ and their common stride. [Packed formula convention](packed-band-transfer.md)

## Adaptive checks and the parity bound

Fix a proof $\sigma$ of exactly $s$ bits. Each challenge is a deterministic procedure, allowed arbitrary computation on $\sigma$, the challenge, and its own scratch. Along every branch it reads at most $q$ words of $F_x$. The challenge can include a proposed RAM address, controller, or memory assignment. Its domain must be independent of the unseen charges, or eligibility must be checked within the same probe budget. Any input-dependent preprocessing used by the tests must appear in the verified proof or be charged to the probes. A proof passes if all its tests pass. Soundness requires every passing proof to establish UNSAT.

Fixing the challenge gives a binary decision tree on the charges of depth at most $K=Cq$, since a packed word can be reconstructed by querying its charge bits. Implicitly put a clause at each rejecting leaf forbidding agreement with the assignment along its path. An inconsistent path with repeated probes contributes only a tautology. Combining these clauses for all challenges gives a width-$K$ CNF whose models are exactly the $x$ certified by $\sigma$. This argument charges neither the number of tests nor their computation between input probes.

All these models have odd parity. Flipping any one charge therefore destroys satisfaction, so each model is isolated in the Boolean cube. The Paturi–Pudlák–Zane satisfiability coding lemma bounds the number of isolated models of any width-$K$ CNF on $n$ variables by $2^{n-n/K}$. In their encoding, each variable of an isolated model has a critical clause. In a random variable order it is last among the variables of that clause with probability at least $1/K$ and hence is forced. Their prefix-code argument converts this saving to the counting bound. Emdin, Kulikov, Mihajlin and Slezkin previously gave the corresponding width versus auxiliary-variable inequality for encodings of parity. [Paturi, Pudlák and Zane, Lemma 2](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/PaturiPudlakZane_1999_cjtcs.pdf) · [Emdin et al., *CNF Encodings of Parity*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2022.47)

Taking the union over $2^s$ possible proofs, at most a fraction $2^{s+1-n/K}$ of odd inputs can be certified. Covering all $2^{n-1}$ odd charges requires $(s+1)K\ge n$. For variable-length proofs of at most $s$ bits, replacing $s$ by $s+1$ in the counting argument covers every possible length. Conversely split the charges into blocks of at most $b$ consecutive bits. Supply the XOR at all internal prefix boundaries. The first prefix parity is zero and the required final parity is one. The challenge selects a block, reads its $O(b)$ charge records and checks its two claimed boundaries. All such checks pass exactly for the genuine prefix parities. This uses $\lceil n/b\rceil-1$ proof bits.

With $d=2$ or $3$, a proposed automatic conversion promising both $s,q\le N^{1/d-\eta}$ on every rejection for any fixed $\eta>0$ fails already on this family. At $d=3$ the same conclusion holds even for $s,q\le N^{1/3}\operatorname{polylog}N$. Each $F_x$ can itself be decided by one linear scan. The tradeoff concerns the proposed format of local verification. On a family with just one prescribed padding string per length, the proof-counting argument has no varying charges to count.

## One additional existential message

Consider instead $\exists\sigma\ \forall u\ \exists v\ A(F_x,\sigma,u,v)$, with total message length at most $h$ bits in fixed padded layouts and at most $q$ input-word probes in $A$. For fixed messages expand the depth-$Cq$ decision tree into a DNF, then merge its top disjunction into the existential choice of $v$. Expanding the remaining message choices gives a depth-four AND/OR circuit of size $2^{O(h+q+1)}$. Exact computation of parity in depth four requires size $2^{\Omega(n^{1/3})}$, hence $h+q=\Omega(n^{1/3})$. This version charges the lengths of all three messages. [Håstad, *On the Correlation of Parity and Small-Depth Circuits*](https://doi.org/10.1137/120897432)

The exponent is attained. Pad with known zero charges to $t^3$ for $t=\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil$. The first message gives the $t$ parities of consecutive length-$t^2$ blocks with XOR one. The second selects one block and proposes $t$ subblock parities whose XOR disagrees with the selected claim. The third identifies a proposed parity contradicted by the actual $t$ charges in that subblock. A correct first message defeats every legal challenge. A wrong block claim is defeated by supplying all its true subblock parities. Both the number of message bits and the final number of probes are $O(t)$.
