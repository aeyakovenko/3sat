# Sampling the current output distribution from inside a universal machine

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** One approach to forcing the [successive probability gains](mass-clock-cells.md) is to sample the early output distribution of a universal machine, then print something to which that distribution assigned too little probability. There is an ordinary efficient universal machine that anticipates an entire specified family of such routines at the very clocks the routines inspect. The construction applies when the number of current-clock samples times the launch probability of that routine stays below one by a fixed margin. Earlier-clock samples can be much more numerous. The comparison is word by word, including words with exponentially small mass.

## The comparison at each deadline

Put $b=\lceil\log_2(m+2)\rceil$, $T_j=2^{3m+jAb}$ for $0\le j\le J=\lfloor m/(Ab)\rfloor$. The fixed integer $A$ is chosen large enough for the bounds below. For a native interpreter $U$ on a fair bit stream, $\mu_j$ is a probability distribution on $m$-bit words plus $\bot$. The value is the published word if $U$ publishes a word of length $m$ by $T_j$, and $\bot$ otherwise. In particular out-of-length publications are included in $\bot$. Write $F_j(\mu_{\le j})$ for the output distribution of a uniform randomized routine that receives $m,j$ and draws independent samples from any of $\mu_0,\ldots,\mu_j$ at indices of its choice. The routine may adapt to every answer and may use its own fresh bits. Its outputs belong to the same alphabet. Excluding the calls, its time and its total number of calls are bounded by a fixed polynomial in $m$. Let $r_j$ bound its number of calls specifically at $j$. Bounds apply on every sequence of replies. The polynomial degrees in the routine and header assumptions below must be independent of increasing the fixed grid spacing $A$.

A branch selecting $(m,j)$ has a specified easily parsed prefix of weight $\lambda_j$ (all weights have an implicit length index $m$). These prefixes are mutually disjoint over all lengths and stages and avoid the other two branch headers used below. Computing a particular header from $m,j$ and its bit length costs $\mathrm{poly}(m)$. Testing which one of the finitely many headers for a specified $m$ and $h\le j$ matched a random prefix also costs $\mathrm{poly}(m)$; reading extra bits for this internal experiment is harmless. For instance use a common branch prefix, an Elias gamma code for $m$, and fixed-width digits for $j$ at that length. Small or invalid cases may diverge.

Suppose $r_j\lambda_j\le 1/4$ throughout. For one ordinary efficient literal universal $U$, simultaneously at every sufficiently large $m$ and every $0\le j\le J$, let $H_j(y)$ denote its probability from the designated stage-$j$ branch alone by $T_j$. This contribution is absent by $T_{j-1}$ for $j>0$. We obtain
$$
\left\|H_j/\lambda_j-F_j(\mu_{\le j})\right\|_{\rm TV}\le 2^{-2m},
\qquad
\lambda_j F_j(\mu_{\le j})(y)\le (1+2^{-m/2})\mu_j(y).
$$
In the first formula $H_j/\lambda_j$ includes $\bot$ as the branch's timeout probability. In the second $y$ is an actual $m$-bit word. Both occurrences of $\mu$ are the laws of the constructed **physical machine**. Thus a later launch of this routine with the same weight can add at most about a copy of the probability already present on every word, and an almost identical distribution already arrived in the designated previous window. A greater launch weight, more dependence on the current distribution, or inspection of the actual program or RAM history instead of just its output lies outside this statement. The conditional prediction for *all* efficient machines involves their full newly arriving mass; this experiment anticipates the designated routines only.

## Making the generic branch available early

Use a fixed prefix for a literal fallback to an efficient native reference $G$ (say header $0$). Take an integer $B$ and internal cutoffs $s_j=2^{3m+jAb-Bb}$ for large $m$, with $A>B$. If $G$ finishes with an $m$-bit word in $g$ virtual steps, regulate publication so that by $T_j$ exactly the successes with $g\le s_j$ have appeared. Runs with $g\le s_0$ can publish immediately. For $s_{j-1}<g\le s_j$, wait if necessary until after $T_{j-1}$ and publish by $T_j$. For $g>s_J$ wait until after $T_J$ if necessary. Computing or comparing logarithmic clock cutoffs for a quick computation only needs $m$ and the bit length of its clock. A counter of $3m+O(m)$ bits is only needed when the elapsed time is already exponential in $m$. The fixed simulation overhead is a polynomial in $\log(g+2)$ times $g$ (including output handling). Take $B$ large enough for timely simulation at the upper end of every gap.

In a gap where waiting is required, the previous physical deadline is at most $g2^{Bb}$. Since $g\ge s_0$ there, $m=O(\log g)$; the waiting and simulation preserve the literal compiler's polylogarithmic slowdown and exact input consumption. Very quick runs must avoid enumerating the whole grid simply to release their output: compare the bit length of $g$ to the first cutoff first. Length counting and this one comparison fit the reference simulation bound. Initial finitely many lengths can be handled directly. At every relevant endpoint the generic contribution now has an exact sampling procedure: simulate $G$ only through $s_j$, marking any other result $\bot$.

Use another header (say $10$) followed by a gamma code for $m$ and exactly $m$ new bits which are published promptly. Its weight on each word, before the first grid clock, is $\ell_m2^{-m}$ with $\ell_m=2^{-2-|\mathrm{code}(m)|}$. This small uniform component will turn a probability error into a pointwise comparison.

## Evaluating a self-referential sample call

Inside the branch for $m,j$ run $F_j$. When it asks for a sample from clock $i\le j$, select the raw interpreter branch by fresh fair bits:

* On the generic branch perform the bounded $G$ experiment through $s_i$.
* On a matching literal header for this $m$, use $m$ independent bits.
* On a matching stage header $h\le i$ of this $m$, execute the stage-$h$ algorithm and return its result. Omit its physical publication wait. A matching $h=i=j$ starts another copy of $F_j$ at the current recursion level.
* All remaining choices give $\bot$. In particular a stage $h>i$ has not published by $T_i$.

The special headers themselves are parsed normally on the top-level input. A successful top-level stage at $j>0$ publishes strictly after $T_{j-1}$ and by $T_j$, and a successful stage zero publishes by $T_0$. Bounded procedures return $\bot$ internally even in cases where the physical top-level branch would diverge. The internal sampling procedure can make independent samples without running the physical waits. This explicitly uses the code of the constructed machine.

At one fixed $j$, treat every lower-stage procedure with its actual cap as already defined. First imagine no cap at level $j$. Each active copy has at most $r_j$ opportunities to make another same-level copy, independently hitting the special raw prefix with probability $\lambda_j$ when it attempts a current-clock call. Adaptive early stopping can only reduce the number. Its same-level tree is stochastically dominated by a Galton–Watson tree with offspring $\mathrm{Binomial}(r_j,\lambda_j)$. For the tree to reach $n+1$ vertices, the first $n$ explored vertices must have at least $n$ children in total. With independent potential offspring preallocated even at vertices never reached, the probability is at most
$$
\mathop{\rm Pr}[\mathrm{Binomial}(nr_j,\lambda_j)\ge n]
 \le {nr_j\choose n}\lambda_j^n\le (e/4)^n .
$$
This also covers $r_j=0$ directly. Thus the uncapped recursion terminates almost surely and defines an ideal law $Q_j$ for one call of $F_j$. Let $\mu_j^*$ use $Q_j$ on the single raw stage-$j$ branch and the actual lower stages, literal and generic branches elsewhere. Independent child subtrees show exactly that $Q_j=F_j(\mu_{<j},\mu_j^*)$.

In the physical program truncate to $\bot$ if the total number of active copies at the *same* level in this evaluation would exceed $n=10m+10$. All descendants at this level share one counter. Each lower-level call starts its own capped evaluation. The law $\widehat Q_j$ of this program is within $\delta=(e/4)^n$ of $Q_j$ in total variation. The difference between $\mu_j^*$ and the actual $\mu_j$ is at most $\lambda_j\delta$. Couple the two runs of $F_j$ until the first differing reply. Every earlier-clock call agrees exactly and at most $r_j$ current-clock calls can disagree. This gives
$$
\|\widehat Q_j-F_j(\mu_{\le j})\|_{\rm TV}
\le (1+r_j\lambda_j)\delta\le 2^{-2m}
$$
at large $m$. In particular $\lambda_jF_j(\mu_{\le j})(y)\le \mu_j(y)+\lambda_j2^{-2m}$. The literal lower bound on $\mu_j(y)$ proves the pointwise statement. The earlier-level errors are already part of the true earlier laws seen on both sides, so there is no accumulating error over the grid.

One stage-$j$ evaluation invokes polynomially many generic simulations through at most $s_j$ and polynomially many complete lower-stage evaluations. Stack and simulation bookkeeping per operation can be bounded by a fixed polynomial in $m$ using an explicit dispatcher; lower-stage evaluations execute its native subroutine directly. With $W_j$ a worst-case time bound omitting publication waits, the implementation has
$$
W_j\le m^{c}\bigl(s_j+1+\max_{h<j}W_h\bigr)
$$
for a fixed $c$ at large $m$ (use zero for the empty maximum). The exponent includes the cap and all sample and field-processing costs. Choose $B>c+3$ large enough also for generic timing and $A>B+c+3$. Then $W_j\le T_j/4$ by induction. Delays for an early stage finishing very quickly can use an ordinary binary counter with polynomial bit arithmetic while the native width is small, then wait on the physical clock once the width accommodates its threshold. A calibrated delay landing strictly inside $(T_{j-1},T_j]$ suffices because the grid ratio absorbs fixed and polylogarithmic overheads. [Native timing conventions and delayed publication](successive-description-times.md)

The numerical constant $1/4$ is inessential. If $r_j\lambda_j\le 1-\eta$ for fixed $\eta>0$, the same binomial tail, now using the exponential moment at a small positive parameter depending on $\eta$, is $\exp(-c_\eta n)$. A larger constant multiple of $m$ gives the same error. At a critical mean the exponential tail need not hold. For example the critical offspring law $\mathrm{Binomial}(2,1/2)$ has extinction probability one, but its total progeny has infinite expectation because each generation has expectation one. An exponential tail would give a finite expectation. The example concerns the cost of this recursive construction.

The routine's ordinary processing time can in fact be as high as $s_j$ virtual RAM steps at level $j$. Simulating this processing, including any fresh bits and unrelated computations it performs, adds only one more $s_j\,\mathrm{poly}(m)$ term per active copy. The recurrence for $W_j$ is unchanged. In particular computations on previous deadlines can accompany the sample queries. An explicit statement of this variant fixes a common polynomial sample bound first, chooses $B,A$ to pay for that bound and the fixed simulation overhead, and then admits the deadline-indexed processing cap $2^{3m+jAb-Bb}$. The requests still deliver the specified samples rather than arbitrary access to a simulation of the current-clock experiment.

## Queries on the same tape and simultaneous routines

A single call may request the *joint* vector of outputs at all clocks through a chosen $i$ on the same random tape. This vector is either all $\bot$ or a word first appearing at one grid index $h$ and persisting thereafter. On a generic run finishing by $s_i$, recover the least $h\le i$ with $g\le s_h$. On a literal use index zero, and on a successful stage branch use its specified release index. All derived tests or repeated observations of this sample require no new random tape. For independent such vector requests, the coupling still differs only when the stage-$j$ branch is drawn in a request reaching the current clock. Thus the same proof holds for routines allowed to inspect these joint histories.

One interpreter can also handle an entire library. Let $F_j$ start by reading a self-delimited finite machine description $e$ from a fresh random stream and then interpret that routine on $m,j$, with common polynomial time and sample caps (including a cap on parsing). A fixed $e$ has constant selection weight $w_e>0$ at all sufficiently large $m$. For every $e$ respecting these caps, the right side of the pointwise comparison simultaneously covers $\lambda_j w_e F_{e,j}(\mu_{\le j})(y)$. This follows from nonnegativity of all terms of the launch mixture, without searching for successful $e$. For example, choose the stage header from a gamma-coded $m$ and a fixed-width index $j$ as above. Then $\lambda_j=O(\log(m+2)/m^3)$, so every fixed routine making at most $m^2$ current-clock calls eventually fits the subcritical condition. The shared processing cap must still be paid by the chosen grid spacing.
