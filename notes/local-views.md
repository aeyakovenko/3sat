# Checking partial, challenge-dependent views in one SAT call

## A weaker witness than a circuit for the whole accepting computation

Let V on an input x of length k read a proof at addresses of its choice, using R uniform random bits. A local circuit W may see the *whole random string* before it gives any answers. Simulate V and record the queried addresses and W's replies. W can also mark a simulation inactive. Call a seed active only if it is marked active and the simulation accepts. A clean seed is active and has no reply in conflict with any reply in an active simulation, including its own. Two replies conflict when they read the same address and give different bits.

All the clean views can be realized by one ordinary global proof. At each used address assign the agreed bit, and fill unused positions arbitrarily. For an adaptive verifier, induction on the query number reproduces the simulated path on a clean seed. If no global proof makes V accept on more than a σ fraction of its coins, the density of clean seeds for any W is at most σ. This argument requires neither a small global proof circuit nor an algorithm inverting the query-address map. `LocalViews.glue` proves the assignment-existence step.

To test the density, write G(r)=∀w B(r,w), where w supplies another seed and two query-slot indices. B demands that r is active and the proposed pair of slots does not witness a conflict with another active simulation. Out-of-range slots cannot witness a conflict. V's run and both lists are recomputed from x and W inside the circuit, so an alleged view cannot falsify the verifier's query sequence. This uses polynomially many copies of W when V runs in poly(k) time.

**One-call test.** Fix constants σ,γ with γ>0 and σ+2γ≤1, using fixed rational thresholds. If the density of G is at least σ+2γ, there exist L=O((R+1)/γ²) shifts s₁,…,s_L such that for *every* r at least a σ+γ fraction of the seeds r XOR sⱼ satisfy G. Independently uniform shifts give independent Bernoulli samples for each fixed r. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound give failure probability at most 2ᴿ exp(−2Lγ²)<1 for a suitable L. Conversely if such shifts exist, averaging over r shows that the density of G is at least σ+γ. This is a threshold version of the random-shift approach to testing a density gap. [Lautemann](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3)

The threshold condition has **one universal block**, despite G already containing a universal quantifier. For fixed r it is equivalent to requiring that for every tuple (w₁,…,w_L), the threshold holds for B(r XOR sⱼ,wⱼ). In one direction use monotonicity. In the other, choose a failing witness independently for every false G, and any dummy witness where G holds. This independent choice is essential; forcing the adversary to use the same conflict on every shift could overestimate the clean fraction. `LocalViews.universal_aggregate` checks the equivalence for arbitrary monotone aggregators.

Guess W and the shifts nondeterministically. Build the ordinary SAT query for a violation, on variables r,w₁,…,w_L, and accept the guess when the deterministic solver answers UNSAT. Suppose R and V's time are poly(k), and W's size bound is s≤2^O(k). The whole explicit query, including gate indices, has length (s+1)poly(k) for fixed gap. The nondeterministic cost using an O(Nᵈ) SAT algorithm is (s+1)ᵈ poly(k), including the usual quasilinear nondeterministic RAM-to-tape simulation. The shifts are part of the guessed certificate. The construction never generates a pseudorandom set of full assignments.

**Consequence for the hierarchy approach.** Choose a language in NTIME(T), T=2ᵏ, outside NTIME(T/k²), and a fixed polynomial-time probabilistic proof verifier with a constant completeness/soundness gap. Such verifiers for NEXP can be obtained by treating the deterministic provers' response tables in the classical two-prover theorem as a single proof indexed by prover identity, its local conversation up through the current question, and the bit position in its reply. Previously supplied response bits are reproduced on an adaptive path by induction. Choose σ as the soundness bound and σ+2γ below completeness. Under the fast-SAT hypothesis, on infinitely many YES inputs *no* local circuit of size ⌊T^(1/d)/kᴬ⌋ has clean accepted density at least σ+2γ, for some constant A. Otherwise the one-call test, with A large enough to absorb all poly(k) overheads, decides this language on a nondeterministic multitape machine in less than the excluded time. Finite exceptions can be hardwired. This strengthens the journal's [exact-witness obstruction](succinct.md) to partial views that can depend on the verifier's private coins. It also identifies a sufficient condition for a SAT lower bound if such local views could be proved small for one hierarchy verifier. [Babai, Fortnow and Lund](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/mip2.pdf)

## Allow a bounded number of disputed addresses

The clean-seed test assigns zero to any view with even one conflicting address. A fractional contribution from such a view can be accounted for with the same single SAT call. Call a marked, accepting view *eligible* if it is internally consistent on repeated addresses. For an eligible seed r, let a(r) count the distinct queried addresses at which two eligible views give opposite replies. Define its contribution as 2^(−a(r)), and assign zero to ineligible seeds. Write μ for the mean contribution over the verifier's seeds.

Construct a random global proof by giving every unanimously answered address its forced bit and using an independent fair bit at each disputed address. Fill untouched locations arbitrarily. For every eligible r, the probability that this proof realizes its entire transcript is exactly 2^(−a(r)). Repeated queries to a single disputed address cost just one factor of two. Whenever the specified bits agree, the same adaptive interaction occurs and accepts. Linearity of expectation therefore gives a deterministic global proof with acceptance probability at least μ. In particular μ≤σ on every no-input.

**One universal check for μ.** Let q be a polynomial upper bound on the number of bit queries. Append q fresh fair bits z to the local seed, and number distinct queried addresses by order of first appearance on r's simulated path. The path and its numbering are computed from r and W independently of z. Test that r is eligible, and that for every position i where its reported bit differs from z at the corresponding address number, no eligible view gives an opposite reply to that address. Failure of the latter condition is witnessed by a second seed and two query slots. Thus this predicate on (r,z) is again universally checkable, with at most poly(k) evaluations of W and poly(k) further gates for eligibility and address comparisons. Its circuit size stays within (s+1)poly(k). For fixed eligible r it succeeds exactly when z matches the r-reply at each of its a(r) disputed addresses. Its density is **exactly μ**. The independent-shift argument above now uses R+q seed bits. No common hash function has to work on all r in this density computation; the *soundness* proof uses a single random global proof and then averages.

Consequently it suffices on a yes-input to find W for which μ≥σ+2γ. For example, if at least an α fraction of all seeds are eligible and each of these has at most h disputed addresses, then μ≥α·2^(−h). The sufficient gap is α·2^(−h)≥σ+2γ. Every previously clean seed contributes one in this variant. A fixed W can receive positive weight even when its clean fraction is zero. This comparison concerns a supplied W; it gives no circuit-size separation after optimizing the inactive mask and all replies.

**Sharpness.** On h fixed proof addresses, h≥1, pick a uniform h-bit target and accept exactly when the h queried bits equal it. Let W report the disclosed target at all h addresses. Every view is eligible and all h addresses are disputed. Any fixed global proof matches one target among 2^h, so its acceptance probability and μ are both 2^(−h). Prepending a dyadic-probability α enabling coin, with unconditional rejection when disabled, gives equality at α·2^(−h). Thus the quantitative gain from counting ambiguous views requires control of the disagreements, the verifier soundness, or further structure of the answers.

Repeating the hierarchy calculation with this modified predicate gives the same conditional lower bound on sizes of local W, now even for those aiming to attain weighted density σ+2γ. The positive premise is weaker: one would need to construct sufficiently small W attaining this weighted gap for a suitable hierarchy verifier. A completeness/soundness gap for ordinary global proofs alone gives no size or overlap bound for W.

## Two attempts to produce the views

**Disclose the local challenge.** For a single-query verifier choose a forward map π and a known label b(r) on each random string. The verifier queries π(r) and wants b(r). If π is a permutation, W can answer b(r) and all views are clean. A circuit on the proof address would instead need to compute b(π⁻¹(address)). For example, on k bits let π map x to its adjacent XOR differences followed by its last bit, and b(x)=x₁. Each forward output costs at most one XOR. The global label is the parity of all k address bits. Any fan-in-two circuit for this bit, even if it need only agree strictly more than half the time, uses at least k−1 gates: omitting an input gives agreement exactly one half, and connecting k input vertices to one output takes at least k−1 binary gates. Here the local answer is one input wire. This explicitly separates the representations at a modest scale; the separation is in k and is insufficient for the T-exponent target. With π provided only as a forward oracle, exactly identifying the parity class of a preimage requires 2^(k−1) probes in the worst case. Fewer probes can all miss the desired address while leaving unqueried inputs of both classes. This oracle fact does not imply an explicit circuit lower bound.

**Use locally satisfying replies, then discard the clashes.** On an even cycle require adjacent bits to differ. A procedure reporting (0,1) on every directed edge passes every individual test. Every edge clashes with its neighbor. With an inactive mask the best compatible collection of these fixed replies has density one half, the maximum matching fraction. The global alternating labeling passes all tests. The constraint system is perfectly satisfiable, but local acceptance alone gives no constant advantage above one half for these replies.

There is a more quantitative version for an expander. On a D-regular graph with edge expansion at least hD, plant bits z(v) and require the parity z(u) XOR z(v) on each edge. Suppose clean accepted edge views have density at least 1−η. Extend these views to a labeling y. The cut between vertices with y=z and vertices with y≠z has at most ηDN/2 edges for N vertices. Expansion implies that up to one global complement, y differs from z in at most ηN/(2h) positions. Moreover, at a vertex incident with a clean edge, *any* active incident view gives its agreed value. Read the first active incident view, defaulting to zero when there is none. At most ηN vertices are incident with no clean edge, by counting uncovered incidences. The recovered labeling thus agrees with z up to a global flip and at most η(1+1/(2h))N errors. With efficient incident-port enumeration on a constant-degree graph, this is a circuit using a constant number of view evaluations. Some verifiers therefore force high-density local views to encode a good global approximation. For XOR constraints a small directed-path certificate can exploit structure beyond a generic local-reply strategy; projection constraints with two reverse choices behave differently. [Rooted transport test](rooted-propagation.md)

The unresolved positive step is to construct a small challenge-dependent W whose clean or ambiguity-weighted accepting fraction exceeds soundness by a constant for a suitable verifier. The SAT assumption pays for checking proposed views and their overlap. Establishing the density requires a separate argument.
