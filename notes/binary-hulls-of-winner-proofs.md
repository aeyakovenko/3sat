# What the binary consequences of a signed PCP can certify

Write $M=2^j$ for the clock of the [signed priority computation](priority-pinned-sat.md). Fix a color $c$. Consider a uniform perfectly complete Boolean PCP for the claim $b_j=c$, with constant query bound, soundness $1-\delta$ for a fixed $\delta>0$, $r=j+o(j)$ fair bits and local computation in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$. The [local PCP for the priority bit](low-randomness-free-winner.md) has these bounds. Explore each seed's constant-depth decision tree to obtain one test on its distinct proof addresses. Call its relation $R_s$. Its binary hull $H_s$ consists of the tuples satisfying every consequence of $R_s$ expressible as one clause of width at most two. This includes pins and, for the empty relation, the empty clause. Equivalently each restriction to at most two positions agrees with some accepted tuple on those positions.

* If ordinary SAT has deterministic time $O(n^k)$ for fixed $k\ge1$, then on every sufficiently late false claim a **single global proof** satisfies all but a fraction $M^{-1/k}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ of the binary hulls. It violates the original local relation while satisfying the local hull on at least a $\delta-M^{-1/k}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ fraction of the seeds.
* Under **linear-time SAT**, a single global proof satisfies **all** the binary hulls on every sufficiently late false claim. At least a $\delta$ fraction of the original tests reject that proof.
* Conversely, if on infinitely many false claims the full conjunction of binary hulls is unsatisfiable, for this one fixed verifier and color, that suffices to exclude linear SAT. Perfect completeness on the true claims is essential. Below we construct a PCP for the very same claim for which the conjunction is always satisfiable.

These statements concern proofs that an unbounded prover may write. Neither a proof table nor its individual bits need be efficiently computable. The color is fixed before asking which stages are false. Applying the statement to both colors takes the larger of two starting scales.

## A short contradiction for distant systems with two choices

Suppose each of $m$ tests offers at most two consistent partial bit assignments. A proof passes a test if it extends at least one offered assignment. An empty list gives a one-test contradiction. Otherwise duplicate a singleton and associate one Boolean selector to each test. Forbid a pair of choices whenever their partial assignments disagree at some shared address. This produces an implicit 2-CNF on the selectors. Joint satisfiability on any subset of tests is exactly consistency of the corresponding choices, since consistent partial assignments glue to a single table.

If every table fails at least $\varepsilon m$ tests, there is an unsatisfiable subset of $O((1+\log m)/\varepsilon)$ tests. Here is a proof which charges tests even when the conflict graph is dense. Each forbidden pair gives the two contrapositive implications between selector literals. In the induced graph on any remaining set of tests, take a selector $x$ and forward balls of radius $R$ about $x$ and $\neg x$. A ball is inconsistent if it contains some $y,\neg y$. Two paths of length at most $R$ from $x$ to such a pair give a path of length at most $2R$ from $x$ to $\neg x$, by complementing and reversing one path. If both balls are inconsistent we get implications in both directions, involving at most $4R+2$ original tests. Their choices are impossible.

Otherwise use a consistent ball of radius $R$ about a literal. Fix $0<\gamma=\varepsilon/2\le 1/2$ and choose $R$ with $(1+\gamma)^R>2m$. For some $i<R$ the next shell has at most $\gamma |B_i|$ literals. Delete all tests represented in the shell. Set all still-present literals of $B_i$ true and their complements false, then remove those assigned tests from the recursion. Every implication out of an assigned true literal reaches another true literal or a deleted test. By contraposition no residual literal can imply an assigned false literal. Thus any solution returned by the recursion extends to the assigned tests. Even if the shell contains the complement of a ball literal, that test is deleted and its cost is charged to the shell. The ball contains $|B_i|$ distinct tests. Every one leaves the recursion at this step, so the total number deleted is at most $\gamma m$. If no short contradiction is found, the resulting joint table passes all retained tests, contrary to the distance assumption.

Two consequences are useful here.

1. Each ordinary binary clause is a choice between the two one-literal assignments making its two literals true. Thus a multiset of $m$ binary clauses that is $\varepsilon m$ clauses from satisfiable has an unsatisfiable subcollection of $O((1+\log m)/\varepsilon)$ clause occurrences. Duplicate occurrences, units, empty clauses and tautologies cause no difficulty.
2. For a verifier using polynomially many random bits with at most two efficiently reconstructible **accepting transcripts** per seed and inverse-polynomial rejection gap, perfect completeness gives a **coNP upper bound**. On a NO instance guess the polynomially many seed names of a short inconsistent subcollection. Reconstruct their partial assignments and decide their selector 2-CNF explicitly in polynomial time. A transcript must agree with itself on repeated proof addresses. This argument allows adaptive queries. Two consistent accepting transcripts may specify different subsets of addresses. Their union of cylinders is still expressible in 2-CNF by distributing the disjunction of the two conjunctions of literals.

Bellare, Goldreich and Sudan already reduce one-free-bit PCPs to 2-SAT. Their Proposition 10.9 gives PSPACE for polynomial randomness for every soundness strictly less than one, including exponentially small gaps. Directed graph decomposition also gives a polylogarithmic short-contradiction bound for ordinary gap-2-SAT: remove both implications of each clause hit by a directed low-diameter cut. If too few clauses were removed for the remainder to be satisfiable, some complementary literals are in one surviving strongly connected component, hence have short paths in the original graph. Yoshida also uses the four-path witness in a bounded-degree property tester. We do not establish priority for the coNP consequence or the deletion argument. ## Applying the bound at the native clock

There are $N=2^r$ tests, and at most $K N$ binary clause occurrences for a verifier-dependent constant $K$. If every proof fails more than $\eta N$ hulls, every proof fails more than $\eta N$ clause occurrences. By the preceding bound some $O(K(1+\log(KN))/\eta)$ occurrences form an inconsistent 2-CNF. A certificate names them by seed and slot. Verify membership in each hull from its full constant-size local table, then check the selected explicit 2-CNF. A nondeterministic checker for $s$ occurrences takes $(s+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time for $s\le M$, including sorting the address names and testing implications. Its [ordinary bounded-run CNF](packed-band-transfer.md) has the same bound after increasing the fixed polynomial. With $s=\lfloor M^{1/k}/(j+1)^B\rfloor$ for large fixed $B$, the hypothesized SAT algorithm decides this certificate search in $o(M)$. Rational power caps suffice for the milestones $k=1,2,3$. For general fixed real $k$, one can try every dyadic cap in increasing order, signalling at the first success. The total through the largest admissible cap is within a constant factor after polynomial slack, and the algorithm need not compute $k$.

Such a signal is sound for the opposite color by perfect completeness. A fixed sound one-color detector can signal within $o(M)$ at only finitely many of these stages. Hence at all sufficiently late false claims there is a table failing at most $O(K(1+\log(KN))/s)$ of the hulls as a **fraction**. Apply soundness to this same table. Each test witnessing the difference has at least three genuine accepting adaptive transcripts, since unions of at most two cylinders are already equal to their binary hulls.

For the exact linear case, form the implication graph directly on the actual proof-address literals. It has $O(N)$ active vertices with $\operatorname{poly}(j)$-bit names. An arc is certified by a seed, local slot and its computed clause. An empty clause can be found by one short existential search. An unsatisfiable nonempty-clause system contains paths $x\to\neg x$ and $\neg x\to x$, each of length at most $O(N)$. Add identity steps to pad walks. Put $L=\lceil \sqrt{C N}\rceil$ for a large fixed $C$. Guess $O(L)$ waypoints for these two walks; every consecutive pair must be connected by a walk of at most $L$ steps. Given a pair, use the linear SAT hypothesis and the bounded-run compiler to decide that short existential walk test in $L\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. With the table explicit, search by a second SAT call for an index for which that deterministic test returns false. A third SAT call asks whether any guessed waypoint table has no such index. The table, original input metadata and all successive clocks fit $L\operatorname{poly}(j)$ after a fixed number of enlargements to the logarithmic overhead. The result is an exact deterministic $M^{1/2+o(1)}$ detector for inconsistency. Bi-immunity again rules out any successful detection at late false scales. This argument gives no way to decide the full local predicates by binary implications.

## An explicit test of the converse

Pad the original verifier to a binary decision tree of constant depth $q\ge1$ at each old seed, retaining all accepting leaves and preserving the answer on every consistent proof. Private Boolean flags mark its nodes. Impose a root-one test, the split relation $p=l+r$ at each internal node, two implications there requiring an active child to agree with the appropriate shared proof bit, and the condition at each leaf that an active leaf is accepting. There are $4\cdot2^q-2$ slots. Pad with two tautological unary tests and sample uniformly among $B=2^{q+2}$ slots per seed. Each test accepts at most three full local tuples. Private addresses carry a distinct seed tag. For a given shared proof the genuine active path violates exactly the leaf condition on a rejected old seed. Conversely, satisfying all slots forces the genuine accepting path. Hence for fixed shared proof the minimum number of violated slots equals the old number of failed seeds, and the overall rejection fraction is divided by exactly $B$.

The only non-bijunctive relation in this construction is the split. Its tuples in parent-left-right order are $000,110,101$. Its binary hull adds exactly $100$. Set only the root active in every tree, and fill the shared proof arbitrarily. This satisfies **all** binary hulls even on a false claim. More precisely, for each fixed shared proof choose the genuine path at its accepting old seeds and the root alone at its rejecting ones. This table obeys all hulls and violates exactly one true split per rejected old seed. No table extending this shared proof can do better even if it may violate hulls. Thus the linear-case obstruction above can hold identically for a PCP of the actual hard sign. A missing ingredient for a SAT exclusion is a uniform verifier for which soundness forces binary inconsistency on infinitely many false scales.
