# Reusing short programs for a shared input

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Consider a sampler that receives an explicit word $x$ before trying to produce $y$. An inputless source can reproduce $x$ and then run the sampler, but paying for a verbatim copy of $x$ can double the code length of interest. I obtain a tradeoff from the short-word coding hypothesis by running this procedure repeatedly on the probability mass of names for *pairs*. The resulting assertion has the same hypothesis as the numeric test. [Numeric equivalence](sampling-at-a-numeric-length.md)

Fix a source $S$ and a positive integer $a$. Take $q\ge2$, $t=2^q$, $m=|x|\le a\log_2(q+2)$, and let $p\ge(q+2)^{-a}$ be the probability that $S(x)$ publishes $y$ by $t$, with $|y|\le q$. Suppose the inputless high-mass deterministic coding assertion holds for each fixed source and each fixed probability exponent on an efficient literal reference $V$. For each integer $1\le r\le\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil$, a single deterministic decoder given $(x,q,r)$ has an exact prefix name for $y$ of length at most

$$
\left\lceil-\log_2 p+\frac{m+O(\log(m+2))}{r}\right\rceil+O(1)
$$

that finishes in $t(q+2)^{O(r+1)}$ steps. All hidden constants are independent of $q,x,y,r,p$. The extra inputs are public. In particular the result does not give an inputless description of $y$ of this length.

## The distribution on the first coordinate

Use a fixed canonical pair encoding consisting of the self-delimited length of the first word, that word, and the remaining bits for the second. Its bit length for $(x,y)$ is $m+|y|+O(\log(m+2))$. A new fixed inputless source runs $V$ on fair program bits until it publishes a valid pair $(x',z)$. Discard $z$, then run $S(x')$ on fresh independent bits and publish $(x',y')$ on its completion. The source receives none of the parameters $q,r$ and uses no cutoff in its own operation.

Use a name cap $L=B\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil$ and native deadlines $T_i=2^{q+(C+iD)\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil}$ for $0\le i\le r$. The constants $B,C,D$ will be fixed independently of $r$. Write $u_i(x,z)$ for the total dyadic weight of exact $V$ names within these two bounds that publish $(x,z)$, and $u_i(x)=\sum_z u_i(x,z)$. These are computable rationals. A self-delimiting literal for $(x,\epsilon)$ gives $u_0(x)\ge2^{-m-O(\log(m+2))}$. Its timely computation and name length fit the bounds for large $B,C$.

For all $i<r$ we have

$$
u_{i+1}(x,y)\ \ge\ c\,u_i(x)\,p
$$

for a fixed $c>0$. Indeed the new source obtains the required pair with probability at least $u_i(x)p$ at a clock $T_i(q+2)^{O(1)}$, by disjointness of exact program names and independence of the second tape. Reading the first output and discarding its second component fit in this clock even if that component used all the steps available to print it. Across the chosen range of $i$, $\log_2 T_i=q+O(\log^2(q+2))\le2q$ for all sufficiently large $q$. Choose $C$ large enough that the entire target pair is short at the source clock. The literal lower bound on $u_i(x)$ makes $u_i(x)p$ inverse-polynomial in $q$, with an exponent depending only on $a$ and the fixed literal encoding. Apply the inputless coding hypothesis to this one source, at the next dyadic clock, to get an exact name of weight at least $c\,u_i(x)p$. Its length fits the cap for sufficiently large $B$, and its completion time fits $T_{i+1}$ for sufficiently large $D$. The machines and the requisite probability exponent were specified independently of $B,D$. The asymptotic comparison $\log T_i\le2q$ then holds by increasing the finite lower threshold for $q$. Small cases can be covered by changing constants.

Since $u_r(x)\le1$, the product of the $r$ successive ratios $u_{i+1}(x)/u_i(x)$ is at most $1/u_0(x)$. The decoder chooses an index $i$ minimizing the ratio, resolving ties by increasing index. Under the normalized distribution $u_{i+1}(x,z)/u_{i+1}(x)$ the atom at $y$ now has mass at least $cp\,u_0(x)^{1/r}$. Enumerate the capped names through the deadlines, retaining their outputs and exact rational weights. In the cumulative interval for each positive atom choose an aligned dyadic cell of width at least one fourth the mass, obtaining a prefix code. A sequential program reader can compare the addresses while retaining previously read bits. The chosen cells are disjoint, so no earlier failed comparison on a valid name goes beyond that name. At most $(q+2)^{O(1)}$ names occur and each has an output whose explicit length is at most $T_r\,\mathrm{polylog}(T_r)$. Even scanning an entire output per comparison in a comparison sort, or comparing every pair of outputs, costs at most $T_r(q+2)^{O(1)}$. This proves the claim.

The proof in fact bounds the extra length by $\log_2(u_r(x)/u_0(x))/r+O(1)$. This quantity measures growth of the *whole* first-coordinate probability, including names paired with unrelated or empty outputs. For an input for which a constant fraction of its eventual marginal probability is already supplied by short timely names, one iteration suffices for constant overhead.

## A test using a total randomized language

For a concrete instance put $T(n)=2^{2^n}$. Use the native computation model of the coding hypothesis for the deciders and all simulations. Suppose a fixed bounded-error decider for a language $A$ runs within $T(n)$ on length $n$ on every coin sequence. From $x=\mathrm{bin}(n)$, one source enumerates all length-$n$ inputs. On each input it takes a majority of $O(n+1)$ independent repetitions. With a sufficiently large coefficient all $2^n$ answers are correct together with probability at least $0.99$. The ordered table has length $2^n$ and is printed by a known dyadic deadline $2^{q_n}$ with $q_n=2^n+O(n+1)$. For instance, $q_n=2^n+K(n+1)$ covers the repetitions, enumeration and the fixed polylogarithmic simulation overhead. The conditional theorem applies with a fixed $a$.

* For every $\epsilon>0$, choosing a sufficiently large fixed $r$ gives a deterministic simulation on every length using at most $\epsilon\log_2(n+2)+O_\epsilon(1)$ advice bits, in $T(n)2^{O_\epsilon(n+1)}$ time. The advice selects one name for the whole table, so it works on every input of that length.
* Choosing $r=\max(1,|\mathrm{bin}(n)|)$ gives constant advice on every length in $T(n)2^{O((n+1)\log(n+2))}$ time.
* There is also an advice-free deterministic machine correct on **all** inputs of infinitely many lengths in $T(n)2^{O(n+1)}$ time. To see this, take the infinitely many integers $n$ for which a shortest description up to a constant can print $\mathrm{bin}(n)$ in $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ time. One construction starts from a shortest program $v$ for an output of arbitrarily high prefix complexity. Pack the consumed $v$ and its running time into the digits of $n$, with $n$ at least that running time. Simulating $v$ and forming $n$ has a name of length $|v|+O(1)$, and $n$ recovers the original output, giving $K(n)\ge |v|-O(1)$. At these lengths, $u_0(x)\ge c_1 2^{-K(n)}$, since an efficient wrapper can print $(x,\epsilon)$, and the literal bound $K(n)\le |x|+O(\log(|x|+2))$ puts the name within the cap. The eventual first-coordinate marginal of $V$ is a lower semicomputable semimeasure and is at most $c_2 2^{-K(n)}$ by the prefix coding theorem. Thus $u_1(x)/u_0(x)$ is bounded on this infinite set. With $r=1$ the true table has a name of bounded length, and one fixed program name occurs infinitely often. Hardwire that name with a uniform timeout to get the claimed total machine. [Construction of timely names and marginal bound](native-coding-delays.md)

In fact one advice-free machine has **positive upper density of correct input lengths**, where correctness at a length still means correctness on every input. Let $s=|\mathrm{bin}(n)|$. Apply the timely-shortest-name construction above to $s$ itself. For each such $s$ and every $2^{s-1}\le n<2^s$, a fixed routine uses the same timely name for $s$, then reads $s-1$ raw bits and prints $(\mathrm{bin}(n),\epsilon)$. All these names meet the cap and clock for their respective $n$, so

$$
u^{(n)}_0(\mathrm{bin}(n))\ \ge\ c_3 2^{-K(s)-(s-1)}.
$$

The superscript records that the deadlines depend on $n$. The total *eventual* pair probability for all first coordinates of length $s$ is at most $c_4 2^{-K(s)}$ by the prefix coding theorem for the length marginal. Consequently the average, over the $2^{s-1}$ lengths in the block, of the ratios $u^{(n)}_1(\mathrm{bin}(n))/u^{(n)}_0(\mathrm{bin}(n))$ is at most $c_4/c_3$. At least half of the block has ratio at most $2c_4/c_3$. All those true tables have bounded-length names with $r=1$. One of the finitely many names works on a fixed positive fraction of the block, and the same name has this property on infinitely many blocks. At their right endpoints the fraction of *all* lengths seen so far on which its timed simulation succeeds is bounded below by a positive constant. The fixed simulator still runs in $T(n)2^{O(n+1)}$ time on every input.

These are consequences of the inputless coding hypothesis. A total randomized language for which every deterministic machine at the last clock is correct on all inputs at only a density-zero set of lengths would contradict that hypothesis and hence exclude linear SAT. No such language has been produced here.
