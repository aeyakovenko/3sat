# A scan-time SAT solver after hiding every name in the dense list projection

**Theorem.** One fixed, globally correct deterministic SAT solver takes \(O(n/\log n)\) native word steps on the following proper ASCII 3-CNFs, with \(n\) the length of the transformed file in bits. Start with any input to the dense list projection in the repository. Injectively rename its variables to any positive decimal identifiers, and independently complement each variable at all its occurrences. Duplicate any clause arbitrarily many times, reorder the clauses and literals, and use arbitrary allowed whitespace. Update the DIMACS header to describe the resulting file. The solver receives the transformed file alone. It needs neither the renaming nor the list parameters. On a formula outside the recognized class it can invoke a general exhaustive SAT algorithm. This is the specific threshold and flag encoding implemented by the projection. The machine has logarithmic-width words, separate ordinary word operations and random access. [Exact list projection](../audits/dense_ascii_chunk_circuit_audit.py) · [Circuit lower bound transferred using the projection](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md)

The circuit lower bound uses the SAT answers on a class that already has this one fast, globally correct solver throughout its signed renamings and orderings. One cannot obtain a superlinear native-time witness against every solver by choosing more such transformations, even if the resulting clause order depends on the solver. The time bound allows zeros in decimal fields when the syntax admits them. Adding unused declared variables or using very long numerical identifiers changes the length charged in the bound.

## Peel the padding and recover the threshold circuit

First remove duplicate normalized triples using the [packed scan](packed-normalization-of-dimacs-clauses.md). The original generator produces no repeated unsigned-order-normalized signed triple, as follows from the distinct binary edges and unit pairs in steps 1–2 and the separate outputs and guards in steps 3–4. Treat signed literals as variable names together with signs. Comparisons of clauses in this proof are comparisons of multisets after sorting their two or three literals. Let \(R=2^r\) denote the unknown number of original records and \(b=\lceil r/4\rceil\). There are eight banks with \(2^b\) flags per bank. Each selector \(s_i\) sends an implication to its chosen flag in each bank. The threshold for two selected rows uses the following three gates per row, starting with both running states false and requiring the final \(t_R\) true.
\[
 g_i=h_{i-1}\land s_i,\qquad
 t_i=t_{i-1}\lor g_i,\qquad
 h_i=h_{i-1}\lor s_i .
\]
A lower-case role such as \(s_i\) may correspond to a *negative* file literal. Every recovered role is checked consistently across all its occurrences.

1. Find the most frequent variable among those occurring in both polarities. On the projected files this is the primary padding variable. It occurs at least \(16R\) times in the row-to-flag clauses alone. A flag occurs at most \(2R+O(1)\) times, any selection or gate variable occurs \(O(1)\) times, and the secondary padding variable also occurs \(O(1)\) times. The two guard variables occur with a single polarity. For each pair of remaining literals \(u,v\), check equal multiplicities of the two clauses \((u,v,\pm\mathrm{pad})\) and replace each pair by \((u,v)\). This is a logical equivalence. Any fixed finite set of exceptions at small \(r\) can also be handled by enumerating assignments to the occurring variables after duplicate removal when the remaining clause count is bounded by a fixed constant.
2. In the resulting binary clauses, group by the *unsigned* pair of variables. Each unit in the source has produced exactly two clauses on the same pair, with the secondary padding literal in opposite polarities. The original binary edges between selectors and flags are distinct. Each bank binary edge belongs to one stage, and each gate binary edge joins a gate output to one of its two inputs. Distinct gate outputs are fresh and only refer to older variables. These unsigned pairs are distinct and disjoint between the three constructions. Thus all repeated unsigned pairs are the unit doubletons, and they share precisely the secondary padding variable. Check these facts, check that this variable occurs nowhere else, and replace the doubletons by their unit literals.
3. Among the surviving ternaries the pair of guard variables occurs together in more than two clauses. No other pair does. Indeed the two companion gates with common operands \(h_{i-1},s_i\) each use that pair once. All other operand pairs occur once, and the pairs involving a gate output and an input do not recur in any other ternary of this circuit. The two guard variables must have a consistent polarity wherever they occur and occur only together in the identified clauses. These clauses can all be satisfied by the same choice of the pure guard. Remove them. The temporary choice imposes no condition on their third literals.
4. For every remaining ternary \((u,v,z)\), identify its gate output by the occurrence of *both* binary clauses \((\neg z,\neg u)\), \((\neg z,\neg v)\). The criterion is invariant under signed renamings. On the true scaffold there is no binary clause between the gate's two inputs, including the inputs shared by an AND and its companion OR. Thus there is exactly one candidate output, with the accompanying binary clauses present in their required multiplicities. Remove these binary clauses. The signed ternary and the unsigned directed graph of gates are retained.
5. In this graph there is exactly one gate output carrying a unit, namely \(t_R\). Starting there, work backwards down the threshold gates. One operand of \(t_i\) belongs to a pair of gates with identical unsigned operands. That operand is \(g_i\); its sibling is \(h_i\). The other operand is \(t_{i-1}\). The backwards walk terminates at the unit on \(t_0\). In forward order, \(g_1\)'s unit operand is \(h_0\); in later rows \(h_{i-1}\) has already been identified. The remaining operand in each row is \(s_i\). Check that all the required fresh variables are distinct, that the walk covers every gate, and that the threshold's three unit occurrences are the prescribed two initial states and final state.
6. This identifies each gate's logical AND or OR role. For an AND the gate's saved ternary must read \((\neg a,\neg b,c)\); for an OR it must read \((a,b,\neg c)\). Each observed sign therefore determines the polarity of its role. Require agreement wherever the same role is assigned twice, including at the three designated units. The gate supplies exactly the appropriate binary clauses by step 4. The checked threshold now means that at least two of the signed literals \(s_i\) are true.

## Recover the eight banks

Inspect the remaining binary clauses touching threshold variables. Each must be an implication \((\neg s_i,f)\). Record its second file literal without yet assuming which bank it belongs to. No other binary occurrence of a threshold variable is permitted. In each bank a stage with previous cursor \(p\), flag \(f\), and next cursor \(c\) contributes
\[
 (\neg p,\neg f),\qquad (\neg p,c),\qquad (\neg f,c).
\]
Its unsigned graph is a triangle. Consecutive triangles share exactly one cursor, disjoint banks share no vertex, and the first cursor of each chain is pinned false. In each triangle the two occurrences of a vertex have the same file sign. At a shared cursor the signs on the two triangles are opposite.

Starting at each of the eight remaining units, find its two triangle neighbors and their joining edge. Its unit literal must agree with its two literals in that triangle. If the triangle has a further triangle, precisely one of the new vertices continues the chain. The other is the flag. On the final triangle, use the neighbor with a row implication as the flag if there is one. If neither has a row implication, either choice works; reject this parsing if both have one. Check the sign rule at every local edge, disjointness of the chains, fresh flags and cursors, and complete coverage of all remaining binary clauses. The row literal for a chosen flag must be opposite to that flag's literal on the triangle. Each row must have exactly one such flag per bank.

An assignment extends through one bank precisely when at most one of its flags is true. The implications force the cursor true after any true flag; a later true flag would contradict its clause with the previous cursor. Conversely setting each cursor to the OR of flags up to that position satisfies the whole chain whenever at most one flag is true. Thus two rows can be selected together exactly when their eight recovered flag names are pairwise equal in corresponding banks. Radix-sort these eight-tuples and look for a duplicate. The reductions and sign checks give sound answers on **every** success of recognition, including inputs outside the construction's syntactic orbit. Malformed candidates or failed structural checks can use the original general solver. All completeness statements about identifying roles above refer to the genuine orbit. ## Native cost with arbitrary decimal identifiers

Let \(B=\lceil n/\log_2(n+2)\rceil\) and let \(m\) be the number of distinct clauses after removing copies. The packed normalization theorem handles the raw file in \(O(B)\) time even when it contains far more than \(B\) literal occurrences. For the unreplicated projection the following simpler parsing bound also suffices. At byte level one can enumerate tokens and digit runs in \(O(B+m)\) time. Use disjoint blocks of \(\max(1,\lfloor \log_2(n+2)/32\rfloor)\) bytes. The table of all blocks costs \(O(n^{1/4}\operatorname{polylog}n)=O(B)\) to generate. At an offset in a block it gives the length of the next run of digits or whitespace and the first nonzero decimal digit when requested. Token and block boundaries account for \(O(B+m)\) iterations on valid width-three inputs. A fixed-size chunk of consecutive normalized digits can be extracted at an arbitrary byte alignment by a constant number of native loads, masks, and shifts. Include its length in its code so that a short last chunk is distinguished from one with trailing zeros. [Native parser for the canonical layout](scan-time-on-the-dense-list-projection.md)

Long decimal identifiers need no conversion to a native integer. Remove leading zero digits and put each digit string into fixed-size chunks. For a token with \(j\) chunks, pad its array to the next power of two with a fresh symbol and make a full binary tree. Over all occurrences this costs \(O(B+m)\) nodes, including all padding. Rank the leaves by their chunk and then, from the lowest height upwards, assign identical labels to equal ordered pairs of child labels. At each height radix sorting can use count arrays bounded by the number of nodes at the *preceding* height, plus a constant. Hence the total initialization and sorting cost over all heights is linear in the total node count. Each token's height and root label together identify its normalized identifier exactly. The original variable bound, even if very long, can be checked by digit length and then chunkwise lexicographic comparison on ties. The work of a tie comparison is charged to the token being checked. A clause count exceeding the input bit length can trigger the fallback early. This gives dense word-sized name indices without restrictions on decimal-name lengths on the proper files.

All frequency counts, clause cancellations, gate pair lookups, and sibling groupings use a fixed number of word radix sorts or direct arrays on these indices. In particular, for step 4 generate the three binary-membership requests per ternary, sort them together with the actual binary clauses, and annotate all gate candidates before tracing any chain. Each bank vertex has degree at most four; reject larger degrees before graph traversal. Walks take \(O(m)\) time. The resulting recognition and evaluation require \(O(B+m)\) steps and words. On the orbit \(m=\Theta(R)\) and there are \(\Omega(R)\) distinct positive identifiers. The decimal lengths of any such distinct names total \(\Omega(R\log(R+2))\), even after an arbitrary injective renaming. Consequently \(m=O(B)\), including files with added whitespace or much longer names. Fixed small source orders have bounded numbers of clauses and variables and satisfy the same asymptotic statement as their serialized fields expand.

The [executable recognizer](../audits/signed_dense_projection_audit.py) uses Python maps to check the structural argument. It does not benchmark the word RAM. PicoSAT independently checked both verdicts on 60 randomly permuted, consistently polarity-switched files. All 60 passed again with additional copies of clauses. Of 360 single-clause mutations, 54 passed structural recognition, and PicoSAT agreed on each. Lean checks the bank and threshold semantics. It does not formalize recovery of the signed graph from ASCII. The native complexity and the recognition theorem are manuscript proofs.
