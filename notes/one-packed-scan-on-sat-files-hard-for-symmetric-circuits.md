# A scan-optimal proper SAT family with exponential symmetric threshold circuit cost

There is a promise of ordinary proper 3-CNF files, of both verdicts, with the following two properties. In the unordered representation by signed clause–variable incidences on $D$ domain points, any Boolean threshold circuit whose wiring is symmetric under domain permutations and which decides the promise needs $2^{\Omega(D)}$ gates on an infinite sequence of sizes. With the same formulas serialized as compact, arbitrarily shuffled and densely renamed ordinary DIMACS files of $N=\Theta(D\log D)$ bits, a single globally correct deterministic SAT program decides the promise in $O(N/\log N)$ word-RAM steps. There are same-length positive and negative files requiring $\Omega(N/\log N)$ original-word loads on every correct RAM, even one correct only on the promise.

The structural source is Cai, Fürer and Immerman. Their cubic graph pairs already admit linear ordered time and have linear counting width; their §7 explains how an order selects a reference point in every pair and a single parity test distinguishes the graphs. Atserias, Dawar and Ochremiak provide the quantitative symmetric-circuit transfer and an exponential symmetric bound for general 3-SAT. The construction below places the two costs on the same sparse proper SAT inputs and gives a native program attaining the packed scan cost on both verdicts. The unrestricted SAT time question remains open. [CFI, Theorem 6.4, Corollary 6.5 and §7](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/pub/opt.pdf) · [Atserias, Dawar and Ochremiak, Lemmas 1 and 5 and §5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07825) · [Unordered SAT circuit model](symmetric-circuits-on-unordered-sat.md)

## From a colored cubic CFI graph to an ordinary proper formula

Start with a finite simple 3-regular base graph $G$ on $t$ vertices. For each incidence $(v,e)$ the CFI gadget has two outer points, called mates, and for each base vertex it has four middle points. Each middle point has one neighbor in each of the three pairs at that vertex. In the construction's reference names $a,b$, the four middle neighborhoods are the triples with an even number of $a$ entries. Each base edge matches the two outer points at one end with those at the other. Its twist bit swaps the two partners. The formula constructor needs the mate relation, the actual outer matching and middle adjacency, all available in the colored CFI presentation. It never chooses a reference member of a pair. CFI's mate pairs are identifiable by their color classes or their specified mates in §6. Their hard pairs remain equivalent with the base vertex colors present. [CFI, §6](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/pub/opt.pdf)

For each outer point $u$, create Boolean variables $X_u,Z_u,T_u$. With $u'$ its mate and $v$ its actual outer matching partner, output

* $X_u\ne X_{u'}$ by padding each of $X_u\lor X_{u'}$ and $\neg X_u\lor\neg X_{u'}$ twice, with $Z_u$ and $\neg Z_u$ as third literals.
* $X_u=X_v$ by padding each of $X_u\lor\neg X_v$ and $\neg X_u\lor X_v$ twice, with $T_u$ and $\neg T_u$.
* For each middle point with neighbors $u,v,w$, output the single clause $X_u\lor X_v\lor X_w$.

Each padded binary clause is equivalent to its two ternary copies under conjunction, including when the pad bit is arbitrary. We print both orientations of each mate or matching pair, using distinct pad names. Each clause has three distinct variable names. There are $6t$ outer points and $4t$ middle points, giving exactly $18t$ variables, $52t$ clauses and $D=70t$ active incidence points. The threshold-circuit input uses these **active points** with unary sorts and the two binary positive and negative incidence relations. Any unused numerical names implicit in the DIMACS header play no role in decision.

An assignment now chooses one member of each mate pair. It obeys the four middle clauses exactly when the three chosen members are a single middle neighborhood: a middle clause is false only when the chosen tuple is its coordinatewise complement, and complementing three bits interchanges even and odd. The outer matching constraints require the choices to match across each actual base edge. In reference coordinates the vertex parity equations have right-hand side zero and a twisted edge contributes one to the XOR of its two endpoint values. Summing shows each connected component must have even twist parity. Conversely, choose arbitrary values for non-tree edges of a component, then propagate its parity demands along a spanning tree from the leaves. The root is consistent exactly when this parity sum is zero. Thus a single base-edge twist changes the verdict on any connected base graph. ## Unordered circuit lower bound on this promise

The output construction is a finite collection of tagged copies of input elements. Variable IDs are $(u,\text{variable tag})$, padded clause IDs are $(u,\text{one of eight signs/types})$, and a middle clause is indexed by its middle point. Positive and negative incidences, and the domains of the tags, use fixed first-order formulas on the colored CFI input. These formulas need only the mate, outer-partner and middle-neighbor predicates. There is no quotient, ranking of neighboring points, or choice of parameters. When CFI's colors are supplied as separate predicates, their same-color relation is expressible at each fixed size by a disjunction using two variables.

Consequently a $C^k$ counting sentence distinguishing the formula structures would give a $C^{k+O(1)}$ sentence distinguishing the two CFI graphs. For each quantified variable one tracks its finite tag in the formula syntax and quantifies its single underlying graph point. A counting quantifier with a numerical threshold can distribute that threshold over the finitely many disjoint tags by a disjunction over the possible contributions, reusing the same graph variable in its branches. Quantifier rank and formula length may increase. Counting width has no such restrictions. CFI's linear separator lower bound on cubic expanders thus forces counting width $\Omega(t)=\Omega(D)$ on the two promised outputs. [CFI, Theorem 6.4 and Corollary 6.5](https://people.cs.umass.edu/~immerman/pub/opt.pdf)

Fix a size. A symmetric circuit on the promise induces a permutation-invariant class of all relational input tables by its action on every bit string. Its values outside the promise may be arbitrary. Atserias–Dawar–Ochremiak convert a fully symmetric threshold circuit of size $s$ into a symmetric LP of size $s^{O(1)}$, and an LP of size $S$ into counting width $O(\log S/(\log D-\log\log S))$ in the range used here. If $\log_2 s\le\delta D$ with sufficiently small absolute $\delta>0$, the induced width is below the CFI lower bound. Thus $s\ge2^{\Omega(D)}$. The circuit may be chosen separately at each input size, with arbitrary depth and nonuniform design. Every permutation of the $D$ points must lift to its wiring and preserve the gate types and output. A sparse byte serialization with names and a physical order is a different input representation from this unordered dense table with $\Theta(D^2)$ relation bits. [Atserias, Dawar and Ochremiak, Lemmas 1 and 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07825)

The conclusion resists a fixed roster of distinguished names if the family includes disconnected base graphs. Take a hard component on $t$ base vertices and $q$ disjoint untwisted copies of the complete graph on four base vertices. The resulting domain has $70t+280q$ points. Fix any $h\le280q$ of those positions individually and require symmetry under every permutation of the others. Place all distinguished points in the fixed easy part. Specialize the easy and cross-component incidence bits to constants. Every permutation of the remaining hard component lifts, giving the same $2^{\Omega(t)}$ bound. In particular, for any fixed fraction strictly below one there is a sequence of sizes with that fraction of publicly distinguished positions and an exponential symmetric lower bound in the entire domain size. Input-dependent constants encoding a verdict or additional order predicates fall outside this statement.

## Keeping variables and clauses in separate sorts

A fixed-copy variant gives the exponential lower bound even when the circuit only has to lift independently chosen permutations of the variable sort and the clause sort, with sort membership public. For all $10t$ raw CFI points, middle and outer alike, allocate three variables $(X_u,Z_u,T_u)$ and nine clause slots indexed by $u$. At an outer point fill the first eight slots with the pair and matching clauses above and the ninth with $X_u\lor Z_u\lor T_u$. At a middle point fill the first eight slots with repeated copies of that same positive filler and the ninth with its real neighborhood clause. Every clause is proper. All slots are occupied. Giving value one to every otherwise free pad satisfies all fillers, preserving the twist verdict.

The two universes are now literally three and nine copies of the same input graph universe. Each output incidence bit is a fixed first-order function of the graph table. Expanding the fixed formulas by ranging quantified graph points over the entire input yields a polynomial-size circuit symmetric in the graph points, with outputs equivariantly indexed by the two tags and two points. A graph permutation $\pi$ sends $(i,u)$ to $(i,\pi u)$ in both output sorts. Every such pair of permutations lifts through any product-symmetric decision circuit. For the graph-side application use the fixed vocabulary of adjacency, a binary same-color relation, and unary middle/outer tags. These predicates are available in or definable with a fixed number of variables on the original colored hard pairs, so the same counting-width lower bound holds in this signature. Thus composition gives a fully graph-symmetric circuit of size $s+\operatorname{poly}(t)$ on the CFI promise with constants in the circuit-to-logic theorem independent of $t$. The same counting-width argument gives $2^{\Omega(t)}$ for $s$. Here the public variable and clause sort sizes are $30t,90t$, respectively. The padded version also supports the native solver and the twist load test below.

There is a different answer if just **one** sort can move. Suppose clause identities are fixed and ordered, and variables can be permuted arbitrarily. For every active variable $v$ take the earliest clause $j$ containing it. At most three variables have the same $j$. Sort their signed incidence columns lexicographically in the fixed clause order and assign the sorted columns to the three public slots $(j,1),(j,2),(j,3)$. An equal column may occur twice or three times and is printed with its full multiplicity. Any excess slots remain inactive.

This canonicalization uses polynomially many variable-symmetric AND/OR/NOT gates. Testing a first occurrence or lexicographic comparison uses the fixed clause coordinates. To write rank $r\le3$, a candidate column must have fewer than $r$ strictly smaller columns in the group and at least $r$ smaller-or-equal ones. Each counting test needs only an OR over ordered tuples of at most three *distinct* variable points satisfying the relevant tests. Permutations of variables act on all witness-indexed gates. For each output incidence bit, OR over the qualifying candidates with that incidence; tied candidates all give the same bit. Consequently all canonical named output bits are fixed by every variable permutation, and one can feed them to an arbitrary ordinary circuit. There are $3m$ public variable slots for $m$ clauses, and the new table size is polynomial. Inactive input variables do not affect decision. This argument applies to **all** proper ternary formulas.

If variables instead have fixed identities and only clause points move, use a public slot for each signed triple on distinct variables. An OR over the clause points reports whether this triple occurs; repeated clauses may be dropped. This is again an invariant polynomial-size canonical input to an arbitrary ordered circuit. A polynomial-size ordinary SAT circuit is equivalent to membership of ordinary SAT in $\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{poly}$ under the usual polynomial encodings. Thus proving a superpolynomial lower bound in either one-sort-only symmetry model for all proper 3-SAT would already prove ordinary SAT is outside $\mathrm{P}/\mathrm{poly}$. On the present twist family, either one-sort model has polynomial-size circuits by the native algorithm. On the fixed-copy variant the two-sort action has exponential cost.

## A globally correct native program

The DIMACS program receives only the raw bytes. It groups clauses by their sorted triple of variable IDs and intersects their three-bit truth tables. Whenever a table expresses equality or disequality of two of its variables with the third free, it records that signed binary equation and removes the table. Graph traversal in the signed equality graph either finds a contradiction or writes each variable as a component bit or its complement. Substitute these expressions into the remaining constraints. At this stage set any pure literal to its satisfying value and discard the clauses it satisfies, recording that choice for model recovery. This deletes the fixed-copy fillers, every one of which contains a free positive pad. Regroup by their three component names, and check that each table is a three-variable parity equation and each remaining variable occurs in exactly two distinct equation scopes. On the CFI files the first components correspond to the two local pairs on each base edge (and independent unused pads), and the remaining scopes are the vertices. Distinct base vertices have distinct incident triples because the base is simple. The program performs these checks without using CFI annotations. A failed structural check invokes a complete SAT solver on the original file.

On success, make the graph whose vertices are the remaining equations and whose edges are their common variables. A connected component is consistent exactly when its right-hand sides XOR to zero. Set non-tree bits to zero and solve along the tree for a model when desired, then extend signed equalities and choose zero for the free pads. These deductions preserve the formula on all inputs meeting the checked conditions. The algorithm can check a returned model on the original active clauses as well.

For a precise packed bound use canonical decimal printing with one separator per field and one newline per clause, in arbitrary clause and literal orders. Write $v=18t$ in the original construction and $v=30t$ in the fixed-copy construction. Assign its $v$ active names in any order either densely or in an interval $[A,A+v)$ with $A$ the least power of ten **strictly greater** than $v$. Then the largest name and the clause count are $O(t)$. Each name has at most $O(\log t)$ decimal digits, and the total physical length is $N=\Theta(t\log t)$ bits. In the interval version all names have the same decimal width. We use a native RAM with $w=\Theta(\log(N+2))$-bit words wide enough to address its input and linear workspace, constant-time ordinary arithmetic and shifts, and a public input length. Counts, indices and masks use $O(1)$ native words.

Here are explicit scanning costs. Take $b=\max(1,\lfloor\log_2(N+2)/32\rfloor)$ **bytes** per chunk. A complete table of the $2^{8b}$ chunk patterns lists their successive runs of decimal digits, sign and delimiter symbols, the length and numerical value of each digit run, and its power of ten. It takes $O(2^{8b}\operatorname{poly}(b))=o(t)$ operations to build, counted from an empty workspace, for large $t$. The scanner fetches each consecutive chunk by masking and shifting at most two packed input words. It combines adjacent fragments of the same name by a multiply and an add, retaining separators at chunk boundaries. Across the file, the number of chunk events is $O(N/b+\#\text{tokens})=O(t)$; canonical files have $O(t)$ delimiters and signs. All promised names and intermediate fragments fit. The globally correct implementation can reject the fast path on numerical overflow, unsupported formatting or an overly large header and reparse with its general solver. Dense keys of size $O(t)$ make each fixed-arity sort a constant number of stable counting-sort passes in $O(t)$ time. Every subsequent graph operation takes $O(t)$ steps. This yields $O(t)=O(N/\log N)$ from the physical bits even though the program can receive either CFI verdict.

For a matching lower bound choose the equal-width interval names, a connected base, and any fixed clause and literal permutation, shared between the base file and all its one-twist changes. Index the records by the outer or middle point that produced them. A twist on base edge $e$ changes only the matching-partner name fields in the matching clauses indexed by the four outer points of $e$. No record widths, signs, printed counts or other fields change. The changed-word sets for the different twists can overlap, but every changed field is in a token of $\Theta(\log t)$ bits belonging to its single base edge. A $w$-bit original word intersects only $O(1)$ such tokens. Every correct execution must observe a differing original word for every twist, since otherwise its deterministic computation on that opposite-verdict file would have the same trace, even with writable input. There are $3t/2$ twists, hence $\Omega(t)$ original-word loads on either parity. The upper and lower bounds both use encoded-bit length; the unordered exponential bound measures circuit gates in its separate representation.

The independent executable checks 1,664 actual shuffled files in the original and fixed-copy representations, including every twist configuration on $K_4$ and $K_{3,3}$, and reconstructs active models on all positive files. It tests a disconnected pair where two odd components have even total parity, nine chunk sizes for cross-boundary decimal tokens and both polarities of the packed-word adversary. A separate test of 250 ordered-clause formulas checks variable-renaming invariance and counts active models before and after column canonicalization, including cases with tied columns. Python-SAT runs CaDiCaL on the same files when installed. Lean checks elementary logic and spanning-tree elimination; the quantitative logic lower bound is imported from the two cited papers.
