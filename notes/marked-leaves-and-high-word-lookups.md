# A marked rejection and exact high-word lookup bounds

Use the 64 complementary four-word SAT files from the eight-step native-clock search. Their entries lie in $4,\ldots,11$, and each physical input word is individually pivotal. Write $H$ for all $8^4$ files with words in this interval. Exactly 3,772 members of $H$ are unsatisfiable. A path that is sound for the native SAT catalog must reject each of them. [Problem, clock and target distribution](coupling-four-word-native-sat-paths.md) · [Exact time with direct addresses](nine-steps-for-direct-addressing-on-four-sat-words.md)

## Four comparisons and a marked rejection

A path of eight steps with four comparisons uses four fresh loads on every target it accepts. All loads have literal low addresses, since preceding raw values are high. Any conjunction of four raw comparisons accepts **at most one** target if it rejects all 3,772 high negatives. To check this, take each pair of the 64 targets and keep native raw/literal comparisons satisfied by both, including both branch outcomes. Of the 195 distinct agreement masks on these files capable of covering two targets, only a subset is eligible for each pair. For 1,336 of the 2,016 target pairs, a negative satisfies every eligible comparison. For the others, intersect the eligible masks in pairs, discard supersets and check that every pair of the resulting masks has a common negative. This covers four comparisons, including repetitions. The cap of one is attained by four direct literal pins on one target. [Exact pair intersections](../audits/four_raw_comparisons_on_complementary_pairs.py)

The preceding three-comparison cap is eight. Suppose the one- and two-comparison capacities could be bounded by 36 and 18 for all native paths. There is a sharper way to combine leaves than summing their individual Kraft allowances. Mark any reachable negative input. Let $F_d$ upper-bound the number of targets in any subtree at comparison depth $d$ and $E_d$ bound that number when the marked negative reaches its root. No target takes a fifth comparison. Thus one can use $(F_4,E_4)=(1,0)$. For a node at depth $d$ let $w_d$ bound a single accepting leaf there. Set $F_d=\max(w_d,2F_{d+1})$ and $E_d=F_{d+1}+E_{d+1}$: an internal node routes the marker into just one child, while a rejecting terminal covers no targets. With $(w_3,w_2,w_1)=(8,18,36)$ this gives $(F_3,E_3)=(8,1)$, $(F_2,E_2)=(18,9)$ and $(F_1,E_1)=(36,27)$. The marked root covers at most 63. It must cover all 64 in a correct program. The unrestricted one- and two-comparison bounds in this calculation remain to be proved. [Integer recurrence and exact raw-guard audit](../audits/four_raw_comparisons_on_complementary_pairs.py)

## Removing low-arithmetic addressing cases

With no stores, let $L$ denote the initial memory function on each file of $H$. Before the only ALU, every register is a literal or one raw column on $H$: loading a high raw address returns zero. The one computed word is therefore $r=f(a,b)$ on raw columns or literals. Even if all four original columns and arbitrarily many additional LOADs are granted, the only additional word tables available after the ALU are $r,L(r),L^2(r),L^3(r)$. Indeed $L(r)$ is either high or zero. For any such starting value further loads alternate between zero and the first high column $P$. In particular $L^4(r)=L^2(r)$ throughout $H$. A two-guard census over this enlarged register set finds **at most eight** targets per sound path. It checks 954 distinct ALU tables and 42,021,624 pairs of eligible guard masks on the whole high suite. One effective comparison is included by repetition. The bound is attained in seven native steps on this target family: compute $r=Q\mathbin{\&}N$ after loading $Q,M,N$, load $L(r)$, and test $M=7$ and $L(r)=8$. The general valid native path for even $P$ shows its full-catalog soundness. [Seven-step indirect path](eight-steps-on-one-four-word-sat-input.md) · [Lookup and store audit](../audits/native_high_guard_families.cpp)

Now suppose exactly four loads, one ALU, and up to two stores occur, with at most two comparisons. Every load is fresh on **every accepted target**. Before the ALU its operands remain simple throughout $H$. A load fresh on a target must use a literal original address, since all raw data are high. Previous stores to literal addresses cannot hit a later fresh read on the target, and raw store addresses are high throughout $H$.

If some fresh load is addressed through the ALU value $r$, the other three loads choose fixed literal low cells on the targets. No second fresh load can use $r$ a second time. The missing cell $j$ is the same on every accepted target and $r=j$ on each one. Restrict to the slice $r=j$. Inductively all later addresses and data are simple there: a fresh load must have a low literal address, a store through a raw column misses it and a store to the same fixed cell would violate freshness on the target. The original two comparisons therefore reduce to two raw/literal tests on that slice. Including the restriction $r=j$ gives a one-ALU three-guard direct path. Its cap is eight on $H$; this assertion on the high suite also follows from the store audit by its separate equality mode.

If all fresh loads have literal addresses, post-ALU stores might address through $r$. Form the fixed set $J\subseteq\{0,1,2,3\}$ of literal addresses of later fresh loads following any such store. Every accepted target has $r\notin J$. On this slice every fresh load still supplies the original input column throughout $H$. For induction, store addresses that are raw always point high, literal stores cannot hit a future fresh cell on the targets, and stores addressed by $r$ miss all the specified future loads. The stores thus disappear from the predicate on this slice. Permit any of the 16 subsets $J$, one unrestricted native ALU on the original data, and two comparisons between any raw columns, literals or $r$. The high-suite census checks 172,349,574 pairs of eligible guard masks across these choices. Each sound path covers at most 16 targets. This step permits writes to original data as well as scratch. It relies on every original load being fresh.

Without any ALU, a high-only path can be partitioned by which previous store supplies a read. Along any fixed slice of these outcomes all values stay simple. A potentially nonconstant store-address match can matter only at a surplus read: a fresh read must address a fixed literal low cell, and a previous store address supplied by a raw column is high. With two comparisons and no ALU there can be one store and one surplus load, or every load is fresh, or no store occurs. At most two slices result. With one comparison the largest cases have two stores and one surplus load, or one store and two surplus loads. In the first there are at most three choices of the last writer, and in the second at most four possible hit patterns. Each slice is recognized by the final raw comparisons plus at most two raw address tests; four raw guards already cover at most one target. Thus none of the zero-ALU cases causes a large leaf.

## The mixed one-ALU path

The remaining one-ALU shape uses five loads, one store and one comparison. Four of its loads are fresh original reads on each accepted target. First suppose the ALU obtains its two operands as simple raw columns or literals throughout $H$. Grant the simulator all four original columns, all literals, this native ALU result $r$, and the closure of $r$ under initial loads described above. A store after the ALU chooses its address and stored value from this set. A store before the ALU chooses both from raw words or literals, since before that first write the initial raw lookup closure is simple. Represent the store with these same words. Any earlier returned simple data, or any computed $r$, are available as registers, so this also covers an ALU occurring later. After the write, unroll up to five updated-memory loads from every available register. There are no new arithmetic operations or further stores. The final comparison reads two members of this set of iterates. This liberal grant permits different operands to use five loads each.

There are 954 possible ALU tables on the high suite and 24 source labels for each of the store address and store value. For every one of the resulting 549,504 choices, evaluate the possible comparison operands on the 64 positive lanes and one block of 64 negatives. Group only to accelerate this first test. If a pair could still accept 37 targets, compare *all* members of the two corresponding groups on every remaining negative lane. The census examines 697,818,670 first-block operand pairs, and 16 comparisons of individual representatives require the remaining-file test. None survives. [Updated-memory lookup census](../audits/one_store_after_one_native_alu.cpp)

Finally suppose a pre-ALU load yields a possibly nonsimple word on $H$. Consider the first such load. The store already occurred. Its address and value and the address of this load are simple, since every earlier value was simple. It selects the stored word or the initial memory word according to equality of two simple addresses. A nonconstant such selection cannot be a fresh read on any target. If its address is a fixed low literal, a raw store address is high on all files, and a literal store address has constant equality. If the read address is high, it reads scratch on every target. This accounts for the sole surplus load. Partition the high files by the outcome of that raw address equality. On either part this load is simple. All remaining target reads are fresh; before the ALU they consequently use fixed literal low addresses and remain simple on the part. The ALU again has two raw or literal operands there.

On a part containing accepted targets, the store has already used a simple address. If all subsequent fresh loads address by a literal, every result is the corresponding raw column on the part: a store to a raw address misses these low cells, and a literal overwrite before its first read would violate target freshness. The part's acceptance test uses the original final comparison and the raw address equality, with at most one native ALU. The read-only two-guard cap eight applies. If a fresh load uses the ALU result, then the other fresh loads are at literals on that part, so $r$ equals the single omitted input address $j$ on every accepted member. Add $r=j$ and use the same induction with this constant address substituted. Now the two raw comparisons (address equality and final comparison) and the fixed value of $r$ are covered by the equality mode, again of capacity eight. Thus at most sixteen targets can be accepted across both parts.

These reductions leave open paths using two or three ALUs with genuine indirection. A failure of either target-cap inequality would disprove this sufficient criterion, without providing an eight-step algorithm for the full catalog.
