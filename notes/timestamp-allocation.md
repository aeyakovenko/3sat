# What can the completion time alone tell a sampler?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Suppose a fixed ordinary sampler publishes a short word $y$ by time $2^q$ with probability $p$. I tried constructing a witness against linear SAT by spreading this probability over completion times that a short program would have difficulty identifying. There is an unconditional single-run transformation that captures at least $\Omega(p/(1+\log q)^2)$ on one integer encoding $y$, within a clock of $2^q\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$. It uses no new simulations of the source and works even at exceptionally small $p$. The squared logarithm is optimal in the model that assigns a tag from the observed timestamp alone. There is a single ordinary sampler that defeats constant-fraction capture by every timestamp rule within the resource bounds below. Its output words nevertheless have short deterministic programs. This last fact identifies the failure of the attempted SAT counterexample.

## One pass through the source

Take $q\ge\max(1,|y|)$. On publication at physical time $v$, put $j=\max(1,\lceil\log_2 v\rceil)$ and $h=\lfloor\log_2 j\rfloor$. Read $\lceil\log_2(h+1)\rceil$ fresh fair bits and interpret them as $l$. Discard $l>h$. Each admissible $0\le l\le h$ has probability at least $1/(2(h+1))$. Select the last integer $k$ in the aligned block of $2^l$ consecutive epochs containing $j$:
$$
 k=2^l\big(\lfloor j/2^l\rfloor+1\big)-1 .
$$
Pad and encode the rank of $y$ by publishing the canonical binary integer
$$
 n'=2^{m+C\lceil\log_2(m+2)\rceil+C}+r(y),\qquad m=\max(1,|y|,k).
$$
Here $r(y)=2^{|y|}-1+\mathrm{val}(y)$ is its length-lexicographic rank. Retain the virtual source clock in a bounded-overhead simulation. Since $k\ge j$, the padded numeric value absorbs the fixed polylogarithmic overhead of simulation and publication for sufficiently large fixed $C$. As in the [numeric sampler](sampling-at-a-numeric-length.md), one wrapper can read $C$ from a constant header before running the source.

Partition the prefix of epochs $[1,q]$ into the completed intervals $[2^a,2^{a+1}-1]$, $0\le a<H=\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor$, and the remaining prefix of $[2^H,2^{H+1}-1]$. Bisect the last block, retaining full dyadic pieces until its prefix is exhausted. At most $2H+1$ aligned intervals occur in all. The scale of each is admissible for every row in it. Some interval contains at least $p/(2H+1)$ of the source probability. All its members can select its common endpoint $k\le q$. Hence some $n'\le2^q(q+2)^{O(1)}$ receives at least $p/[2(H+1)(2H+1)]$ raw probability by $O(n')$. Publishing other integers on the remaining tapes causes no difficulty. [Lean proof for dyadic prefix coverage](../lean/TimestampAllocation.lean)

There is a consequence for **arbitrary** sampling probabilities. Assume the [constant-bit numeric comparison](dilated-program-mass.md) on one efficient reference $V$. For any ordinary source $S$ and efficient target reference $U$, its best single-program weight $A$ then satisfies
$$
 A^U_{2^q(q+2)^{O(1)}}(y)\ \ge\ \frac{c\,P^S_{2^q}(y)}{(1+\log_2(q+2))^2}.
$$
Compile the wrapper on $V$ and apply its numeric comparison at $n'$. A resulting program carrying a constant fraction of the certified mass has time at most $n'\,\mathrm{polylog}(n')\,O((1+\log q)^2/p)$. When $p\ge 2^{-2q}$ this time has logarithm $O(q+1)$, so projection from the integer back to the word on $U$ costs a constant header and a factor polynomial in $q$ in the time allowance. If $p<2^{-2q}$, the short literal for $y$ already supplies the claimed weight. The same lower bound holds with the full discounted mass $B^S_{2^q}(y)$ in place of $P^S_{2^q}(y)$. Indeed select, by the [deadline integral](dilated-program-mass.md), a time $s\ge t=2^q$ with $P_s\min(1,(b+2)t/s)\ge B_t/3$, where $b=\lceil-\log_2 B_t\rceil$. Rounding $s$ upward to a dyadic deadline costs at most two in the time ratio. We can again assume $b\le2q+1$, giving $\log_2 s=O(q+1)$. Apply the preceding result at $s$ and use $A_{av}\ge\min(1,a) A_v$ to return to allowance $t(b+2)(q+2)^{O(1)}$. Thus the bit loss in this transfer is at most $2\log_2(1+\log_2(q+2))+O(1)$, even when the sampler cannot infer $q$ from $y$.

## The optimal fraction under a timestamp-only rule

Consider $N$ ordered epochs. A source with output $y$ can have any distribution $w$ over the epochs up to an unknown cutoff $s$. A stochastic rule given just the arrival epoch $i$ chooses a tag $k\ge i$ or abandons the run. Let $K_{ik}$ be its substochastic matrix. Only tags $k\le s$ count for cutoff $s$. If there are many distinct tags at a common epoch, merging them only helps the rule. Define its guaranteed fraction by
$$
 c(K)=\min_{1\le s\le N}\;\min_{w\in\Delta([1,s])}\;
               \max_{k\le s}\sum_{i\le s}w_i K_{ik}.
$$
For $N\ge2$ the dyadic rule gives $c(K)=\Omega(1/(1+\log N)^2)$, and **every** such rule satisfies
$$
 c(K)\le\left(\frac{4N}{(N+1)H_N-N}\right)^2,
 \qquad H_N=\sum_{a=1}^N 1/a.
$$

For each cutoff $s$, finite minimax gives a probability vector $v_s$ on $k\le s$ with $\sum_k K_{ik}v_s(k)\ge c(K)$ for every $i\le s$. Set $X_{ik}=\sqrt{K_{ik}}$ and $Y_{sk}=\sqrt{v_s(k)}$. Both have row Euclidean norms at most one. For $i\le s$, their row inner product is at least $\sqrt{c(K)}$ by nonnegativity and squaring. For a fixed tag $k$, only $i\le k\le s$ can contribute. Its total weighted contribution satisfies
$$
 \sum_{i\le k\le s}\frac{X_{ik}Y_{sk}}{s-i+1}
 \le 4\,\|X_{\cdot k}\|_2\|Y_{\cdot k}\|_2.
$$
To verify this Hilbert-matrix estimate, write $a=k-i$, $b=s-k$. With $z_b=(b+1)^{-1/2}$, the sum of $z_b/(a+b+1)$ over $b\le a$ is at most $2/\sqrt{a+1}$, and the sum over $b>a$ is also at most $2/\sqrt{a+1}$ by an integral bound. Symmetry gives the analogous column estimate. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to the product of $X_{ik}\sqrt{z_b/z_a}$ and $Y_{sk}\sqrt{z_a/z_b}$ with common weights $1/(a+b+1)$. Summing over tags and using Cauchy–Schwarz once more gives at most $4N$. The row lower bound gives at least $\sqrt{c(K)}((N+1)H_N-N)$. This proves the estimate. The triangular incidence matrix of initial segments and its logarithmic factorization norm have an established literature. The square here comes from requiring agreement **probability** from the two stochastic vectors. Priority for this timing formulation is unestablished. [Matoušek, Nikolov and Talwar](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.1376)

A fixed number of extra probes has a precise limitation in an opaque-source version of the experiment. Condition on a first hit for $y$ having row distribution $w$, and give the rule at most $R$ independent additional probes, each revealing a fresh $w$-row with probability $p$ and giving the same null answer otherwise. Compare with its behavior when all additional answers are null. That behavior defines an ordinary timestamp kernel independent of $w,s$. The two executions can differ with probability at most $1-(1-p)^R\le Rp$. For some $w,s$ their captured fraction is therefore at most the displayed upper bound plus $Rp$. In particular, preserving a uniform positive fraction at sufficiently many epochs requires $\Omega(1/p)$ probes in this interface. The probe count puts no lower bound on the physical time of an arbitrary RAM algorithm that has the source code.

## A single ordinary sampler defeating bounded timestamp rules

The preceding minimax adversary chooses a different distribution for different rules. That dependence can be implemented by an ordinary stream machine running well before its first scheduled output. For specificity, enumerate programs for rules receiving the phase word $y$, an epoch $j$ in binary, and private fair bits, then returning a proposed nonnegative epoch tag. The following construction defeats every such rule that on these inputs uses at most $(\log_2(j+2))^{O(1)}$ fair bits and $2^{(\log_2(j+2))^{O(1)}}$ steps on every path. The rule may contain the source algorithm as a fixed subroutine. Only the arrival epoch and phase word are observed on the actual run. Tags may include extra information; discarding everything except the claimed numeric epoch only increases the mass credited to each tag. The probability bound concerns tags within the query horizon for which the source has actually finished within the claimed time allowance.

Read a gamma code for a positive phase $z$, require the next $z$ fair bits to be zero, and otherwise abandon the run. Set $y=\mathrm{bin}(z)$. Test rule index $\nu_2(z)$, the exponent of two dividing $z$. Every fixed index occurs at unbounded phases. Set $J=2^z$, $M=2^{\lfloor z/4\rfloor}$, $D=M$, and $j_i=J+iD$ for $1\le i\le M$. At large phases these epochs lie in $[J,2J]$. Use $u=\lceil z^{3/2}\rceil$, which is much smaller than $D$, and $R=2^{\lfloor z/3\rfloor}$. Small phases can publish directly. For each $i$ simulate the tested rule on each reported epoch in $[j_i-u,j_i+u]$, on all $R$-bit fair tapes through $2^R$ steps each. Ignore timeouts, excess reads and malformed tags. For any fixed eligible rule these caps eventually include every path at its assigned phases.

Bucket a tag $k$ by the largest $b\le M$ with $j_b-\lfloor D/4\rfloor\le k$. Ignore tags with no bucket or bucket smaller than $i$ in row $i$. Average the bucket distributions over the $2u+1$ reported epochs of each row. This produces a rational substochastic matrix with the triangular support required above. The retained probability for any *one* reported epoch is bounded above componentwise by $2u+1$ times this average. This factor also covers a choice of actual completion epoch within the indicated window depending on the sampled row.

Find the prefix $s$ and row distribution $w$ minimizing maximum capture over buckets through $s$, searching only distributions in multiples of $1/M^3$. Rounding the exact minimizer on its support costs at most $M/M^3$ in total variation (round down and distribute the missing units). The finite search therefore attains $O(1/(\log M)^2)+1/M^2$. Its cost, including building the matrix, is $2^{O(R+M\log(M+2))}\operatorname{poly}(J)$ steps on a fixed universal implementation, below $2^{J/2}$ for large $z$. Indeed there are at most $(M^3+1)^M$ count vectors, and all simulated fractions have bit lengths $O(R+\log J)$. Storing a long tag, clocking a computation, and naive simulation with polynomial overhead in $2^R$ all fit this estimate. The tested program index here is at most $\log_2 z$. Self-simulation inside a tested rule remains subject to the same finite clock.

Draw a row according to the resulting dyadic $w$. Wait for order $2^{j_i}$ native steps and publish $y$. A depth-$j_i$ binary-tree traversal gives a delay between fixed constant multiples of $2^{j_i}$, with initialization and the entire phase computation asymptotically smaller. Even a fixed polylogarithmic simulation overhead changes its log completion time by only $O(z)$, inside the window used for the matrix when the simulation faithfully executes the waiting steps. The native construction alone suffices. Write $q_s=j_s+u$. The whole launch probability $p_z=2^{-z-|\mathrm{gamma}(z)|}$ of the phase has completed by $2^{q_s}$. In particular $p_z\ge(q_s+2)^{-2}$ for large $z$.

Fix any constant $a$. A credited tag $k$ must satisfy $k\le q_s+a\log_2(q_s+2)$, even allowing a polylogarithmic enlargement of the query clock. If the completed source run must be timely at $2^k$ up to another fixed polylogarithmic factor, its tag from row $i$ must also satisfy $k\ge j_i-\lfloor D/4\rfloor$ at large $z$: the actual publication epoch differs from $j_i$ by at most $u$, and the logarithmic slack is $O_a(z)$. Such a tag belongs to a bucket from $i$ through $s$. Therefore its phase mass is at most
$$
 p_z(2u+1)\left(O((\log M)^{-2})+M^{-2}\right)
       = O(p_z/\sqrt z).
$$
The construction also handles rules allowed extra equality probes into this *same* ordinary source. Each such probe may report the timestamp of $y$ on a fresh run up to a chosen cutoff, returning a single null answer otherwise; it discloses no other output or timing on a null answer. During the phase construction simulate the tested rule with all these replies null. If at most $2^{z/2}$ probes are permitted, a real replay ever differs from this simulation with probability at most $2^{z/2}p_z$. This follows by coupling up to the first non-null answer, for every initial row, including when the cutoffs and the decision to continue are adaptive. The matrix bounds the all-null behavior, and the possible additional captured fraction is $o(z^{-1/2})$. The rules still have the stated local computational caps on all-null histories. All finite programs of this type occur in the same enumeration, taking a zero-probe rule as a special case.

No knowledge of the selected horizon $q_s$ is supplied to the rule. The inverse-polynomial high-mass transfer using [independent source simulations](sampling-at-a-numeric-length.md) can still synchronize these runs. More directly, $y=\mathrm{bin}(z)$ has a prompt deterministic gamma-code description. Making the **distribution of finishing times** adversarial in this way does not make the **output word** hard for short deterministic programs. This is the unresolved step for the unrestricted linear SAT goal.

## Finite checks and a worst input for dyadic endpoint selection

In the dyadic rule take an integer $H$, let $h=\lceil\log_2(H^2+2)\rceil$ with $h<H$, and set $B=2^{H+1}$, $q=B-2^h-1$. Use equal source mass on the $H-h$ epochs $j_l=B-2^{l+1}$ for $h\le l<H$. All these rows use the same $\lceil\log_2(H+1)\rceil$ sampling bits. A selected scale $a\le l$ in row $j_l$ gives the distinct endpoint $j_l+2^a-1$ in the row's own interval $[j_l,j_l+2^l-1]$. These intervals are disjoint across rows. Every scale $a>l$ gives $B-1$, beyond even $q+H^2$. Consequently the largest allowed tag mass is exactly $1/((H-h)2^{\lceil\log_2(H+1)\rceil})$. This construction witnesses the order of loss for the specific upper-bound rule; the minimax proof covers other rules.

The minimax, integral estimates and uniform diagonal run-time bounds remain manuscript proofs.
