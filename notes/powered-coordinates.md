# How many coordinates can stay multilinear in a small Boolean representative?

Let cₘ be the bit that says a binary m-by-m matrix itself consists of one directed Hamiltonian cycle. Fix a field F and a constant D>0. Let G be any formal polynomial over F, computed by an arithmetic circuit of size ≤mᴰ, whose values on Boolean matrices equal cₘ. Call an ordered coordinate (i,j) **unpowered** when G has individual degree at most one in Xᵢⱼ. This includes coordinates absent from G. Degrees refer to the output polynomial after cancellation. Equivalently a powered coordinate appears with exponent at least two in some monomial of nonzero coefficient.

**Consequence.** For all sufficiently large m there are at most

    m^{5/3} exp(O_{D,F}((log m)^{3/4}))

unpowered coordinates. The same bound holds for every small representative satisfying these hypotheses. In particular the fraction of coordinates powered somewhere tends to one. The Boolean function has a polynomial-time test. This conclusion concerns the distribution of individual degrees in its arithmetic representatives. It does not require or assert that a single monomial repeats all these variables. The [partial multilinearization argument](partial-multilinearization.md) considers the stronger question of how powered coordinates occur together in supports.

## A sparse host that a set of unpowered coordinates must avoid

Use the bounded-degree cycle construction in [partial-multilinearization.md](partial-multilinearization.md). Applied to a complete directed graph on k vertices, the directed splitting construction of Ejov, Haythorpe and Rossomakhine gives a host H on h=O(k²) vertices with indegree and outdegree at most three. Its cycles correspond bijectively to the original cycles and retain the chosen original arcs. Thus setting the internal arc weights to one in the formal HC polynomial of H gives HCₖ. This uses only their directed splitting procedure. Further conversions to undirected cubic graphs are unnecessary. [Ejov, Haythorpe and Rossomakhine, §4](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/62/ajc_v62_p045.pdf#page=8)

Take disjoint sets A,B of M ambient vertices each. Consider the bipartite graph of unpowered arcs from A to B. Suppose that one can place a distinct tail oᵤ in A and a distinct head iᵤ in B for each u∈V(H), so that every host arc u→v corresponds to an unpowered slot (oᵤ,iᵥ). Leave those slots as distinct free variables. Fix iᵤ→oᵤ to one for each u, inserting every unused ambient vertex along one of these forced paths. Fix every other matrix entry to zero. Choosing a value for the free slots now gives a spanning single directed cycle precisely when the chosen free arcs form a spanning single cycle of H. Contraction of the forced paths proves both directions.

The restricted G is multilinear in all its free variables and agrees with this cycle indicator everywhere on their Boolean cube. It is therefore the unique multilinear extension, over F. Its homogeneous degree-h component equals HC(H): each true assignment has exactly h ones, hence the indicator monomial for that assignment contributes its own support monomial in degree h. Extract this component with O((mᴰ+1)(h+1)²) gates by carrying the first h+1 homogeneous components at each gate. Projecting its weights as above now gives HCₖ. Field size places no restriction on this formal extraction.

Choose k=⌈exp(C(log m)^{3/4})⌉ with C sufficiently large depending on D and the absolute constant of the HC bound. Then h=m^{o(1)}. The published lower bound k^{c(log₂ k)^{1/3}} for HCₖ exceeds the extracted circuit size: its logarithm grows at least as a constant times C^{4/3} log m, whereas the overhead above mᴰ has logarithm O(log k). We take m large enough for the characteristic-dependent threshold in the published theorem. This rules out the proposed host anywhere in the unpowered arcs. [VP–VNP preprint, Theorem 1.1](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00954v1)

## From host avoidance to the number of unpowered coordinates

Write α for the density of those arcs in A×B. Sample three columns from B independently with replacement. Let U⊆A be their common neighbors. Convexity of x³ gives E|U|≥Mα³. Call a triple of distinct rows bad if they have fewer than h common unpowered neighbors. For any fixed bad triple, the probability it lies in U is at most (h/M)³. Hence the expected number of bad triples in U is at most binom(M,3)(h/M)³ ≤ h³/6. If Mα³≥h³+h, some U has size at least h after removing one vertex from each of its bad triples. Every remaining triple has at least h common neighbors. This argument applies when h≥3 and M≥h; below that size the stated arc bound follows trivially. [Fox and Sudakov on dependent random choice](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3271)

Place all h tails injectively in the remaining rows. Each host head has at most three specified incoming neighbors. Enlarge the corresponding set of rows to a triple if necessary. Their common column neighborhood has size at least h, so choose the h heads in succession, avoiding each previously used column. This embeds the forbidden host. Consequently every disjoint M-by-M block contains O(h M^{5/3}) unpowered arcs.

Finally choose a uniformly random pair of disjoint row/column sets of size M=⌊m/2⌋. Any ordered pair of distinct vertices falls from the first set to the second with probability M²/(m(m−1)), bounded below by an absolute positive constant. Averaging the block bound and including the m diagonal entries gives O(h m^{5/3}) unpowered coordinates on the whole matrix.

Functional lower bounds for arithmetic circuits agreeing on a cube were studied by Forbes, Kumar and Saptharishi, including under depth and individual-degree restrictions. The present deduction permits arbitrary circuit depth and concludes something about individual degrees. It combines the recent HC theorem with a classical degree-reduction gadget and dependent random choice. Priority for this combination has not been established. [Forbes, Kumar and Saptharishi](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2016.33)
