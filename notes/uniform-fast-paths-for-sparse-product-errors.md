# Uniform quadratic-time tests for sparse binary matrix-product errors

Fix $0<\epsilon<1$. A single deterministic logarithmic-word RAM detects **every** binary matrix mismatch with $1\le\mathrm{wt}(AB-C)\le d^{1-\epsilon}$ in $O_\epsilon(d^2)$ native operations on $d$-square matrices. Each reported mismatch is genuine on arbitrary input. It gives a globally correct SAT solver an $O_\epsilon(N)$ positive fast path on the corresponding compact DIMACS files of length $N=\Theta(d^2)$. Exact product verification under the external sparsity promise also takes $O_\epsilon(d^2)$ steps. The setup and the matrices used in the tests are uniformly generated within the same time bound.

## Moments that detect sparse errors

Choose a constant $q=2^b$ and represent each row and column address by $\ell=\lceil\log_q d\rceil$ symbols of $\mathbb F_q$. Pad the matrices with zero rows and columns to side $n=q^\ell<qd$ for $d>1$. All digit values are distinct elements of this field. Binary matrix multiplication continues to give the same entries in its characteristic-two extension. For $\alpha\in\{0,\ldots,q-1\}^\ell$ use

$$
u_\alpha(i)=\prod_{h=1}^\ell i_h^{\alpha_h},\qquad v(\alpha)=\prod_{h=1}^\ell(\alpha_h+1),
$$

with zero exponent giving one even at digit zero. Given an error budget $K$, compute the checks

$$
u_\alpha^\mathsf T (AB-C) u_\beta, \qquad v(\alpha)v(\beta)\le K.
$$

To see why these suffice, let $S\subseteq\mathbb F_q^{2\ell}$ be the support of a nonzero error, of size at most $K$. In graded lexicographic order on all monomials, keep each monomial whose evaluation on $S$ is linearly independent of those preceding it. These kept monomials span every function on $S$ because point indicators are polynomials, for example the product of $1-(X_h-a_h)^{q-1}$ over coordinates for the point $a$. The kept set therefore has precisely $|S|$ elements. It is closed under divisors. If a divisor were a combination of earlier monomials on $S$, multiplying by the complementary factor would put its multiple in the span of earlier monomials as well. The relations $X_h^q=X_h$ on the grid also ensure every kept exponent is less than $q$. A kept monomial with exponents $\gamma$ has precisely $\prod_h(\gamma_h+1)$ distinct divisors, all kept. Thus this product is at most $K$. The error, viewed as a nonzero coefficient vector on $S$, has nonzero dot product with some basis evaluation. The two halves of that monomial supply a tested pair. This is the hyperbolic cross universal interpolation argument specialized to a finite grid. [Sauer, *Prony's method in several variables: symbolic solutions by universal interpolation*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.03944)

Write $H_q=\sum_{a=1}^q1/a$. Bound the indicator of the test condition by $K/(v(\alpha)v(\beta))$ and sum over all pairs. Then the number of tests is at most

$$
K H_q^{2\ell}\le K H_q^2 d^{2\log_q H_q}.
$$

Since $H_q\le1+\ln q$, choose a fixed power of two large enough that $2\log_q H_q<\epsilon/2$. Hardwire a rational $\lambda$ with $1-\epsilon<\lambda<1-\epsilon/2$. For instance take $K=2^{\lceil\lambda\lceil\log_2 d\rceil\rceil}$. This covers the promised errors, is $O(d^\lambda)$, and yields $O_\epsilon(d)$ tests. All rounded thresholds are dimension-dependent integers that the program can compute.

## Charging word operations

Let $U$ have rows $u_\alpha^\mathsf T$. The $n\times n$ transform $U$ is the $\ell$-fold tensor product of the fixed $q\times q$ matrix $(z^a)_{0\le a<q,\ z\in\mathbb F_q}$. To obtain $UA$, group $q$ rows at a time by their common digits other than the current digit, apply this one-digit transform, and iterate along the $\ell$ digits. Store each row's $n$ field elements as $b$ bitplanes packed across columns. Multiplication by a known field scalar acts on these bitplanes by a fixed binary linear map. Adding rows is bitwise XOR. A group of $q$ rows therefore costs $O_q(\lceil n/w\rceil)$ native operations with word width $w=\Theta(\log d)$. Over all groups one digit costs $O_q(n\lceil n/w\rceil)$. All digits cost $O_q(d^2)$ since $\ell=O(w)$. Ordinary masks, shifts and separate arithmetic operations suffice. One may pack $2^{\lfloor\log_2 w\rfloor}$ lanes per word to address a lane without division. The $q$-ary digit groups and consecutive strides are enumerable by nested counters and shifts since $q$ is a power of two.

Compute $BU^\mathsf T$ by transposing first, and compute $UCU^\mathsf T$ by two transforms with a transpose between them. A constant number of transposes, even by moving individual field elements, costs $O_q(d^2)$. For each tested pair compare the appropriate length-$n$ dot product of $UA$ and $BU^\mathsf T$ to the transformed entry of $C$. A field multiplication on these fixed $b$ bits costs $O_q(1)$ native operations, so each test costs $O_q(d)$. Storing the transformed arrays in packed bitplanes requires $O_q(d^2/w)$ words, which for sufficiently large $d$ fits the address space alongside the packed input.

The time bound includes listing the tests. Enumerate the $n$ index tuples as base-$q$ integers. Compute their volumes $v$ once by deleting the last digit and multiplying the shorter volume by that digit plus one. The empty tuple has volume one, and leading zero digits do not change it. Every volume is at most $n$. Precompute $\lfloor K/v\rfloor$ for each volume by shift-and-subtract division in $O_\epsilon(n\log^2d)$ work. Now loop over $n^2$ pairs, using one inequality to select a test. The pair enumeration and all selected dot products together take $O_\epsilon(d^2)$ steps. The finite field representation, scalar maps and small dimensions can be fixed once for the chosen $\epsilon$. There is no advice depending on $d$.

## A clock on the other verdict

On a candidate file, match the public bits and recover the single varying bit for every matrix entry using the [compact matrix mismatch projection](compact-bit-projection-of-matrix-mismatch.md). A dimension-tagged template can be checked in linear time. On the unpadded canonical templates for power-of-two sides one can instead try the candidate sides whose $3d^2$ distinct data bits fit in the file, in increasing powers of two. Each candidate of side $d$ takes $O(d^2)$ work, and the geometric sum is $O(N)$. Check the full syntax and length before using the shortcut. Upon a match, a nonzero computed moment certifies SAT. If no discrepancy is found or the template fails, a complete solver on the preserved original gives a globally correct algorithm. Its claimed clock applies to **nonzero** sparse errors.

A globally correct SAT solver meeting an $O(d^2)$ clock on **every** equal-product template $F_{A,B,AB}$ would give deterministic $O(d^2)$ verification on *arbitrary* triples. Compile $F_{A,B,C}$ in $O(d^2)$ steps and simulate the fixed solver up to its equal-case clock, rounding the implicit constant upward and enlarging it for finite small dimensions. Return equality upon a timely UNSAT output, and mismatch on a timely SAT output or a timeout. Every equal product finishes within the clock and every unequal product can only return SAT. Conversely, given an unrestricted deterministic $O(d^2)$ matrix verifier, template recognition and a fallback give a globally correct SAT solver meeting this clock on every template. Thus for any promised set of error matrices containing zero and allowing arbitrary factors, a globally correct solver that is fast on *both* verdicts would already attain the honest-product clock. This point holds even for the singleton error family $\{0\}$.

Künnemann gives a deterministic sparse-error verification algorithm with a soft-quadratic bound through $d$ errors for polynomially bounded integer matrices. Bennett, Gajulapalli, Golovnev and Warton study parity checks for sparse verification and the limits of fixed linear tests for arbitrary errors. Here the finite alphabet, packed word model and external promise are part of the runtime statement. Priority for the exact uniform binary bound in this model has not been established. [Künnemann, ESA 2018](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2018.56) · [Bennett et al., APPROX/RANDOM 2024](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2024.42)

## One computable error matrix per dimension captures the uniform obstruction

There is a particular, computably specified sequence of nonzero matrices $E_d$ for which the following are equivalent.

1. Some globally correct SAT RAM solves every projected triple with $AB-C=E_d$ in $O(d^2)$ steps.
2. A uniform deterministic RAM verifies arbitrary binary matrix products in $O(d^2)$ steps.

Only SAT-positive templates appear in the first assertion. The construction can even keep just one chosen positive triple per dimension and use the same equivalence for that smaller test suite.

Enumerate obligations $(P,k,h)$ consisting of a program, a positive integer clock multiplier and a starting dimension. Use a standard effective encoding in which these integers are at most the enumeration index plus one. At dimension $d$ consider the first $d$ obligations, omitting those already served and those with $h>d$. For each candidate, exhaust all unequal triples of this dimension and simulate $P$ for $k d^2$ steps on each corresponding canonical file. An obligation has a witness if some simulation fails to return SAT within the clock. Serve the least indexed witnessed obligation, record its lexicographically first witness and mark it served. If no obligation has a witness, record a fixed triple with a single nonzero error. Define $E_d$ from the recorded triple. Bounded simulation makes this a total effective rule even for programs that fail to decide SAT. With the ordinary effective finite ISA, recomputing all earlier choices through $d$ takes $2^{O(d^2)}$ time. The output sequence thus has a fixed description when given $d$; the computation that produces a test is allowed exponential time.

Suppose a correct program $P$ is fast on the chosen triples but lacks any positive $O(d^2)$ clock on *all* unequal products. Choose $k$ larger than its clock constant on the chosen triples and $h$ above that clock's finite threshold. For this fixed obligation there are witnesses at infinitely many dimensions, since a finite set of exceptions would give $P$ an eventual positive clock on all unequal triples. Each smaller-index obligation can preempt it at most once. Thus its own witness is eventually recorded at some $d\ge h$, contradicting the chosen-triple clock. Therefore $P$ has a positive $O(d^2)$ clock on all unequal triples. Run it with that clock and declare equality on timeout. Every mismatch reports SAT in time, and correctness precludes a SAT answer on any equal triple. This yields the second assertion. For the converse, feed the matrices from a matching template to the general verifier and use a complete SAT fallback outside the template.

This is a machine-enumeration characterization of the remaining problem. It establishes neither assertion. The efficient promises above give fast actual algorithms from structure that can be processed within the allowed time. An arbitrarily slow uniform specification of a singleton error family offers no comparable guarantee.

In particular, a uniform routine that returns one nonzero coordinate of this particular $E_d$ in $O(d^2)$ time would imply the general verifier. On each template a complete solver can first check that coordinate with one length-$d$ dot product, return SAT upon disagreement, and fall back otherwise. More generally the same fast path works for any collection of possible errors whose nonzero supports are all hit by a publicly computable list of $O(dw)$ coordinates produced in $O(d^2)$ time. Pack the rows of $A$ and the columns of $B$ in a common lane order within $O(d^2)$ time. One dot product requires $O(\lceil d/w\rceil)$ wordwise AND and XOR operations, followed by parity of the accumulated word. A parity table for chunks of $\lfloor(\log_2 d)/2\rfloor$ bits costs $O(d)$ to construct and handles a $w$-bit word in a constant number of lookups. This covers all listed coordinates in the same quadratic budget. The frozen family is small enough to specify by a single rule; its coordinate-generation time is the unresolved condition.


The preservation statement concerns each individual program. In fact no $O(d^2)$ locator or uniformly generated $O(dw)$-coordinate list can hit the selected support eventually, even if a separate general matrix verifier is fast. A program checking that list can deliberately delay on each miss, and at every large dimension a singleton error outside the list supplies such a miss.
