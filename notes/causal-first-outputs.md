# Predicting when new words first appear

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Here is a test for the linear SAT goal using a list of only about $\sqrt q$ numbers at output length $q$. The $j$th number is the fraction of all $q$-bit words first produced by short programs during time interval $j$. If SAT takes linear deterministic time, no fixed causal forecaster with $o(K)$ bits of advice can predict this $K$-bin histogram with vanishing total absolute error at infinitely many lengths on a timely chosen grid. At a resolution chosen after the experiment, a short accurate description always exists. The distinction concerns **when the resolution is chosen**. [Unconditional approximation](regular-clock-scale.md)

## The forecasting experiment

Fix the efficient plain interpreter $V$ with known input length, one final output per halting computation, and a literal compiler for any fixed native algorithm. Give this auxiliary interpreter enough address bits for the exponential clocks below, for instance $(|p|+2)^2$ bits on program $p$. Its deterministic steps have uniformly constructible polynomial-in-word-width Boolean implementations. A simulation of the decoding algorithms below on $V$ incurs at most a polynomial in $q$ overhead when the total clock is $2^{O(q)}$ and $|p|=q-O(1)$. The hypothetical SAT algorithm itself runs on the project's ordinary log-word RAM. [Interpreter specification and single-clock construction](full-cube-advice.md)

Let $N=2^q$. For any integer cutoff $t$, let $E(t)$ contain the $q$-bit words printed exactly and halted on by some program of length at most $q-2$ by time $t$. The number of eligible programs is $N/2-1$. Put $p=\lfloor\log_2 q\rfloor$, $F=2^{\lfloor p/2\rfloor}$, and choose a power of two $K$ dividing $F$, tending to infinity with $q$. Partition the same time interval at
$$
 t_j=2^{q+j(F/K)\lfloor q/F\rfloor},\qquad 0\le j\le K.
$$
All grids share their endpoints. Adjacent cutoffs have ratio at least $2^{\lfloor q/F\rfloor}\ge 2^F$, and $t_K\le 2^{2q}$. Define $m_j=(|E(t_{j+1})|-|E(t_j)|)/N$. These are nonnegative and sum to less than $1/2$. Two names for the same word contribute a single first appearance.

Suppose one fixed algorithm computes $K(q)$ before the experiment in time $2^q q^{O(1)}$. A fixed forecaster $P$ gets $q,K$, advice $z$, bin $j$, and the exact integer counts $|E(t_i)|$ for $0\le i\le j$. It can take $t_j q^{O(1)}$ steps at each call. Use clocked computations, with a default zero output on timeout, and a dyadic forecast in $[0,1]$ with $q$ fractional bits. Rounding an arbitrary probability to this precision adds at most $K/N$ total error.

**Claim.** If SAT is decidable in $O(n)$ deterministic time, for each such grid program and forecaster there exist constants $\alpha,\epsilon>0$ such that, for every sufficiently large $q$ and every $z$ of length at most $\alpha K$, its forecasts on the true history have $\sum_j |P_j-m_j|\ge\epsilon$. Forecasts need not obey a prior total-mass promise.

## Choosing bins against forecasters that see the past

An allocation algorithm reserves $\beta N$ short names, where $\beta=2^{-a}$ is fixed below. Let $s=2\lfloor\beta K/128\rfloor$, a positive even number eventually, and set $B=\lfloor 8s/\beta\rfloor\le K/8$. We choose exactly $s$ bins. Each adviser raises an alarm when its forecast is at least $\beta/(4s)$. Cap its alarms at $B$ and pad them when the number of remaining positions equals the number of remaining permitted alarms. This makes exactly $B$ flags for each adviser. If its total forecast is at most 1 then it raises at most $4s/\beta$ raw alarms and every raw alarm belongs to the padded set: once padding starts every remaining bin is flagged. Any adviser with forecast sum exceeding 1 already has absolute error greater than $1/2$.

For an arbitrary fixed set of $B$ flags, the chance that a uniform $s$-subset hits at least $s/2$ flags is at most
$$
 \binom{s}{s/2}(B/K)^{s/2}\le 2^{-s/2}.
$$
Indeed, take $s$ ordered draws without replacement and choose which $s/2$ draws hit. For $\alpha=\beta/1024$, summing this bound over fewer than $2^{\alpha K+1}$ advice strings gives a quantity strictly below one for large $K$.

The choice can be made **one bin at a time after seeing its flags**. With $n$ positions left and $u$ selections owed, the number of allowed completions is $\binom nu$. For each adviser with $b$ flags owed and $h$ hits already, count bad completions against any fixed arrangement of its $b$ remaining flags. The count is
$$
 \sum_{i+h\ge s/2}\binom bi\binom{n-b}{u-i},
$$
with binomial coefficients zero outside range. By permutation symmetry the count is the same for every arrangement. When the current flag is revealed, take such an arrangement with that flag first. The bad count splits as the sum of its two branch counts, including infeasible branches with zero capacity. Sum over advisers. Since total bad count is strictly below capacity, at least one decision preserves strict inequality. Choose the first such decision. At the end there is one completion and zero bad advisers. All binomial coefficients use $O(K)$ bits, and the work per bin apart from obtaining the forecasts is polynomial in $K$ per adviser. This is the method of conditional probabilities applied to quotas, with both future histories and future flags allowed to change. [Raghavan](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1986/5986.html) · [Lean proof of the online choice and quota identities](../lean/CausalForecastChoice.lean)

## Turning selections into actual first outputs

Assume a linear-time SAT solver. Within $t_j q^{O(1)}$ time we can recover the exact size of $E(t_j)$ or a specified bit of the word of a given rank in its complement. Guess a length-$N$ membership table, scan it for the requested count bit or complement rank bit, and universally select one entry. A one entry requires a program and valid bounded transcript producing that word. A zero entry requires that every proposed program and transcript fails verification. The number of quantifier blocks is fixed. The table, computations and transcripts have size or construction cost $t_j q^{O(1)}$. Sort memory records by address and time to check RAM reads, including initialization, in quasilinear encoded size. Eliminating the fixed number of blocks under the SAT assumption retains that bound. All bit queries refer to the unique correct table. In particular, the check of $E(t_j)$ has a finite clock even when a simulated program includes the decoder itself. [Fixed-block compilation and memory verification](linear-quantifiers.md) · [Fortnow's quasilinear reduction](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/npvsnl.pdf)

On a raw payload $v$ of length $q-a$, take $v\bmod s$ as a zero-based ordinal among the selected bins and $\lfloor v/s\rfloor$ as its ordinal among missing words. Recompute the common allocation up to the indicated selection, then unrank the missing word at that bin's starting cutoff $t_j$ and print it. There are more than $N/2$ available words at each start. By summing the costs through bin $j$, the decoder, including native simulation, takes at most $2^{\alpha K+O(1)} t_j q^{O(1)}$ steps. The gap $t_{j+1}/t_j\ge2^F$ absorbs this bound for all large $q$. The update can explicitly compute every earlier count and run every adviser with the exact history. No parallel-evaluation assumption is needed. One fixed header codes $V$, the SAT solver, the clocked grid and forecasting programs, and a decoder for the self-delimiting parameter $a$. Choose $a$ large enough to pay for the header, its $O(\log(a+2))$ parameter encoding, and two bits of savings. This gives the reserved $\beta N$ eligible names at every large $q$.

In each selected bin the different ordinals give at least $\lfloor\beta N/s\rfloor\ge\beta N/(2s)$ distinct words absent at its start and present at its end. They can also be printed by other names inside the same bin. Each adviser of total forecast at most 1 misses at least $s/2$ selected bins. Its total underprediction on those bins is at least $\beta/8$. This proves the claim with $\epsilon=\beta/8$ for the specified forecast representation. The unconditional theorem here is the implication from linear SAT. The finite choice rule is formalized in Lean. The RAM reductions and timing argument are mathematical proofs in this note.

## Attempts to obtain the SAT lower bound

* **Predict from exact past counts.** A good causal predictor with $o(K)$ advice at infinitely many lengths would suffice. Delaying a simulation until $t_{j+1}$ lets it see the answers after its forecasting deadline. Simulating all potential names up to $t_j$ to choose outputs inside the next bin costs about $N t_j$ steps, exceeding the budget on the finest grid. On coarser clocks with gaps exceeding $N$ times the simulation overhead, that same exhaustive procedure can implement the allocation unconditionally. This limits what an extension to the quadratic or cubic SAT target by widening the clocks could show.
* **Use a smooth empirical distribution.** Cumulative probabilities always have accurate step-function descriptions at fixed uniform error with $O(\log K)$ bits, by listing the positions of fixed quantiles. That error can hide a constant absolute error in the masses of the individual bins. A profile supported on alternating bins and a uniform profile illustrate the loss.
* **Use a short description from a coarser resolution.** The regularity argument gives a positive unconditional approximation for the same physical measure. It places a short description on some grid among a growing family. A rule selecting an accurate grid by the first cutoff at infinitely many lengths remains the missing step. Simply splitting the names equally among the grids loses the mass needed for the forecasting argument.
