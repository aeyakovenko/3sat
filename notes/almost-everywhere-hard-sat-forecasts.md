# A balanced, short-label 3-SAT distribution hard to forecast unconditionally

For every fixed positive integer $A$ there is a uniform family of ordinary explicit 3-CNFs on a logarithmic-word random-access machine with no space restriction. At stage $j$ the family has common encoded length $N_j=2^j(j+1)^{O_A(1)}$, with $N_j\ge2^j$. A public label of $O_A(\log(j+2))$ bits specifies the formula. Take the label **uniformly**. Exactly half the labels give a uniquely satisfiable formula, and the rest give an unsatisfiable formula.

For every fixed deterministic RAM predictor $D$, there exists a fixed $C$ such that, at **every sufficiently large stage**, even with an arbitrary common advice string of length at most $\lfloor\log_2(j+1)\rfloor$, its accuracy at clock $N_j/(\log N_j)^C$ satisfies
$$
           \left|\Pr[D=\mathrm{SAT}]-\tfrac12\right|\le(j+1)^{-A}.
$$
Here $D=\mathrm{SAT}$ denotes equality of the predicted bit to the true verdict, including on negative instances. The machine can use random access to the entire explicit formula and may receive the stage and public label for free. A fixed default bit on timeout gives a total prediction. The bound also holds for either choice of default. Consequently a fixed solver whose timely answers are all correct can finish on at most $2(j+1)^{-A}$ of the draws at this clock. The advice supplied to such a solver must preserve correctness of its timely answers. The quantifiers include every allowed advice at each late stage. There is no SAT-time hypothesis.

The support in this version has only $(\log N_j)^{O_A(1)}$ elements. All query-dependent bits occupy publicly specified positions, and the rest of the formula can be generated from the label alone. The comparison concerns fast **computation** given that short label. We prove an advice-time tradeoff below, including the case of no advice. All clocks here remain below $N_j$.

## Choosing a hidden word against complete forecast tables

Put $M=2^j$. Let $b=b(j)$ be a nonnegative advice bound computable from $j$ in polynomial-in-$j$ time, with $b\le\beta j$ eventually for some fixed $\beta<1/2$. The headline uses $b=\lfloor\log_2(j+1)\rfloor$. Choose
$$
 H=j+1,\quad l=\lceil\log_2(j+2)\rceil,\quad h=b+(2A+2)l,\quad Q=2^h.
$$
The native program on public stage $j$ simulates the first $H$ binary reporter texts in length-lexicographic order, with a bounded universal interpreter on a zero input and stage metadata. Each receives at most $\lfloor M/H\rfloor$ interpreter operations. A reporter may stream complete binary truth tables on $(x,\tau)\in\mathbb F_2^h\times\{0,1\}$ in a prescribed order. Keep at most $2^{b+1}-1$ complete tables per reporter. Ignore incomplete final tables, invalid outputs and excess tables. The reader can charge the bit-output operations and array writes to work, or reserve the indicated total table storage explicitly.

For a table of predicted SAT verdicts, undo the terminal pin on its response: a predicted YES gives proposed checksum $\tau$ and a predicted NO gives $1-\tau$. Let $g(x)\in\{-1,0,1\}$ be the average over the two pins of the sign $(-1)^{\text{proposed checksum}}$. Its correlation at a candidate word $a\in\mathbb F_2^h$ is $c_g(a)=Q^{-1}\sum_x g(x)(-1)^{a\cdot x}$. If the true answer is $[a\cdot x=\tau]$, the table has accuracy $(1+c_g(a))/2$. Walsh orthogonality gives $\sum_a c_g(a)^2=Q^{-1}\sum_x g(x)^2\le1$. For $K\le H(2^{b+1}-1)$ retained tables choose the lexicographically first $a_j$ minimizing $\sum_g c_g(a)^2$. Its value is at most $K/Q$, the average upper bound. Every retained forecast therefore has absolute advantage at most $\tfrac12\sqrt{K/Q}<(j+1)^{-A}$. The empty portfolio can use the zero word.

Compute the correlations by binary Walsh transforms, using integer sums before division by the common $Q$. Splitting by one input digit replaces two entries by their sum and difference. There are $O(hQ)$ operations per table, and summing squares across tables requires only $O(j)$ bits per entry. The full time for these operations and the final scan is $2^{2b}\operatorname{poly}_A(j)=o(M)$. The native simulation clocks sum to at most $M$. Fixed sparse bank simulation, clearing touched positions, and the finite-word implementation take $O(M)$ in total with a fixed sufficiently large word coefficient; any shared small arithmetic tables of size $2^{j/4}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ can be prepared once. Each reporter has a fresh bank and the same stage convention. Execute no unclocked varying program. Hardwire arbitrary conventions at finitely many small stages. Thus one uniform deterministic native RAM outputs $a_j$ in $O(M)$ time. This is the elementary Walsh diagonal used earlier for polynomial-time languages. ## The ordinary formula and the fixed reporter for a heuristic

Compile that program on its canonical input into the parsimonious, fixed-clock, ordinary [near-native gate-table 3-CNF](projected-parity-holes.md#a-parsimonious-ordinary-3-cnf-near-the-native-clock), exposing the $h=O(j)$ bits of its output register(s) at their final slots. The word width is sufficient to store $h$ bits or use constantly many words. The native program and its run are independent of $(x,\tau)$. Append $h$ input variables with fixed-position signed pins encoding $x$, AND gates multiplying them by the respective output bits, a running XOR, and the single terminal pin asserting the XOR equals $\tau$. Gate equivalences and the entire sorted chronological witness have unique extensions. Fixed-width clause slots and the shared false-variable padding if needed give width exactly three with distinct variables. Pad with satisfied fixed slots to ensure at least $M$ occupied bits. All inputs at each stage have identical binary bit length, and distinct public labels have distinct strings. An individual word can be computed from $(j,x,\tau)$ and its address in $\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ steps. Printing a formula needs only $N_j\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ time. Fairness and model counts follow for every stage, even if $a_j=0$.

To target a particular $D$, use a reporter which knows this fixed compiler and program text. It enumerates all binary advice strings of length at most $b$, and for each advice runs $D$ on every $(x,\tau)$ with the physical formula generated at the positions $D$ reads. Supply the public metadata and reset work memory between queries. Clearing touched cells or generation tags give a fresh bank without scanning its numerical address range. One-time shared interpreter setup and polynomial-in-$j$ work per input are allowed. Cap each simulation at
$$
               T_j=\left\lfloor\frac{M}{2^{2b}(j+1)^B}\right\rfloor
$$
steps of $D$, using one fixed timeout bit. The reporter is deterministic, uses the [native word-width simulation](native-unary-clock-band.md), and prints a complete table per advice, including tables for incorrect advice. With polynomial-in-$j$ local word generation and fixed-machine simulation costs its total non-setup time is at most $2^{b+1}(2Q)(T_j+1)\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$. The polynomial accounts also for serialization. For sufficiently large fixed $B$ the $T_j$ term fits $o(M/H)$, and the remaining $2^{2b}\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ and the one-time setup do as well. The reporter is fixed once $A,b(\cdot),D,B$ and the timeout bit are fixed. Interpreter overhead for its fixed code is bounded by a fixed factor. Both timeout choices may be handled by separate fixed reporters. Eventually the native program runs both, and their tables necessarily entered the minimization. The universal stage program only reads tables; it does not call its formula compiler. The later reporter can calculate every formula word without knowing the word selected by the minimization. This order of construction avoids requiring a solver to simulate a completed run while generating an input word.

The two fixed timeouts give the early-answer statement. When all timely answers for the given advice are correct, averaging the accuracies with default zero and default one gives $1/2+p/2$, where $p$ is the timely halting fraction. Both accuracies obey the same bound. For $b=\lfloor\log_2(j+1)\rfloor$ the factor $2^{2b}$ is polynomial in $j$, and $N_j/M$ is also polynomial in $j$, giving the length-based clock in the statement. For $b=\lfloor\sqrt j\rfloor$ one instead obtains $\Theta(\sqrt{\log N_j})$ advice bits at time $N_j 2^{-2\lfloor\sqrt j\rfloor}/(\log N_j)^{O(1)}$. For $b=\lfloor\beta j\rfloor$, $0<\beta<1/2$, the time scale is $N_j^{1-2\beta}/(\log N_j)^{O(1)}$. Independent random bits bounded by the advice allowance can also be enumerated as additional advice, provided the sum of original advice bits and fixed-length tape bits fits the budget, with their split fixed or encoded. The unrestricted-randomness heuristic case is not covered.

## A nearly sharp tradeoff for absolute advantage and advice

There is a weighted variant of the same native program. Give each complete table from reporter number $i$, starting at zero, weight $2^{-i-1}$. Minimize the weighted sum of squared coefficients. The sum of these weights over all reported tables is at most $2^{b+1}$. At the chosen word every table from a **fixed** reporter $i$ has absolute correlation at most $\sqrt{2^{i+1}2^{b+1}/2^h}$. Multiply all weights by $2^H$ for exact integer computations. This adds $H=O(j)$ bits to the accumulator length. For each fixed word-RAM predictor and the printed stage family the absolute advantage bound becomes $O_D(2^{-(h-b)/2})$; both its table-generating reporters have fixed indices.

More generally choose constructible $b,h$ with $0\le b<h=O(j)$ and $b+h\le(1-\xi)j$ for a fixed $\xi>0$. The weighted table transforms take $2^{b+h}\operatorname{poly}(j)=o(M)$ native work. The reporter for a predictor can still finish on the advice-by-label grid with per-input cap $M/(2^{b+h}(j+1)^B)$ for sufficiently large fixed $B$. In particular choose $b=\lfloor\alpha j\rfloor$ and $h=\lfloor\beta j\rfloor$, where $0\le\alpha<\beta$ and $\alpha+\beta<1$ are fixed computable rational constants. At every sufficiently late stage the resulting family has time cap $M^{1-\alpha-\beta}/(j+1)^B$, with absolute prediction advantage at most $O_D(M^{-(\beta-\alpha)/2})$. This is a time, advice and error tradeoff at fixed choices of parameters; changing them can change the formula family.

The advice exponent in this bound is sharp up to a fixed factor even when the hidden word is arbitrary. Let the advice contain $b$ true coordinates of $a$. Predict their known contribution at the corresponding bits of $x$, and on the remaining $m=h-b$ bits use the quadratic $x_1x_2+\dots+x_{m-1}x_m$ when $m$ is even. On each two-bit block the correlation against every linear form is $+1/2$ or $-1/2$, as one sees by summing four inputs. Independence gives absolute correlation exactly $2^{-m/2}$ and the known pin turns this into half that absolute advantage. For the headline choices $m$ is even; for the other tradeoffs one may adjust $h$ by one to make it even. This predictor needs only the public short label and the supplied advice. The sign of its correlation may be negative. The upper bound on absolute advantage accommodates both signs; an extra advice bit can orient the forecast. For perspective, $h$ advice bits containing the actual $a_j$ give a fast perfect predictor on the promise, since the public label is available. Thus advice cannot be allowed without charge; the gap between $h$ and $b$ is only $O_A(\log j)$. This version extends the earlier Walsh argument to a single physically explicit SAT family with uniform labels and clocks just below native time. Worst-case-to-average-case hardness amplification, average-time hierarchies, and Walsh decoding are established subjects. Historical priority for this precise RAM/SAT consequence has not been established. [Goldreich and Levin](https://doi.org/10.1145/73007.73010) · [Cai and Selman on average-time hierarchies](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539796311715) · [Earlier conditional randomized SAT distribution](seed-filter-advice.md)

A contradiction with **linear** time would require the simulation and the formula encoding to fit within a clock larger than an arbitrary fixed multiple of the encoded length. Here the native stage already spends time simulating the forecast tables, and its own explicit computation formula has $M\operatorname{poly}(j)$ bits. The address and compiler costs as well as the number of tested labels go in the opposite direction. This establishes average-case prediction hardness only at the displayed sublinear clocks. The unrestricted linear, quadratic and cubic time exclusions remain open.
