# Can unreliable predictions certify a hierarchy computation?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** Consider a computation with a long truth table. A partial verifier might predict some linear functions of the table without knowing a single accepting history. How weak could these predictions be and still contradict cubic SAT? The first construction below allows errors, abstentions, and different advice at every length. The second treats perfectly reliable claims separately and needs only an unbounded number of linearly independent claims. Both constructions are ordinary deterministic exponential-time languages. Neither supplies the missing predictor for its own language.

## Coding one length

At source length n let N=2ⁿ, b=2n and identify a binary table Y with the polynomial Σ_{i<N}YᵢXⁱ over the binary field. Let F be the set of monic irreducible binary polynomials of degree b, I its cardinality and t=⌊(N−1)/b⌋. Encode one Boolean question by a modulus f∈F and a b-bit mask v. The answer is the binary inner product of v and the coefficient vector of Y modulo f. The uniform distribution below is on these I·2ᵇ valid questions. We use n large enough that I>t. There are I=(1+o(1))2ᵇ/b moduli. In a field of order 2ᵇ the elements of degree below b lie in proper subfields, with at most Σ_{d≤b/2}2ᵈ elements altogether. Each degree-b irreducible has b conjugate roots.

Two different tables have equal residues at at most t moduli. Each such modulus divides their difference and the moduli are relatively prime. On every other modulus exactly half of the masks agree. Consequently the normalized inner product of the two sign codewords is between zero and δ=t/I, inclusive. Polynomial remainder codes, Chinese remaindering with errors, Hadamard encoding and the use of list decoding for hardness amplification all have precedents. The conditional test below uses the resulting pairwise inner-product bound. [Goldreich, Ron and Sudan](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~oded/PSX/crt.pdf) · [Sudan, Trevisan and Vadhan](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.2000.1730)

A verifier receives n, f, v, a proposed answer bit, and optional read-only advice. Treat a bit as certified when there exists a witness accepted within the clock. Predict a bit if exactly one of the two bits is certified. Otherwise abstain. This convention even permits a verifier that accepts both bits on some questions. Write C and E for the numbers of correct and incorrect predictions, M=C+E, Q=I·2ᵇ, and G=C−E. The predicted sign function g has values in {−1,0,1}, with zero on abstentions. Its mean square is p=M/Q and its normalized inner product with the sign codeword of the true table is G/Q.

**List bound.** For any such g, at most 2Q² tables satisfy

    G² > M t 2ᵇ.

For a proof, among tables meeting the strict inequality with positive G take the smallest such correlation τ=G/Q. If there are L of these tables with sign codewords c₁,…,c_L, Cauchy–Schwarz for the uniform mean gives

    L²τ² ≤ ⟨g,Σᵢcᵢ⟩² ≤ p ⟨Σᵢcᵢ,Σᵢcᵢ⟩ ≤ p (L + L(L−1)δ).

The positive denominator τ²−pδ is at least 1/Q². This follows from the strict inequality between the integers G² and M t 2ᵇ. Hence L≤p(1−δ)Q²≤Q². Apply the same argument to −g for the negative correlations. The bound includes discrepancies arbitrarily close to the threshold. For a sound partial verifier E=0, it suffices to certify more than t·2ᵇ questions. For a total predictor, a bias in success probability over one half larger than √(t/I)/2 suffices. Here t/I is asymptotic to 2⁻ⁿ.

One cannot generally select a unique table just from weak agreement. Fix an even h≤min(b,N) and use tables of degree less than h, so each residue is the table polynomial itself. A predictor that computes the sum of the products of consecutive pairs of the first h mask coordinates has absolute correlation 2^(−h/2) with every one of these 2ʰ tables. Indeed the four-term signed sum for one pair has absolute value two regardless of its two target bits, and the sums multiply over the pairs. This attains the inverse-square list-size scale inside this particular code. [Rothaus](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(76)90024-8)

## A fixed ordinary computation that excludes the whole list

We specify the source language H before choosing a SAT solver or a verifier. Use a prefix-free encoding of ordinary deterministic multitape programs. For each prefix e at length n its block has k=2^(n−|e|) slots. On a source input in that block interpret the suffix as its slot j. Simulate program e on the explicitly given n, e and j for at most 2^(150n) of **its own** steps. Use its output bit if timely and use zero on timeout, malformed input or malformed output. A direct universal simulation, even charging a quadratic overhead, places H in deterministic multitape time 2^{O(n)}. A program can generate the complement of a purported fast decider on its own addressed input, so the usual diagonal obstruction applies to this universal table. The list argument exploits many separate slots belonging to the same program.

Suppose a deterministic logarithmic-word RAM decides ordinary SAT of m bits in O(m³) time. Fix an ordinary nondeterministic verifier R for coded bit claims with a clock n^{O(1)}2ⁿ, uniformly over each advised query. For any fixed advice of at most N bits the assumed solver can tabulate R's sign function in RAM time 2^(7n+o(n)). Enumerate all 2ᵇ monic degree-b modulus candidates and 2ᵇ masks, and query acceptance of each proposed bit via a quasilinear explicit SAT encoding of the bounded computation. The advice fits in the query length. Test irreducibility by repeated squaring and polynomial gcd, for example checking for factors through degree ⌊b/2⌋ using the polynomials X^(2ʲ)−X. Computing the table of a proposed Y on the same domain costs less. Evaluate one remainder per modulus in N·poly(n) bit operations, then compare its dot products to g. Thus evaluating the displayed integer test for a given advice and Y fits the same deterministic clock. No call to H occurs in this computation. [Quasilinear RAM reductions](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

Construct an ordinary deterministic program D_R, containing the hypothesized SAT algorithm as code. On input n, a block identifier e and a slot, use the k slots of that block and consider **all** advice strings of length at most ⌊k/2⌋. There are fewer than 2^(⌊k/2⌋+1) such strings, each giving a list of at most 2Q² potentially well-predicted tables. For fixed e and all sufficiently large n their union projects to fewer than 2ᵏ block patterns: log₂ Q ≤ 4n while k grows exponentially. To find the lexicographically first missing pattern, build it a bit at a time. Given a prefix, the test is

    ∃ k-bit extension w of this prefix
      ∀ allowed advice a and N-bit table Y,
        Y restricted to the block differs from w, or its score for R_a fails the strict test.

All quantified strings have explicit length O(N) including a length header for advice. For fixed w, the existence of a counterexample a,Y has an explicit quasilinear SAT reduction from time 2^(7n+o(n)). Decide it in RAM time 2^(21n+o(n)) and complement the bit. This is an ordinary deterministic subprogram, including the actual SAT solver. A second reduction for the outer existential then costs 2^(63n+o(n)) RAM time to solve. Greedy prefix selection takes at most O(k) calls, hence 2^(64n+o(n)) total. A multitape machine can simulate this RAM by scanning its list of address/value pairs at each access in time 2^(128n+o(n)), below the specified simulated-step cutoff. Where no missing pattern exists the program can return zero. It outputs the selected pattern's requested slot. These clocks charge the multiple quantifiers and the cost of writing the SAT inputs.

Now take e to be the code of D_R. At every sufficiently large n, the very definition of H puts that missing pattern on its block. If H's table had passed the strict score test for **any** allowed advice, this pattern would be in the list. Thus under cubic SAT, for every fixed R and all sufficiently large n,

    (C−E)² ≤ (C+E) · 2ᵇ · ⌊(2ⁿ−1)/b⌋

on the actual H table, simultaneously for all advice of length at most 2^(n−|e|−1). In particular this covers every advice family of o(2ⁿ) bits. Constants in the advice bound depend on the verifier and simulated solver. No decoder needs to recognize which advice is accurate or which lengths are favorable.

An ordinary verifier violating this inequality, even at infinitely many unrecognized lengths with sublinear-in-N advice and many wrong answers, would exclude cubic SAT. At advice length N one can store the truth table and answer every query in N·poly(n) time. Storing values at known moduli instead uses b bits per residue and predicts all 2ᵇ masks at each stored modulus. The threshold t+1 for this strategy already accounts for essentially N bits of stored values. Neither strategy yields the required smaller advice.

## Fewer claims when all of them are sound

Here a different diagonal language J suffices. A claim is a bit equation ℓ·Y=a over GF(2) for the length-n source table. Allow any fixed, uniformly enumerable family of claim indices of O(n) bits with normals ℓ∈{0,1}ᴺ computable in 2^{O(n)} time, with a fixed exponent for the family. The CRT-mask questions above are one example. A unit normal claims an individual source answer. The following construction does not assume small pairwise correlations.

For length n, enumerate deterministic predictor programs 1,…,n and truncate each query after 2^(20n) simulated multitape steps. Maintain a consistent list of binary linear equations on the N coefficients, initially empty. At stage i simulate program i on the candidate claims in canonical order. If it proposes a claim whose normal lies outside the span of the preceding equations, add the equation with the **opposite** bit as right-hand side and end the stage. Otherwise add nothing. Independence guarantees consistency with all earlier choices. Take Jₙ to be the lexicographically first full solution. All simulations, normal computations and elimination take at most 2^{O(n)} time, with a fixed exponent. This defines J on ordinary input strings. Variants of diagonalization against consistently correct partial algorithms are well established in the literature on resource-bounded immunity. Here signed linear equations allow a claim about many source answers at once. [Mayordomo](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(94)00023-C) · [Orponen, Ko, Schöning and Watanabe](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/iui/Ulmer_Informatik_Berichte/1991/UIB-1991-01.pdf)

Under cubic SAT a fixed nondeterministic n^{O(1)}2ⁿ-time claim verifier has an ordinary deterministic simulated program i that decides whether it proposes each bit, within this cutoff eventually. A single explicit SAT query costs 2^(3n+o(n)) RAM time, or 2^(6n+o(n)) on tapes. At every length n≥i beyond this timing threshold, either one of its claims is false or the rank of **all** its claimed normals is at most i−1. Indeed a newly imposed opposite bit remains true of Jₙ forever, while no more than i−1 independent equations precede this stage. Consequently a fixed verifier that makes no false claim at the tested lengths and achieves unbounded ranks along them would suffice to refute cubic SAT. It need not explain any particular coefficient of J, and the claimed rank can grow arbitrarily slowly. This version has no free nonuniform advice.

**Attack the soundness premise using sparsity.** Jₙ always has at most n one bits. In a minimum lexicographic solution, the columns at the one positions are linearly independent: a nontrivial dependency among those columns would let us switch all selected ones to zero without changing any equation. The rank is at most n. Knowledge of the support is therefore only O(n²) advice bits even for an arbitrary family of normals. Guessing that support can certify false parities as well as true ones.

For the specific family consisting solely of the CRT-mask queries, even the *location* of the sparse support is constrained without knowing the choices of the simulated programs. Order the table bits from low polynomial degree to high. Let B be the product of the distinct moduli used by the chosen equations, of degree d≤nb. They all have nonzero constant term, so X is a unit modulo B. When d>0 the residues of X^(N−d),…,X^(N−1) form a binary basis modulo B, since they are a common unit times the ordinary power basis. For n large enough that d≤N, every polynomial can consequently be replaced by one supported on those final d positions with exactly the same chosen remainders. Lex minimization sets every earlier coefficient to zero. At d=0 the entire solution is zero. Thus listing the at most n nonzero offsets in a *known* tail of length nb needs only O(n log(n+2)) bits of advice. This also exposes a failed shortcut: certifying any of the many known zero coefficients directly would ask a unit-vector question outside this restricted family. On any b consecutive coefficients the residues of the powers span the full field, so a nonzero single CRT mask cannot vanish identically on the entire known tail of possible support. Admitting unit queries into the diagonal would change the tail argument. The sparse language J therefore cannot simply be substituted for the language H that must defeat even o(N) advice.

A tempting source of claims is that several indexed computations apparently end in the same bit. Merely knowing that at least one member of a block has a given bit does not give the parity of that block. Two assignments, one with one matching position and one with two, already give opposite parities. Forcing exactly one matching position per block would impose a known linear equation. A version of the diagonal respecting those equations can invert only claims independent *modulo the already imposed equations*. Padding the truth table with known parity checks therefore cannot furnish the independent information missing from this test. Another possibility is to guess a compatible completion independently for each claim. The resulting bits need not form a compatible table: if the only two completions are 01 and 10, each position can separately be claimed as one, but 11 violates their common odd-parity equation. No fast ordinary verifier producing the independent information for J, or the score surplus for H, has been constructed.

The finite experiment checks the cleared correlation inequality for all 65,536 fully specified predictors on a small code, a further 4,000 partial predictors, and 65,536 pairs of claim menus. Lean checks cancellation of a nonconstant binary mask character, the integer-gap deduction, exclusion of a missing projected pattern, and the consistency step given a direction independent of prior constraints.
