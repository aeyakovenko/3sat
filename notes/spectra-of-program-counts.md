# A single count matrix and two subgraphs of actual output words

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** I tested two ways to simplify the program-fiber condition. A single matrix counts every producing name with the same weight in a suitable fixed characteristic. There is also a real-variable test that only needs to separate a chosen positive fraction of hard words from the easy words in one rectangle. Its dual produces two dense unweighted graphs on the actual hard and easy words. In every tested square block, the difference of their adjacency matrices has spectral norm within a constant factor of the square root of its side length.

Use even $q$, $N=2^q$, $L=2^{q/2}$ and the fixed plain deadline $T=N(q+2)^{\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil}$. For a hypothetical deterministic $O(n^d)$ SAT solver on the unrestricted logarithmic-word RAM, put $J=L$ for $d=1$. For $d=2,3$ use $J=2^s$, the largest power of two with $2^{2s}7^{sd^2}\le N$. Both halves of each word have $\log_2 J$ low bits. The resulting square blocks have side $J$. The easy set $E$ consists of the actual outputs of eligible programs of length at most $q-2$ by $T$. [Program-fiber definitions and block construction](parity-of-program-fibers.md)

## Ignore early outputs for all three exponents

For linear SAT take $\tau=N$. For $d=2,3$ take the largest positive integer $\tau$ with $J^2\tau^d\le7^s$. Across all three cases write $E_0$ for the actual outputs by $\tau$ and $E_1=E\setminus E_0$. The verifier can reject early cells without storing their mask.

For $d=2,3$, give the verifier the upper row and column prefixes in each local advice record. These cost $O(q)$ bits. At each of the $J^2$ cells, the existence of a name that produced that word by $\tau$ has a quasilinear-size SAT reduction from guessing the name and simulating it. Under the hypothesized solver a test costs $(\tau+q)^d\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$. The total fits $7^s\mathrm{poly}(q)$ by the displayed cutoff. A single common suffix sample is qualified for all advice including all prefix choices. This preserves the two SAT eliminations and $J^2$ prefix-extension calls of the block construction. It gives an exponential cutoff in $q$ also for quadratic and cubic SAT and applies to the program-fiber parity tests. The linear early-name test operates on its single queried word. ## One matrix in a fixed characteristic

Let $m_x$ count all eligible names that print $x$ and halt in $(\tau,T]$. Its entries are ordinary nonnegative integers and $\sum_x m_x<N/2$. For any integer $a\ge2$, fewer than $N/(2a)$ nonempty fibers have $m_x=0\pmod a$. This follows by charging at least $a$ names to each such word.

Fix the universal verifier for a factor pair over an advised prime, taking its modulus from the advice before selecting the prime. Reject early cells and accept when the product entry is zero. The dense-advice result at density $1/4$ gives a fixed contamination lower bound $\gamma N$ before choosing any fixed modulus. A block product modulo a fixed prime uses Strassen's recursion in $7^s\mathrm{poly}(q)$ time. Bound malformed advice uniformly, for example by limiting the modulus length to $q$ and explicitly supplying the dense factors. Choose one prime $p>4/\gamma$. A rank-$r$ binary-indexed factor pair over $\mathbb F_p$ costs $O(Jr\log p+q)$ bits per block.

It follows that ranks $o(J)$ and $o(N)$ counted zero/nonzero errors in approximants to the *single* matrix $m_x\bmod p$ would contradict the assumed SAT clock. For the linear case the factors may be given globally. More generally they can differ block by block, provided the rank bound is uniform, or fails only on blocks occupying $o(N)$ cells. The accepted hard words number at least $N/2-o(N)$, while accepted easy words number at most $N/(2p)+o(N)$. The actual nonzero values of the approximants can be arbitrary in $\mathbb F_p$. To avoid choosing a prime by an unspecified constant, one can instead take $a=2^{\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil}$, supply factors over $\mathbb Z/a\mathbb Z$, and ask for inner dimension $o(J/\log(q+2))$. Strassen multiplication also works in this ring and nonempty zeros now occupy $o(N)$ cells.

## A real score on one chosen section

Fix $\delta>0$. In an arbitrary rectangle $R=U\times V$ select any set $H\subset R\setminus E$ of at least $\delta N$ genuine hard words. Let $D=E_1\cap R$. Put $Y=+1$ on $H$ and $Y=-1$ on $D$; the other cells carry no constraint. Under the $O(n^d)$ hypothesis there are constants $a,c>0$ such that every real array $S$ satisfying $\|S_b\|_*\le a J^{3/2}$ on all its $J$-square blocks satisfies
$$
\sum_{x\in H\cup D}(1-Y_x S_x)_+\ge cN.
$$
Here $\|\cdot\|_*$ is the sum of the singular values. An unrestricted real score can depend on the entire interpreter image and the choice of $H$. The constants are uniform over $R,H$ at a fixed $\delta$. In particular a sequence of such sections admitting unit-margin scores with block nuclear norms $o(J^{3/2})$ and only $o(N)$ margin violations would exclude the respective SAT clock, even when the scores at those exceptional entries are unbounded. The last assertion follows directly from the sign-error argument below.

To encode one nonzero real block with nuclear norm $t$, sample a singular component $uv^\mathsf{T}$ with probability equal to its singular value divided by $t$. Given the component, independently round each coordinate of $\sqrt J u$ and $\sqrt J v$ to one of its two adjacent integers, with the probabilities giving its original value as the expectation. The resulting integer vectors $a',b'$ satisfy $\|a'\|_1,\|b'\|_1\le 2J$ by Cauchy–Schwarz and $\|a'\|_2,\|b'\|_2\le2\sqrt J$. Thus $ta'(b')^\mathsf{T}/J$ is unbiased for the whole block after averaging over the component, with expected squared Frobenius norm at most $16t^2$. The average of $k$ independent samples has expected squared error at most $16t^2/k$. Each integer vector costs $O(J)$ bits using signed unary magnitudes with an entry terminator. To obtain the sign of the average one needs only the integer dot products. Its positive common scale requires no advice.

Fix the verifier that evaluates these integer factors, restricts to $R$ and rejects early words. Supply the rectangle through its row and column bits in each block. Choose $k=\lfloor\lambda J\rfloor$ for a fixed small $\lambda>0$ so all factors and the two bit vectors fit the advice allowance. For $d=2,3$, parse the factors into matrices, pad their inner dimension to $J$, multiply by Strassen over the integers and test positive entries. Throughout its $s$ levels the additions and subtractions enlarge operand sizes by at most polynomial factors in $J$, so intermediate integers use $O(q)$ bits. These operations and the early tests fit the block clock. For $d=1$ a scan of all the advice and the selected scalar product fits $N\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$.

By averaging select one integer approximation per block. With $a$ sufficiently small its squared approximation error summed over all blocks is an arbitrarily small constant times $N$. Zero blocks use zero factors. A cell with $Y_xS_x\ge1/2$ that gets the wrong sign costs at least $1/4$ in squared error. The number of other constrained cells is at most twice the total hinge loss. If that loss were below $cN$ for small enough $c$, the verifier would accept at least $\delta N/2$ hard words and fewer easy words than its fixed contamination threshold at that density. This proves the bound. The same reasoning directly charges a small set of margin violations by its number of cells. Margin representations, approximate trace norms and spectral witnesses have an established role in communication complexity. ## Unweighted subgraphs with square-root spectral discrepancy

Under the same SAT assumption there are subsets $H'\subset H$, $D'\subset D$ of size $\Omega(N)$ each, such that **in every block**
$$
\big\|(1_{H'}-1_{D'})_b\big\|_{\mathrm{op}}=O(\sqrt J).
$$
The set $H$ may be selected after seeing all programs. Thus failure of this subgraph property on some fixed-density section at infinitely many lengths is another sufficient condition for the target lower bound.

Write each hinge as $\max_{0\le w_x\le1}w_x(1-Y_xS_x)$. Finite-dimensional bilinear minimax over the compact real nuclear-norm balls and the weight box, followed by nuclear/spectral duality in each block, gives weights supported on $H\cup D$ with
$$
\sum_x w_x-aJ^{3/2}\sum_b\|(wY)_b\|_{\mathrm{op}}\ge cN.
$$
In particular the weight is at least $cN$, while the average block norm is at most $\sqrt J/a$. Drop blocks with norm above $B\sqrt J$ for a sufficiently large constant $B$. They account for at most $N/(aB)$ weight, as no cell has weight above one.

In each surviving block independently include cells with probabilities $w_x$. The difference from its expected signed matrix has operator norm $O(\sqrt J)$ with probability at least $3/4$. For fixed unit vectors $u,v$ apply Hoeffding to the signed bilinear form. The squared widths of its summands sum to at most $\sum_{ij}u_i^2v_j^2=1$. Apply the tail at a sufficiently large constant times $\sqrt J$ to $1/4$-nets of sizes at most $9^J$ on the two unit spheres, and use the net approximation to bound the operator norm. The edge count falls below its mean minus $O(J)$ with probability below $1/4$ by the same inequality. Choose a simultaneous success separately in each block. This loses $O(N/J)$ edges overall. The graph has total size $\Omega(N)$. Against the two all-ones vectors, each block's signed sum has absolute value $O(J^{3/2})$. Their total imbalance is $O(N/\sqrt J)$, so both signs have $\Omega(N)$ edges.

The resulting graphs have positive density. More generally a signed array of total absolute mass one supported on $s$ cells has Frobenius norm at least $1/\sqrt s$, whereas a block spectral cap $C\sqrt J/N$ gives global Frobenius norm at most $C/\sqrt N$. Hence this normalized discrepancy cannot be witnessed on $o(N)$ cells. I found no choice of $H$ where the actual late outputs violate the subgraph property, and no construction of the real score or modular factors for the full interpreter. The spectral and modular criteria remain conditional. Priority of this application is unestablished.
