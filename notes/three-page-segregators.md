# A three-page directed graph that resists small segregators

For every sufficiently large $N$ there is an ordered acyclic graph on $N$ vertices with all successive spine edges. Its **entire** edge set splits into three noncrossing matchings. There are absolute constants $a,b>0$ such that after any deletion of at most $aN/\log N$ vertices, at least $bN$ surviving vertices each still have at least $bN$ distinct strict ancestors. In particular Santhanam's $(o(N),o(N/\log N))$ segregator hypothesis fails already on three pages. On two pages planar fragmentation gives that segregator property. All directions in the construction go forward in spine order.

Wittcop's November 2026 manuscript proves a many-page version and leaves the smaller page counts starting at three outside its construction. Galil, Kannan and Szemerédi constructed three-page graphs with large undirected separators. Dujmović, Sidiropoulos and Wood constructed three-page bipartite expanders. The direction and topological order of the routes matter for an ancestor bound. We found no prior proof of this exact three-page ancestor statement in the sources reviewed. Historical priority remains unverified. [Santhanam, Theorem 2.7](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2001/022/) · [Wittcop, §5](https://www.academia.edu/181369785/Pushdown_Graphs_Without_Small_Segregators) · [Galil, Kannan and Szemerédi](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02122679) · [Dujmović, Sidiropoulos and Wood](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.05020) · [Paul, Pippenger, Szemerédi and Trotter](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)

Three-page butterfly and Beneš book embeddings go back to Games. Pavan, Tewari and Vinodchandran use alternating identity pages and a third page to route directed reachability. The construction below batches an entire monotone matching on a single interface, so the number of vertices grows by a constant factor through the layout and port replacements. This preserves the congestion estimate at the deletion scale in question. [Games](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01840445) · [Pavan, Tewari and Vinodchandran, Theorem 6.2](https://cse.unl.edu/~vinod/papers/ptv_cc12.pdf)

## A butterfly with many possible start and finish layers

Fix $h\ge1$ and write $w=2^h$. Use $3h+1$ macro layers of width $w$, labeled by $h$-bit words. The first $h$ transitions and the last $h$ transitions preserve the label. At the $i$-th middle transition, $0\le i<h$, either preserve the label or flip bit $i$. Choose any start layer among $0,\ldots,h-1$ and finish layer among $2h+1,\ldots,3h$. For each of the $w^2$ source-target label pairs $(x,y)$ choose the forward path that sets each bit to its target value on that bit's designated transition.

For a fixed pair of layers, precisely $w$ of these paths pass through a specified label at any intervening macro layer. At a middle layer a fixed subset of coordinates comes from $y$ and all remaining coordinates come from $x$. Prescribing this word fixes exactly $h$ of the $2h$ bits independently. The same count applies in the identity sections. The extra identity layers ensure that the available starts and finishes each constitute a constant fraction of the final vertex set.

## Routing every stage on three pages

Replace each macro transition by four successive interfaces of width $w$, identifying end layers of adjacent transitions. In spine order list the micro layers in temporal order, arranging labels in increasing numeric order in even layers and decreasing order in odd layers.

* Put the entire identity matching on every interface, alternating pages 1 and 2 by interface parity.
* For middle transition $i$, put the flip edges with source bit $i$ equal to zero on page 3 at its first interface. Put the flip edges with source bit $i$ equal to one on page 3 at its third interface. All other interfaces carry no page-3 edges.

On the first restricted domain the flip is addition of $2^i$ and on the second it is subtraction of $2^i$. Both are increasing partial injections. Such edges nest on a single interface because its two label orders are opposite. Different interfaces on the same page have disjoint pairs of layers. Hence each page is a noncrossing matching. To implement a macro edge, follow the old label to its designated flip, then follow the new one. A path preserving its label uses just identities.

For each fixed choice of start and finish layers, a micro vertex with label $z$ is used by at most $2w$ prescribed paths. If it lies strictly inside a macro transition, the path has $z$ at the start or the end of that transition. Each condition holds for exactly $w$ pairs, and their union has size at most $2w$. At a macro boundary the count is $w$. These bounds include paths incident to the selected endpoints.

## Putting the entire spine on the same three pages

The preceding pages carry at most three edge incidences at each micro vertex, one of each color. Replace each micro vertex by a consecutive block of twelve positions. Use up to four slots $1,4,7,10$ (numbered from zero), assigning all incoming chord endpoints first, then a representative, then all outgoing chord endpoints. Preserve the order of blocks and retain each chord's color. Its page is still a noncrossing matching because the old page had at most one incident chord per block.

Add all consecutive edges on the full new spine. At each chord endpoint assign its incoming and outgoing spine edges the other two colors in a fixed order. A chord endpoint is internal to its block. Consecutive such endpoints on the spine are at distance at least three. In the gap between them, the first and last edge colors are already fixed. Fill the other edges with adjacent colors distinct. With one edge left to assign, choose the color different from its two neighbors. At unconstrained ends, alternate any two allowed colors as needed. Spine edges cannot cross a chord since their endpoints are consecutive. Within a page no spine edge touches a chord or another spine edge. This proves the matching condition even under the convention that all spine edges must be drawn on the three pages.

Lift every prescribed path to the new graph by following the internal spine from its incoming chord endpoint to its representative and then to the outgoing chord endpoint. At the physical start and finish, take just the outgoing or incoming part. A given position in a block is used only by paths that used its old micro vertex, so the $2w$ congestion bound holds on the final physical graph. Its order is $N=12w(12h+1)$.

For each of the $h^2$ choices of endpoint layers, deleting at most $w/4$ physical vertices destroys at most $(w/4)2w=w^2/2$ of the prescribed paths. Surviving paths have distinct ordered endpoints, including across the choices of layers. Thus at least $(hw)^2/2$ strict ancestor pairs survive between the $hw$ start and $hw$ finish representatives. At least $hw/3$ finishes have at least $hw/4$ such ancestors, by averaging using the upper bound $hw$ for any one finish. Now $hw=\Theta(N)$ and $w=\Theta(N/\log N)$. All maps, layouts and certificates of reachability are explicit. This proof uses only bit fixing and counting. For sizes between these values, take the largest construction that fits and append a directed spine tail. The last original vertex has no chord. Extend the spine coloring by alternating two colors, starting with one different from that of its last edge. Consecutive construction sizes have ratio bounded by three for large $h$, so the two asymptotic guarantees survive with absolute constant adjustments.

## Boundary and verification

For these particular graphs and every fixed sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, the minimum size of an $\varepsilon N$-segregator has order $\Theta_\varepsilon(N/\log N)$. For the upper bound delete complete micro layers at evenly spaced positions, using $O(1/\varepsilon)$ such layers. All chords connect adjacent micro layers and the spine passes through each deleted layer. An appended spine tail can be cut at the same spacing with another $O(1/\varepsilon)$ deletions. Each surviving piece thus has at most $\varepsilon N$ vertices for large $w$. For the lower bound take $\varepsilon<b$. For any two-page graph, drawing the pages above and below the spine gives planarity. Recursive planar separators reduce components to size $m$ with $O(N/\sqrt m)$ deletions. Taking $m=\lceil N/\log\log N\rceil$ yields the requested $o(N)$ ancestor bound with $o(N/\log N)$ deletions. [Lipton and Tarjan](https://doi.org/10.1137/0136016)

There is also a fixed deterministic three-stack transducer whose actual push-to-pop edges realize this example. Supply one finite-alphabet command per physical vertex. At the earlier endpoint of a nonspine chord, push an input payload bit onto the stack of that chord's color. At its later endpoint pop and output the bit. Every physical vertex is the endpoint of at most one nonspine chord. On each stack its chord intervals form a noncrossing matching, so induction on the completed intervals gives the correct last-in-first-out pairing. Idle positions carry a no-op command. With controller successor edges included the event graph of this transducer on these streams is exactly the constructed graph. This is an existence result about a particular execution. An alternative machine for the same language or output task can have different dependencies.
