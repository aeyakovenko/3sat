# A single variable delay and one binary product give an exponentially hard forecast

There exist positive constants $P,k$, an integer $s$, two fixed negative seed positions, and two disjoint constant-cardinality sets of positive delays $L(W),R$ with the following property. The input is one integer $W$ in ordinary binary, of length $n$. All delays are fixed integers except **one** member of $L(W)$ of the form $PkW+c$ for a fixed integer $c$. Fix any harmless convention on finitely many small $W$. Set $z_t=1$ at the two negative seeds, zero at every other negative time, and for $t\ge0$ use the autonomous update over $\mathbb F_2$
$$
 z_t=\left(\bigoplus_{a\in L(W)} z_{t-a}\right)
     \left(\bigoplus_{b\in R} z_{t-b}\right).
$$
There is no clock input. The language $z_{Pk W^5+s}=1$ is in deterministic tape time $2^{O(n)}$ and is EXPTIME-complete under polynomial-time many-one reductions. Its fixed simulated machine includes the simulation, diagonal, counter and calibration modes described here and in the linked checks of proof techniques. For **every** correct deterministic logarithmic-word RAM program $P_0$, every sufficiently long width beginning with a particular fixed prefix then requires $\Omega_{P_0}(W^4/n^{c_{P_0}})$ steps for a constant $c_{P_0}$, with every available work address allowed. A separate linear-length hierarchy reduction gives an exponential lower bound without using the narrow width of the predicting RAM. Two initial ones are minimal for any nonzero future under this update with disjoint banks. With at most one initial one, before a first positive output a single historical position would have to be read through both banks at the same instant.

If the last adjustable delay is fixed as well, the shift register has a fixed finite state space and the stream is eventually periodic. A fixed program can evaluate its bit at a fixed polynomial in $W$ in $O(n)$ steps by reducing $W$ modulo the fixed eventual period and treating finitely many early widths separately.

If either parity bank contains a single tap, sparse initialization permits an exact polynomial-time endpoint algorithm even for arbitrary lags in binary. In this fixed one-parameter family it uses $O(n)$ bit operations. [Singleton-bank proof and audit](singleton-bank-feedback-prediction.md)

The theorem is about predicting the specified bit. The last section gives the precise additional statement needed about ordinary SAT. Universal computation through local updates and quantitative hardness via cellular automata have precedents, particularly Gurvich and Vyalyi's fixed higher-dimensional difference game. Prediction hardness of networks of elementary operations is also well studied. Kutrib and Malcher also study the connection between length-preserving queues and cellular automata. Here the restrictions are a one-dimensional time stream, a homogeneous product of two parities, disjoint tap sets, two fixed seeds, and only one binary-parameter delay. Priority for this combination is unestablished. ## Compiling a fixed controller to one product

Use blocks of $P$ ticks; a computational slot is a distinct residue $s_i$ in each block. Choose a set of residues for which congruent sums of three marks imply equality of the two multisets, allowing repeated marks. Here is one explicit choice for $m$ slots in a given topological order. Take a prime $q>\max(m,3)$ of order $O(m+1)$, $B=3q$, and
$$
 s_i=B^2 i+B(i^2\bmod q)+(i^3\bmod q),\quad 1\le i\le m.
$$
Set $P>3\max_i s_i$. Congruence of triple sums is ordinary equality, and base-$B$ addition has no carry. The three power sums modulo $q$ determine the three elementary symmetric functions by Newton's identities and hence the multiset of roots. This also gives uniqueness of two-sums by adding a common mark. The controller is fixed, so $P$ is constant.

Give signals separate copies for left-bank and right-bank uses. An edge from source slot $i$ to destination $j$ contributes its positive physical delay to the designated bank; its residue is $s_j-s_i$. Use distinct source and destination slots even for a logical signal that returns next period. Several designated inputs in one bank implement XOR. If needed, a copy gate can use a known one in the other bank.

Suppose an earlier one at slot $h$ arrives through a left edge $i\to j$, and another at $h'$ through a right edge $k'\to l$ at the same time. The triple condition gives
$$
              \{h,j,k'\}=\{h',l,i\}\quad\text{as multisets}.
$$
Here $i\ne k'$ by source coloring and $i\ne j$, $k'\ne l$ by absence of self-edges. Thus $h=i$, $h'=k'$ and $j=l$. Off all computational slots at least one parity is zero. At a computational slot, the two-sum condition likewise implies that every arrival comes from its designated source through an edge to that destination. This preserves each bank's intended XOR even when a destination asks for the same signal from two different blocks. Equal tap values in different banks would require the same ordered pair of slots, which their colors forbid. Induction starts with historical ones only at designated residues.

Seed only the two copies of signal $K_1$ in block $-1$. In every nonnegative block produce both copies of $K_0$ from the product of the preceding block's $K_1$ copies. Produce both copies of $K_1$ from the now-available $K_0$ copies. All four signals are consequently one forever at their slots. Ordinary AND is one product; NOT of $x$ is $(K_0+x)K_1$ with appropriate copies. Distinct gate and buffer slots remove self-edges. A fixed synchronous finite-state controller with an all-zero initial-state encoding thus costs a fixed number of slots. Read any state bit from the preceding block's stored result and evaluate the current combinational gates in topological order. A finite-state machine can give special outputs during startup via that initial state.

The controller can read a single bit $o_{u-D}$ from its output in a block $D$ blocks earlier, using **one long tap**. Gate a single left-colored copy of the old output against a local one at a dedicated import slot. Produce further left and right copies from this imported bit using only bounded local taps. Every other edge is in the present or immediately preceding block. The physical delay of the import is $PD+s_{\rm in}-s_{\rm out}$; older outputs are zero before the run. The two nonzero negative seeds are private to $K_1$ and are distinct from the output slot.

## Reconstructing a tape from a one-bit delay line

Take a fixed nearest-neighbor rule $f$ on a finite alphabet, with blank encoded by the all-zero $k$-bit symbol and a permanent wall. A one-tape machine on an interval can be implemented by such a rule: a cell records its tape tracks and an optional head state, and a moving head changes the adjacent cell on the next update. A head can detect an adjacent wall, with an extra bounded number of steps if necessary; the wall itself stays unchanged. The local rule preserves a headless blank next to a wall, in particular $f(0,0,\mathrm{wall})=0$. The machine starts on a blank cell immediately to the right of the wall with a distinguished head state. Its verdict track at cell 1 starts at zero. In each diagonal mode only the final, stationary complemented-verdict write can change that track.

The finite controller sends one bit per block into the delay line and receives the bit from $D=k(W-2)$ blocks earlier. Group $k$ consecutive bits as symbols. While sending a symbol previously prepared, buffer the incoming symbol and the two before it. At the end of the group, compute the next symbol. Override the first two outputs to be $O_0=\mathrm{wall}$ and $O_1=\mathrm{head}$. The input at symbol index $j$ is $I_j=O_{j-(W-2)}$, with zero symbols before startup. Thereafter the exact recurrence is
$$
 O_j=f(I_{j-3},I_{j-2},I_{j-1})
     =f(O_{j-W-1},O_{j-W},O_{j-W+1}),\qquad j\ge2.
$$
Buffer sizes, the phase modulo $k$, the initial overrides and the local rule are fixed and independent of $W$. The one physical growing tap is $PkW+c$ as asserted.

Read $O_{rW+i}$ as cell $i$ of row $r$, $0\le i<W$. The first row has the one wall, the head at cell 1, and blanks elsewhere. For its final cell the only nonblank ghost neighbor can be $O_0=\mathrm{wall}$, which leaves a blank there. For subsequent rows an interior cell sees the three preceding-row cells. At column zero its center is the old immutable wall, so even an incorrect left neighbor leaves a wall. At column $W-1$ the right neighbor is column zero of the **current** row, equal to the preceding row's wall. Induction therefore verifies every cell of every row. The interval between successive encounters with the wall has $W-1$ usable tape cells. When finished, the machine latches its verdict on a track at cell 1. If $\beta$ is the output bit of that track, in row $W^4$ its physical observation time is $P(k(W^5+1)+\beta)+s_{\rm out}=PkW^5+s$.

## Putting a hard language into the single input integer

Choose one fixed finite-interval machine. On an initially blank interval it measures the length $W$, computes its ordinary binary representation, and parses a self-delimiting program and word from its high bits, ignoring padding. This needs $O(W^2)$ steps on a single tape with a fixed number of tracks. For instance traverse all usable cells, marking each in turn while incrementing a binary counter near the left end; add the missing wall cell to the count. A walk to the wall and back costs $O(W)$ per mark. There is room on extra tracks to preserve marks and the counter. In its ordinary simulation mode the machine then simulates the parsed program on the parsed word, returns to cell 1 and latches the verdict. On valid padded reductions the simulation fits in the interval and the time budget. No condition on invalid programs is needed for predicting at the specified finite time.

For any $L\in\mathrm{EXPTIME}$ fix a tape decider with time $2^{p(m)}$ on $m$ input bits, increasing $p$ to cover a fixed polynomial simulation overhead if needed. Given $x$, form the binary expansion of $W$ as a leading one, self-delimiting descriptions of that fixed program and $x$, and sufficiently many trailing zero bits. For example encode a string by its length in unary, a delimiter and its bits. Use $O(p(m)+m+1)$ bits in total, with padding large enough that the universal simulation after decoding uses fewer than $W/2$ tape cells and fewer than $W^2$ steps. Measuring the interval dominates startup, and the full run fits in $W^4$ rows for large $W$. Small source lengths can be padded individually. The reduction writes only the binary integer and takes polynomial time. Direct tape evaluation of at most $O(W^5)$ scalar updates, even with a scan of all recorded bits for each, costs $2^{O(n)}$ on arbitrary $n$-bit $W$.

For the quantitative bound choose a single deterministic tape-time hierarchy language in $\mathrm{DTIME}(2^m)$ outside $\mathrm{DTIME}(2^{m/2})$. The simulation overhead can be absorbed in $p(m)=O(m)$; the produced binary input has length at most $C m$ for a fixed $C$. A deterministic word RAM using $t$ instructions on the resulting file is tape-simulable in $O((t+n)^2\operatorname{polylog}(t+n+2))$ time by storing and searching the list of memory accesses (logarithmic-word arithmetic has polynomial-log bit cost). Thus even a $2^{\epsilon n}$ RAM clock with $2\epsilon C<1/2$ would contradict the chosen hierarchy. This proves a lower bound on a problem with a fixed physical rule and one free integer; EXPTIME-completeness alone would not give its dependence on encoded length. The exponential upper bound was on tapes; it uses addresses beyond an input-log-word RAM's fixed-width range.

## A prefix of slow inputs for each actual prediction program

Use a separate prefix mode in the fixed interval machine above. It recovers the entire binary input $w=\operatorname{bin}(W)$, reads a self-delimiting deterministic RAM program and width convention from a header, and runs that program on **all of $w$**, including the header and the arbitrary remaining padding. If the program halts, latch the complement of its verdict at cell 1. The forecast uses the register at its fixed observation time even when the simulation has yet to halt.

Fix a standard logarithmic-word program $P_0$ correct for this forecast, and use its own header. Its width is at most $C_{P_0}\log(n+2)$ with its actual rounding rule. The full native address space has $n^{O_{P_0}(1)}$ words. A universal interval machine can represent the guest bank and registers in $n^{O_{P_0}(1)}$ cells near the left wall and simulate each word instruction there in $n^{O_{P_0}(1)}$ tape steps. Setup including measuring $W$ takes $O(W^2)$ steps; returning to cell 1 after guest halt takes at most $W$ more. If the guest halted faster than $c W^4/n^{c_{P_0}}$ for suitably chosen constants, the opposite verdict would already be latched in row $W^4$. Correctness rules this out. The same bound holds on every sufficiently large width with that prefix, which occupies a fixed positive fraction at every large bit length.

A second mode handles simulations with wider words but few elapsed instructions. Interpret the header as a program using widths up to a specified constant times $n$ bits; length-dependent widths and the two successive ISA phases of a compiler and a SAT solver can be computed from their input lengths. Keep a list of address/value pairs for touched words only. After $t$ steps this needs $O(tn+\operatorname{poly}(n))$ cells (two $O(n)$-bit words per record, plus fixed scratch tracks for arithmetic and decoding) and $O((n+t)^2\operatorname{poly}(n))$ tape time. The fixed exponent on the polynomial may depend on the guest interpreter convention, and wall overrun may cause the mode to abandon the guest. A guest that halts within $c W/n$ instructions for sufficiently small guest-dependent $c>0$ fits inside $W$ cells and latches well before the observation for all large widths. Thus every correct wider-word guest takes $\Omega(W/n)$ on every sufficiently large width with its own prefix. The stronger $W^4/n^{O(1)}$ estimate above uses the narrower address space. We have not established the existence of a correct input-logarithmic-width RAM for the unpadded language. A width of $O(n)$ bits suffices for a direct algorithm using a ring buffer of $O(W)$ words and $O(W^5)$ steps.

## A polynomial-time language on the standard logarithmic-word RAM

For each fixed integer $q>1$ there is a total language with a deterministic $O(M^{5q})$-time logarithmic-word RAM decider on $M$ input bits. Every correct such decider takes $\Omega_P(M^q/\log M)$ steps on infinitely many valid inputs, with unrestricted workspace. For $q=4$ this gives an explicit quartic-over-logarithmic lower bound for predicting the same recurrence. Both the upper and lower bounds now use the same input-length word convention.

For $n=|\operatorname{bin} W|$ put $M=2^{\lceil n/q\rceil}$ and encode
$$
       x_W=1^n 0\,\operatorname{bin}(W)\,0^{M-2n-1}.
$$
Accept exactly the strings of this form with forecast bit one. Omit the finitely many $n$ for which the padding would be negative. A decider checks the total length, leading one of the binary integer and zero tail in $O(M)$ steps. On a valid file $2^{-q}M^q\le W<M^q$. A ring buffer of $O(W)$ words and an $O(\log M)$-bit tick counter suffice to reach the observation time in $O(W^5)$ steps. All fixed-width constants, including the one needed to represent this time, may depend on $q$ and the fixed recurrence.

For the lower bound, fix a correct decider $P$ of the padded language. A wider-word program $Q$ on $\operatorname{bin}(W)$ constructs the valid padded file in $O(M)$ instructions and simulates $P(x_W)$, including its native word width, in $O(1)$ wider-word instructions per step. A fixed number of separate memory regions and masks suffices. Each program's width is at most a constant times $n$, since $\log M=\Theta_q(n)$. Hard-wire $P$ and $q$ in $Q$ and handle the finite omitted widths directly so that $Q$ is a correct forecast program on every width. For every sufficiently long $W$ with $Q$'s own wider-word simulation prefix, the preceding diagonal gives $O_P(M+T_P(x_W))\ge c_Q W/n$. Since $q>1$, $M=o(W/n)$. This proves the claimed bound. In particular $P$ has genuine slow inputs at every sufficiently large supported length $M$.

For $q=4$, a uniform compiler for this total language with worst-case work $o(M^4/\log M)$ and output length $o((M^4/\log M)^{1/k_0})$ would exclude $O(N^{k_0})$ SAT for $k_0=1,2,3$. Here $N$ counts encoded SAT bits. A standard RAM can run the compiler and the hypothetical solver with sufficiently large fixed word-width coefficient, since the reduction's output length and the proposed SAT clock are polynomial in $M$. The direct forecast decider establishes membership in P. This argument provides a reduction budget; constructing a decision-preserving compiler within it is open.

There is also a tally restriction on which all correct programs of bounded description length can be forced to be slow on one common all-zero word. A single nonuniform advice bit per length decides its verdict on that promise. On the full block of $2^r$ widths sharing all but their final $r$ bits, exactly $2^r$ advice bits per prefix are needed for uniformly fast prediction when $r$ is fixed. ## What a SAT compiler would have to achieve

Let $V(W)$ be this single forecast bit. Suppose a uniform deterministic procedure produces ordinary explicit SAT instances with verdict $V(W)$ in time $2^{(\gamma+o(1))n}$, with encoded bit length at most $2^{(\eta+o(1))n}$. If $\max(\gamma,k_0\eta)<\epsilon$ for $k_0=1,2$ or $3$, then SAT has no deterministic $O(N^{k_0})$ RAM solver. Here the compiler and solver must compose within the claimed time. For example a standard tape-output compiler or a RAM with a larger logarithmic address coefficient for its output-size parameter can be simulated on a tape in quadratic time; in using such compilers take $\epsilon$ with slack already for that simulation relative to the tape hierarchy. More explicitly the composition has tape time $2^{(2\max(\gamma,k_0\eta)+o(1))n}$, and $\epsilon$ may be chosen with $2\epsilon C<1/2$. This compares elapsed work across models without assuming the original RAM could address an exponentially long output tape. A $2^{o(n)}$-time and $2^{o(n)}$-length compiler would exclude every fixed polynomial SAT clock.

The wide-word mode gives an explicit sufficient target. For $k_0\in\{1,2,3\}$, a uniform compiler with work $o(W/n)$ and output length $o((W/n)^{1/k_0})$ would rule out $O(N^{k_0})$ deterministic SAT. Compose it with a putative SAT solver as a wide-word guest. Its total instructions are $o(W/n)$; its word widths on both inputs fit $O(\log W)$. This uses only the bound on time and the number of words a guest can touch in that time. In particular a SAT solver can use its entire ordinary workspace. Charging a SAT solver on expanded files as if it still had only $O(\log n)$ word width would give an unjustified stronger threshold.

A narrower sufficient lemma can be posed on a polynomial-time stratum. Fix a deterministic multitape language in $\mathrm{DTIME}(m^{2k_0+1})\setminus\mathrm{DTIME}(m^{2k_0+1/2})$. Use the ordinary simulation mode, tag its fixed decider, encode the $m$ input bits and add only a linear number of padding bits. After the width has been decoded, the simulated source computation on this stratum takes polynomial time in $m$. If this stratum alone had a compiler using $m^{k_0+o(1)}$ RAM work and $m^{1+o(1)}$ output bits, the putative SAT solver and compiler together would give $m^{2k_0+o(1)}$ tape time for the excluded language. This criterion does not ask for a short encoding of every exponential-time computation.

There is a necessary lower bound even on a proposed compiler's **encoded size**. An ordinary ASCII DIMACS file of length $N$ with $v$ occurring names has $v=O(N/\log(N+2)+1)$ since the $v$ distinct decimal names themselves take $\Omega(v\log(v+1))$ bits. The header can declare more unused names. Enumerating only the occurring variables takes $2^v\operatorname{poly}(N)$ tape time. Hence a $2^{o(n)}$-time correct compiler cannot have $N=o(n\log n)$ on all widths; some constant multiple of $n\log n$ is necessary infinitely often. For a polynomial-time compiler, its brute-force decider itself has ordinary logarithmic width on $n$: store the polynomial-size file, occurrence map and assignment counter in polynomially many cells. On this decider's fixed diagonal prefix the stronger native bound forces $v\ge4\log_2 W-O(\log n)$ at every sufficiently large width there. In particular every such compiler has an entire prefix with $\Omega(n\log n)$ output bits. This is a lower bound for this proposed reduction's outputs. It supplies no lower clock on a SAT program that receives such an output.

Direct circuit unrolling gives a unique trajectory and a formula with $O(W^5)$ sets of constant-size update clauses, each with $O(\log W)$-bit names. The resulting exponent is too large. The stream also shows where linear fast-forwarding first loses its justification. If the update is instead any fixed XOR of the taps, the generating series of the stream is $H(X)/Q(X)$ with $Q=1+\sum_a X^a$ and $H$ the finitely many contributions from the fixed historical ones. To extract degree $t$, use $1/Q=\prod_{i<b} Q(X^{2^i})\pmod{X^{2^b}}$ for $2^b>t$. Pick one monomial per factor and one term in $H$. A guessed choice contributes iff their weighted degrees sum to $t$. There are $O(\log W)$ choices of constant-bit size when $t=PkW^5+s$. Group equal taps by bit masks and check the degree sum using a constant number of $O(\log W)$-bit integer multiplications and additions. This gives a uniform near-linear **parity** network for the affine version in matching characteristic. For the quadratic recurrence the product of two past parities is pointwise in time, so the ordinary rational-series identity does not apply. The [compressed-history verifier](grammar-certificates-for-a-single-delayed-product.md) implements an existential CNF route for short grammar-compressed tape histories. A fixed accepting mode needs $\Omega(W^4)$ grammar productions. Another fixed mode forces $\Omega(W^4)$ linear syndrome bits even for checking the final verdict alone.
