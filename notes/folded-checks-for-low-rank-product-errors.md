# The cost of folded polynomial checks for low-rank matrix-product errors

Let $A,B,C$ be binary $d$-square matrices and let $E=AB+C$ over $\mathbb F_2$. For $r=\lceil d^\rho\rceil$, $0<\rho\le1$, there is a deterministic nonuniform Boolean circuit that accepts every correct product and rejects every product with $1\le\operatorname{rank} E\le r$. Its gate count, using the November 24 rectangular multiplication exponent, is

$$d^{2+\rho^2/4+o(1)}.$$

For example, rank at most $\sqrt d$ costs $d^{33/16+o(1)}$ gates. No condition is imposed on the number, positions, or density of the erroneous entries. The circuit computes the classical Forbes–Shpilka checks on a geometric grid. For the **whole vector** of these checks on $AB$, the exponent $2+\rho^2/4$ is also a formal bilinear lower bound. The one-bit decision has only the upper bound here. The distinction applies even when the detector is presented as a binary-wire tensor network. [Forbes and Shpilka, *On Identity Testing of Tensors, Low-Rank Recovery and Compressed Sensing*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0663) · [Rectangular exponent statement and scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/lean/docs/107.md)

## Hitting every low-rank error

Choose $K=\mathbb F_{2^s}$ with $4d<2^s\le 8d$ and a primitive element $\gamma$. Write $P_E(X,Y)=\sum_{u,v=0}^{d-1}E_{uv}X^u Y^v$. Test the values

$$P_E(\gamma^i,\gamma^{i+t}),\qquad 0\le i\le2d-2,\quad 0\le t<r.$$

For a nonzero error, let $k$ be the least $u+v$ of a nonzero entry. Write its nonzero positions on this antidiagonal as $(u_m,v_m)$ with increasing $u_m$. In the submatrix on these rows and these decreasing columns, entries above the diagonal have $u_m+v_n<k$ and vanish. Its diagonal is nonzero, so the number $h$ of those positions is at most $\operatorname{rank}E$. The coefficient of $X^k$ in $P_E(X,\gamma^tX)$ equals $\sum_m E_{u_mv_m}(\gamma^{v_m})^t$. If $h\le r$, the first $h$ of these coefficients cannot all vanish because the $\gamma^{v_m}$ are distinct and their Vandermonde matrix is invertible. The resulting nonzero univariate polynomial has degree at most $2d-2$ and is tested at $2d-1$ distinct points. Correct products give zero at every point. This also proves the hitting claim over $K$ for errors of rank at most $r$ over $K$. [Forbes–Shpilka](https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~shpilka/publications/ForbesShpilka11.pdf)

## The gate bound on physical binary data

Put $V_{iu}=\gamma^{iu}$ for $0\le i\le 3d-3$ and $0\le u<d$ (more rows may be padded). Each test compares the $(i,i+t)$ entry of $(VA)(BV^\mathsf T)$ with the corresponding entry of $VCV^\mathsf T$. All three transforms can be computed together in $d^2\operatorname{polylog} d$ bit gates. For one vector $c$, its values $\sum_u c_u\gamma^{iu}$ at all requested indices follow from one degree-$O(d)$ convolution. Use $iu=\binom{i+u}2-\binom i2-\binom u2$, reverse the sequence $c_u\gamma^{-\binom u2}$, convolve it with the precomputed sequence $\gamma^{\binom n2}$, and scale the outputs by $\gamma^{-\binom i2}$. Run the transform on $O(d)$ rows and columns. Cantor and Kaltofen give quasilinear arithmetic cost for each convolution. One addition or multiplication in $K$ takes $O(s^2)$ binary gates by polynomial-basis arithmetic with a fixed irreducible modulus of degree $s=O(\log d)$. The circuit can hardwire its field constants. [Cantor and Kaltofen, *On fast multiplication of polynomials over arbitrary algebras*](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/91/CaKa91.pdf)

Partition $i=0,\dots,2d-2$ into consecutive groups of $r$. The columns $i+t$ needed by any group lie in an interval of length at most $2r-1$. Each group therefore needs one product of shape $(r,d,2r)$ after padding. Use two square-ended rectangles of shape $(r,d,r)$ per group. A formal rectangular algorithm over the fixed field $\mathbb F_2$ lifts with its same constants to $K$, so the family of growing fields introduces only the bit cost per operation above. There are $O(d/r)$ groups. For any available rectangular upper bound the total exponent is at most $1-\rho+\omega_{\mathbb F_2}(\rho,1,\rho)$, with the quadratic preprocessing included. The November 24 formula gives $\omega(\rho,1,\rho)=1+\rho+\rho^2/4$. Constant fan-in Boolean gates and the corresponding copy trees have the same size exponent, since total gate fan-in bounds the number of used wires. The conclusion also applies on the compact bit projection into DIMACS SAT by extracting the designated bits and complementing the equality verdict. [Rectangular exponent](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/lean/docs/107.md) · [Native bit projection](compact-bit-projection-of-matrix-mismatch.md)

## The formal cost of materializing all the checks

Take $C=0$ and retain all output values. Work over an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_2$. The first $d$ rows of $V$ form an invertible Vandermonde matrix. Consequently the first $d$ transformed rows of $A$ and first $d$ transformed columns of $B$ are free independent input coordinates after separate invertible linear changes.

Let $b=\lfloor r/2\rfloor$, for sufficiently large $d$ that $r\ge2$. Split the first $d$ transformed indices into $k=\lfloor d/(2b)\rfloor$ disjoint consecutive blocks of size $2b$, discarding the remainder. In each block take the first $b$ rows and last $b$ columns. Every such $(i,j)$ has $1\le j-i\le 2b-1< r$, so its inner product is among the requested checks. Different blocks use disjoint rows of the first factor and disjoint columns of the second factor. Projecting to these outputs and setting unused input coordinates to zero gives $k$ independent copies of the matrix multiplication tensor $T=\langle b,d,b\rangle$.

Here is the factor $k$ without an additivity assumption on tensor rank. Write $a=\widetilde R(T)>1$ for asymptotic tensor rank and $S=\bigoplus_{h=1}^k T$. For fixed $d$ and $k>1$, choose $m=\lfloor n\log k/\log(a+\epsilon)\rfloor$. For all large $n$, $R(T^{\otimes m})\le (a+\epsilon)^m\le k^n$. Thus $T^{\otimes (m+n)}$ is a linear restriction of $k^n$ copies of $T^{\otimes n}$, which is $S^{\otimes n}$. Taking asymptotic ranks, then $n$th roots and limits, gives $\widetilde R(S)\ge ka$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. The $k=1$ case is immediate. Over the algebraic closure asymptotic rank is at most border rank, by the usual interpolation of a degeneration after tensor powering. [Bini, *Relations between exact and approximate bilinear algorithms*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02575865)

For a single fixed shape $(b,d,b)$, powering is ordinary matrix multiplication on dimensions $(b^n,d^n,b^n)$. Blocking and padding therefore give $a\ge d^{\omega(\log_d b,1,\log_d b)}$. Explicitly, a rank algorithm on one fixed large tensor power is a recursion on blocks, with only fixed-size linear maps at each node. Its arithmetic exponent is bounded by the log-rank exponent, up to a vanishing recursion slack. Interpolation and integer rounding between successive powers do not affect that exponent. Now $b=d^{\rho+o(1)}$, $k=d^{1-\rho+o(1)}$. The sample vector has border rank at least $d^{2+\rho^2/4-o(1)}$. The batched algorithm gives the reverse exponent, since taking the mixed bidegree of a formal circuit uses at most two new products of a left and a right linear form per multiplication gate. Both bounds also hold at the exponent level for the formal bit-coordinate map over $\mathbb F_2$. For the lower bound, extend the scalars of that map and recombine each group of $s$ binary output coordinates with the fixed basis of $K$. This recovers exactly the $K$-valued sample polynomials on formally independent factor variables.

A Boolean circuit can decide whether all checks vanish without producing any of these polynomials. Signed tensor networks have additional freedom to sum over internal bits. In fact the earlier inclusion–exclusion construction already gives such networks of volume $d^{2+o(1)}$ for $r\le\sqrt d$, over every coefficient field. The folded construction yields a deterministic Boolean circuit on those same physical bits. [Earlier signed-network construction](residual-lines-and-the-ranks-needed-for-matrix-verification.md)

The lower bound therefore supplies a cost for this particular approach to turning a decision algorithm into an algebraic matrix product. It leaves the full bit-network and unrestricted RAM lower bounds open. Priority for this combined gate bound and formal-vector exponent is unestablished.
