# A uniform graph for the half-read SAT cut

At every sufficiently large $m$ a deterministic, uniformly constructible width-three formula on $2m$ data pins realizes the half-read reuse bound. Its length in the specified packed encoding is $N=\Theta(m\log m)$. On every input every correct solver reads all but at most **nine** private signs. For every fixed correct solver some genuine suffix, starting immediately after the $m$th distinct pin read, either rereads $\Omega(m)$ of its earlier pins or retrieves $\Omega(m/\log N)$ distinct nonzero earlier-written work words before overwriting them. If initially zero work addresses also count, this alternative holds on all but a $2^{-\Omega(m)}$ fraction of the inputs, including after conditioning on either verdict. The construction requires no random seed or expensive search for its graph. [Half-read argument and machine conventions](half-read-reuse.md)

## Large cut rank and very few unread signs in the same quadratic function

Let $l$ be the largest even integer with $l^2\le 2m$. Use the Margulis–Gabber–Galil multigraph on $n=l^2$ vertices $(i,j)\in\mathbb Z_l^2$. Its eight neighbors are $(i\pm 2j,j)$, $(i\pm(2j+1),j)$, $(i,j\pm 2i)$, and $(i,j\pm(2i+1))$, with coordinates reduced modulo $l$. Its degree-counting adjacency matrix has second eigenvalue at most $5\sqrt 2<8$. Form a simple graph $G$ by deleting loops and retaining each edge once. Add $2m-n=O(\sqrt m)$ isolated vertices. Its degree is at most eight. [Jimbo and Maruoka](https://doi.org/10.1145/22145.22155) · [Isabelle proof for these precise eight neighbor maps](https://isa-afp.org/browser_info/current/AFP/Expander_Graphs/Expander_Graphs_MGG.html)

For every partition of the $2m$ vertices into $m$ and $m$, each side contains at least $n/3$ of the core vertices for sufficiently large $m$. If one side contains $s$ core vertices, the number of multigraph arcs leaving it is at least $(8-5\sqrt 2)s(n-s)/n$. Indeed apply the adjacency eigenvalue bound to the centered indicator of this core subset and subtract the resulting internal arc count from $8s$. Removing multiplicities divides this bound by at most eight; loops did not contribute. Consequently $\Omega(m)$ distinct simple edges cross. In this crossing bipartite graph take an induced matching greedily. After selecting $uv$, discard crossing edges incident to any neighbor of $u$ or $v$ on the opposite sides. At most $2\cdot 8^2$ edges are discarded per choice. The cross adjacency submatrix on the selected pairs is an identity matrix. Hence its binary rank is $\Omega(m)$ even on the least favorable balanced partition. Matching arguments of this type have been used for ordered diagram lower bounds on graph CNFs. [Bova and Slivovsky, expander graph CNFs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6979531/)

On the data bits $x_v$ use the verdict
$$
 f(x)= \bigoplus_{u<v}x_u x_v\quad\oplus\quad
       \bigoplus_{uv\in E(G)}x_u x_v .
$$
This is the quadratic form of the **simple complement** of $G$. Its balanced cross adjacency differs from the one just analyzed by an all-ones matrix of rank one. There is a constant $\kappa>0$ such that every balanced cut has complement rank at least $\kappa m$ for large $m$. In a coordinate subcube, a free pair forming an edge of the complement retains coefficient one after arbitrary substitutions elsewhere. The Boolean quadratic cannot be constant there, as witnessed by the XOR of its four evaluations on that pair with all other bits fixed. Therefore the free vertices of any monochromatic subcube form a clique in $G$. There are at most nine such vertices, including in the padded graph. A deterministic halting transcript that leaves more than nine signs unread would give a monochromatic subcube of a prohibited dimension.

Although the complement has quadratically many edges, its value needs only $O(m)$ bounded-fan-in gates. In a sequential scan with prefix parity $p$ and accumulated pair parity $e$, process $x_i$ by $e\gets e\oplus p x_i$ and then $p\gets p\oplus x_i$. Finally XOR one product for each edge of $G$. A uniform generator emits each edge only at its smaller endpoint and removes any duplicate in the at most eight local slots. It may instead reserve eight gate templates per vertex, using fixed zero contributions in the skipped slots. Pin inputs in a separate bank, enforce all gate equivalences in width three, and force the final bit. Each satisfiable formula has a unique satisfying assignment; its encoded size is $\Theta(m\log m)$ bits. Pin spacing can use a fixed constant number of dummy clauses for the specified standard word width to ensure one native input word reveals at most one private sign. Computing the square size and the fixed neighbor slots and writing the regular layout takes $O(m)$ native word steps using ordinary word arithmetic.

## Substituting any positive rank rate in the reuse argument

Write $w_0=1+\lceil\log_2(Uv)\rceil$ for the address-and-value cost in the half-read proof. Choose $r=\lfloor\delta m\rfloor$, $k=\lfloor\eta m/w_0\rfloor$ with positive constants $\delta,\eta$ so small that $4H_2(\delta)+2\eta<\kappa/16$. This is possible for **every** fixed $\kappa>0$. With unread allowance nine, the recorded set-and-controller catalog in that proof has logarithm at most $em$ for large $m$, with $e<\kappa/8$. Indeed the two pin-list costs are bounded by $\log_2 V(2m,r)$ and $\log_2 V(2m,r+9)$, respectively, and the work-list cost is at most $\eta m+O(\log m)$. Here $V(n,j)=\sum_{i\le j}\binom ni$ with the sum restricted to $0\le i\le n$.

Some fixed read set and controller then retain at least $2^{(1-e)m}$ rows under the contradiction premise that all runs meet both caps. The sign matrix on these rows has sign rank at least $2^{(\kappa-e)m/2}$. This follows from Forster's bound and the full sign matrix's operator norm $2^{m-\operatorname{rank}_2(B)/2}$ for cross adjacency $B$. On the same rows the positive-label decision-tree representation has log rank at most $[H_2(\delta)+\eta+o(1)]m$, strictly less. This gives the worst-case disjunction for **nonzero** incoming words. In the distributional argument counting all incoming addresses, the exponent after resampling is at most $[e+(H_2(\delta)-\kappa+\eta)/2+o(1)]m$, also negative. These constants replace the high-rank rate $0.9$ of the independently chosen sparse graph. No strength of the gap beyond a positive rate is needed. [Detailed rank and catalog arguments](half-read-reuse.md) · [Forster](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000002000193)

The family also admits the fast paths from the half-read argument. Check the fixed format, accumulate $e$ while validating and packing the pins, and retrieve the packed endpoint bits when processing the sparse graph terms. An alternative validation retrieves endpoint bits directly at their pin-clause locations instead, using a constant number of work registers. Both algorithms can fall back to a complete SAT solver on unrecognized inputs and take $O(m)$ word steps on this family. The finite audit checks cut ranks, the complement perturbation and every assignment of a 16-pin instance. It supplies finite tests of the reduction and uses the cited expander gap for the asymptotic theorem. [Finite graph audit](../audits/expander_cut_sign_audit.py)
