# Recovering a hierarchy computation from sparse certified field values

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** A partial verifier may be able to certify outputs on a small fraction of inputs while abstaining everywhere else. To use such a verifier in a time-hierarchy argument, change the hierarchy language so that its answers form a polynomial. A line through a requested input can carry enough certified answers to determine its value. This yields a precise one-sided coverage target. It also permits a bounded number of positions with false claims if the verifier can afford three quantifier blocks.

## The encoded computation and the one-sided test

Use a length-by-length deterministic hierarchy decider H. On an m-bit input with a valid self-delimiting program prefix e, simulate program e on that whole input for m·2^⌊m/2⌋ simulated multitape steps. Output zero if it accepts by the cutoff and one otherwise, with a fixed convention on malformed inputs. One can scan a representation of all visited tape cells each simulated step, at a total cost of 2ᵐ poly(m), including universal program and clock overhead. Every fixed deterministic multitape decider running in O(2^(m/2)) halts in time on its own prefix at every sufficiently large length. H disagrees with it throughout that prefix at each such length. This uniform construction supplies the form of the hierarchy used below. Let P on the m-dimensional space over a finite field of size q extend all length-m answers of H by multilinear interpolation on the Boolean cube. Specifically sum H(u) over all Boolean u, weighting the term by the product of zᵢ where uᵢ=1 and 1−zᵢ where uᵢ=0. P has total degree at most m and agrees with H at every Boolean point. The coded language accepts (m,z,a) precisely when a=P(z). There are qᵐ YES rows at source length m, one per z. Its ordinary decider evaluates the sum in 2^(2m) poly(m,log q) time for the field representations below. Drawing lines through the point to be decoded is the classical polynomial local-correction construction. [Hennie and Stearns](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/tss/files/papers/HennieStearns66.pdf) · [Babai, Fortnow, Levin and Szegedy](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/check.pdf)

A uniform verifier takes z, a proposed value and a witness, which may include a choice of partial test. Its total guessing and deterministic checking time is U. Assume every accepted label is correct, and let C be the number of points z at which some witness succeeds. Fix q−1>m. If

    C > m (qᵐ−1)/(q−1),

every Boolean value H(x) can be recovered. If x is covered, use its witness. Otherwise the L=(qᵐ−1)/(q−1) affine lines through x partition the qᵐ−1 other points into sets of size q−1. One line contains at least m+1 covered points. Guess a nonzero direction, distinct nonzero parameters for those points, their labels and their witnesses. Check the witnesses and interpolate the degree-at-most-m restriction of P at parameter zero. For decision ask whether either kind of certificate returns one. A NO input cannot admit a false positive. A YES input with the claimed coverage always admits a certificate. In particular, no location algorithm for the covered points and no probability of finding a good line are needed. The threshold is exact for this single-line pigeonhole step. Given an uncovered x, choosing any m points on every such line attains equality without a usable line.

For a concrete clock choose the field with q=2ᴮ where B is the greatest power of two at most m. Its representation is uniform in poly(m) time. Start over the binary field with the absolute-trace-one element a=1. On doubling, adjoin s with s²+s=a, an irreducible quadratic because its right side has trace one. Indeed t↦t²+t has kernel of size two and image contained in the trace-zero hyperplane, hence equal to it. Carry forward as the next trace-one element as, since the relative trace of s is one. Ordinary quadratic-extension arithmetic iterated log₂B times costs poly(B) bit operations. At small m where the threshold cannot be met use finite exceptions.

If U≤2^(m/16) with ordinary polynomial bit cost in m per word operation and uniformly available tests, the certificate for one line gives an explicit SAT input of length 2^(m/16+o(m)). The certificate can use read-only copies of the candidate points and local work memory, charged through the normal quasilinear RAM-to-SAT simulation. A cubic ordinary SAT solver spends 2^(3m/16+o(m)) RAM time. Direct multitape simulation by scanning the previous word writes costs at most 2^(3m/8+o(m)), below the excluded clock. Thus under the cubic hypothesis every such fixed verifier lies at or below the coverage threshold at **every sufficiently large m**. On the fast decoder's own program prefix its soundness rules out reporting one, which would make H zero. At these lengths it halts reporting zero, so H is one and that input has neither a direct nor an interpolating certificate. Hence exceeding the threshold on any infinite subsequence of source lengths would suffice for a contradiction. No procedure needs to recognize the subsequence. The required fraction of YES rows is only about m/q, exponentially small in m for this field choice. The larger input length of a coded field evaluation is explicitly charged. [Quasilinear simulation](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

This argument also eliminates length-dependent advice. Fix a polynomial-time computable advice length a(m) and one verifier taking an additional string of that length. The clocked decoder on a source input x reads the last a(m) bits of x and supplies them as advice to every field-label test. Let e be its fixed diagonal program prefix. Whenever a(m)≤m−|e|, for every proposed advice string w there is an x with prefix e and suffix w. If that w made the label tests sound on this length and pushed coverage above the threshold, the decoder would be correct at this particular x. On its own prefix H gives the opposite answer as soon as the simulated decoder halts. Thus no w works on any sufficiently large length satisfying the width inequality. The advice can name a menu of partial verifiers. Its correctness need only hold for the selected w at the particular tested length. Neither a uniform way to find good advice nor soundness for the other advice strings is used. For example a(m)=m−⌈log₂(m+2)⌉ works eventually for every fixed verifier.

## When some certified labels are false

A single poisoned line could make the existential interpolation test accept the wrong answer. There is a different decoder. Let E count points with at least one certified *wrong* label, and C count points with a certified correct label. They can overlap. For each line through x normalize its direction by setting its first nonzero component to one. For b in {0,1}, let g_b on this line be the maximum, over univariate f of degree at most m with f(0)=b, of the number of distinct nonzero parameters t where f(t) has a certificate. Define the score A_b as the number of distinct pairs (line,j) with 1≤j≤g_b−m. Then

    A_{H(x)} ≥ C−1−mL,           A_{1−H(x)} ≤ E.

For the first inequality take the true line restrictions and exclude x. For the second, on each line a polynomial with the wrong center value agrees with the true restriction in at most m places. Each further certified position costs a distinct erroneous point on that line. The lines are disjoint away from x. Scores count pairs, even if a point or polynomial admits many witnesses.

The loss of m per line in this particular score is sharp. On each line choose m distinct nonzero parameters. Add to its true restriction a nonzero multiple of the polynomial with exactly these m simple roots, choosing the multiple to flip the center value. At every nonzero parameter issue both labels. Each line now scores q−1−m for both center values, and has exactly m positions with a correct label alone. These competing polynomials may depend on the line. With a single global alternative, the two Boolean tables P=0 and Q=∏ᵢ zᵢ already show that C−E=qᵐ−(q−1)ᵐ can be compatible with opposite values at the all-one input. Issue both labels everywhere, merging them where they coincide. Either table gives C=qᵐ and E=(q−1)ᵐ for that same menu.

The following concrete use of approximate counting makes the costs explicit. Assume C−1−mL>0 and C−1−mL≥(1+1/p(m))E for a fixed positive polynomial p. For this variant take B the greatest power of two at most m/256, and U≤2^(m/256). A pair counted by A_b has an existential certificate naming the polynomial and m+j distinct certified parameters with their labels. Sorting the parameters checks distinctness. This costs at most q U poly(m)=2^(m/128+o(m)). Cartesian powers of polynomially many independent pairs have the same bound in the exponent. Encode pairs uniquely with polynomially many bits.

Test the two sets for emptiness first. If both are nonempty, take their Cartesian powers for a fixed polynomial number of copies large enough that the larger size is at least 32 times the smaller. Here is a deterministic three-block procedure for comparing those sizes with that promise. For a set of a-bit strings with an existential membership test, affine binary hashes to k bits have independent uniform values on distinct inputs. Write μ for the set size divided by 2ᵏ. A random hash's zero bucket is occupied with probability at least μ/(1+μ) by the second moment and at most μ by the union bound. Thus the threshold μ≥4 versus μ≤1/4 has a constant gap. Repeat independently and take majority. With M a sufficiently large constant times log(a+2) repetitions the high-case acceptance fraction exceeds 1/2 and the low-case fraction is below 1/(R+1), where R=Mk(a+1) is the random bit length and 1≤k≤a. The concentration bound follows for the Bernoulli hit events from their independence across repetitions.

In the high case R+1 suitable translates of the accepting random strings cover every R-bit string: random independent shifts leave expected fewer than one uncovered. In the low case no R+1 translates cover the space by the union bound. Guess the shifts, universally quantify a random string, and existentially show that one shifted string has a majority of occupied buckets. The last block supplies a member and its witnesses for each required bucket. Try k through the entire polynomial range. For a nonempty smaller set of size S and a larger set of size at least 32S, take the least k with 2ᵏ≥4S. The larger set must pass at k; the smaller must fail there and at every higher k. Comparing largest passing thresholds distinguishes the sizes even when intermediate-gap responses are arbitrary. Give a set that passes no threshold the lowest score. This uses standard hashing and set-size lower bounds. [Sipser](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808762)

Under cubic SAT, eliminating the three existential/universal/existential blocks by quasilinear reductions, using complementation at the universal block, raises the checking exponent by a factor of at most 3³. The tape simulation costs at most another factor of two in the exponent. The result fits time 2^(54m/128+o(m)) on a multitape machine. This is strictly below 2^(m/2). Give the decoder a fixed default on ties or failed gap tests so it always halts within that clock. A length meeting the noisy coverage premise would make it correct on every Boolean input, including its own prefix. Thus this premise too must fail at every sufficiently large length under the hypothetical solver. The suffix-advice argument applies verbatim, with the noisy gap replacing exact soundness and coverage at the selected advice. Nothing in a decision algorithm for ordinary SAT yet supplies C or controls E for this coded hierarchy computation.

[The Lean proof](../lean/ErasureHierarchy.lean) verifies the finite pigeonhole step, the charge for a wrong label and both aggregate surplus-score inequalities given the per-line agreement bound. Field interpolation, the root bound, hashing and the machine clocks are manuscript arguments.
