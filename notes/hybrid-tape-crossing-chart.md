# Two quantified blocks for a multidimensional work tape with polynomial random-access storage

Fix an integer $d\ge1$ and $0\le\omega<1/d$. The machine has read-only random access to its packed input, one unrestricted nearest-neighbor $d$-dimensional writable tape, and random-access writable storage of at most $T^{\omega+o(1)}$ words at its time bound $T$. Words and addresses have $O(\log T)$ bits. A fixed number of word operations and cell accesses per step suffices. The bound permits sparse addresses provided the number of occupied words obeys the stated cap. Conventions charging every touched word also imply the needed cap. The geometric tape starts blank and can have a fixed finite alphabet or one logarithmic word per cell.

Put $D=d+1+d\omega$. A deterministic SAT solver on this machine cannot run in $O(n^c)$ on every ordinary explicit input of $n$ encoded bits if

$$
1\le c < \frac{1+\sqrt{1+4D(d+1)}}{2D}.
$$

One application of the simulation already proves the proposed exclusion for $c<\sqrt{(d+2)/D}$. The stronger displayed threshold uses a finite iteration. At $\omega=0$, the one-application exponent is the van Melkebeek–Raz bound. Williams obtains larger exponents on subpolynomial random-access storage by a longer alternation trade. The argument below extends his crossing and checkpoint rules by charging the entire auxiliary memory at each crossing. The translated-grid component bound suggested the exponents. A verifier also needs to certify its claimed computation. Historical priority for the polynomial-store extension has not been established. [van Melkebeek and Raz](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~dieter/Research/sat-t.html) · [Williams, §7, especially Lemmas 7.1–7.2 and 7.7](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf) · [Translated-grid bound](mixed-tape-dimension-threshold.md)

## A short description checked in small space

Use $H$ as the scale for powers. Suppose a fixed machine has a deterministic clock $T=H^{a+o(1)}$ and choose integer tile side $u=H^{x+o(1)}$ with $a\omega\le x\le a$. Idle at halts through the clock. Partition the geometric tape by translated cubes of side $u$. A nearest-neighbor move crosses a boundary for exactly one residue of the shift on its movement axis. Averaging gives a shift with at most $O(T/u)$ crossings. Record all the maximal periods spent within single tiles in chronological order, including their start and end times. At every shared time record the exact controls, coordinates and auxiliary memory, using a sorted sparse list of address/value pairs. The initial controller and memory are fixed. Include the final controller and memory and require the desired halting output. The description, including a second order of its periods, has

$$
 H^{a(1+\omega)-x+o(1)}
$$

bits. A constant number of endpoint records is absorbed since $x\le a$.

The second order groups periods by tile and then by chronological index within a tile. Supply forward and inverse permutation arrays and check at every index that the lookups are inverse. Check the two orders agree on tile coordinates, endpoints, the initial condition, and a gapless partition of the clock into positive lengths. A verifier then visits each tile in the second order. It maintains all the geometric tape cells in that tile, preserving them across its successive periods. At the beginning of a period it restores the declared auxiliary memory and controller, then executes exactly its stated number of steps on the actual read-only input. The head must stay within the indicated tile until the outgoing move on the final step of a nonfinal period. Adopt the convention that a transition reads and writes its current cell before its unit move. Compare the resulting controller, position and *entire* auxiliary store with the recorded endpoint. The next chronological period uses that same endpoint, even if processed earlier in the second order. In the final period compare the exact final state irrespective of the tile reached by its last move.

These checks establish a real computation. Induct on chronological periods. On entering a tile, its current contents result exactly from the earlier periods of that tile already covered by the induction. All the other starting data are the preceding verified endpoint. Determinism therefore forces the entire next period to coincide with the real run. Conversely the actual crossings and states pass all checks. Lean verifies this chronological gluing step from a shared controller chain and the individual tile chains. [Tile proof](../lean/HybridTileCuts.lean)

Use a private array for one tile, with a list of touched locations or generation stamps so that switching tiles costs only for cells actually accessed. A balanced dictionary implements sparse auxiliary addresses in logarithmic time per operation using space proportional to its size, and a sorted traversal compares a whole endpoint. Abort if an intervening store exceeds the declared space cap. Time to restore and compare snapshots summed over periods is $H^{a(1+\omega)-x+o(1)}\le H^{a+o(1)}$. Index checks, the accumulated simulation times and clearing private storage obey the same time bound. The verifier has random read access to the original input and immutable description. Its private workspace, including all counters and dictionaries, is at most $H^{h+o(1)}$ bits for $h=\max(dx,a\omega)$. Layout and clock caps also bound invalid descriptions.

## Two blocks and the square root

Guess the description and $H^z$ periodic checkpoints of this deterministic verifier in a single existential block. Only the private state enters each checkpoint. A single universal choice selects an adjacent pair. Replay its $H^{a-z+o(1)}$ steps with the immutable original input and description, checking both endpoints. Check the initial and final conditions as well. A first false private checkpoint following a true one cannot pass this test. Thus the resulting prefix has precisely the two blocks $\exists,\forall$, and the universal selector needs only $O(\log H)$ bits. The relevant time exponents are

$$
 a(1+\omega)-x,\qquad z+h,\qquad a-z.
$$

Taking $a=1$, $x=(1+2\omega)/(d+2)$ and $z=(1-dx)/2$ makes their maximum $\rho=D/(d+2)$. Here $x>\omega$, $dx\ge\omega$ and $z>0$. Rescaling gives a two-block simulation in $T^{\rho+o(1)}$ time.

Assume a hybrid SAT solver of exponent $c$. Any clocked one-block native computation of time $H^b$, on public input of at most $H^{b+o(1)}$ bits, gives an ordinary locally readable explicit 3-CNF of $H^{b+o(1)}$ bits. Complement the SAT answer for a universal block. Simulating the solver on the virtual formula takes hybrid time $H^{bc+o(1)}$ and auxiliary storage $H^{bc\omega+o(1)}$. The compiler's tables and address arithmetic cost polylogarithmic work space. Fixed-limb arithmetic and dictionaries allow a different constant width coefficient at polylogarithmic cost per step using space proportional to the auxiliary words being simulated, up to logarithmic factors. Use the available geometric tape for the solver. A fixed number of tracks or a constant-size block can represent any fixed enlargement of its symbols. Input word generation reads through to the public input. All these overheads fit within the displayed exponent slack even at $\omega=0$. [Packed compiler with local input access](packed-band-transfer.md)

A two-block computation can be put in the form $\forall y\,\exists z\,R$, with both strings of length at most its clock times the word width. First perform one such slowdown for the existential suffix. The outer universal computation can simulate the resulting hybrid program on an unrestricted native RAM, using a dictionary for tape coordinates as needed. A second slowdown therefore gives a single hybrid computation with exponent multiplied by $c^2$. In particular the deterministic tail of the final reduction uses just the allowed storage and geometric tape.

To track the contradiction, take the padded complementary universal two-block machine of source clock $n^2$. It cannot have a two-block simulation of opposite starting sign in $O(n^{2-\delta})$ time for any $\delta>0$. On its own fixed program prefix the universal machine can invert a timely opposite-sign simulation through the source deadline. The universal machine preserves two blocks by exchanging the quantifiers on guesses and complementing the final answer. A polynomial clock, sparse native addresses, and fixed-width normalization allow the usual fixed-prefix simulation with at most polylogarithmic overhead. Taking slightly smaller exponents absorbs that cost. The two SAT slowdowns and the speedup just proved would give the forbidden exponent when $c^2\rho<1$. [Williams, Theorem 2.4 on complementary speedup](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/sat-journal-final.pdf) · [Native instruction and simulation conventions](native-unary-clock-band.md)

## Iterating the inner slowdown

Ignore strict subpolynomial slack in the following exponents and absorb it at the final strict inequality. Set

$$
 e_1=\frac{d+2}{D},\qquad
 e_{i+1}=\frac{d+1+e_i/c}{D}.
$$

For each fixed $i$, under the SAT hypothesis a hybrid computation of time $H^{e_i+o(1)}$ on an input of $H^{1+o(1)}$ bits has an existential description of $H^{1+o(1)}$ bits followed by a universal selector of $O(\log H)$ bits and deterministic time $H^{1+o(1)}$. The base case is the rescaled one-application bound. For the step put $a=e_{i+1}$ and $x=a(1+\omega)-1$. The tile description now has exponent one, and the private workspace exponent is $h=dx$, since $a\ge1$ gives $dx\ge a\omega$. Take $z=a-e_i/c$. Then

$$
 z+h=Da-(d+e_i/c)=1,\qquad a-z=e_i/c.
$$

Consequently the outer description and all checkpoints together have exponent one. Collapse just the universal selector and its final replay into hybrid time $H^{e_i+o(1)}$, using one SAT slowdown. Its entire public input, including the already guessed outer description, fits in $H^{1+o(1)}$ bits. Applying the induction hypothesis to that hybrid computation gives another existential guess, which merges with the first. Its new universal selector is the only surviving universal block.

In the range claimed in the theorem, $1\le c<e_1$ and $cD>1$. The $e_i$ increase towards $E=(d+1)/(D-1/c)$. In the open range, $e_{i+1}>e_i\ge e_i/c$, so $z\ge0$, $h\le1$, and $a\omega\le x\le a$. For the last inequality, $h\le1$ implies $x\le1/d\le a$. The inner replay exponent $e_i/c>1$ covers the public input.

The displayed threshold is exactly $c^2<E$. Choose finite $i$ with $e_i>c^2$. After collapsing the source $\Pi_2$ diagonal of clock $n^2$ into hybrid time $n^{2c^2+o(1)}$, supply it as a computation on the padded scale $H=n^p$ where $\max(1,2c^2/e_i)<p<2$. The true $n$-bit input is the nonpadding part of a public string of length $H$. The machine can get this string virtually from the original input and the padding convention. Recover the source length from an explicit header. The scale changes word width only by a fixed factor. The induction gives an opposite-sign two-block simulation in time $n^{p+o(1)}$, contradicting the same hierarchy. One may enlarge $c$ and $\omega$ by arbitrarily small rational margins inside the strict exclusion and use rational powers for every fixed recurrence. This also supplies constructible storage and time caps when the original promise contains a subpolynomial factor. The proof leaves the unrestricted-workspace SAT objectives open.

## The logarithmic endpoint for a linear-time solver

For each fixed $d$ there is also a constant $B_0$ such that ordinary SAT cannot simultaneously have hybrid time $O(N)$ and auxiliary space $O(N^{1/d}/\log^B(N+2))$ words for $B\ge B_0$. Here $N$ counts encoded formula bits and the geometric tape remains unrestricted. It suffices to prove the statement at a single sufficiently large fixed integer $B$. The following accounting uses a common worst-case source clock.

Take a complementary two-block diagonal of clock $t=n^2$ and put $L=\lceil\log_2(t+2)\rceil$. Write $q_0\ge1$ for a fixed exponent covering bit/word conversions, setup, local input access, simulation of the fixed instruction set, and sparse dictionary overhead. For a fixed dimension and instruction set its degree can be chosen independently of $B$ and of the solver text. The text contributes a multiplicative constant to instruction interpretation and local template dimensions. For example, to divide fixed-limb words at a changed width one can use binary long division in $O(L^2)$ steps and $O(L)$ words, avoiding preprocessing tables of polynomial size. The two quantified strings cost at most $O(tL)$ bits each. The first bounded existential computation can be given a public string of this length and clock $O(tL^{q_0})$. Its fixed-width 3-CNF has $O(tL^{q_0+3})$ bits. Under the hypothesized linear solver, simulation of its answer in the remaining one-block computation takes $O(tL^{2q_0+3})$ steps. Compiling that computation a second time gives $O(tL^{2q_0+6})$ bits. Both solver inputs can be regularly padded to length at least $t$, and both logarithms are $\Theta(L)$. The outer hybrid solver and its virtual input generator fit a fixed constructible clock $T=O(tL^K)$, $K\ge3q_0+6$, with $\log T=\Theta(L)$. Its auxiliary workspace is at most $S=O(T^{1/d}L^{K-B})$ words using the fixed-power storage overhead for the simulation and the polylogarithmic setup. For all sufficiently large lengths the latter fits even for large fixed $B$. Enlarging $K$ once covers the constant number of simulations. [Charge of the three logarithms per ordinary formula](packed-band-transfer.md)

There is a fixed $q\ge1$ for which the crossing description has at most $(1+T/u)(1+S)L^q$ bits, up to a constant. The deterministic checker takes $O((T+\text{description length})L^q)$ time and $O((u^d+S+1)L^q)$ private bits. These bounds follow from the array, chronological index and dictionary checks above. Computing fixed powers of $L$ and rounded tile sides uses $\operatorname{poly}(L)$ time with fixed polynomial degree independent of the chosen powers. Repeated multiplication by $L$ has a number of repetitions depending on those fixed powers. Integer $d$-th roots can be rounded by binary search. All resulting integers use $O_d(\log T)$ bits at sufficiently large lengths.

Take $u$ within a constant factor of $T^{1/d}/L^A$. Let $g$ be a fixed nonnegative integer covering any polylogarithmic overhead of the complementary universal interpreter on the fixed native instruction set. First choose a fixed positive integer $A$ such that $dA/2>K+2q+g+3$, then take $B>A+2K+2q+g+3$. This gives $S=O(u^d)$ also when $d=1$. The description costs $O(T L^{A+K+q-B})$ bits. The term from the one extra endpoint fits the same bound, and the positive power of $T$ in the prescribed upper cap for $S$ absorbs the additive one. The checker now takes $O(TL^q)$ time and $O(TL^{q-dA})$ private bits. Use $\lceil L^{dA/2}\rceil$ equal temporal segments. Writing their checkpoints and replaying one segment each cost $O(TL^{2q-dA/2})$, allowing a further $L^q$ for normalized private state access and comparison. The selector is one universal choice of $O(\log L)$ bits. Both the description and this checkpoint simulation take $O(t/L^{g+2})$ time by the choices of $A,B$. The original input and setup of size $n\operatorname{poly}(L)$ also fit. The opposite-sign two-block diagonal therefore runs in $o(n^2)$, giving the contradiction at this endpoint. The exponent on the logarithm has not been optimized.
