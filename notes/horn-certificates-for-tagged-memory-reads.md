# A pair of swapped reads defeats local Horn certificates with parity weights

The signed-graph proof of the dense list circuit bound tests whether a proposed payload arrived at its claimed tag. After collapsing the left equalities, it must distinguish a graph of self-loops from one containing any nonloop. I tried replacing the XOR readout on those edges by an arbitrary weighted choice of Horn constraints. This would make the verifier a forward-propagation problem, and would test whether this representation retains the information needed to certify RAM memory reads. A two-address transposition already defeats the replacement. It does so even with arbitrarily many shared bits at each address, local existential auxiliary bits, and a finite menu of constraints of arbitrary size. [Original tagged test and its one-bit readout classification](matrix-list-parity-graphs.md)

## Same-length test on two addresses

Associate a domain $D$ to each vertex. Each directed edge independently chooses a label $e$ of field weight $a_e$ and imposes a relation $R_e$ on the states of its two endpoints, in that order. The output is the sum over edge-label assignments of their weight products multiplied by **one SAT decision bit** for the simultaneous constraints. A satisfiable choice contributes just once, however many assignments it has. Equal relation types can be combined by summing their weights. For the two-address test both addresses and all the tested edges have the same type. Vertex-specific external names, comparisons of identifiers outside the oracle, and constraints coupling the edge choices are additional resources.

Let $L$ be the value on one loop. Two independent loops have value $L^2$. On the directed two-cycle, interchanging the two edge labels preserves satisfiability and the product of the two weights. In characteristic two the unequal pairs cancel. The value of the cycle is therefore
$$
 \sum_e a_e^2\, [\exists x,y:\ R_e(x,y)\land R_e(y,x)] .
$$
Assume $D$ has a common commutative binary operation $m$ preserving each relation coordinatewise. A reversed pair in $R_e$ gives the diagonal pair $(m(x,y),m(x,y))$ in $R_e$. A diagonal pair conversely supplies a reversed pair. Hence the displayed value is
$$
 \sum_e a_e^2 [\exists x:\ R_e(x,x)] = L^2.
$$
This proves the equality of the readouts on two separate correct self-reads and on their transposition. Bit-vector Horn relations have precisely the needed closure under coordinatewise AND. Existential projection preserves that closure by taking the meet of the local witnesses as well. The same proof works for dual-Horn relations with OR. No fixed dimension bound on the interface was used.

For single Boolean endpoint states there is an exact stronger identity with *arbitrary* relations. A relation with a reversed pair but no diagonal must be precisely disequality. Thus the value on the transposition minus the value on the two separate loops is the **square of the total weight of the exact disequality relation**. Even including every other Boolean relation with arbitrary characteristic-two weights cannot replace this relation. In the original XOR readout the two labels impose equality and disequality with unit weight. A loop accepts one label and the double cycle accepts two choices modulo two, giving the required difference. The [Lean proof](../lean/HornCycleReadouts.lean) checks symmetric-pair cancellation over a finite list in $\mathbb F_2$ both the exact Boolean disequality coefficient and the double-cycle identity for arbitrary domains with a common commutative preserving operation.

## A family of actual incorrect read transcripts

Write each distinct word $i$ to its own address $i$, then read every address once, proposing reply $\pi(i)$. A constant-register machine can generate the writes and reads in $O(N)$ instructions. The genuine transcript has $\pi$ equal to the identity. In the residual graph an edge connects the correct and proposed payload identities. A self-read is a loop and swapping two proposed replies gives a two-cycle. This test has the same number of reads and writes, the same chronological addresses and the same multiset of proposed payloads on the genuine and false transcripts.

Suppose a checker's access to an identifier in this residual graph consists of a type from a palette of $q$ types. Its relation menu may depend on the two endpoint types. Menus for identically typed ordered pairs must be the same, and the readout factors on disjoint components as above. Pair vertices within types arbitrarily. There are $b=\sum_t\lfloor n_t/2\rfloor\ge (N-q)/2$ disjoint pairs, where $n_t$ counts addresses of type $t$. For each of the $2^b$ choices of which pairs to transpose, component factorization and the two-address identity give exactly the genuine transcript's readout. All except the identity contain an incorrect read. Consequently a characteristic-two checker of this form distinguishing every permutation requires $N$ distinct types. This type count is sharp in this interface: with unique types a local predicate can compare the endpoint types for equality. A general RAM can also test this particular workload directly in linear word time. The bound applies to this proposed verifier of the genuine computation, not to the complexity of producing that computation.

## Prime-characteristic covers

There is a version without a two-sheet restriction. Over a field of characteristic $p$, choose a regular cover of degree $p^a$ of a finite constraint instance, with a deck group of that order acting freely and transitively on each fiber of variables and constraint occurrences. Require local menus and weights to be pulled back from the base, and each relation to be closed under coordinatewise meet on a semilattice. The total weighted decision readout on the cover equals the base readout raised to the $p^a$ power.

Indeed, choices of labels form group orbits. Every non-singleton orbit has cardinality divisible by $p$ and contributes zero. Every invariant choice comes from one choice on the base. If the lifted constraints have a solution, take the pointwise meet of its group translates. This is an invariant solution, so it descends to the base. Conversely any base solution lifts. The weight for the lifted choice is its base weight raised to $p^a$, and Frobenius commutes with summation. In particular $p$ separate correct loops and one directed $p$-cycle have the same readout. This uses the familiar orbit cancellation from modular homomorphism counting and the symmetrization of CSP solutions by a symmetric polymorphism. Historical priority for these general mechanisms is established; the tag-check application and the exact disequality coefficient are recorded without a priority claim. [Kazeminia and Bulatov, *Counting Homomorphisms Modulo a Prime Number*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol138-mfcs2019/LIPIcs.MFCS.2019.59/LIPIcs.MFCS.2019.59.pdf) · [Butti and Dalmau, *The Complexity of the Distributed Constraint Satisfaction Problem*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-022-10091-y)

The [exhaustive Boolean audit](../audits/horn_cycle_readout_audit.py) checks every menu with zero-one weights on all 16 possible binary Boolean relations. It also checks the cover equality with explicit label choices on small prime cycles. The measured limitation requires identical local treatment of both copies of an address type. A solver or a certificate that uses the order or numerical bits of the full identifiers can distinguish the two cases.
