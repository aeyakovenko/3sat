# Short parity refutations from low-weight changes of checker outcomes

Let a succinct local verifier pose $m$ linear equations over $\mathbb F_2$. Write $U\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m$ for the subspace of all attainable vectors of left-hand sides, and $b$ for the requested right-hand sides. A parity refutation is a subset of equation occurrences whose XOR is $0=1$. It is enough to guess its row names and check the sum. The statement below allows changes to an arbitrarily large number of proof bits when analyzing which row outcomes they affect.

**Theorem.** Suppose $d=\operatorname{dist}(b,U)$ rows must be violated. Let $C\subseteq\mathbb F_2^m$ be any subspace generated by Hamming-weight one or two vectors. Put
$$
 s=\dim C/(C\cap U),\qquad t=\dim U/(C\cap U).
$$
If $d>2s$, there is a parity refutation using at most
$$
 (t+1)\left\lfloor\frac m{d-2s}\right\rfloor
$$
original rows. A canonical choice for $C$ is the span of **all** vectors of weight at most two in $U$, including changes that require many coordinated proof-bit flips. This gives $s=0$. The conclusion also applies if the matrix of equations admits an unknown change of basis on the proof variables followed by a rank-$r$ perturbation of a matrix with at most two nonzero entries per column. In that case one can take $s,t\le r$.

With $C=U$, the underlying component algorithm for maximum satisfiability of linear equations of variable occurrence at most two is classical. The bound here accounts for the quotient dimensions, the Hamming distance, and the number of named *original rows* needed by a succinct verifier. We have not established historical priority for the bound. [Crowston, Gutin, Jones and Yeo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1110.5915)

## Transferring violations inside a component

View a weight-two generator $e_i+e_j$ as an undirected edge between equation occurrences $i,j$. Mark a component as grounded if it contains a unit generator. Inside an ungrounded connected component, edge sums along paths allow any pair of coordinates to be flipped. Thus the generators on that component span exactly its even-weight vectors. A grounded component permits all vectors. These statements include isolated singleton vertices and disconnected graphs. Write $p$ for the linear map that takes the parity on each ungrounded component and ignores all grounded ones. Then $\ker p=C$.

Set $W=U+C$. The rank of $p(W)=p(U)$ is $t$. Choose $s$ generators of weight at most two representing a basis for $C/(C\cap U)$. Their images span $W/U$. Every $w\in W$ can be moved to $U$ by flipping the coordinates of at most $s$ such generators. Hence $d'=\operatorname{dist}(b,W)\ge d-2s$. The rank-perturbation claim follows because the images of either matrix modulo the other's column space are spanned by the perturbation columns. Invertible operations on proof coordinates preserve $U$.

For a fixed $z\in p(W)$, the coset of words attaining $p(w)=z$ violates precisely one equation in each ungrounded component where $z$ differs from $p(b)$, after the best choice of representative. One may choose which row in each such component pays for its parity. For $L=\lfloor m/(d-2s)\rfloor$, there are fewer than $d-2s$ components with more than $L$ rows. If a vector in $p(W)$ matched the required parity on every component of size at most $L$, it could be extended to a word in $W$ differing from $b$ in fewer than $d-2s$ positions. This contradicts the distance.

Choose a basis for $p(W)$. Each small component imposes one equation on its $t$ coefficients. This linear system is inconsistent by the preceding paragraph. Its coefficient matrix has rank at most $t$, so at most $t+1$ component equations sum to $0=1$. Indeed select independent coefficient rows spanning all of them and solve their equations. A violated dependent equation, added to its basis representation, gives the contradiction. The disjoint union of the selected components is now a parity refutation on at most $(t+1)L$ original occurrences: its parity functional vanishes on $W$, hence on $U$, and takes value one on $b$. No certificate of the chosen graph, components, basis or perturbation is required.

For arbitrary row weights with total $H>0$, let $\lambda$ be the sum of the weighted support costs of the chosen $s$ quotient generators. If the minimum violated weight is $D>\lambda$, the same bound is $(t+1)\lfloor H/(D-\lambda)\rfloor$. To see this, move from $W$ to $U$ at cost at most $\lambda$. If all components of size at most $L$ passed, choose the lightest row of each failed large component. Their combined weight is at most $H/(L+1)<D-\lambda$. Weights may be hidden from the verifier.

## Applying the bound to the actual signed computation

Let $M=2^j$ and fix one sign of the [priority computation](priority-pinned-sat.md). Assume ordinary SAT takes deterministic time $O(n^k)$ for a fixed $k\in\{1,2,3\}$. Use the uniform constant-query, locally generated PCP on the short scale. On a true claim its genuine proof satisfies every parity equation entailed by the full local relation at each seed. Permit the empty parity $0=1$ for an empty relation. A checker guesses row names comprising seeds and subsets of their constant-size query lists, with a right-hand side. It regenerates the local relations and checks each implication. Sort the proof addresses in the selected rows, cancel every even multiplicity, and check that the sum is $0=1$. This computation uses $(g+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ nondeterministic time for at most $g\le M$ names. The ordinary bounded-run formula also has $(g+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ encoded bits. For a sufficiently large fixed $B$ and $g=\lfloor M^{1/k}/(j+1)^B\rfloor$, the hypothesized SAT decider signals in $o(M)$ time if a refutation exists. Its signal is sound for the opposite sign. Bi-immunity excludes successes on all sufficiently late false scales. [Uniform PCP and scale](low-randomness-free-winner.md) · [Bounded computation encoding](packed-band-transfer.md)

Consequently, at each such false scale, take **any** nonempty collection of locally entailed parity rows and any comparison space $C$ as above. For $m$ equally weighted occurrences one obtains
$$
 \frac{\operatorname{dist}(b,U)}m\ \le\ \frac{2s}m+\frac{t+1}g.
$$
If the right-hand side were strictly smaller, the theorem would produce a refutation of length at most $g$. The same conclusion holds with repeats, since a pair of identical occurrences in a witness can be canceled. The collector and its choice of $C$ can be nonuniform. Only the short row names enter the sound checker. For weights the corresponding bound is $D/H\le\lambda/H+(t+1)/g$.

For example, choose any one entailed parity test per original seed, using $0=0$ where desired. If its gap at a false scale is at least a fixed $\delta>0$, then for the canonical space the quotient rank is at least $\delta g-1$. If the original PCP gap is $\delta$ and the parity relaxation has near-two-coordinate repairs with small quotient dimensions, a single proof instead passes all but the fraction displayed above of the selected parity checks while failing at least $\delta$ of the genuine tests. Finding an entailed parity relaxation that retains a gap and has small quotient cost on infinitely many false scales would give the SAT contradiction. The construction of such a relaxation for these claims remains open.

## Why both parameters appear

There are systems with constant row width where the shortest parity refutation takes $\Omega((t+1)/\delta)$ rows, with $s=0$ and violation fraction $\delta$. Begin with $12h$ width-three equations on $h$ variables having minimum violation at least $3h$, such that every set of at most $\rho h$ rows is independent for a fixed $\rho>0$. Random triples and charges have these properties by the [small-set expansion and counting argument](horn-gap-rounds.md). Its minimum inconsistent subcollection consequently has more than $\rho h$ rows.

For each old equation $a\cdot x=b_i$, make a chain of $L\ge2$ rows using fresh private bits $z_1,\ldots,z_{L-1}$. The first imposes $a\cdot x+z_1=b_i$, the interior rows impose $z_{j-1}+z_j=0$, and the last imposes $z_{L-1}=0$. Their sum is the original equation. For fixed old variables the chain can place a required violation in just one row. Also, a row sum eliminating every private bit selects either all $L$ or zero rows of each chain. Thus the minimum number of violations stays unchanged, the violation fraction is $\Theta(1/L)$, and the minimum refutation length increases by exactly $L$. Choose $C$ generated by the private columns. Now $s=0$ and $t\le h$. The theorem itself gives $t+1=\Omega(h)$ from the linear lower bound on the original core, so the stated order is attained. Using the canonical, possibly larger $C$ also leaves $t+1=\Theta(h)$ by the same estimate.

The distance correction can matter even when $t=0$. Take $m=2s$ and the space $C$ of independent pair flips $e_{2i-1}+e_{2i}$. Set $U=0$ and $b$ equal to the all-ones word. The distance to $U$ is $2s$ and the distance to $U+C$ is zero. This proves sharpness of the Hamming transfer step itself. In this example the original equations have unit-size refutations.
