# Selecting whole snapshots in a SAT certificate

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A verifier for a rejecting SAT computation checks one interval at a time. Its reads inside a description can be adaptive, yet the indices of its two descriptions stay fixed throughout the check. On the packed RAM the encoded cost of selecting among independent snapshots is linear in their total bit length, up to the cost of the largest selected row, metadata and a lower-order term. As a consequence, a hypothetical $O(N\log^a N)$ SAT solver has unsatisfiable instances on which the sum of the shortest descriptions at a prescribed grid of times is $\Omega(N/\log^{a+3}N)$ bits. The statement imposes a decoding-time bound and places no space bound on the solver. ## Selecting a row of hardwired data

Suppose $r$ rows each contain $c\ge1$ bits, and each has a distinct identifier in $\{0,\ldots,P\}$. A Boolean circuit takes a binary identifier and outputs the corresponding row, giving the zero vector if the identifier is absent. Form a single binary trie on the $\lceil\log_2(P+1)\rceil$ selector bits. From a prefix indicator obtain its two child indicators by conjoining it with the next bit or its negation. The tree has $O(P)$ gates and supplies one indicator $e_i$ for each identifier. Build it once for all buckets. Bucket-local row identifiers occur as wire names in the construction. Partition them into groups of size at most
$$
 h=\min\{r,\max(1,\lfloor\log_2(c+1)\rfloor)\}.
$$
For each group compute every subset OR of its indicators. Appending one indicator extends the table by taking the OR of this indicator with each entry in the preceding table. The empty subset has value zero. For each column in each group the stored bits give a mask selecting one entry of this table. OR these entries over all groups to give the output column. This is the classical subset-tabulation construction for OR circuits, applied to a one-hot selector. [Lupanov's method and OR circuits](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5432968/)

The construction takes
$$
 O\big(\lceil r/h\rceil(2^h+c)\big)
$$
bounded-fan-in gates after the shared trie. The mask occurs as a *wire index* in the emitted circuit. There is no circuit operation for separately loading each hardwired bit. A uniform constructor computes the masks by scanning at most $rc$ bits, maintaining a mask of at most $O(\log N)$ bits when the input size is polynomial in $N$. It generates the subset tables in increasing mask order and emits standard Tseytin clauses. The construction time is $O(rc + \text{encoded circuit length}+\operatorname{polylog} N)$ native steps, including the cost of writing explicit variable indices. A second, independently selected row costs a second copy.

When $r$ is large, grouping *columns* gives a second bound. Partition the $c$ columns into blocks of size at most $k=\max(1,\lfloor(\log_2 r)/2\rfloor)$. In one block, group the $r$ row indicators by the bit pattern of that row in the block. Compute the OR of each pattern class. For a given column, take the OR of the class wires whose pattern has a one in that position. These gates give the selected column bit even if all bucket indicators are zero. One block costs $O(r+k2^k)=O(r)$ gates since $k2^k=O(r)$ for this choice of $k$. All blocks cost $O(r\lceil c/k\rceil)$ gates. The constructor can accumulate a running OR wire indexed by each pattern while scanning the rows, initialize $2^k$ entries per block, then enumerate these entries for the $k$ output columns. Scanning at most $rc$ input bits plus writing the emitted clauses covers its time. Both constructions use the actual row pattern to choose wire indices at construction time.

For variable lengths $s_i$, write $S=\sum s_i$ and $u=\max(1,\max_i s_i)$. Group nonempty rows by their lengths rounded up to powers of two. For occupied bucket $j$ let $c_j$ be that power and $r_j$ its number of rows. Pad its rows with zeroes. Then $\sum r_j c_j\le2S$ and $\sum c_j\le4u$. Empty rows use a public zero length. Run the row circuit for each bucket on the same identifier. For bit position $k$, OR together the outputs from buckets having $c_j>k$, indexing columns from zero. This uses $O(u)$ further gates over *all* positions. Allocate through the greatest padded length and expose the true selected length as a field of the selected metadata. The reader enforces its usual out-of-range convention, including on a final partial word.

Write $L=\max(2,\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil)$. For $P\le N$ and polynomial total data the binary variable names have $O(L)$ bits. Define
$$
 B=L\sum_j c_j\left\lceil r_j/h_j\right\rceil,\qquad
 h_j=\min\{r_j,\max(1,\lfloor\log_2(c_j+1)\rfloor)\}.
$$
Using only the row-grouped construction gives $O(B+uL+P L)$ encoded bits, with the last term for the shared trie. When all rows have one declared width $u$, take $c=u$ in one bucket. For the stronger bound on arbitrary rows use the following three cases in the uniform constructor. A power-of-two threshold with exponent $\lfloor L/8\rfloor$ implements the comparisons.

* For $c_j\ge 2^{\lfloor L/8\rfloor}$, the row-grouped construction costs $O(r_jc_j+c_jL)$ encoded bits, since either $h_j=r_j$ or $h_j=\Omega(L)$.
* For smaller $c_j$ with $r_j\ge 2^{\lfloor L/8\rfloor}$, the column-grouped construction has $k=\Omega(L)$, so its encoded cost is $O(r_j c_j+r_j L)$.
* A remaining bucket has $r_jc_j=O(N^{1/4})$. A direct circuit ORs indicators separately for each column, costing $O(r_jc_jL)$ encoded bits. There are $O(L)$ buckets because every row has polynomially many bits.

Across all buckets, constructing and writing the selectors takes
$$
 O\big(S+P L+uL+N^{1/4}L^2+\operatorname{poly}(L)\big) \tag{A}
$$
native steps and the same bound in encoded bits. The $S$ term includes direct inspection of the hardwired data. The trie can be emitted in increasing prefix order and a bucket reuses each required identifier by address, regardless of the order of IDs within that bucket. Bucket assignment, offsets and fixed-width metadata can be computed using $O(PL)$ preprocessing steps. Each bucket list can be built by counting rows and then placing them using prefix offsets. The exponent $1/4$ in (A) can be replaced by any fixed $2\epsilon>0$ by using $2^{\lfloor\epsilon L\rfloor}$ in the cases (with constants depending on $\epsilon$).

## A certificate for an arbitrary advertised schedule

Let $F_x$ be the explicit packed 3-CNF for the bounded nondeterministic interpreter on $n_0$ input bits with clock $H=\Theta(n_0^2)$. The padded encoding has length $N=\Theta(H\log^3 H)$. A packed input word of $F_x$ is available from $x$ in constant time after polylogarithmic setup. Fix a correct SAT solver with time $T=\lceil C N L^a\rceil$, $a\ge0$ fixed, and pad halts with idle steps. A certificate supplies increasing native times $0=t_0<\cdots<t_P=E\le T$, $1\le P\le N$, controller states, and independent descriptions at internal times. Set $G=\max_i(t_{i+1}-t_i)$. The last controller must be in a rejecting halt at $E$. Immediate halts at time zero can be checked directly. An honest description returns the exact value at every legal writable address of its one native snapshot. It receives $F_x$, its *actual time* $t_i$, its own packed row and true length, and the queried address. This interface permits an arbitrary subsequence of the times with the retained descriptions unchanged. [Virtual formula and bounded signed diagonal](packed-band-transfer.md)

One fixed evaluator with query cap $1\le\tau\le N$ suffices. This may be a fixed decoder, or a fixed bytecode interpreter whose instruction words and data are inside the selected row. Use a fixed-width guest ISA with a bounded number of registers, constant-time random instruction fetch from the bounded row, and a fixed $O(L)$ width allowance for guest addresses and words. Check bounds on all guest accesses. Scratch starts blank for each call, for example with a fresh call tag per simulated call and a separate presence field. Timeout, malformed instructions and illegal access receive the public default. The cap counts actual evaluator steps. An ordinary fixed decoder in this ABI running in $O(L^d)$ steps admits $\tau=C_D L^d$ for a fixed $C_D$, including the simulation overhead. An unrestricted collection of word-width constants would require a separate simulation analysis.

The nondeterministic rejecting-run recognizer guesses $P$, all timestamps, the common positive query cap, true lengths, controller states and row payloads. It enforces a fixed polynomial bound on the total data (a cap $S\le N^2$ suffices for the corollary). The directory comes first so the branch pays for its actual payload. Before constructing the formula the recognizer checks ranges, strict increase, the initial state and the final rejecting halt, and computes $G$ and the maximum row length. With fixed-word fields these checks take $O(P)$ native steps for a directory of $O(PL)$ bits. The verifier can enforce $E\le N^2$ for all large $N$, so it need not compute the real exponent in the analytical upper bound $T$. Genuine schedules ending by $T$ meet this format bound. Optional row-specific decoder clocks must be bounded by the common declared cap. The public layout gives each endpoint an $O(L)$-bit metadata header with its timestamp, CPU state, true payload length, and any such clock. Endpoints zero and $E$ have genuine headers and empty payloads. Thus the $P+1$ complete rows have total $S+O(PL)$ and maximum $u+O(L)$. The decoder gets a view of its original payload and true length.

The recognizer writes one ordinary SAT instance for existence of a defect. An existential selector chooses a valid interval $i\in\{0,\ldots,P-1\}$ and any legal address. Apply the two whole-row selectors to the *same* global choice bits. In the second selector the indicator of right endpoint $j\ge1$ is the wire $e_{j-1}$, and the right endpoint zero has indicator false. Both output banks now contain the selected headers as well as the payloads. Reject an out-of-range selector as a possible defect witness. Any explicit interval or endpoint flags used by the bounded checker are wired to the same bits or their fixed predicates. Replay from the stated controller using a fresh write overlay. Reads use the latest local write, with a separate presence bit, followed by the start decoder, or zero at time zero. Check the ending controller. At an internal endpoint compare the ending word at the *selected address* to the next decoder, including when the segment never touched this address. An honest certificate has no defect. With no defect, comparison at every address identifies the whole next memory, so induction from the true start forces an actual rejecting halt by $E$. [Finite replay argument](../lean/SolverSnapshotReplay.lean)

The checker needs $R=O((G+1)(\tau+1)+\operatorname{poly}(L))$ native steps. It receives the original $x$, the global constants such as $G$ and the cap, and two selected endpoint buffers. It never queries the full directory. A public-source reduction for random reads uses $O(RL^3+mL^2+\operatorname{poly}(L))$ encoded bits for $m$ words. Here $O(n_0+1)$ words suffice for the external inputs and each selected buffer uses $O(1+u/L)$ words. Replace each source-bit constant for a buffer by equality to the corresponding selected-row output wire. At a partial final word pin the remaining bits to zero. The metadata and selector headers of a constant number of words can use source pins in exactly the same manner, or fixed initial-register constraints. A signal on a pin has a single Boolean value in each satisfying assignment, so the authenticated read-only bank returns the same actual bits on repeated or adaptive accesses. The whole set of rows and their headers enters only through the selectors. All intermediate lengths and clocks are polynomial in $N$. Width and bank headroom can be chosen in advance as for the fixed hierarchy opponent. [Public-input routing and its uniform constructor](materialized-input-census.md)

Consequently the successful branch for any honest schedule with **arbitrary $1\le P\le N$** costs at most
$$
 O\left((G+1)(\tau+1)L^{a+3}
 +n_0L^{a+2}+(S+P L+uL)L^a+\operatorname{poly}(L)\right). \tag{1}
$$
Constructing the defect formula and running the assumed solver on it are included. In (A), $N^{1/4}L^2=o(n_0 L^2)$ because $n_0=\Theta(\sqrt{N/L^3})$. This absorbs the small-bucket cost into the term already allocated to $x$. This works for early $E$ and polynomial $P$ with no regularity or minimum width of the rows. The common row selector applies to the explicit verification of one segment. An arbitrary RAM access sequence that changes rows at each probe has a different input cost.

For clocks that vary from row to row, write $\tau_i\le N$ for the declared cap, $\tau_0=\tau_P=0$, and $g_i=t_{i+1}-t_i$. The start decoder is invoked $O(g_i+1)$ times, including any final lookup in the local write overlay. The end decoder is invoked at most once. The constructor can compute
$$
 D=\max_{0\le i<P}\big((g_i+1)(\tau_i+1)+\tau_{i+1}\big)
$$
from the guessed directory in $O(P)$ native steps since each product occupies a constant number of words. Using $R=O(D+\operatorname{poly}(L))$ replaces $(G+1)(\tau+1)$ by $D$ in (1). This preserves the gain from pairing expensive starting snapshots with short ensuing gaps.

A header can also advertise $b_i$, a bound on the number of distinct work addresses read before their first write in interval $i$. Cache each first decoded read in the same presence-tagged overlay as a write. Count distinct such incoming addresses and signal a defect *before* issuing the first read that would exceed $b_i$. This bounds even a false certificate's tentative replay. The freely selected endpoint address costs up to one additional start query. Consequently the time factor in (1) can be replaced by
$$
 \max_i\big(g_i+(b_i+1)(\tau_i+1)+\tau_{i+1}\big).
$$
On the genuine run choose its actual count in each interval. Caching a read preserves the current value, and an overflow is an additional objectively testable defect, so the authenticity induction still applies.

## Sum of the shortest independent snapshots on a prescribed grid

Fix a decoder with exponent $d$ in the preceding interface. Split the entire padded run into $P_0=\lceil L^{2a+d+7}\rceil$ equal intervals up to rounding. At each sufficiently large *source padding length* $n_0$ there is an unsatisfiable $F_x$ for which the sum of the minimum description lengths at the internal grid times is
$$
 \Omega(H/L^a)=\Omega(N/L^{a+3})\quad\text{bits}.
$$
A missing description has infinite length. For $a=d=0$ the grid has $\lceil L^7\rceil$ intervals and the bound is $\Omega(N/L^3)$ bits in total.

Use the fixed recognizer above as the opponent encoded at the beginning of $x$, followed by padding. Its distinguished signal is the halt sign sought by the bounded interpreter. If the interpreter observed that signal before its deadline, $F_x$ would be satisfiable by construction, and that very branch would certify that the correct solver rejected $F_x$. It follows that $F_x$ is unsatisfiable and no signal occurs in time. The fixed program incurs a constant factor per simulated step on its own padding lengths. This is a one-branch argument. [Signed-diagonal convention](solver-snapshot-descriptions.md)

Suppose that the total of the minimum lengths were $S_0\le\eta H/L^a$, for a fixed small $\eta>0$. Keep times zero and $T$. Delete internal times whose shortest description exceeds $v=\sqrt\eta\,H/L^{a+1}$. At most $\sqrt\eta\,L$ times are deleted, so the surviving maximum gap is at most $(1+\sqrt\eta L)\lceil T/P_0\rceil$. Its total length is at most $S_0$ and $u\le\max(1,v)$. Use the same true rows at their original timestamps in (1), with $\tau=O(L^d)$. Since $T=O(HL^{a+3})$, replay contributes $O(H/L+\sqrt\eta\,H)+o(H)$ and the row terms cost $O((\eta+\sqrt\eta)H)+o(H)$. The public $n_0$ and polylogarithmic terms are $o(H)$. Choosing $\eta$ sufficiently small *after fixing the recognizer and its interpretation constant* gives an honest signal within the hierarchy deadline. The recognizer does not need to compute $\eta$, the shortest rows or the deletion set. It admits all the candidate schedules. This contradiction proves the stated bound.

The earlier shared-pool interface lets a decoder address bits shared among many times. The present argument charges independent descriptions and bulk-selects two of them. For the same prescribed grid it forces at least one independent snapshot of $\Omega(N/L^{3a+d+10})$ bits. The existence of these descriptions at smaller total cost for a general fast solver remains open. Priority for these actual-run consequences has not been established.
