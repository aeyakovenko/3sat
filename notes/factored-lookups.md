# The time cost of evaluating a one-tape history

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** The one-tape two-fifths-space simulation builds an implicit circuit for the controller history of a deterministic computation with time cap T. I tested whether deterministic local replay and the uniqueness of the next controller transcript make its circuit evaluator fast enough for the [simultaneous time and space criterion](simultaneous-space.md). Two distinct costs arise in the published evaluator. ## One local lookup can compute the parity of the number of satisfying assignments

There is a fixed deterministic one-writable-tape machine for which the following task is ⊕SAT-hard under polynomial-time reductions. Given the ordinary input, a polynomially bounded cap, a first-round suffix Y, and field vectors, evaluate the homogeneous extension of one first-round replay flag. The history vector can be the true augmented Boolean initial controller word. Both augmentation coordinates are one, and every non-Boolean data coordinate can take the same field value α. This is a statement about the local kernel on supplied arguments.

Place a CNF F with m variables on the read-only tape. In the block containing the starting writable head, give each variable two data cells. Regard 01 and 10 as its two legal encodings. The machine accepts exactly when every pair is legal and the decoded assignment satisfies F. It performs every legality and clause check, accumulating the answer in finite control. A uniform implementation walks a fixed sweep for each literal. For example the reduction can supply one read-only selection mask per literal, with one position per variable. The head scans that mask in tandem with the pairs and updates the truth value only at its unique marked position. Delimiters and resets depend only on this public layout. It checks the CNF without branching its head movement or stopping time on the stored assignment. The scan uses a polynomial number of steps and stays in O(m+1) writable positions. A binary input alphabet suffices by fixed-length coding of the masks, signs and delimiters. Two data symbols may use codes differing at one bit each under a chosen fixed tape-symbol encoding.

Take the grid offset with a block beginning at the writable origin and choose a polynomial cap large enough for the scan and for block width b=⌈T^(1/5)⌉² to contain all these positions. The scan halts before crossing a boundary. The final writable and read-only head positions and elapsed time can all be computed by running the schedule on dummy data. The two possible final controls record acceptance and rejection. Let Y be the accepting terminal controller repeated for the full first round. A terminal visit keeps its controller unchanged. Fix the initial history to its true value, fix every scratch, marker and padding bit of the raw block to its intended Boolean value, and vary the 2m designated bits. The replay flag for the initial block equals one precisely on the legal encodings that satisfy F. The uniqueness of the reference suffix holds for each of these raw Boolean blocks, including the ones that lead to rejection.

In the homogeneous extension with augmentation one, a Boolean assignment u contributes its flag times a product of zⱼ over its one coordinates and 1−zⱼ over its zero coordinates. Give all 2m designated coordinates the same value α outside {0,1} in the simulation's characteristic-two field. Each satisfying encoding uses exactly m ones, hence the lookup is

    [α(1−α)]^m · (# satisfying assignments of F modulo 2).

The factor is nonzero. Reading a single zero test recovers the parity. In the four-element subfield one may take α²=α+1, making the factor exactly one. Lean checks the resulting lookup identity for every arity and arbitrary Boolean predicate by expanding the two bits of each pair. [Lean proof](../lean/FactoredLookup.lean) The converse for each output bit of polynomially clocked explicit kernels is also elementary. Guess the Boolean arguments, compute the Boolean kernel and the requested bit of its field-weighted contribution, and accept that branch if the bit is one. The parity of the accepting branches is the requested bit, using the binary field basis and a polynomial-time field representation. Weighted Boolean summation is classical, and Williams previously related fast CNF multilinear-extension evaluation to faster satisfiability testing. The specialization above concerns an actual first-round kernel, a Boolean history, and only one distinct non-Boolean field value. [Williams, Section 5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/017/download/)

For normalized arguments with only h non-Boolean coordinates **in total**, enumerating 2ʰ Boolean completions computes the lookup. In particular h=O(log T) gives polynomial time for the bounded replays with ordinary input access. The calculation above shows why bounding only the number of *different field values* leaves a counting problem. It does not prove a time lower bound for parity, or that this argument is encountered from the evaluator's clean initial pool. The published additive-register procedure permits arbitrary initial contents; its interpolation loops then translate those contents by scalar multiples of the true child words. Appropriate initial pool contents realize the hard point at a specified sample. Correctness of the final additive result may allow an implementation to bypass that sample altogether.

## Changing the extension helps some replay predicates

A Boolean formula of size s has an arithmetic extension of size O(s) and degree O(s). Replace AND by multiplication and NOT by 1−x, expressing disjunctions by De Morgan's rule. Count repeated inputs separately in the leaves. Homogenization gives a homogeneous extension with polynomial-size circuit by multiplying lower-degree terms by the needed powers of the augmentation coordinate. This gives the CNF and its pair-legality restriction above a cheap *alternative* extension. The interpolation identity used in the additive evaluator requires homogeneity and agreement on augmented Boolean inputs. Its validity does not depend on multilinearity; the interpolation degrees and field size would have to accommodate the new degree bounds.

A universal version of this proposal has a further consequence. There is a fixed one-writable-tape machine whose first-round flag on a Boolean restriction computes the Circuit Value Problem. At each input length m, put only a fixed public schedule on the read-only tape and the complete length-m circuit description and input bits in the raw writable block. Evaluate them with a fixed polynomial-time deterministic tape machine. Its movements can be made data independent with polynomial overhead: simulate its finite-alphabet space-time diagram for a fixed polynomial number of ticks on two tracks, computing each next row by a full sweep using a constant-size window of the preceding row. Include the simulated state and head position in the cell alphabet and make halted configurations absorbing. A final full sweep reads the accept flag. The sweep lengths and the polynomial number of rounds can be specified on an ordinary public tape of length poly(m). Pin the other tracks and padding to the initial configuration. Increase the polynomial cap so that every writable sweep is internal to the starting block. As before the two possible terminal suffixes depend only on the outcome bit. All public parameters, including the candidate accepting suffix, depend only on m.

Suppose these restricted flags have polynomial-size arithmetic circuits over characteristic-two fields with O(log m)-bit elements computing polynomials of degree poly(m) agreeing on Boolean inputs. No uniform construction is required. Valiant, Skyum, Berkowitz and Rackoff then give polynomial-size arithmetic circuits of depth O(log² m). With a binary field basis and hardwired structure constants, field addition and multiplication use polynomial-size Boolean circuits of polylogarithmic depth. Circuit Value would therefore have nonuniform Boolean NC circuits. Hardwiring a polynomial-size Boolean circuit description into the resulting evaluator for its input length gives NC/poly circuits for the function of its remaining input wires. Thus the universal extension proposal entails P/poly = NC/poly. The case of an individual diagonal machine need not have this consequence. This argument addresses arithmetic circuit representations of low-degree polynomials; arbitrary fast sequential algorithms for evaluating field points do not by themselves supply those circuits. [Arithmetic circuit parallelization](https://doi.org/10.1137/0212043)

## The fixed RAM diagonal admits cheap extensions for its local rules

The universal obstruction can be bypassed for the actual diagonal J in the [simultaneous-resource calculation](simultaneous-space.md). The following variant uses the preprint's abstract history and checkpoint circuit directly. It changes the local kernels, so it makes no claim about the default multilinear lookup of an arbitrary physical one-tape machine.

Let t be a constructible polynomial bound on the number of steps of the *fixed RAM deciding J*, and w=O(log t) its word width. Keep a chronological append-only array of t records of O(w) bits each, allocating one slot per simulated step. Reserve a constant number of records instead if the fixed instruction set has more than one write per step. Unused slots receive a null flag. Each bounded-fan-in RAM instruction is implemented by a constant number of full passes over the array. To read an address, scan the earlier records in time order, carrying the latest matching value and a flag; for an untouched address use the RAM's initial value. An input-word query uses the public binary input. A pass for an append carries the proposed record from the CPU into its predetermined slot. The sweep schedule, including visit addresses, append times and destinations, depends only on t. Fixed dummy passes continue after halt. The state passed between blocks consists of a constant number of data words and public counters. Thus its width is c=O(log t).

Partition the array into blocks of b records. Each pass visits ⌈t/b⌉ blocks. There are R=O(t(1+t/b)) visits. Form epochs of b visits, split into rounds of k visits with b=k², and define the same history H and epoch-start block A as in the preprint. For the power bounds use k=⌈t^(2/5)⌉, b=k² and pad final passes and epochs; E=⌈R/b⌉=O(t^(2/5)). The common word width is O(b log t). The update on semantic words holds by the same local replay induction. At each visit the public schedule specifies the input and output block, and the candidate transcript supplies all controller values. Malformed records may be given the same bitwise transition convention throughout.

For a fixed block, each of its slots is written at most once. At any proposed visit within an epoch, that slot's contents equal either its epoch-start bits or the payload of the incoming controller at the unique earlier append visit in this epoch. Which slice to take is determined entirely by the public indices. This identity holds for bitwise replay even when the proposed controllers are inconsistent. The Lean lemma `readSlot` checks the underlying last-write identity for arbitrary payloads, including repeated writes. [Lean proof](../lean/FactoredLookup.lean) Consequently each visit can be checked directly against H, Y and A. For a read, test the candidate record's address, valid bit and time, AND it with nonmatches of all later eligible records in the block, and OR the surviving value bits. Include the incoming accumulator when the block has no match. An append changes its public slot, and the checkpoint rule reads off the resulting slots by the same selector. Check each outgoing controller and AND all the visit flags. CPU transitions on O(w) bits have polynomial-size bounded-depth truth-table formulas, even for an instruction with nontrivial arithmetic. An indexed bit of the public input can be selected by an OR of address comparisons. All selectors, table predicates and constants can be generated in polynomial time and polylogarithmic work space by iterating over the public indices and simulating the fixed word operations.

This gives Boolean formulas with polynomial size and constant depth of unbounded AND, OR and NOT for each local output bit, including the accumulator checks. Any gates shared in this description may be unfolded with polynomial overhead because the unbounded-fan-in depth is constant. Implement the AND and OR gates by balanced binary trees and arithmetize to get polynomial size and polynomial degree. For separate homogeneity use one augmentation coordinate per word argument. At additions equalize the argument degrees with powers of the respective augmentation coordinates, and at products add the degrees. One may assign each leaf one degree unit per argument and homogenize a subtree to its number of leaves in each argument. Powering a field element uses O(log t) multiplications. The resulting polynomial can be evaluated directly by traversing the balanced expression in polynomial time and polylogarithmic extra space with the supplied argument vectors held read-only. A common polynomial upper degree for all coordinates and predicates is obtained by padding with augmentation powers. The field can be enlarged to exceed the total interpolation degree, including the product over blocks, while retaining O(log t)-bit elements.

Thus one can arrange for **every local arithmetic lookup for this diagonal** to have polynomial time and polylogarithmic auxiliary space on arbitrary field arguments. The weighted-path space account still gives O(t^(4/5) polylog t) bits, since each epoch contributes O(k log t) weight and a word contains O(b log t) bits. Choosing t of order n^(49/40) up to logarithms recovers the separate space exponent 49/50 with fast local lookups. The global schedule still has superpolynomially many calls. This isolates the remaining problem for this representation: how to share history computations or certify their suffixes while retaining the sublinear-in-t space bound.

## Recursive calls still enumerate exponentially many candidate suffixes

Grant the published evaluator a unit-time oracle for every local field lookup. Write k=⌈T^(1/5)⌉, b=k², E=⌈⌈1+T/b⌉/b⌉ and c for the bit width of one controller, including its elapsed-time field. Each history round enumerates all 2^g possible suffixes with g=kc. Fix one outer interpolation sample, one output coordinate, one block i and a nonzero inner interpolation sample. At every epoch e≥1 and for each candidate suffix at H[e,k], the literal schedule calls Add(A[e,i],s',q) at that sample. The checkpoint A[e,i] has the previous epoch's final history H[e−1,k] as a dependency. Its ordinary interpolation loops call Add on that history at least once with a nonzero scalar. All these calls occur regardless of whether replay passes.

Consequently the call tree starting at H[E−1,k] has at least 2^(g(E−1)) descendant calls to H[0,k]. For large T this is 2^Ω(T^(2/5) log T), since E=Θ(T^(1/5)) and c=Ω(log T). This is a lower bound on the operations of that schedule, even for inputs whose modeled machine halts immediately. It does not apply to implementations that reformulate the history computation, skip calls using semantic information, or retain shared results at a new space cost. On the polynomial cap for the RAM diagonal in the simultaneous-resource criterion, this clock exceeds the tolerated 2^O((log n)²/log log n). A faster local replay by itself would leave the recursive enumeration intact. A verifier attaining the required simultaneous bounds for the diagonal remains missing.
