# Opposite SAT verdicts with identical timed RAM replies at bit distance two

An ordered collection of single-bit edits lets one turn a failure of the proposed exhaustion into an algorithm for *arbitrary* SAT. The conversion costs up to one round per variable. In particular, if proper ASCII DIMACS SAT has no quadratic deterministic algorithm, then for **every finite list of unrestricted, possibly incorrect RAM programs** and every fixed deadline $C(N+1)$ with positive integer $C$, arbitrarily large canonical files have the following property. There is a common file and a set of its one-bit neighbors on which every listed program gives the same timed reply across the set. At least $\Omega(\log N)$ of the neighbors are satisfiable and at least $\Omega(\log N)$ are unsatisfiable, where $N$ is the common encoded bit length. The replies include timeouts. Any two neighbors differ in exactly two bits and have identical byte histograms. Each variable occurs at most four times.

Here the unrestricted RAM class permits any sufficiently large fixed positive integer logarithmic word-width coefficient, as in the [interpreter theorem](anchored-interpreter-for-ram-exhaustion.md). The host constructed in the proof can use a larger fixed coefficient than the observers. The same conclusion for a quadratic deadline follows from the cubic exclusion. Under the cubic exclusion at a linear deadline, the multiplicity on *each* side can instead be $\Omega(N/\log N)$. For a fixed list the colliding files of each verdict are a positive fraction of the entire family of one-bit neighbors **designated by the construction**. A decoder even given an arbitrary private truth table for that particular family errs on at least that fraction if it only sees the reply vector.

These implications also admit a growing time multiplier and a growing universal program census, specified below. They are conditional consequences of the proposed unrestricted lower bounds. The reductions and their running-time estimates are unconditional. The list-pruning idea has precedents in work on Mahaney's theorem, left sets and partial information about SAT. The physical distances, large mixed classes of native replies and explicit clock growth are the features tracked here. Priority for their combination has not been established. ## One common formula for ordered cutoffs

Normalize a source $F$ of $n$ input bits to its dense, duplicate-free core. Write $v$ for its number of occurring variables. The number of distinct printed names gives $v=O(1+n/\log(n+2))$, and the same bound holds for the number of clauses after normalization. Order the $2^v$ assignments by their binary integer value. A cutoff $e$ has answer YES if an assignment of value at most $e$ satisfies $F$. On a satisfiable source with least witness $\mu$, the cutoff answer is exactly $[\mu\le e]$.

Given $J$ ordered cutoffs and $U$ copies per cutoff, build one formula with $D=JU$ switch bits. Keep $F$ once. For each cutoff compute $x\le e$ on the same guessed assignment with $O(v+1)$ constant-fan-in gates. Constrain each of its $U$ switches, when true, to imply this *shared* comparison. Convert the gates to proper 3-CNF, padding short clauses with fresh variables forced false if needed. Next use the [signed occurrence copies and the sorted degree-four physical switch construction](physical-selector-parity-cubes.md). Turning on a single switch changes the digit 2 to 3 at its own fixed byte. All other switches stay off. The selected comparison is the only required cutoff. Thus the one-hot neighbors have exactly the desired answers, in monotone order by cutoff. On a satisfiable source their common all-off center is satisfiable. The placements may use separate words for the fixed width coefficients of the observers.

For $J,D\le n^{O(1)}$ the logical size before printing is $O(1+n/\log(n+2)+J(v+1)+D)$. The layout needs linearly many additional names and rows in that count plus $O(n/\log(n+2))$ names if reserved for scale. Minimal decimal identifiers require $O(\log(n+2))$ bits each. We may pad with independent clauses to ensure $N\ge n$, giving
$$
 n\le N=O\bigl((J+1)n+D\log(n+2)\bigr).
$$
The constants are uniform when the polynomial cap on $J,D$ and the width coefficients used for *placement* are fixed. To construct the clauses in native $O(N)$ time, keep the $v$-bit cutoffs as arrays, generate the gate rows, assign consecutive names outside the reserved switch positions, and radix-sort the constant-arity integer tuples by counting sort on the dense name range. Printing digits can use the packed compiler of the layout note or precomputed decimal strings for the consecutive identifiers. Building a fresh copy per timed trial also fits an $O(N)$ budget. The explicit Python fixture below uses short clauses padded by a common false literal before occurrence splitting.

## Dropping intervals using only the observed replies

Let $s$ be the number of programs. A reply is YES, NO or timeout. Their joint value has at most $K=3^s$ possibilities. Start with the empty prefix. At each depth extend every surviving prefix by both bits in increasing order. The resulting list contains at most $2K$ prefixes. Use the **upper endpoint of each prefix interval** as a cutoff, including the endpoints of intervals that follow gaps from previous pruning. Give each cutoff $U=K(r-1)+1$ distinct switches, for a desired multiplicity $r\ge1$. Simulate all the observers to deadline on every one-hot neighbor. For each cutoff select its most frequent joint value, which occurs at least $r$ times. Break ties deterministically, and keep just the first prefix with each chosen value. At most $K$ prefixes survive. At depth $v$ evaluate $F$ on each retained assignment. Accept exactly when a retained assignment passes.

All negative sources are answered correctly. On a positive source on which the algorithm fails, consider the first discarded prefix containing $\mu$. Its endpoint is $e_j\ge\mu$. There must be an earlier prefix with the same chosen reply vector. Its endpoint $e_i$ lies below $\mu$ because the prefix intervals at this depth are disjoint and ordered. All copies of $e_i$ give UNSAT, and all copies of $e_j$ give SAT. At least $r$ of each have the same *entire* reply vector. This is a statement about actual runs on distinct files even for programs that return false answers elsewhere. Any globally sound program in the list must time out on that whole mixed class. No global interpretation of a color is used across different lists, lengths or source formulas. [Lean check of the loss of a least witness](../lean/OrderedCutoffPruning.lean)

### A certificate for an exhaustion search using only a satisfying assignment

For an actual search on a finite list, enumerate pairs $(F,w)$ where $w$ satisfies $F$. Recompute the deterministic pruning run, returning a certificate when the terminal list contains no satisfying assignment. The certificate consists only of $F$ and $w$, since the verifier can rerun all bounded trials and evaluate the retained assignments. On acceptance, the queried files contain a mixed class because the least witness exists and is lost somewhere. The verifier needs no UNSAT oracle and need not know the least witness. If the observer list is the complete anchor census, then each representative already fails on at least one of the queried files, regardless of whether its output on a particular file was sound. Supplying the anchor covers the remaining parameters. This certifies the existence of the three-file obstruction among the queried inputs. To actually name its two opposite members one can find the least witness by a separate offline search and inspect the first lost interval. Even when the source and a satisfying assignment are given, the proof above does not predict which stage will supply such a certificate for an unrestricted clock.

The audit runs this search to completion on a source with a known satisfying assignment of integer value 5. The tested observer loads word zero, masks its low ten bits, loads from that address, masks the low bit and branches. These are five stored instructions. After 24 timed trials at three depths the search rejects the satisfiable source. The first lost interval has upper endpoint 7 and an earlier cutoff is 3. The program gave the same reply on copies with both verdicts. This is a complete failure certificate for this observer.

At the smallest nontrivial arity, the audit exhausts **all 256 subsets** of the eight sign clauses on three variables. It executes the same observer on 6,120 generated files. The search gives 127 false negatives. The single clause $(x_1\lor x_2\lor x_3)$ already supplies a failing satisfiable source, and the empty core is accepted. One clause is therefore minimal for failure of this particular pruning procedure and observer. Its second LOAD address is determined by the public first word. Each packed word contains at most one switch, so at least three of the four copies of any cutoff get the center reply. Every cutoff has the same majority at that depth and the search always keeps only the all-zero prefix. Of the 128 subsets that include the positive clause, only the full eight-clause set is unsatisfiable. This accounts for all 127 failures. The stated failures are the pruning algorithm's decisions on source formulas. The observer runs on the generated cutoff files. Run `python3 ordered_cutoff_pruning_audit.py --full-three-variable` with `pycosat` installed to reproduce the full census.

The same argument applies to arbitrary deterministic adaptive observers whose combined center runs inspect at most $p$ original data words. Choose more than $2p$ copies per cutoff in distinct words. Each unseen active word reproduces the center replies even if a machine overwrites it before its first read. A strict majority of every cutoff group then has the center vector, making the same one-clause source fail. The probe budget counts accesses on the actual baseline of each host. At linear time per encoded bit a RAM has enough time to visit every designated word. A [separate exact tradeoff](one-hot-data-after-a-streaming-pass.md) also permits prior information from a streaming pass.

For a fixed list and constant multiplier, take $r=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil$ at deadlines $O(N^q)$ with $q=1$ or $2$. Each trial costs $O(N^q)$ including its initialization, $J\le2K$, $N=\Theta(n)$ and there are $O(r)$ trials per depth. The complete search costs $O(n^{q+1})$. Under the corresponding time exclusion it must fail at arbitrarily large source lengths, since one can hardwire answers at any finite set of smaller lengths. For a linear deadline and $r=\lceil n/\log_2(n+2)\rceil$, the same calculation gives $O(n^3/\log^2(n+2))$ time. More generally, for $r=1$ even a deadline $O(N^q\log(N+2))$ fits the $O(n^{q+1})$ search. In the mixed family any positive and negative copy together with the [short UNSAT anchor](three-files-for-the-unrestricted-sat-clock.md) is a defeating triple if the list includes all the anchor's complete parameter representatives and the anchor itself is chosen for that deadline. One physical bit of distance could never suffice for identical joint replies once physical bit parity is observed.

Taking all polynomial exponents gives an equivalence. SAT lacks a polynomial-time deterministic RAM algorithm if and only if, for every fixed positive integer $q$, multiplier and finite list of RAM programs, arbitrarily long one-hot families as above contain a distance-two opposite pair with identical replies through that polynomial deadline. The forward implication uses the same fixed-list construction for exponent $q$ and its $O(n^{q+1})$ search. In the reverse direction a singleton list containing a polynomial-time solver separates the two verdicts at its own exponent and sufficiently large multiplier. In this equivalence the host width coefficient may depend on the fixed exponent. At one prescribed exponent the loss of one assignment-search round per variable remains.

## How fast the universal clock can grow on the hard lengths

This version bounds the relation between a time multiplier and the lengths where a witness is forced. Fix $q=1$ or $2$. For each positive integer $C$ use the width-four template and anchor $A_C$ for the deadline $C(N+1)^q$. Write $L_C$ for the bit length of the anchor. Enumerate **all** two-bit terminated descriptions at its visible width, including those that fail the anchor, with the first terminator as the end of the description. Their number $s(C)$ is at most $O(L_C^2)$ and $L_C=O(\log(C+2)\log\log(C+4))$. Every parameter passing the anchor matches one representative at all lengths. These are counts for the particular fixed universal template, which simulates any fixed solver at its own constant cost. [Interpreter and prefix count](anchored-interpreter-for-ram-exhaustion.md)

For a source of length $n$ choose $b=\lfloor(\log_2(n+2))^{1/10}\rfloor$ and take the largest integer $C\le b$ such that $K=3^{s(C)}\le b$. For the finitely many initial sizes where there is no such $C$, solve the source by exhaustive search. A uniform algorithm generates the small anchors, evaluates capped powers, and enumerates prefixes only for the chosen $C$. Integer roots and bit lengths may replace real logarithms by their floors. The count bound gives, at large $n$ and some fixed $c>0$,
$$
 C\ \ge\ 2^{c\sqrt{\log\log n}/\log\log\log n}.
$$
Indeed, substituting this size of $C$ gives $s(C)\le c'\log\log n$ with $c'$ as small as needed. The finite stage computation costs at most $O(b\,\mathrm{polylog}(n+2))$.

Run the pruning procedure with $r=b$. Now $J\le2b$, $D\le2b^3$, $N=O(bn)$, and there are at most $b$ representative programs and multiplier $C\le b$. All simulations are of one fixed width-four code with a different immediate. The host can use a sufficiently large *fixed* width coefficient. Each simulation gets a fresh, initially zero memory region of the guest address-space size, with the packed input copied to its beginning. The number of simulations, their largest address and hence the total address range are polynomial in $n$ even when $b=n^{1/10}$. The host separates regions by arithmetic on their bases, so it need never spend their address-space size on initialization. After setting up the width, each operation on a width-four guest word takes a bounded number of host instructions because the guest code is fixed and its parameter fits. This avoids a simulation constant depending on a growing list of arbitrary programs. Computing the visible widths and deadlines costs at most $O(N)$ per setup. Tallying the colors as base-three integers costs $O(s(C))$ per copy. Store their counts in indexed arrays of size $K\le b$.

The total time, including construction and the terminal assignment checks, is
$$
 O\!\left(n+\frac{n}{\log(n+2)}\,b^5(bn)^q\right)=O(n^{q+1}).
$$
Thus the exclusion of $O(n^{q+1})$ forces arbitrarily large common lengths $N$ with a mixed class containing $b$ copies of each verdict and identical full template signatures at deadline $C(N+1)^q$. The multiplier tends to infinity at least as fast as $2^{\Omega(\sqrt{\log\log N}/\log\log\log N)}$ **along these lengths**, and $b=\Omega((\log N)^{1/10})$. The comparisons between the clocks at different lengths use the same chosen stage $C$ throughout each host. The claim about $N$ follows from $n\le N=O(bn)$.

Under the cubic exclusion, take $q=1$ and $b=\lfloor n^{1/10}\rfloor$ instead. The displayed time is $O(n^{2.6})$ for large $n$. Here $C\ge2^{\Omega(\sqrt{\log N}/\log\log N)}$ by the same count estimate, and the class has $\Omega(N^{1/11})$ copies of each verdict. This last conclusion already follows from the exclusion of $O(n^{2.6})$. The result locates collisions at cofinally growing multipliers under the quantified time hypothesis. It gives no unconditional instance on which every representative fails at every multiplier. An explicit generator that supplies those ordered pairs would still have to establish agreement for programs that inspect the clauses and the cutoff computation.

Its 15 physical one-hot files each have 68,689 bytes. In one class of the parity of every other byte, at least four files have each SAT verdict. The audit parses the inputs, verifies the positive assignments and checks the negative cases with PicoSAT.
