# Predicting the actual forecast from a periodic linear rule and its errors

Fix a binary prefix, and append $h$ zero bits. The resulting width is $W=w_0 B$ for a fixed positive integer $w_0$ and $B=2^h$. Its bit length is $n=h+O(1)$. The forecast has a constant number of taps, with one lag $AB+c$ for fixed $A>0$ and fixed signed $c$. The observation time $H=PkW^5+s$ is less than $B^6$ for every sufficiently large $h$. Use the actual initialized binary stream, including its two fixed negative seed positions. [Definition of the forecast](one-variable-delay-and-one-binary-product.md)

A period-$q$ predictor gives a subset of the physical taps for each residue modulo $q$. At tick $t$ it predicts the XOR of that subset using the **true** preceding stream bits. Let $e$ count wrong predictions on $0\le t\le H$. The subsets can vary with the width. They need not identify which bank made a product zero. Then the entire actual table $t\mapsto z_t$, through $H$, has a bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit of size at most
$$
                (e+1)(q+1)^{30}\operatorname{poly}(n).
$$
The exponent 30 is a convenient upper bound. We take $1\le q\le H+1$, which suffices on this finite interval. In particular, a fixed-period rule with a short error list gives a circuit nearly linear in the number of errors up to factors polynomial in $\log W$. No locations of errors need be generated to use the nonuniform conclusion. An algorithm for recovering the errors from the rule alone is given below.

## Splitting the series by phase

First supply the $e$ error times as impulses. The actual stream is the unique solution through $H$ of the resulting *linear* periodic recurrence. For phase $j$ put $Z_j(x)=\sum_{t\ge0,\ t\equiv j\ (q)}z_t x^t$. Extend the linear recurrence beyond $H$ with no additional impulses. The vector $Z$ solves
$$
    (I-M(x))Z=S(x),\qquad
    M_{j,k}(x)=\sum_{a\text{ enabled at }j,\ k\equiv j-a\ (q)}x^a.
$$
Each exception at time $u$ adds $x^u$ to source phase $u\bmod q$. Each negative seed at $v$ and tap $a$ contributes $x^{v+a}$ exactly when $v+a\ge0$ and that tap is enabled at its phase. The total number of these seed contributions is bounded by a constant. Cancellations are taken in $\mathbb F_2$. Every lag is positive, so $Q=\det(I-M)$ has constant coefficient one. A unit impulse at $u$ contributes $x^u P_{u\bmod q}(x)/Q(x)$ to the sum of the phase series, where $P_j$ is the sum of the entries of column $j$ of the adjugate. Thus $e+O(1)$ shifted coefficients of rational series suffice.

Every monomial in $Q$ and in each $P_j$ has exponent $vB+u'$ with $|u'|,v=O(q)$ and $v\ge0$. Indeed it is a product of at most $q$ entries drawn from a constant alphabet of delay monomials and the identity. There are $O((q+1)^2)$ exponent pairs per polynomial. Retain their coefficients modulo two even when different pairs specialize to the same integer exponent. The coefficients can be constructed in time polynomial in $q,n$. For instance replace $x^{AB+c}$ by $X^cY^A$ and every short lag by its power of $X$. Clear the fixed negative powers in each row by multiplying by a fixed power of $X$. The determinants and minors then have degree $O(q)$ in either variable. Evaluate on a grid of distinct nonzero points in a binary extension field of size $O(q+1)$ with sufficiently large constant, take ordinary determinants there, and interpolate in both variables. Undo the known row shifts. Field operations and constructing this small field cost polynomial time in $q+1$, including brute search for an irreducible of logarithmic degree if desired. This calculation uses no univariate array of length $W$. In the bit circuit the resulting finite supports may instead be hardwired.

## Folding the binary carries

Since $Q(0)=1$ and the characteristic is two,
$$
  \frac1{Q(x)}\equiv \prod_{i=0}^{6h-1} Q(x^{2^i})
                       \pmod{x^{2^{6h}}}.
$$
Multiplication by $Q(x)$ telescopes via Frobenius to $Q(x)^{2^{6h}}$, which is one to the required precision. To find one coefficient at $t<B^6$, choose one exponent pair $(u_i,v_i)$ at each factor. Count modulo two the choices satisfying $\sum_i2^i(u_i+Bv_i)=t$. Signed values of $u_i$ are allowed.

Write $i=jh+k$ with $0\le j<6$, $0\le k<h$. Process the six **factor rows at the same within-block bit position** together at each $k$. There are seven numerical blocks $j=0,\ldots,6$, with the last one accounting for the $v_i$ from the sixth factor row. Its target bits are all zero. At position $k$ in block $j$ add $u_{jh+k}$ from its factor row if $j<6$, $v_{(j-1)h+k}$ if $j>0$, and the current signed carry. The parity must match bit $jh+k$ of $t$. Subtract that bit and divide by two to obtain the next carry. Guess the six initial carries for blocks 1 through 6. Start block zero with zero carry. At the end require each block's outgoing carry to equal the initial carry of the next one, and the seventh block's outgoing carry to be zero. All carries have magnitude $O(q+1)$. For any successful choices the actual carries are unique, by scanning the numerical blocks in ordinary low-to-high order. Conversely these equations with zero exterior carries imply the desired integer sum, including for negative local offsets.

For each guessed boundary tuple keep the parity of paths into each current seven-carry tuple. There are $O((q+1)^6)$ boundary tuples, $O((q+1)^7)$ current tuples and $O((q+1)^{12})$ choices of factor terms in a column. The allowed target bit pattern and the outgoing carries of each transition are fixed by the tuples and terms. Thus XOR and Boolean selection gates implement one column with $O((q+1)^{25}\operatorname{poly}(n))$ gates, including copies and wires. After $h$ columns sum the permitted terminal parities. To handle numerators and sources evaluate the coefficient at $t-u-d$ for each numerator exponent $d$ and source time $u$. An out-of-range or negative index gives zero. The integer subtraction and range test cost $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ per term. There are $O((e+1)(q+1)^2)$ shifted terms. This proves the claimed circuit bound. ## Finding the error positions from the rule alone

The preceding circuit uses the error times as constants. They can also be recovered deterministically in time polynomial in $q,n,e+1$. Start with any proposed list of impulses and form the corresponding candidate sequence $y$. Regard the carry calculation as a length-$h$ weighted branching program over $\mathbb F_2$. At layer $k$ its transition matrix is chosen by the six timestamp bits $k+jh$, $0\le j<6$. Its output is a row vector times the selected matrices times a terminal column. We call this a folded representation. It has width and generation cost polynomial in $q,n$ per shifted coefficient. Arithmetic coefficient evaluation over finite fields and equivalence of linear representations are established tools. The use here of a growing lag with an $h$-bit offset, and the recovery of the errors in the initialized nonlinear recurrence, require the carry and causality arguments. [Bostan, Christol and Dumas on coefficient evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00545) · [Kiefer on weighted-automaton equivalence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.01217)

To compute a shifted coefficient at $t-s$ in the same column order, guess the five boundary borrows between the six blocks. At each layer subtract the six known bits of $s$ with their current borrows, feed the resulting digits to the unshifted representation, and advance the borrows. Match all five boundaries at the end, with zero initial borrow at the low end and zero final borrow at the high end. Each allowed subtraction follows exactly one consistent borrow path. A shift outside $[0,B^6)$ contributes zero. A direct sum adds the terms from the various source times and numerator monomials. To represent $y_{t-a}$, shift its nonnegative part and add the finitely many indicators $t=a+v$ with $v$ a negative seed. Equality to a fixed timestamp is a constant-width test. The failed-recurrence bit is
$$
       y_t+
          \left(\sum_{a\in L(W)}y_{t-a}\right)
          \left(\sum_{b\in R}y_{t-b}\right).
$$

An ordinary tensor product of two linear representations implements their pointwise product in the same column order. Multiply by the interval indicator $0\le t\le H$, using the same constant-number-of-blocks borrow test. The resulting representation has polynomial width in $q,e+1$.

To check whether a word of $h$ columns makes the scalar output nonzero, propagate the linear span of the reachable row vectors from the initial row. At each layer take the span of the images of a basis under the allowed symbol matrices, and at the last layer test against the terminal column. This is exact by linearity. To recover the least failing timestamp, try fixing its binary digits to zero in decreasing order of their *integer significance*, keeping zero exactly when a nonzero output remains possible. Recompute the same span test with the selected digit constraints at their original layers. The column reading order need not coincide with significance order. There are $6h$ such tests.

Start with no impulses. If $t$ is the first failure, the candidate already equals the true stream at every earlier tick by deterministic induction. Adding an impulse at $t$ flips that bit and leaves all earlier bits unchanged because every lag is positive. Previously inserted impulses remain correct. The failures therefore occur in strictly increasing order and are precisely the disagreements of the supplied rule on the true history. After $e$ insertions one final span test finds none. The result is the exception list and a polynomial-size evaluator, with no need to print $H+1$ individual bits. Lean verifies prefix agreement and repair for arbitrary strictly causal Boolean rules. On four small nonlinear runs the folded test located all 39 errors, matching direct simulation on 5,314 timestamp checks and 1,032 interval checks. The time bound and folded construction are manuscript arguments. [Lean proof of first-defect repair](../lean/ForecastFirstDefect.lean) · [Folded recovery audit](../audits/forecast_defect_recovery_audit.py)

One can include a phase bias $b_j$ in the rule. Its source in phase $j$ is $b_jx^j/(1-x^q)$. Multiplying the common denominator by $1-x^q$ and clearing that factor in all numerators preserves the $O(q)$ coordinate ranges and polynomial running time. In the zero-bias circuit bound both the source count and the displayed power of $q$ refer to the original XOR-subset rules.

## A concrete test for each proposed SAT clock

Fix $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ and the solver-independent sequence of zero-padded forecast widths from the learned-clock construction. If ordinary SAT has a deterministic $O(N^k)$ unrestricted-workspace RAM solver, then **every** period-$q$ linear predictor on these widths satisfies
$$
                (e+1)(q+1)^{30}
                    \ \ge\ W^{1/k^2}/n^{O(1)}
$$
for every sufficiently large width. This is the contrapositive of the circuit lower bound there, using a circuit computing all times with a suitable value outside the horizon. For a fixed period it requires roughly $W$, $W^{1/4}$ or $W^{1/9}$ wrong predictions respectively, up to polynomial factors in $\log W$. The period, the rule and its error locations may all be nonuniform. Finding predictors violating the inequality by an exponential-in-$n$ factor infinitely often would refute the corresponding SAT clock. The argument does not establish that the actual learned prefix admits such predictors. [Exact hypothesis, prefix and double use of SAT](one-fixed-forecast-prefix-for-clocked-sat.md)

## A stronger error threshold for a fixed period

Use a new fixed guest with two procedures. The first is the earlier search for whole-history circuits by bounded nondeterministic acceptance, with the partial-certificate verifier below installed in its optional directly checked branch. The second recovers successive exceptions, using ordinary SAT for its existence-of-a-failure tests. Both report success only on a certified zero. On an arbitrary input the latter can take $h$ to be the number of trailing zero bits of $W$ and $w_0$ its odd part, and abandon this procedure if $h=0$ or $H\ge 2^{6h}$. On the zero-padded widths of the resulting header the odd part is constant, and this choice only changes $h$ by a constant relative to the specified padding. Thus the code is fixed before its own header is determined.

For the second procedure, write $S_i$ for the $i$ already established error times of a trial rule. Generate a circuit for the candidate stream with just these impulses, including every shifted numerator and the negative seed cases. For fixed $q$ its construction work and number of gates are $(i+1)n^{O(1)}$: the constant-width coefficient representation depends on the fixed odd part and rule, and each impulse adds a constant number of shifted copies. Its defect circuit asks if there is a time $t\le H$ satisfying the nonlinear mismatch and any required fixed leading timestamp bits. The Boolean formula for that query, including proper decimal 3-CNF names, can be generated in $(i+1)n^{O(1)}$ steps and bits for $i\le H+1$, since $\log(H+2)=O(n)$. Use $O(n)$ adaptive SAT queries to recover the first such time, or one to establish absence of defects. This determines the correct endpoint after $e$ insertions. Under the $O(N^k)$ SAT hypothesis the learned correct clock answers all these trials for every sufficiently large width. The complete successful fixed-rule path for a zero endpoint costs
$$
                   (e+1)^{k+1} n^{O(1)}
$$
wide steps by summing the bounds for $i=0,\ldots,e$. This estimate includes regenerating the graphs and serializing the files. The truth of the entire corrected forecast is checked even when some early certificate was too small to settle the verdict.

Enumerate all finite binary descriptions in shortlex order, skipping malformed rule encodings immediately. A concrete common schedule uses stages $j=0,\ldots,n$, running the first $j+1$ trials and the earlier whole-history search from their starts for at most $2^j$ steps each. Assign fresh simulation memory to each restart. Fixed-program step accounting on the wide guest, or a step simulation with polynomial-in-$n$ overhead, suffices. The $o(W/n)$ startup for the common selected solver is shared across all trials. An individual static rule that terminates within $t$ steps is reached in $t n^{O(1)}+n^{O(1)}$ total work for all sufficiently large widths, whenever the displayed budget is relevant. For fixed $q$ there are only finitely many different rules; hence the rule may be chosen independently at each width among *all* rules of that period.

If $O(N^k)$ SAT exists, success is sound on every sufficiently large width of this common guest's own prefix. Its observation is consequently zero, and a subdeadline successful execution would latch one, a contradiction. The arbitrary-circuit criterion applies here too because the first procedure is included. For fixed $q$ the phase biases add only constantly many shifted terms to the coefficient circuit after clearing $1-x^q$. Combining the two time estimates, for each fixed $q$ there is a constant $C_q$ such that every period-$q$ affine rule on every sufficiently large zero-padded width of **this** prefix incurs
$$
           e+1 \ \ge\ \frac{W^{1/\min(k^2,k+1)}}{n^{C_q}}.
$$
The respective exponents for linear, quadratic and cubic clocks are $1,1/3,1/4$. Thus, for example, proving that a fixed period attains $O(W^{1/4-\epsilon})$ errors infinitely often on the cubic-clock prefix, for some $0<\epsilon<1/4$, would exclude that SAT clock. Neither finite samples nor prediction on a different program prefix supplies this missing infinitely-often upper bound.

## A finite combinatorial witness for a necessary condition

There is a way to test this specific error condition directly on a trace. Partition the times by a fixed period $q$. In one phase write $c_{00},c_{01},c_{10},c_{11}$ for the counts of the four true two-bank parity pairs. Among affine functions of the **two bank parities**, the exact minimum error count in this phase is $\min c_{ij}$. Each of $0,x,y,1+x+y$ differs from $xy$ on just one of the four points, a different point in each case. Every affine function differs somewhere, since its four truth-table values sum to zero while those of $xy$ sum to one. Summing the minima over the phases gives the exact optimum for this restricted family and an upper bound on the optimum with individual tap bits available. Thus a fast SAT clock would require, at some phase of every sufficiently large canonical width on the common guest, at least $W^{1/\min(k^2,k+1)}/n^{O_q(1)}$ occurrences of **each of the four pairs in that same phase**. With $q$ equal to the fixed physical slot period, a phase outside the gate slots cannot witness this, since the output is always zero there. This pins the necessary condition to a gate slot.

For the full family let $m$ be the fixed number of individual taps. Write $u_t$ for the $(m+1)$-bit vector consisting of a one and those true tap bits at time $t$. Call a subset $J$ of one phase inconsistent if $\bigoplus_{t\in J} u_t=0$ and $\bigoplus_{t\in J} z_t=1$. Let $b$ be the maximum number of pairwise disjoint such subsets, each of size at most $m+2$, over all phases. If $e_*$ is the smallest possible error count over all affine phase rules then
$$
                         b\ \le\ e_*\ \le\ (m+2)b.
$$
Any rule must err in each inconsistent subset. Conversely, inconsistency of the remaining equations $u_t\cdot v=z_t$ in a phase puts the final unit vector in the span of the augmented rows $(u_t,z_t)$. Express it in a basis to get an inconsistent subset of size at most $m+2$. Repeatedly delete the whole subset until the remaining equations in all phases are consistent. The final rules err at most as many times as the total number deleted, proving the upper bound. This gives a packing criterion to try to upper-bound along an infinite sequence of actual widths. A packing found by exhaustion at any finite collection of widths supplies only evidence about those widths.

For the specific controller compilation using ordinary two-input AND and NOT gates, this count can be exact even for individual taps. Take $q$ a multiple of the physical slot period. At an AND slot with exactly one intended incoming tap in each bank, the other physical taps read zero by two-sum isolation. Each occurrence of each of the four input pairs therefore supplies an inconsistent quadruple when grouped with one occurrence of each other pair. This gives $\min c_{ij}$ disjoint quadruples. Off slots use the zero predictor, and at copy, import, constant and NOT gates one bank is known, or the two incoming values agree, so an affine predictor is exact there. For a wiring using only these gates the sum over ordinary AND phases of $\min c_{ij}$ equals both $e_*$ and $b$. The general inequalities above apply also when a gate multiplies two nontrivial parities of several signals.

## Verifying a partial description of the actual stream

Fix the physical tap banks and put $n=|\operatorname{bin}W|$ on widths $W=H_0 2^h$ for fixed $H_0$. A certificate supplies two length-$h$ layered binary linear representations of width at most $w$, in the same six-block folded order. Their bits $K_0(t),K_1(t)$ pin the stream only at marked times in $[0,H]$. Check that the pins are disjoint there and that $K_0(H)=1$. At negative times extend the pins by the **actual** two-seed initialization, supplying exactly one label. For a bank $B$ and a value $j$, build the following list of Boolean functions of $t$: the indicator that $\sum_{a\in B}K_1(t-a)=j$ in $\mathbb F_2$, and one unpinned indicator $1+K_0(t-a)+K_1(t-a)$ per tap. Some list entry is one exactly when the bank can attain parity $j$ consistently with the pins: without any free bit its parity is forced, and a free predecessor can flip the parity. For a claimed zero, test that $K_0(t)UV$ vanishes throughout the horizon for **each** pair of possible-one list entries $U,V$ from the two banks. Their disjoint tap sets make the two choices compatible. For a claimed one, test $K_1(t)U$ for every possible-zero entry from either bank. These are separate zero tests, so alternative witnesses cannot cancel in characteristic two. Induction on $t$ proves that every accepted pin is correct. Intermediate times may carry no pins.

Writing the supplied dense matrices and boundary vectors takes $O(h(w+1)^2+\log(w+2))$ bits and that many native steps when their entries are given. Shifted pins use the fixed-number-of-blocks subtraction transducer, the underflow test and explicit tests for the two negative seeds. The parity and free-bit alternatives use direct sums and have width $O_r(w+1)$, where $r=|L\cup R|$ is fixed. Every product has at most three factors, hence width $u=O_r((w+1)^3)$, including the fixed interval test. Propagating a basis of reachable row vectors through the 64 possible digit columns at each layer and eliminating over $\mathbb F_2$ costs $O_r(hu^3)$ elementary operations. Including the overlap, endpoint, serialization and horizon checks, the deterministic logarithmic-word RAM verifier takes $T=O_r(n^{O(1)}+h(w+1)^9)$ steps. Ranges and dimensions are checked against the explicit description before allocating scratch space; a sufficiently large fixed multiple of $\log(n+|p|+2)$ bits per word addresses the arrays for a certificate string $p$. The ordinary bounded-run CNF for guessing and checking a certificate can be printed with construction work and encoded length $O((T+n+|p|)\operatorname{polylog}(T+n+|p|+2))$ at the chosen budget. The existence condition below does not require an algorithm to discover the matrices.

The common guest above installs this verifier in its directly checked branch **before choosing its prefix**, using the same trailing-zero convention and declining this branch if the horizon does not fit. Under deterministic $O(N^k)$ SAT, $k=1,2,3$, the existing single bounded-acceptance query forces guessed length plus checking time at least $W^{1/k}/n^{O(1)}$ at every sufficiently large zero-padded width of that guest's own prefix. Only the outer SAT call incurs the exponent $k$. Thus for any $\epsilon>0$, existence on infinitely many of those eventually-zero widths of partial certificates with $w\le 2^{(1/(9k)-\epsilon)n}$ would exclude that SAT clock. Correctness of the checker supplies no such width upper bound. [Once-checked proof branch and prefix quantifiers](one-fixed-forecast-prefix-for-clocked-sat.md)

At fixed certificate width the infinite-padding question admits a finite algebraic check, including if the layer matrices change with the width. [Finite lifts and an explicit calibration](finite-lifts-for-forecast-certificates.md)
