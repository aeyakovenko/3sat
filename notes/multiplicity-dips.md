# A one-witness advantage near the first omitted output

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Let M=2ⁿ and let v be the first n-bit word omitted by the native RAM interpreter at its B=M²⁰ cutoff, on all names shorter than n. Compare the number of passing short proofs at v to the counts at its actual predecessors. False positives at v are allowed. Neither the length of the useful interval nor the number of false positives needs to be supplied to the decoder. [Native source and interpreter costs](native-missing-word.md)

## The multiplicity test

Let W(z) count the R-bit strings accepted by a fixed verifier on (n,z), with 1≤R≤M. Assume the verifier circuit of size M·poly(n) and R are uniformly constructible in M·poly(n) time. Under linear-time SAT there is a constant C, depending on this verifier and the solver, such that for all sufficiently large n and **every** 1≤K≤v,

    min over v−K≤y<v of (W(y)+1) ≤ (W(v)+1) · 2^[C(R+1)/(K+1)].       (1)

Thus finding even one such verifier for which, infinitely often, all predecessors in one trailing interval have **at least one more** passing proof than v, with K/[(R+1)(W(v)+1)] unbounded on those instances, would exclude linear SAT. This follows from log₂(1+1/a)≥1/[(a+1)ln 2] for a≥1. A constant number of exceptional predecessors can also be allowed as described below.

Make exactly one extra witness for each label by extending its witness space with a flag and accepting just one string in the new branch. Take s=max(1,⌊M/(R+1)⌋) independent copies of the enlarged relation. Then the count is exactly (W(z)+1)ˢ. The amplified circuit has O(M²poly(n)) size and at most M+1 witness bits. Round its base-two log count to an integer b(z) with additive error bounded by an absolute constant, as justified below. All b(z) lie in [0,S] for S=s(R+1)+O(1).

For any finite array b in this range, consider all integer thresholds t=1,…,S. At an index z with b(z)<t, let gₜ(z) be the distance to the preceding index with b<t, using −1 as the previous index at the first crossing. Here a crossing means an index below the threshold, even if the adjacent index was also below it. Put gₜ(z)=0 otherwise. The gaps telescope, so Σ_z gₜ(z)≤M. At index z write D for the positive part of min_{z−K≤y<z} b(y)−b(z). For each t from b(z)+1 to b(z)+D one has gₜ(z)≥K+1. Consequently the scores

    H(z)=max over 1≤K≤z of (K+1) · max(0, min_{z−K≤y<z} b(y)−b(z))

have total at most MS. Give H(0) the value zero. Sort by decreasing score, breaking ties by increasing position. The one-based rank of a positive-score position is at most MS/H(z). The table, scores, and ranks are computable under the hypothesis within B steps of the native interpreter. A fixed decoding program needs only a raw suffix of ⌈log₂(rank)⌉ bits giving rank−1. The definition of v therefore forces H(v)≤C₀ S. After allowing for the constant errors in both b values and dividing by s, this gives (1), since 1/s=O((R+1)/M). The same calculation with S−b gives the reverse bound on an upward excursion relative to the preceding maximum. Lean verifies the conservation of each threshold budget and the charge for an arbitrary trailing window. [Lean proof](../lean/MultiplicityDips.lean)

For a fixed number d of exceptions, use the distance from z to the (d+1)-st preceding index with b<t, again using −1 for a missing index. Across all z below t, the sum of these extended gaps is at most (d+1)M: each ordinary gap is charged at most d+1 times. In windows K>d replace the minimum by the (d+1)-st smallest preceding b. The same argument gives (1) with the (d+1)-st smallest actual count on the left and a constant allowed to depend on d. It is not necessary to identify the exceptional predecessors.

Two explicit verifiers attain the exponent's order at the actual v. For example let R=M, K=2^⌊n/2⌋ and D=⌊R/(2K)⌋. Give the two constructions cyclic phases (z mod 2K) and ((z+K) mod 2K). For either phase j set q=D(2K−1−j) and accept the nonzero witnesses whose last R−q bits are zero. This gives W(z)+1=2^q and needs only R·poly(n) uniform circuit size. At each z≥K one of the two phases is at least K. Its preceding K positions lie in the same decreasing part of the cycle, with minimum count ratio exactly 2^D after adding one. A fixed native program with a raw payload prints every sufficiently short binary value left-padded to n bits, so v≥K eventually. At least one of these two fixed verifiers realizes the displayed dip at v at infinitely many lengths. This example needs no knowledge of v and explains why a large absolute decline alone is insufficient.

## Unknown false-positive count and a cubic SAT solver

There is a stronger route for an **additive** advantage. It works even under the cubic-time SAT hypothesis. Let i=W(v), and write dₖ for the number of positions in [v−K,v) having at most i witnesses. With the same uniform verifier and source, cubic SAT entails, for all sufficiently large n and all 1≤K≤v,

    (K+1)/(dₖ+1) ≤ C₂ (i+1) [log₂(i+2)]².                            (2)

For any supplied integer i≤M, tabulate the positions z where W(z)≤i. The complementary predicate asks for i+1 pairwise distinct witnesses. Include the acceptance circuit for each and compare each pair; the explicit encoded SAT query has size O(M³poly(n)) for R≤M. Tabulate the M answers in at most M¹⁰poly(n) time under cubic SAT. Sort the tabulated positions by their best trailing ratio of window length to the number of tabulated positions in the window ending there, with the current position counted. At threshold D, at most M/D such positions qualify, by the one-sided gap inequality in the earlier interval proof. The actual v has ratio at least (K+1)/(dₖ+1) for the supplied value i=W(v). The ranking postprocessing fits M³poly(n) time. [Gap ranking](predecessor-batches.md)

Encode i before the raw rank payload, using a fixed prefix code of length at most log₂(i+1)+2log₂log₂(i+2)+O(1). For example, prepend an Elias gamma code for the bit length of i+1 and omit the known leading 1 of that integer. If the ratio exceeded the right side of (2) for a sufficiently large fixed C₂, the rank would fit the remaining bits of a name shorter than n. Running the described decoder would print v before B. For i>M, (2) is automatic. The threshold is chosen after the actual omitted output is known; the verifier does not receive it.

Accordingly, a verifier for which (K+1)/[(dₖ+1)(W(v)+1)log²(W(v)+2)] is unbounded along some sequence of lengths and windows would exclude O(L³) deterministic SAT on L encoded input bits, hence also O(L²). This allows adversarial false-positive witnesses at v and does not require identifying which predecessors have an extra witness. For a known computable i(n)≤M obtained within M¹⁰poly(n) time, its fixed program replaces the prefix code, reducing that factor to a constant at lengths where W(v)=i(n).

### A sharper packing lemma when every predecessor gains a witness

When dₖ=0, the two logarithms in (2) can both be removed. For any array of nonnegative integer labels b(0),…,b(M−1), let g(z) be the distance to the last preceding position with label at most b_z, taking that position to be −1 if none exists. The corresponding nearest-smaller query has long been studied in sequence algorithms. The tail estimate below also uses the numerical value of the label. Priority for this estimate has not been established. [Related nearest-smaller queries](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.0395)

For every integer t≥1,

    #{z : g(z) ≥ t(b_z+1)} ≤ ⌈M/t⌉.                                    (3)

Partition the positions into consecutive blocks of length t with a possibly shorter final block. Let mⱼ be the minimum label of block j, numbered starting at 0. Define the integer clearance

    Lⱼ = min({j+1} ∪ {mⱼ₋ₐ+a : 1≤a≤j}).

A qualifying label i=b_z in block j satisfies i<Lⱼ. First, g(z)≤z+1≤(j+1)t gives i≤j. Next, if i≥max(mⱼ₋ₐ,a), a point of label at most i in block j−a lies at distance strictly less than (a+1)t≤(i+1)t, a contradiction. Within block j the qualifying labels strictly decrease, since any earlier qualifying label at most the later one lies less than t places away. Each is at least mⱼ and less than Lⱼ, so there are at most max(0,Lⱼ−mⱼ) such positions. This choice gives Lⱼ₊₁=min(Lⱼ,mⱼ)+1. The count in block j is therefore at most Lⱼ+1−Lⱼ₊₁. Telescope using L₀=1 and Lⱼ≥1, defining the terminal L by the same formula. Lean checks the clearance implication from the earlier block bounds, the count of decreasing labels and the telescoping budget. [Lean proof](../lean/MultiplicityDips.lean) This proves (3). In fact the exact finite maximum is **⌊M/t⌋**. Concatenate t copies of any putative example. A marked endpoint stays marked in every copy: if its previous low label was the virtual index −1, a low label in an earlier copy can only make the distance larger than or equal to the virtual distance. Other previously witnessed distances are identical. Equation (3) on length tM now gives t times the original number of marked endpoints at most M. Place a zero at positions t−1,2t−1,… and fill the others with labels larger than M to attain the floor for every M,t. [Exhaustive finite test](../audits/multiplicity_dips_experiment.py)

Under cubic SAT, tabulate b_z=min(W(z),M) exactly by binary-searching the bounded existential thresholds used above. The extra O(n) queries per position leave the native clock unchanged up to a polynomial in n. Rank endpoints by g(z)/(b_z+1). Equation (3) bounds the number of high-scoring endpoints, and native incompressibility therefore gives g(v)≤C₃(b_v+1): apply (3) with t the integer part of that score, accounting for the ceiling and the finitely many small n. Consequently, if every predecessor in a trailing window has at least one more witness than v, then **K+1≤C₃(W(v)+1)**. For W(v)≥M the inequality is automatic; otherwise capping preserves the comparisons with W(v). This pays neither the witness length nor the code length of its unknown count.

A single publicly specified exception budget d=d(n) can grow. Among the labels, mark each z admitting a window ending there of length at least t(d+1)(b_z+1) with at most d earlier labels ≤b_z. There are O(M/t) marked endpoints, with an absolute constant independent of d. For t≥32 retain each index independently with p=1/[2(d+1)]. A given marked z is retained and all its low predecessors in the chosen window are discarded with probability at least p/2. The number of retained higher predecessors there has mean at least [t(b_z+1)−1]/2, independently of this event. Its variance is at most its mean; with probability at least 1/2 at least t(b_z+1)/8 such positions remain. In the thinned array z therefore qualifies for (3) at ⌊t/8⌋. The expected length is pM. The ceiling in (3) contributes at most one, absorbed into O(pM/t) because existence of a marked endpoint requires t(d+1)≤M. Comparing expectations gives the bound; for t<32 enlarge the absolute constant. Thus, fixing an efficiently computable d(n) in the cubic-SAT decoder, every trailing window at v with at most d earlier counts ≤W(v) has K+1≤C₄(d+1)(W(v)+1). The decoder ranks by the longest qualifying window divided by (d+1)(b_z+1). Its constant can depend on the program specifying the public budget.

### Sharp cost of allowing an arbitrary number of exceptions

The same packing rate for (3) fails if the window can decide its own tolerable exception count. In fact there is an exact finite-array characterization. Let P(i)>0 be any penalty, let D_z be the largest over windows ending at z of their length divided by the number of positions in the window with label ≤b_z (including z), and range over all finite label arrays. Then

    sup over M,b,t>0 of (t/M) · #{z : D_z ≥ t·P(b_z)} = Σ_{i≥0} 1/P(i). (4)

For the upper bound use the one-sided weak gap inequality for the set {z:b_z≤i} at threshold tP(i), restrict to endpoints of label i and sum over i. For the converse fix H, t and a large L. Begin with L entries of label H+1. Append, in descending order i=H,…,0, a group of ⌊L/[tP(i)]⌋ copies of i. At the j-th position of group i the entire prefix has length at least L and exactly j labels at most i. All the appended positions qualify. Write A=Σ_{i=0}^H1/P(i). Taking L→∞ gives a lower bound A/(1+A/t) on the supremum; then send t→∞ and H→∞. This proves (4), including when the right side diverges. In particular the penalty i+1 in (3) yields an infinite weak constant here. Penalties such as (i+1)[log₂(i+2)]^(1+ε) give finite constants for each ε>0. For an effectively evaluable penalty with these bounds, the cubic-SAT rank argument uses tabulated capped counts and all windows simultaneously. For instance one can use a rational majorant, within a constant factor, given by rounding binary logarithms and powers with a fixed rational ε. The divergence can in fact be realized at the **unchanged native first omitted output**. For any fixed rational P(i)≥1 computable in poly(n) time for i≤n with divergent reciprocal sum, take H=n, L=n² and append groups of sizes ⌊L/P(i)⌋ in descending i. Let A be their total length. Use a periodic sequence with A burn-in entries of label n+1 followed by these A group entries. Two fixed verifiers use phases shifted by A, so at each sufficiently large v one lands inside a group. The original source gives v larger than the polynomial period. Take the trailing window back to the start of that period. Its length is at least A and its count of labels no larger than i is at most L/P(i). Therefore D_v/P(i)≥A/L≥Σ_{i=0}^n1/P(i)−(n+1)/L, which tends to infinity. Realize a label i exactly as a witness count using ⌈log₂(n+2)⌉ bits and accepting just integers smaller than i. One of these two uniform polynomial-size verifiers attains the divergence along an infinite subsequence. Thus the missing word's semantic origin cannot rescue the dimension-free i+1 penalty for arbitrary adaptive exceptions. An improved bound would need an additional property of the specific verifier being tested.

## Approximating projected counts within the native clock

For a subset of {0,1}ˡ recognized by an existential circuit of size V, hash its candidate l-bit strings by a random affine binary map to j bits. Do not hash the auxiliary witness. If the subset has A elements, the zero bucket count has mean μ=A/2ʲ and variance ≤μ by pairwise independence. Thus the probability of a nonempty bucket is ≤1/4 for μ≤1/4 and ≥4/5 for μ≥4, the latter by the second-moment inequality. The seed has d=O((l+1)²) bits. Bucket nonemptiness has an existential circuit of size O(V+d) before index-encoding overhead. [Stockmeyer's approximate counting](https://doi.org/10.1137/0214060)

Use an explicit fixed-quantifier test: there exist O(d+1) shifts such that for every seed at least half of the shifted seeds have a nonempty bucket. At probability ≥4/5 independent shifts work simultaneously at all seeds with positive probability by a Chernoff and union bound. At probability ≤1/4 averaging the condition over seeds rules out every choice of shifts. Bucket witnesses for the selected majority form one inner existential block. Test emptiness separately; for A>0 take the largest accepted j between 0 and l. The resulting integer lies within three of log₂ A, including at the endpoints. The matrix has size O((d+1)(V+d)polylog(V+d+2)). Under linear SAT the fixed quantifier blocks can be eliminated at quasilinear cost. Running all l+1 thresholds suffices. This uses the classical shift method; the precision boost above comes from independent witnesses and requires only this constant-error log estimate. [Lautemann](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3) · [Encoded quantifier elimination](linear-quantifiers.md)

For (1), l≤M+1 and V=M²poly(n) suffice. Tabulating M log estimates therefore takes O(M⁶poly(n)) time. Compute the window minima incrementally for each endpoint and sort the scores within the same budget. Addresses are O(n) bits, and the native simulation incurs poly(n) overhead, leaving room below M²⁰.

## Many proof sources with rare evidence

As a second test, give the verifier an M-bit source a and an independent M-bit evidence string u. A uniform M·poly(n)-size circuit may reject or report an n-bit label. Choose a uniformly among the sources with nonzero acceptance count Aₐ, then choose u uniformly among that source's accepted strings. Call the resulting label law μ. Both conditionings may be arbitrarily rare. Assume there is a live source at the lengths considered. Under linear SAT any interval I=[v−K+1,v]⊆[0,M−1] satisfies μ(I)≤C₁K/M, with a constant depending on the uniform construction. In particular if a fraction α of the live sources each gives I conditional mass ≥β, then αβ≤C₁K/M.

For each live source put j(a) equal to the deterministic approximate log-count above. Given a, compute this bin in M⁵poly(n) time. For each label x and bin j, count the pairs (a,u) with j(a)=j and output x; denote this count by Cₓⱼ. The bin computation and output scan have an existential computation-transcript circuit of size M⁵poly(n). Approximate the logarithms of these *projected* counts, detecting zeros exactly. Do this for all O(M) bins and M labels. There are at most 2M counted bits in each outer test. The outer table takes M¹⁰poly(n) time: each of O(M) thresholds per entry uses O(M²) shifted copies of the bin predicate. This avoids enumerating the 2ᴹ possible sources.

Let estimated positive counts be powers of two and estimated zero counts be zero. Assign x the weight Σⱼ 2⁻ʲ Ĉₓⱼ. It approximates Σₐ Cₐ(x)/Aₐ within absolute constant factors, where Cₐ(x) counts evidence that reports x. The latter weights have total equal to the number of live sources. No probability lower bound on being live enters the normalization. Rank endpoints by their largest average estimated weight on a trailing interval. For any threshold λ>0 and total weight P, at most P/λ endpoints qualify: take a witnessing interval for the rightmost qualifying endpoint, then iterate strictly to the left of that interval. The disjoint chosen intervals cover all qualifying endpoints. Their total length is at most P/λ. Computing with a common dyadic denominator uses O(M) bits per weight. The same rank decoder now gives the claimed interval bound within the native clock. [Related one-sided rank bound](predecessor-batches.md)

## Why counts of small consistent proof fragments can tie exactly

Test a concrete candidate that counts accepting local proof fragments. On a graph with an edge label ℓₑ∈{0,1}, an assignment gives each vertex a bit and passes edge uv when its endpoint bits sum to ℓᵤᵥ modulo two. For any forest F, every edge labelling has exactly 2^(|V(F)|−|F|) satisfying assignments on the touched vertices. Choose one root bit in each component; all other values are forced. This calculation retains shared vertices between queried constraints.

There are N-vertex graphs with at least 3N edges and girth exceeding g=⌊(log₈ N)/3⌋ for arbitrarily large N. Independently include each edge with probability 8/N. With probability tending to one there are at least 7N/2 edges by the variance bound. The expected number of cycles of length through g is at most Σ_{j=3}^g 8ʲ/(2j)=O(N^(1/3)). With probability tending to one deleting one edge per short cycle costs at most N/2 edges. On such a graph, zero labels give a fully satisfiable instance. There also exist labels for which every global assignment violates at least 1/16 of the edges: fix an assignment, use independent fair edge labels, bound the probability of at most e/16 errors by 2^(−e(1−H₂(1/16))), and take the union over 2ᴺ assignments. Here e≥3N and binary entropy H₂(1/16)<1/2.

Consequently, every fixed collection of fewer than g edge checks has **identical exact proof multiplicity** on this satisfiable instance and this constant-gap no-instance. This survives conditioning on, or mixing over, subgraphs chosen independently of the labels. Global consistency across a cycle, or a choice using more information about the input, can distinguish them. In particular local checkability alone supplies no multiplicity dip on the actual omitted word. A verifier producing the advantage in (1) on those inputs remains missing. [Local proof verifiers and their guarantees](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/polylogPCP-abs.html)
