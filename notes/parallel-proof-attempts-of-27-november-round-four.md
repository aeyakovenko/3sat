# Parallel proof attempts of 27 November, round four, and why each stopped

On 27 November, a fourth round of parallel attempts took up the open steps left by round three, together with four directions proposed by an external review. Each attempt had about an hour or less and wrote a note. **None bridges the gap; the linear, quadratic and cubic questions remain open.** The results published as separate notes, listed below, were each checked by an independent referee and revised. This note records the other attempts, with the exact reason each stopped, so that later work does not repeat them. None of the attempts recorded here was refereed: their lemmas are attempt history, not verified theorems. Where one of them corrects an entry of round three, the entry here says so; the round-three note itself is unchanged. [Round three](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-round-three.md)

The external review's four directions became three lines of attempts in this round and one continued line. The three were nine versus ten AND gates for a matrix-verification bit, six-slot scans that store an even multiple of the cursor (published), and a two-parent clause gadget. The fourth direction, shared-bank charts, had already been started by round three's landmark chart, and this round carried it beyond permutation inverses.

## Attempts recorded here

**Nine or ten AND gates for one matrix-verification bit (open; 116 of 282 first-gate orbits excluded).** The bit [AB = C] for 2×2 matrices over F₂ needs 9 or 10 ANDs in the XOR–AND model: 9 by the journal's two-point bound, 10 by Strassen plus a three-AND conjunction. The external review asked which. Its claims reproduce once its exclusion rule is made precise: a prefix is excluded when some restriction path is redundant or keeps none of the seven graph planes through the pair. A two-level adaptive restriction test, gate elimination with the two-point bound at every node, rules out 60 of the 282 symmetry orbits of first gates (2,096,384 of 2,794,155 first-gate planes); a third level adds nothing. A second attempt fixed the circuit's gates instead of letting them vary per branch and excluded 56 more orbits, 47 at depth 3 and 9 at depth 4, leaving 166 orbits (285,547 planes) open; that exclusion rests on C++ programs spot-checked by a Python brute force, with no proof logs. Splitting the bit into its two row checks costs 11, since one row check needs exactly 5 ANDs (a CaDiCaL UNSAT at 4, once the two-point bound forces the first gate). Direct SAT search fails at this size: given a known 10-gate circuit with its first gate fixed, CaDiCaL found no answer in 600 s on 512 of the 4,096 inputs. Next is depth 4 on 65 more orbits (about 2.7 core-hours), with excluded orbits as a filter for every input-only gate. Either value is a finite circuit constant with no bearing on RAM time. [Seven ANDs for a 2×2 product](seven-ands-for-a-two-by-two-binary-matrix-product.md)

**A two-parent clause gadget (partial; a control, not a source of hard instances).** The external review proposed replacing two variable-disjoint clauses (a∨b∨c) and (d∨e∨f) by six clauses on two fresh variables; the gadget is not parsimonious and raises the deficiency by two per use. Its claims check exhaustively, and its criterion is proved: on a minimally unsatisfiable formula the replacement keeps minimal unsatisfiability exactly when the formula without the first clause has a model with e = 0, f = 1 and one with e = 1. So some ordering of the second clause works iff the first clause's private models take at least two values on the second clause's variables (checked on 2,042 hosts and 1,088,352 orientations, with no mismatch). Eligible pairs of untouched clauses survive every application, and each application makes two new pairs eligible, so one eligible pair yields an infinite chain of minimally unsatisfiable formulas, along which deficiency, eligible pairs and treewidth bounds grow (the lower bound went from 3–5 to 6–7 in seven steps). The plain inverse rewrite is not confluent: an explicit 11-clause overlap from a real chain has two normal forms. A generalized inverse that lets terminals coincide returned the seed on every tested history, 48 minimal chains and 4,290 disjoint-parent histories of up to 25 steps, but proving its confluence needs a syntactic invariant that inverse steps preserve, and none was formulated. If it holds, one linear-time program decides the whole closure of any fixed seed, and growth of these measures under such rewrites is no evidence of RAM hardness. **What a sound digest must know (dead for the potential form).** The round-three digest entry derives the native milestone from a sound equality digest with an o(t)-bit description for every run of the diagonal machine. This attempt asked whether soundness forces such a digest to be a potential, the reduction mod N of a strictly separating integer score. It does not. For indicator digests modulo a prime p, explicit sound digests that are not potentials exist at every length 4 ≤ T ≤ (p−1)/2 (proved, and checked on 911 pairs), and an exhaustive classification at small N finds more at lengths 2 and 3. On the journal's one-cell OR program with ℓ stages, where one-sided scores were already known to need range 2^ℓ, every potential needs N ≥ 2^ℓ + 1, while the least N of a sound equality digest is 3, 4, 6 and 10 for ℓ = 1 to 4. So the potential form is strictly stronger; for the diagonal machine itself this needs the OR chain to embed in its run, which was not checked. The attempt also corrects the soundness definition behind the round-three entry. Read literally, over every legal memory history, it makes indicator digests unsound for a trivial reason, since a memory history may plant a store of the false value; a public schedule repairs it with two counters of about 2·log₂(2t+1) bits in all, and with data-dependent addresses no repair is known, which is where the round-three entry's gap lies. A manuscript recount, inheriting round three's unverified citations, lets the evaluator spend o(t/w) steps per record, not only O(1). Scherk's theorem and the Savchev–Chen and Yuan result are cited from memory. **The two-block case of the opcode barrier (dead for the proposed route).** Round three's opcode-barrier entry left the general two-block case needing every correct verifier to have, on some table, a first proof that stays accepted when genuine labels change outside a small set. Read as the round-three attempt wrote it, with completeness only, that step is false: a verifier with one query and O(log T) proof bits pins the responses at two keys of one vertex, one of them a decoy, so no accepted proof is closed for any set. With soundness the step is equivalent to the target itself, by round three's theorem for closed first proofs, so it is no intermediate step; this corrects the round-three entry. The proposed mechanism, anchoring at positive tables whose decoys all carry flag 1, is killed by an explicit transformation: any verifier stays correct at a cost of O(log T) more proof bits and max(q, 3) queries, and at every such table its only accepted proof is one fixed proof, closed for no set. Both counterexamples were checked on all 4,516,756 tables of DAGs with T ≤ 3 and chains with T ≤ 6. For three blocks nothing is settled: a one-query ∀∃ verifier, checked on 60,308 tables, shows that the criterion's b must count every choice bit before the last change of player, and the relabeling lemma fails inside a round where the final ∃ block names fresh labels. Side results: a correct two-block verifier is exactly a cover of the positive tables by 2^b classes, each separated from the negative tables at q queries (proved), and iterating the relabeling lemma over proofs gives b ≥ log₂(T/q) − O(1) on chains (checked on simulated transcripts only). Next is a density statement over all classes of such a cover. **Two-block games with explicit linear claims (partial).** Round three's path-parity entry left as its missing step a counting game with few blocks, and this round proposed to bound such games by a rank. The attempt took public invertible GF(2)-linear runs and two-block games whose moves are explicit linear claims about cut states, priced by their support, with a verifier that replays one window of at most s steps. If the routing is a function of the transcript, every sound protocol pays at least the band weight μ([0, s]), the least number of point functionals whose pullbacks give the output covector; if it also ignores claimed values, it pays the sum of μ over disjoint bands of s cuts; and in the ∀∃ order no protocol with s < T is sound. These are proved, and checked exhaustively at D ≤ 4, T ≤ 5 and s ≤ 3 (5,000 and 10,906,696 cases for the first two, 472 for the third, no violation). The least weight of a single-shape protocol equals a chain quantity in all 13 instances computed and exceeds the band bound in 3. Rank is the wrong measure: the output depends on one covector, so one dense claim per cut suffices, and a large live interface at every cut buys nothing. μ ≥ c·D is proved only for a family computing the parity of the input, which has a trivial solver outside the class, and was measured at 0.12 to 0.31 of D on random runs with D = 16. Games like round three's halving game, whose claims are transfer-matrix entries over index intervals named in O(log S) bits, are not covered, and neither is k ≥ 3. **Shared-bank charts beyond permutation inverses (partial).** Round three's landmark chart covers the seed note's runs, whose forced memory is a permutation inverse. A linear-time program on the same files makes the last-preimage table forced memory instead (checked at instruction level for both loops, at small sizes). The cycle-landmark chart does not extend to it: every decoder that probes the map only along forward orbits and at written points must write each leader that has no preimage, about (e⁻¹ − e⁻²)m ≈ 0.23m of them on satisfiable block maps (the class bound proved, the count by formula and sampling). A chart with exponents u = v = 3/4, or 4/5 with an explicit cut list, still exists through the journal's certified index, which borrows Fiat–Naor at manuscript level; a Hellman-type chart at 2/3 is not established. The counting method of the seed note's Corollary 6 cannot refute shared-bank charts on any family: the largest bound it can certify is below m·W/(Q+1), so it can never show more than u + v ≥ 1 (proved). In the overwrite family B[f(i)] ← B[f(i)] + B[g(i)] with chained reads every word is forced, and the one decoder class tried, a last-writer index with memoized recursion, stores 9–12% of all values under a greedy rule for every 64 ≤ Q ≤ m^{3/4} (measured on one to three maps per size). That family is a candidate refuter and nothing more: refuting the charts needs a static data-structure lower bound beyond S·T ≈ N, which the counting method cannot give. **The real check circuit for the packed emitter (partial).** Round three's packed emitter writes the compiler criterion's failure test with a RAM program, conditional on serializing the check circuit, for which it used an XOR placeholder. This attempt added a serializer for arbitrary gate lists inside the RAM program, and a generator of the real check circuit of the compiler note: scanner, hash updates, initial-record, block, clause and pair challenges, range checks and read guards. The serializer reproduces round three's files byte for byte on all 69 of its parameter sets; with the real circuit, packed RAM, bytewise RAM and a Python reference write byte-identical failure tests on five parameter sets of up to 243,589 gates, at a flat 219.6 word steps per gate clause when packed. The gate list agrees with a separate implementation of the failure predicate on all 16,384 challenges; CaDiCaL finds an honest witness UNSAT and each of eight corruptions SAT, and for an unsatisfiable formula every assignment fails. The circuit has Θ(log⁴ M) gates, polylogarithmic as the criterion needs. Two stand-ins remain, so the criterion is still conditional: the compiler's local indexer is the identity, with a general indexer circuit by chronological simulation not written, and the generator runs in Python, not on the RAM. The criterion's own open step, a decision-preserving compiler of length o(s log² s), is untouched. **Leftovers with a literature answer (partial).** Three questions from round three were taken to the sources. Lozhkin's 2024 paper, read in full, gives the De Morgan formula depth of the multiplexer with n select lines as n + 2 for 2 ≤ n ≤ 5 and n ≥ 10; it proves only the upper bound for 10 ≤ n ≤ 20 and quotes the rest from Lozhkin–Vlasov 2011, which was not accessed. An explicit depth-6 formula for four select lines, correct on all 2^20 inputs, and a hand proof that uses Khrapchenko's bound from memory give selector depth SD(4) = 6. Round three's tree-evaluation entry found SD = 2, 4 and 5 for one to three select lines, the top-down value n + ⌈log₂(n+1)⌉, and the round-three attempt asked whether that holds for every n. It does not, at n = 4, 5 and 8–13 by explicit formulas and for n ≥ 14 by the quoted bound n + 3 (n = 6 and 7 stay open), so composition-built formulas for TE(h, 4, 1) have depth exactly 6h against top-down 7h. A game for tree evaluation that asserts only true node values is a Dymond–Tompa strategy, so by Chan's theorem the best one asserts b·(Peb^R − 1) bits on its worst path, for the tree's reversible pebbling price Peb^R. That lies between b((d − 1)h + 1) and b·dh for complete d-ary trees, and for d = 2 it is exactly b·(h + g⁻¹(h) − 1) with g⁻¹(h) = Θ(log* h) (Chan, Lauria, Nordström and Vinyals), so the top-down game is not optimal from height 3 on; TE(3, 2, 1) has a formula of depth 10, below the composition bound 12 (checked on 20,000 random inputs). Whether round three's rounds-against-pebbles strategy is in Kalyanasundaram–Schnitger stays open, since the paper could not be accessed. ## What the routes were missing

The alternation route still lacks its premise. Its two-block case has no intermediate step of the closure kind. Such steps are either false or the target itself, and padding transformations costing O(log T) bits destroy both properties tried at one chosen table, so a usable statement must constrain every class of a verifier's cover. The digest route keeps its open step in equality-digest form, since soundness does not force a potential, and its live case is data-dependent addresses. Counting games with explicit linear claims are priced by a weight, not a rank, and the transfer-matrix games that round three named are not covered.

The truth-claim route now has a lower bound inside its frame game. On an overwrite ladder touching C cells the frame game is worth at least C − 1, so on ladders with a constant fraction of cells it cannot beat walking the state chain by more than a constant factor. A bound of T − o(T) needs a reversible-pebbling argument. On ladders with o(T) cells, such as Θ(T/log T), the frame value is o(T), so the frame game cannot give Ω(T) there ([frame-game upper bound](frame-game-upper-bound-on-overwrite-ladders.md)). "Normal form plus pebbling" is limited from two sides: the public-address normal forms must be kept apart, and an online conversion to write-once memory costs Ω(log m/log log m) per step on average, so pebbling after it gains at most O(log log t).

The compiler route's failure test is now written down to two stand-ins, the compiler's local indexer and the generator on the RAM. Black-box routes through the diagonal compile stop at length o(s). The √(n log n) floor that the counting route meets under a quasi-linear collapse cannot be lowered by an argument that relativizes to the packed interface. Shared-bank charts survive beyond permutation inverses at exponent 3/4, at manuscript level. Refuting them needs a static data-structure lower bound beyond S·T ≈ N, which counting cannot certify.

On the program-size line, six-slot scans that store an even multiple of the cursor need Ω(N²/log⁴N) steps to answer every equality file correctly, and the six-instruction map stands at 929 classes excluded and 14 open (932 after a round-five note on prologue branches and 969 after a round-six note on one-verdict loops, exit code and LOAD loops without arithmetic). This round's finite constants, nine or ten ANDs and the selector depth, bear on no running time. [Barriers](what-any-proof-of-superlinear-sat-time-must-get-past.md)
