# Which occurrences can carry SAT hardness in equations over six permutations?

Consider an explicit list of word equations over the fixed group $S_3$. Each word must multiply to the identity. A token is one of six constants, a named unknown, or its inverse. Use a fixed finite-alphabet encoding with signed decimal names, separators and equation terminators. Let $n$ be its bit length. Unused names have no effect. Normalize numerical names by removing leading zeroes. Reject malformed files and files exceeding the occurrence limit during the initial scan. Let $k$ count names occurring three times, and require every other name to occur at most twice, globally.

* An unrestricted deterministic logarithmic-word RAM can eliminate all unknowns of multiplicity one or two in $O(n)$ steps. It obtains a Boolean circuit with $O(k+1)$ gates and inputs that recognizes which assignments to the three-occurrence names extend to solutions.
* At multiplicity three, feasibility has the same $O(n)$, $O(n^2)$ and $O(n^3)$ RAM decision questions as ordinary explicit CNF-SAT. The hardness direction uses words of at most three tokens. Every three-occurrence name appears twice positively and once negatively. Every other unknown in the constructed instances occurs exactly twice, with opposite signs. Only one nonidentity constant is needed.
* The first assertion and the bounded-density case of the October counting preprint yield an $O(n)$ algorithm throughout $k\le c\log n\sqrt{\log\log n}$ for a sufficiently small positive constant $c$. The elementary enumeration bound below is independent of that preprint.

This tests an algebraic induction that adds paired occurrences to enlarge a hard instance. Growing that part of the input while keeping the number of branch names fixed gives inputs in the linear-time band. The general case still calls for a running-time lower bound for actual RAM programs. The hard direction below transfers all three deadlines to an explicit graph coloring problem. Goldmann and Russell previously proved NP-completeness of equation systems over every fixed nonabelian group. Our question here is the occurrence boundary, including physical encoding length and what can be eliminated within a linear deadline. Priority for these more specific conclusions is unestablished. [Goldmann and Russell](https://doi.org/10.1006/inco.2002.3173)

## Eliminating the paired names

Mark the three positions of each three-occurrence name. Temporarily regard each as a prescribed group element, keeping its name and sign symbolically. Join the two occurrences of each twice-occurring name in the graph of equations. For a component containing a singleton, root a spanning tree at an equation with that singleton. Assign arbitrary values to internal variables other than the tree-edge names and the chosen singleton. Solve each nonroot face for its parent edge in postorder, then solve the root for the singleton. This works for **every** choice of the marked values. The other components can be processed separately.

Write each remaining equation around an oriented polygon. Give an empty word an identity side, and insert an identity side if a polygon has no unglued sides. Glue sides of each paired unknown so that the arrows for its positive value agree. Constants and marked occurrences are boundary edges. The quotient is a compact surface, possibly nonorientable. At a quotient vertex the link consists of corner intervals with endpoints paired by side identifications. It is connected by the definition of the vertex, and is a circle or an interval. Edge labels extend the prescribed boundary labels precisely when they can have product one around every face.

For a connected component, let $p_i$ be the ordered label product on its $i$th boundary circle, using the induced boundary orientations in the orientable case. Write $C(p)$ for a conjugacy class, $D$ for the set of all commutators and $Q$ for the set of all squares in $S_3$. The criterion is
$$
 1\in D^g\prod_i C(p_i) \quad\text{for genus }g,
 \qquad
 1\in Q^h\prod_i C(p_i) \quad\text{for }h\text{ crosscaps}.
$$
Products here are products of subsets. They commute for conjugacy-invariant subsets. The criterion follows from the surface group presentation, by trivializing a spanning tree and using paths to transport each boundary loop to the basepoint. Conversely, the presentation supplies flat edge labels with the required boundary classes. To get the individual prescribed labels $d_i$ on a circle, let $e_i$ be its obtained labels and choose $a_1$ conjugating their total product to the prescribed product. Solving $a_{i+1}=e_i^{-1}a_i d_i$ goes around the circle back to $a_1$. The vertex changes $e_i\mapsto a_i^{-1}e_i a_{i+1}$ now give the actual individual labels. Distinct boundary circles have disjoint vertices. In $S_3$ every element is conjugate to its inverse, so a boundary of a nonorientable surface can be read in either direction. This is the classical quadratic-word surface mechanism. [Quadratic equations and normal forms](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01929v1#S1.SS1) · [Surface group representations with boundary classes](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008439524000420)

The concrete obstruction can be read off without a general group algorithm. Write $A_3$ for the identity and the two 3-cycles, $T$ for the three transpositions and $C$ for the two 3-cycles. In $S_3$, $D=Q=A_3$, $TT=CC=A_3$, and $TC=T$. An odd number of transposition boundary products always rejects. With even parity, the only further obstruction is a genus-zero orientable component having no transposition boundary and **exactly one** boundary with a 3-cycle product. Any number of identity boundaries is harmless.

Tries over the fixed alphabet intern all occurring names in $O(n)$ native steps. Build adjacency lists for pairs of polygon corners at the glued endpoints. Ordinary graph traversals count connected components, faces, edges and quotient vertices, and detect orientability by transporting a polygon orientation across paired sides. Trace each boundary cycle and compute its fixed products. Euler characteristic with the boundary count gives the genus or crosscap count. Every list traversed has size $O(n)$. All group and subset operations have constant cost.

For a boundary containing marks, keep only its cyclic sequence of signed marked tokens and one precomputed product of constants between consecutive marks. A boundary free of marks contributes a known conjugacy class. Fold these known classes and the handle or crosscap factor into one constant subset per component. Resolve unmarked components once. There are at most $3k$ retained components, $3k$ marked boundaries and $3k$ marked tokens in total. Hence an assignment to the $k$ names can be tested by $O(k+1)$ operations, each a fixed table on constantly many bits. This constructs the claimed circuit and gives $O(n+(k+1)6^k)$ native time by enumeration with polynomial working space. The topology can have arbitrarily many handles, faces and paired names. It contributes only the precomputed constants to this circuit.

Encode the circuit by a constant number of 3-CNF clauses per gate and reject the two unused three-bit encodings of each group element. This gives $O(k+1)$ variables and clauses. Declaring extra unused variables if needed ensures a fixed upper bound on clauses per variable while preserving feasibility. The physical dense-name file has $O((k+1)\log(k+2))$ bits. Even this output fits in $O(n)$: the $k$ distinct marked names alone have total spelling length $\Omega(k\log(k+2))$ over a fixed alphabet. The cited deterministic counting algorithm takes $\operatorname{poly}(k+2)2^{O(k/\sqrt{\log(k+2)})}$ bit operations on that file. At the claimed cutoff its exponential factor is at most $n^{1/2}$ by choosing $c$ sufficiently small. Polynomial factors in $k$ and polylogarithmic simulation overhead still fit within $O(n)$ time. Its working space is polynomial in $k+2$ bits, addressable at that cutoff. For general input sizes the circuit construction and brute-force bound stand separately. ## Encoding arbitrary SAT with three occurrences

Start with an explicitly listed simple graph to be three-colored. Direct its edges arbitrarily. Use vertex variables $X_v$, a private variable $K_e$ at each edge and $c=(123)$. For an edge $v\to w$ impose
$$
                   X_vX_w^{-1}=K_e c K_e^{-1}.
$$
The conjugates of $c$ are exactly the nonidentity even permutations. The edge forces its endpoints to have the same sign and to be distinct. Each parity coset has three elements, so each component gives a proper coloring. Conversely, use the three even permutations as colors, and choose a conjugator on each edge. Thus the encoding works even with a satisfying assignment in which all the shared vertex values lie in the abelian subgroup $A_3$. The witnesses on individual edges supply the disjunction between the two possible nonzero differences.

First replace each edge test by the three words
$$
 X_v X_w^{-1} P_e^{-1},\qquad Q_e c^{-1}K_e^{-1},
 \qquad P_e K_e Q_e^{-1}.
$$
Setting each to the identity fixes $P_e=X_vX_w^{-1}$, $Q_e=K_ec$ and enforces the edge test. Each private variable has one positive and one negative occurrence. To limit vertex occurrences, remove vertices of degree at most two iteratively. A coloring extends to them in reverse removal order. For each surviving vertex split its appearances into incidence copies, recording the sign $s_j\in\{+1,-1\}$ of the old appearance. Use a fresh positive variable $Y_j$ at that position. Add the two-token words $Y_j^{s_j}Y_{j+1}^{-s_{j+1}}$ cyclically at this vertex. They make all the represented values $Y_j^{s_j}$ equal. Every copy now has one positive external occurrence and two ring occurrences of opposite signs. The minimum degree ensures distinct names in each ring word. Empty graphs can map to an empty system.

There is a control on precisely the same incidence pattern. Replace $c$ by a transposition $t$. Its conjugacy class is all three odd permutations, so each edge now simply demands opposite parities. The system is solvable exactly when the encoded graph is bipartite, a linear-time graph search. To change just **one bit** of the entire group file, use a shared name $D$ for the coefficient, with a separate copy at each use, one extra copy for the pin $D^{-1}g=1$, and cyclic equality words just as for vertex copies. Normalize the signs of those copies by the same rule. Encode $g$ in a fixed three-bit constant field, with the 3-cycle coded 001 and the transposition 011. A bit flip in that pin now selects the two predicates. If the graph was obtained by degree peeling, both modes concern the same surviving graph. On these files the rings and edge records can be read in their construction order. This isolates a value test on a shared layout. SAT-hardness of the 3-color mode in the usual complexity sense supplies no unconditional superlinear time bound.

For clarity about encoding size, reduce proper 3-SAT to graph coloring by the following constant-size gadgets. Use a global Boolean symbol $s$ and encode each clause $(a\lor b\lor c')$ by two not-all-equal triples $(a,b,d)$ and $(\neg d,c',s)$ with fresh $d$. For fixed $a,b,c',s$, a choice of $d$ exists exactly when these four bits are not all equal. A global complement allows $s$ to be false. Use a palette triangle, adjacent complementary literal vertices both joined to the third palette color, and one fresh triangle for each not-all-equal triple. Join each vertex of the fresh triangle to its corresponding literal. Such a triangle extends exactly when those literal colors are mixed. These operations and degree peeling take linear time in the graph size on the word RAM.

Intern names and remove duplicate ordered clauses by tries. If there are $m$ retained proper clauses then $m\log(m+2)=O(n)$ by counting distinct finite-alphabet clause strings. Hence this graph, the ternary equation words and the equality rings all have $O(m+1)$ items and $O(n)$ **encoded bits**, and can be rendered in $O(n)$ native steps. General unbounded-width DIMACS first uses the journal's uniform linear-time, linear-output conversion to proper 3-SAT. In the other direction the elimination circuit above gives a SAT file of $O(n)$ bits in $O(n)$ time. These two reductions preserve each requested deterministic time exponent on the ordinary logarithmic-word RAM, with unrestricted workspace and the usual fixed width constant for a composed simulation. ## Exhausting all one-variable attachments

Consider an arbitrary system of the above equation words with one external token $z$ or $z^{-1}$, occurring **once**. Every internal name occurs at most twice. Define its table to be the subset of the six possible values of $z$ that extend to a solution. Across systems of every finite size, exactly **16** of the 64 unary tables occur: the empty set, the full group and the seven nonempty subsets of each parity coset.

To prove completeness of the list, consider the component of the external equation in the graph of paired names. If it contains a singleton internal name, the spanning-tree solution works for every fixed $z$. Other components can only make the whole table empty. Otherwise apply permutation parity to every equation in the component and add the results modulo two. Each interior occurrence pair cancels, irrespective of the two signs on that variable. The fixed constants therefore determine the parity of $z$. This puts the whole table inside a single three-element coset.

Every listed table is attainable. A fixed equation $z=a$ gives a singleton. The equation $z=a u c u^{-1}$ for a 3-cycle $c$ gives the other two elements of the coset of $a$. The same equation for a transposition gives a full coset, with the choice of $a$ selecting which one. The word $zu$ accepts all values, and adjoining an impossible constant equation gives the empty set. These witnesses have bounded size. Thus even very long one-token attachments of this kind can be replaced by one of sixteen constant descriptions. Six evaluations of the surface test find the replacement in $O(n)$ time. This is an exhaustion over all sizes within the specified attachment rule.

A handle gives a concrete failure of a proposed enlargement. In one graph edge test, multiply the permitted conjugate of $c$ by a fresh commutator $[a,b]$. The set of possible products is $D C=A_3$. This edge now permits **every** pair of labels of the same parity, including equal labels. The resulting system is solvable exactly when the graph **with that edge deleted** is 3-colorable. For the reverse direction, encode its coloring using three even permutations at all vertices. In particular the enlargement turns the unsatisfiable $K_4$ system into a satisfiable one. Fresh paired temporaries split the enlarged edge word back into ternary words if required. This example rules out preserving the verdict by this handle insertion on arbitrary input.

The kernel attacks the proposed growth step by exposing its cost. Enlarging just the paired part, even with noncommuting constants and nonorientable components, cannot by itself force repeated hard processing of that part. To turn this formulation into the requested theorem one must force an unrestricted decider to spend superlinear time on growing residual systems, after allowing it to use this preprocessing. The reductions alone give no such lower bound.
