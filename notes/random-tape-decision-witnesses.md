# Decision bits of a randomly isolated witness with the hash tape available for free

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** If all satisfying assignments of a formula are hard to read locally, isolating one assignment turns its bits into SAT verdicts. The isolation hash may itself be as long as an assignment. Giving a decoder unrestricted random access to that hash can invalidate the original lower bound. Here a separate proof handles the hash as a genuinely random public input, even when the decoder's advice is chosen after seeing it.

## Statement on ordinary SAT decisions

Assume a deterministic solver for explicit 3-SAT on the packed logarithmic-word RAM takes $O(N^d\operatorname{polylog}N)$ time, $d\in\{1,2,3\}$. There is a short-input family of explicit formulas with a distinguished proof table of $v$ bits. Write $H=2^r$, where $r$ is the length of its short input $x$. The encoded lengths after isolation are $H\le N\le H\operatorname{polylog}H$ after fixed padding. For a public random tape $R$, an integer hash level $m$, an index $i$ and a Boolean pin $c$, the formula asks whether a satisfying proof survives the hash and has bit $c$ at $i$. On a tape that isolates at level $m$, there is exactly one surviving proof $\pi$. The answer for $c=1$ is then $\pi_i$. For each $i$ precisely one of the two pins is satisfiable.

Fix any deterministic evaluator. It gets $x,m,i$, independent random access to the **entire** tape $R$ and the actual decision formula, and one advice string shared over all $i$. The advice may depend arbitrarily on $x,m,R$. Each invocation starts in its specified fresh state. Its full per-index running time, including ordinary computation and memory access, is $q$. We may fix the pin to one. A solver answering both pins can be used on this restriction.

**Tradeoff.** There is a constant $C$ and there are infinitely many positive inputs $x$ on which, for every level $m$ and all dyadic budgets $b+1,q$ with
$$
 (b+r+1)(q+1)\ \le\ H^{1/d}/r^C ,
$$
the probability over $R$ of the following joint event is at most $2^{-r}$. This event requires isolation of a proof and the existence of advice of at most $b$ bits that makes all indexed decisions correctly within the clock $q$. Dyadic rounding changes the budget by a constant factor. A fixed universal evaluator can include the lookup program in the advice. The constant may depend on the reference solver, verifier and evaluator.

On each such input one level isolates with probability at least $3/16$. Even conditional on isolation at that level, with probability $1-O(r^2 2^{-r})$ the displayed product must exceed the threshold for any successful advice and query budget (enlarge $C$ to cover rounding). The same public tape works over all requested coordinates. In particular for polylogarithmic advice, per-coordinate time is at least $N^{1/d}/\operatorname{polylog}N$, with log powers depending on the fixed budgets. For polylogarithmic lookup time, the advice has this lower bound instead. The quantifiers allow preprocessing to inspect every bit of the tape before selecting the advice.

## Constructing the domain and the two pins

Choose a language in nondeterministic multitape time $2^r$ outside nondeterministic time $2^r/r^2$ by the nondeterministic time hierarchy. Apply the short PCP theorem to the pair language with explicit part $x$ and one fixed implicit symbol, accepted exactly when $x$ belongs to the hierarchy language. At a negative $x$ the permitted implicit set is empty, so proximity soundness applies. It has perfect completeness, soundness at most $1/2$, proof length $H\operatorname{poly}(r)$, at most $r+O(\log(r+2))$ random bits and verification time $\operatorname{poly}(r)$. Fix uniform bounds and pad the proof and coin lengths. The soundness statement concerns every fixed proof oracle at a negative input. If proof length is counted by the number of effective query addresses, use a padded sorted dictionary of address–bit pairs for the addresses touched by any coin string on the honest proof. There are at most $H\operatorname{poly}(r)$ records of $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ bits. Fixed binary search with a default on misses makes every proposed dictionary, including a malformed one, a single total answer function. Its added time per PCP check is polynomial in $r$. [Ben-Sasson, Goldreich, Harsha, Sudan and Vadhan, Theorem 2.5](https://people.seas.harvard.edu/~salil/research/polylogPCP.pdf) · [Williams, Theorem 3.7](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

Take the set $W_x$ of all $v$-bit proof tables passing **every** PCP coin string. The deterministic expanded verifier tries all coin strings against one common proposed proof and uses $H\operatorname{poly}(r)$ steps. Apply the nested random affine Toeplitz hash to this proof table. For level $m$, impose $m$ zero output bits and pin the $i$th proof bit. Each surviving proof has a unique complete extension when the verifier is compiled with a deterministic record-sorting network, equivalence gates, and fixed unused bits. The full tape comprises the Toeplitz diagonals and offset, all independent random bits. Checking the hash uses quasilinear binary convolution. These static bounded-run formulas have fixed layout for given dimensions. A requested formula word is computable from the short parameters in $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ time and at most $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ tape accesses. The verifier's full scan of the proof is represented in its clauses; the formula-word generator need never execute that scan. Immutable tape data enter through indexed initialization or input-pin records. Supply the index and pin as fixed bits of the expanded verifier’s input. To vary a bit by one sign occurrence in ordinary width-three syntax, use a clause with the chosen signed literal and two other literals already forced false. ## Replacing the long random tape for a finite test

Here is the averaging step for any reader of the proof, regardless of how it obtains its bits. Suppose on a fraction $\beta$ of full tapes there exists advice of length at most $b$ producing a table that passes every PCP check. This follows from correct decision answers on an isolated proof. Fix the level. Some one advice string $z$ works on at least a $p=\beta 2^{-b-1}$ fraction of tapes. Default to a fixed Boolean output on timeout or malformed replies, so the clocked reader defines a complete proof on every tape.

Sample $u=\lceil 4(b+\log_2(1/\beta)+2)\rceil$ independent PCP coin strings and run all their checks with this same $z$ and tape. Counting physical accesses made by the decoder and local formula generator, the combined tests touch at most
$$
 h=u(q+1)\operatorname{poly}(r)
$$
independently named random-tape positions on any path. Choose a field of characteristic two with $s=O(r)$ bits per element, large enough for distinct addresses and all budgets. Replace the uniform tape by evaluations of a uniform polynomial of degree less than $h$ on distinct address names, taking a fixed output bit or the full field word as appropriate. One can use independent field values for individual bit addresses throughout. On every path of the combined test the transcript distribution is unchanged. Fix the verifier coins and a transcript. Repeated reads must agree, and on each first visit the specified replies have their uniform probability, by interpolation on at most $h$ different positions. This applies to adaptively chosen addresses. No equality of distributions *conditioned on isolation* is asserted.

For a fixed replacement polynomial let $a$ be the acceptance probability of **one** PCP check against the complete table produced by the decoder. Its advice and tape stay fixed, so its $u$ checks have joint acceptance probability $a^u$. The average of $a^u$ over replacement polynomials is at least $p$. It follows that one polynomial has $a\ge3/4$, since $(3/4)^u<p$. At a negative input every fixed polynomial and every advice still yield $a\le1/2$. The surviving positive predicate requires high acceptance by a proof table. The replacement seed need not isolate anything. ## Paying for one SAT call

Guess $z$, $m$, and the $h$ coefficients. A degree-$s$ irreducible binary polynomial specifying the field can be guessed and checked in $\operatorname{poly}(s)$ time by repeated Frobenius squaring and polynomial gcd tests. All subsequent evaluations use the same field and coefficients. Amplify the single-check acceptance gap by independent repetitions and a threshold strictly between $1/2$ and $3/4$. In $O(\log(r+2))$ repetitions, the amplified verifier uses $\ell=\operatorname{poly}(r)$ random bits and has acceptance fraction at least $1-1/(10(\ell+1))$ on the good seed, at most $1/(10(\ell+1))$ for every negative seed. For example use threshold $5/8$ and a sufficiently large repetition constant. The actual $\ell$ is $O((r+\log(r+2))\log(r+2))$.

Guess $\ell+1$ translations of this **short verifier coin space**. On the positive case some list of translations of the accepting set covers the whole space, since the expected number uncovered by random translations is at most $2^\ell/[10(\ell+1)]^{\ell+1}<1$. On a negative input no such list covers it by the union bound. Guessing all shifts costs $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ bits.

A defect now has one candidate uncovered coin string. For each translation, simulate the amplified verifier and its deterministic proof-bit evaluator. Guess the random-tape replies used along these simulations and retain their field addresses. Check that every translation rejects, and that all guessed replies come from the single coefficient list at those addresses. This last step can be done **after** the adaptive simulations in time $(h+J+1)\operatorname{poly}(r)$ for $J$ requested values. Construct a product tree of the monic factors $X-a_j$, reduce the supplied polynomial at its root and propagate remainders down the tree. Duplicate points cause no problem. Fast multiplication and division by monic polynomials give quasilinear field-operation cost. On a bounded-word RAM all field and indexing work costs fixed powers of $r$. Alternatively, encode a coefficient of a binary extension as a polynomial of degree less than $s$. Use bivariate Kronecker substitution and quasilinear integer multiplication, extract coefficients in characteristic two, and reduce modulo the field polynomial. These algorithms do not require a root of unity in the chosen field. An accepted guessed transcript is genuine by induction over its reads. [Borodin and Moenck](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/Papers/fast-modular-transforms.pdf) · [Cantor and Kaltofen](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/91/CaKa91.pdf) · [Harvey and van der Hoeven](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2021/193-2/p04)

There are only $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ translated checks, so $J\le(q+1)\operatorname{poly}(r)$. The whole physical data block and defect clock each cost at most
$$
 (b+\log(1/\beta)+1)(q+1)\operatorname{poly}(r)
$$
bits or word steps. Include coefficients and all advice as ordinary public input to the bounded defect machine. The packed formula reduction charges that data once. One deterministic SAT call on its explicit formula certifies absence of a defect. Together with the outer nondeterministic guesses this gives time $((b+\log(1/\beta)+1)(q+1))^d\operatorname{poly}(r)$. Guess constructible dyadic budgets subject to the announced cap and use fixed timeouts. On erroneous guesses a covering certificate is impossible on every negative input. Standard nondeterministic tape simulation guesses the RAM records and verifies them in address-time order in fixed additional powers of $r$.

With $\beta=2^{-r}$, choose $C$ large enough to put this computation within $O(2^r/r^2)$. If every sufficiently large positive instance admitted a level and budgets with the claimed event probability greater than $2^{-r}$, the computation would decide the hierarchy language within its excluded bound. Finitely many positive exceptions can be hardwired. This proves the stated infinite positive family. The union over dyadic budgets has only $O(r^2)$ terms at the fixed isolating level.

## A short random query on which actual decisions take time

The exact-coordinate result can be strengthened to a prediction problem with balanced answers. Fix $k>0$ and $\delta>0$. On each isolated tape, a random query of length at most $(1+\delta)\log_2 v+O(\log r)$ asks for one coded bit of the same proof. Its two Boolean pins give opposite SAT verdicts. On the hard positive inputs above (using the universal bit reader and increasing $C$), the probability over a public tape that it isolates a proof **and** admits a budgeted predictor achieving uniform-query success at least $1/2+r^{-k}$ is at most $2^{-r}$ per level and budget. The bound holds when the predictor sees the whole physical query formula and its short parameters. All random choices of the query-dependent fields enter as fixed data of the formula. Only $O(r)$ literal-sign positions have to vary with the query for fixed source, tape and hash level.

To construct the queries, choose a power of two $a$ comparable to $r^A$, with $A$ a sufficiently large fixed constant, and put $w=\lceil\log_a v\rceil$. Represent address digits by a fixed size-$a$ subset of a binary extension field $F$. Pad the proof grid to $a^w<a v$ entries (allow equality with $v$). Let $P$ denote the interpolating polynomial of degree less than $a$ per variable that gives these bits on the grid. Its total degree is at most $w(a-1)$. Given $y\in F^w$, the value $P(y)$ can be obtained by tensor-product Lagrange interpolation in $v\operatorname{poly}(r)$ time, including the padding and all field operations. The expanded deterministic verifier checks just **one** such evaluation per formula, together with the original checks and isolation constraints. Pin the binary inner product of $P(y)$ with a word $t\in F$ to the bit $c$. Use uniform independent $y,t,c$. The Boolean field coordinates here are in a fixed basis. The incidence layout can take these inputs as sign-pinned public bits to one fixed clocked machine.

Write $\epsilon=r^{-k}$. Fix a pin $c$ on which the predictor has the claimed advantage and convert its verdict prediction to a prediction of the scalar bit. For a fraction at least $\epsilon$ of the field points $y$, the inner-product word for $P(y)$ has agreement at least $1/2+\epsilon/2$ with the predictor as $t$ ranges over $F$. At each $y$ enumerate field values meeting this threshold in fixed order. Parseval's identity for the Boolean characters bounds the list length by $1/\epsilon^2$. Some fixed list rank therefore gives a scalar-valued oracle agreeing with $P$ on at least a constant times $\epsilon^3$ of $F^w$, with a default output at missing ranks. Computing the list costs $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ predictor calls and work when $|F|=\operatorname{poly}(r)$. All calls use the **same** public tape and original advice. On timeouts define the tentative predictor's output by a fixed convention.

Take the field size to be a power of two between a sufficiently large constant times $(1+w(a-1))/\epsilon^6$ and twice that bound. The multivariate reconstruction theorem now gives a randomized oracle algorithm for $P$ at each point with success at least $2/3$, from a list of algorithms of $\operatorname{poly}(r)$ description size and query time. Existence of the right description suffices here; it is additional advice for this particular tape and predictor. Repeat with independent coins $O(r+1)$ times. The error at any one proof address falls below $1/(2v)$, so a single fixing of the entire $\operatorname{poly}(r)$-bit coin string works at all original grid addresses. The resulting stateless bit reader computes the actual proof everywhere with $b+\operatorname{poly}(r)$ bits of advice and $(q+1)\operatorname{poly}(r)$ time. The public-tape theorem applies after absorbing these polynomial factors. Finally $\log |F|\le (A+1+6k)\log r+O(1)$ for large $r$. Choosing $A$ with $(1+6k)/A<\delta$ gives the short query length including $t,c$ and rounding. No full evaluation table on $F^w$ is included in the SAT input. [Sudan, Trevisan and Vadhan, Lemma 28 and Theorem 29](https://people.csail.mit.edu/madhu/papers/1999/stv-journ.pdf)

In particular, fix a correct ordinary deterministic SAT solver and give it no preprocessing advice. Run it until the allowed cutoff $q$, then give a default verdict. The better of two default bits gives prediction advantage at least half the fraction of actual runs that halted by $q$, since completed runs were correct. Thus on almost all isolating tapes at the stated level, at most $2r^{-k}$ of these *actual decision runs* finish before the no-advice cutoff. One can also run the solver on both pins, stopping if either finishes and inferring the opposite verdict on the other. This costs at most twice the cutoff and one default bit. Consequently, except for at most a $2r^{-k}$ fraction of the checksum parameters, **both the satisfiable and the unsatisfiable input** take longer than the common cutoff (absorbing the constant cost in $C$). Infinitely many hard sources and the clock, which is $N^{1/d}/\operatorname{polylog}N$, are as above. This conclusion concerns a fixed solver across the query family. It entails neither a long run at every query nor a superlinear run. ## What would turn this into a running-time separation?

The inputs left after fixing a public tape are ordinary SAT instances of both verdicts. A single bit query to a hypothesized linear solver may still take time proportional to the full encoded formula. Even with no advice, the lower bound on that query time falls below the encoded length by logarithmic factors. The argument would contradict an unrestricted solver if its runs on **all** the relevant pins could be converted into readers below the product threshold while retaining the consistency of one proof. No such conversion has been proved. The long-tape removal uses classical limited independence, probabilistic proof checking and the translate-cover argument. Priority of this quantitative application is unverified. [Lautemann](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3) · [A limit on reducing the polynomial seed length](coset-sum-tape-obstruction.md)
