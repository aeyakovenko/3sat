# From the matrix multiplication paper to a lower bound for unit-clause SAT circuits

**Consequence of the cited list-volume theorem.** There is a constant $\sigma>0$ and a sequence of encoded lengths $L$ at which bounded-fan-in Boolean circuits deciding explicit SAT require $\Omega(L(\log L)^\sigma)$ gates. This conclusion holds on formulas consisting of two equally long lists of unit clauses, the first positive and the second negative. Within each list, every variable name is distinct. The first list can even be required to be sorted. A decision circuit may answer arbitrarily outside this promise. The encoding uses binary variable names of $O(\log L)$ bits and permits unused names. The same lower bound holds for width-at-most-three SAT, or for width exactly three if repeated literal positions are permitted. The strongest occurrence promise here counts occurrences in the original unit clauses. A direct Boolean reduction below also gives the bound for ordinary dense-name 3-SAT with three distinct variables per clause. A separate parity contraction proves it for dense systems of binary XOR equations. [Dense parity-graph proof](matrix-list-parity-graphs.md)

The source is the *volume of list distinctness* theorem of the November 24 matrix multiplication preprint. For each fixed finite field it supplies a $\sigma>0$. Put $N=2^w$ and $n_w=2wN$. On a list of $N$ records of $2w$ bits, $D_w$ returns one if every record is distinct. For every sufficiently large $w$ and $1\le t\le N$, every trivalent bit tensor network computing the product of $t$ independent $D_w$ answers has volume greater than $t n_w w^\sigma$. Tensors can have arbitrary entries in the field. Contraction sums over binary internal wires. The source obtains the network lower bound before using a hypothetical matrix multiplication speedup to derive a contradiction. We use the list theorem itself. We checked the cited statements and the transfer below. The preprint's full list theorem and its dependencies have not been independently re-proved here. [Theorem on list volume](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/build/obstruction.tex) · [Proof and signed selectors for tagged permutations](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/build/list.tex)

## A direct reduction to 3-SAT with dense identifiers

For the ordinary 3-SAT circuit consequence there is a simpler reduction that avoids signed summation and sparse names. Split each input record into $x_i=(a_i,b_i)$ with $a_i,b_i\in[0,N)$. Use a row-selection bit $s_i$ for each row and two banks of $N$ Boolean flags $H_a,K_b$. Include the two binary clauses
$$
 (\neg s_i\lor H_{a_i}),\qquad (\neg s_i\lor K_{b_i}).
$$
Require at most one true flag per bank and at least two true row selectors. These last requirements have a fixed, input-independent 3-CNF of $O(N)$ clauses and auxiliary variables. For example, scan a bank with a prefix-OR bit. Forbid a new true flag when the preceding prefix bit was true. For the selectors maintain two prefix bits meaning “at least one” and “at least two”. Update the second by OR-ing its previous value with the AND of the current selector and the previous first bit. Use ordinary width-three gate equivalences, fix the starting bits, and assert the final second bit. The circuit has constant work per position. Any setting of its input bits has a unique consistent gate evaluation.

Two selected rows must agree on both chunks by the two bank constraints. Conversely, two occurrences of one record can be selected, with precisely their common flags set. Thus satisfiability is **exactly** presence of a duplicate. No isolation promise, oracle, or field identity is involved. The identifiers can be numbered densely in $O(N)$ slots, all in $O(w)$ bits. In a fixed-width serialization the only varying fields are the indices of the two positive bank literals per row. Aligned power-of-two banks let each bit of an index be a constant or one input bit, with dummy padding if desired. Alternatively a constant-offset addition for each index costs $O(w)$ Boolean gates per record and gives contiguous identifiers without padding. The formula length and total bit wiring cost are both $O(Nw)$, and the displayed list uses $\Theta(Nw)$ bits. For an exact-three encoding that requires three *distinct* variables per clause, expand a binary clause $C$ into $(C\lor z)\land(C\lor\neg z)$ with fresh $z$. For a unit clause use the four sign choices on two fresh variables. Both give constant expansion, with a fixed layout and dense names.

Composing a decision circuit for these 3-CNFs with this bit map and reversing the answer gives a circuit for $D_w$. Its trivalent network has volume $O(s+Nw)$ for an $s$-gate circuit. The paper's one-list bound gives the same $\Omega(L(\log L)^\sigma)$ circuit lower bound at these lengths, also for ordinary dense-name exact 3-SAT. All gate constraints, signs and clause positions are fixed ahead of the data. Only the two references to bank flags per record depend on its bits. The multi-list signed argument below gives the stronger syntactic restriction to units with at most two occurrences and the batched product bound in its sparse encoding.

A separate eight-bank construction gives a literal bit projection to dense ASCII decimal names, with every declared variable present and exactly three distinct variables per clause. The same review identifies the upstream Lean theorem used by the one-list specialization. [Circuit audit and ordinary ASCII encoding](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md)

## One SAT answer tensor, with two ordered lists of literal positions

For $x=(x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1})$ use positive units named $(0,i,x_i)$, in increasing $i$ order. Each name uses $1+3w$ bits. Choose internal binary wires for $N$ proposed values $y_j$ of $2w$ bits and $N$ proposed row indices $r_j$ of $w$ bits. Enforce $y_0<\cdots<y_{N-1}$ by adjacent comparators. For each $j$ a selector chooses one of two **negative** unit names:

| Choice | Name | Tensor weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inactive | A private reserve $(1,j,0^{2w})$ | $1$ |
| Active | $(0,r_j,y_j)$ | $-1$ |

The leading bit makes every reserve distinct from every active name. Whenever the comparisons pass, the positive names are pairwise distinct by their row indices and the active negative names are pairwise distinct by their values. This is true for *every* choice of the row indices and selectors. Off-promise values on failed comparisons are multiplied by zero.

Fix $x,y,r$ with $y$ strictly increasing. A selected unit conflicts with the positive list exactly when $x_{r_j}=y_j$. Write $B=\{j:x_{r_j}\ne y_j\}$. A selector set $E$ gives a satisfiable formula precisely when $E\subseteq B$. The signed sum of its SAT answers is
$$
 \sum_{E\subseteq B}(-1)^{|E|} =
 \begin{cases}1&B=\varnothing,\\0&B\ne\varnothing.\end{cases}
$$
In characteristic two, this is simply the parity of the $2^{|B|}$ admissible selector sets. It is a sum of **decision answers** for unit SAT. Counting assignments to the variables of a unit formula would give a different tensor.

The remaining contribution counts strictly increasing lists satisfying $x_{r_j}=y_j$ for every $j$. Such a list requires $N$ distinct source records. If the source records are distinct, the sorted list and its row indices are unique. If any repeat, no such list exists. The network therefore computes $D_w(x)$ in every characteristic, without an extra distinctness test or a factorial coefficient.

Use a fixed binary clause serialization with a binary header and fixed-width names of the declared size. Encode zero-based identifiers directly or shift them by one with a linear-size bit adder. A sparse range of $O(N^3)$ potential names suffices; each actual instance has at most $2N$ distinct names. There are $L=\Theta(Nw)$ bits. All constants, comparators, name multiplexers, deterministic copies, and selector weight factors use $O(Nw)$ additional trivalent volume. Comparisons propagate a constant number of state bits per bit position. The fanout of one selector into $O(w)$ positions is included. There is a unique consistent wire assignment for each deterministic gate network after its inputs are fixed. This construction uses neither an oracle for untrusted indices nor a data-dependent wire that reads $x_{r_j}$; the latter equality is enforced by the SAT tensor.

More generally, take any single trivalent network of volume $V$ that returns the product of $t$ promised unit-SAT answers, allowing arbitrary wiring between lists. Attach $t$ independent copies of the surrounding construction to its input ports. Every surviving term meets the promise simultaneously on every list. Distributivity and the preceding identity give the product of the $t$ distinctness answers, with total volume $V+O(tNw)$. Hence for all large $w$ and $1\le t\le N$,
$$
 V=\Omega(t L (\log L)^\sigma).
$$
A bounded-fan-in deterministic Boolean circuit with $s$ gates on $L$ input bits gives a trivalent network over $\mathbb F_2$ of volume $O(L+s)$. Expand each constant-arity gate into binary Boolean gates, copy its output using equality tensors, and pin the final output to one. Even unbounded fanout charges only the total number of input pins plus external bits. Applying the result with $t=1$ gives the stated circuit bound. The multipliers in the contraction need not be realizable by a deterministic many-one reduction. The argument uses them in the lower-bound model.

## Testing the transfer on a RAM

The same promised formulas have a deterministic $O(N)$-step, $O(N)$-word algorithm on a logarithmic-word RAM. Radix-sort the $2N$ tagged variable names, retaining the sign of each literal. A constant number of stable counting-sort passes with radix $N$ handles all $1+3w$ name bits. Each pass initializes $N$ counters and distributes $2N$ records. A final adjacent scan detects a variable with both signs. Packed input decoding costs $O(N)$ for this fixed-width layout. Both the number of records and the number of counters fit even if each record needs a fixed number of machine words. In encoded bits the time is $O(L/\log L)$. Thus a general conversion of such fast RAM computations into exact networks of volume $O(L(\log L)^{o(1)})$ would contradict the source's explicit theorem on this very family.

An intermediate model makes the loss quantitative. Suppose all input and memory access positions are fixed by length and time, each machine word has $O(\log L)$ bits, and each step's controller and word update has bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit size $g(\log L)$. Unroll a time-$T$ clock. The last writer of a fixed read position is fixed before the input is supplied, so draw wires from that writer (using conditional writes as multiplexers if necessary). This costs $O(L+T(g(\log L)+\log L))$ gates, including initialization, copied bits and conditional moves. Hence every such solver on this promise obeys
$$
 T=\Omega\!\left(\frac{L(\log L)^\sigma}{g(\log L)+\log L}\right)
$$
at the indicated lengths. For linear-size word updates this exceeds the number of records by a power of the logarithm. In the radix algorithm the destination of a counting-sort update depends on the current record. There is no predetermined last writer. The word-RAM example also rules out extending the circuit exclusion to word-RAM time simply by changing units.

The same argument measures a property of **adaptive** solvers. Fix a solver, an indicated length and its worst-case clock $T$ on the unit promise. Pad its trace and reserve a constant number of ordered read and conditional-write slots in each step. For every data-memory read slot $u$, let $P_u$ contain all write slots that are the last actual writer for that read on some promised input. For an input read slot use the set of possible source word indices instead. Default-zero data need no writer. The sets may be supplied nonuniformly, since the target lower bound is for circuits. For each candidate earlier write, test its enable bit and equality of its address to the current address. Select the most recent passing value, or zero. On every promised input that value is precisely the real memory reply: whenever a last writer exists, it belongs to $P_u$, and no later enabled matching write exists. For input reads, select the candidate with the requested index. Conditional reads can return a fixed neutral word when disabled. Induction on the slots now implements all promised runs in Boolean circuit size
$$
 O\!\left(L+T(g(w)+w)+w\sum_u |P_u|\right).
$$
All addresses have $O(w)$ bits. The tests, priority selection and data multiplexing cost $O(w)$ per candidate; read results at branches earlier in the same step can be wired forward. A fixed program's control cost is included in $g$. Thus for **every** such RAM solver running in $T=O(N)$ steps on this promise with $g(w)=w^{1+o(1)}$, one has $\sum_u |P_u|=\Omega(Nw^\sigma)$. The sets count alternatives across inputs, not simultaneous work on a single run. An unrestricted $O(L)$ clock also has an additional factor of $w$ in its allowed number of word operations. This is the precise loss in trying to use this circuit argument to rule out linear word-RAM time.

## Testing the arithmetic lower bound directly

The paper extracts a bilinear algorithm from a formal arithmetic circuit by substituting $A+uX,B+vY$ with $u^2=v^2=0$ and reading the mixed $uv$ coefficient. Over $\mathbb F_2$ consider the alternative output polynomial $Q_{ij}=\sum_k X_{ik}^2 Y_{kj}^2$. It computes the correct ordinary matrix product on **every binary input**. After the same substitution its mixed coefficient vanishes at every base point, since $(a+ux)^2=a^2$ and $(b+vy)^2=b^2$. More generally over any field $h(x)=3x^2-2x^3$ fixes zero and one with zero derivative at both, so inserting $h$ at every input kills these local variations at Boolean base points for constant work per variable. This refutes direct mixed-coefficient extraction from an arbitrary representative of a Boolean function. Transferring the rank bound by this route would require control of the cost of recovering a formal representative with the desired mixed coefficient. ## A quantitative use for the unrestricted SAT search

A separate, positive use of the **upper** bound is available in the journal's rigidity experiment. At block side $J$, the [program-fiber construction](parity-of-program-fibers.md) and its [fixed program version](fixed-program-parity.md) compute binary products, use two hypothesized $n^k$ SAT simulations and reconstruct a sample or a matrix by up to $J^2$ adaptive queries. Their clock for this part is $J^2(J^{\log_2 7})^{k^2}\operatorname{poly}(q)$ at scale $P=2^q$. For every fixed rational $a<1/(2+(9/4)k^2)$ one can instead use $J=2^{\lfloor aq\rfloor}$. Choose $\epsilon>0$ so $a(2+(9/4+\epsilon)k^2)<1$. Over $\mathbb F_2$ the paper's asymptotic rank upper bound supplies one *fixed* finite bilinear base algorithm with exponent below $9/4+\epsilon$ (enlarge the base and leave a smaller internal slack). Hardwire its binary coefficient tables and recursively tensor the base algorithm, padding arbitrary side lengths to its next power. This is a uniform deterministic bit algorithm at exponent at most $9/4+\epsilon$ with logarithmic overhead absorbed in the slack. The resulting aggregate cost is at most $P^{1-\Omega(1)}\operatorname{poly}(q)$ for this part. The admissible block-side powers are therefore arbitrarily close from below to **$1/11$ for quadratic SAT and $4/89$ for cubic SAT**. These are powers of the program experiment's $2^q$ scale. For the fixed-program version this means choosing a new, fixed program text with the enlarged block parameter, with $q=2m$ for its two $m$-bit coordinates. Its earlier Strassen-based text still computes its originally specified outputs. The missing mathematical input is still a low-rank approximation to the specified *actual program outputs* at infinitely many scales. [Pinned square-rank corollary](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/build/intro.tex)

A second candidate would verify a genuinely global certificate for a sign of the [priority computation](priority-pinned-sat.md) by generating three Boolean matrices of side $H$ and checking a prescribed parity $\mathrm{tr}(ABC)$ over $\mathbb F_2$. A chart of $b$ guessed bits and entry generation cost $D$ gives ordinary nondeterministic verification in $(b+H^2D+H^{9/4+\epsilon})\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time, including the final inner product with $C$. At native scale $M=2^j$, the range $1/(3k)<\log_M H<4/(9k)$ allows cubic enumeration to exceed the $M^{1/k}$ budget while multiplication fits with fixed exponent slack. This criterion also needs a sound chart, short entry generation and infinitely many genuine successes. A batch of parity contradictions on at most $3H^2$ addresses already has a contradiction using at most $3H^2+1$ original rows, by taking a basis of the augmented binary rows. In that case the rows can be guessed and their XOR checked directly; enumerating all triangles adds no advantage. Freivalds' random vector cannot simply be an existential certificate of a correct matrix product, since a wrong product can agree on a chosen vector.

The unit-clause proof establishes a consequence of the cited preprint. Priority for this corollary is unverified.
