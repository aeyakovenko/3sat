# How many shared answers can hide the missing output?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Let $M=2^n$ and let $v$ be the actual first $n$-bit word missed by the native interpreter at cutoff $B=M^{20}$ on all names shorter than $n$. Different names can depend on the same unknown result of a long subcomputation. Two names which output $x$ on opposite answers certify that some name outputs $x$, even when neither name has its own fixed outcome. I tested how far such sharing can go. A family of universally injective tables that can hide the actual hole needs essentially $n$ queried bits along some row, even when the answers obey global affine constraints. A separate bound applies to ordinary programs with no injectivity promise. ## Injective completions and dyadic probabilities

Let a common vector of hidden Boolean answers range over a nonempty affine subspace $E$ of a Boolean cube over $\mathbb F_2$. Each of the $M-1$ names outputs a label by a deterministic tree adaptively querying at most $k$ bits of the same vector. Assume that every allowed assignment gives an injection and some allowed assignment gives a table missing $v$. This last premise holds if the family contains the actual completed table. No distributional assumption is made about that particular assignment.

Analyze the uniform distribution on $E$. An individual path fixes a conjunction of bits. Its probability is zero if inconsistent, and otherwise $2^{-r}$ where $r\le k$ is the codimension it cuts out in $E$. Consequently every row's probability of returning $x$ is an integer multiple of $2^{-k}$. Injectivity makes the probability that $x$ is the unique missing word exactly one minus the sum of those row probabilities. Every possible hole therefore has mass at least $2^{-k}$, and **there are at most $2^k$ possible holes**. The local bound can use the largest codimension of a feasible path returning $x$ in place of $k$, taking zero for an empty list of paths. In the independent-bit case the indicator that $x$ is absent is also a real multilinear polynomial of degree at most $k$.

Lean checks the finite identity counting absences across injective tables and the support bound after clearing the common denominator. The affine path probabilities are proved above. [Lean file](../lean/SharedAnswerMass.lean)

The cardinality bound is exact for every $0\le k\le n$. Start with one name at each label except $M-1$. Choose a block of $2^k$ labels containing $M-1$ and a $k$-bit common selector naming one label $y$ in it. The name at $y$, if present, outputs $M-1$; all other names return their own labels. Every table is injective and omits the selected $y$. An exhaustive experiment tested all 331,776 families of three-name injections on the four settings $(a,b,a\mathbin{\oplus} b)$. The maximum possible-hole counts at exact query depths zero, one and two were respectively one, two and four. [Experiment](../audits/shared_answer_coverage_experiment.py)

For the native consequence, assume $k\le n$, affine systems with at most $M^2$ coordinates and equations, and trees and equations constructible in $M^8\operatorname{poly}(n)$ deterministic target-RAM time. The trees then have at most $M^2$ leaves in all. Compute the marginal mass at each leaf by binary Gaussian elimination, costing at most $M^6\operatorname{poly}(n)$ per leaf, and accumulate exact integer multiples of $2^{-k}$. Rank possible holes by their labels or by decreasing mass. The whole calculation fits the native clock, including polynomial-in-$n$ interpretation overhead. A fixed native decoder can emit $v$ given its raw rank payload. Incompressibility therefore gives at least $M/O(1)$ possible holes and mass at most $O(1/M)$ at $v$. Hence $k\ge n-O(1)$, with the same bound for the local maximum codimension at $v$. This implication for explicitly constructed trees is **unconditional**. If an efficient construction of such trees for the actual completion, with $k\le n-\omega(1)$ infinitely often, relies on a hypothetical cubic SAT algorithm to meet the same construction clock, that would exclude cubic SAT. The needed construction is unproved. [Native rank argument](predecessor-batches.md)

There is also a bounded-multiplicity version. Permit timeouts and suppose at most $A$ names return any one label on each allowed assignment. Let $m_x$ denote its multiplicity and $D$ the expected total number of missing labels. The exact identity

$$
\mathbf{1}_{m_x=0}=\sum_{j=0}^A(-1)^j\binom{m_x}{j}
$$

expresses each absence probability as a sum of joint path probabilities for at most $A$ distinct rows. Each feasible joint path has codimension at most $Ak$. Thus at most $D2^{Ak}$ labels can ever be absent. More precisely, at a possible hole $x$ the mass is at least $2^{-\kappa_x}$, where $\kappa_x$ is the maximum codimension of any feasible intersection of at most $A$ paths from distinct names returning $x$, including the empty intersection. When these masses can be tabulated inside $B$ and $v$ is absent for at least one assignment, ranking the normalized masses forces $\kappa_v\ge n-\log_2 D-O(1)$. For example, the foregoing explicit budgets allow direct intersection enumeration for $A\le6$ and $k\le n$: at most $O(M^{2A})$ tuples, each costing $M^6\operatorname{poly}(n)$. The numerical support bound holds without an enumeration-time premise.

## Many output labels competing for the same rules

Drop the injection promise. A published rule consists of a conjunction of shared-answer literals and one output label $x$. On some actual assignment in $E$, every satisfied rule guarantees that $x$ really is printed by a short name by $B$. Call $x$ forced if the union of its rules covers all of $E$. Then $v$ is unforced, and every true predecessor of $v$ can be tested for this stronger form of certification. Let $L$ be the total number of rules over **all** labels. Assume the same construction and affine-system budgets, with at most $\operatorname{poly}(n)$ literal positions per conjunction.

Unconditionally determine the forced labels having at most $n$ rules each, obtaining a set $S_0$. Inclusion–exclusion uses at most $2^n$ intersections per label. Gaussian elimination gives each intersection's exact cardinality in $E$; the numbers have at most $M^2+O(n)$ bits, even for intermediate signed sums. All $M$ answers fit $M^8\operatorname{poly}(n)$ time. Also tabulate the heavy set $H$ of labels with more than $n$ rules. Its density is at most $L/[M(n+1)]$.

For every $0\le K\le v$ the earlier gap rank bound gives at least $(K+1)/C_0-1$ elements outside $S_0$ in $[v-K,v)$. The one-sided density bound for the computable set $H$ puts at most $C_1 K |H|/M$ heavy labels in the same interval. Both constants depend on fixed decoders. Since each forced label outside $S_0$ is heavy, the number of **genuine but unforced** predecessors in that interval is at least

$$
(K+1)/C_0-1-C_1 K L/[M(n+1)].
$$

In particular if $L=o(Mn)$ this is a positive fraction on every growing trailing interval. Every unforced label has some legal assignment avoiding all its certification rules; these assignments may differ across labels. For a single depth-$k$ tree per name, $L\le(M-1)2^k$, so $2^k=o(n)$ suffices for this obstruction even with unrestricted sharing of the queried bits. [Gap and one-sided density inequalities](predecessor-batches.md)

For one target, three queried bits already encode arbitrary 3-CNF unsatisfiability: one name per clause returns the target when the clause is falsified, and a dummy otherwise. The target is forced exactly for unsatisfiable formulas. Width two on the free cube gives 2-SAT after complementing each term. The aggregate rule bound explains why the three-bit construction at one target does not give true predecessor coverage at the native hole. [Aspvall, Plass and Tarjan](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(79)90002-4) · [Schaefer's classification](https://doi.org/10.1145/800133.804350)

Nonlinear promises can carry the entire difficulty. Take hidden flags with at most one flag $b_p$ set. Name $p<M-1$ returns $M-1$ when $b_p=1$, otherwise $p$. The holes are exactly the allowed flagged positions, together with $M-1$ when the all-zero flag is allowed. Restrict the flag patterns by any existential circuit predicate on the selected label, using auxiliary hidden bits. This realizes any nonempty projected set of possible holes with one query per row. The quantifier or nonlinear constraint replaces the affine counting step. A proof that the actual hole belongs to such a small, quickly recognizable projected set would advance the SAT argument; arbitrary flags give no such proof.
