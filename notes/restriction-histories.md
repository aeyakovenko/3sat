# How many errors can a succinct history survive?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** A satisfying history can differ from an easily evaluated assignment at many more than the usual circuit-size threshold and still give a hierarchy contradiction. The useful parameter is the number of clauses incident to the corrections. On bounded-occurrence encodings this gives a Hamming-distance threshold approaching the entire clock in its exponent. Restricting a SAT decision procedure to neighborhoods of increasing radius tests a candidate source of approximate assignments.

## Verify scattered corrections without putting the correction table in a SAT query

Let F have M clauses of width at most three. A circuit D of description length at most U maps a clause index to its signed variable names. Use O(log M)-bit names, and charge the cost of generating D. Suppose each variable occurs in at most Δ clauses. A candidate witness consists of a circuit W of size s for an assignment indexed by variable name and h distinct coordinates at which its bit is to be flipped. Represent the domain by valid variable names. Unused names may be set freely. Write S=max(1,s,U,log M). Assume deterministic SAT on m explicit encoded bits costs O(mᵈ), for d=2 or 3.

There is a nondeterministic check with time

    Õ(Sᵈ + (1+hΔ) S^(d−1) + h (1+Δ) (U+log M)ᵈ).

Logarithmic factors in M and the description sizes are suppressed. With a supplied reverse-incidence algorithm listing all clauses of any named variable in time polylog M, the last term can be replaced by Õ(h(1+Δ) polylog M). For typical local reductions U=polylog M, either bound gives Õ(Sᵈ + (1+hΔ) S^(d−1)), with fixed powers of log M suppressed. Here and below s and h are upper bounds that the verifier can cap, and lengths and clocks are computable.

1. Guess W and the correction coordinates. For each coordinate guess a list of its incident clause indices, of length at most Δ. Evaluate D on entries to confirm incidence. Certify completeness of this one variable's list by asking whether any clause index in each gap between successive entries, including the two ends, mentions this variable. Each query uses D and two index bounds. A SAT answer invalidates the list. This step is unnecessary when reverse incidence is already available.
2. Sort and deduplicate the union J of these occurrence lists. Its size is at most hΔ. Check every clause in J directly under the corrected assignment, using an ordinary sorted table for correction membership and evaluating W on its at most three literals.
3. Partition the ordered domain of clause indices into O(1+|J|/g) consecutive segments, each containing at most g elements of J, for g=max(1,⌊S⌋). For example split immediately after every g-th listed element and include the trailing interval. In each segment make one SAT query for an index outside its local list whose clause is false under W. The query circuit computes D and at most three evaluations of W. Range comparisons and exclusions cost O((g+1)log M) gates. Incorporate input and gate-name bit lengths in the explicit Tseitin encoding. Each query costs Õ(Sᵈ). Outside J the corrected assignment and W agree on every occurring variable, so these tests cover every remaining clause.

For h=0 the same construction directly checks W. For h>0 it never embeds the full table of h corrections in a single SAT input unless that table is already small. Guessing, checking the numeric ranges and order, sorting, and evaluating the touched clauses fit the stated bound on a word RAM. For h much larger than s, raising h+s to the power d would give a strictly weaker analysis. If many corrections have no occurrences they can be omitted. More generally hΔ can be replaced in the ledger by the sum of the sizes of the verified incidence lists, while retaining the O(h) separate completeness tests.

## A bounded-degree encoding of a hierarchy computation

Take k input bits and a nondeterministic hierarchy language of time T=2ᵏ outside nondeterministic time T/k² on multitape machines. Jahanjou, Miles and Viola's local reduction produces an equisatisfiable width-three formula with T poly(k) clauses and a poly(k)-size circuit enumerating clauses. The construction above can be applied to a bounded-degree version of this formula. The following conversion supplies both directions of incidence locally without having to invert the original clause map. [Local reductions, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3171) · [Time hierarchy and simulation](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

Allocate a fresh Boolean value for each of the three literal **occurrences** in every clause, interpreted as the value before applying its sign. Require the signed values in the three slots to satisfy their original clause. Carry each occurrence's unsigned name and proposed value through a fixed bitonic sorting network keyed on name. At the input, pin the name bits to the outcome of the original clause generator on that slot. After the sort require adjacent equal names to have equal proposed values. Pad to a power of two using a dummy key from a separate namespace. Implement sorting and checking by Boolean gates with equivalence clauses and the required units. Satisfiability in either direction follows by consistent copying or by reading the common value in each equal-key group. Sorting can break ties either way, provided payloads follow their records. [Batcher's sorting network](https://doi.org/10.1145/1468075.1468121)

Here the number of occurrence records is T poly(k), there are O(log² T) layers, and each comparator has a poly(k)-size circuit. Use binary fanout trees within each comparator for the decision controlling many bitwise multiplexers. Each wire between components participates in a bounded number of gates or clauses. A gate's output has bounded fanout, so each variable occurs in at most an absolute constant number of clauses. To enumerate either a clause or the neighbors of a variable, decode a layer, wire and position in a fixed comparator or equality gadget. Comparator partners are obtained by XOR with the stage's power of two. Original clause checks reference only three consecutive occurrence slots, and a name unit or literal sign reads the original clause generator on that index. This gives poly(k)-time enumeration in both directions. Use regular padded local gadgets and constrainless unused slots or local tautologies on fresh variables if needed for indexing. The new number of clauses and variables is T poly(k), and all indices have O(k) bits. Witness distances below concern the new encoding, including its auxiliary wires.

For α≥0 and β≥0 with dα<1 and (d−1)α+β<1, use S=T^α poly(k) and h=⌊T^β⌋. A suitable small circuit within h errors of any true assignment would be guessed and checked within time T^(1−ε) for some fixed ε>0. A nondeterministic tape simulation of a polynomial-clock RAM computation uses polylogarithmic overhead by guessing and sorting its access transcript, including the deterministic SAT calls. A false input can never pass. If this proximity held on all but finitely many true inputs, hardwiring the exceptions would contradict the hierarchy. Thus **on infinitely many satisfiable outputs, every satisfying assignment is farther than h from every allowed circuit**. With any fixed s=poly(k), the same argument yields h=⌊T/kᴬ⌋ for sufficiently large fixed A, accounting for all logarithmic losses. This permits almost T errors in the exponent, where a single SAT call containing the correction list only allowed about T^(1/d). No claim of a constant fractional distance in the full T poly(k)-variable assignment follows.

## Query the solver on neighborhoods to predict forced bits

For a specified variable v, start with v and take r rounds of adding clauses incident to reached variables and then every variable in those clauses. Call the conjunction of all reached clauses Fᵣ(v). Since degrees and clause widths are constant, its explicit encoding takes at most cʳ poly(k) bits for some absolute c>1, and it can be generated in cʳ poly(k) RAM time with a suitable c by the bidirectional indexing just described. Restrict v to zero and run the hypothesized SAT algorithm on this smaller formula. Predict zero if the answer is SAT and one otherwise. On a globally satisfiable instance the prediction agrees with every globally satisfying assignment at every v where the neighborhood forces its bit.

This prediction has a circuit on the name v of size at most Cʳ poly(k) for a fixed C>1 depending on the machine and d. The input x and its clause generator may be built into the circuit. For a direct bound, unroll a t-step logarithmic-word deterministic RAM computing one predicted bit. Retrieve each dynamic read by scanning the at most t preceding addressed writes with a priority selection, with polylogarithmic Boolean overhead per possible match. Fixed initial input memory is small apart from the neighborhood generated by the machine itself. This costs Õ(t²) gates and suffices since t=c^{O(r)}poly(k). Outside the variable-name range assign any default bit. This construction uses the decision algorithm on real explicit subformulas and makes one prediction uniformly for all centers v.

For every fixed β<1 with β≥0, choose α>0 satisfying the two strict inequalities above, then a small constant δ>0 so that r=⌊δ log₂ T⌋ gives Cʳ poly(k)≤T^α on large inputs. On infinitely many satisfiable outputs **each** global solution has more than T^β coordinates where it takes value one although zero has a locally satisfying extension at radius r. At each such coordinate one also has a local extension to value one by restricting the global solution. This yields quantitative local ambiguity even at radii proportional to the logarithm of the computation clock. Taking fixed r allows the inverse-polylogarithmic loss version. This argument applies to a nondeterministic hierarchy language. Its computation need not have a unique accepting trace.

**Allow decision-directed pins.** Fix any global solution a on one of the hard outputs and reveal true values on any p variables, chosen adaptively with unlimited information about a. Add those units to each neighborhood test. A center farther than r variable-to-variable incidence steps from every pin sees exactly the old optimum zero/one feasibility bit. Consistent pins outside its local formula can be assigned independently. At most p cʳ centers can change (increase c by a constant if needed). Therefore more than h−p cʳ coordinates still disagree with a under the revised prediction. The inequality holds simultaneously for every selection rule and every choice of p consistent pins because the original distance guarantee already holds for every a. In particular an adaptive method reconstructing every remaining bit by the radius-r rule requires p>=(h−q)/cʳ on those inputs if it allows q prediction errors. This counts revealed variables and rounds of local propagation. The solver may extract other global information from the given formula that these pins do not describe.

**Test the repair premise.** A single failed local constraint under a prediction gives no small upper bound on the needed corrections, even with a unique satisfying assignment. Take an equality chain on L bits with the first bit pinned true and predict zero everywhere. Precisely the anchor fails and all L bits must change. For a center more than r edges from the anchor, both bit values extend locally. The all-one solution itself has a tiny circuit. The hierarchy argument above rules out *all* small predictors on its hard instances at the stated error radii. Explicit Hamming-ball search also has a long history, generally with exponential dependence on the radius. Here nondeterminism supplies corrections and succinct SAT checks certify the constraints between them. [Moser and Scheder](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.4067)

A route to the requested time lower bound would need to obtain sufficient predictions for a hierarchy verifier despite this conditional obstruction, for example by exploiting other structure when many locally feasible guesses disagree with every complete computation. The assumption that a solver answers restrictions correctly gives the local extension tests. It has not supplied the needed global agreement.
