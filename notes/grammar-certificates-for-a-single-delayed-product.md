# How small can a SAT witness be for a delayed-product history?

For the [one-variable-delay forecast](one-variable-delay-and-one-binary-product.md), there is an ordinary SAT verifier whose size depends on the grammar size of an actual history. The verifier checks equality of compressed strings and the local tape transition; its work is polynomial in the logarithm of the expanded clock per grammar rule. On an entire positive prefix of the forecast inputs, however, **every** such grammar needs $\Omega(W^4)$ rules, matching an upper bound for every history. The accepting bit on that prefix is constantly one.

## A verifier for a compressed trajectory

After the fixed boot, let $C_0$ be the initial $W$-cell row and $C_1,\ldots,C_H$ its successive configurations with $H=W^4$. Use one terminal at column $i$ of step $j$ for the ordered pair $(C_{j-1}[i],C_j[i])$, marked first, last or interior in its row. Concatenate the $H$ rows of terminals. A straight-line program (SLP) has terminal rules or rules concatenating two earlier rules, with a designated start rule; it expands to exactly one word. Its size $g$ counts rules. The alphabet is constant for the fixed tape machine.

Given a guessed grammar of at most $g$ rules, first discard unreachable rules and compute expansion lengths bottom-up, saturating at $WH+1$. Require total length $WH$. Check all of the following.

* The row-marker projection equals the $H$-fold repeat of the prescribed $W$-symbol marker pattern. The comparator pattern has a grammar of size $O(\log W+\log H)$ by binary repetition.
* A fixed finite automaton checks the local transition at every column using old symbols and the proposed new center. Retain two consecutive terminals in its state as needed. At the first column check the permanent wall; for the last column take the wall as its right neighbor. Flags delimit the rows. Calculate the automaton's state transformation at every grammar rule by composition on its constant state set.
* Write $U,V$ for the entire old and new symbol projections, respectively. Compare the first $W$ symbols of $U$ with the specified boot row. Compare $U$ after dropping its first $W$ symbols with $V$ after dropping its last $W$ symbols. Finally check the verdict track at cell 1 of the last new row.

The vertical comparison forces each proposed row to start where the previous transition finished. Determinism therefore identifies exactly one expanded history; different grammars may describe it. Letter projections preserve the grammar size. A prefix or suffix ending at a specified index requires following one root-to-terminal path using the expansion lengths and collecting at most one neighboring subtree at each step. This creates $O(g)$ additional rules even in an unbalanced grammar. Equality of two strings compressed as SLPs is deterministic in $O(g\log(WH+2))$ time on a RAM with words large enough for their lengths, via recompression. Polynomial factors in $\log(W+g+2)$ suffice for bit costs and smaller native words. [Jeż, *Recompression of SLPs*](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2017.2) · [Jeż, detailed algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3244)

For $g\le W^{O(1)}$ the computation, including reading the grammar, is checkable by ordinary proper 3-CNF of $O((g+1)\operatorname{polylog}(W+2))$ bits, constructible in the same bound up to logarithmic factors. Guess the grammar and a clocked deterministic verifier transcript. Check instructions and word arithmetic locally; sort address/time records and check each inherited value. Convert these circuits to clauses using equivalences and pin the final verdict. Small clauses can be padded with fixed false anchors. The constants in the logarithmic factors depend only on the fixed verifier. No oracle for compressed equality or tensor-network cancellation is assumed in the SAT formula.

For $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ a uniform guarantee $g\le W^{1/k-\xi}$ on every *accepting* history, for fixed $0<\xi<1/k$, would meet the wide-word compiler threshold. Emit the verifier with that grammar budget; rejecting forecasts have no accepting trajectory of any size. The next construction falsifies this candidate guarantee.

## A positive prefix exhausting the grammar bound

Give the fixed tape machine an additional prefix mode, alongside the simulation and diagonal modes. After measuring $W$, reserve $m=4\lfloor\log_2 W\rfloor-B$ adjacent cells of a designated data track, with a unique marked delimiter immediately to their left and at least $2m+2$ cells of clearance on either side, for a sufficiently large fixed integer $B$. For large $W$ this fits. Count in binary through all $m$-bit strings on these cells. Use the one tape head to increment from the low end, flip trailing ones, change the next zero, and return to the low end. A marked end cell detects overflow. The total number of flipped trailing ones over a full count is less than $2^m$, apart from an $O(m)$ endpoint allowance; the returns and fixed changes take $O(2^m+m)$ time altogether. At some configuration each possible digit string is displayed. The head and the finite control can be on a separate track. Pick $B$ large enough that startup, the complete count, and return to cell 1 occupy fewer than $W^4$ rows for all sufficiently large $W$. Latch one.

For every displayed value and each $j=0,\ldots,m$, take the length-$(2m+2)$ substring starting $j$ cells before the marked delimiter in that row. All these windows contain the full display. Project the new-symbol track to the delimiter and digit data. The unique delimiter's offset distinguishes $j$, and the following $m$ bits distinguish the value. Thus the expanded word of pairs contains $\Omega(m2^m)$ different substrings of length $2m+2$. A binary concatenation grammar with $g$ rules has at most $g(a-1)$ distinct length-$a$ substrings for $a\ge2$. Descend through the parse tree until the occurrence crosses a production boundary. The rule and its left-side length identify the substring. Hence $g=\Omega(2^m)=\Omega(W^4)$. For fixed-length binary encoding of the terminals the same aligned windows have bit length $O(m)$ and remain distinct. The substring bound is standard in grammar compression. [String attractors and grammar boundaries](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.05314)

Allowing a rule to extract a substring creates no new factors. Allowing a rule $X\mapsto X^a$ with a binary repetition count introduces at most $b-1$ new length-$b$ factors: an occurrence crossing copies is specified by its positive distance less than $b$ to the first crossed copy boundary. This works even when $|X|<b$. The same lower bound therefore applies to grammars with these two extra operations.

The bound survives approximate storage. One symbol substitution, insertion or deletion can destroy at most $a$ distinct old length-$a$ factors, since any destroyed factor had an occurrence meeting the edit boundary. Thus any grammar of $g$ rules for a word at edit distance $e'$ from the paired history satisfies $g+O(e')=\Omega(W^4)$ on this prefix, by counting at $a=2m+2$. Keeping any constant fraction of the distinct displayed rows also preserves the order of the lower bound even if all other rows are discarded and the retained rows are reordered; the counted windows lie entirely inside each display row.

Conversely the fixed tape implementation changes only the old head cell and its immediate neighborhood at a step. Keep immutable linked stacks for the diagonal pairs $(a,a)$ of tape symbols strictly to either side of the head. For the left stack store its SLP in left-to-right order by appending each pushed terminal; for the right stack prepend instead. Link each pushed node to its previous stack, so a pop selects an existing rule. Each transition row is the concatenation of two intact stacks and constantly many old/new pairs at the head and its immediate neighbors, with their fixed endpoint marks. Pop or push only a bounded number of symbols to build the next stacks. This uses $O(1)$ new binary productions per step, including concatenation of the row roots, after $O(W)$ initialization. It also handles a stationary head and indefinitely many visits to the same cell. Every genuine history thus has a grammar of size $O(W+H)=O(W^4)$. The worst-case number of rules for this certificate scheme is $\Theta(W^4)$.

A proof system recognizing the counter operation symbolically could accept this easy branch immediately; its behavior on the self-simulation branches is the outstanding question.

The finite audit checked 1,048,576 transition rows at width 32, including all 8,192 counter displays in a reduced example. It rejected 30 damaged histories covering vertical consistency, local rule, frame and endpoint tests. The recompression time bound uses Jeż's algorithm.

## Two fixed substitutions for the counter transitions

The ripple counter itself has a short description if a rule can apply a fixed substitution to every letter of an earlier word. Let $P_m$ contain the **actual paired transitions** from HOME on the left delimiter with $m$ zero digits through the first arrival of CARRY on the right delimiter $R$. It ends just before executing the overflow instruction there. Work first with compact rows holding the delimiters and the digits. For $b=0,1$ let $\theta_b$ replace a paired $R$ cell by a paired digit $b$ carrying its old and new head states, followed by a plain paired $R$. Other cells are unchanged. Keep row markers separate while building the word, then mark the endpoint cells by a fixed recoding.

The base $P_0$ is the single transition from HOME on the left to CARRY on $R$. For $m\ge1$ the chronological word is
$$
 P_m=\theta_0(P_{m-1})\, B_m\, \theta_1(P_{m-1})\, E_m.
$$
The first half ends with CARRY on the high zero and all lower digits zero. $B_m$ sets the high bit, walks back over those lower digits and restores HOME. The second half ends with CARRY on the high one. $E_m$ is the one transition clearing that bit and entering $R$. This identifies both configurations of every transition across the joins. Each $B_m$ consists of $O(m)$ explicitly writable rows of $O(m)$ cells. Reusing the earlier root under both substitutions gives $O(m^3)$ rules through level $m$. Constant blank padding on each side can be generated from its binary length by substitutions that double padding runs and optionally insert one cell at a delimiter. Overflow and the final repeated stationary row add $O(\log W+\log H)$ rules for the blank-framed counter with a stationary accept on $R$. The audit reproduced all 5,030 transition rows of the counter prefixes through nine bits. [Counter substitution and transition audit](../audits/grammar_feedback_trace_audit.py)

The polylogarithmic count describes the initialized blank-framed counter segment. A separate boot, return to cell 1 or arbitrary input background must also be represented in a certificate for the entire original computation. For example, a fixed arbitrary prefix and suffix of each row can be attached in $O(W)$ insertion rules from a finite substitution alphabet. Static auxiliary symbols on the active digits can be retained when inserting each high digit, using one substitution per possible auxiliary symbol and bit. Boot and return runs of $s$ steps have the preceding $O(W+s)$ stack construction. This counter prefix has constant accepting verdict. The construction gives no bound for the simulation and diagonal branches. Jeż's equality theorem used by the proper-SAT verifier applies to ordinary SLPs. Verifying arbitrary certificates with nested substitution rules at comparable cost would require its own algorithm.

## Checking a row-aligned substitution witness by ordinary SAT

Restrict the syntax as follows. A base history is one paired row stored by an ordinary SLP. A concatenation joins two lists of complete rows. A substitution applies one member of a fixed finite dictionary to every row of a child. Its map $f$ takes each single cell to a nonempty word of bounded length. On each reachable paired letter $(a,b)$ require $|f(a)|=|f(b)|$ and emit the pointwise pairing of the two images. Keep row delimiters fixed and exclude them from cell images. Track the occurring alphabet at every node and reject an unaligned application. The two high-bit insertions obey this condition on the counter rows. Let $g$ count all rules, including all shared base-row SLPs. The verifier requires vertical consistency at every reachable history node; this is an explicit requirement on certificates. A later substitution may otherwise hide an earlier mismatch.

After pruning unused nodes, propagate row counts and the multiplicities of each letter of the fixed alphabet. Cap row counts at $H$ and expanded lengths at the size of the framed $WH$-cell history, including its delimiters. Saturated reachable nodes can be rejected since all maps are nonerasing. Check that the root has $H$ rows. It suffices to compare the root's first old row with $C_0$, then check each internal join: every single paired row already has equal old and new widths. At a concatenation compare the last new row of the left child with the first old row of the right child. At a substitution, equality of any two consecutive interface configurations is preserved by applying the same $f$ to each. Induction proves vertical consistency at the root, and its correct first width then forces width $W$ throughout the root trajectory.

One requested endpoint follows one path down the grammar. A concatenation chooses its first or last child. A substitution records one letter map. Given this list from outermost to innermost, start with the identity spelling of each letter. To append an inner map $f$, make the new spelling of $a$ by concatenating the current spellings of the letters in $f(a)$. The fixed alphabet and dictionary cost $O(1)$ ordinary SLP rules per layer. Substitute the final spellings into the corresponding old or new projection of the base-row SLP. This builds an ordinary SLP of $O(g+1)$ rules for that endpoint, irrespective of row width. All joins and the root endpoints together require $O((g+1)^2)$ rules. Compare endpoint strings with compressed equality and check the root's final verdict cell. For the forecast's prescribed structured $C_0$, use its explicit $O(\mathrm{polylog}(W+2))$-rule description. Supplying an arbitrary initial data row separately incurs the cost of its description.

A horizontal check solely on rows before substitution would be unsound. Instead evaluate the fixed finite automaton for marked rows and legal transitions on the fully transformed root. Let $M$ be its finite monoid of state transformations. For every grammar node $X$ and every assignment $\eta$ of a member of $M$ to each paired alphabet letter, tabulate the monoid product of the expanded word of $X$ under $\eta$. There are a constant number of assignments, depending on the fixed verifier. Concatenation multiplies two products. At a substitution node evaluate the child under the assignment sending $a$ to the product of $\eta$ along the image of $a$. Unused ill-typed letters can be assigned an arbitrary value after checking synchronization on the reachable ones. Apply the automaton's true letter assignment at the root. Base SLPs use the same constant-size multiplication tables. This costs $O(g+1)$ operations. Together these checks give a deterministic verifier in $O((g+1)^2\operatorname{polylog}(W+g+2))$ work and an ordinary proper-SAT encoding of the same order up to logarithms by the preceding bounded-computation reduction.

For the two high-bit insertions themselves, all join comparisons share an ordinary SLP with $O(g+1)$ rules. Require exactly one right delimiter $R$ per endpoint. Store an endpoint as three pieces: the prefix before $R$, the tagged $R$ itself, and the suffix after it. A bottom-up pass on each base SLP computes the needed prefix and suffix fragments, projecting onto old and new cells separately and capping delimiter counts at two. Concatenation inherits its first and last triples. For $\theta_b$, append one digit $b$ carrying the former $R$ head state to the prefix, replace $R$ by an unmarked $R$, and reuse the suffix. This takes $O(1)$ extra rules per endpoint. Concatenate all the left endpoints to be compared, separated by a fresh symbol, and do the same for the right endpoints. One compressed equality verifies them all. A common final chain of padding substitutions adds only one endpoint-path comparison to the prescribed initial row and one final verdict lookup. The proper-SAT certificate cost in this special case is $O((g+1)\operatorname{polylog}(W+g+2))$. The finite audit checks the shared join comparison against independent endpoint compilation. It also checks damaged joins, a substitution preserving alignment but invalidating the local tape rule, and a substitution with unequal expansion lengths on a reachable pair. [Row-grammar audit](../audits/grammar_feedback_trace_audit.py)

This certifies more than the accepting bit of the initialized counter segment: it checks the genuine execution described by the witness. The outstanding compiler requirement is a sufficiently small certificate of this kind on every positive simulation and diagonal input, including startup. The richer syntax has no such size bound here.
