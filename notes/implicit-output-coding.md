# One short program for all bit queries about a long sampled word

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Assume deterministic linear-time SAT in the unrestricted-workspace model of the project. Let a fixed native stream sampler publish a finite word, and write $P_t(y)$ for the probability it publishes $y$ by time $t$ on fair input. Fix an integer $b\ge1$. For every $t\ge2$ and $y$ with
$D=\lceil-\log_2P_t(y)\rceil\le(\log_2 t)^b$, there exists **one** program of length $D+O(1)$ with the following behavior. Given any bit position of $y$, it returns that bit in $t\,\mathrm{polylog}(t)$ time. It can also report an out-of-range position within the same bound for indices encoded in $O(\log t)$ bits. The program works for all positions of the same word and all constants are uniform in $y,t$. It receives neither the deadline nor a prior index for the word. Its length may be much smaller than the number of bits needed to specify $t$. The word can occupy the sampler's full output capacity.

The coding applies to any fixed Boolean test of the recovered word with an auxiliary query, as long as checking the test at the relevant epoch fits its simulated clock up to a fixed polylogarithmic factor. For the position queries above, every bit test has this property. This result uses only the [near-clock counting and interval decoder](near-clock-coding.md). The following is the additional step that avoids guessing an entire list of large outputs.

## Distinguishing all members of a batch with short field elements

At epoch $q\ge1$ use step bound $s=2^q$ and allowed depths $0\le d\le q^b+5$. A word is new for this batch if its factor-32 lower probability estimate has first attained $2^{-d}$ at an eligible epoch. Word length is bounded only by the native output capacity at that epoch. A word available by time $s$ has at most $s\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$ explicit output bits. Evaluating the new-request predicate on a given word costs $s\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$, including comparisons to earlier epochs. Earlier epochs incapable of producing a word of its length contribute probability zero. At most $2^d$ distinct words qualify since each has true mass at least $2^{-d}$.

Represent a word injectively as the coefficient vector of a polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$, using a leading coefficient 1 to distinguish lengths. All degrees are at most a fixed capacity bound $H=s\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$ that is computed from the native convention. Take $k=2d+\lceil\log_2(H+2)\rceil+2$. A field $F$ of size $2^k$ can be specified by the lexicographically first irreducible binary polynomial of degree $k$. Its coefficients can be found one bit at a time by bounded existential completion and a universal check for a monic factor of degree strictly between zero and $k$, using polynomial time in $k$ under linear SAT. Such a polynomial exists for every positive $k$.

Fix two distinct qualifying outputs. The difference of their polynomials is nonzero and has at most $H$ roots in $F$. Fewer than $2^{2d}H+1$ field elements are bad for some pair, so some $\alpha$ makes evaluation at $\alpha$ injective on this whole batch. Use the least such $\alpha$. To test a candidate, universally quantify two explicit words of length at most $H$ (self-delimit their lengths within the fixed bound), check their new-request predicates and compare their evaluations. Horner evaluation by word scanning costs $H\,\mathrm{poly}(k)$. The fixed number of quantified blocks and the $k$ adaptive prefix-completion tests (guess an extension before checking the pair condition) to find the least good $\alpha$ can therefore be resolved in $s\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$ time under the hypothesis. This is deterministic even though the identities of the qualifying outputs may be unknown to the decoder.

Each batch member now has a unique $k$-bit field value. A proposed value qualifies exactly if some explicit new-request word evaluates to it. This existential test fits the same clock. Apply the prefix counts, approximate arithmetic subdivision and exact bit-consumption construction of the [longer-output coding proof](near-clock-coding.md) with $h=k$. Its width estimates require counting tuples of polynomially many such field values and can use a fresh explicit output witness when checking a tuple component. Only distinct field values are counted. The batch reserves at most twice the ideal number of code slots after normalization. Once the short code identifies a field value in the selected batch, ask whether a qualifying word with that value exists with the selected bit equal to one (or with length exceeding the position). Injectivity forces the correct unique answer. The decoder tests previous batches independently of the requested position. In particular the same code works for every bit.

Given $t,y$ in the statement, epoch $q=\lceil\log_2 t\rceil$ permits $d=D+5$ and its estimate meets the threshold. The code from the first eligible epoch has length $D+O(1)$ and its clock fits $t\,\mathrm{polylog}(t)$. If the winning epoch is much earlier than $t$, a long encoded query can first be compared to its output bound, charging its bit length separately. For $O(\log t)$-bit indices this fits the stated clock.

## A narrower target for a counterexample

A counterexample may therefore focus on the complexity of **one representation serving every coordinate**. For each proposed short program one may choose a different failing coordinate. To contradict the consequence, a fixed ordinary sampler must have heavy outputs for which this defeats all programs of length $D+c$ within $t(\log(2+t))^A$, for arbitrarily large time and length allowances $A,c$ and one fixed $b$. The program may inspect every cell reachable in its time budget. A lower bound against a data structure limited to a fixed number of probes would leave this case open.

I tried to turn the hash value into an equally fast program printing the whole long word. The decoder has a fixed-block uniqueness test for the output. Repeating it once per coordinate gives time $|y|\,t\,\mathrm{polylog}(t)$. The existence of one consistent vector of answers does not itself supply a deterministic RAM routine that writes that vector in the smaller clock. Naik, Regan and Sivakumar, Definition 4.1, allow an arbitrary witness relation in their formulation of search reducing to decision. Under the present hypothesis an empty witness with the SAT decider as checker would already meet that definition for SAT. It would convey no bits of a specified satisfying assignment. [On Quasilinear Time Complexity Theory](https://cse.buffalo.edu/~regan/papers/pdf/NRS95.pdf)

## Printing the whole word from a low-width subset of its random tapes

Fix an integer $a\ge1$ and put $q=\lceil\log_2(2+t)\rceil$. Let $F_{t,y}\subseteq\{0,1\}^t$ contain the random tapes on which the sampler publishes $y$ by $t$; unused trailing bits are free. Suppose $F_{t,y}$ contains the accepting set of a length-$t$ branching program that reads the tape once in its original order, has width at most $2^{q^a}$, and accepts a fraction at least $2^{-q^a}$. Then, under linear SAT, **one deterministic program prints all of $y$** within $t\,\mathrm{polylog}(t)$ steps, using at most $\lceil-\log_2 P_t(y)\rceil+O(1)$ bits. The bound on code length uses the whole fiber's mass. The branching program can depend nonuniformly on both $y$ and $t$.

To obtain a timely index, combine layer and node into a state, giving at most $(t+1)2^{q^a}+2$ states including terminals. Nisan's generator with block length $m=C(q^a+q+1)$ and tree depth $k=\max(0,\lceil\log_2(t/m)\rceil)$ produces at least $t$ bits. The finite-state test groups the steps of the given program into $m$-bit blocks and ignores the final padding. For sufficiently large absolute $C$, Nisan's block theorem gives acceptance error less than $2^{-q^a-1}$, so at least one seed drives the sampler to $y$. It suffices for the decoder to know the sampler. The branching program serves only in the existence proof. [Nisan, Sections 2–4](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/CourseWeb/Math268_2013W/Nisan_PRG.pdf)

The seed consists of one $m$-bit root and $k$ hash functions. Nisan's binary convolution family with an additive shift uses $3m-1$ bits per hash. In the recursive tree a node labeled $x$ has children $x$ and $h(x)$, using the hash for that level. A depth-first traversal uses $2^k-1$ hash evaluations at $O(m^2)$ bit operations each. Including seed parsing and writing the leaves, expansion costs $O((1+t/m)m^2+km)$ operations. A fixed native procedure can thus read the encoded $t$ and seed (together $O(m(q+1))$ bits), generate the tape and simulate the sampler by $t'=t\,\mathrm{polylog}(t)$. The short-index theorem at $t'$ gives the asserted description since $P_{t'}(y)\ge P_t(y)$ and $\log t'=O(q)$. [Nisan, Sections 2.2 and 3](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/CourseWeb/Math268_2013W/Nisan_PRG.pdf) · [Short-index coding theorem](near-clock-coding.md)

For instance, a sampler with a write-only output and polylogarithmically many bits of accessible configuration between coin reads has a branching program of this width for each individual fiber. Include elapsed time, the output position and a flag recording any mismatch against the fixed $y$. The transition between coin reads can incorporate the entire deterministic segment. This gives full printing whenever $-\log_2 P_t(y)\le q^a$ for some fixed $a$, including words as long as the sampler can write. For an unrestricted sampler, a dense low-width subset of a fiber suffices even when the complete fiber has high width.

### A heavy fiber with no dense low-width subset

One fixed sampler reads the Elias gamma code $h(m)$ of a positive integer, followed by two consecutive $m$-bit strings $x,z$. It stores $x$, reads $z$ in order, and prints $0^m$ exactly when $x\cdot z=1$ over $\mathbb F_2$. Otherwise it runs forever. Choose a uniform deadline $t_m=O(m\,\mathrm{polylog}(m+2))$ large enough for every successful run with that header. Distinct headers give distinct output lengths. Since $|h(m)|=2\lfloor\log_2 m\rfloor+1$ and each nonzero $x$ accepts half the $z$ strings, the exact mass is

$$P_{t_m}(0^m)=2^{-|h(m)|}(1-2^{-m})/2.$$

Consider any width-$w$ ordered read-once branching program whose accepted $t_m$-bit tapes all produce $0^m$. Fix the header $h(m)$ and any unused suffix after $z$. At the cut after $x$, each reachable state gives a rectangle $A\times B$ of accepted pairs. Its rows are the $x$ reaching that state, and its columns are the $z$ leading from it to acceptance with the fixed suffix. There are at most $w$ such rectangles. In a nonempty one, choose $x_0\in A$ and put $D=\operatorname{span}\{x-x_0:x\in A\}$. Then $A\subseteq x_0+D$, and each $z\in B$ lies in $D^\perp$ with $x_0\cdot z=1$. Since $B$ is nonempty, the latter functional is nonzero on $D^\perp$. Consequently $|A|\le 2^{\dim D}$ and $|B|\le 2^{m-\dim D-1}$.

Averaging over suffixes and multiplying by the header probability bounds the accepted fraction by $2^{-|h(m)|}w2^{-m-1}$. Every fixed width bound $w\le 2^{(\log(2+t_m))^a}$ therefore yields mass smaller than $2^{-(\log(2+t_m))^b}$ for all sufficiently large $m$, for every fixed $a,b$. This disproves the proposed dense-subset condition for general heavy fibers. The deterministic program that reads $h(m)$ and prints $m$ zeros already achieves the explicit coding bound for this example.

### Allowing any fixed order of the random bits

The preceding sampler becomes easy to recognize if corresponding $x$ and $z$ bits can be interleaved. A second fixed sampler works for every variable order, even one chosen separately at each deadline. After $h(m)$ it reads $N=m^2$ independent bits $u_v$ on an $m$-by-$m$ square grid and one more fair bit $z$. It prints $0^m$ if

$$z+\sum_{\{v,v'\}\text{ a grid edge}}u_v u_{v'}=1\pmod 2,$$

and runs forever otherwise. Reading in row order takes $O(m^2\,\mathrm{polylog}(m+2))$ time and only the preceding row and current scanning data in memory. At a matching deadline $t_m$, every $u$ accepts exactly one $z$, giving the exact mass $2^{-|h(m)|-1}$.

Take even $m$ and fix the header, $z$, and the unused suffix. In any proposed read-once order, cut after half of the grid bits have appeared. At least $m/4$ grid edges cross the cut. If at least $m/4$ rows contain both sides, each contributes a horizontal crossing. Otherwise both a wholly left row and a wholly right row must exist, since each side contains $m^2/2$ vertices. Every column then contributes a vertical crossing. The crossing graph has maximum degree four, and greedily selecting an edge and discarding all edges incident to a neighbor of either endpoint discards at most $32$ crossing edges per selection. The chosen edges form an induced matching of size at least $m/128$. The cross-cut adjacency matrix $M$ over $\mathbb F_2$ therefore has rank $r\ge m/128$, as the submatrix on matched endpoints is an identity matrix.

An accepting rectangle $A\times B$ at this cut has constant quadratic value. Evaluating it on the four combinations of $a,a_0\in A$ and $b,b_0\in B$ and adding cancels all terms internal to one side, giving $(a-a_0)^T M(b-b_0)=0$. Thus, for nonempty $A,B$, their difference spans $U,V$ satisfy $M^T U\subseteq V^\perp$. If the left side has $p$ variables, $\dim M^T U\ge\dim U-(p-r)$, hence $\dim U+\dim V\le N-r$. Each rectangle covers at most $2^{N-r}$ pairs. The $w$ states at the cut cover at most a fraction $w2^{-r}$ for every fixing, regardless of where the fixed coordinates occur in the order. Averaging over $z$ and the suffix gives unconditional accepted mass at most $2^{-|h(m)|}w2^{-m/128}$. Along even $m$, this rules out any dense sound subset of the stated width in any fixed permutation of the random tape. Encoding $m$ still suffices to print the whole output.

In fact this grid test can be added **after any sampler**. Buffer its output $y$, set $m=\lfloor\sqrt{|y|}\rfloor$ (round down to an even integer for the cut argument), and run the balanced grid test on fresh bits. Publish the original $y$ precisely on success. Use a fixed instruction schedule for the gate and copy the answer on success. Every finite completed run of the original sampler has exactly half of its extensions pass. Eventual masses are halved, and all the mass published by native time $t$ in the original is available up to this factor by $t\,\mathrm{polylog}(t)$ on the modified sampler.

For a word $y$ and a finite deadline, group the successful tapes according to the exact prefix consumed by the original sampler. These prefixes are disjoint. For each contributing prefix, all passing gate inputs finish by the same deadline because the gate schedule and the buffered word are fixed. Restrict any sound width-$w$ ordered branching program to that prefix and fix the unused suffix bits and the balance bit. This preserves width and leaves a fixed permutation of the grid variables. The rectangle estimate above bounds the accepted fraction by $w2^{-\Omega(m)}$. Average over the fixed bits and sum with the original prefix weights. Even **relative to the modified output's own timely mass**, the fraction captured by the branching program is at most $2w2^{-\Omega(\sqrt{|y|})}$ at large lengths. Allowing a different order for each word and deadline preserves the bound. Thus an ordinary sampler can acquire this structural obstruction on every long output while retaining its outputs and their probabilities within a fixed factor and a polylogarithmic time slowdown.

The finite fingerprint experiment tested 2,743 batches, including collisions at field elements other than zero and one. At the first injective field element all 35,170 bit-position and length tests passed. [Finite-field experiment](../audits/implicit_output_experiment.py)

## Polynomial witnesses for subsets of a fiber

A different sufficient condition for fast full printing is that some nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree $d=\mathrm{polylog}(t)$ on the first $t$ random bits vanishes outside the fiber for $y$. The coefficient field can be arbitrary. Its support contains an input of Hamming weight at most $d$: take the variable set of an inclusion-minimal nonzero coefficient and evaluate on its indicator. Specifying the positions of the ones and the tape length then gives a timely index of $O((d+1)\log(t+2))$ bits. Applying the short-index coding theorem charges the final program only for the full fiber's surprisal up to a constant. The decoder need not know the polynomial or its coefficients.

One can force this second sufficient condition to fail on the same ordinary sampler as the branching-program condition. After the grid test, also require a strict majority of an odd number $r=2\lfloor |y|/2\rfloor+1$ of fresh bits. Evaluate both tests on fixed schedules and publish $y$ only when both pass. All masses from completed source programs now retain exactly one quarter after the time allowance above. The width estimate still holds after fixing all majority bits. Every accepted tape for $y$ has more than $r/2$ ones in total, wherever its majority block begins. Hence a nonzero multilinear polynomial supported on the modified fiber vanishes at every input of weight at most $\lfloor r/2\rfloor$. The coefficient argument forces degree at least $\lfloor r/2\rfloor+1$ in every field. This holds for the genuine fiber at any finite deadline at which the word appears. Thus a fixed modification simultaneously forces high degree and small coverage by ordered branching programs on long outputs, while leaving deterministic descriptions of the words themselves available at their original printing costs. A proof based on either kind of certificate would need a property of how the sampler generates the answer beyond these tests on its tape.
