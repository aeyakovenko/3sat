# Where a four-port refutation must reach on the native pair

At stage $j$ put $M=2^j$. The two explicit formulas in the [priority-pinned construction](priority-pinned-sat.md) differ in one public terminal sign and have opposite satisfiability verdicts. Every fixed correct deterministic algorithm on the enriched promise needs $\Omega_D(M)$ steps on **both** pins at every sufficiently late stage. The direct native replay gives an $O(M)$ fallback. Thus a uniformly sound detector that refutes one of the two actual pins in $o(M)$ time on infinitely many stages is impossible. Run it on both presentations under its fast cutoff before invoking the fallback. The scale, pin, and location of the sign change are public throughout.

Here is a locally generated four-port realization with the following property. Given a quotient vertex identifier, its at most four neighbors in either graph are computable in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. A constant-size publicly located set of edges includes every difference between the two graphs. Write $A$ for the set of their endpoints in either presentation. There are $M\operatorname{poly}(j)$ graph records with $O(j)$-bit identifiers. We use the quotient graph with its inequality edges for all searches. The port instances realizing these graphs are generated locally as well.

## Building both presentations without a global orientation

The ordinary formula comes in fixed rectangular clause slots. There is a uniform way to avoid needing a fast *inverse* list of all uses of an arbitrary native variable. Give each of the three occurrences per clause a guessed value, verify its clause using its sign, and carry a record (variable name, occurrence index, value) through a fixed bitonic sorting circuit ordered by the first two fields. Adjacent records with equal names must have equal values. This is equivalent to the original CNF in both directions. Input name bits are pinned by the local formula generator; the original sign change only negates one specified occurrence in its clause check. The sort has $O(S\log^2 S)$ comparators for $S$ occurrences rounded to a power of two, using tagged dummy records. Each comparator uses $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ Boolean gates on $O(j)$ bits, with fan-out expanded by explicit binary copy trees. Both predecessors and all consumers of a gate can be listed locally. Only its own template, the adjoining time layers and its partner wire obtained by XOR with one public stage mask are involved. Template lookup and construction for a single comparator, initial row, or final row costs $\operatorname{poly}(j)$. Name pins and the separate clause checks have fixed slots per occurrence. Impose the local gate checks and each final comparison or clause separately; a global output gate is unnecessary. A fixed bounded-fan-out Tseitin layout now has locally indexable occurrence slots for each of its variables. Its clause and variable counts are $M\operatorname{poly}(j)$.

Use the [NAE triangles and equality splitters](mst-forests-and-four-port-coloring.md#four-ports-suffice-for-full-3-sat) on this bounded-occurrence formula. Represent a high-use palette color by a binary tree of ternary splitter cells with one dedicated leaf per syntactic use; reserve a power-of-two range of leaves for each regular use family. At a splitter the two centers are adjacent, each seeing all three equal terminals. Two cells can share a terminal of degree four. Their parent, children, and incident edges are indexed by binary heap arithmetic. Apply the same fixed slots to the two literal representatives of each Boolean variable, reserving both signs at the one mutable use. An unused leaf receives no external edge. Counts and names of the internal vertices are independent of the terminal sign. The switch now changes only a constant number of edges at explicit neighboring slots. These rules give a simple degree-at-most-four quotient candidate with local bidirectional incidence, without a scan of the native computation. Ordinary fixed pins on name bits can use dedicated fixed local clause checks; only the terminal sign changes *between* the two candidates.

To realize this candidate as actual directed head-to-tail row paths, choosing a global Euler tour would require an additional access analysis. Instead form a fixed 23-vertex fixture from two copies of the [12-vertex equal-color block](mst-forests-and-four-port-coloring.md), identifying the second terminal of the first copy with the first terminal of the second. Give each original vertex a fixture. Distribute its four labeled incidence slots in two pairs between the free terminals. External edges can be oriented by their public labels, independently of any distant data. The free terminals have degree two internally, so each takes up to two external arrows. The internal block edges impose equality of the two terminal colors, with every common color attainable. This preserves colorability.

In this fixture **every** external profile extends to a balanced internal orientation. For one free terminal the external incoming/outgoing counts $(a,b)$ have six possibilities with $a+b\le2$. For either pair of profiles the internal indegree at a vertex with internal degree $d$ must be between $\max(0,\lfloor(d+a+b)/2\rfloor-a)$ and $\min(d,\lceil(d+a+b)/2\rceil-a)$. Internal vertices take $a=b=0$. The [finite orientation audit](../audits/local_four_port_orientation_audit.py) finds and independently checks a witness for all 36 pairs. It makes one item per internal edge that must fill a slot at one of its endpoints. At each vertex reserve the prescribed minimum number of mandatory slots and make the difference up to the maximum optional. Dummy items can fill only optional slots. A perfect matching is precisely a feasible orientation, and counts of its actual heads verify the needed inequalities directly. The single 12-vertex block fails exactly the two profiles with all four external arrows incoming or all four outgoing. The two copies have 42 internal edges, so the full table costs constant work to construct and use. [Lean verifies all 36 head assignments by kernel reduction and proves that the six external terminal profiles are exhaustive](../lean/LocalFourPortOrientation.lean).

At each balanced vertex arrange its incident ports in alternating order and connect consecutive ports by head-to-tail rows. These local paths give exactly the fixture graph after contraction. Vertex, edge, and row identifiers can use contiguous blocks of fixed sizes for each template. If identical record counts are needed on the two presentations, unused external edge slots can name private isolated inequalities; unused row slots can be filled by private independent satisfiable pairs. A changed connection changes at most constantly many local orientation tables and row slots. For a triangle-free quotient, optionally repeat the 12-vertex decoration on the now locally balanced graph, putting all outgoing edges of a vertex at its first terminal and all incoming at its second. Neither exceptional single-block profile can occur. All-external triangles are excluded by this bipartite placement and two-block triangles by simplicity of the input. Each additional orientation lookup is still local.

In the first 23-vertex realization each fixture already has **17 internal vertices of degree four** regardless of external incidences. Each native presentation has at least $\Omega(M)$ original verifier vertices even before this replacement. Thus the proposed encoder itself has $\Omega(M)$ four-port components on each pin, including on its actual colorable instance. An algorithm branching on the complete set would spend its clock before exploiting the semantics of the pin. The following estimates address the smaller critical obstruction.

## Detecting a small refutation from the public pin

Let $H$ be any vertex- and edge-minimal non-three-colorable subgraph of the actual negative graph. It is connected and contains a changed edge because the other graph is colorable. Write $s=|V(H)|$ and $b$ for its number of vertices of degree four *in $H$*. All its other vertices have degree three. Kostochka and Yancey's $3|E(H)|\ge5s-2$ and the handshake identity give $s\le3b+4$. This measures genuine branching inside the refutation rather than merely inherited degree in the full graph. [Density theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1173) · [Application to original row counts](four-port-obstruction-size.md)

Search both pins from the public set $A$. Every connected witness on at most $s$ vertices meeting $A$ is visited by some walk of length at most $2(s-1)$, by a traversal of a spanning tree. Enumerate these walks with four choices per step, record their vertex sets, skip sets larger than $s$, pull back the induced edges by local neighbor queries, and test all colorings. Finding an uncolorable set soundly refutes the corresponding actual pin. At $s=O(j)$ this deterministic search costs $O(48^s\operatorname{poly}(j))$. In particular for every sufficiently late stage, **every** such $H$ satisfies

$$s\ \ge\ \frac{j-O(\log(j+2))}{\log_2 48},\qquad b\ \ge\ (s-4)/3.$$

Indeed the algorithm with $s=\lfloor(j-B\log_2(j+2))/\log_2 48\rfloor$ fits $o(M)$ for sufficiently large fixed $B$. A more precise upper bound on connected-set enumeration would change the constant. The conclusion applies unconditionally to any fixed local complementary-pair encoder meeting the neighbor-access and anchor conditions above.

Two stronger tests use the hypothesis that ordinary SAT with unrestricted workspace takes $O(n^r)$ time, $r\in\{1,2,3\}$, on encoded input length $n$.

* The union of radius-$R$ balls about $A$ has $O(3^R)$ vertices because maximum degree is four. Enumerate this union by breadth-first search with a balanced dictionary on the vertex identifiers, form its induced edges by local queries, and ask the assumed solver about its standard 3-color CNF. For $R=O(j)$ this costs $O(3^{rR}\operatorname{poly}(j))$ deterministically, including generation and both pin tests. No critical $H$ can be entirely contained in this union at $R=\lfloor(j-B\log_2(j+2))/(r\log_2 3)\rfloor$ for large enough fixed $B$ and all late stages. Some vertex of each refutation is genuinely far from **every** point of $A$ in full-graph distance, even allowing paths outside $H$.
* Guess a non-three-colorable induced set of at most $s$ vertices. Check and sort distinct identifiers, enumerate all their at most four neighbors, construct the induced graph, then run the assumed SAT algorithm on its explicit 3-color CNF and accept exactly on its negative answer. This uses $O((s+1)^r\operatorname{poly}(j))$ nondeterministic time for $s\le M$. Eliminate this **outer** guessing by the [bounded computation encoding](packed-band-transfer.md) and one more application of the same deterministic SAT algorithm. Including both pins, cost is $O((s+1)^{r^2}\operatorname{poly}(j))$. Setting $s=\lfloor 2^{j/r^2}/(j+2)^B\rfloor$ for large $B$ gives a sound $o(M)$ refuter if any such set exists. Thus on every sufficiently late stage every critical $H$ needs $s> 2^{j/r^2}/(j+2)^{O(1)}$ vertices, and $b\ge(s-4)/3$. This second argument uses the assumed solver twice. For a 4-Ore obstruction the [composition-tree certificate](four-port-obstruction-size.md) verifies non-three-colorability in $O((s+1)\operatorname{poly}(j))$ time, including checking its terminal edges against local adjacency. Eliminating just the existential guess gives $O((s+1)^r\operatorname{poly}(j))$ deterministic time in this case. Every 4-Ore obstruction on the actual negative side at all sufficiently late stages must therefore have more than $2^{j/r}/(j+2)^{O(1)}$ vertices. The density theorem gives the same exponent for its genuine degree-four vertices.

The unconditional logarithmic obstruction, the conditional radius estimate, and the conditional critical-size estimate all price work on the actual pin presentations. The explicit 23-vertex encoder supplies no saving on the full vertex set and we have no smaller critical witness on its actual negative inputs. These results leave deterministic linear, quadratic and cubic SAT time open.
