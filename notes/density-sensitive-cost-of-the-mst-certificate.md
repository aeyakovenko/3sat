# Residual calls of the DLMST certificate on a dense graph

Before invoking the final MST decision-tree theorem, one can run a variant of Algorithm 1 and expose its dependence on graph density. Let $m,v$ be the numbers of edges and nonisolated vertices of a nonempty undirected multigraph. Write $x=m/v$ and $L_x=\log(2+x)$. With $O(m)$ weight comparisons outside its exact-MSF calls, the procedure can produce a forest $F$ for which
$$
       |F|+|U(F)|+\sum_i |E_i|
           =O\big(v(1+x)^{4/5}\big).
$$
Here $U(F)$ denotes edges unsupported by $F$, and the sum counts all inputs to exact-MSF calls made inside the certificate procedure. This quantifies the internal reduction for unbounded density. Once the final MST algorithm is available, it can itself return an exact forest in linear comparisons. The result here concerns the certificate *before* using that conclusion, and gives no faster final MST algorithm. The derivation relies on the paper's correctness and its charging of failed projections. [DLMST, Algorithm 1, Lemma 3.7 and §3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31187v1#S3.SS6)

## Fewer representatives per batch

Use integer $\gamma\ge1$, depth $j$ from one, $t=\gamma j^3$ and $r=8t^3$ as in the paper. At each vertex, sort its $k=\lceil r\ln(4r)\rceil$ lightest admissible neighbor representatives, or all when fewer exist. The neighbors in each list are distinct. Independently pick each vertex with probability $1/r$, then add a neighbor from each missed full list. The expected portal count is at most $v_c/r+v_c e^{-k/r}\le5v_c/(4r)$. Declare only the prefix of each list through its first portal, or the whole list if it has no portal. A list of any length has expected retained length at most $\sum_{i\ge0}(1-1/r)^i=r$, and the repairs can only shorten it. Normalize the portal count by $2v_c/r$ and the sum of prefix lengths by $4rv_c$. The expected sum of the two ratios is at most $7/8$. Hence one can find, by enumeration after sorting the lists, a choice with at most $2v_c/r$ portals, all full lists hit and at most $4rv_c$ total declared edges. Take the first portal edge as the candidate spoke whenever it exists, including on short lists. This search uses no further weight comparisons.

Lemma 3.2's dichotomy still holds for each unrejected batch edge $e$. If either endpoint declares a representative for its neighbor of weight at most $w(e)$, that representative supports $e$ after the forest update. Otherwise consider each endpoint. If the neighbor representative is in its sorted list but past the retained prefix, the portal terminating the prefix costs at most that representative. If the neighbor is omitted from the selected list, the original minimum-representative argument gives a full list of edges no heavier than $e$, which the portals hit. This uses the paper's treatment of an unrejected edge in the admissibility search, including an edge skipped after a lighter representative was found. Short lists without portals are declared in full. Thus each endpoint lacking direct support obtains a candidate spoke at most $w(e)$. Earlier lighter spokes remain usable, weights can only improve, and spokes change only at the original block boundaries. The schedule and temporal error argument of §§3.3–3.4 consequently still apply. Each call has $t$ batches and $t^2$ rounds, so total vertices across its children are at most $2t^3v_c/r=v_c/4$. At each depth the total number of edges is at most $m$, as each original edge goes to at most one child.

## Tracking all comparison and residual terms

Put $E_c=v_c\log(2+m_c/v_c)$ for a nonempty call $c$. The history logarithm in Lemma 3.10 is $O(t^2 E_c)$. Indeed the spoke choices are bounded by concavity of $\log(1+\deg)$ and the forest and maxima-profile choices are bounded by binomial coefficients with $O(v_c)$ selected items out of $O(m_c+v_c)$. This keeps the logarithm where the manuscript relaxes to a square root. The schedule in Lemma 3.11 and the preference estimates in §3.5 thus charge $O(t^2 E_c+m_c/t)$ bad or mistaken edges per call. Using the paper's original union-bound sample, the total comparison cost for preference estimation in the $t^2$ rounds is at most $O(t^3 E_c)$. The sharper fixed-sample estimate is also available. [Preference sampling](ordered-maxima-for-single-edit-sat.md)

The perspective $v\log(2+m/v)$ is increasing in each argument and superadditive across calls. Jensen's inequality and the per-depth totals bound the sum of the $E_c$ at depth $j$ by
$$
       4^{1-j}v\big(L_x+(j-1)\log4\big).
$$
Thus any fixed power of $j$ multiplying this charge sums to $O(v L_x)$. Rejected edges and unsupported final edges are charged to errors by Lemma 3.7. Summing over depth gives $O(\gamma^2 v L_x +m/\gamma)$. Even charging an additional factor $j$ for testing each first invalid projection preserves this estimate, since $\sum j/j^3$ converges.

At each call the sizes of exact-MSF inputs for existing forests, child forests, and declared edges, apart from the charged rejections, are $O((t^2+tr)v_c)$. Summing the geometrically decreasing vertex totals bounds these by $O(\gamma^4 v)$. This proves the parameterized residual bound
$$
 O\big(m/\gamma+\gamma^2 v L_x+\gamma^4 v\big).
$$
To count other comparisons, each original edge enters at most one batch across the recursion. Basic batch scans therefore cost $O(m)$ in total, plus the per-batch vertex overhead. Lemma 3.3 uses $O(k\sum_u\log(1+\deg_B u))$ successful tests in a batch. Charging $O(j)$ comparisons per test and bounding the logarithmic sum by $O(E_c)$ gives $O(jtk E_c)$ across the $t$ batches. Sorting the selected lists fits this charge: a list of size $a_u\le\min(k,\deg_B(u))$ costs $O(a_u\log(1+a_u))\le O(k\log(1+\deg_B(u)))$. Combine the earlier bounds for estimates and failed tests. The comparison total outside exact calls is
$$
                   O\big(m+\gamma^4\log(2+\gamma)v L_x\big).
$$

For large $x$ take $\gamma$ an integer within a constant factor of $x^{1/5}$ and use $\gamma=1$ for bounded density. The residual terms $m/\gamma$ and $\gamma^4v$ are $O(v x^{4/5})$ and $\gamma^2v L_x$ also fits. The extra comparison term is $O(v x^{4/5}L_x^2)=O(m)$ for large $x$. This yields the stated certificate. The short-prefix rule is needed here to remove the logarithm from the residual estimate.

This use of sampling and alteration is elementary. Some intermediate manuscripts in the repository also retain logarithmic history entropy. Priority for the combined density estimate is unverified. For a SAT transfer, the counting inputs would have to concern actual solver runs and the residual instances would have to have independently controlled native cost. These remain open conditions. ## Removing the extra logarithm in the exterior comparison charge

There is also a batch implementation needing $O(|B|+v_c)$ ordinary selection comparisons and $O(rv_c\log(2+|B|/v_c))$ successful admissibility tests before charging the depth of each such test. It declares $O(rv_c)$ edges and uses at most $2v_c/r$ portals. This changes the exterior bound above to $O(m+\gamma^4 v L_x)$.

Process unresolved vertices in stages $i=1,2,\ldots$. At stage $i$ put $p_i=1/(r i(i+1))$, $k_i=\lceil\ln(16)/p_i\rceil$. For each unresolved vertex select up to $k_i$ lightest admissible representatives. Retire short menus and declare them in full. For the full menus choose at most $2v_c p_i$ new portals so that both the number and the **sum of original batch degrees** of the unresolved, unhit vertices fall by at least a factor of two relative to the beginning of the stage. To see existence take a Bernoulli $p_i$ sample. A full menu is missed with probability at most $1/16$. Normalize its sample size by $2v_c p_i$ and its missed count and missed degree by half their respective starting totals, omitting zero denominators. The expected sum of the three ratios is at most $1/2+1/8+1/8$. Fix a sample with sum less than one. This uses no weight comparisons after the menus are known. Retire hit full menus, declare their selected representatives and use a selected neighbor in the portal set for their candidate spoke. Unhit full menus proceed to the next stage. Cache failed tests throughout the batch. Omitting an already rejected candidate is harmless under the original admissibility rule.

Write $V_i,M_i$ for the unresolved vertex count and total degree at stage $i$. Then $V_i\le2^{1-i} v_c$, $M_i\le2^{1-i}2|B|$. The retirement rule at each endpoint gives the same support-or-spokes dichotomy with its own quota as the original batch rule. The stages terminate, use at most $\sum_i 2v_c p_i=2v_c/r$ portals, and declare at most $\sum_i k_i V_i=O(rv_c)$ edges. The repeated scans cost $O(\sum_i(V_i+M_i))=O(v_c+|B|)$ comparisons. Lemma 3.3 charges at most $O(k_i V_i\log(2+M_i/V_i))$ successful tests at stage $i$, with zero charge if $V_i=0$. The perspective is increasing in both arguments, so summing the geometric upper bounds gives $O(rv_c\log(2+|B|/v_c))$. The depth and failed-test charges then sum exactly as before. This variant obtains the density corollary without sorting lists or charging a logarithm in $\gamma$ for successful tests.
