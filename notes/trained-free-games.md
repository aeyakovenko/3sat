# Replacing one free-game prover by a short list of labeled questions

For a two-prover cooperative free game with a constant completeness–soundness gap, one side's entire response function can be replaced by a best response to $O(1+b)$ labeled examples, where that side sends $b$ answer bits. When Bob sends one bit, the list contains only constantly many of Alice's questions and answers, even if her alphabet is exponentially larger. Some constant-gap games nevertheless have value one on **every** restriction to fewer than a linear number of Alice questions, measured in her answer length. The two assertions concern different tests of the list.

## Training a strategy for the full game

Let the questions have any product distribution on finite sets $X,Y$. The two cooperating provers answer in finite alphabets $A,B$. Write $v$ for the game's value and $b=\lceil\log_2|B|\rceil$. Allow an independent private verifier tape $z$ in the Boolean predicate $V(x,y,u,d;z)$. Deterministic optimal strategies exist. For a list $L=((x_i,u_i,z_i))_{i=1}^t$ define $g_L(y)$ to be the first maximizing $d\in B$ for the sum $\sum_i V(x_i,y,u_i,d;z_i)$ in a fixed order. For *every* list this is a single legal function of $y$, even when the labels are inconsistent. The optimal full-game score over all lists and unrestricted Alice responses, denoted $v_t$, satisfies
$$
 v-2\sqrt{\log(2|B|)/(2t)}\ \le\ v_t\ \le\ v.
$$

Fix an optimal Alice strategy $f$. Draw independent training examples with $u_i=f(x_i)$ and fresh $z_i$. For fixed $y,d$, the empirical score $\widehat s_y(d)$ averages $t$ independent bits with mean $s_y(d)$. Hoeffding's lemma bounds the moment generating function of either signed deviation by $\exp(\lambda^2/(8t))$. Bounding the exponential of the maximum by the sum and minimizing over $\lambda>0$ gives $\mathbb E_L\max_d|\widehat s_y(d)-s_y(d)|\le\sqrt{\log(2|B|)/(2t)}$. Comparing the maximizers of the two scores costs at most twice this deviation. Average over $y$ and then fix one list at least as good as the average. This argument never asks for accuracy simultaneously on all possible $y$.

If $v=1$, at every supported $y$ one response is perfect against $f$. Almost surely its empirical score is one. The probability of choosing a response of loss greater than $u$ is at most $\min(1,|B|e^{-tu})$. Integrating in $u$ gives $1-v_t\le(1+\log |B|)/t$. Both estimates permit arbitrary question supports. They give a finite description of a global strategy; the optimal value of the game restricted to the training questions can be much higher. The sample-and-best-response algorithm of Aaronson, Impagliazzo and Moshkovitz uses a simultaneous bound over $(y,d)$ in Theorem 39. Their full subgame theorem is a separate statement. [AIM, Theorems 39 and 45](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.6848)

For the specified empirical rule, the linear dependence on $b$ cannot be removed at constant error. Take $b$ equiprobable Alice questions, one Bob question and no Alice answer. A Bob answer is a $b$-bit vector and the predicate reads its bit at Alice's index, requiring one. The globally all-one vector wins. With lexicographically first maximizers, a list meeting $k$ distinct indices yields ones exactly on those $k$ indices. Every length-$t$ list therefore achieves at most $t/b$.

## Uniform costs on a signed priority input

A single prover sends $L$ before seeing either fresh question, receives Alice's actual question, and returns her answer. The verifier samples Bob's question independently, computes $g_L(y)$, and uses the original private payoff tape. The optimal acceptance is exactly $v_t$ by the legal-strategy upper bound and the ability to submit any fixed list. Let $q_X,q_Y$ count uniform seed bits for sampling the questions, $a,b$ count answer bits, $r$ count auxiliary payoff bits, and $u$ upper-bound the word work for one question generation and predicate replay. Training records may contain the sampler seeds themselves. Assume total fixed-width answer encodings with a specified order and constructible caps. For a known constant gap choose $t=O(1+b)$ so the loss is less than a quarter of the gap. The public messages and private seeds occupy
$$
 B=O\big(t(q_X+a+r)+q_X+q_Y+a+b+r+1\big)
$$
bits. When $b=O(1)$, direct enumeration of Bob's replies costs $O(tu+B+1)$ steps with bit-by-bit input preparation, and packed copies can cost less. Games with a general private-coin implementation must supply the indicated conditional payoff sampler or an equivalent independent auxiliary tape.

For arbitrary $b$, the terminal replay in the [constant-alternation simulation](statistical-proof-alternation-cost.md) can existentially select $d$, then universally select $d'$, checking that $d$ beats $d'$ on the empirical score or ties it with $d\le d'$. Check the fresh payoff using the same $d$. Exactly one $d$ passes every comparison. Its definition uses $L,y$ only, even though the replay also knows the other data. The two scores need only $2t$ predicate evaluations on any path. If there is a transcript mismatch in the private-coin simulation, use its existing replay rule before this terminal payoff check. Negated occurrences dualize both quantifiers. A fixed dyadic rescaling of acceptance permits the threshold convention of the simulation theorem. With fixed gap and constant block count, the path work and total choice bits are bounded by $D=O(tu+B+1)$ up to constructible clock overheads. The symmetric construction interchanges the provers.

If a uniform game decides the true priority sign at scale $j$ with this gap, the resulting bounded-alternation algorithm is subject to the [signed lower bound](priority-pinned-sat.md) with $M=2^j$. Under deterministic $O(N^k)$ SAT on ordinary encodings, $h$ quantifier blocks of cost $D$ can be eliminated in $D^{k^h}\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time when all lengths are polynomial in $M$ and metadata are locally computable in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ time. Each elimination compiles one bounded existential RAM run and complements where needed. Thus $D\le M^{1/k^h-\eta}$ on all large scales for some $\eta>0$ contradicts that hypothesis. For linear SAT this excludes any fixed power saving $D\le M^{1-\eta}$. For quadratic and cubic SAT, an unspecified sublinear verifier time alone gives no contradiction. These are criteria on a game for the actual sign.

There is also an unconditional test when enumerating questions and answers is affordable. For a deterministic Boolean payoff on uniform bit-string questions, enumerate every training list. Tabulate $g_L(y)$ by scanning $B$, then for each Alice question find its best response by scanning $A,Y$. This computes $v_t$ exactly and distinguishes a constant gap. Up to polynomial factors in the message lengths and a fixed-gap constant in $t$, its time is at most
$$
 (1+u)\,2^{\,t(q_X+a)+q_X+q_Y+a+b}.
$$
Consequently $t(q_X+a)+q_X+q_Y+a+b=o(j)$ and $u\le M^{1-\eta}$ uniformly on the priority inputs are already incompatible with their unconditional sign lower bound.

## Two quantifier blocks for a two-sided gap

For uniform bit-string questions and a deterministic payoff, an alternative dispenses with approximate counting inside the alternating simulation. Put $K=O_{\rm gap}(1+\max(a,b))$. By AIM's Corollary 46 the expected value of a uniform $K$-by-$K$ restricted game is between $v$ and $v+$ a quarter-gap, taking all questions on a side with fewer than $K$. Use a fixed number of independent restrictions and average their optima. Hoeffding makes the event that this average exceeds a public intervening threshold have probability at least $2/3$ in the high case and at most $1/3$ in the low case, with a strict margin for sampling approximations. Membership of that event means there exist answer tables whose average score exceeds the threshold. This step checks all panel cells so costs $E=\widetilde O(K^2(u+1)+R+W)$, where $W=O(K(a+b+1))$ is the answer-table length and $R=O(K(q_X+q_Y+\log(K+2)))$ is the seed length. For a domain of size $2^q$ much larger than $K^2$, sample with replacement and treat a collision as failure, with arbitrarily small constant error by choice of cutoff. For domains of size $O(K^2)$, sample without replacement with a sparse shuffle, drawing each bounded index via a sufficiently long uniform word reduced modulo the current range. An extra $O(\log(K+2))$ bits per draw makes the total variation error arbitrarily small. A fully retained side can be enumerated in its natural order. AIM's single restriction also yields a one-prover statistical simulation with just one payoff evaluation by sampling a hidden pair of panel indices after receiving both tables. [AIM, Corollary 46 and Theorem 47](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.6848)

For the two-block bound, amplify the good-seed event by taking the majority of $\ell=O(\log(R+2))$ independent seeds. Existentially provide the tables of each asserted successful test. Choose constants so both error probabilities are at most $\delta=(R\ell+3)^{-3}$. Put $R'=\ell R$, and take $h=\lceil (R'+1)/\log_2(1/\delta)\rceil$, increasing it to at least one. For every list of $h$ shifts in $\{0,1\}^{R'}$, demand a common offset whose translates by those shifts all satisfy the amplified event. In the high case such an offset exists by $h\delta<1$. In the low case independent uniform shifts have expected number of common offsets at most $2^{R'}\delta^h<1$, giving a list with none. This is a $\forall\exists$ test since all tables for all the majority witnesses follow the common offset. Its total cost is $\widetilde O((R+1)(R+W+E+1))$ including every payoff replay and all shifts. Thus the preceding SAT slowdown has the explicit exponent $k^2$ on this cost. This elementary shift construction is a parameter count for the usual probabilistic hierarchy simulation. [Lautemann](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3)

## A constant-gap game with perfect restrictions

Let $H$ be an $N$-by-$a$ binary matrix of full column rank and let $b_0$ be a target vector. Suppose every $Hm$ differs from $b_0$ in at least $\delta N$ positions and every subset of fewer than $d$ equations of $Hm=b_0$ is consistent. Encode $m$ with a binary code $C$ of relative minimum distance $\rho$. Alice receives a uniform row $i$ and gives a whole $a$-bit message $m_i$. Bob receives an independently uniform code coordinate $y$ and gives one bit. Require both the designated row equation on $m_i$ and equality of Bob's bit to $C(m_i)_y$.

Fix Bob's truth table and take a closest $C(m_*)$. Every other codeword is at distance at least $\rho/2$ from the table, by the triangle inequality and closest choice. On at least $\delta N$ rows, $m_*$ violates the equation, so Alice either fails immediately or uses another message that is caught with probability at least $\rho/2$. The value is at most $1-\delta\rho/2$. Yet any restriction to fewer than $d$ Alice rows is perfect even on the entire set of Bob questions. A simultaneous solution of those equations supplies a common message to Alice and the corresponding codeword to Bob.

Explicit matrices with $d=\Omega(a)$ and constant $\delta$ follow from the binary CSS construction of Panteleev and Kalachev. In their chain complex let $U=\operatorname{im}\partial_2$, $Z=\ker\partial_1$, with $U\subset Z\subseteq\mathbb F_2^N$. Take the columns of $H$ to be a basis of $U$ and $b_0\in Z\setminus U$. Linear quantum distance gives both $\operatorname{dist}(b_0,U)=\Omega(N)$ and weight $\Omega(N)$ for every $\lambda\in U^\perp\setminus Z^\perp$. An inconsistent set of rows would supply a $\lambda$ supported there with $H^T\lambda=0$ and $\lambda\cdot b_0=1$, in this very difference. Moreover some inconsistency witness uses at most $a+1$ rows: choose independent coefficient rows, solve them, and add one violated row. Hence $a=\Theta(N)$. Basis extraction and selection of $b_0$ use ordinary polynomial-time binary elimination on their explicit matrices. This game uses classical answers throughout. [Panteleev and Kalachev, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.03654)

For $C$ take a binary output indexed by $u,v\in\mathbb F_Q$ with $2a\le Q<4a$ a power of two. Regard the $a$ input bits as coefficients of a polynomial $P_m$ of degree below $a$ and output $\operatorname{Tr}(uP_m(v))$. A nonzero difference vanishes at fewer than $a$ values of $v$; at every other $v$, half the $u$ have trace one. Thus $\rho\ge1/4$, Bob's question has $O(\log a)$ bits, and both question domains have polynomial size in $a$. This establishes the necessity of $\Omega(\log|A|)$ retained Alice questions for unextended subgames, even with one-bit Bob replies and constant soundness gap. It does not give a lower bound on arbitrary strategy descriptions or on algorithms allowed to inspect the matrices. Birthday repetition gives another mechanism for constant gaps from independent question sets in dense CSP reductions; its hypotheses concern the base incidence game. [Manurangsi and Raghavendra](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2017.78) · [Finite checks](../audits/trained_free_game_audit.py)
