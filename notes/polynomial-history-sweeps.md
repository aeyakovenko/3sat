# Verifying RAM history columns given by polynomials in the time index

Consider the ordinary Boolean RAM tableau of the [aligned history checker](sheared-history-charts.md). At a square horizon $B^2$, write $B=2^m$ and split the time index into $m$-bit halves $x,y$. Each bit column has a baseline, a list of rectangle flips and a list of supported XOR diagonals. Rectangles have dyadic prefix depths $(a,b)$ with $a\le b$. Each diagonal is restricted to an arbitrary interval in $x$. The full chart includes the instruction and routing gate columns. Keep these conventions and allow the baseline to be any Boolean function of at most $c$ multilinear polynomials over $\mathbb F_2$ in the joint bits of $x,y$, each of degree at most $d$. Here $c$ and local gate arity are absolute constants. List each feature's squarefree monomials explicitly and charge their representation to $s$, the total chart length in logarithmic words. Monomials may involve both coordinates.

**Exact checking theorem.** For some constant $C$ depending only on the fixed gate templates and feature budget, there is a deterministic checker using at most
$$
 (1+s)(m+1)^C K^C,\qquad K=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(m,D)}\binom mi,\quad D=C_0(d+1)
$$
word operations, where $C_0$ is a fixed integer. It decides whether every local gate is valid. In particular $d=o(m)$ incurs only $2^{o(m)}$ overhead per word of chart. The estimate uses the Boolean value of a universally quantified gate test. It does not require computing the number of bad rows.

## Testing a gate on the full grid

Use coordinates $(j,z)=(y,x\oplus y)$. After any required fixed XOR translation, each rectangle remains a rectangle in these coordinates, and each supported diagonal becomes $O(m+1)$ rectangles. A chronological increment with no low-half overflow is a fixed XOR on each trailing-ones case, which restricts just the retained coordinate $j$. An overflow fixes $j$ and yields a one-dimensional check, with fixed suffix bits of the other coordinate. Sorting-direction bits can enter as degree-one features. Initial and final conditions are singleton tests. The details of the local circuit and the row maps are in the [aligned-prefix proof](sheared-history-charts.md).

Fix a gate, one such branch, and a vector $p$ of flip parities for its constantly many input and output columns. The Boolean indicator $G_p(j,z)$ of a forbidden tuple is a polynomial of degree at most $D$. Indeed, the truth table on the constantly many features and guard bits has a multilinear polynomial of bounded degree. Substitution composes the feature polynomials with affine maps and reduces repeated variables by $X^2=X$. This never increases the degree beyond the stated factor. A product of monomials on disjoint sets of axis variables splits into one monomial per axis, so on the grid
$$
 G_p(j,z)=\nu_D(j)^{\mathsf T} A_p\nu_D(z),
$$
where $\nu_D$ lists all squarefree monomials of degree at most $D$ on one axis, including the constant. Its length is $K$. The coefficients are fixed during a sweep. Sort and cancel duplicate monomials while parsing. Expand the fixed substitutions and constant-arity truth tables once for the gate, reducing in the Boolean quotient. At most $K^2$ distinct monomials survive in a joint polynomial. Expanding a single original monomial under the XOR shear costs at most $2^{\min(2m,d)}\le K^{O(1)}$, and ordinary polynomial multiplication on this finite coefficient table takes $K^{O(1)}$ bit operations. This avoids rescanning the chart for every pair tested. Reject malformed ranges, degrees and monomial masks.

An integer interval splits into $O(m+1)$ dyadic intervals. On a dyadic interval intersected with fixed coordinate bits, omit inconsistent pieces. For a surviving piece, fix its constrained bits and use the points with at most $D$ ones in its free positions. Their evaluation vectors span those of every point on the piece. To see this, any linear functional vanishing on the selected vectors gives a polynomial of degree at most $D$ vanishing on these points. After substitution of the fixed bits, its coefficient indexed by $S$ is the XOR of the evaluations whose supports are subsets of $S$. For $|S|\le D$ all those terms vanish. The polynomial vanishes everywhere on the piece, proving the span statement by finite-dimensional duality. A basis for a union of pieces can be obtained by Gaussian elimination, retaining original points whose vectors increased the rank.

Sweep the $j$ endpoints of the pulled-back rectangles. Between consecutive endpoints the flip mask depends on $z$ alone. Use a segment tree on sorted $z$ endpoints. Each node stores a basis of the evaluation vectors at allowed ordinates for each possible mask. The initial leaves have zero mask and the interval bases just described. Merge two children by taking a basis of the union in each mask. A range flip permutes the mask labels with XOR, allowing lazy propagation. At each $j$ slab compute its own basis, including all fixed-bit restrictions. For every mask test the gate on every pair from the slab basis and the corresponding root basis. Bilinearity implies zero on the full product exactly when these tests all vanish. Each bin at the root represents actual ordinates throughout this slab. This last property would fail if a constraint on the two axes were imposed after taking the separate spans. The low-half overflow case uses the same argument with one coordinate fixed. There are $O((1+s)\operatorname{poly}(m+1))$ updates, probes and local test types counting pullbacks, a constant number of bins per node and polynomial work in $K,m+1$ per basis operation. The bound on $K$ for $D=o(m)$ follows from $\binom mi\le (em/i)^i$ for $1\le i\le m$. [Formal span lemma](../lean/PolynomialChartSpan.lean) · [Finite tests](../audits/polynomial_chart_audit.py)

## Transfer to the explicit SAT question

Use the deterministic computation that evaluates the pinned bit $b_j$ at native time scale $M=2^j$. An honest chart under a given verifier clock can be guessed and verified by a bounded nondeterministic RAM. The ordinary packed 3-CNF compiler charges the verifier clock times a polylogarithmic factor in $M$ on polynomially related clocks. The formula enforces the specified final sign and therefore has no witness for a false sign. Both the generator and the verification algorithm are fixed. A public clock bounds all guesses including malformed alleged degrees. If arbitrarily large true scales admit charts of size $s\le M^{1/k-\epsilon}$ and degree $d=o(\log M)$ for one fixed implementation and some fixed $0<\epsilon<1/k$, choose a public rational power strictly between $1/k-\epsilon$ and $1/k$ as the verifier clock exponent. It eventually covers the displayed checking cost. A hypothetical $O(N^k)$ SAT algorithm on the resulting ordinary formula would then give a sound detector taking $o(M)$ on infinitely many scales for at least one color. This contradicts the native detector theorem, for $k=1,2,3$. The proof uses no need to predict a degree schedule or its rate of convergence. Whether such compact certificates exist for an implementation remains open. [Pinned theorem](priority-pinned-sat.md) · [Compiler](packed-band-transfer.md)

## A single real load column that defeats all sublinear degrees

The earlier affine obstruction uses an inner product times two low-bit indicators. That table itself has degree at most four. The following fixed word computation handles every degree sequence $d=o(\log M)$ with the same implementation.

Use a local sample grid of side $B_0=2^{\lfloor j/2\rfloor}$, where $B_0^2=\Theta(M)$. Put $B_0/2=2^n$ and $h=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$. For $n\ge4$ and $n$-bit $u,v$, set $q=u\mathbin{\mathrm{shr}}h$. Use $p(u)=1$ if $q=0$, otherwise $p(u)=\min(h,q\mathbin{\mathrm{and}}(-q))$. Arithmetic negation here is on unsigned native words with wraparound. The output sample is zero unless both coordinates are odd. On odd-odd coordinates set
$$
 F(2u+1,2v+1)=[(u\mathbin{\mathrm{and}}v)\mathbin{\mathrm{and}}(2^{p(u)}-1)=0].
$$
This is a constant number of ordinary word operations. Every row has even parity because $p(u)\le h<n$ leaves the highest bit of $v$ free.

Use the fixed clock alignment in the [affine construction](geometric-history-certificates.md#testing-the-obstruction-on-the-same-hard-signed-bit). Initialize a separate two-word bank with values zero and one. Use the reserved initial slots for padding, and in the designated slots load from the bank at the address $F(i,z)$. XOR the returned bit into the checksum at exactly the same designated slots. All counters and addresses are independent of the replies. A sufficiently large constant slot width $R$ implements these operations, including branches padded to the same instruction count. Compute $n,h$ from the native scale, and handle finitely many small scales directly. The phase fits the prescribed square clock and the original $O(M)$ computation fits alongside it. Its checksum is zero, so XORing it with the original output gives exactly $b_j$. Corrupting a single designated reply flips that output. In global coordinates the sampled times are $(Ri+1,Rz+c_0)$ for constants $R,c_0$ with $R$ a power of two and $0\le c_0<R$. Restriction sets fixed low bits and, when needed, fixed high padding bits. An interval pulls back to an interval, an XOR track pulls back to at most one such track, and a polynomial preserves its degree.

Consider even an approximate chart of this one genuine load bit, using $r$ arbitrary axis-parallel rectangles, $f$ arbitrary supported XOR tracks, and $e$ erroneous entries. The baseline, as a Boolean function of $c$ degree-$d$ features, has degree at most $cd$. Form the XOR of the four entries in each disjoint block with coordinates $2u,2u+1$ and $2v,2v+1$. On the target the result equals $F(2u+1,2v+1)$. On the predicted baseline it still has degree at most $cd$ in the remaining bits. One rectangle affects the parity of at most four blocks. A full track intersects each touched block in exactly two cells, and restricting its row interval can split at most two row pairs, each selecting only one column pair. A fragment therefore affects at most two block parities. Each erroneous entry affects one. [Block lemmas](../lean/DominoHistory.lean)

Choose the smallest positive dyadic integer $p$ with $2p>cd$. For every fixed $c$ and $d=o(n)$, eventually $p\le h$ and the high part of $u$ has at least $1+\log_2 p$ bits. On the subcube $q\equiv p\pmod{2p}$ exactly $1+\log_2 p$ upper bits are fixed, $p(u)=p$, and the low $p$ bits of $u$ remain free. The target there is the polynomial $\prod_{i<p}(1+u_i v_i)$ of exact Boolean degree $2p$. Its top monomial has coefficient one. After adding the restricted lower-degree baseline it still has degree $2p$ in $a=2n-1-\log_2 p$ free bits. A nonzero degree-at-most-$k$ Boolean polynomial on $a$ bits has weight at least $2^{a-k}$ for $k\le a$. Indeed write it as $A+xB$. For $B\ne0$, the weights of $A$ and $A+B$ sum to at least the weight of the degree-at-most-$(k-1)$ polynomial $B$. For $B=0$ the weight doubles, with the trivial bound one handling $k\ge a$. This is the binary Reed–Muller minimum-distance bound. [Saptharishi, Shpilka and Volk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.09092)

Consequently
$$
 4r+2f+e\ \ge\ \frac{2^{2n}}{2p\,4^p}.
$$
The lower bound is $\Omega_{c,d}(M)$ for every fixed degree, and $M^{1-o(1)}$ for each sublinear degree sequence. It permits unaligned rectangles and all offsets. In particular a power saving on chart length cannot follow just from the running time, the short input, and sensitivity of this real memory column. A verdict-specific proof could verify the checksum identity directly, bypassing those loads. Finding such a saving for a sound verdict is the unresolved step. The full machine construction and asymptotic bounds have manuscript proofs. Historical priority for the chart results is undetermined.
