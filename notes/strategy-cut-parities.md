# When a merged strategy must recover every native word

Consider a strategy that keeps one of at most $K$ labels from the first part of a play. The second part may inspect the original memory. There is an alternating RAM game with $m$ independently chosen $w$-bit words before the cut and a single answer bit afterward for which every always-winning strategy satisfies
$$
 \Pr[\text{at least }d\text{ of the original words remain unread}]
 \;\le\; \min\!\left(1,\binom md\frac{K}{2^{wd}}\right),
 \qquad 1\le d\le m .
$$
The probability is over independent uniform choices by the opponent. For $K$ a power of two with $mK/2^w=o(1)$, one explicit strategy attains this bound up to a factor $1-o(1)$ simultaneously for every $d$. Thus allowing a merged strategy to ask for true memory values can force it to retrieve **every** original word on almost every play. The labels may represent arbitrary subsets of memories.

## The game and its cut

On an inert input of length $n$, take $m=n$ and the journal's native width $w=12\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil$. Player 0 first selects $m$ words $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ and the machine stores them at distinct fixed addresses. Set all temporary registers to fixed values at the cut. The only memory dependent on the choices is this array. Player 0 next selects $m$ words $y_1,\ldots,y_m$. Player 1 must commit to a single bit, after which the machine accepts exactly when that bit equals
$$
 f(x,y)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^m \langle x_i,y_i\rangle,
 \qquad \langle u,v\rangle=\bigoplus_{j=1}^w (u_j\land v_j).
$$
A fixed program can use individual bit choices, shift them into a word and clear the temporary register at each store. It can verify the answer by scanning the arrays and the bits of their words. This costs $O(mw)$ ordinary instructions per play. A winning strategy exists on every input.

Give the continuation the entire query $y$ for free. Before it sees $y$, the strategy retains a label $H(x)$ with at most $K$ possible values. Afterward it may compute freely, keep all past probe replies and ask for any $x_i$ adaptively. All information about $x$ available after the cut must come through the label or these probes. A later copy fetched from a previously probed word has no additional charge. Arbitrary labels, nonuniform decoders, and overlapping permissible abstract nodes are covered whenever a label is selected before the future challenge. For overlapping nodes fix a selection consistent with the winning strategy on each $x$. A history-dependent choice during the suffix requires the history on which it depends to pass through the charged interface. A replay machine with access to further uncharged functions of $x$ has a richer interface.

The earlier binary-coordinate SAT construction gives a worst-case sketch-size tradeoff for this inner product with a fixed arrival order. The word Fourier dimension also occurs in the earlier work-memory cut. The result here concerns the distribution of the *number of individual native words omitted* by an always-correct strategy. The sketch and the adaptive probes are the usual systematic data-structure model. The query set is the entire space of masks. ## Distribution of the omitted words

Put $q=2^w$. Fix a set $S$ of $d$ addresses, the data $x_{-S}$ outside it, and the coefficients $y_{-S}$ there. For each nonempty label fiber $F$ of possible $x_S$, choose any $u\in F$. If the strategy omits $S$ on one member of $F$ for a given $y_S$, its probe replies, answer, and omission of $S$ are identical throughout $F$. This follows by induction over its adaptive probes. Correctness forces
$$
 y_S\in \operatorname{span}_{\mathbf F_2}\{v-u:v\in F\}^{\perp}.
$$
If this span has dimension $b$, a uniform $y_S$ belongs to its annihilator with probability $2^{-b}\le1/|F|$, since $F$ fits in a translate of the span. Multiply by the chance $|F|/q^d$ of the data falling in that fiber. Summing over all nonempty labels gives $\Pr[S\text{ omitted}]\le K/q^d$. A union bound over $S$ proves the opening inequality. A zero-error randomized strategy with a bound on the number of labels for each seed satisfies the same estimate by fixing its coins. On the finite set of inputs and queries the seeds giving a wrong answer anywhere have probability zero. [Adaptive indistinguishability in Lean](../lean/StrategyCutReadback.lean)

For the matching strategy suppose $K=2^s\le q$ and fix an $s$-dimensional linear space $A\subseteq\mathbf F_2^w$ of masks. Store the $s$ parities that give $\sum_i\langle x_i,a\rangle$ for a basis of $A$. From $y$ choose a most frequent mask $a\in A$, counting zero occurrences if all coefficients lie outside $A$. The stored sketch supplies $\sum_i\langle x_i,a\rangle$. Adding $\langle x_i,y_i+a\rangle$ for every $i$ with $y_i\ne a$ recovers the answer, so all occurrences of $a$ can be omitted. For a fixed $S$ of size $d\ge1$, the event that all its coefficients equal one common mask from $A$ and every coefficient off $S$ lies outside $A$ has probability $Kq^{-d}(1-K/q)^{m-d}$. These events are disjoint over the choices of $S$. Consequently the same strategy satisfies
$$
 \Pr[\text{at least }d\text{ omissions}]\ge
 \binom md\frac{K}{q^d}(1-K/q)^{m-d}.
$$
An empty outside set contributes a factor of one. If $mK/q=o(1)$ the last factor tends to one uniformly over $d$. For $d=1$ the strategy's exact probability of an omission is $1-(1-K/q)^m$. At $K=1$ the optimal policy computes the answer by reading exactly the words with nonzero coefficient.

With the native $m=n,w$ above, $q\ge(n+2)^{12}$. Even $K\le n^4$ (four times $\log_2 n$ bits of retained information) gives probability at most $n^{-7}$ of missing a single word. The two diagonal certificate budgets $n^{8/a}/(\log n)^{4+\epsilon}$ with $a=2,3$ bound the **number of explicit vertices**, so a cut whose sole summary is a vertex identifier would fit this label cap. A parameterized node carrying additional concrete values has to count those values or allow the continuation to retrieve them. The game forces these distinctions despite every input having a winning strategy and a short program for the verdict. A direct replay that retrieves all $m$ words still fits in $O(mw)$ time here. Applying the result to the clocked diagonal would require identifying such a game in every admissible speedup, or forcing many separately charged reads whose total exceeds the clock. ## An equivalent implementation of the padded diagonal

Let $M$ be any alternating program, including the deterministic interpreter that computes the entire padded $J_8$. Prepend two challenges of the above form with fresh scratch memory. First player 1 generates the words and player 0 must commit to the parity. Then player 0 generates fresh words and player 1 must commit. An incorrect commitment loses immediately. After two correct commitments, restore all touched scratch cells and registers to the original initial state and start $M$ on the unmodified input. Each prologue takes $O(nw)$ instructions per play and every nondeterministic choice can still be a single bit.

The new program has a finite win for a player on precisely the same inputs as $M$. A player with a win for $M$ answers its own challenge correctly and uses the original strategy after two correct answers. Conversely, fix honest answers and fixed generating choices for the opponent during the prologue of a purported winning strategy. The opponent can prevent itself from losing before the start of $M$, and the winner must survive its own challenge. The remaining strategy wins $M$. A finite prefix game has only finitely many concrete plays on a fixed input, so unrolling the prefix preserves finiteness in the forward direction.

Consider any finite explicit winning DAG for the new program, of either sign. Fix a reachable start of the eventual winner's own challenge, taking a truthful opponent through the first challenge if necessary. Its opponent is free to select every array $x$ there and every subsequent query $y$. The program makes no load from $x$ between the cut and the commitment. At a shared node the fixed suffix of the proof determines the answer from the future choices alone. Two different arrays reaching the same node would have to give equal answers for every $y$, although some coordinate mask distinguishes them. Thus **at least $q^m$ cut vertices** are required on every winning proof. In an enlargement of the representation which allows a shared node to retrieve original words, the omission bounds apply there. For $J_8$ itself this supplies one equivalent alternating implementation whose proofs exceed both diagonal budgets on every input, even where the simulated opponent halts immediately. The high-clock criterion quantifies existentially over implementations. This construction leaves open whether another implementation of an adequate predictor has short proofs.

## Many answers with a single native verification scan

A multi-round game produces a larger *total* cost for a merged decoder. Fix $m=L=n^2$. Player 0 selects the $m$ original words once, as before. Partition their fixed addresses into $g$ balanced groups. In each of $L$ rounds, player 0 independently selects a new $w$-bit coefficient for each group. The query mask of each word is the coefficient of its group. Player 1 commits to the parity. Player 0 can contest the answer, in which case the program scans $x$, checks the answer, and halts with that verdict. Alternatively player 0 may continue, in which case the program erases all round-specific data, including the announced bit. Reaching the end of all $L$ rounds accepts. The control, counter and scratch at the start of each round depend only on the round and length. One finite RAM program implements this using bit choices. Every play costs $O((m+gL)w)$ native instructions, since at most one contest occurs.

On the all-continue path, let the merged strategy have at most $K$ possible labels at each round boundary. Its original data array $x$ remains available in a fixed representation. All other retained information about $x$, including every cached probe result, previous answer and auxiliary register, must pass through the label. As in the single challenge, give it all current masks freely. Allow arbitrary true reads of the original array when computing the current answer. The quantity below counts the **decoder's true reads** on an all-continue path. The native program itself scans $x$ only on a contest. Winning requires the decoder's answer in every round to be correct even on the all-continue path because player 0 can contest immediately after any particular answer.

For one round condition on all earlier query masks, taking the earlier continue choices as fixed. Do not condition on the announced answers. Then $x$ is still uniform and the current label is a function of $x$; for a randomized zero-error decoder also fix its coins. The fresh group masks are independent of this information. For a fixed set $S$ of $d$ addresses in distinct groups, the masks on $S$ are independent uniform words. For fixed $x_{-S}$ and label, omitting $S$ still forces $y_S$ to annihilate the differences in the fiber. The masks outside $S$ may correlate with $y_S$, but the two data arrays agree outside $S$, so their contributions cancel for every query. Thus the probability of omitting at least one word in each of $d$ groups is at most $m^dK/q^d$, by summing the bound over the choices of $S$. It holds after averaging over the earlier masks as well.

* With $g=1$ and $K\le n^4$, take $d=1$. A union over all $L$ rounds bounds the probability of *any* omitted word in *any* round by $LmK/q\le n^{-4}$. Except on this fraction, the decoder uses at least $Lm=n^4$ true reads on the all-continue path. The longest native play takes $O(n^2\log n)$ steps.
* With $g=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil$, the same fixed program handles every fixed polynomial bound $K\le n^A$. Choose an integer $d>(A+2)/10$. The probability of a round with omissions in $d$ groups is at most $L m^d K/q^d\le n^{2+A-10d}=o(1)$ after the union over rounds. Otherwise each round retrieves at least $m-(d-1)\lceil m/g\rceil$ words. The total is at least $(1-O_A(1/\log n))n^4$ on these paths. The longest native play takes $O(n^2\log^2 n)$ steps.

Thus even if the proof-size cap counts extra true probes only along a path, this particular label-and-probe implementation requires more than $n^{8/a}/(\log n)^{4+\epsilon}$ for $a=2,3$. The bound survives if the game is used as a prologue for a player required to answer correctly before continuing to another game. To preserve both possible winners in the two-challenge wrapper, on a contest that finds the current responder correct, **advance to the next prologue or to $M$**, clearing scratch, while an incorrect responder still loses immediately. A prologue with no contested answers also advances. An honest opponent can therefore force entry into $M$ when the responder plays correctly. Two such prologues use at most two verification scans per play. In the one-group version a symbolic rule allowed to retain a whole $w$-bit word across rounds can accumulate $\bigoplus_i x_i$ when the data arrive and use it for every answer. This needs $2^w$ distinct concrete label values. That simple strategy illustrates exactly which persistent information the lower bound charges.

## Allowing wrong answers and changing the data representation

There is a second bound for a decoder with errors. Write $U=q^m$, take $0\le r\le m$ and let $D_r=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom mj(q-1)^j$. Under uniform independent data and queries, if its overall error probability is $\delta$, then
$$
 \Pr[\text{at most }r\text{ distinct probes}]\le 2\delta+\sqrt{K D_r/U}.
$$
Fix a nonempty full-data label fiber $F$. Truncate the decoder to signed output zero when it would exceed $r$ distinct probes. For fixed $y$ its resulting vector on $F$ lies in the restrictions of the Walsh characters supported on at most $r$ whole words. Indeed, a decision-tree leaf indicator fixes at most $r$ whole words, and each fixed-word indicator expands in characters of that word alone. Repeated probes may be answered from the known replies. This subspace has dimension at most $D_r$. Let $P$ be its real orthogonal projector on $\mathbb R^F$. The correct-answer vector $v_y=((-1)^{f(x,y)})_{x\in F}$ satisfies $\mathbb E_y[v_yv_y^\top]=I$ by Walsh orthogonality. Hence $\mathbb E_y\|Pv_y\|^2=\operatorname{rank}(P)\le D_r$. The truncated output vector has norm at most $\sqrt{|F|}$. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its expected inner product with $v_y$ by $\sqrt{|F|D_r}$. Sum over the label fibers and divide by $U$ to bound the overall signed correlation by $\sqrt{K D_r/U}$. This correlation equals the cheap-run probability minus twice the probability of a cheap wrong answer. Fixing independent coins also proves the randomized statement.

For exact decoding on **every** query with a uniform cap $r$, the same argument implies the worst-case bound $\log_2 K\ge mw-\log_2 D_r$. For any individual fiber, Walsh inversion gives this directly since the restricted characters for all $y$ span $\mathbb R^F$. A binary linear covering supplies the converse within $O(\log(mw+2))$ bits for the charged cut label. Start with all query masks supported on at most $r$ nonzero words, a set of size $D_r$. If its translates by a current binary subspace leave fraction $u$ uncovered, adjoining one extra generator leaves fraction at most $u^2$ for some generator by averaging over a uniform shift. After $s$ generators the count uncovered is at most $U\exp(-2^sD_r/U)$. It reaches zero for $s=\log_2(U/D_r)+O(\log(mw+2))$ with integer rounding, or one can use all $mw$ generators. Save the inner products with the generators. Every query differs from a linear combination of them on at most $r$ words. This construction permits arbitrary offline work to pick generators and a representation of each query.
