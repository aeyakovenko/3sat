# A fixed family of lengths and short intervals of first-production times

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** The exact allocation of halting probability to output words can be moved close to the final production deadline under linear SAT. One may prescribe the lengths and the smaller clocks before seeing any of the outputs. This yields a necessary condition about a specific family of programs with no restriction on their work memory.

Use the globally prefix-free native stream RAM from the earlier length experiment. Its word width after t steps is 4⌈log₂(t+2)⌉. In this note define heavy lengths and feedback masks for this machine with the length-dependent full cutoff B(m)=2^(20m). The Kraft bound Σe_j≤1/2 still holds because the programs chosen for different outputs are distinct, irrespective of deadlines. Hence there are infinitely many successful masks, one in each phase, and the failed masks have total normalized size at most 1/2. Earlier feedback outcomes can be recomputed from scratch before the next window. Simulating every name shorter than m through B(m), sorting its outputs and scanning all lengths up to a previous block of scale N' takes 2^(O(N')) native steps. The spacing of successive windows gives 2^o(m) time at a current length. At every offered block take the first h=2^(j−b_s)+1 lengths. [Stream model and verification of native runs](time-by-length.md) · [Window definitions](adaptive-window-lists.md)

**Clock selection.** Assume linear deterministic time for explicit SAT on the target RAM. Let D_m be a *uniform* integer deadline for every selected length, computable in poly(m) on its O(m)-bit representation, with 2^(2m)≤D_m≤B(m). Impose the following chronology condition: all earlier selected lengths l≤m have D_l≤D_m. A globally nondecreasing clock suffices. For a sufficiently large fixed a, there is a fixed decoder prefix which prints distinct words of total normalized mass exactly 2^(−d)Ω, each first produced by any short name during

    ( ⌊D_m/m^a⌋, D_m ].

Here Ω is the native stream machine's halting probability, d is a decoder-dependent constant, and the words occur at the predetermined selected lengths. The conclusion holds on the *same feedback schedule* for each such clock, always defining the schedule with B. As in the exact allocation, the submass on failed windows differs from 2^(−d)Ω by a computable real. [First-in-first-out construction and exact mass](fifo-producer-credits.md)

**Verification.** Put t_m=⌊D_m/m^a⌋. Count the distinct target words with a specified prefix that have been produced by t_m. Guess a sorted list of distinct target words with that prefix and of the required cardinality. Universally select a row and existentially check one native run of a short exact name for the designated word, including the elapsed cutoff. The encoding and deterministic construction cost (2^m+t_m)poly(m), with fixed alternation depth and a single bounded transcript in the matrix. Subtract from the size of the prefix class to count words absent by t_m. Rank and lexicographic unranking use polynomially many such tests. Under linear SAT, fixed-block elimination and executing the resulting program on the stream RAM multiply the cost only by a fixed power of m. [Uniform fixed-block elimination](linear-quantifiers.md)

There is no need to inspect a longer future length before dispatching at m. For an earlier l≤m in the current or a prior window, either l≤m/40 and D_l≤2^(20l)≤2^(m/2), or l>m/40 and t_l≤40^a D_m/m^a by chronology. The exponential lower bound on D_m absorbs all the explicit word lists. There are at most m smaller lengths. Simulating the halting inputs eligible at the current window and maintaining their queue also costs only a polynomial in m. Previous-window feedback replays fit 2^o(m). The total to allocate and unrank at m is therefore at most (D_m/m^a)poly(m)+2^((1+o(1))m). The polynomial exponent is independent of fixed a: a fixed algorithm treats a as a parameter, the clock registers have O(m) bits, there are a fixed number of quantifier eliminations, and the factors depending only on a are constants. Choose a larger than that exponent. The compiled decoder now finishes by D_m. Since D_m≤B(m), an unpaid credit persisting throughout a successful window would cause every word at its good block's h selected lengths to have a short name by B(m). This clears all credits as in the exact allocation proof. All paid words were absent at t_m; their first production is in the claimed interval.

**Test of the lower bound.** As an example the clock can be D_m=2^(10m) throughout the feedback schedule. On infinitely many failed windows a positive fraction of the offered blocks then have a positive fraction of their selected lengths on which a positive fraction of target words first appear in the specified interval. Apply the term test for random series to the failed-window mass, as in the word-completion calculation. An independent upper bound making that window-average fraction tend to zero for every fixed a for some single admissible D would contradict linear SAT. Choosing a new D after inspecting the release counts does not in general give the uniform timely deadline selector in the hypothesis. Separate fixed D can have different constants and different windows of high release. Their phase totals obey a common constraint proved below. I found no such vanishing bound. ## The same phases work for every fixed clock

One can synchronize different verifiers and deadlines more tightly than the latest window-by-window statement suggests. Let τ_r be the last window of phase r, with τ_0=0. Define P_r=Σ_{τ_{r−1}<s≤τ_r}q_s, the sum of the normalized mask sizes during the phase, including its success. These positive rationals are computable without a clock bound and sum to at most 3/2. Define a grant schedule independent of the verifier and its deadline by making a halting program p eligible at

    u(p) = max(t_p+1, |p|²+1, 2),
    I_r  = Σ_{p : τ_{r−1}<u(p)≤τ_r} 2^(−|p|).

I_r is computable by finding the end of phase r and simulating bounded programs for that bounded time. Its series sums to Ω. Fix any decoder with savings parameter d and an initialization window s_*. Delay early grants until max(u(p),s_*). Choose s_* large enough that m>|p|+d for every eligible p, including the finitely many small lengths for which |p|²≤|p|+d. Every credit present during a successful window is gone by its end. Consequently the queue is empty at *both* boundaries of every phase beginning after s_*. Conservation of credit gives the exact identity

    (actual output mass assigned by this decoder in phase r) = 2^(−d) I_r.    (1)

The I_r are independent of the chosen clock D, even when its decoder needs a different d or start window. This identity uses the exact word-by-word assignment, in which every funded cell yields a different word outside E. The same proof applies to the tape construction with its corresponding U and feedback schedule. [Queue clearing and finite conservation](fifo-producer-credits.md)

There are a constant η>0 and an infinite set of phases independent of *all* these decoders on which I_r≥ηP_r and 2^(−r)=o(I_r). Indeed Ω is random, so the term test gives I_r≥δ a(r) on infinitely many r, where a(r) is the universal discrete semimeasure. The computable summable sequence P_r is at most a constant multiple of a(r), and a(r) dominates a constant times 1/[r(log(r+2))²]. The positive-term perturbation for zero terms is the same as in the word-completion proof. [Bienvenu and Shen, Theorem 13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5028)

Deducting at most 2^(1−r) for the last, successful window from (1) leaves at least 2^(−d−1)ηP_r in the failed windows on every sufficiently late phase in this infinite set. For each fixed admissible deadline, this is a positive fraction of the total target slots by normalized weight: their weight on the selected lengths is between P_r and 2P_r. The constant and the finite starting exception may depend on the deadline. In particular *every finite library of deadlines has simultaneous positive release fractions on the same arbitrarily late phases*. The individual words, lengths, blocks and windows producing those fractions may differ. A suitable infinite set can be recognized after computing each phase. The tests in the limit-measure argument use this retrospective information. [High-phase definition](phase-limit-measures.md)

An exclusion criterion follows. Choose a fixed finite library of uniformly specified clocks as above. Suppose that for every sufficiently large fixed integer a the minimum across that library of the normalized weight of target words first appearing in (⌊D_m/m^a⌋,D_m] on failed windows, divided by P_r, tends to zero as r tends to infinity. Then linear SAT is impossible. A different clock can attain that minimum in every phase, and no algorithm need find it. For a finite library use one common sufficiently large a in the construction. For fixed distinct exponential rates the intervals can be disjoint at large m. Pigeonholing over k such rates alone bounds the smallest release fraction only by 2/k. With k fixed this does not give the required convergence. If k grows, the savings parameters and constants for the added clocks matter.

The earlier native argument found specially selected blocks where almost all words at a segment of lengths first appear close to a common deadline. Here the feedback masks and the segment in each block are specified independently of the secondary clock D. The selected subtree has an exactly prescribed mass and its outputs are cheaply recognizable under the hypothesis. Lean checks the finite phase ledger identity, and the finite experiment compares schedules that spend their credits in different windows. The infinitary and clock estimates are manuscript arguments.
