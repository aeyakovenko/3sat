# Long intervals of hard memory on a prescribed SAT verdict

Fix a deterministic RAM which gives a sound signal on one designated sign of explicit 3-SAT instances, and gives that signal within $N^{c+o(1)}$ steps on every instance of that sign, with $c\ge1$. Here $N$ counts encoded bits. The machine can use arbitrary workspace. Fix a uniform deterministic memory decoder, with the genuine read-only formula, native instruction time, a string and a work-word address as inputs. Call a time *cheap* if one string of at most $N^e$ bits lets this decoder answer every work-word address within $N^d$ steps per query, starting with fresh private scratch. The same string must serve all addresses. Fixed polylogarithmic factors in either allowance are permitted. CPU registers and control need a constant number of extra words per checkpoint.

Suppose also that there is a sound nondeterministic UNSAT recognizer with a successful branch within $N^{r+o(1)}$ steps on every unsatisfiable instance, $r\ge1$. The following strict inequalities may always be evaluated at $r=c$. For a negative signal use the original machine as the reference. For a positive signal and any fixed rational exponent $c'<c+\varepsilon$ with $c'>c$, choose a clock of order $N^{c'}$ bounding all the positive signal times, increasing its constant to cover finite exceptions. Run to that cutoff, give a positive verdict exactly when the signal was seen, and give a negative verdict otherwise. This gives a reference for each such slack; strictness lets us use one sufficiently small slack in the proof. An exact constructible $N^{c+o(1)}$ cutoff is not needed.

**Window theorem.** For $0<b<c$ and $e,d\ge0$, the following conditions force arbitrarily large instances of the designated sign with a block of $\lceil N^b\rceil$ consecutive strictly pre-signal times, every one of which fails to be cheap.

| Designated sign | Chart size condition | Segment time condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UNSAT | $r(c-b+e)<1$ | $b+d<1$ |
| SAT | $r(c-b+e)<1$ | $r(b+d)<1$ |

In fact the block can be selected from the disjoint windows starting at instruction times $1,1+\lceil N^b\rceil,\ldots$. The two rows assert results on their own input families. As one example, set $c=r=8/5$, $e=d=1/100$, and $b=79/80$. A solver meeting this negative time bound has arbitrarily large unsatisfiable inputs with $\lceil N^{79/80}\rceil$ consecutive states that each defeat the stated description and query budgets. At linear time on both signs, take $c=r=1$, $e=d=1/3$, $b=1/2$. On arbitrarily large formulas of each sign, every one of $\lceil\sqrt N\rceil$ consecutive states fails that joint budget. On positives, $c=r=4/3$, $e=1/16$, $d=1/32$, $b=11/16$ gives another such interval. [Finite exponent checks](../lean/SignedSnapshotWindows.lean)

## One chart and its sign

Assume every full internal window contains a cheap state on every sufficiently large instance of the chosen sign. Let $E$ be the time of the first signal and $w=\lceil N^b\rceil$. Pick a cheap state from each complete window in times $1,\ldots,E-1$. Keep time zero and the terminal signal as uncharged endpoints. The chart has $O(1+E/w)$ entries, and its maximum gap is at most $2w$. With no complete window the whole computation fits one segment. Give the true endpoint times, controllers and one independently chosen description per internal entry. The packed chart has at most $N^{u+o(1)}$ bits where $u=c-b+e$. [Finite window bounds](../lean/PersistentSnapshotIntervals.lean)

The outer nondeterministic verifier guesses the chart and checks its structure, including entry lengths, offsets, time order, gaps and clock. A bounded nondeterministic defect machine with indexed read access to that already checked chart chooses a segment. Replay it with a fresh overlay of its stores, tagging zero stores too. Unless overwritten, a work read uses the proposed starting decoder, with the genuine read-only formula and capped time. Give malformed outputs or timeouts a fixed default. For an internal endpoint compare the controller and challenge an arbitrary legal work address against the next decoder, including addresses not touched in the replay. The first segment has genuine zero initial memory, the last must end in the specified signal, and crossing an earlier halt is a defect. An immediate initial signal is tested directly. The defect machine uses $N^{v+o(1)}$ steps where $v=b+d$. It never scans the whole chart. With no defect, induction identifies the actual states at all cut times and forces a genuine signal. This soundness argument covers all inputs, including those on which honest descriptions are unavailable. [Checkpoint proof](solver-snapshot-descriptions.md)

## Iteration for a negative signal

For each fixed bounded nondeterministic machine with $m$ words of public input, horizon $R$, and $H=\max(2,m,R)$, let $W(q,r)$ denote sound nondeterministic certification of its nonacceptance in time $(R^q+m^r+1)H^{o(1)}$. Locally generate the ordinary padded 3-CNF of length $H^{1+o(1)}$ for timely acceptance. Initial materialization and the UNSAT reference give $W(r,r)$. The packed construction charges all public data and allows a native solver or decoder to fetch an individual formula word in polylogarithmic time. Each induction stage uses its own fixed machine, with an ordinary genuine formula for that machine. [Bounded computation and word-width conventions](packed-band-transfer.md)

On source nonacceptance the formula is unsatisfiable. Guess the chart of its negative signal and certify there exists no defect using $W(q,r)$. The new call has horizon $H^{v+o(1)}$ and public input of $m+H^{u+o(1)}$ words including the chart and source input. Its time, including the guess and structural checks, gives $W(q',r)$ for

$$
q'=\max\{ru,\;vq\}.
$$

Indeed the inherited term $m^r$ is charged on this call and the new $H^{q'}$ is at most a constant times $R^{q'}+m^r+1$ whenever $q'\le r$. With $ru<1$, $v<1$, and $q_0=r$, all iterates satisfy this inequality. For $i\ge1$ the closed form is $q_i=\max(ru,v^i r)$, so a finite $i$ has $q_i<1$. Using $R=m^K$ for sufficiently large fixed $K>r$, the resulting certificate of nonacceptance of a bounded universal nondeterministic interpreter runs in $o(R)$. On this certifier's own padded program, the interpreter accepts precisely if it sees an accepting branch of that program within its clock. The certifier accepts precisely on the interpreter's nonacceptance. Fixed-opponent simulation and logarithmic word widths are charged in the [complementary diagonal](cumulative-sat-memory.md). This is the contradiction. For the $8/5$ example, the floor $ru=249/250$ and multiplier $v=399/400$ give the first strict crossing after 188 updates. [Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas, Theorem 2.1](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf)

## A single check for a positive signal

Use the fixed unary nondeterministic hierarchy language with upper tape time $R=n^K$ and excluded time $O(R/\log(n+2))$, for a sufficiently large fixed $K$. Its ordinary bounded-acceptance formula has $N=R^{1+o(1)}$ bits. Every formula word can be generated from $n$ alone in polylogarithmic time, since the public source string is unary. A nondeterministic verifier guesses the chart of the positive signal and materializes the formula for its defect machine. The defect instance has only the chart and short parameters as initialized public data, and length $R^{\max(u,v)+o(1)}$. A single application of the UNSAT reference to that instance gives a sound nondeterministic positive test in $R^{r\max(u,v)+o(1)}$ RAM steps, plus time to process the unary input. On a positive input the honest chart passes. On a negative one a passing chart would give a false positive signal. Simulate the bounded nondeterministic RAM on tapes by guessing its chronological access records and checking them in address-time order. Sorting and word arithmetic cost only polylogarithmic overhead. The two positive inequalities put the source language inside its excluded time bound. [Unary hierarchy construction and RAM-to-tape accounting](positive-witness-hierarchy.md) · [Seiferas, Fischer and Meyer, Theorem 5](https://doi.org/10.1145/322047.322061)

The proof takes the native window width from the actual encoded formula length, including its padding. For nonrational exponents, one may use a rational upper approximation to the window exponent when constructing the verifier. Every larger window contains a cheap time under the premise about *all aligned* smaller windows once the larger width exceeds twice the smaller width. Approximate the remaining caps from above too. With strict margins and a fixed finite number of simulations, these changes preserve the final crossing. The cheap-time premise is needed only at the original smaller widths.

Eliminating $b$ gives $r(c+e+d-1)<1$ on negatives and $r(c+e+d)<2$ on positives. The former pointwise threshold already follows from the [negative cumulative theorem](cumulative-snapshot-descriptions.md), and its polylogarithmic-latency positive counterpart follows from the [positive cumulative theorem](positive-witness-hierarchy.md). The sliding interval and the polynomial latency are explicit here. Under quasilinear SAT time the earlier [negative interval result](persistent-snapshot-query-cost.md) also has logarithmically precise, pointwise size–time products. A linear-time permutation-inversion routine can sustain a difficult indexed state throughout a long interval of work on that state. [Linear-time construction](inverse-snapshot-frontier.md) An unconditional upper bound on the cost of describing some state within the required windows of every fast SAT solver remains missing.

## Simultaneous witnesses for several solvers

Fix any finite collection of solver, decoder and parameter choices satisfying the inequalities for the **same sign**, and a common UNSAT reference exponent $r$. There are arbitrarily large formulas of that sign on which every member has its own hard window. The intervals and the actual runs can differ. To prove this, suppose instead that on every sufficiently large formula of that sign some member has a cheap time in every full window. Guess the member and its chart together. On the other sign soundness holds for every choice of the member. Use $u=\max_i(c_i-b_i+e_i)$ and $v=\max_i(b_i+d_i)$ in the same certification recurrence. Finiteness gives the strict margins simultaneously. Short input-length exceptions can be hardwired. Applying the finite assertion to successive prefixes of a countable collection gives a single sequence of increasing formula lengths that eventually witnesses hardness for each fixed member. This diagonal selection gives no efficient construction of that sequence.
