# How much can a clause order reveal about later input reads?

Take the satisfiable repeated-unit and unsatisfiable implication-chain 3-CNFs from the scan-time construction. In each of the $B$ movable words change the tag tautology to
$$
  (t_i\lor\neg t_i\lor x_{1+f(i)}),\qquad i\in\{0,\ldots,B-1\}.
$$
Here $f$ is any table with values in $\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$, $2\le M\le B$. The tag $t_i=2^{\ell-2}+i$ identifies the word, and the third literal exposes its payload $f(i)$. The formula allows every permutation of the words. The SAT verdict depends on the logical clauses and is the same for every table and order at each sign. As before, a sound verdict requires loading every movable word because one can reverse the verdict by modifying the logical clause inside any unread word. A fixed correct solver checks both families in $O(B)$ word steps by accepting any in-range payload in its tag tautology and checking the rest of its templates. [Physical encoding, sensitivity and scan algorithm](scan-time-register-complexity.md)

At the first load of each fresh position, ask a predictor for the **whole pair** $(i,f(i))$ before the returned word is revealed. It knows the solver, sign, current position and entire previous interaction. On a first read, all other bits of the word follow from the pair and the sign. The predictor can compute arbitrarily on the past. It has no other access to the physical array or to $f$. One fresh prediction is charged per movable position, regardless of the solver's access order or repeated loads.

**Sharp rate.** For $M=B$, every fixed $\epsilon>0$ and all sufficiently large $B$, all but a $2^{-\Omega_\epsilon(B\log B)}$ fraction of tables force more than $(1/2-\epsilon)B$ misses on **every** physical ordering, simultaneously for both signs and every sufficiently short predictor and sound monitor that reaches its verdict. Conversely, there is one uniform predictor such that for every table, either sign, and **every fixed correct total deterministic solver**, some order lets it predict at least $(1/2-O(1/\log B))B$ pairs correctly. Thus choosing an order using the entire table can reveal half of the future words to a predictor that gets the past for free. For $M=\lceil B^\alpha\rceil$, $0<\alpha\le1$ fixed, the sharp leading fraction of misses is $\alpha/(1+\alpha)$.

## Reconstruction with overrides

Fix the sign, solver and predictor. On a particular signaling run, encode the set of missed discovery ordinals in a $B$-bit mask. At each missed ordinal also send the true pair. Run the solver against a simulated input. Predetermined words have fixed answers, and a table indexed by destination supplies repeat reads. At a new position use the prediction or its override to generate and cache the actual word. This reproduces the real computation inductively. Every tag is discovered, so the transcript recovers $f$ even if the order was chosen *after seeing the table and the predictor*. A malformed description or one that loops produces at most one recovered table. No simulation deadline is necessary for this count.

For a collection of at most $2^u$ combined choices of solver, predictor and any hindsight advice, the fraction of tables for which some order admits a signaling run with at most $q$ misses is at most
$$
 2^{1+u+B+q(\lceil\log_2 B\rceil+\lceil\log_2 M\rceil)-B\log_2 M}.
$$
The extra bit covers both signs. Pad the correction list to $q$ entries. The proof counts tables directly and pays no $\log(B!)$ union bound for choosing an order. Taking $q$ below $B\log M/(\log B+\log M)$ by $\epsilon B$, and $u\le c_{\alpha,\epsilon}B\log B$ with small enough positive constant, gives the stated exceptional fraction. In particular this includes circuits with a sufficiently small constant times $N/\log N$ gates predicting load replies from the actual time within a polynomial cutoff. All states and times used by the predictor in the decoding are the ones generated from the reconstructed replies.

## Choosing an order that carries the tail payloads

Reserve the $h$ tags $0,\ldots,h-1$ for the first $h$ discoveries and arrange the remaining $q=B-h$ tags in increasing order. This refers to the order of discovery, which can differ completely from numerical order of physical addresses. Each tail pair now has a known tag. Its payload needs $m=\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$ bits. Choose $h$ such that
$$
 \sum_{j=1}^h\lfloor\log_2 j\rfloor\ \ge\ (B-h)m.
$$
When $M=\lceil B^\alpha\rceil$, one may take $h=\alpha B/(1+\alpha)+O_\alpha(B/\log B)$. Indeed the sum is at least $h\log_2 h-(1+\log_2 e)h$, by integrating $\log x$ and accounting for floors. For this choice of $h$, $\log h=\log B+O_\alpha(1)$, and increasing $h$ by a large enough constant times $B/\log B$ covers all rounding losses. The least valid $h$ can also be found by summing these integer capacities.

To transmit the tail payloads, concatenate their fixed-width fields in tag order and pad the final bit string with zeros to the available capacity. Maintain an array of the remaining front tags and an inverse array. When $j$ remain, use the next $\lfloor\log_2 j\rfloor$ bits as an index below $j$, emit that tag, and remove it by exchanging it with the last active tag. The decoder sees the actual tag and recovers the same bits from its current inverse position. It learns all $q$ tail payloads by the end of the front. It can now forecast every remaining pair exactly. Invalid unexpected tags may put the predictor into a fixed default mode. Initializing the two arrays costs $O(B)$. Reading or emitting one packed chunk, updating the inverse positions, and advancing the bit-stream cursor need $O(1)$ word operations per new pair. The current floor logarithm is maintained by decrementing at powers of two.

For an adaptive solver, supply the prescribed pairs lazily to its first visits and cache all assigned positions. Any partial injection of these tags extends to a legal full permutation. If the solver could run forever without discovering a new word, it would do so on a total completion. If it signaled early, the still unread word would contradict soundness by sensitivity. Since it halts correctly on every presentation, induction produces all $B$ discoveries and one fixed physical order realizing the prescribed sequence. This last assertion uses totality on the family. A monitor stopping within a prescribed cutoff on only some orders need not stop by that cutoff on the order constructed here. Permutation coding already uses the $h!$ orders of $h$ distinct symbols to convey information. The present upper calculation uses a simple integer code. The additional issues here are the reconstruction lower bound over *every* order and realizing the encoder's chosen sequence along an arbitrary solver's adaptive first reads. The random-order card-guessing calculation and this favorable-order rate have different quantifiers. In the latter, the order can carry information about the unseen payloads. Even an unconditional error bound that survives this choice coexists with a scan-time SAT decision. To use unpredictable input words for a time lower bound one must also force computational work beyond their initial loads. Priority for this rate in the stated SAT execution model has not been established.

The rate also holds using genuine source and destination variables in a cycle of XOR constraints. Individually flipping a positive fraction of the physical variable-name bits then reverses SAT, even on the hard-to-predict orders.
