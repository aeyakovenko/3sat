# Short descriptions of the actual memory at many cuts of every SAT solver

On the explicit quadratic 3-CNFs, fix any deterministic SAT solver and any deterministic procedure for reconstructing an entire memory snapshot. Give the procedure the original formula, a short advice string chosen *after the whole run*, and even the complete observed native trace, controller and work state through the **start** of the current discovery epoch. Its task is to produce the exact work array at the epoch **end**. Computation, scratch, reads of earlier data and reads of still-unseen data are free. The charge counts distinct data pins first learned during the epoch that the procedure inspects while constructing the array, together with those that the true suffix rereads. The procedure is given the end controller. The names of the newly learned pins and the native end time are available only if encoded in the controller, the advice, or the data it probes. The RAM may use arbitrarily large workspace and arbitrarily much time.

Divide the first $m$ distinct pin discoveries into complete consecutive epochs of $t$ new pins. There exist positive constants $a,\beta,\delta,c$ such that for every integer $t$ between $C_M\log m$ and $am$, except on at most $2^{-ct}$ of uniform inputs, **at least $c\lfloor m/t\rfloor$ pairwise disjoint discovery epochs on the actual run** meet the following conclusion. For each such epoch, every advice of at most $\beta t$ bits either fails to reconstruct the complete true work array or the combined procedure and suffix inspect more than
$$
     r=\left\lfloor\frac{\delta t}{\log_2(2+2m/t)}\right\rfloor
$$
distinct data pins learned in that epoch. The exceptional bound also holds, with an absolute factor, on each verdict. In fact the conclusion holds at every high-rank epoch of this width on these inputs. For $t$ of order $m$ this gives a checkpoint at which linearly many bits of advice or linearly many old data pins are required, unless the native suffix itself accounts for the reads. The unit here is a designated data pin; the physical formula has $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ bits. A procedure that uses only the history and advice, and never queries new input, is allowed.

This is an exact *whole-array* statement. A separate indexed decoder could inspect a few *different* old pins per requested word while inspecting many pins across all words. The theorem counts this union when the decoder is used to produce the array. It complements the indexed, polylogarithmic-latency snapshot theorem using diagonal inputs. ## Removing the endogenous split even when the advice changes on the companion input

Fix a full start prefix $\pi$ with earlier pin set $P$. The epoch then learns a set $X$ of $t$ new pins and leaves an unseen set $Y$. Given $\pi$, the bits outside $P$ are independent before the epoch. High rank means that the binary cross adjacency of $X$ into $Y$ has rank at least $\alpha t$, for a fixed $\alpha>0$. Every full run has at least $\alpha\lfloor m/t\rfloor$ such epochs after adjusting $a,\alpha$. Conditional on $\pi$, write $\lambda$ for the probability of high rank and some short advice yielding exact reconstruction with at most $r$ combined old-pin reads. The deterministic procedure can be fixed nonuniformly for a scale or a start prefix, as long as the same procedure is used across the unobserved input choices.

Sample a public fill independently and keep the observed $P\cup X$ bits while filling $Y$. Given the end prefix the original and companion are iid completions, so the joint event has probability at least $\lambda^2$. The successful headers on the two completions need not agree. Fix one fill and catalog $(X,c_0)$ for successful companions. Encode the end controller $c_0$, one successful companion advice, and the positions of the at most $r$ observed $X$ bits which differ from the fill during reconstruction or the subsequent suffix. Use the given start history on $P$, the corrected fill elsewhere, and the chosen advice to reconstruct the complete array, then run the suffix from it. Both computations are exact on this companion by instruction induction. Let $T$ consist of all pin positions seen on either computation outside $P$. Then $T$ includes all but at most nine of $Y$, and $|T\cap X|\le r$. Another list of at most $r+9$ positions recovers $Y$ and hence $X$. The analyst does not supply $X$ to the procedure during replay. For $s=\lfloor\beta t\rfloor$, the number of catalog pairs satisfies
$$
 J\le m^{O_M(1)}2^{s+1} V(2m,r)V(2m,r+9).
$$
For each pair its true end-prefix rows admitting a cheap companion form a cylinder $A\times\{0,1\}^Y$ of mass $u$. This holds even with postselected advice because the fill is fixed and the event on that companion depends only on the row. Across the catalog the cylinders are disjoint.

## Equality with an arbitrary real snapshot costs no vector dimension

On a fixed cylinder the true array at epoch end is a function $C(x)$ of the $t$ row bits. Fix one original advice string $z$ and a column $y$. Run the reconstructor on the physical input. Start a hypothetical suffix from its output $C'$, using the specified controller and the actual formula. Abort with the zero vector on divergence, a malformed output, or an attempted $(r+1)$st distinct query of an $X$ pin in the combined computations. Otherwise return the vector $g(x,y)e_{C'}$, where $g$ is the simulated verdict sign and $e_{C'}$ the unit basis vector indexed by that entire array. The vector can have an arbitrarily large finite dimension. Each column is a tree of depth at most $r$ in the row bits once distinct queries are cached. Thus its vector coefficients have Walsh degree at most $r$ in $x$ and its pointwise norm is at most one.

The row vector is $1_A(x)e_{C(x)}$, with expected squared norm $u$. Its inner product with the column-dependent vector vanishes when the offered memory differs from the actual memory. On equality the simulated suffix is the genuine one. Multiplying by the true verdict sign therefore counts *exactly* the successful original inputs with this advice on the cylinder. Expanding the vector-valued tree in the $V(t,r)$ possible old-pin characters and applying Cauchy–Schwarz with the normalized sign-matrix operator norm gives an upper bound $2^{-\alpha t/2}\sqrt{u V(t,r)}$ on their mass. The argument does not pack the whole array into a register, charge its bit length, or assume any limit on decoder running time. [Spectral calculation](sparse-reply-sat-cuts.md)

Summing over pairs and over the independently chosen original advice, and averaging the fill, gives
$$
 \lambda^2\le 2^{s+1}\sqrt{J V(t,r)}\,2^{-\alpha t/2}.
$$
For $r$ as above, $\log_2 V(2m,r+9)=O(\log m)+O(\delta\log(2/\delta)\,t)$, using $\binom ab\le(\mathrm ea/b)^b$ and $V(n,r+9)\le V(n,r)V(n,9)$. The same estimate holds for the other pin lists. Choose $\beta,\delta$ small, then $C_M$ large. The display gives $\lambda\le2^{-c_0t}$ uniformly over all starting prefixes. A union bound over at most $m/t$ epochs leaves $2^{-ct}$ after one further increase of $C_M$. The same argument also gives simultaneous dyadic widths or any fixed polynomially many prescribed reconstructors by summing their individual tails when their catalog constants are uniform.

In the special case of a codebook requiring no input queries, this excludes every one of $2^{\beta t}$ arbitrary candidate arrays, chosen with knowledge of the starting prefix and the end controller, whenever the native suffix rereads at most $r$ of the epoch's pins. The identified obstruction for elapsed time is quantitative. An $O(m)$-step machine can store one packed word for each tile of logarithmically many bits and later read all these words across one common cut. It can pay for the memory information and finish within $O(N/\log N)$ on this very family. ## Replaying the live suffix on fresh continuations

There is also a theorem for a description that only tries to regenerate what the suffix actually uses. No complete proposed array is needed. Keep the same fixed start history, end controller and short advice interface. Give a fixed simulator the original formula, and let it produce a verdict. For a continuation call its replay successful if it gives the true sign, covers all but nine of the unseen pins $Y$, and inspects at most $r$ of the epoch's $X$ pins. Count the union of its own pins and the native pin queries it replays. Reported native queries can equivalently be executed as dummy input reads. In particular, every exact replay of the genuine suffix, including the actual input-read positions and with the specified combined probe cap, satisfies this test.

At every high-rank epoch of almost every real run, **no single advice string** of at most $\beta t$ bits can achieve success on a fraction greater than $1/2+2^{-c_1t}$ of the independent unseen-pin continuations of that actual prefix. The exceptional probability is $2^{-\Omega(t)}$ for suitable constants. The advice can depend on the entire observed prefix, including its whole true array. Its success probability is tested with that same advice on the different continuations. This distinction is essential: one hindsight verdict bit could predict a single fixed continuation. The preceding whole-array result permits advice to be chosen independently on each completion by checking the physical memory for equality.

For the proof fix the start prefix. For each good end transcript choose one advice with success probability $p\ge p_0=1/2+\epsilon$. Write $\mu_0$ for the mass of high-rank end transcripts for which such an advice exists. A public fill makes the chosen simulator succeed on mass at least $p_0\mu_0$. Run the read-set catalog argument on these successful companion simulations: knowing their controller, chosen advice, old-pin corrections and the symmetric-difference list recovers $X$. The coverage requirement on unseen pins is precisely what the last list uses. Keep the advice as part of each catalog entry. For its row set $A$, membership means the end transcript is good, its chosen advice is this one and the fixed fill succeeds. The retained cylinders are disjoint and their total mass is at least $p_0\mu_0$.

For fresh uniform $y$ simulate this same advice with output $g\in\{-1,0,1\}$, abstaining on a pin-budget violation or divergence. Its column tree has old-pin degree at most $r$. Every retained row has $\mathbb E_y[Fg]\ge 2p_0-1=2\epsilon$, since *all* successful continuations give the true sign. On the others an incorrect sign contributes at worst minus one. Consequently
$$
 2\epsilon p_0\mu_0\le\sum_A\mathbb E[1_A Fg]
 \le 2^{-\alpha t/2}\sqrt{J V(t,r)}.
$$
The right-hand side is exponentially small in $t$ with the same parameter choices. Pick a sufficiently small $c_1>0$, set $\epsilon=2^{-c_1t}$ and union-bound the epochs as before. This gives a limitation on regenerating actual future behavior, even if every cell that never affects the suffix is omitted from the representation. It still allows the solver to pay for the necessary reads in linear elapsed time.

In particular this tests an **indexed** decoder of the epoch-end memory. Give it the starting history, end controller, full formula, and one header chosen at the end prefix. Invoke it on first reads of inherited addresses, cache its returned words, and forward all subsequent native input probes during the simulated suffix. Suppose a call inspects at most $h\ge1$ pins from $X$. For almost every run at every high-rank epoch, for *each* such short header at least a $1/2-2^{-c_1t}$ fraction of its fresh continuations satisfy one of the following conditions. The native suffix rereads more than $r/2$ distinct epoch pins, it inspects more than $r/(2h)$ distinct inherited addresses, or the decoder fails to supply a genuine word at one of those addresses. Otherwise the simulation is faithful, it covers the unseen pins, and its combined pin cost is at most $r$. The header may encode a dense state and the test concerns only addresses actually consulted. For this corollary one header is tested across continuations; selecting a new header for each completed input was allowed in the whole-array theorem above.

The use of discovery epochs has precedents in the chronogram method for dynamic data structures, including lower bounds for one-bit answers. Here the single ordinary SAT input is available from the beginning and the solver determines the epoch boundaries by its own read order. Fixed-order streaming and read-restricted branching programs have related results, as do linear-time branching programs with a size restriction. Priority of the above joint quantifiers remains unverified. [Larsen, Weinstein and Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.03575) · [Ajtai](https://cims.nyu.edu/~regev/toc/articles/v001a008/index.html)
