# Recovering a unique witness by voting across RAM traces

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A parity program can require linearly many corrections to every individual trace gradient while the coordinatewise majority of four trace gradients is its exact coefficient vector. I obtain a deterministic near-linear extraction criterion from linear SAT when each coefficient is predicted with positive bias and the dependence of each prediction on the baseline has bounded Fourier norm. The traces can be arbitrarily long and branch at different instructions.

## A finite search for a successful vote

Let an explicit formula $F$ have one satisfying assignment $w\in\{0,1\}^m$. Under the unrestricted linear SAT hypothesis a deterministic RAM $P$ computes $\langle w,s\rangle$ for any mask $s$ in time $T=(|F|+m)\operatorname{polylog}(|F|+m+2)$. At an arbitrary baseline $b$ run $P(b)$, freeze its control and addresses, and take the formal gradient of that straight-line calculation with respect to perturbations of $b$. Write $g_b$ for the resulting $m$-bit vector. Compiling only the executed trace and running reverse accumulation takes $\widetilde O(T)$ deterministic time. All bits of the prediction arrive together. [One-trace construction and its failure example](unique-witness-batches.md)

Suppose a family produces an $m$-bit baseline from a seed of $\lambda$ bits in time $U$, and for each coordinate $i$ a uniform seed satisfies
$$
\Pr[g_b(i)=w_i]\ge\tfrac12+\eta .
$$
No independence across coordinates is needed. With $k$ independent seeds, where $k$ is an odd integer of order $\eta^{-2}\log(m+2)$ with a sufficiently large coefficient, the chance of the wrong majority at any specified coordinate is at most $\exp(-2k\eta^2)$. The union bound across coordinates is smaller than one half. Thus a list of $k$ seeds exists whose bitwise majority is the whole unique assignment.

There is a **deterministic** way to use this existence statement under linear SAT. Guess the seeds, run their traces and compute their majority vector, then test that vector in the original $F$. For a partially specified seed list, existence of a successful completion is checkable in $\widetilde O(k(T+U+\lambda))$ time under linear SAT by the RAM transcript reduction. Recover a successful list one bit at a time with this test and run it once. The total time is
$$
\widetilde O\big((1+k\lambda)\,k(T+U+\lambda)\big).
$$
For a formula with a unique projection, also guess the remaining bits when validating its majority. For a general parity RAM without $F$, validate the candidate by checking agreement with the actual $P$ on all masks, which uses one additional fixed quantifier block. This remains quasilinear per test under the same hypothesis. The numerical guarantee requires publicly specified valid bounds on seed length and number of repetitions. A known polylogarithmic bound suffices for the near-linear conclusion. [Fixed-block elimination](linear-quantifiers.md)

The predicate may instead guess up to $e$ coordinate indices and flip the corresponding majority bits before checking $F$. If the coordinate margin holds except on an unknown set of at most $e$ positions, some seed list has correct majority everywhere outside that set. The list of flips costs $O(e\log(m+2))$ further description bits and $O(e\log(m+2))$ bit operations per candidate. This allows the positive-margin assumption to fail completely at a few positions.

## Short seeds for predictions depending on dense mask parities

An arbitrary uniform baseline takes $m$ bits. Here is a sufficient condition that removes this cost. Define $f_i(b)=(-1)^{g_b(i)+w_i}$, with exponent modulo two, and write $\Lambda$ for an upper bound on the sums of absolute values of its Fourier coefficients over the parity characters, simultaneously in $i$. Assume $\mathbb E_b f_i(b)\ge 2\eta$ under the uniform baseline. For a distribution whose bias against each nonconstant character is at most $\delta$, Fourier expansion gives
$$
\big|\mathbb E_{\rm gen} f_i-\mathbb E_{\rm unif} f_i\big|\le \delta\Lambda .
$$
Taking $\delta\le\eta/\Lambda$ retains a correctness margin of at least $\eta/2$. The fixed unknown sign $(-1)^{w_i}$ changes none of the norm bounds.

For completeness take $\ell\ge1$ with $2^\ell\ge m\Lambda/\eta$. Draw $x,y$ independently and uniformly from $\mathbb F_{2^\ell}$ and output the low binary coordinate of each $yx^j$ for $0\le j<m$ in a fixed polynomial basis. For any nonzero parity mask the bias equals the probability over $x$ that its associated nonzero polynomial of degree below $m$ vanishes. Indeed for any $x$ outside this root set, multiplication by the nonzero value is bijective and the low bit of its product with $y$ is balanced. The bias is thus at most $(m-1)/2^\ell$. The construction uses $2\ell$ random bits and $m\,\mathrm{poly}(\ell)$ time. If the field modulus has not been precomputed, guess one monic degree-$\ell$ irreducible polynomial once for the whole list. Polynomial squaring modulo that polynomial and Euclidean gcd tests verify irreducibility in $\mathrm{poly}(\ell)$ bit operations, using the standard criterion with $X^{2^\ell}-X$ and the prime divisors of $\ell$. It costs $O(\ell)$ additional bits to recover the same modulus for the final evaluation. [Alon, Goldreich, Håstad and Peralta](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~oded/p_aghp.html) · [Naor and Naor](https://doi.org/10.1137/0222053)

For example, suppose each coordinate prediction, as a *function of the baseline*, has a parity decision tree of depth $d$. The trees need not be known, and each question may be a parity of all $m$ bits. A leaf's indicator is the product of at most $d$ factors $(1\pm\chi)/2$, each of Fourier norm at most one. The norm of a product is at most the product of the norms. Summing the at most $2^d$ signed leaf indicators gives $\Lambda\le2^d$. In particular when $d$ and $1/\eta$ are polylogarithmic in $T$, one can take $\ell=O(\log(m+2)+d+\log(1/\eta))$, and all seed search, trace replay and witness production cost $\widetilde O(T)$. This condition constrains the semantic prediction functions. The RAM computing the underlying parity stays unrestricted.

## Four traces can beat the best single trace by a linear factor

Use two selector-mask bits and four equal blocks of $L$ data bits. The desired answer is the parity of all $4L$ data bits, independently of the selectors. The selectors choose a hidden block. In that block read each data bit and branch on it, adding a public one to the accumulator only on a true branch. In every other block XOR the data bits into the accumulator directly. Use fixed-order loops, single-bit data locations, and fixed address calculations independent of the data. Use the selectors only to choose one of four instruction paths. The RAM time is $O(L)$.

At every baseline all control choices of the frozen trace are constants. The tangent of the output is one on the visible blocks and zero on the hidden block, independently of the data bits of the baseline. Every named tangent wire of this trace has support on at most **one** hidden data coordinate. In the hidden branch the only mask-dependent guard needed is the single data bit. Its accumulator update contributes a constant to the frozen execution. Other instructions have no access to hidden data. Consequently every correction list must include at least $L$ named wires at this baseline, since a missing one on every hidden coordinate must be supplied. The bound holds for every baseline, including those whose data bits are all ones.

Across the four selector settings each data coefficient is predicted correctly three times and each selector coefficient four times. Four trace evaluations and a majority recover the complete vector. Each signed prediction depends on only two baseline bits. The discrepancy between the single- and multiple-trace costs concerns this specific trace representation. The parity function in the example has a transparent direct implementation. [Lean verification of the list lower bound, vote and finite union argument](../lean/MultitraceWitnesses.lean)

## Why a fixed public sample can fail

Given any predetermined list $B$ of distinct baselines, one can test membership by scanning the list and comparing bits sequentially. Use branching comparisons with fixed bit addresses, so on a frozen trace each such test has a single-coordinate tangent and its recorded success flag is a constant. Compute dense parity with the opaque, branch-on-each-bit implementation exactly on $B$ and with an ordinary XOR chain elsewhere. The time is $O(m(|B|+1))$. Every baseline in $B$ gives zero output gradient and only single-coordinate corrections, while every baseline outside $B$ gives the exact full gradient. For $|B|\ll2^m$ the uniform prediction accuracy tends to one. This defeats that fixed list when it is chosen independently of the program. The counterexample leaves the program-dependent seed search intact. At the other extreme, using the branch implementation everywhere makes all coordinate predictions fail for every baseline and every pool of traces.

## Arbitrary gradient behavior at every baseline

A stronger obstruction applies to unrestricted dependence on the baseline. For any Boolean vector computation $H(s)$, append the literal XOR-AND expression
$$
\sum_{i=1}^m (s_i^2+s_i)H_i(s)
$$
to a parity program, XORing it into the result. Every summand is zero on Boolean inputs. But at an arbitrary Boolean baseline $b$, in direction $v$, the formal change of $s_i^2+s_i$ is $b_i v_i+b_i v_i+v_i=v_i$. The product rule now gives $v_i H_i(b)$ for the entire summand, independently of the tangent computed for $H_i$ because its multiplier has value zero. Thus the vector added to the formal gradient is **exactly $H(b)$ simultaneously at all baselines**. The construction adds the cost of one evaluation of $H$ and $O(m)$ simple gates or instructions. If $H$ uses an arbitrary RAM computation, run it in separate memory and then perform the displayed literal gates on its outputs. Freezing any path through $H$ leaves this calculation valid. A circuit simplifier that cancels the Boolean-null subexpressions changes the extracted formal gradient. The obstruction concerns the specified literal trace representation. [Lean check of the silent value and its nonzero variation](../lean/MultitraceWitnesses.lean)

This identifies the precise difficulty of one proposed derandomization. Consider uniform deterministic procedures returning an **explicit successful list of baselines** in quasilinear time whenever an ordinary native parity program is supplied with an efficient verifier for its unique coefficient vector and every coordinate has constant positive uniform margin. Allow $O(\log(m+2))$ baselines, with a fixed target margin such as $1/6$. The existence of such a procedure is equivalent up to polylogarithmic factors to deterministic quasilinear search for an accepting input to a native bounded-time predicate that accepts at least two thirds of its strings. Public instance data, random-string lengths and clocks are charged in both formulations.

In one direction, the verifier for $k$ independently chosen baselines replays the traces and validates their majority vector. With the amplification constant enlarged it accepts more than two thirds of all lists. An algorithm for dense-predicate search returns an accepting list directly. In the other direction let $D$ be any such dense predicate on $m\ge1$ bits. Use a parity program with promised vector $(1,0,\ldots,0)$ and its trivial efficient unique verifier. Compute $s_1$ by a branch returning the constant zero or one, so its frozen output gradient is zero. XOR in the single null expression $(s_1^2+s_1)D(s)$. Its predicted first coefficient at baseline $b$ is exactly $D(b)$ and all other predictions are zero. The time is the cost of $D$ plus $O(m)$, and its coordinate marginals meet the promise. A successful explicit list must contain an accepting input to $D$, which can be located by replaying $D$ on the listed strings. This equivalence concerns arbitrary supplied parity programs and the actual return of baselines. A coefficient-only algorithm can immediately answer the particular lower-direction example from its verifier. The proof establishes no reduction from dense search to arbitrary coefficient-only output or to the particular parity program compiled from a fixed SAT solver.

I do not have a proof of positive coordinatewise bias or a small spectral norm for the parity programs produced by an arbitrary linear SAT solver. Hence these gains do not settle SAT's linear-time lower-bound problem or the unrestricted decision-to-search question. The small-bias construction and majority amplification are classical. Priority for this trace-based use is unestablished.
