# Finding a valid forecast certificate from a distribution on matrix layers

On the original learned forecast program, a valid fixed-width cycle would refute the proposed SAT clock. For growing widths it is important to distinguish the amount of nondeterministic information from the deterministic work to find a particular certificate. Here are two sufficient conditions for the latter search on the physical folded timestamp test. Neither condition has been established on the original SAT program. [Certificate definition](periodic-linear-prediction-on-dyadic-forecast-widths.md) · [Known exhaustive search and actual clock files](witness-search-on-the-forecast-clock-files.md)

Use $h$ layers of width $w$, fixed boundary vectors, and at each layer independently draw one letter uniformly from its public matrix alphabet. Boundary vectors can also be chosen in two separate dummy layers. Write $E(s)$ for the endpoint pin of schedule $s$ and $d(s)$ for the number of violated pairs (local test, timestamp). A valid schedule has $E(s)=1$ and $d(s)=0$. In the physical format there are a constant number of tests, each a pointwise product of at most three width-$O(w+1)$ binary linear representations, including the fixed arithmetic masks. Each timestamp column has six bits.

**First-moment search.** If $\sum_s E(s)(1-d(s))>0$, one can find a valid schedule in $2^{O(w+1)}\operatorname{poly}(h,w)$ work for independent uniform raw matrix entries. With an explicitly listed alphabet also charge polynomial time in its size and the cost of computing its matrices. To compute the first term count endpoint-hitting schedules. For each separate defect test count pairs of one schedule and one timestamp for which the endpoint hits and every factor of that test outputs one. Advance the endpoint on its fixed timestamp and advance the other factors on the same freely chosen timestamp column. All see the same schedule choice. Each tuple of binary factor rows takes $O(w+1)$ bits. Count histories reaching a state as integers, retaining multiplicity when several paths merge. Subtract the pair counts from the endpoint count.

For the complete raw alphabet, direct iteration over $2^{O(w^2)}$ letters would lose the stated exponent. For a fixed tuple of incoming rows and a fixed timestamp column the tuple of outgoing factor rows is affine over $\mathbb F_2$ in the shared raw entries of this layer. This uses the factors separately. Shifts, finite-state masks and direct sums preserve the entrywise affine dependence. Compute a basis of the image after fixing any already prescribed entries. Each of the at most $2^{O(w+1)}$ distinct images has the same known number of preimages. Integer counters require $O(h(w+1)^2)$ bits. Now fix successive matrix entries to keep the signed sum positive on the remaining choices. At the final schedule this forces a valid endpoint. A constant-factor average number of defects above one gives no such certificate just from the signed sum.

**Density search.** Suppose at least a fraction $\delta>0$ of the raw schedules are valid. There is a deterministic search of cost

$$
2^{O((w+1)^3\log((h+2)/\delta))}\operatorname{poly}(h,w).
$$

For each test and fixed schedule let $f_s(t)$ be its binary defect bit. Form the multilinear polynomial

$$
P_s(X)=\sum_t f_s(t)\prod_{j,\ell:\ t_{j,\ell}=1}X_{j,\ell}
$$

over a binary extension field. Distinct timestamps use distinct monomials, and the degree is at most $6h$. For random independent field elements in a field of size $Q>12(h+1)/\delta$, an invalid schedule with a hitting endpoint has every test fingerprint zero with probability at most $6h/Q<\delta$. One nonzero test suffices without any independence assumption across tests. For a valid schedule this acceptance probability is exactly one. Take $Q$ the next power of two beyond the threshold. Searching an irreducible polynomial of degree $\log_2 Q$ by enumeration costs $Q^{O(1)}$, within the stated work. [Schwartz on polynomial identity testing](https://doi.org/10.1145/322217.322225)

Compute the joint acceptance count over remaining schedule choices and all field-point choices exactly. For a fixed layer and its six field elements the summed transition of a test is the sum over 64 timestamp columns of its ordinary tensor-product transition multiplied by the corresponding squarefree monomial. A test therefore needs one aggregate row of $O((w+1)^3)$ field elements. Store the tuple of such rows for all tests and the exact binary endpoint row. Advance the integer path counts over all raw schedule letters and the $Q^6$ possible field tuples. The $2^{O(w^2)}$ raw letters fit this bound by direct iteration. No affine-image shortcut on a tensor-product row is assumed. The number of states is $2^{O((w+1)^3\log Q)}$, and the path counts take $O(h(w^2+\log Q+1))$ bits.

Initially the joint acceptance probability is at least $\delta$. Fix schedule bits successively without decreasing the conditional probability, still averaging over the field points. At the end every invalid schedule has probability strictly below this maintained value. Validate the resulting schedule exactly. A dyadic lower estimate for $\delta$ suffices, and estimates can be dovetailed with exact final checking.

On the witness-search-normalized header the first-moment condition with a cheap alphabet cannot hold infinitely often at $w=o(h)$ because its deterministic search would fit the known witness-generation deadline. For the raw density condition, any nonzero valid density there must satisfy $(w+1)^3\log((h+2)/\delta)=\Omega(h)$ on the relevant large widths. To handle unknown widths or densities through a single fixed generator, try all width and dyadic-exponent pairs with product below a sufficiently small multiple of $h$, aborting at the common work bound. Inverse-polynomial density in particular needs $w=\Omega((h/\log(h+2))^{1/3})$. These restrictions apply to that modified program. Density here concerns choices of certificate matrices. The density of complete Boolean assignments to the final physical SAT file is a different quantity. Hirsch gives a deterministic linear-time search for fixed-width CNFs with constant density of full satisfying assignments. [Normalized header and subdeadline witness replay](witness-search-on-the-forecast-clock-files.md) · [Hirsch](https://edwardahirsch.github.io/edwardahirsch/papers/ljigpl98.pdf)

The finite audit tests the first moment, shared choices and actual reconstruction on a small recurrence, including the affine image counting step. At four layers there were 12 endpoint hits and five defects among those hits. In a control, eight of sixteen schedules were valid but the endpoint-hitting schedules had 72 defects in total. The signed test correctly abstained. At three layers in the dense control, four of eight schedules were valid and there were 20 endpoint defects. Over $\mathbb F_8$ every valid schedule passed all 512 field points, and every invalid schedule passed exactly 64. The fingerprint dynamic program matched all 4,096 schedule–point pairs at every one of the 15 schedule prefixes and recovered a schedule with zero defects. The general resource bounds are manuscript arguments. [Exact moment, fingerprint and recurrence audit](../audits/forecast_defect_moment_search.py)
