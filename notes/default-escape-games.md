# Adaptive departures from a predicted Boolean proof

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A single circuit can predict a choice at every OR gate of a computation, with adaptive exceptions chosen separately on each play. I tested whether this permits bounded-space verification of long computations whose full proofs have high pathwidth. It gives a conditional density theorem for the actual first-hole construction. I also constructed uniform true circuits requiring exponentially many exceptions against every predictor with an $n$-bit encoding.

## Checking every possible challenge with a bounded stack

Use the uniformly indexed circuit for the statement that $U(p,n)$ halts with output $z$ within $B=2^{20n}$ steps. It has $N=2^{O(n)}$ gates. Replace each bounded-fanin gate by a constant-size AND/OR/NOT circuit and use two polarities per gate to move negations to the inputs. Gate names still have $O(n)$ bits and children are computable in polynomial time in $n$. A defender selects one child at an OR, and a challenger selects one at an AND. At a terminal the defender wins exactly when its bit is one. [Uniform computation circuit](forcing-pebble-layouts.md)

An explicit Boolean circuit $D$ takes a gate name and a remaining budget $i$ and predicts a default port. Define $W_i(g)$ by giving the defender at most $i$ deviations on each play. The adversary chooses AND ports without restriction. At an OR with default child $a$ and other child $b$, $W_i(g)$ is $W_i(a)$ or, when $i>0$, $W_{i-1}(b)$. At AND both child values must hold. Terminals retain their input bit. Induction on gate rank identifies this recursion with existence of a winning strategy. Following that strategy in the original game proves the actual root true.

There is a nondeterministic test for the **complement**. At an AND, guess a child to refute. At an OR with positive budget, save the default child and current budget, recursively refute the other child with budget one lower, then restore the default child and continue in the current frame. At budget zero just continue to the predicted child. A zero terminal returns successfully, and a one terminal rejects the whole branch. The two checks at an OR require successive successful subruns. Thus the machine accepts exactly when the defender lacks a budgeted winning strategy. Each recursive stack push consumes one deviation, even when the current path crosses exponentially many gates. The scan through default and AND edges needs one frame. [Finite game identities in Lean](../lean/DefaultEscapes.lean)

If there are at most $H$ OR visits on any root-to-terminal path, every completed refutation has at most

```
V(H,r) = sum_{j=0}^{min(H,r)} binom(H,j)
```

terminal occurrences. In a refutation, AND nodes have one selected child and every OR node has its default child and at most one other child. Record one bit for each visited OR, writing one for the other port. The root-to-leaf codes are prefix-free, since any two different leaves must diverge at some OR before either code ends. Zero padding to length $H$ is injective and all words have weight at most $r$. The prefix tree contains at most $(N+1)V(H,r)$ gate occurrences because every such occurrence lies on a leaf path of length at most $N$. A trial ending in early rejection also fits the bound by completing its finite search tree arbitrarily with all required children and ignoring the terminal bits. The discrepancy count in binary search trees is classical. [Harvey and Ginsberg, Limited Discrepancy Search](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/1995/80)

On a multitape machine, for $r=\mathrm{poly}(n)$, size-$s$ encoded $D$ can be retained and evaluated by scanning its topologically ordered gate records with a sequential table of their truth values. This uses $O(s+\mathrm{poly}(n))$ space and $\mathrm{poly}(s,n)$ time per visit. Total branch time is at most $(N+1)V(H,r)\mathrm{poly}(s,n)$, with $O(s+\mathrm{poly}(n))$ space. Without further information use $H=N$.

## A consequence of a linear SAT program

Fix rational $0<\sigma<5$ and take $s=2^{\lfloor\sigma n\rfloor}$ bits. For all sufficiently small fixed rational $c>0$, put $r=\lfloor cn/\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil\rfloor$. The trivial $H=N$ gives $\log_2 V(H,r)=O(rn)=O(cn^2/\log(n+2))$. Assume a deterministic unrestricted-space logarithmic-word RAM algorithm solves explicit SAT in $O(m)$ time on $m$ encoded bits. The bounded nondeterministic reachability simulation, applied with explicit input $D$, decides the refutation predicate or its complement in time $2^{\sigma n+O(\sqrt c)n+o(n)}$. Guess $p$ and $D$ in one outer existential block to decide whether a given $z$ has a budgeted winning proof. That extra block costs only a polylogarithmic factor via a transcript for the deterministic subcomputation. Choosing $c$ sufficiently small gives an exponent strictly below five. [Bounded reachability and verification of RAM transcripts](linear-quantifiers.md)

For $d=n$ or $2(n-1)$, let $w$ be the least $d$-bit word omitted by every $|p|<d$ at clock $B$. The predicate just described excludes $w$. Under linear SAT the omission-rank decoder gives a constant $C$, independent of $n,K$, such that for all large $n$, in every interval $[w-K,w)$ with $0\le K\le w$, at least $(K+1)/C-1$ genuine timely outputs have the following property. **Every** timely short program for the output, with **every** default predictor of encoded size at most $s$, has the property that every winning strategy uses more than $r$ deviations on at least one adversarial challenge. The challenge may depend on the strategy. To check clocks, representing the guessed membership table and ranking its $2^d$ positions costs $2^{2d+o(n)}$ in addition to checking one universally selected entry. Thus the RAM exponent stays below five and the two quadratic tape and universality simulations fit $B$. [Omission ranks](predecessor-batches.md)

The distinction between long and wide trees can matter. The same argument allows polynomial $r,H$ with $\log_2 V(H,r)\le \epsilon n^2/\log_2(n+2)$ for sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$. Require that the path bound $H$ hold for the particular circuit instance before using a shorter timeout. A guessed path exceeding $H$ can be checked in $N\,\mathrm{poly}(n)$ time and $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ space. For example when $H=r\le \epsilon n^2/\log_2(n+2)$, every defender move can be arbitrary, and the budget still permits verification.

One can instead impose a hard timeout on searching for a refutation and accept $z$ if some $p,D$ has **no** refutation within that timeout. Let the visit cap be $2^{\lfloor\epsilon n^2/\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil\rfloor}$, keep $r$ polynomial, and use the same clock calculation. On each length where *every* $p,D$ at the actual $w$ admits a refutation below the cap, the same omission density applies. This test can include falsely claimed outputs elsewhere. The two premises that would contradict linear SAT are cheap refutations at $w$ and absence of such refutations for some $p,D$ at all but a vanishing fraction of an unbounded interval preceding $w$. I have no evidence establishing both premises for the actual universal machine.

## A succinct true circuit forcing exponentially many deviations

There is a polynomial-time locally indexed family of fan-in-two AND/OR circuits of size $2^{O(n)}$, with true roots, for which every predictor with an $n$-bit encoding fails with budget $r=\lfloor 2^n/4\rfloor$ for all sufficiently large $n$. Fix an encoding with exactly $2^n$ potential predictor strings, padding shorter valid descriptions to length $n$ or, for unrestricted variable-length descriptions, enumerate fewer than $2^{n+1}$ instead. Interpret invalid descriptions as a constant predictor.

Let $h=2^n$. The game has a chain of $h$ OR gates. Taking port $b$ at stage $j$ leads to an AND of the next stage and a Boolean computation checking $b=u_j$. After the last stage the continuation is true. The two AND ports let the challenger either inspect the claimed bit or postpone inspection. At every stage on the path postponing all inspections, a winning defender must predict the correct $u_j$, since the challenger could inspect it immediately. The labels of all chain gates are fixed before the bits $u_j$ are constructed.

Construct the sequence in $2^{O(n)}$ deterministic tape time. Maintain a positive integer weight, initially one, and a remaining-budget counter for each predictor. At stage $j$ call every predictor at the public gate label with its own current counter. Choose $u_j$ to be the less heavily predicted bit, breaking a tie deterministically. Double the weight of each correct predictor and subtract one (saturating at zero) from each incorrect predictor's counter. Initially each counter is $r$. Even after the counter saturates we include the predictor in every round. Each round multiplies total weight by at most $3/2$, since the doubled weight was at most half the total. Therefore a predictor correct on $a$ rounds has $2^a\le 2^{n+1}(3/2)^h$. Its errors are at least $(1-\log_2(3/2))h-(n+1)>r$ for large $n$. Until its first excess error its counter is precisely the actual budget on the continuation path. At that stage the default move is false under inspection and the true move is disallowed. [Littlestone and Warmuth's weighted-majority method](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~lreyzin/papers/littlestone94.pdf)

There are at most $2^{n+1}$ weights of at most $h+1$ bits, $h$ rounds, and each predictor call costs polynomial time in $n$ on a fixed multitape machine. Scanning the records to call the predictors, sum weights and apply updates takes $2^{O(n)}$ time in total. The same time suffices to compute any requested $u_j$ from $n,j$. Implement each check by the signed, locally indexed time-by-cell computation circuit of that fixed algorithm and wire its root to the appropriate stage. Multiplying by $2h$ copies preserves the $2^{O(n)}$ size. Gate names carry a tag for a chain vertex or for a tableau location and stage, so their child function never needs to calculate $u_j$. This resolves the dependency between the predictor's gate input and the construction of the challenge bit. With unrestricted choices the true move is available at every stage and every requested true check has a winning strategy, so the root value is one.

The same diagonalization works for predictors with sequential memory and a much larger computation budget. Fix a constant $a>0$ and enumerate binary descriptions of length at most $n$ of deterministic multitape predictors. Give each predictor the continuation play so far, its remaining budget and its private state, and allow up to $2^{an}$ steps per round. Use a specified default answer and reset rule on timeout. Simulate every predictor on the same evolving forced sequence, feeding it the new outcome after choosing each bit. Storing all private states after $h$ rounds and simulating all moves still costs $2^{O_a(n)}$ native time, even with polynomial overhead for universal simulation and sequential scanning. The weight calculation uses only the observed current predictions and hence forces the same error bound. This version includes predictors that compute fresh advice from earlier play. The game family may depend on $a$.

This example can be attached by AND to any value circuit without changing its represented function. Predictors still have fixed chain labels under a tagged relabeling, which can be incorporated directly into the weighted construction. A semantic proof that all stages are true can use induction and the identity $(q\wedge x)\vee(\neg q\wedge x)=x$. The counterexample concerns gate-by-gate strategies. Its predictor length is $n$, well below the $2^{\sigma n}$ allowance of the first-hole criterion. Extending the obstruction to that larger advice regime or finding a formula-specific strategy on the actual predecessors remains open. I have not established historical novelty for the combined construction.

## What an exponential predictor obstruction would establish

For infinitely many lengths, the following three existence statements are equivalent up to the positive constants in the exponents. Here $\mathsf E=\mathrm{DTIME}(2^{O(n)})$, gates have bounded fan-in, and all game families have polynomial-time local indexing and $2^{O(n)}$ gates at parameter $n$.

1. A language in $\mathsf E$ requires at least $2^{\alpha n}$ gates on infinitely many input lengths for some $\alpha>0$.
2. A family of true games has no zero-deviation winning default predictor of encoded length at most $2^{\beta n}$ on infinitely many parameter lengths for some $\beta>0$.
3. A family of true games admits no strategy winning with budget $\lfloor 2^{\gamma n}\rfloor$ relative to any default predictor of encoded length at most $2^{\gamma n}$ on infinitely many parameter lengths for some $\gamma>0$.

The indexed gate value gives **2 implies 1**. Choose a constant integer $A$ large enough to encode each gate name at parameter $n$ in $m=An$ bits. Define a language on strings of these lengths by evaluating the named gate, with value zero on invalid names and on other lengths. Iterating once over topologically numbered gates, even scanning a tape table to look up both children, takes $2^{O(m)}$ time. For another locally indexed acyclic numbering, an explicit topological sort takes polynomial time in the expanded graph size. A length-$m$ circuit for this language yields a predictor that looks up the first child's value at every OR and selects it when true. Local indexing costs $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ gates and the predictor ignores its budget. Its choices maintain truth throughout a play from any true root. An ordinary $t$-gate circuit can be encoded in $O((t+\mathrm{poly}(n))\log(t+n+2))$ bits after adding the local indexing. Thus at the hard parameters in 2 the indexed gate language requires at least $2^{\alpha m}$ gates, for example with $\alpha=\beta/(2A)$ and all large $n$.

For **1 implies 3**, replace the weighted bit sequence in the chain construction by the $h=2^n$ truth-table bits of the hard language on length $n$. Evaluating a specified bit still takes $2^{O(n)}$ time, giving the required uniformly indexed games. Fix a length where the gate lower bound in 1 holds. Suppose a size-$s$ predictor has a winning strategy of budget $r$. The challenger's always-continue play forces the successive OR choices to equal all $h$ truth-table bits. List the positions where those choices disagree with the predictor. There are at most $r$ such positions. At position $j$, the current budget is exactly $r$ minus the number of listed positions strictly less than $j$. A Boolean circuit with the list hardwired compares $j$ against each listed position, counts the strict inequalities with binary adders, and tests equality against any list entry. It evaluates the predictor once on the chain label for $j$ and the computed budget, then flips its prediction exactly when equality holds. It computes the entire hard language slice using $O((s+r+1)\mathrm{poly}(n))$ gates, including indices and comparisons. Taking $s,r\le 2^{\alpha n/3}$ contradicts 1 for large hard $n$. Therefore 3 holds with $\gamma=\alpha/3$. Finally **3 implies 2** with the same predictor and exponent. A strategy with virtual budget zero can be followed at any larger actual budget. Whenever the actual budget is positive a mismatch costs one affordable deviation. When it reaches zero the predictor again makes exactly its original zero-budget choices. More generally, to follow a strategy at a smaller virtual budget, maintain actual budget at least virtual budget. At strict inequality any single move preserves the invariant, and at equality the default choices coincide. Lean also checks one-step monotonicity for budget-dependent predictions.

The implication from gate values applies to all gates of the chosen game, so a claim that one particular game has no exponential-size predictor already yields an unrestricted Boolean circuit lower bound for its indexed-value language. The reverse direction relies only on exact correction at the listed indices and works for worst-case hard truth tables. Exponential worst-case circuit hardness for $\mathsf E$ is the hypothesis used in classical hardness-versus-randomness results when it holds at every sufficiently large length. The equivalence above preserves the infinitely-often quantifier. [Impagliazzo and Wigderson](https://doi.org/10.1145/258533.258590)

Lean checks game soundness, the exact refutation recursion, the one-round integer potential inequality, and exact recovery of a target bit from its error indicator and prefix error count. The uniform constructions, time simulations, correction circuit and asymptotic equivalence are manuscript proofs.
