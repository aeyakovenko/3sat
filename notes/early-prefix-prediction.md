# What an early producer must do inside a gap of exact savings

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** I tried to build a fast universal compiler that suppresses near-deadline improvements by supplying early descriptions of the same words. The following unconditional test discards a class of such early descriptions. It applies to the actual native source and includes predictors that access arbitrary linear sketches of the whole program. If the proposed compiler leaves a sufficiently long gap of nonrandom columns, *genuine early programs* must evade the test on a family of words of random total weight.

Fix an efficient native prefix machine, $T(m)=2^{3m}$, $t(m)=\max(1,\lfloor T(m)/m^a\rfloor)$, and $E_{a,d}$ as in the clocked-complexity experiment. Fix an integer $b\ge1$. A predictor gets the output length $m$, the phase $r$, the parameters $D,k,\kappa,b$, the exact input-name length $\ell$, and parity-query access to its $\ell$ bits over $\mathbb F_2$. An external timer enforces its deadline. Its prediction is one string of a specified length $\kappa$. Use a decidable prefix-free language for predictor descriptions that includes the type tag. Count every description $P$ of at most $D-3$ bits. Either of two types can qualify.

- A bounded-rank predictor has $t(m)$ steps and may receive at most $s=\lfloor b\log_2 m\rfloor$ independent answers. Additional dependent queries are allowed. Bit probes and a fixed linear sketch followed by arbitrary bounded computation are examples.
- A predictor may use a layered branching program of width at most $m^b$ and at most $t(m)$ layers. Given the public data, time layer and state label, its local transition generator has $m^b$ steps to supply a terminal label, a deterministic transition or a parity form and its two successors. Arbitrarily many independent bits can be accessed. An actual successful path using $q$ tests of joint rank $u$ qualifies when $|P|+q-u\le D-3$. In particular an ordinary read-once branching program may process all name bits, in a data-dependent order, through its bounded state. The criterion also permits some linear dependencies and repeated reads.

Choose $a_0(b)$ sufficiently large in the proof below. Fix $a\ge a_0(b)$.

There are constants $H,c>0$ independent of $D$ and $a\ge a_0(b)$ such that for each $D\ge4$, one can specify distinct outputs, with positive computable word weights $2^{D-m}$ at lengths $m=2^r$, satisfying

$$
m-D-H\le K_T(y)<m-D .
$$

On these words, **no** actual early name shorter than $m-D$ has its $\kappa$-bit output prefix correctly predicted by any allowed predictor. The derivative of their total weight is at least $c2^{-K(D)}$, with the fixed ordinary halting probability as denominator. The assertion uses a shortest ordinary description of $D$; uniform computation of that description is unnecessary. The question of whether qualifying early names exist is decided separately, by bounded simulation.

In particular, suppose the actual columns $E_{a,d}$ for $D<d\le D+H$ all have derivative zero. Among the displayed words, those that **do** have a name shorter than $m-D$ finishing by $t(m)$ still have derivative at least $c2^{-K(D)}$ in their total weight. Thus at every such gap, the early replacements must cover a random subseries on which every early producer of the required length evades the entire predictor family. An unrestricted program may inspect its full payload; the argument gives no upper bound on that subseries. [Two-clock columns, derivatives and conditional Kraft accounting](exact-saving-blocks.md)

## Counting prefix predictions without running every possible name

At one length fix $m,D,\kappa$. For every $\ell<m-D$, take all $2^\ell$ inputs of exactly that length. For each predictor of the first type compute its histogram of output prefixes on this domain. Explore its decision tree only when a new linearly independent form is queried. A consistent leaf fixing $j$ independent parity values contains exactly $2^{\ell-j}$ inputs. Gaussian elimination determines whether a new form is dependent and supplies its unique forced value in that case. Abort paths that time out, exceed the rank budget or fail to emit a valid prediction. This gives exact integer histograms in $2^s(t(m)+1)\mathrm{poly}(m)$ time per predictor and name length. On this bounded computation, the virtual machine has $O(\log(t+m))=O(m)$-bit words. A query to an $\ell$-bit form and its elimination incur only polynomial-in-$m$ additional work. One implementation replays all $2^s$ padded answer sequences from scratch. Each recorded output then receives weight $2^{\ell-s}$ per replay: a leaf reached after $j$ independent answers occurs $2^{s-j}$ times, giving its exact integer fiber size. This replay bound avoids copying an entire configuration at a branch. Lean checks the leaf-mass upper bound, the power-of-two likelihood identity, and the light-bin counting inequality. [Lean proofs](../lean/PrefixPrediction.lean)

For the second type, evaluate a different histogram. Give an independent fair answer to every parity test, ignoring its form. Propagate these probabilities over the width-$m^b$ program, merging states in the same time layer with the same number of tests so far. Discard paths after $\ell+D$ tests. All masses have dyadic denominator at most $2^{\ell+D}$. Invalid local descriptions abstain. The calculation takes $t(m)m^{2b+O(1)}$ steps, including local transition generation and exact counters. Multiply the resulting distribution by $2^\ell$ to get its histogram. On any actual consistent path with $q$ tests of joint rank $u$, the path probability of a uniform true name is $2^{-u}$, because the answers define an affine subspace. Its probability under this independent-answer calculation is $2^{-q}$. Thus the number of true names on qualifying paths predicting any specified prefix is at most $2^{D-3-|P|}$ times the corresponding histogram entry. The discarded paths cannot qualify since $u\le\ell$. This proof uses no rank computation in the width-$m^b$ state space.

Weight each histogram of either type by $2^{D-3-|P|}$. This factor is at least one for the first type and covers the likelihood ratio for the second. By Kraft's inequality the sum of these factors is at most $2^{D-3}$. The total over all bins and all name lengths is therefore at most $2^{D-3}\sum_{\ell<m-D}2^\ell<2^{m-3}$. Choose a $\kappa$-bit bin $\rho$ of minimum total. Fewer than $2^{m-\kappa-3}$ pairs of a predictor and an input can correctly predict $\rho$ on qualifying paths. In the cohort of all $2^{m-\kappa}$ words $\rho z$, at most an eighth can have a correctly predicted early name shorter than $m-D$. For each covered word choose one such name and predictor. Distinct words use distinct names since the native producer is deterministic. This counts all program lengths and does not assume that a payload can be parsed as a header and a suffix.

The same $\rho$ gives the same eighth bound if each predictor receives a bijectively recoded name at each input length. This finite count holds for *any fixed family* of such bijections, even if they are nonlinear or noncomputable, because each histogram is invariant under permutations of its domain. The set of covered words may depend on the family. A recoding with fibers of size at most $B$ multiplies the covered fraction bound by $B$. For the assertion about computable subseries take the identity recoding, or fix a uniformly computable family beforehand.

## Producing a large cohort using the actual clocks

Use fixed $D$, a shortest ordinary program $q$ for it, and $k=|q|$. The grant decoder reads $q$ and a conditional program $p$ with data $(D,k)$. For a halting $p$ it computes the phase index $r=r(p,u)\ge6+3(|p|+u+1)$ from the program and runtime $u$. The index is injective and efficiently computed in $\mathrm{poly}(r)$ time. The phase receives weight $w_r=2^{-|p|}$; empty phases have zero. Put

$$
m=2^r,\qquad \kappa=D+k+|p|+e .
$$

The constant parameter $e$ pays for its own self-delimiting encoding and the fixed native decoder header. Compute the minimizing prefix above, consume exactly $m-\kappa$ raw bits, and emit $\rho$ followed by these bits. At each sufficiently late nonempty phase, $\kappa\le r$ and $2^D\le m$ for this fixed $D$. The whole histogram computation and the construction of the output take $(t(m)+1)m^{C_b}$ steps at large $m$, with a fixed exponent $C_b$ including universal interpretation and clock arithmetic. One chooses $a_0(b)>C_b$. For uniform constants, the decoder always does this calculation with the baseline $a=a_0(b)$. Clock arithmetic treats that exponent as numeric data, so the implementation overhead exponent is fixed before choosing $a_0$. Any predictor halting by the earlier deadline for a larger $a$ also halts by this baseline deadline with the same output. The cohort and its estimate therefore work for every $a\ge a_0$. Every word in this cohort has a timely name shorter than $m-D$. Their total weight in units of $2^{D-m}$ is exactly $2^{-k-e}w_r$.

To discard words with $K_T(y)\le m-D-h$, bound their weight by $B_h(D,r)=2^{D-m}\#\{y:|y|=2^r,\ K_T(y)\le m-D-h\}$. This upper bound uses *all* words at the chosen length and is computable independently of the shortest header for $D$. By summing geometric contributions word by word, $\sum_{D,r} B_h(D,r)\le 2^{1-h}$. The marginal and conditional Kraft request argument in the linked note gives a constant $C_1$ independent of $D,h$ with

$$
\sum_{r>R}B_h(D,r)\le C_1 2^{-k}(h+1)^2 2^{-h}\sum_{r>R}w_r.
$$

Choose $h$ so that $C_1(h+1)^22^{-h}<2^{-e-2}$, and put $H=h-1$. The eighth lost to possible predictions and the amount lost to these overly short timely names still leave a constant multiple of $2^{-k}$ times the phase tail. The exact retained sets are decidable, for fixed $D,k$, by bounded simulation, inspecting the actual names and predictions. Dividing the synchronized inequality by the ordinary halting-probability tail gives the claimed derivative bound, since the conditional grant machine uniformly simulates the ordinary one. [Weighted tail comparison and its Kraft machine](exact-saving-blocks.md)

Finally, a retained word with no qualifying early name satisfies $K_t(y)\ge m-D$ and $K_T(y)<m-D$. Its actual increment is at least $2^{D-m}$ in one of the stated columns. Membership is decidable by its length and those bounded simulations, so the sum of the columns minus these particular credits is left-c.e. Positivity of the derivative makes the credited subseries have derivative zero when all those columns have derivative zero. Removing it from the retained family proves the assertion about actual early programs.

The minimum-prefix calculation is feasible because the histogram of a bounded-rank affine decision tree has an exact small enumeration. Extending this calculation to the output-prefix fibers of arbitrary unrestricted early programs is the outstanding step. Related work studies when an efficient coding or information-symmetry theorem can supply a short description for a frequent output of a sampler. The present finite calculation only asks for a light prefix and pays explicitly for the predictor class.
