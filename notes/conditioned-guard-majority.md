# Reading a majority through one conditioned guard query

Consider the ordinary guarded SAT formulas with a Reed–Solomon guard and an empirical proof test for a fixed original monitor. Condition just the guard on one legal irreducible polynomial describing its field. This can be done in the formula generator by hardwiring the presentation in the guard verifier. Keep the original empirical disjunct, its verifier, and the original monitor program as constants. The new query is a related ordinary SAT instance with the same asymptotic encoded length. Fix this field when sampling the original codeword as well. [Guard and moment estimates](guarded-code-rejections.md)

There is a fixed, dimension-dependent transformation of raw inputs for which this single SAT query reads the majority of a prescribed list of linearly many bits. Its error on a uniform codeword paired with a uniform independent table is exponentially small. One transformed symbol needs at most two original symbol reads when furnished with an independently preprocessed public mask. In contrast, any algorithm reading $o(d)$ original symbols, given the mask and no input-dependent advice, has $o(1)$ advantage over guessing the majority. This allows a different class of native queries from the conditional overwrites and fixed XOR translations considered earlier. [Completion queries](conditional-completion-rejections.md)

## A coordinate subcube with exact distances

Write $C\subseteq\{0,1\}^L$ for the binary image of the fixed-field code, $L=bd$ and $R=\lfloor L/16\rfloor$. It is a binary linear code with at most $2^{L/4}$ words. Since $1/4+H_2(1/8)<1$, the union of radius-$2R$ Hamming balls about these words misses some point for every sufficiently large $L$. Subtract a nearest codeword from such a point to obtain $w$ with $\operatorname{dist}(w,C)=|w|\ge 2R+1$. Every binary $v$ supported on the support of $w$ satisfies

$$\operatorname{dist}(v,C)=|v|.$$

Indeed, for each $c\in C$, the triangle inequality gives $|w|\le \operatorname{dist}(w,c)\le |w|-|v|+\operatorname{dist}(v,c)$. The zero codeword attains the resulting lower bound. In coding terminology $w$ and each of these $v$ are coset leaders. This downward-closure property is classical. [Borges-Quintana, Borges-Trenard, Márquez-Corbella and Martínez-Moro, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5568)

Choose and fix any $2R+1$ positions $S$ in the support of $w$. On an original pair $(X,Y)$ define $e(Y)$ by retaining the bits of $Y$ at $S$ and setting the rest to zero. Feed the conditioned instance the two arrays

$$U=Y,\qquad V=Y\oplus X\oplus e(Y).$$

The fixed mask uses at most $L$ public bits and can be consulted one symbol at a time. On every $X\in C$ the guard on $U\oplus V$ is true precisely when $|e(Y)|\le R$, since translation by $X$ preserves distance to $C$. Thus the guard detects whether the majority of the selected bits is zero.

## Retaining the original empirical disjunct

For $X$ uniform in $C$ and $Y$ uniform independent, the joint array $(U,V)$ is exactly $u$-wise uniform in symbols, where $u=\lfloor d/4\rfloor$. For any specified at most $u$ positions of $V$, conditional on the entire $Y$ their joint values are independent and uniform by interpolation of $X$. The selected $U$ coordinates are already independent uniform. This covers selections observing both partners at one location. Raw bits at the input pins of the conditional query are given by these actual arrays.

For each fixed header and challenge shift, the $j$-th moment of the empirical score therefore equals its moment on the old reference law as long as $j(h+1)\le u$. Here $h$ is the fixed raw-probe cap of a row of the original empirical verifier. On the reference law the fresh score is at most $1/4$, and the ordinal-blind moment calculation bounds the empirical $L^j$ norm by $3/8$ at an even $j=\Theta(d/(h+1))$. The original union bound over headers and shifts makes the chance that this empirical disjunct is true at most $2^{-\Omega(d/(h+1))}$. It uses its original self-referential language and its original far reference law throughout. On the majority-zero event the guard guarantees satisfiability deterministically. On the remaining event the conditioned formula is unsatisfiable except with the displayed absolute error probability. Pinning the branch selector as well, to require the fixed-field guard, makes the majority query exact on every $X\in C$ and every $Y$.

To bound the direct cost of predicting this bit, expose an adaptive algorithm's raw reads on the original center. Its observations of $X$ give no independent information about the unseen $Y$ after conditioning on its transcript. Distinct $Y$ symbols disclose at most $t=qb$ selected fair bits in $q$ raw probes. For $g=2R+1$ total selected bits and $t<g/2$, the absolute bias conditional on the transcript is $O((|A|+1)/\sqrt{g-t})$, where $A$ is the signed sum of the exposed selected bits. This follows by bounding each point mass of the remaining symmetric binomial by $O(1/\sqrt{g-t})$. The successive newly exposed signs are centered independent fair coins conditional on prior reads, so $\mathbb E[A^2]\le t$ even for adaptive stopping. The overall advantage is $O((\sqrt{qb}+1)/\sqrt L)$; averaging also handles algorithmic randomness. The same bound up to the exponential error applies to predicting the conditioned native verdict.

More generally, choose any odd $1\le g\le 2R+1$. Reserve $R-(g-1)/2$ always-one slots and $g$ variable slots in the same coset leader support. The guard now tests the majority of the $g$ variable bits. Divide the $Y$ bits into $B=\lfloor L/g\rfloor$ disjoint groups of size $g$. One transformed decision per group retrieves $B$ independent fair majority bits, all correctly except with probability $B2^{-\Omega(d/(h+1))}$. A local substitution that rewires these bits into the prescribed slots may need $O(b)$ source-symbol reads per output symbol. The same raw-query estimate for an individual group uses $g$ in place of $L$. Even with unlimited computation, an extractor reading no raw input needs at least $B$ bits in total of input-dependent advice and oracle answers to recover the full vector with probability greater than one half (indeed its success is at most $2^{a+j-B}$ for $a$ fixed-length advice bits and $j$ adaptive binary replies). This counts the information in batched verdicts without assuming independence among the queries.

The same transformation applies to unconditioned native verdicts of a *new fixed-presentation monitor*. At the outset choose the field polynomial by Shoup's deterministic algorithm over $\mathbb F_2$, in $\operatorname{poly}(b)$ time. Use only this field in the guard and then define the monitor to query that ordinary formula. All original distance-from-rejection and marginal arguments for the two laws go through with just this single code. In particular, the old moment proof applies to this monitor's own fresh soundness and empirical test, and the claimed running clock still fits. For this version no guard pinning is needed at query time. Under a fast SAT clock, all the untransformed hard centers outside the original small exceptional probability are timely rejections of this same monitor. [Shoup, deterministic field construction in small characteristic](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

## Random access to the code polynomial's coefficients

The query can also extract information from $X$ itself. Fix any binary linear functional $f$ of the coefficient vector of the input polynomial $P$, where $X$ is the evaluation table of $P$. With the same types of public preprocessing and genuine SAT queries, $O(\log(d+1))$ decisions recover $f(P)$. A preset menu of polynomially many functionals, including every coefficient bit in the field basis, works on the same centers outside an exponentially small exceptional probability. Each requested bit can be recovered independently of all other coefficient values.

Choose the support $D$ of a coset leader of size greater than $L/6$. This stronger choice is possible by the same volume argument since $1/4+H_2(1/6)<1$. Let $J$ be the $u$ field positions having the fewest bits in $D$. They contain at most $(u/d)|D|$ bits of $D$. The remaining target pool $D'$ therefore has more than $L/8$ bits. Interpolating the $u$ field values in $J$ to get $P$ is linear over the field and hence over the binary base. Consequently there are fixed binary linear forms $f_j$ on one $b$-bit field element such that

$$f(P)=\bigoplus_{j\in J} f_j(X_j).$$

For a threshold $0\le k\le u$, reserve one target bit in $D'$ for each donor $j\in J$, with at most three targets in any field position. Indeed truncating each row's available count to three retains at least $(3/b)|D'|>3d/8>u$ slots for large $b$. Place the value $f_j(X_j)$ at this target. Put constant ones at $R-k$ other positions of $D'$ and zeros everywhere else, obtaining $e_k(X)$. There is room since $u\le R$ and $|D'|>2R$ for large $b$. This map uses only bits of the donor rows. Every donor row is unchanged by the map $Z=X\oplus e_k(X)$, so this map on the *whole ambient array* is a bijection, in fact an involution. Each $Z$ symbol uses at most four source symbols even before using any codeword promise.

The raw substitution is $(U,V)=(Y,Y\oplus Z)$. On an independent uniform pair of arrays it is an ambient bijection. On the actual codeword law, the outputs at any $\lfloor u/4\rfloor$ raw positions still agree in distribution with independent uniform symbols: the needed $X$ coordinates number at most four per selected position, and the independent $Y$ remains available throughout. Thus the earlier moment argument works with $j(h+1)\le\lfloor u/4\rfloor$. For example, with $u=\lfloor d/4\rfloor$, the previous choice of an even $j\le d/[256(h+1)]$ still fits. Independently of this probability estimate, on every true codeword the fixed-field guard is true precisely when

$$\sum_{j\in J} f_j(X_j)\le k,$$

where the sum counts ones as an integer. This follows from $\operatorname{dist}(X\oplus e_k(X),C)=|e_k(X)|=R-k+\sum_j f_j(X_j)$. A binary search on $k$ gives the sum and its parity. A union bound over all $u+1$ possible thresholds and the specified menu of linear forms covers adaptive binary search and arbitrary later choices from that menu. This is a per-menu high-probability statement; it does not assert that the empirical disjunct vanishes simultaneously for arbitrary forms chosen from the entire dual space. Pinning the guard selector bypasses this qualification for the conditioned variant.

Some coefficient bits are genuinely nonlocal on the original distribution. For example, conditioning on any adaptively revealed $u-1$ or fewer distinct evaluation values leaves the leading coefficient uniform, since the kernel of those evaluations contains a degree-$(u-1)$ polynomial with leading coefficient one. This includes all queries of $Y$, which is independent. Thus any nonzero binary linear functional of the leading coefficient is exactly fair given this raw transcript. The decisions just described recover this bit using whole native calls. In contrast with the earlier Frobenius substitution for a separate full-field code-membership comparator, this works on arbitrary $d$ distinct evaluation points and can read a lower coefficient without first reconstructing the higher ones. [Earlier comparator](conditional-completion-rejections.md)

Choosing the coset leader and the layouts uses public nonuniform preprocessing. For an individual coefficient functional the lists of donor masks, addresses and target positions use $O(L+ d\log L)$ bits, and a fixed menu can keep a separate directory per functional. A hypothetical linear solver can still spend a full encoded-instance clock on each comparison. These global linear functionals have no proved connection to the possibly nonlinear bits of a proof for the actual solver's rejection. For the unchanged guard permitting every field presentation, distance to the union of codes can shrink after the shift by $X$; the extraction uses fixed-field conditioning or the revised monitor.
