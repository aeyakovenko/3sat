# How often can dense suffix patterns be reused?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** I tried storing a shared dictionary of arbitrary suffix sets and giving each prefix its own translation or field-coordinate change. With $P$ prefixes, a dictionary of $o(P)$ full suffix tables and a short relabeling per prefix would cost $o(N)$ bits. For the linear SAT goal this could describe a large set of hard words. The construction below proves an unconditional obstruction. Covering a fixed positive fraction of the cube with only $o(N)$ easy words requires $\Omega(P)$ different suffix patterns even at a fixed positive Hamming tolerance and after independent projective semilinear relabelings. I also constructed sparse XOR lists that pass this diversity test and still include a fixed positive fraction of easy words. [Target for sparse lists](xor-sumset-advice.md)

Write $q=u+b$, $P=2^u$, $M=2^b$, $N=PM$, where $u,b\to\infty$. Use a fixed, polynomial-time computable split with $(1+u/b)/\sqrt M\to0$. This includes the quadratic and cubic suffix widths $\lfloor q/5\rfloor,\lfloor q/10\rfloor$. A word $p\Vert z$ supplies a prefix $p$ of $u$ bits and a suffix field element $z\in K=\mathbb F_M$ in polynomial basis. Let $S_p$ be the set of suffixes accepted at $p$. Write $E_q$ for the words actually printed and halted on by the stipulated short programs at the fixed deadline. Suppose $\sum_p|S_p|\ge\delta N$ and $|S\cap E_q|=o(N)$ along an unbounded sequence of lengths, for a fixed $\delta>0$.

There exist constants $\eta,c>0$ such that the following holds at all sufficiently large lengths in the sequence. For **any** choice of one permitted permutation $g_p$ per row, there are at least $cP$ rows of density at least $\delta/2$ whose transformed masks $g_p(S_p)$ have pairwise symmetric difference more than $\eta M$. In particular, any set of prototypes approximating all these dense rows within a sufficiently small constant fractional error needs $\Omega(P)$ members. The permutations arise from projective fractional linear transformations and field automorphisms on $K\cup\{\infty\}$. To get a permutation on the $M$ ordinary suffixes, delete $\infty$ from the cycle containing it; this changes at most one finite argument. In particular every binary XOR translation is allowed. The permutations can be different and nonuniform in all prefixes.

## A family of actual easy suffixes

Fix a large constant $r$, put $\rho=2^{-r}$ and let $\pi:K\to\mathbb F_2^r$ select $r$ basis bits. Encode $p$ injectively in $s=\lceil u/b\rceil$ field coefficients $c_1,\ldots,c_s$ by padding. Take $m$ the least integer $1\bmod 8$ at least $\max(17,2s+9)$. Let $\xi$ be the residue of the indeterminate in the polynomial basis. Its minimal polynomial has degree $b$, so its conjugates under the $b$ field automorphisms are all distinct. Form
$$
D_p(w)=w^m+w^{m-2}+\xi w^{m-6}+\sum_{i=1}^s c_i w^{2i-1},
\qquad H_p=\{z:\pi(D_p(z))=0\}.
$$
The additive-character estimate on a polynomial of odd degree gives $|H_p|/M=\rho+O(m/\sqrt M)$ uniformly in $p$. Indeed every nonzero binary character of the projected value equals $(-1)^{\operatorname{Tr}(\lambda D_p(z))}$ for a nonzero $\lambda\in K$. Eventually every row therefore has at most $2^{b-r+1}$ easy suffix candidates. A decoder carries the prefix and a $(b-r+1)$-bit ordinal, reconstructs $q$ from its payload length, constructs the first irreducible polynomial of degree $b$, scans the field and unpacks the ordinal. The scan and irreducibility search cost $M\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$ time. Choose $r$ so its self-delimiting encoding and the fixed header fit with two bits of net saving. Every $p\Vert H_p$ consists of genuine members of $E_q$. Trace codes built from functions with controlled poles have an established history in the construction of sequences with low correlation. [Cochrane and Pinner on rational character sums](https://www.math.ksu.edu/~cochrane/research/stepmix.pdf) · [Low-correlation sequences from function fields](https://www.cse.ust.hk/faculty/cding/JOURNALS/it031.pdf)

For different $p,p'$ and arbitrary permitted permutations $g,g'$, these sets satisfy
$$
|g(H_p)\cap g'(H_{p'})|/M=\rho^2+O(m/\sqrt M). \tag{1}
$$
Expand both zero-fiber indicators in their binary characters. Terms with just one nonzero character have the preceding bound. With two nonzero characters, use invariance of the absolute trace under Frobenius to absorb each semilinear part into its field coefficients (including the trace multiplier, marker and payload). Each expression is now a trace of a polynomial with the indicated conjugate coefficients evaluated at a fractional linear function of the common coordinate. Its only pole on the projective line has order $m$. Distinct poles leave an odd pole in the sum, excluding every rational function of the form $h^2+h+\text{constant}$.

If the poles coincide, choose a fractional linear coordinate $w$ with its pole there. The two arguments then have the form $A_i w+B_i$ with $A_i\ne0$. In the trace polynomial, even powers can be reduced by $c w^{2j}\mapsto c^{1/2}w^j$. No such replacement reaches degrees above $m/2$. At the odd degrees $m,m-2,m-4$, the coefficients in the individual term with trace multiplier $\lambda_i$ are
$$
\lambda_i A_i^m,\qquad \lambda_i A_i^{m-2},\qquad
\lambda_i A_i^{m-4} B_i^2.
$$
Here $\binom{m}{2}$ and $\binom{m}{4}$ are even and $\binom{m-2}{2}$ is odd, as follows from the last three binary digits. If a coefficient differs, a positive odd power survives reduction. If all three agree, division of the first two gives $A_1^2=A_2^2$, so $A_1=A_2$, then $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$ and $B_1=B_2$. Use their common affine argument as coordinate. Different Frobenius choices now differ at the fixed marker of degree $m-6$. For identical Frobenius choices the two distinct prefixes differ at a positive odd payload degree. In both cases the highest surviving degree in the common argument is odd. Thus every nontrivial joint character has a nonconstant reduced phase. The rational-function Weil bound is $O(m\sqrt M)$ since the degree and total pole orders are $O(m)$. Exceptional finite arguments under the projective conventions change only $O(1)$ summands. The Fourier coefficients of each fiber indicator have absolute sum one, giving (1). A finite experiment checked 611,328 reduced-polynomial signatures and 1,048,576 pointwise Frobenius identities. The estimate for all lengths uses the preceding proof. [Finite-field experiment](../audits/projective_row_markers_experiment.py)

## From pair correlations to distinct patterns

For $t$ distinct prefixes after arbitrary permitted relabelings, let $V(z)$ be the average of $1_{g_p(H_p)}(z)-\rho$ over those prefixes. By (1) and the individual densities,
$$
\mathbb E_z V(z)^2\le 1/t+O(m/\sqrt M).
$$
Choose $\eta=\rho\delta/32$. At least a fixed positive fraction of prefixes have $|S_p|\ge\delta M/2$ and $|S_p\cap H_p|\le\eta M$. The first follows from the global density, the second fails on only $o(P)$ rows because $H_p$ is actually easy. Suppose a fixed prototype $D$ is within $\eta M$ of each of $t$ such transformed masks. It has density at least $\delta/2-\eta$, while each corresponding transformed easy set intersects it in at most $2\eta M$ cells. Thus $|\mathbb E_z 1_D(z)V(z)|\ge\rho(\delta/2-\eta)-2\eta\ge\rho\delta/4$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $t=O_{\rho,\delta}(1)$ at all large lengths. Greedily retaining a row and discarding every row within $\eta M$ of it proves the asserted packing.

There is a shattering consequence. Sample $C u$ suffix positions independently for a large fixed $C$. The probability of failing to distinguish a given separated pair is at most $(1-\eta)^{Cu}$. A union bound over at most $P^2$ pairs gives some such sample with at least $cP$ different restrictions. By the Sauer–Shelah bound, $\sum_{i=0}^d\binom{\lceil Cu\rceil}{i}$ restrictions suffice for a family of VC dimension $d$. Thus the transformed row masks have VC dimension $\Omega_{\delta,r}(u)$; there is a set of that many suffixes on which all binary patterns occur. In the cubic split $P=N/2^{\lfloor q/10\rfloor}$, so this also forces $\Omega(P)$ prototypes for a suffix universe of size only about $N^{1/10}$. Strong extraction gives the underlying connection between pair correlations and large-set sampling. [Haussler on packing and VC dimension](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~djhsu/coms4995-s20/haussler-packing.pdf) · [Almost-universal hashing and strong extraction, Lemma 10](https://lucatrevisan.github.io/pubs/brst02.pdf)

## Sparse XOR lists can pass this test

At either target width $b=\lfloor q/5\rfloor$ or $\lfloor q/10\rfloor$, split the suffix into two parts of about $b/2$ bits. For every row include the entire first part as one list, supported on those coordinates. The other list lies in the second part, of size $B=2^{\lfloor b/2\rfloor}$ before selection. Include all second-part values whose last $r_0$ bits are zero, for a fixed sufficiently large $r_0$, and choose the other second-part values with independent fair coins for each row. The two-list XOR mask consists precisely of the selected cylinders. It uses $O(\sqrt M)$ entries per row. Its union contains a constant fraction of the full cube consisting of words ending in $r_0$ zeros; a fixed decoder appends those bits.

For two different rows and any two relabelings, the expected fractional Hamming distance is at least $(1-2^{1-r_0})/2$. Outside both compulsory cylinders the two row bits are independent and fair. Changing one free choice in either row changes the fractional distance by at most $1/B$, and at most $2B$ choices matter. The bounded-differences inequality gives probability $\exp[-\Omega(B)]$ for distance below $1/4$, taking $r_0\ge4$. The number of pairs of rows and permitted transformations is $2^{O(q)}$ since the projective semilinear group has at most $bM^3$ elements. At these widths $B/q\to\infty$, so there exists a selection in which **all** row pairs stay separated after all these relabelings. This family meets the list-size and pattern-diversity requirements with many easy words still present. A successful cover must control which outputs of the interpreter occur in the masks.
