# When a very sparse oracle row has no short witness for either parity

The ordinary pinned SAT program is hard for deterministic one-sign detectors. A possible way to shorten a signed formula is to find a short nondeterministic witness for its correct sign on infinitely many scales. The following oracle construction tests whether sparsity and determinism alone can provide that witness. Earlier row-emptiness examples give a one-word witness for the opposite answer. Here both answers require a full scan on the same fixed instruction, even when each row contains arbitrarily slowly growing numbers of marks. ## A sharp statistical query bound on each sufficiently late promised input

Fix a computable nondecreasing unbounded sequence of positive integers $B_j$, for example $1+\log^* j$, and a computable generous clock $H_j\ge M_j$ for each scale. Take $M_j=2^{j!}$ for $j\ge 2$. There is one recursive two-word instruction $O(a,i)$ returning a bit. It vanishes off the pairs with $a=M_j$ and $0\le i<M_j$. The row $x_j(i)=O(M_j,i)$ has at most $B_j$ ones. On the promise input $0^{M_j}$, its length and $j$ may be supplied for free. A fixed ordinary controller with this instruction computes the parity $b_j$ of the row using $M_j$ nonadaptive probes, constant registers, and $O(M_j)$ steps.

Let a fixed uniform one-prover verifier claim one fixed sign $b_j=c$. Allow private fair coins, arbitrary adaptive oracle calls, arbitrary rounds and unlimited prover computation. Cap the verifier at $H_j$ native steps, rejecting on overflow. Messages and coins under the cap have finite computable bounds, for instance by charging each received word and each word of randomness. Word width on these inputs is $C\lceil\log_2(M_j+2)\rceil$ for a fixed coefficient. Suppose every strategy has acceptance probability at most a fixed rational $s<1$ at each false scale, with $0\le s$. Then, at **every sufficiently large scale**, for every prover strategy with acceptance probability $p$,
$$
 \mathbb E[\#\{\text{distinct positions probed in row }M_j\}]\ \ge\ (p-s)M_j.\tag{1}
$$
This is also a statement about the truncated strategies for an unbounded-work verifier sound without the cutoff. The existential-witness case takes $s=0$, a deterministic verifier and a prover supplying a nondeterministic path. On every sufficiently late scale, **each** accepting path within the clock must read **all** $M_j$ distinct positions. Each parity occurs infinitely often.

The constant in (1) is optimal. Using disjoint coin events of probabilities $s$ and $p-s$, accept immediately on the first, scan and check the claimed parity on the second, and reject otherwise. On true instances the expected query cost is exactly $(p-s)M_j$. Dyadic probabilities use a bounded number of coins for fixed parameters. General probabilities can be approximated by dyadics.

## Constructing a single recursive instruction

Enumerate triples consisting of a clocked verifier, claimed sign and rational soundness bound. Include the verifier's width coefficient, with its finite message and coin conventions. Every triple has an index $e\ge0$ and can win at most once. At stage $j$ consider the unretired indices $e<\min(j,B_j-1)$ in increasing order whose width coefficient is at most $j/2$. Every fixed index is eventually eligible at all stages after its activation, up to retirement. At these widths no verifier at this stage can even form $M_{j+1}$ as one operand. Indeed $\log_2 M_{j+1}=(j+1)j!$ while $\lceil\log_2(M_j+2)\rceil=j!+1$. Earlier rows have already been permanently set. All remaining operand pairs accessible to this trial, other than those in the current row, have the fixed answer zero or the already determined earlier answer. The construction can search the finite games using arbitrary time. Most indices have no assigned action at any given stage.

For the current index $e$, search over all candidate rows $x$ of weight at most $B_j-e-1$ and all deterministic prover strategies for this capped verifier on $0^{M_j}$. A strategy assigns one response per observable public history, including message boundaries. Let $p$ be its acceptance probability and $q$ the expected number of distinct current-row positions it reads. These rational numbers are computable exactly by enumerating the private tapes. Search for a pair satisfying $p-q/M_j>s$. If none exists, go on to the next index. If one exists, this index wins and retires. If the candidate's parity already falsifies its claimed sign, fix this row. Otherwise choose a coordinate $h$ of smallest probe probability. The sum of the coordinate probe probabilities is $q$, so that probability is at most $q/M_j$. Flip bit $h$. The parity changes and the weight increases by at most one. Against the old prover strategy and the same private coins, acceptance can decrease only on paths probing $h$. Consequently the modified row has a strategy accepting the false claim with probability at least $p-q/M_j>s$. Set the row permanently and end the stage. If no index wins, use the all-zero row.

There are finitely many candidate rows, private tapes and public strategies per trial. No future stage changes an accessible pair used in a winning violation. Membership in the resulting table is recursive by boundedly many completed stages for any supplied row. Only the verifier, rather than the oracle construction, has the advertised time cap.

Now fix a sound triple of index $e$. It can never win. Only finitely many indices precede it, each winning at most once. At all sufficiently late stages the test for $e$ is reached and fails. The eventual row is zero or was set by a later index $f>e$. In the latter case its final weight is at most $B_j-f\le B_j-e-1$. Thus **the final row itself was among the candidates of the failed test for $e$**. This is the purpose of reserving one unit of sparsity per priority. No deterministic prover on that row violates (1). The inequality is linear in the acceptance probability and expected probes, so conditioning on the prover's own random tape gives it for all randomized strategies. If either parity occurred only finitely often, a detector for the other sign could hardwire the last exception, accept at every later scale and make no queries. This contradicts (1) with $s=0$. The argument works for every chosen computable $H_j$, even $2^{M_j}$, with the resulting opcode allowed to depend on the clock. A fixed recursive instruction can always be evaluated eventually without any oracle queries by recomputing its construction. The finite clock is essential to the nondeterministic query assertion.

## Why an unbounded row count matters

For any sequence of oracle rows with a fixed upper bound on their weights, take the largest weight $m$ that occurs infinitely often. After a finite hardwired cutoff all weights are at most $m$. A nondeterministic detector guesses $m$ distinct in-range positions, checks all are marked, and signals the parity of $m$. This detector is globally sound on the promised scales and signals infinitely often using at most $m$ oracle probes. It works when $m=0$ as well. Thus an arbitrarily slow divergence of the allowed sparsity and a fixed sparsity bound genuinely differ for the every-late-scale, both-sign witness theorem.

For an efficiently computable cap $B_j$, a two-block alternating test for either parity guesses an ordered list of at most $B_j$ marks, checks its length parity, order and that each position is marked, then universally checks one index for an omitted mark. Scanning the list on one challenge costs $O(1+B_j)$ word steps. This verifies an exact answer even on the very same oracle as (1).

One can make the uniform all-zero circuit approximate the *entire* opcode arbitrarily closely. Given any computable nondecreasing unbounded bound $h(w)$, choose lengths $M_j=2^{L_j}$ with $L_{j+1}>(j+1)(L_j+1)$ and $h(L_j)\ge j^2$, and take $B_j\le j$. For width $w$ the largest visible row index $j$ has $w\ge L_j$, and all visible rows together contain at most $\sum_{i\le j}i\le j^2\le h(w)$ marks. The test still has the same free scale and length on promise inputs, and the spacing still prevents a stage verifier from addressing any later row. This gives a uniform constant approximation and a recursive hidden exception set. A list of the exact marks supplied as trusted input immediately determines the row parity.

## Every dyadic scale with queries to future rows

A second fixed recursive opcode gives a nondeterministic bound on **all** dyadic scales $M_j=2^j$. On each sufficiently late scale every accepting path of each sound detector of either sign through clock $H_j$ makes more than $\lfloor M_j/4\rfloor$ oracle probes in total. The row-weight allowance may still be any computable nondecreasing $B_j\to\infty$. This variant allows a detector to address later rows.

Use the same one-win priority enumeration, restricted here to deterministic witness checkers or nondeterministic machines. A previous winning stage preserves the values of the actual pairs on its chosen path, even for queries to future rows. The total number of these path assignments made strictly before stage $j$ is at most $\sum_{i<j}\lfloor 2^i/4\rfloor<M_j/4$. Completed rows are also fixed in full. Losing searches impose no assignments.

For each unfinished row $r\ge j$, let $P_r$ count its already fixed positive entries. At index $e<\min(j,B_j-1)$ search for a branch signaling with at most $\lfloor M_j/4\rfloor$ total probes under some completion whose positive count on every unfinished row $r$ is bounded by $\max(P_r,B_r-e-1)$. Only finitely many pairs are accessible during a clocked trial. Equivalently search the finite branches, guessing and sharing answers at repeated pairs; for each resulting row the already fixed positives and newly demanded positive cells must fit the stated cap. All other free cells can provisionally be set to zero. If the index wins, preserve the values of every pair on the branch. Fill the current row with its fixed positives and zeros elsewhere, then change one still free cell if necessary to force the parity opposite the sign just claimed. Such a cell exists even after both the previous freezes and the new path. Before the row is filled, foreign probes have fixed at most $B_j-1$ positive positions in it: this follows by induction from the maximum in the search cap and $e\ge0$. The flip stays within $B_j$. Use the current fixed entries and no extra marks at a stage with no winner.

A sound index $e$ never wins. The finitely many winning indices at most $e$ assign only finitely many path cells. Choose a cutoff beyond their stages and every row to which they assigned cells. Take $B_j\ge e+2$ beyond this cutoff. At any row $r$ beyond it, every foreign contribution to $P_r$ came from a winner $f>e$. Such contributions have combined count at most $B_r-e-2$ by induction on the freezes: a later winner must preserve the existing positives within $\max(P_r,B_r-f-1)$. At its own final stage the row has weight at most $B_r-e-1$, including its possible extra bit. Therefore the actual final oracle is an admissible completion under the search for $e$ at **every** sufficiently late stage. No earlier requirement stops the search there. Any fast accepting branch on the final oracle would win the search, contradicting soundness. This version trades the sharp full scan on spaced scales for a constant fraction on all dyadic scales.

## A bound on an existential SAT encoding

Suppose a fixed oracle-assisted deterministic generator on a promised scale produces an explicit existential oracle circuit satisfiable only when its claimed row parity is correct. For every satisfying witness, consider all current-row positions queried during generation and while evaluating the gates on that witness. If the combined computation is within the chosen clock, their union is the **entire row** at every sufficiently late satisfiable scale. The generator followed by guessing the witness and evaluating the circuit is a sound nondeterministic detector. If the output is an ordinary CNF, all the row probes must already occur in generation. A locally accessed encoding has the same implication for the union of probes over the local calls actually made by its sound nondeterministic assignment checker. This refutes a generic oracle-respecting compression from sparsity or a short prescribed scan alone. The unconditional question for ordinary SAT concerns fixed explicit word operations and remains open.

The single-coordinate query coupling and finite-priority techniques have classical precedents. The scope here is the sharp advantage-dependent inequality on every sufficiently late promised input and the sparsity boundary for a fixed recursive opcode. Historical priority for that combination has not been established.
