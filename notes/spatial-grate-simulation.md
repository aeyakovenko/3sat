# The one-head cost of preserving a two-tape computation graph

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Let $G_T$ be the undirected shadow of one of the robust $T$-step two-plane trajectories in the journal. For each fixed dimension $d\ge2$, the minimum number of step vertices in a single-head $d$-dimensional tape trajectory whose access graph contains $G_T$ as a minor is

$$\Theta_d(T^{1+1/d}).$$

In particular a single planar head needs $\Theta(T^{3/2})$ steps. A single one-dimensional head cannot realize the minor at any length for sufficiently large members of the family. The theorem concerns preservation of the access graph. The machine's Boolean output might be computed on a different access graph. [Two-plane grate construction](two-plane-directed-grates.md) · [PPST, especially §§2 and 5](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)

## Fragmenting a trajectory with marked times

Use one vertex per step, edges between consecutive steps, and edges between consecutive visits to each writable cell on its own tape. A sequential stream may drive the head motions. Only chronological and writable-memory edges enter the graph. More generally, give each of at most $h$ memory banks a fixed injective placement of its touched addresses in $\mathbb Z^d$, and assume at most one touched address per bank per step. A step with several accesses can be split into constantly many steps when their multiplicity is bounded. The proof also covers edges from the last write to each later read that uses it. Physical tape placements and fixed tape isometries are special cases.

For a $U$-step trace choose arbitrary anchors $a_t\in\mathbb Z^d$, set $a_{-1}=a_0$, and let $\rho_t$ be the sum of the coordinate ranges of the finite set comprising $a_{t-1},a_t$ and the positions touched at step $t$. For any integer cube side $L\ge1$ put

$$D(L)=\sum_{t=0}^{U-1}\min(1,\rho_t/L).$$

Given $m$ marked step vertices and any integer $r\ge1$, some deletion of at most

$$ D(L)+(hL^d+1)m/r $$

vertices leaves every undirected component with at most $r$ marked vertices. Also the entire graph has pathwidth at most $\lfloor D(L)\rfloor+hL^d+1$.

**Proof.** Give the side-$L$ cubic grid an independently uniform integral shift modulo $L$ in each coordinate. In coordinate $j$ a step's set intersects two intervals with probability at most its range in that coordinate divided by $L$. Some shift therefore has at most $D(L)$ steps whose set intersects multiple cubes. Delete all these steps. Each remaining step belongs to its anchor's cube. Equality of placed addresses keeps each surviving memory edge inside a cube. For consecutive surviving adjacent times $t,t+1$, the set at $t+1$ contains both anchors, which keeps the chronological edge inside as well.

Order the surviving times of a cube chronologically. At any boundary in this order at most $hL^d+1$ distinct earlier vertices have an edge to the later side. For consecutive-visit edges each of the cube's at most $L^d$ addresses per bank has at most one crossing edge. For last-write edges to many readers, every edge for the same address crossing this boundary has the same earlier writer. Both assertions hold after vertex deletion because the surviving edges were present in the original graph. Only one chronological edge can straddle the corresponding physical time. Split the order into chunks of at most $r$ marks by cutting after each $r$th mark when another mark remains. Delete the earlier endpoints just counted at every cut. There are at most $m/r$ cuts over all cubes. Now every component stays within a chunk. For pathwidth, use a bag containing each next vertex and the earlier vertices with edges reaching that vertex or later in its cube order. These bags cover every edge and each vertex's bags form an interval. Concatenate the orders of the cubes, then insert all initially deleted steps into every bag. If no steps survive, use one bag of all steps.

For one geometric head use its actual position as anchor. Nearest-neighbor motion gives $D(L)\le U/L$. With all steps marked, choosing $L\asymp_d r^{1/(d+1)}$ gives a deletion of $O_d(U/r^{1/(d+1)})$ for components of order at most $r$. With arbitrary marked steps and $r$ a fixed positive fraction of their number, choose $L\asymp_d U^{1/(d+1)}$ for a deletion of $O_d(U^{d/(d+1)})$. Loui's one-worktape space simulation uses generalized crossing sequences and features the same spatial exponent. [Loui, 1979](https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/148972)

## What survives a minor model

The grate construction gives constants $c,\eta>0$ such that after any deletion of at most $cT$ step vertices, at least $\eta T^2$ ordered pairs still have directed paths. In the undirected shadow with component orders $n_i$ the number of such pairs is at most $\sum_i n_i^2\le T\max_i n_i$. Thus some surviving component contains at least $\eta T$ vertices. This also gives treewidth $\Omega(T)$. In a width-$w$ tree decomposition, associate each vertex with a containing bag. Removing a centroid bag for these weights leaves components of at most half the original mass, by connectedness of the bags containing each vertex. Repeat on induced components larger than $\eta T/2$, restricting the decomposition to their vertices. The depth is bounded by $\lceil\log_2(2/\eta)\rceil$ and at each depth fewer than $2/\eta$ bags are required. Total deletions are $O_\eta(w+1)$, which must exceed $cT$.

Suppose a host single-head trajectory contains this shadow as a minor. Choose one marked host vertex in each of the $T$ pairwise disjoint, connected branch sets of a minor model. Apply the marked separator with $r=\lfloor\eta T/2\rfloor$. Mark a *target* vertex as damaged whenever its host branch set contains any deleted host vertex. This costs at most the total number deleted, even if several deletions hit one branch. Every target component of undamaged vertices lifts to a connected host subgraph containing all its distinct marks because surviving branch sets are intact and adjacent branch sets have an actual host edge. Unused host vertices need no charge. Consequently all undamaged target components have at most $r$ vertices. The grate bound forces more than $cT$ deletions. Since the host separator has size $O_d(U^{d/(d+1)})$, this proves $U=\Omega_d(T^{1+1/d})$.

For $d=1$ the one-head consecutive-visit graph is planar. Arrange times along a horizontal line. Put each nontrivial return arc above if its excursion goes right and below if it goes left. A same-side interleaving $a<c<b<e$ is impossible. In a right excursion from $a$ to $b$ the head at $c$ is strictly to the right of the cell at $a,b$. A right excursion from $c$ to $e$ could not return left of its starting cell at $b$ without revisiting that cell before $e$. Reflection covers left excursions. Stationary steps give only chronological edges. Minors preserve planarity. Repeated planar separators of order $O(\sqrt T)$, applied for the same constant number of rounds as above, contradict the surviving giant component in $G_T$ for large $T$. [Lipton and Tarjan](https://doi.org/10.1137/0136016)

## Matching realization for every sparse graph in dimension at least two

Let a simple undirected graph have $T$ vertices and $E=O(T)$ edges. In a box of side $O_d(1+T^{1/d})$ reserve one distinct all-even lattice point per edge. The points with at least one odd coordinate form corridors through which any two docking sites can be joined in $O_d(1+T^{1/d})$ steps. For an explicit route use the site one step in the positive first direction from each reserved point. Both docking sites have odd first coordinate. Move the second coordinate to an odd reference value, change the first and remaining coordinates, and then restore the destination's second coordinate. Throughout this route either the first or the second coordinate is odd. Allow a one-cell margin around the box.

Visit the target vertices in any fixed order. In the time block for vertex $v$, visit each incident edge's docking site, step onto its reserved point once, and return to its docking site. Perform all travel outside the reserved points. Include one stay at a corridor site per block so isolated vertices also get nonempty blocks. The blocks partition the simulated times, and each is connected by chronology. Each edge has exactly two visits to its dedicated point, yielding a direct consecutive-visit edge joining the required blocks. Incidental visits to corridor points produce additional edges that can be dropped. There are $2E$ excursions with travel and $T$ block markers. This costs $O_d(T^{1+1/d})$ steps and proves the matching upper bound since the grate shadow has $O(T)$ edges. A fixed input-driven nearest-neighbor machine for each fixed $d$ can follow the sequence of directions on a sequential input stream. This is an offline realization of the graph and does not charge the problem of recovering a graph from arbitrary program input.

## How far apart two heads must get

Return to the physical two-plane grate. Apply separate lattice isometries to the two head paths $p_1,p_2$, including any relative translation. Use the transformed first head as anchor. Then $\rho_t\le1+\|p_1(t)-p_2(t)\|_1$ in the transformed frame. Mark all times and set $r=\lfloor\eta T/2\rfloor$. Choose $L=\lfloor\lambda\sqrt T\rfloor$ with a fixed sufficiently small $\lambda>0$ so $(2L^2+1)T/r\le cT/2$ for large $T$. The surviving giant component forces $D(L)>cT/2$. Since every summand is at most one, this implies $\|p_1(t)-p_2(t)\|_1\ge c'\sqrt T$ on at least $c'T$ times, with some fixed $c'>0$. The constants hold for every choice of these isometries. The scale is tight. In the original construction each plane arranges $O(s)$ tiles of side $q=\lceil\sqrt s\rceil$ in a nearly square array. The paths between tiles can stay in its bounding box, which has diameter $O(q\sqrt s)=O(\sqrt T)$ since $T=\Theta(sq^2)$. In the common array frame every pair of head positions is within this distance. [Tile layout](two-plane-directed-grates.md) For arbitrary RAM placements the general form instead charges the within-step ranges and the changes of anchor. Random access imposes no useful upper bound on either.

## Sharpness on one native trajectory

The one-head bound $O_d(U/r^{1/(d+1)})$ for unmarked component size is sharp. Take an odd $s\ge3$. In one sweep visit all $s^d$ sites of $[s]^d$ exactly once, by a nearest-neighbor serpentine order that includes every edge parallel to a chosen axis. Order the parallel lines by a serpentine path in the remaining $(d-1)$ coordinates and scan each full line, reversing direction at each line change. Since $s$ is odd, the sweep goes between opposite corners, independently of the chosen axis. Reverse entire sweeps as necessary so a new sweep starts where the preceding one finished, using a stay for repeated endpoints. Group $d$ sweeps with distinct principal axes into a layer and take $s$ layers. The number of step vertices is $U=d s^{d+1}$.

The $d$ visits to one site in one layer form a connected branch set by consecutive visits to that cell. These sets are disjoint. Horizontal edges in each of the $d$ directions are supplied by chronology in the associated sweep. Each last visit in a layer and first visit in the next are consecutive visits to their common cell. The resulting minor is the $(d+1)$-dimensional side-$s$ grid, with exactly $d$ time vertices per branch set.

Write $q=d+1$. A grid subset $A$ of size at most $s^q/2$ has at least $c_q|A|^{1-1/q}$ boundary edges inside the grid. By the discrete Loomis–Whitney inequality some coordinate projects onto at least $|A|^{1-1/q}$ occupied lines, of which at most $|A|/s$ are full. Each remaining occupied line supplies a boundary edge, and $|A|/s\le 2^{-1/q}|A|^{1-1/q}$. If $z$ time vertices are removed, discard from the grid every struck branch set, at most $z$ of them. When surviving time components have size at most $r\le U/2$, each remaining grid component has at most $r/d$ vertices. For $r\ge d$, sum the edge bound over these components. At most $2qz$ boundary edges enter struck grid sites, hence $2qz\ge c_q(s^q-z)/(r/d)^{1/q}$ and $z=\Omega_d(U/r^{1/(d+1)})$. For $1\le r<d$ every branch set must be struck, giving the same order for fixed $d$. [Discrete Loomis–Whitney, Theorem 2.5](https://cs.stanford.edu/~chrismre/papers/paper49.Ngo.pdf)

Finite checks reconstructed every required grid edge for $d=1,2,3,4$ and $s=3,5$, up to 12,500 actual time vertices. A separate construction on 18 vertices and 36 edges verified exactly two visits at every reserved edge point in dimensions 2, 3 and 4. Run `python spatial_grate_audit.py` from the repository root to repeat both calculations. [Finite construction checks](../audits/spatial_grate_audit.py)

The conclusions use fixed $d$ throughout. The geometric separators, the minor realizations and their lower bounds are manuscript proofs. I have not established priority for their combination.
