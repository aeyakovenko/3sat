# How many copies of a formula buy a backbone query?

Let \(S\subseteq\{0,1\}^v\) be the set of possible assignments on \(v\ge1\) designated variables. Its one-literal extension table specifies emptiness and, when nonempty, which of the two values occur at every coordinate. A query with budget \(b\ge1\) asks for at most \(b\) members of \(S\) satisfying an arbitrary joint relation; other existential variables are allowed. A decision query returns one bit. A witness query also returns a satisfying tuple and its auxiliary witness, with arbitrary legal tie breaking. The relation may depend on all preceding replies. Lower bounds assume the procedure sees \(S\) only through this interface.

* With a single copy and witness answers, exactly \(v+1\) queries are necessary and sufficient in the worst case.
* With up to \(b\) copies and witness answers, the optimum is \(\Theta(v/b+\log(v+2))\). The upper bound uses \(O(v+b)\) copies summed over all calls for \(b\le v\). Thus a logarithmic call count still has to pay for linearly many copies on some input.
* With decision answers and unrestricted copies, the exact optimum is \(\lceil\log_2(3^v+1)\rceil\) calls. For any fixed block size \(b\ge2\), ordinary explicit SAT on a formula of \(n\) encoded bits gives a procedure with at most \((\log_2 3+1/b)v+O(b)\) decision calls, **each of size** \(O_b(n)\). The call-count coefficient can thus approach \(1.585\) using bounded replication. Fewer than linearly many one-bit replies require information about the formula from outside this interface.

Janota, Lynce and Marques-Silva already give the \(v+1\) witness upper bound. Marques-Silva and Janota obtain logarithmically many witness-oracle queries by making independent copies and maximizing the number of satisfiable requirements. Here the number of independent hidden models per query is charged explicitly. Relative priority of the bounds for this specific interface is unestablished. [Backbone algorithms, Propositions 5–6](https://sat.inesc-id.pt/~mikolas/bb-aicom-preprint.pdf) · [Marques-Silva and Janota, Propositions 1–3](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/031/download/)

## Three obstructions

On any first single-copy query with a nonempty filter, let the oracle return some permitted assignment \(a\) and choose
\[
    S=\{a\}\cup\{a\mathbin\oplus e_j:1\le j\le v\}.
\]
Each neighbor uniquely witnesses the opposite value at its own coordinate. On this instance every neighbor must actually be returned. Deleting an unseen neighbor preserves every prior successful reply, including the auxiliary witnesses. Every prior negative answer stays negative under deletion. If the first filter was empty, reply negatively and choose the center on the first nonempty filter. This gives the extra first query as well as the \(v\) neighbor replies. The matching procedure asks for one model, then tests the opposite literal separately at each remaining coordinate. An empty initial reply finishes immediately. For \(b\) copies, fix any center in advance: at least \(v\) neighbor appearances are necessary on its full star, hence at least \(\lceil v/b\rceil\) calls.

Within the same family take the chain consisting of the empty set, the center alone, and then the center with its first \(j\) neighbors for \(j=1,\ldots,v\). The profiles of all \(v+2\) levels are distinct. For any joint query that succeeds somewhere, take one witness at its first successful level, and return that very witness at every higher level. A query then has at most two different replies on the remaining chain. A binary decision tree needs depth at least \(\lceil\log_2(v+2)\rceil\), regardless of how many copies a query may request.

For the decision-only lower bound, all \(3^v\) nonempty profiles occur on subcubes and the empty profile occurs too. A transcript of \(q\) adaptive bits distinguishes at most \(2^q\) outcomes. Even a randomized algorithm with a fixed worst-case call cap and success probability \(1-\delta\) on every profile needs \(2^q\ge(1-\delta)(3^v+1)\), by averaging over uniform profiles and then its coins.

There are small proper formulas for these examples. For the star, define unique prefix parities \(p_i=x_1\oplus\cdots\oplus x_i\). Require the sequence \(p_i\oplus(a_1\oplus\cdots\oplus a_i)\), initialized with zero at \(i=0\), to be nondecreasing. This sequence flips at exactly the deviations from \(a\), so there is at most one deviation. Pinning \(x_j=a_j\) deletes precisely its neighbor. Prefix XOR equivalences and the monotone conditions take \(O(v)\) clauses and names. Pad units and binary clauses using three additional false anchors forced by seven proper clauses. All extra values are uniquely determined functions of \(x\) independent of the center and the deletion set, even if a filter can inspect them. A formula encoding a subcube only needs pins and optional always-satisfied anchored clauses for occurring names. The bit lengths are \(O(v\log(v+2))\).

## Witness batches and their actual sizes

Ask for a reference model, or finish on the empty answer. Keep the set of coordinates not yet seen opposite their reference value. A threshold-\(j\) query asks for \(j\) models whose combined discrepancies on this set cover at least \(j\) distinct coordinates. It succeeds iff at least \(j\) remaining coordinates are flexible: one witness per flexible coordinate suffices. Remove every newly witnessed coordinate after a successful answer.

Repeat threshold \(b\) until the first failure or no coordinates remain. After failure fewer than \(b\) flexible coordinates remain. Test powers of two in descending order starting with the largest at most \(b-1\). Before threshold \(j\) fewer than \(2j\) flexible coordinates remain. Success removes at least \(j\), and failure means fewer than \(j\) were present. After threshold one all survivors are backbones. This uses at most \(\lfloor v/b\rfloor+O(1+\log(b+1))\) queries. Each successful full batch pays for \(b\) different coordinates; the one failed full batch costs \(b\) copies and the final geometric sweep uses fewer than \(2b\) copies. Take \(b=\min(b,v)\) if more copies are allowed. Combined with the two lower bounds this proves the stated order over the entire range.

For each address compute the OR of its discrepancies across the \(j\) independent assignments, then sum these one-bit flags by a balanced tree of binary adders and compare with \(j\). The sum of adder widths is \(O(v)\). The extra gate count is \(O(jv+v)\). On a normalized proper DIMACS input with \(v\) designated occurring names, dense renaming and clause deduplication give \(c+v=O(1+n/\log(n+2))\) for \(c\) retained clauses. The generated query has \(O(j(c+v)+1)\) records of \(O(\log(n+2))\) bits when \(j\le v\). Hence each call fits \(O(jn)\) encoded bits and the aggregate materialized length fits \(O(n(v+b))\). A witness service must return the assignments; a decision-only RAM clock does not supply this ability at the same cost. ## An optimal binary transcript

Order the nonempty profiles lexicographically, giving the statuses *only zero*, *only one*, *both* the digits \(0,1,2\). Give the empty profile rank zero and a nonempty profile with ternary integer \(z\) rank \(1+z\). Inclusion of model sets cannot decrease any digit after a first model is present: both singleton statuses can only change to *both*. Thus the rank of the union of any finite list of actual models is at most the true rank.

For a nonempty \(S\), all statuses can be exhibited simultaneously by \(\max(2,v)\) models. In a minimum-cardinality cover of the attained literal values, every member has a private witnessed literal. If the cover has at least three members, opposite literals cannot both be private at the same coordinate: a third member realizes one of them. So the members have private witnesses at distinct coordinates. Smaller covers need at most two models. Repetition pads the list to its public length. Lean checks that deletion exposes a private value and that the chosen private coordinates are distinct whenever the cover has a third member. [Private-coordinate proof](../lean/BackbonePrivateCoordinates.lean)

A positive rank threshold is therefore one decision query: guess the indicated copies of \(F\), compute their visible status digits, and compare their rank with the public threshold. An empty \(F\) necessarily fails every positive threshold. Binary search among the \(3^v+1\) ordered ranks achieves the counting bound. Lex comparison with a known ternary threshold takes only linear additional gates once the digit flags are present.

For short queries apply this search independently to blocks of at most \(b\) variables. First test emptiness once. Each full block then has \(3^b\) possible outcomes and needs at most \(\lceil b\log_2 3\rceil\) decisions. Its threshold uses at most \(b\) copies of \(F\), or two for a final singleton. Dense renaming gives \(O(bn)\) encoded bits per call. The reduction improves the number of decision bits over the two-pins-per-variable procedure. The hidden constants for query materialization and solving grow with the block size, so this does not establish a smaller leading running-time constant.

## Several literals at once

For \(1\le r\le v\), let the hidden models on \(v\) marked variables be all weight-\(r\) words. Each positive query pinning the ones of an \(r\)-subset has exactly one witness. Deleting an unreturned weight-\(r\) word preserves the transcript of any positive-copy witness procedure and changes that entry. Thus even with the promise of **at most one deletion** the undeleted instance needs at least \(\binom vr\) returned models in total to recover the full \(r\)-literal table. For \(r=1,2,3\) the full sphere has a fixed-width prefix counter with \(O_r(v)\) functionally defined auxiliaries and clauses; deleting one pattern adds the single clause negating its selected bits, padded if necessary. The surviving auxiliary values agree exactly. An algorithm examining the original clause list may spot this deletion directly. None of these interface bounds establishes a deterministic RAM lower bound for ordinary SAT. [Finite oracle checks](../audits/backbone_copy_query_audit.py)
