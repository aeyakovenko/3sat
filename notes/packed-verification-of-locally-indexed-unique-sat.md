# What lets a local DIMACS indexer beat the native clock?

On the journal's logarithmic-word RAM with a packed input, well-formed proper ASCII DIMACS with $M$ bits has a nondeterministic verifier using $O(1+M/\log(2+M))$ steps and upfront binary guesses. It has exactly one accepting path for each satisfying assignment to the **occurring** names. A fixed family requires $\Omega(M/\log M)$ word probes even on its uniquely satisfiable file. Arbitrarily sparse, long canonical decimal names are allowed. The lexical and semantic promises are part of the statement. The multitape bound with comparably short guesses of Salamon and Wehar uses a different clock. [Salamon and Wehar, Lemmas 19 and 21](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-023-10119-x)

## Evaluating a packed file under the guessed assignment

Let $b$ be a power of two of order $\log M$ **bytes**, with $256^b\le M^{1/8}$ for large $M$. Read a raw block in constantly many packed word operations. Guess the truth bits of all positive decimal identifiers with at most $b$ digits, an array of size below $10^b$. Also guess a vector of $\lceil M/b\rceil$ bits for distinct longer names, assigned on their first occurrence. The vectors are guessed before the table construction.

A finite-state lexical filter skips the header and comment lines and replaces their bytes with whitespace. For every possible block and incoming filter state, tabulate the filtered word and outgoing state. In the filtered stream the tokens are signed literals or the delimiter zero, separated by whitespace. Append synthetic whitespace at the ends. A block with a whitespace byte has at most two endpoint fragments, before its first and after its last whitespace. All tokens strictly between those whitespaces are complete and have at most $b$ digits. For each possible filtered block, precompute the effect of its interior tokens on two Boolean flags: whether prior completed clauses have passed and whether the current unfinished clause already has a true literal. A literal consults the guessed short-name array and a zero closes the clause. The same table supplies the two endpoint fragments and their lengths. A block without whitespace supplies just its continuation fragment. Enumerating all tables takes $O(256^b\,\mathrm{polylog}(M+2))$ work.

Process the endpoint fragments in order, completing any open token on encountering its delimiter. Keep its sign separately. Names with at most $b$ digits fit in a word and index the short array. For longer names split the digit sequence into pieces of $b$ bytes aligned to the **start of that name**. Keep at most one unfinished piece as a packed carry. Appending a block-sized fragment produces constantly many complete pieces by shifts and masks. A sparse trie stores edges indexed by the current node number, the packed piece, and its length. One RAM address suffices: the node count is $O(M/b+1)$, the piece has at most $8b$ bits, and the journal's word width has ample headroom. Nodes and terminal bits are initialized on first insertion into the initially zero memory. Number a new long-name terminal in encounter order and take its truth bit from the next guess. If a token of exactly $b$ digits remains short, it can retain its complete first piece until another digit arrives. The total number of pieces traversed is $O(M/b+1)$, including one final partial piece per long occurrence. Applying a finished boundary token and the precomputed interior effect costs constantly many operations per block.

To obtain the asserted accepting-path count, require unused guesses to be zero. Mark short names from boundary tokens as they arrive. Mark the code of each distinct filtered block whose interior transformation was applied. After the scan, enumerate these marked block types and mark every short name in their interiors. This costs at most $O(256^b\,\mathrm{polylog}(M+2))$ even if a type occurred many times. Check the short-array flags and the unused tail of the long vector in $O(10^b+M/b)$ steps. Each satisfying assignment now induces exactly one complete guess string. The total cost has the claimed order. On a full-declared-variable zero/one-model promise, this also preserves unambiguity: any positive file with exactly one full model has no unused declared variable.

## Two classes of bit indexers that can be batched

Let $t$ be the advertised native clock and $M=o(t\log t)$ the bit length of a virtually supplied formula. In the short-payload hierarchy application $t$ and the root input length are polynomially related, with ample fixed word width. A deterministic packed generation time of $o(t)$ suffices for the unambiguous check above. The following are two separate sufficient conditions.

1. **Ordered branching program.** Suppose the output bit as a function of its binary position has a locally uniform layered branching program reading the position once, most significant bit first. There is a fixed slack $\delta>0$ with width at most $t^{1-\delta}$, and a state transition or output takes $\mathrm{polylog}(t+2)$ time, including its reads of the native input. Precompute the $O(t^{1-\delta}\log(t+2))$ transitions. Choose a power of two $B=\Theta(\log t)$ bits fitting in a word. If there are enough positions, tabulate each state's suffix truth table at the bottom $\log_2 B$ levels by concatenating the two child words. Traverse the complete tree of high-address prefixes, reusing each prefix's state for its two children. There are $O(1+M/B)$ nodes in this tree, including padding to a power of two. At each leaf emit one packed suffix word. A shorter address has a single word. Total time is $O(t^{1-\delta}\mathrm{polylog}(t+2)+1+M/B)=o(t)$.
2. **Adaptive address-bit probes.** Suppose the bit indexer, on its fixed native payload, has a decision tree with at most $C\log t$ leaves over the output address bits, in any order. Its non-query work per node is $\mathrm{polylog}(t+2)$. This includes at most $\log_2\log_2 t+O(1)$ adaptive address-bit probes provided the indexer accesses that address only through those probes. Unfold the tree; inconsistent branches can be removed. Each accepting leaf specifies a subcube. Group them into a fixed number of groups of at most $(\log_2 t)/8$ leaves. Reserve the low $\log_2 B$ address bits for one word and split the remaining high bits into two nearly equal parts. For each part and each assignment of its bits tabulate the bitmask of compatible leaves in each group. This costs $O(\sqrt{2+M}\,\mathrm{polylog}(t+2))$. Tabulate each leaf's word of allowed low addresses. For each group and every subset of its leaves, tabulate the bitwise OR of their low-address words; this costs $O(t^{1/8}\mathrm{polylog}(t+2))$. A high address selects two compatibility masks. Intersect them, fetch the subset's precomputed word and OR the answers from the constant number of groups. This gives each packed output word in $O(1)$ steps and all words in $o(t)$.

These generation bounds also work for ordinary decision-valued output families. For a purported compiler of zero/one-branch computations to zero/one-model formulas, they imply an unconditional impossibility at the linear milestone. Start with the one-bit-advice unambiguous tape hierarchy at time $O(n^6)$ versus $O(n^5)$. A fixed word RAM can guess a fixed $O(n^6)$-bit choice block upfront, simulate the advised machine and reject noncanonical unused choices. Use the actual advice for that length and a sufficient virtual clock $t=O(n^6)$. Each new verifier keeps only the preceding payload, code, and binary clock as public data. It guesses the two assignment vectors upfront and then deterministically generates and verifies the packed result. A fixed sufficient new clock is $o(t)$ while $t>n^4$: the displayed generation bounds are explicit in the locally obtained $M,t$, and the assignment and parser take $O(M/\log(2+M))=o(t)$. The payload has $n+O(\log^2 n)$ bits over $O(\log n)$ rounds. Thus repeat until $t\le n^4$ and simulate the final unambiguous computation on tape in $O(n^4\,\mathrm{polylog}(n+2))$. Guess read replies and check the unique consistent chronological trace by deterministic sorting. This contradicts the hierarchy on its prescribed advice. This argument concerns a compiler covering the fixed verifiers under the short-payload promise, including a uniform structural decoding bound for those verifiers. ## General local bit access

Fix variables $1,2,3,5$ false and $4$ true with a constant-size uniquely satisfiable proper 3-CNF. Seven clauses force $1,2,3$ false, then pad the pins for $4,5$ with distinct false names. Append $R$ copies of the clause $(4,2,3)$. Replacing its first decimal digit by $5$ flips one ASCII bit and makes the formula unsatisfiable. On the unmodified formula every successful path of a sound nondeterministic tester must observe each of the $R=\Theta(M)$ editable bits: an unobserved edit has the same answers on that path. This proves the packed lower bound and an $\Omega(M/B')$ lower bound when at most $B'$ physical bits are disclosed per probe, even at adaptively chosen positions. The zero-edit and single-edit files admit an indexer using just a binary defect ordinal. Thus a tester confined to output-bit probes cannot obtain a general short clock by supplying more nondeterministic proof bits. A simulator could still exploit the code and payload that generated the file.

There is a separate consequence for a generic algorithm inspecting that code. Suppose **every** fixed polylog-time bit indexer of promised zero/one-model proper files with $M=\Theta(t)$ and short payloads admits an $O(t)$-time nondeterministic recognizer for satisfiability. For any fixed $A$ and circuit size exponent, partition all assignments to a $d$-input Boolean circuit $C$ into blocks of $2^{\lfloor A\log_2 d\rfloor}$ consecutive assignments. Use one clause from the last construction per block. Change $4$ to $5$ iff $C$ accepts some assignment in the block. The local indexer can test that whole block in $d^{O(1)}=\mathrm{polylog}(t+2)$ operations. Take $t=\Theta(2^d/d^A)$; the small original payload containing $C$ can be padded to $t^{1/6}$ if required by address widths. This formula has its single satisfying assignment precisely when $C$ is unsatisfiable. The hypothesized recognizer would give nondeterministic Circuit-UNSAT time $O(2^d/d^A)$ for every fixed $A$ on polynomial-size circuits.

Williams's argument then gives $\mathsf{NEXP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{P}/\mathrm{poly}$. In the proof, if $\mathsf{NEXP}\subseteq\mathsf{P}/\mathrm{poly}$, a fixed nondeterministic exponential-time hierarchy language has polynomial-size witness circuits. Guess one and check that the circuit for a violated local clause is unsatisfiable. Its input length is $n+O(\log n)$ and its size is a fixed polynomial in $n$. One sufficiently large fixed $A$ absorbs the polynomial lost through the extra input bits and any fixed polylogarithmic cost of simulating the RAM on tape, yielding a faster nondeterministic algorithm for that language. This applies to a *generic* verifier, independently of whether any of these particular indexers is a reduction from bounded native acceptance. No uniform generic verifier for unrestricted bit-local indexers or superlinear SAT time bound is established.
