# Can cancellation in the program names prove a SAT time lower bound?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** I replaced the question of whether a word has a short program with parity questions about which of its programs finish. A low-rank approximation for a sufficiently large fraction of those parity tests would prove a lower bound. The fraction can tend to zero as fast as $2^{-o(q^2)}$. There is a calculation for a genuine nonlinear program family below. The required upper bound for the whole interpreter is open.

Use the fixed length-aware plain interpreter, the encoded-input RAM conventions and the deadline $T=2^q(q+2)^{\lceil\log_2(q+2)\rceil}$. Work at even $q$, put $N=2^q$ and $L=2^{q/2}$, and display all words as an $L$-by-$L$ binary matrix, with the two halves of a word as its row and column. For each word $x$, let $F_x$ be its set of names of length at most $q-2$ that print exactly $x$ and halt in $(\tau,T]$. Name sets for different words are disjoint. Encode a name $v$ by $2^{|v|}+\operatorname{bin}(v)$ in a fixed polynomial basis of $K=\mathbb F_{2^{q-1}}$, with the empty payload value zero. Positive indices through $2^{q-1}-1$ name every eligible program once. For the linear test take $\tau=N$ and call a word early if any eligible name prints it and halts by $\tau$. Early entries may receive arbitrary answers in all rank approximations. The linear test also works with $\tau=0$ and all entries counted. For the quadratic and cubic tests use this latter convention. [Interpreter, deadline and dense-advice theorem](full-cube-advice.md)

Select a polynomial $P$ by taking its $q$ field coefficients for degrees $0,\ldots,q-1$ independently and uniformly. Define the binary matrix
$$
B_P(x)=\sum_{i\in F_x}\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}(P(i))\pmod 2.
$$
Every matrix here counts actual executions. For $d=1,2,3$, define $J_1=L$ and, for $d=2,3$, $J_d=2^{\lfloor q/(2+d^2\log_2 7)\rfloor}$. If explicit SAT has a deterministic unrestricted-space $O(n^d)$ algorithm, there are positive constants $c,\kappa,\epsilon$ such that at every sufficiently large even $q$,
$$
\Pr_P\!\left[\begin{array}{l}\text{some binary matrix of rank at most }\kappa J_d\\
\text{differs from }B_P\text{ on at most }\epsilon N\text{ counted entries}\end{array}\right]\le 2^{-c q^2}. \tag{1}
$$
Ranks are over $\mathbb F_2$. In particular, for a positive attack it suffices to prove that at infinitely many even lengths a fraction $2^{-o(q^2)}$ of these matrices have rank $o(J_d)$ after changing $o(N)$ counted entries. This conclusion involves neither the cost of finding the seeds nor the cost of constructing the rank factorizations. Rigidity here has its usual matrix Hamming-distance meaning. Efficient conditional constructions of rigid matrices have a substantial literature. This test concerns the particular parity matrices above. [Alman and Chen on explicit rigidity](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1322297) · [Alman and Williams on probabilistic rank](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05558)

## Why easily represented parity tests cannot jointly cover the range

For the linear target use one fixed verifier. Its advice contains a framed list of binary factorizations and it accepts $x$ if every factorization evaluates to zero there and $x$ is outside the early set. Under the SAT assumption an existential test for an early name takes $N\,\mathrm{poly}(q)$ deterministic time. Guess one name and run the interpreter to $N$, then use the uniform quasilinear reduction and the solver. The verifier can use the fixed-alternation version of the dense-advice theorem directly. For a rank-$r$ matrix the two factors take $2Lr$ bits, plus framing. Parsing is bounded on every advice string; reject malformed data. At density $1/4$ the theorem gives constants $\eta,\gamma>0$ such that at large lengths every such accept set with at most $\eta N$ advice bits and density at least $1/4$ contains at least $\gamma N$ words printed by an eligible name by $T$. Decrease $\gamma$ to at most $1/4$. The verifier allows varying list counts, so these constants are fixed before the number of parity tests is chosen.

Here is the corresponding qualification for $d=2,3$. Split each half of a word into an upper prefix and $s=\lfloor q/(2+d^2\log_2 7)\rfloor$ low bits. Each pair of upper prefixes gives a $J$-by-$J$ block, $J=2^s$. A global rank-$r$ binary matrix restricts to rank at most $r$ in each block. With at most $\eta J^2$ advice bits, the verifier computes the whole block of jointly zero entries by multiplying each pair of factors. The total inner dimension across well-formed factors is $O(J)$ from the encoding size. Cap the list length at $q$, which allows every fixed number of factors eventually. Pad each multiplication to two square $J$-by-$J$ matrices and apply Strassen’s seven-product recursion over the binary field. This costs $J^{\log_2 7}\mathrm{poly}(q)$ across all factors; rank-zero factors can be omitted. The decoder computes $s$ exactly as the largest integer with $2^{2s}7^{sd^2}\le 2^q$. [Strassen’s algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02165411)

For a fixed density threshold a uniformly sampled subset of $J^2/2^a$ cells hits every sufficiently dense advised mask in a positive fraction of the sample, simultaneously, by the union bound for small enough $\eta$. Two SAT simulations decide whether a partial sample has a qualifying completion. The first tests a guessed violating advice for a supplied sample. The second tests a guessed sample completion. Recover the least qualifying sample by $J^2$ such queries. The cost is
$$
J^2 (J^{\log_2 7})^{d^2}\mathrm{poly}(q)\le N\,\mathrm{poly}(q).
$$
Every pair of upper prefixes followed in their respective halves by a sampled pair of low indices is therefore easy. The decoder takes both upper prefixes and a sample ordinal. A fixed saving $a$ pays its header and the two required bits, as in the suffix construction. If the global accept set has density $1/4$, a fixed positive fraction of blocks have density at least $1/8$. Thus at least $\gamma N$ easy words again belong to the accept set for a fixed $\gamma>0$. Here every block has its own short advice and no early-word test is required. The elementary cubic multiplication algorithm would instead give denominators $14$ and $29$. [Common sample, constants, RAM simulations and witness recovery](suffix-block-sat-exponents.md)

Now fix an integer $k$ with $1/[2(k+1)]\le\gamma/8$ and a constant number $\ell$ with $2^{-\ell}\le\gamma/8$. Fewer than $N/[2(k+1)]$ words have more than $k$ names in $F_x$. Empty fibers produce zero for every seed. On nonempty fibers of size at most $k$, a uniform seed gives a fair parity. Evaluations of a random field polynomial of degree less than $q$ at any $m\le q$ distinct indices are independent uniform field values: the first $m$ columns of the evaluation matrix form an invertible Vandermonde matrix. Taking the absolute trace gives independent fair bits. Disjoint nonempty fibers of size at most $k$ therefore give independent fair parities on any choice of up to $\lfloor q/k\rfloor$ of those fibers.

Draw $\ell$ independent polynomials. Suppose each has an approximant as in (1), and that fewer than $\gamma N/2$ small nonempty fibers have all $\ell$ parity bits zero. Choose $\epsilon\le\gamma/(8\ell)$ and $\kappa$ small enough for all $\ell$ factor pairs to fit the advice cap, globally for $d=1$ and in each block for $d>1$. More than $N/2$ words have no eligible name by $T$. The verifier accepts at least $N/2-\ell\epsilon N\ge N/4$ of them. Every accepted easy word outside the approximants' error set is a missed small fiber or has more than $k$ late names. Under the linear early-word convention any other easy word is rejected. Thus fewer than $\gamma N$ easy words can be accepted, contradicting the preceding qualification. The entire list of approximants may depend on all sampled seeds.

## Why the fraction of easy seeds is exponentially small in the seed length

Write $W$ for the number of missed small, nonempty fibers. With independent polynomial draws, their miss indicators have mean $2^{-\ell}$ and are $2h$-wise independent for $h=\lfloor q/(2k)\rfloor$. Use only counted entries if early words are omitted. In the expansion of the centered $2h$-th moment, every term with a singleton index vanishes. A surviving term involves at most $h$ different words. Each term has absolute expectation at most one. Enumeration of the distinct indices and assignments to positions gives
$$
\mathbb E(W-\mathbb EW)^{2h}\le (h+1)N^h(2h)^{2h}.
$$
Since $\mathbb EW\le\gamma N/8$, Markov's bound at $\gamma N/2$ gives a probability at most $(h+1)[(2h)^2/((3\gamma/8)^2 N)]^h=2^{-\Omega(q^2)}$. If a fraction $\beta$ of seeds admits the stated approximations, independence puts probability $\beta^\ell$ on all of them doing so. The previous paragraph puts this event inside the tail just bounded. Taking the fixed $\ell$th root proves (1). The probabilities are summable over even $q$. Under the same SAT hypothesis almost every infinite fair bit sequence, read from its beginning at each length as the coefficient bits, gives hard matrices at all sufficiently large even lengths. This last conclusion only uses the union bound on each tail. [Finite tuple count in Lean](../lean/FiberMomentTuples.lean) · [Exact small-field syndrome and moment checks](../audits/fiber_selector_syndrome_experiment.py)

Merely exhibiting an exceptional seed would carry little information. For any field polynomial $Q$ of degree at most $\lfloor(q-1)/2\rfloor$, $P=Q^2+Q$ is an allowed selector and has trace zero at every index. The kernel of the map on formal polynomials consists of the two binary constants. These seeds alone give a soft fraction at least $2^{-(1/2+o(1))q^2}$. The distinction between an exceptional example and the frequency in (1) is essential.

## A single hard power in every short progression

There is a stronger test that only weights each name by one monomial. Write $m_0=|K^\times|=2^{q-1}-1$. Under the $O(n^d)$ SAT hypothesis there are fixed $w,\kappa',\epsilon',c'>0$, where $w$ is an integer, with this property at all large even lengths. **In every length-$w$ progression of exponents modulo $m_0$ with step coprime to $m_0$, some exponent $e$ has the following property:** for all but a fraction $2^{-c'q}$ of scalars $a\in K$, the single matrix
$$
x\longmapsto \operatorname{Tr}\!\left(a\sum_{i\in F_x} i^e\right)
$$
cannot be approximated to $\epsilon' N$ counted errors at rank $\kappa' J_d$. The constants are uniform over the start and step, even if these are chosen nonuniformly after inspecting the program outputs. Thus a possible positive construction could pick its *own* exceptionally simple run of powers. For example, runs of consecutive exponents of unbounded length at infinitely many $q$, each exponent in the run having a $2^{-o(q)}$ fraction of scalars with rank $o(J_d)$ and error $o(N)$ (uniformly along the runs), would suffice.

Take $w=2k$ with $k$ fixed as above. For a progression starting at $b_0$ with step $s_0$, a random weighted combination of its powers acts on the name $i$ as $i^{b_0}Q(i^{s_0})$, where the $w$ coefficients of $Q$ are uniform independent field elements. The nonzero index convention matters: the map $i\mapsto i^{s_0}$ is injective on names and $i^{b_0}$ is a nonzero multiplier. Any $2k$ evaluations are therefore independent and uniform by Vandermonde, including for exponents wrapping around the modulus. With $\ell$ independent draws the miss indicators of different small fibers are pairwise independent. Hence $\operatorname{Var}(W)\le N$ and $\Pr[W\ge\gamma N/2]\le C/N$ by Chebyshev. The same deterministic contradiction applies whenever all $\ell$ weighted sums have good approximants. For each exponent $e$ let $p_e$ be the fraction of scalars admitting approximate rank $\kappa J_d/w$ with error $\epsilon N/w$. If all coefficients are such scalars, adding the $w$ approximants yields rank at most $\kappa J_d$ and at most $\epsilon N$ errors for their weighted sum. Independence gives $(\prod_{e}p_e)^\ell\le C/N$, forcing some $p_e\le(C/N)^{1/(w\ell)}$. This argument uses only pairwise independence between bounded fibers and proves the stated single-power bound.

I tried to realize a run by putting all possible $i^e$ into small subfields. If these values lie in a binary space of dimension $d_e$, at least $2^{-d_e}$ of scalars annihilate them identically. In fact the binary span of the multiplicative subgroup $(K^\times)^e$ is the subfield it generates: the span contains one, is closed under multiplication and is a finite integral domain. At every sufficiently large even $q$ a primitive prime divisor $p$ of $2^{q-1}-1$ has $\operatorname{ord}_p(2)=q-1$. If $p$ does not divide $e$, the subgroup of powers still has order divisible by $p$ and cannot lie in a proper subfield. A unit-step progression of fixed length has at most one exponent divisible by $p$, since $p\ge q$. Thus every other exponent has full field span. The arithmetic construction fails even when the start and step are tuned separately at each length. [Zsigmondy’s primitive-divisor theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01692444)

Restricting to **actual** output fibers could lower those span dimensions. There is a useful counting check. For the progression above, the vectors $(\sum_{i\in F_x}i^e)_e$ are distinct for different nonempty fibers of size at most $k$. Subtract two vectors: their disjoint union contains at most $2k$ indices, and the scaled Vandermonde matrix rules out the zero syndrome. Consequently $M_0$ such counted fibers force the sum of the binary dimensions of their coordinate spans to be at least $\log_2 M_0$. A small span in every coordinate would already require few distinct small fibers. Low *matrix rank* can still occur when the scalar values span the whole field, as the inverse calculation below illustrates for separated products.

## Lower-degree tests and a positive calculation

The constant-size-fiber argument has a deterministic version. For the first $k$ field power sums $C_j(x)=\sum_{i\in F_x} i^j$, $0\le j<k$ (use $i^0=1$), every nonempty fiber of size at most $k$ has some $C_j(x)\ne0$ by Vandermonde. For the linear goal, if all these field matrices have approximate rank at most $o(L/q)$ on all but $o(N)$ counted entries along common lengths, their factorizations cost $o(N)$ bits for fixed $k$. Accepting jointly zero entries again gives a contradiction once $k$ exceeds the constant threshold. Equivalently, a candidate construction for every fixed $k$, at arbitrarily large common lengths for its first $k$ matrices, suffices. Over the binary program bits there is another deterministic test. If all squarefree monomial parities through degree $e$ vanish on a nonempty fiber, that fiber has at least $2^{e+1}$ names. Split on a coordinate that actually varies; the two slices both have vanishing moments through $e-1$. This bound is sharp on an $(e+1)$-dimensional coordinate face. It explains why a single unweighted parity can erase an entire image of paired names. [Minimum-distance argument in Lean](../lean/SparseFiberMoments.lean)

I tried to prove the required low ranks by parametrizing the producing names in reverse, as functions of the row and column of the output. Suppose one distinct inverse name on a domain $D$ is represented by a field-valued matrix $I$ of rank $r$ over $K$, where only its values in $D$ matter. Suppose the indicator of $D$ has binary rank $s$. For every polynomial of degree less than $q$, its contribution $1_D\operatorname{Tr}P(I)$ has binary rank at most
$$
s(q-1)(1+r)^{\lceil\log_2 q\rceil}.
$$
Frobenius preserves the rank bound on entrywise powers $I^{\circ 2^j}$. Hadamard multiplication multiplies rank bounds, by expanding rank-one tensors. Expanding $P$ and summing $r^{\operatorname{popcount}(j)}$ over exponents below the next power of two gives the display before the trace and domain factors. A field rank-one matrix has trace of binary rank at most $q-1$ by expanding one factor in a binary basis. Contributions of distinct inverse branches add over $\mathbb F_2$, so their rank bounds add. A common exceptional set of $o(N)$ output cells can be discarded. A decomposition for which these bounds sum to $o(J_d)$ would suffice, even if the factors and domains depend on the length.

For example, a native branch on two nonzero $m$-bit field elements prints $(y,z)=(ab,a^2b)$ in two equally padded halves. Choose fixed padding to pay for the native compiler. Its domain of unpadded nonzero coordinates is a rectangle. In reverse, $a=z/y$, $b=y^2/z$. Packing the two recovered elements into the ordinary name index is a constant plus two binary-linear embeddings into $K$. Each packed field product has rank at most $m$ over $K$: expand the row-dependent factor in a binary basis and apply the corresponding embedding to each basis element times the column-dependent factor. The inverse name matrix thus has rank at most $2m+1$. For this branch every selected parity has rank $2^{O((\log q)^2)}$ with $q=2m+O(1)$. In the second example of the preceding image-cover note, even a choice among many pointwise separation rules failed on the forward map $(ab,a^2b)$. Here we count this native branch on its specified inputs and can take $\tau=0$. Other branches can print the same words. No low-rank decomposition of their combined parity matrices has been obtained.
