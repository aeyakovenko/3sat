# Detecting a gap in succinct two-occurrence checks by estimating a matching

The [mixed parity and clause theorem](two-occurrence-parity-clauses.md) detects constant gaps by naming individual rows. Its three-answer example has constant gap and requires linearly many names. A different test still detects the gap if the rows can be generated from their indices by a small circuit. It estimates the size of a matching without constructing the graph.

**Priority consequence.** Suppose SAT has a deterministic polynomial-time algorithm. Fix a circuit-description exponent $d$, a rational $\delta>0$, and a finite library of constant-size matching gadgets as defined below. At every sufficiently late false scale $j$ of either sign, every entailed selection of at most one signed library test per PCP seed, generated by a circuit of description length at most $(j+1)^d$ and using each address in at most two tests, has a single proof table violating strictly fewer than $\delta 2^r$ selected tests. Here $r$ is the verifier's randomness and inactive seeds contribute zero violations. Tests may be of different types. In particular this applies to mixtures of parity equations, binary clauses and signed exact-one predicates of bounded arity. No generator for a constant-gap selection on these false scales has been found. The conclusion also holds with a fixed number of slots per seed by including the slot in the index and rescaling $\delta$.

## A reduction that counts defects

Represent a predicate on $q$ bits by a finite undirected graph, $q$ distinct port vertices and a specified set of mandatory vertices. For a tuple, remove the port vertex of each bit set to one. The tuple passes exactly when the remaining graph has a matching covering every remaining mandatory vertex. Signs can be chosen individually on each literal.

* For prescribed parity use a clique on the ports, with zero or one extra vertex, all mandatory. Choose the total vertex-count parity to be the required parity.
* For exactly one use the complete bipartite graph between $q$ ports and $q-1$ internal vertices, all mandatory. For an empty clause use a single isolated mandatory vertex.
* For a clause on two signed bits use their falsity indicators as ports. Join the two mandatory ports to each of two internal vertices, one mandatory and one optional. The gadget accepts exactly when zero or one port is removed.
* Pins and conjunctions on disjoint port sets follow by isolated vertices (a mandatory port forces removal, an edge from a mandatory internal vertex forces its neighbor to stay) and disjoint union. An unconstrained unused port can be an isolated optional vertex.

The reduction of two-occurrence exact SAT to matching, including a double-copy construction for singletons, predates this application. General matching representations and Boolean edge CSPs have also been studied. [Prior matching reduction, Section 3.1](https://www.brics.dk/DS/04/5/BRICS-DS-04-5.pdf) · [Kazda, Kolmogorov and Rolínek](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03124)

Make one gadget per selected row. For two equally signed occurrences of the same address, add an edge between their ports. Selecting this edge consumes both. For opposite signs, introduce a mandatory vertex adjacent to both ports, whose chosen edge consumes exactly one. For a sole occurrence, join its port to a fresh optional vertex. Every matching covering all mandatory vertices in this graph decodes to a satisfying assignment, and conversely. Take two identical copies of the graph. Join each optional vertex to its counterpart by a new edge. A satisfying assignment now yields a perfect matching by duplicating the same witness and matching every remaining optional vertex across copies.

Let $D$ be the minimum number of failed rows. Every matching in the double graph leaves at least $2D$ vertices unmatched. To prove this, remove cross-copy edges and work on each copy. Interpret selected wire edges as proposed literal values. Equal signs agree, and a matched opposite-sign midpoint gives complementary values. At an unmatched opposite-sign midpoint choose one of its two previously unconsumed ports to have value one. This change can spoil at most one row. Any row untouched by these changes and with no uncovered mandatory gadget vertex passes by its internal matching. Thus the number of failed rows in the recovered assignment is at most the number of uncovered mandatory gadget vertices plus the number of uncovered mandatory midpoints in that copy. These vertices were also uncovered in the double graph, since only optional vertices have cross edges. Sum over the copies.

Pad the graph to exactly $2Km$ vertices for $m=2^r$ seed slots and a library constant $K$ which can be a power of two. Give each slot enough vertices for the gadget, singleton endpoints and midpoints owned by the lesser of the two occurrences. Unused slots are optional isolated vertices joined to their mirrors. The graph has bounded degree, and a uniform vertex is sampled by a seed, slot and copy. Its maximum matching size is $Km$ in the satisfiable case and at most $Km-\delta m$ when $D\ge\delta m$.

## Obtaining a deterministic signal from short metadata

Given a putative generator, verify its well-formedness, distinct row addresses and local entailment for every seed, and the two-occurrence bound for every address. A counterexample consists of one invalid seed, or three distinct active seeds exhibiting a shared address. Each is a polynomial-size witness in $j$, since local PCP work is $\operatorname{poly}(j)$. Under the SAT hypothesis these checks are deterministic polynomial time in $j$. The same search with a prefix restriction finds the other occurrence of a queried port, if present, in polynomial time in $j$. This supplies local graph adjacency even for an inverse address map that was never supplied by the generator. [Uniform PCP at the signed scale](low-randomness-free-winner.md)

On bounded-degree graphs, the maximum matching size has a randomized additive $\epsilon |V|$ estimate using constantly many adjacency queries for every fixed $\epsilon>0$. Take $\epsilon<\delta/(5K)$ so the estimated ranges in the two promised cases are separated. The published constant-expected-work version can be amplified and truncated at a fixed multiple of its expectation. Uniform vertex names have $O(r)$ bits. Random local order comparisons can use fresh random ranks on demand, with $O(r+1)$ bits per rank and negligible collision probability after truncation. Thus the tester has a fixed polynomial time and randomness bound in $j$. [Nguyen and Onak](https://onak.pl/download/publications/Nguyen_Onak-constant_time_approximation.pdf)

On invalid generators deterministically reject. For valid generators the tester recognizes the promise of a constant gap versus full satisfiability with bounded error. The usual translation of a bounded-error computation into two polynomially bounded quantifiers separates these promised cases even if intermediate gaps have arbitrary outcomes. Under $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$, which follows from the assumed SAT algorithm, these quantifiers collapse to deterministic polynomial time. Existentially choose the circuit description within the fixed bound and collapse that quantifier as well. On a true claim no valid generator can trigger the resulting deterministic signal. On any false claim with a $\delta$-far valid selection some generator triggers it. Its $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ running time is $o(2^j)$, contradicting the [priority bi-immunity condition](priority-pinned-sat.md) if triggered infinitely often. This argument establishes the consequence and does not require uniformly constructing the witness circuits. [Lautemann](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3)

**Size of the missing selector.** Give the decision procedure the circuit allowance $s$ in unary and the scale data in binary. For a fixed library and gap, every deterministic step above, the bounded-error simulation on promised inputs, and the outer guess run in $(s+j+1)^C$ after collapse under the hypothesized polynomial-time SAT algorithm, for some fixed $C$. The collapse is uniform in $s$. The circuit itself may vary arbitrarily with the scale. A tester on short scale metadata can construct its own unary allowance $s=\lfloor 2^{\eta j}\rfloor$, taking $O(s+\operatorname{poly}(j))$ time, for a fixed positive rational $\eta<1/C$. Its complete decision time is $o(2^j)$. Consequently no valid gap-preserving selector with description length at most $2^{\eta j}$ exists on any sufficiently late false scale. This is a circuit-description lower bound for the selector under the SAT hypothesis. A selector may fail to exist even without a size bound.

Under **linear-time SAT**, the forbidden description length can be raised to $\lfloor 2^j/(j+1)^B\rfloor$ for a fixed $B$. Here the representation is a straight-line Boolean circuit in gate order, with binary predecessor names, described in $s$ bits. On the native $O(j)$-bit-word machine it takes $(s+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ steps to check the encoding and evaluate it on any one seed, for $s\le2^j$. The counterexample search for validity and every individual inverse-neighbor query have a nondeterministic computation with that time bound; all quantified seed or address names have length $\operatorname{poly}(j)$. Each bounded computation can be compiled into SAT with only a $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ overhead in encoded bits and construction time since its log clock and log input length are $O(j+\log(j+2))$. Reading the supplied selector can be charged inside this bound. The linear SAT hypothesis restores a deterministic $(s+1)\operatorname{poly}(j)$ bound for the calls. At most $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ calls suffice, including prefix searches. The matching test uses a fixed number of queries after truncation, uniformly in $s$; its random tape has $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ bits because all vertex, port and random rank names refer to the $O(2^r)$-vertex graph. Lautemann amplification, the shift lists and verifying a list of translated outcomes require only $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ such tapes and evaluations. Eliminate each of the two quantifier blocks in turn by the bounded-run encoding, deterministic complementation, and linear SAT. This incurs another $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ factor at each of a fixed number of levels. Finally guess the selector's $s$ bits and eliminate this last nondeterministic computation in the same manner. The resulting uniform detector takes $(s+1)(j+1)^{B_0}$ time for a constant $B_0$ independent of $s,j$. Taking $B>B_0+1$ puts it below the priority clock, proving the claim. [SAT slowdown and encoded length](packed-band-transfer.md) · [Explicit shift accounting](trained-free-games.md)

An occurrence-count consequence needs no short contradictory subset. Suppose an efficiently generated family already uses one signed gadget from the library per seed and has gap $\delta$ on the false scales of interest. For each address retain its lexicographically first two port occurrences; skip any row with a discarded occurrence. With $H=\sum_x(\deg(x)-2)_+$ this discards at most $H$ rows. Under the SAT hypothesis the retention rule is computable in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ for a uniform generator running in $\operatorname{poly}(j)$: the existence of two earlier occurrences has a short witness. Thus circuits of size $\operatorname{poly}(j)$ implement the pruned selection. At every sufficiently late such false scale $H>(\delta-\epsilon)2^r$ for every fixed $0<\epsilon<\delta$ (with the threshold depending on $\epsilon$), since otherwise the selector would retain gap at least $\epsilon$ with two occurrences. This estimate assumes local implications of the original PCP on every seed.

## Three accepting tuples and fanout

For any relation with at most three accepting tuples, the nonempty relation satisfies the delta-matroid symmetric exchange axiom exactly when every two tuples differ in at most two bits. If $u,v$ have distance at least three, an exchange from $u$ towards any differing coordinate could only reach a third tuple. Its distance from $u$ is at most two and its support of differences cannot contain all the required coordinates. Conversely, at distance one or two an exchange can go directly to $v$. For three distinct tuples let $a$ be their coordinatewise majority. Their supports of difference from $a$ are disjoint. Pairwise distance at most two implies each has size at most one, since at most one support can be empty. After pins and sign changes the relation is consequently either exactly-one on three bits or at-most-one on two. Two accepted tuples a distance at most two apart give pins and possibly a free bit or a parity equality. Hence **every** symmetric-exchange choice of up to three local answers in the two-occurrence regime falls under the matching estimate, including tests on more than three ports.

Degree-two gadgets over symmetric-exchange relations preserve symmetric exchange on exposed ports, each occurring in at most one row. One can take products and then identify private coordinate pairs, as in Definition 47 of Kazda, Kolmogorov and Rolínek. Equivalently, start with full solutions extending two terminal tuples and flip a differing terminal toward the second solution. Use local symmetric exchange to restore the affected row, flipping at most one further still-different coordinate. A private coordinate can transfer the defect to only one neighboring row. Every flip moves an incidence toward the second solution, so the walk stops, changing at most one further terminal. The projected relation therefore cannot be the three-way equality $\{000,111\}$. Parities, binary clauses and signed splits all satisfy exchange. This rules out an exact gadget that copies one proof bit to three exposed ports within these occurrence restrictions. The closure and the obstruction to fanout are prior work. [Definition 47 and the following paragraph](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03124)

This leaves a concrete alternative for a gap-preserving compilation of the actual signed computation. Such a compilation into these local constraints would have to address proof-bit reuse in a way different from a network of exact equality copies. The short-core barrier itself permits a constant-query statistical detector on succinct matching instances.
