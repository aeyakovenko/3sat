# Fitting three columns with one late generator

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** For all sufficiently large $J$, every binary matrix on $k=\lfloor0.2883J\rfloor$ rows and $J$ columns can be approximated with binary rank at most $\lfloor J/16\rfloor$ and fewer than $J^2/16$ errors. The construction fits a horizontal slab, adds generators fitted to disjoint triples of columns, then uses the whole column space on the remainder. It bounds where the first nonzero row of the canonical rigid matrix can occur. The generator and message searches here have no near-quadratic time guarantee. [Canonical matrix and fixed-block covering argument](lex-factor-responses.md)

Write $H$ for binary entropy in bits. Let $k/J\to\lambda$, $r/J\to\rho$, and reserve $t=\lfloor\tau k\rfloor$ late generators and two further generators for translations. Set $r_0=r-t-2$, $R=\rho/\lambda$, $R_0=R-\tau>0$. Fit $\alpha J+o(J)$ early columns with $\alpha=A(\rho-\lambda\tau)$ and $A>1$. Choose radii $s,d,e$ with
$$
H(s)>1-1/A,\qquad
1-R<H(d)<1-R_0,\qquad
H(e)+e\log_2 3>2(1-R_0),
$$
where $s,d<1/2$, $e<3/4$, and $\alpha+3\lambda\tau\le1$. Then the error divided by $kJ$ is at most
$$
\alpha s+\lambda\tau e+(1-\alpha-3\lambda\tau)d+o(1).
$$

**The early generators.** By the binary linear sphere-covering bound, fix a constant-length row code on $B$ coordinates, of dimension at most $B/A$ and radius at most $sB$. Divide the early columns into horizontal blocks of $B$ and round each row independently. The coefficient columns of these rounded rows form at most $r_0$ early generator vectors $g_j$ in $\mathbb F_2^k$, after rounding the slab width down to a whole number of blocks. Pad with zeros to $r_0$. Every slab column uses only its block's $O(B)$ generators. Independently flip each bit of each generator with probability $p=k^{-1/3}$ to obtain a generator map $G:\mathbb F_2^{r_0}\to\mathbb F_2^k$ with image $C$. Each fitted slab bit changes with probability at most $Bp$, yielding $o(kJ)$ additional errors with probability tending to one, by Markov. All randomness follows the deterministic choice of the original matrix and its row-code approximants. [Proof of the fixed-block sphere-covering bound](lex-factor-responses.md)

In either the early or the late message space of dimension $m=\Theta(k)$ take a linear subspace $L$ of dimension $m-o(k)$ whose nonzero vectors have weight at least $k^{2/3}$. A random parity check with $O(k^{2/3}\log k)$ rows avoids all lower-weight nonzero vectors by a union bound. Under independent flips of the generator bits at any fixed centers, each nonzero $u\in L$ has $Gu$ pointwise within a multiplicative $1\pm\epsilon_k$ of uniform, with $\epsilon_k=\exp(-\Omega(k^{1/3}))$. For distinct nonzero $u,v\in L$ the pair $(Gu,Gv)$ has the same pointwise estimate relative to independent uniform vectors. At any coordinate the three nontrivial parity biases are at most $(1-2p)^{k^{2/3}}$ in absolute value. Fourier inversion on two bits and independence across coordinates give the bound, absorbing the factor $k$ in $\epsilon_k$.

**Three columns.** For a triple $a,b,c$ take the two residuals $a+b+z_1+Gu$ and $a+c+z_2+Gv$, where $u,v\in L$. The translations $z_1,z_2$ are independent uniform $k$-bit columns, independent of $G$ and shared across *all* triples. Count the message pairs for which the two residuals, viewed as a four-symbol word, have each nonzero symbol exactly $j=\lfloor ek/3\rfloor$ times. The normalized size $v_4$ of this type is $2^{k(H(e)+e\log_2 3-2)+o(k)}$. With $M=|L|$, the mean count is exactly $M^2v_4$, exponentially large under the stated slack. Indeed the two translations make the first tested residual pair uniformly distributed even conditional on all of $G$.

For two tested message pairs, their two differences in $L$ have rank zero, one, or two. Rank two gives joint hit probability at most $(1+\epsilon_k)v_4^2$. The first residual pair is independent of the image of the differences by the uniform translations. Rank zero is the same tested pair. There are at most $3M$ rank-one neighbors of each pair. In rank one, one nonzero binary projection of the two residuals is preserved, and on its fiber the displacement is pointwise near-uniform. The projection of a uniform word of the chosen type is uniform on the $\binom{k}{2j}$ words of weight $2j$, by coordinate symmetry. Consequently the joint hit probability is at most
$$
(1+\epsilon_k)v_4^2\,2^k/\binom{k}{2j}.
$$
The rank-one contribution divided by squared mean tends to zero exponentially, since $H(2e/3)\ge[H(e)+e\log_2 3]/2>1-R_0$. The first inequality is entropy subadditivity for the two binary marginals of the four-symbol law. The diagonal contribution is the reciprocal of the mean. The second moment therefore gives failure probability $O(\epsilon_k)+2^{-\Omega(k)}$, enough for all $t$ triples by a union bound. The four-symbol sphere threshold and balanced-type dependency argument are established in generalized covering-code theory. Here the early generators have fixed input-dependent centers and sparse perturbations. [Li, Shangguan and Wei, 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24856)

For a hit, choose a late column $g$ as the coordinatewise majority of $a$, $b+z_1+Gu$, and $c+z_2+Gv$. Approximate the three original columns by $g$, $g+z_1+Gu$, and $g+z_2+Gv$. A row uses exactly one error when its two residuals are not both zero and zero otherwise. Total errors are $3j\le ek$. Each new generator may now receive fresh independent flips at rate $p$, causing at most three extra errors per flipped bit, $o(kJ)$ in total with probability tending to one.

**Remaining columns.** Put $v_2=2^{-k}\sum_{i\le dk}\binom ki$. The early space has radius-$dk$ neighborhood of normalized size at least $Mv_2/2$ with probability tending to one. Indeed the sum of the individual ball masses at the early subcode messages is $Mv_2$. Each distinct pair intersects in expected normalized mass at most $(1+\epsilon_k)v_2^2$, by near-uniformity of the difference and averaging over translations. Since $Mv_2$ is exponentially small, Markov and the first two inclusion-exclusion terms give the bound. This property holds simultaneously with the slab and triple estimates.

Condition on good early generators, translations and chosen majority columns. Only the independent fresh flips on the late generators are now random. A late separated message subspace has $M'=2^{t-o(k)}$ elements. For each nonzero message its image has probability within a factor $1\pm\epsilon_k$ of the early neighborhood mass to land in any translate of that neighborhood. For distinct nonzero late messages use the joint pointwise estimate. The expected hit count is at least $(1-\epsilon_k)(M'-1)Mv_2/2$, exponentially large because $H(d)>1-R$. By the second moment each remaining target column misses with probability $O(\epsilon_k)+2^{-\Omega(k)}$. A union bound suffices for all $J$ target columns. The two translations and the two generator layers together use at most $r$ dimensions.

**A certified point.** Take the terminating decimals
$$
\rho=1/16,\quad \lambda=0.2883,\quad \tau=0.032,\quad A=1.516,\quad
s=0.0634,\quad d=0.2331,\quad e=0.4122 .
$$
The four positive margins $H(s)+1/A-1$, $H(d)+R-1$, $1-R_0-H(d)$ and $H(e)+e\log_2 3-2(1-R_0)$ exceed $0.000426$, $0.000164$, $0.03183$ and $0.000538$, respectively. These can be checked using rational arithmetic by writing each positive argument $x=2^m y$ with $1\le y\le2$, taking $z=(y-1)/(y+1)$, and enclosing $\ln y$ between $2\sum_{j=0}^{15}z^{2j+1}/(2j+1)$ and this sum plus $2z^{33}/[33(1-z^2)]$. Apply the same bounds at $y=2$ to divide by $\ln 2$. The proposed error fraction of $J^2$ is exactly $1952743120382103/31250000000000000<1/16$ before the vanishing term.
