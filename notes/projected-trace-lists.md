# How many alternative executions can a short verifier leave open?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Consider a certificate that checks several RAM transfers together and allows auxiliary labels. Even if the true run passes the checks, many other transcripts can pass as well. I measured the cost of this ambiguity by counting distinct projected transcripts. Under a near-linear SAT algorithm, a polynomial-time nondeterministic tape machine with sublinear-power space and advice that includes the real rejecting transcript among its possibilities must also permit at least $2^{N^{1-o(1)}}$ distinct transcripts on a solver-dependent family. Here $N$ counts encoded formula bits. The statement below gives the uniform rate. Different auxiliary labels with exactly the same projection count just once.

There is also an unconditional tradeoff. Fix the polynomial time cap on the tape machine. For every deterministic sound UNSAT recognizer that signals on every unsatisfiable formula, there is an $\epsilon>0$ for which rejection time times the smallest combined cost of advice, tape space and logarithmic list size is outside $O(N^{1+\epsilon})$ on unsatisfiable inputs, with ones added to the two factors. The proof gives a positive $\epsilon$ depending on fixed machines and the tape clock. A linear RAM can use enough space or generate enough alternatives to satisfy these conclusions.

## The projected transcript

Use the [packed RAM and signed bounded computation formulas](packed-band-transfer.md). Number instruction rows from zero. Each row has fixed fields for controller, input and writable memory events, and the post-controller, in the native within-instruction order. There are constantly many slots and $O(\log N)$ bits per row for a polynomial-length trace. Fix all inactive fields to zero. Supply a time horizon $E$ in binary. The final controller must signal rejection. The first and consecutive controllers, decoded instructions, immutable input accesses, and local transitions have their usual checks. A halting trajectory can be padded to a fixed valid clock in the near-linear assertion. For a stopping-time assertion use the first signal and its terminal controller. The representation convention covers a signal at initialization.

Use one fixed clocked nondeterministic multitape interpreter. It receives the actual formula read-only, the horizon, a bitstring $z$ containing program and advice, and a workspace cap $s$. Head positions on polynomial-length read-only inputs cost $O(\log N)$ bits to describe. Enforce a fixed clock $N^C$ and $s$ work bits on the interpreted computation, with its ordinary write-only stream of output records. Equivalently use a fixed transducer and put only its data in $z$. Universal tape simulation and its counters cost $O(s+\log N)$ bits and polynomial time. Each record has an instruction index and a candidate row. Treat the output of a successful branch as a **multiset** of such records. Order of emission can vary by branch. Repeated, absent and out-of-range rows are allowed in that multiset and can fail validation later. Only successful branches enter the family. The output alphabet is polynomial in $N$ for the fixed row layout. A fixed-bit stream implementation checks formatting and emits a reserved error symbol for an ill-formed terminated record. Polynomially many words of local auxiliary labels can be generated and discarded during a run.

Let $Y$ be the multiset containing the real row at every prescribed time. For $(z,s)$ with $Y$ among the outputs, write $K$ for the number of **different** multisets from successful branches. All such $K$ are at least one. Let
$$
R_C(F,E)=1+\min_{z,s}\left(|z|+s+\lceil\log_2 K\rceil\right).
$$
Use $+\infty$ if the family of presentations is empty. Constants for fixed-word fields and optional signal conventions can be fixed before the verifier is built. The statistic includes networks whose public directory prescribes introductions and forgettings of log and auxiliary variables in a path decomposition. Guess a label at introduction, check a local relation while all its variables coexist and forget the label at its last use. Emit each block's rows once. The working space is the simultaneous bit length of the live labels plus actual checker scratch and schedule bookkeeping. Multiple valid auxiliary extensions may represent the same RAM history. The directory, local predicate descriptions and any nonuniform transition data go in $z$. A relation with many incident transfers can be checked at once when its charged time and memory permit. This argument also covers a tape transducer with no supplied graph.

## Testing a single labeling for a memory defect

The following is a nondeterministic polynomial-time tape test with $O(\log N)$ extra bits over generation. Guess a type of native defect and its indices before simulating the transducer. For a format defect, check a reserved error, a malformed index or row, or count up to two occurrences of a chosen required index. Zero or two occurrences expose missing or duplicate rows. For a controller defect, retain the indicated row or two consecutive indicated rows and compare after emission. To test a writable read, guess its timestamp (instruction and slot) and address. Retain its claimed returned word and require that this read actually occurs at that address. Scan every emitted store to this address with a strictly smaller timestamp. Keep the maximum such stamp and its word, defaulting to zero when none occurs. Compare the two words. This includes earlier stores in the same instruction. The order of arrival of the rows has no effect when there is one per time. If there are duplicates or absent rows, the format test already detects them. The CPU check recomputes each chosen transition from its claimed replies and the real formula. Any supplementary requirement that a particular signal be the first can be challenged by an earlier signal. With all tests passing, induction along native time gives exactly the genuine run with the stated signal.

For any imposed filter, make **two separate queries** using the same generating rule and filter. The first asks if some successful output passes. The second asks if some successful output passes and has a native defect. Require the first answer to be yes and the second to be no. One accepted projection then exists and is a genuine rejecting run. No coupling between distinct auxiliary labelings is needed. Requiring only the absence of a defective output would allow an empty presentation to certify anything. The Lean file checks the permutation invariance of the maximum timestamp, concatenation of scans, the nonempty-filter implication, the finite union bound on excluded keys and the use of a separating key on projections. ## Isolating the genuine projection

Choose a power of two $B>N^C+1$. Number possible record symbols from zero through a polynomial bound, including the error symbol. Encode a multiset $U$ by the integer $a_U=\sum_{r\in U}B^{\operatorname{index}(r)}$ with repetitions. Even malformed outputs have multiplicity less than $B$, so this is injective on all outputs in the clock. Every $a_U$ has at most $J=N^{O(1)}$ binary digits. A fixed nonzero $|a_U-a_Y|$ has at most $J$ distinct prime divisors. Among the first $1+(K-1)J$ primes there is one $p$ avoiding all differences with $Y$. The elementary prime-count estimates used in string fingerprinting give $O(\log(K+1)+\log N)$ bits for $p$. Give $p$ and $a_Y\bmod p$ as a filter. On an arbitrary emission order, accumulate the residue using repeated modular squaring for $B^{\operatorname{index}(r)}$. This costs $O(\log p+\log N)$ work bits and polynomial time within polynomial bit caps. No branch computes $K$, generates this list of primes, or computes the target residue from an external copy of $Y$. An honest certificate can choose the filter after seeing the execution. On dishonest guesses any $p\ge2$ is sound for the two queries. [Karp on string fingerprinting, Section 5](https://users.ece.northwestern.edu/~nickle/randAlg/Karp91.pdf)

Suppose now that a fixed deterministic RAM decides SAT in $O(N\log^a N)$ for fixed $a\ge0$. Let $F_x$ encode acceptance of the bounded signed source on $n_0$ input bits with clock $H=n_0^2$. Its packed formula has $N=\Theta(H\log^3 H)$ bits. The tape predicates can compute every formula symbol from $x$ with polynomial time and polylogarithmic work space. Their physical input consists of $x$, the directory and filter, and binary caps. On an honest filter supplied above, each predicate has input length plus workspace $O(n_0+R_C(F_x,E)+\operatorname{polylog}N)$ and polynomial time. Enforce honest fixed interpreter bounds on the generating stage in *both* checks. Use fixed, sufficiently large polynomial clocks and space overheads for the monitoring stage, so it can always see a possible defect on a generated branch. The true solver horizon is polynomial. The outer verifier need only try polynomial bit caps, as any cheaper presentation already fits.

Under the assumed SAT bound, the deterministic bounded tape reachability algorithm takes time at most the input-plus-space term multiplied by $2^{O(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})}$ here. The original proof for linear SAT incurs a fixed polylogarithmic cost per eliminated block. Changing $a$ changes the constant in this exponent. Run this computation twice and test the two signs above. Guessing or reading all the data is within the same bound. Encode this fixed verifier in the signed source, padded to each sufficiently large $n_0$. Any success by that deadline would simultaneously make its own $F_x$ satisfiable and certify its genuine rejection, so $F_x$ is unsatisfiable. Its real rejecting run has no such timely certificate. Absorbing the polylogarithmic conversion from $N$ to $H$ into the constant gives
$$
 R_C(F_x,E)\ \ge\ N\,2^{-D\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}}
$$
for a fixed $D$ and every sufficiently large source padding length. In particular, for $|z|+s\le N^\theta$ with fixed $\theta<1$, the number of alternative projections is at least $2^{N\,2^{-O(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})}}$. All fixed polynomial clock bounds used in one assertion are chosen before $D$. [Accelerated reachability](linear-quantifiers.md) · [Conversion to SAT and source width](packed-band-transfer.md)

## Where the alternatives first go wrong

Take presentations in which each output has one row per time, respects the initial condition, CPU transitions, immutable input and continuity of controllers, and gives the required final signal. It may obtain wrong replies on writable reads. Within each chronological block enforce each local store and cache the first incoming word at each address until overwritten. A path presentation can take the genuine boundary controllers as public data for these tests, paying $O(P\log N)$ for $P$ blocks. On the promise that the genuine log is also present, any different log has a first wrong reply. Just before it, the entire prefix, effective address and instruction slot agree with the actual run. Local consistency puts it at a first incoming read of its block.

Let $D_0$ contain the actual read timestamps at which some admitted log diverges for the first time. Store those times and their true words as a filter and require the candidate to agree at all of them. Under the one-row-per-time convention a scan of the public directory at emission suffices; polynomial time and $O(\log N)$ extra tape bits suffice. This kills every false projection, wherever its later rows might go. Thus on the hard family
$$
 |z|+s+(1+|D_0|)\log N\ \ge\ N\,2^{-O(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})}.
$$
The same argument applies to any other set of genuine timed replies that distinguishes the real log from every alternative. It still applies after supplying further cheap constraints and genuinely observed values, with their descriptions charged to the directory. The alternatives may differ at many subsequent reads. First divergence only certifies the genuine prefix up to the one selected read. [First-difference isolation in Lean](../lean/ProjectedTraceLists.lean)

## Probability of outputting the actual run

A randomized transducer supplied with independent fresh bits can put different masses on its possible outputs. Let $p_*>0$ be its unconditional probability of success *and* projection equal to $Y$, within the same clock and space caps. Write $u=\lceil\log_2(1/p_*)\rceil$. Then
$$
 |z|+(s+u+\log N)\log N\ \ge\ N\,2^{-O(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})}.
$$
For a proof it suffices to consider $s,u,|z|$ below $N$, and polynomially many coin positions $M$. Choose a random prime among the first $16J\,2^u$ primes and test the multiset residue against the genuine residue, also testing success. This is only an analysis of a distinguisher. Every actual output passes its residue test; a successful false output passes with probability at most $2^{-u}/16$ over the independent prime. For each fixed prime, the acceptance predicate on the fresh sequential coin flips is a length-$M$ read-once branching program of log width $O(s+u+\log N)$, with $F_x,z$ and the target residue fixed. Include the simulation clock, tape-head positions and residue in a configuration. Deterministic work between coin flips can be folded into the nonuniform layer transitions. Pad unused random positions.

Nisan's generator with error $2^{-u}/8$ has seed $O((s+u+\log N)\log N)$ for this class. For **each** prime, its probability of acceptance is at least $p_*-2^{-u}/8$. Average over primes again. On the generator's seed distribution all false outputs together contribute at most $2^{-u}/16$ to this average. Some seed therefore actually produces $Y$. This averaging accounts for repeated rows even when emission order depends on the seed. In the usual recursive construction each output block is obtained by composing at most $O(\log M)$ seed-supplied pairwise-independent hashes on $O(s+u+\log N)$ bits. Binary Toeplitz matrices with a random shift give such hashes with linear stored seed per level and quadratic bit evaluation time. Evaluating indices successively uses at most linear-in-seed scratch and polynomial time in $N$. Supply the one good seed and generator parameters in a deterministic presentation. It has list size one. Include this fixed polynomial-clock deterministic mode when choosing the common hard family. [Nisan's PRG and its parameters](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2020.25)

## What happens to the verdict under speculative reads?

Consider a different presentation with a **coverage guarantee on satisfiable inputs**. A fixed tape machine generates possible *accepting* logs up to the solver's valid worst-case clock. For every legal value of its public parameters and every satisfiable input, some successful branch within the charged clock and space emits the genuine accepting log. For example, execute the solver's instructions, supply arbitrary work-memory read replies, check the genuine immutable input, and output a candidate only if it accepts. Tracked subsets of times or addresses can be arbitrary parameters if the generator always enforces true memory there and leaves all genuine paths possible. One may compose the generator with any fixed polynomial-time streaming test with this same one-sided coverage guarantee. An arbitrary unverified choice of a predicate that rejects all logs would violate the guarantee. Count the bits in all nonuniform parameters, and charge the actual workspace of the composition.

Under near-linear SAT the preceding signed construction gives a family of unsatisfiable formulas on which such a generator with $|z|+s\le N^\theta$, $\theta<1$ fixed, has at least
$$
 2^{N\,2^{-O(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N})}}
$$
**distinct false accepting projections**. This can hold on the same family as the earlier assertions by adding one verifier mode for the fixed generator. Here a coverage guarantee is needed for the parameter values the verifier can guess. If it holds only under particular space caps, the verifier must enforce a cap under which coverage is guaranteed for every satisfiable input. One may use a fixed cap function computable within the verifier's budget, or derive a safe cap from the public directory before running the test. A guessed artificially short cutoff that could discard the real path on a satisfiable instance is ineligible.

To prove the assertion let a *challenge* $j$ name one possible native defect, as in the streamed monitor above. Include malformed records, missing or duplicated times, CPU and controller faults, incorrect terminal acceptance and an incorrect load at a named time, slot and address. For a fixed challenge use a deterministic monitor on a generated stream with a yes/no output. With duplicates, define ambiguous fields by a fixed, order-independent convention (for instance use the lexicographically least matching record and retain the duplicate flag). If a selected read does not occur, this particular memory-fault challenge fails. A format challenge still works at a missing or duplicated index. Thus every flawed projection fails some true-run check and a genuine accepting projection evades them all, independently of emission order and label multiplicity.

For $K$ different accepting projections, fewer than $1+K^2J$ primes can be excluded by all nonzero pairwise differences of the multiset integers. Guess a modulus $p$ of $O(\log(K+1)+\log N)$ bits separating every pair, including the empty-list case by taking $p=2$. Test the statement
$$
 \forall h<p\quad \exists j\quad
 \text{no admitted accepting projection with residue }h\text{ evades }j .
$$
The inner complementary query simulates one branch, computes the residue and monitors that single named fault. There are a fixed number of extra quantifier blocks of size $O(\log p+\log N)$. After tape reachability, eliminate them under the assumed near-linear SAT bound, charging the same subexponential-in-$\log N$ factor as above after adjusting constants. On any satisfiable input, a genuine accepting projection survives by coverage and its residue invalidates the displayed statement for every choice of $p$. On an unsatisfiable input each isolated nonempty bucket contains one faulty projection and has a successful challenge. Empty buckets permit any challenge. A small $K$ would therefore give a too-fast sound UNSAT certificate at the verifier's own negative input. This proves the count. The bucket-to-challenge map can be computationally unbounded since its entries are supplied *after* the residue is selected. [Bucket soundness and separation in Lean](../lean/ProjectedTraceLists.lean)

More generally take any tag of charged short length computed by an allowed space-bounded tape routine on each emitted stream. Guess its program and advice if desired and enforce its caps. It must produce a tag for every successful generating branch, with a defined default on timeout. Replace one challenge by a list of $k$ challenges and test whether some branch evades every one on the list. Running the monitors together costs $O((k+1)\log N)$ bits in addition to generation and tag evaluation. On the same hard input **some single tag value** has the property that every such list is evaded by at least one false accepting branch with that tag, provided the summed advice, tag lengths, space caps and list size in bits are below $N\,2^{-D'\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}}$ for sufficiently large fixed $D'$. This follows by negating the displayed test with the stronger bucket challenge. The surviving branch may change with the challenge list. Even if a tag depends on emission order, a genuine branch still defeats its own bucket.

For chronological CPU-valid simulations this implies a simpler statement. Every set of $k$ selected read timestamps and addresses can be kept consistent by some false accepting run on the hard formula, with $(k+1)\log N$ within this budget. To test a chosen timestamp without knowing its address in advance, guess its address before the simulated run and confirm it when that read occurs. For a fixed slot at which the branch makes no work read, it suffices to confirm that case; the constraint is then vacuous. Hold the latest stored word for each designated actual or guessed address throughout the scan. This permits imposing consistency on *all* reads of one selected address for the same cost. Any list that intersects every false accepting run's faulty reads would rule out speculative acceptance and certify UNSAT. The coverage argument is valid because the genuine accepting run always passes every such monitor on a satisfiable input.

The hitting-number conclusion by itself has a sharp limitation. A constant-rejecting RAM can read $m$ distinct fresh zero cells in order and accept immediately on seeing a nonzero word. For every slot there is a false accepting trajectory with that slot as its only bad read. It takes $m$ fixed times or addresses to meet them all. One streaming test enforcing zero on the known fresh addresses removes every false acceptance and preserves every genuine execution. In this particular program there are only polynomially many false accepting projections even before applying the test: the first nonzero time and word determine the entire padded trajectory. The same fresh-cell guard can precede any solver on a disjoint address range. The lower bound after *every* admissible cheap monitor is what survives such padding. It leaves open whether actual SAT programs always admit a stronger monitor that gives the desired time contradiction.

## Removing the fast SAT hypothesis from a product bound

Fix $C$ and a deterministic sound UNSAT recognizer $A$ complete on unsatisfiable inputs. For the real prefix up to its first signal at time $E$, the function $(1+E)R_C(F,E)$ on those inputs is outside $O(N^{1+\epsilon})$ for some fixed $\epsilon>0$.

Suppose such a product bound holds at $c=1+\epsilon$, with $\epsilon\le1/100$. Since $R_C\ge1$, every UNSAT instance has a bounded rejection signal in $O(N^c)$ time. Every materialized SAT instance has a quasilinear nondeterministic certificate for a satisfying assignment. Thus an explicit SAT instance of either verdict has a sound nondeterministic certificate for that verdict in $O(N^{c+o(1)})$ time. For a self-input with $E\le\sqrt N$, replay $A$ directly on the virtual formula, and certify the genuine signal in $o(H)$. Otherwise an honest presentation and its isolating filter have total dominant input-plus-space $N^{1/2+\epsilon+o(1)}$, including $n_0$.

Use a fixed exponent $C'$ bounding the time of the monitored tape predicates on all allowed data. For example restrict all guessed bit lengths and the signal time to $N^2$; the fixed interpreter and arithmetic above then have polynomial clocks independent of $\epsilon\le1/100$. Choose an integer $d\ge 8(C'+1)$ and set $\epsilon=1/(1000(d+10))$. Use $d$ constant levels of reachability subdivision with fanout $2^{\lceil(C'/d)\log_2 N\rceil}$. This covers all allowed transitions. The first quantified circuit with these endpoints and multiplexers costs at most $N^{1/2+\epsilon+C'/d+o(1)}$ on honest guesses. The two separate signed queries can each be certified for the required verdict; there are at most $2d+20$ blocks per query including the endpoints and sign bookkeeping. Freeze outer variables as pins, encode the innermost block or its negation as an explicit SAT instance, and append its bounded certificate (or universally test invalidity of a contrary certificate) to the preceding block. Each such elimination multiplies the size exponent by at most $c$ up to logarithmic factors. A fixed number of eliminations gives honest total time exponent at most
$$
 (1/2+\epsilon+C'/d)(1+\epsilon)^{2d+20}<0.7.
$$
Indeed $C'/d\le1/8$ and $(1+\epsilon)^{2d+20}\le(1-(2d+20)\epsilon)^{-1}=500/499$. Guessing the actual filter is covered. Sign-correct certificates for the two answers refer to the same public presentation, time, caps and filter. As before, a successful branch of the signed verifier on its own padded description both makes $F_x$ satisfiable and demonstrates a genuine UNSAT signal. Its resulting negative input supplies the forbidden $o(H)$ honest branch under the assumed product bound. [Two-sign elimination with bounded UNSAT signals](pebbling-live-producers.md)

Classical work relates bounded-width existential encodings to communication complexity. Prime fingerprinting and read-once generators likewise supply prior ingredients. The earlier notes used fingerprint collisions between two independently generated logs at an omitted output, and path decompositions of a particular producer graph. The projection statistic here permits arbitrary auxiliary local relations and output orders, then measures the remaining distinct histories of an actual solver. Priority for the resulting tradeoffs is unestablished. To exclude unrestricted linear time one still needs an upper bound on such a presentation for every fast SAT solver or a different contradiction.
