# Advice needed to certify most seeds in the common SAT family

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Assume a fixed deterministic $O(N\log^a N)$ solver for packed 3-SAT. Use the existing small-list first-offer family, with $L=\lceil\log_2(H+2)\rceil$, $k=L^2$ seed bits, $\ell=\lfloor\log_2 L\rfloor$, $P=2^{\ell+1}-1$, native offer clock $M=\lfloor H/L^K\rfloor$ and encoded formula length $N=\Theta(HL^3)$. At most $P$ seeds give a formula of truth different from its proposed bit $b$. Each of the original $\ell$-bit programs forces both bits to be wrong at a seed if it offers both bits there and withholds everywhere else. These two properties of the existing generator suffice here. [First-offer construction and uniform clocks](common-solver-seeds.md)

Let $u=u(H)$ be a fixed polylogarithmic-time computable integer with $1\le u\le A\log_2 L$ for some fixed $A$, and set $\delta=2^{-u}$. Give a certifier arbitrary advice chosen for the scale, public $H,r,b$, and virtual access to $F_H(r,b)$. It can guess verification data. Count a seed as covered if the certifier can signal the proposed sign on at least one of its two formulas before its clock. Require every such signal to be correct anywhere in the family at that scale. For some fixed $J$, at time $T=\lfloor M/L^J\rfloor$, covering at least $1-\delta$ of the seeds requires

$$\Omega\bigl(P\log_2(1/\delta)\bigr)$$

bits of combined program and advice. A fixed deterministic verifier with $O(P\log_2(1/\delta))$ advice bits covers at least that fraction, on *both* pins, in $\operatorname{polylog}H$ time. In particular constant coverage of one half already costs $\Theta(\log H)$ bits, compared with the $\Theta(\log\log H)$ bits needed to name a single clean seed prefix. The lower bound applies to this particular family of formulas at every sufficiently large scale. The generator, seed distribution, exception set and original budget $M$ are the same for every $u$. The polynomial in the logarithmic loss in $T$ may depend on the choice of $u$ and $A$.

## Choosing exceptions that intercept longer programs

For each binary description $x$ of length at most $t$, let $f_x(r)$ mean that some timely branch of its decoded verifier, with its indicated advice, signals $b$ at $(r,b)$ for some $b$. Length-delimit the fixed program and allow the remaining advice to be read at random. Invalid descriptions give the empty set. Count all descriptions, including those that make false claims. There are fewer than $2^{t+1}$, and a single positive membership computation is a bounded existential test with $T\operatorname{polylog}H$ work when $t=\operatorname{polylog}H$. Its simulations read the rectangular formula for $(H,r,b)$ virtually. The checker text and SAT reference are fixed. No test needs the SAT truth of that formula.

Define a bounded-quantifier predicate $D(x)$ which holds for coverage at least $1-\delta$ and fails for coverage at most $1-3\delta/2$. Take $s=\lceil32(k+1)/\delta^2\rceil$ shifts. Ask whether there exist $k$-bit shifts $v_1,\ldots,v_s$ such that for every $z$, at least a fraction $1-5\delta/4$ of the seeds $z\oplus v_i$ satisfy $f_x$. In the high case choose independent uniform shifts. At each $z$, the probability of missing the threshold is at most $\exp(-s\delta^2/8)$ by Hoeffding's inequality. A union bound over $2^k$ values of $z$ proves existence. Conversely the mean shifted fraction over uniform $z$ equals the true coverage for every fixed list of shifts. This rules out the low case. The final existential block supplies successful computation branches for enough distinct shift indices. The work for all of them is $sT\operatorname{polylog}H$, still $T\operatorname{polylog}H$. [Earlier shifted threshold test](local-views.md) · [Lautemann's shift method](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3)

Take $m=2^{\ell-C}$ for a fixed sufficiently large compiler margin $C$, and $t=\lfloor mu/4\rfloor$. There exists an ordered list of $m$ seeds hitting every $f_x$ for which $D(x)$ holds. Indeed a uniform independent list misses any one of these sets with probability at most $(3\delta/2)^m$. The union bound is strictly less than one since

$$2^{t+1}(3\delta/2)^m\le 2^{1+mu/4-m(u-\log_2(3/2))}<1$$

for sufficiently large scales and $u\ge1$. Repeated seeds are harmless.

Order these lists lexicographically by their $mk$ bits. Compute the first valid list $S$ by asking whether the current prefix followed by zero extends to a valid list. This is a fixed-depth bounded-quantifier question: an extension exists for which every $x$ of length at most $t$ either fails $D$ or has $f_x(S_i)$ at some index $i$. The shifts, prefix, indices, list and program descriptions all have $\operatorname{polylog}H$ bits in this regime. Branch witnesses for the clocked simulations use $T\operatorname{polylog}H$ bits. Flattening the fixed number of Boolean connectives and quantifier blocks preserves that work bound. At one innermost block materialize the packed bounded computation, call the near-linear SAT reference and complement its bit when appropriate. Repeat a fixed number of times. Thus one extension query costs $(T+1)\operatorname{polylog}H$. The $mk$ adaptive queries together take the same form of bound. Choosing $J$ larger than the combined fixed log exponents fits this whole computation in $M$, including interpretation and comparison to the input seed. The exponents in the reductions are independent of increasing the fixed margin $J$, since all time bounds have logarithm $O(L)$. [Reduction with short public data](packed-band-transfer.md) · [Quantifier elimination](linear-quantifiers.md)

For every $i<m$ install a possible original-pool program with a fixed instruction prefix and $\ell-C$ payload bits specifying $i$. This program computes $S$ and offers the requested sign on either pin precisely when the seed is $S_i$. The externally supplied text length gives the payload length. $C$ can absorb the fixed interpreter, the original checker text and the encodings of the fixed resource constants; a binary literal for $C$ itself costs $O(\log C)$ bits. The simulated candidate $x$ in a density test runs only for its prescribed $T$ instructions, even if it tries to reconstruct $S$. All computations refer to syntactically specified formulas, so the definition uses no answer about whether a selected seed is already exceptional.

Each $S_i$ is the unique offering seed of its own short original-pool program. The existing source therefore flips both of its pins. If some scale-sound advised program of length at most $t$ covered a fraction at least $1-\delta$, it would satisfy $D$ and offer a requested sign at some $S_i$. Both proposed signs there are false. This contradiction proves the lower bound since $m=\Theta(P)$. The argument makes the short programs' *actual* first offers intercept dense longer programs. A counting lower bound for representing an arbitrary exceptional set alone would leave open the structure of these particular exceptions. [Earlier counting gap for a computationally generated set](hitting-sampled-batches.md)

## Matching advice and zero-error running time

Partition the seed cube by the first $q=\lceil\log_2(P/\delta)\rceil$ bits, so that the number $B$ of buckets is between $P/\delta$ and $2P/\delta$. Mark buckets meeting the at most $P$ exceptional seeds. On an unmarked bucket, certify the proposed sign at either pin. At most $P/B\le\delta$ of seeds lie in marked buckets. Encode the marked subset by its rank among subsets of $[B]$ of size at most $P$. Since $P\le B/2$, this costs at most $\log_2(P+1)+P\log_2(eB/P)+O(1)=O(Pu)$ bits. Standard binomial counting unpacks a rank by iterating over bucket positions, subtracting the number of subsets skipped by each chosen bit. The table and integer operations cost $\operatorname{poly}(B,P)$ bit operations. Here $B=\operatorname{polylog}H$, so the decoder fits the stated clock. Neither the advice nor the rank has to be computed by the verifier. An $\ell+1$-bit prefix with no exception exists separately since there are $P+1$ such prefixes; this yields only $1/(P+1)$ coverage. [Carter, Floyd, Gill, Markowsky and Wegman on approximate membership](https://research.ibm.com/publications/exact-and-approximate-membership-testers)

The same lower bound applies to errorless randomized certifiers with independent fair random bits or words. Any timely offering run uses a finite random prefix of positive probability, so guessing its coins gives an admissible branch. For an always-correct randomized decider with combined description at most $t$, a fraction greater than $\delta-P/2^k$ of seeds consequently have this property: on both pins every run takes more than $T$ before giving its verdict. Indeed a fraction greater than $\delta$ has no branch that offers a matching sign on either pin, and we may remove all exceptional seeds. The distributional expected time is therefore at least $(\delta-P/2^k)T$ for uniform seeds and either pin choice. This concerns zero error at every input in the scale. A predictor that simply returns the proposed pin already has tiny distributional error.

The work lower bound concerns $T=N/\operatorname{polylog}N$. A linear-time solver may spend more than this budget on all inputs. The standard shift and approximate-membership ingredients have prior literature. Priority for their use to pin down the advice cost of this signed generator remains unverified. The quantified RAM and asymptotic estimates here are manuscript proofs. Lean checks the implication from interception to low coverage, the error propagation in the estimates, and the finite count underlying the rebalanced distribution below. [Checked finite implications](../lean/SeedFilterAdvice.lean)

## Rebalancing gives a distribution hard for two-sided randomized decisions

One can sample the exceptional seeds in a way that conceals their status from short randomized tests. Here $\eta=2^{-\lfloor(\log_2 L)/4\rfloor}=\Theta(L^{-1/4})$. For a fixed sufficiently large $J'$ let $T'=\lfloor M/L^{J'}\rfloor$. There is a uniformly samplable distribution on the same explicit SAT formulas under which *every* randomized decision program of time $T'$ and combined code/advice length at most $c\sqrt L$, for a fixed $c>0$, succeeds with probability at most

$$\frac12+\frac{\eta}{2}+\frac{P}{2^{k+1}}.$$

The program may receive the actual public seed $r$ and pin $b$. It gets its own fresh random bits. The sampler's private choice of component remains hidden. Across both components it uses a uniform pin $b$. On each pair whose signs both flip, exactly one pin is satisfiable. The same holds on every pair with neither sign flipped. Consequently the marginal probability of SAT differs from $1/2$ by at most $P/2^{k+2}$. This distribution uses a specific component-sampling procedure and does not claim a lower bound on a uniformly drawn formula.

**Construction.** For a candidate description $x$, set $g_x(r)=\Pr_{b,\omega}[A_x(H,r,b,F_H(r,b);\omega)=b]$. The program is clocked and returns a fixed default bit on timeout or malformation. A deterministic decision algorithm is a special case. Each $g_x$ takes values in $[0,1]$, irrespective of how many coin tosses the program uses within its clock. Let $m=2^{\ell-C'}$ for a large constant $C'$, and let $t'=\lfloor m\eta^2/128\rfloor$. This is $\Theta(\sqrt L)$ asymptotically. An independent uniform list of $m$ seeds has positive probability of satisfying

$$
 \left|\mathbb E_r g_x(r)-m^{-1}\sum_{i<m}g_x(S_i)\right|\le\eta/4
$$

for every $|x|\le t'$. Hoeffding bounds the failure probability for one row by $2\exp(-m\eta^2/8)$, and there are fewer than $2^{t'+1}$ rows. This reasoning samples the real-valued expectations over private coins. It does not enumerate coin tapes as additional program descriptions.

To select a list deterministically, use the following bounded-quantifier test to estimate either mean within $\eta/8$. For the first mean the uniform random string indexes $(r,b,\omega)$, and for the second it indexes $(i,b,\omega)$, with $S_i$ accessed in the supplied list. The length is $q=O((T'+1)L+k)$ since a native random instruction can supply only a logarithmic-width word. To compare the mean to a threshold separated by $\gamma=\eta/64$, average $O(\gamma^{-2}\log(q+2))$ independent tests on a longer random string of $R$ bits and accept the corresponding threshold. Choose a large constant so that at either promised extreme the amplified error is at most $1/[8(R+1)]$. All these lengths are $T'\operatorname{polylog}H$. An additive gap comparison for the original mean now asks whether the accepting set of this amplified test covers the $R$-cube under the span of $d=\lceil\log_2(R+1)\rceil$ shifts. At high acceptance its uncovered fraction starts at most $1/2$. For an uncovered fraction $v$, averaging over a further shift makes the new uncovered fraction at most $v^2$ for some choice. After $d$ such choices it is strictly less than $2^{-R}$. At low acceptance all $2^d\le2(R+1)$ translates together cover less than one. Guess $d$ generators, universally choose a base string, and existentially choose the $d$ coefficients giving an accepted translate. The $Rd$ generator bits and the simulation of one selected translate cost $T'\operatorname{polylog}H$ per branch. This step accommodates private randomness as long as the native time allows. [Construction and proof of the span test](near-native-counting.md) · [Viola on quasilinear alternating simulation of probabilistic time](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/DagSemProc.06111.11)

Test thresholds on a dyadic $\gamma$-grid, use zero as a default, and take the largest accepted threshold. Below the true mean minus $\gamma$ every threshold accepts; above it plus $\gamma$ all reject. The $O(1/\gamma)$ tests give a deterministic estimate within $2\gamma\le\eta/8$ even when some intermediate comparisons give arbitrary answers. Require for every $x$ that the two deterministic estimates differ by at most $\eta/2$. A list meeting the sampling estimate $\eta/4$ passes this predicate. Every passing list has actual discrepancy at most $3\eta/4<\eta$. The universal description quantifier, the shifts, and list extension use a fixed number of levels of bounded SAT elimination. One may first run the deterministic mean-estimation routine inside the universal test, paying its polylogarithmic number of threshold comparisons in its clock. Take the lexicographically first passing list $S$ by the same $mk$ prefix queries as before. Under the near-linear SAT hypothesis the total time remains $T'\operatorname{polylog}H$. Choose $J'$ to put it below $M$, allowing also the fixed checker text and width normalization. Increasing $C'$ absorbs the fixed code and $O(\log C')$ bits needed to specify its own margin. If a candidate tries to determine whether its input belongs to $S$, its attempt is included in the clocked tests.

As before, each cheap short program indexed by $i$ offers both pins only at $S_i$. Thus every $S_i$ has truth $1-b$ at both pins in the original generator. With probability $1/2$ sample uniform $r$, and otherwise sample $i$ uniformly and use $r=S_i$. Then sample $b$ and write $F_H(r,b)$. The entire list is computable within $M$ under the reference hypothesis and output costs $O(N\operatorname{polylog}N)$ time. For a particular test write $\mu=\mathbb E_r g_x(r)$ and $\nu=m^{-1}\sum_i g_x(S_i)$. Its success on the uniform component is at most $\mu+P/2^k$, since only the exceptional seeds can spoil the prescribed pin. On the list component its success is exactly $1-\nu$. Averaging proves the bound. All probabilities include the candidate's independent random coins. A test that only sees the formula can ignore the extra public parameters. The same sampler and success bound apply to every fixed randomized program once its code fits, as well as to the stated nonuniform descriptions at each scale.

Enumerating a small menu of deterministic prediction rows and choosing a weakly correlated parity by Walsh orthogonality is an unconditional comparison available for other families with only polylogarithmically many public seeds. That argument also allows enumeration of a polylogarithmic number of random tapes. This construction uses the fixed near-linear solver to select pseudorandom exceptions *inside this existing first-offer family*, including for tests that use up to $T'$ random words. The time hierarchy alone supplies no such selection procedure. The sampler and the hidden-membership computation both have more time than the predictors; the success bound is consistent with deterministic $O(N)$ SAT. ## How much advice breaks the two-sided sampler?

For its stated advantage scale $\eta$, the order $m\eta^2$ of the description cutoff is tight. More precisely, for every power of two $1\le B\le m$, there is a deterministic predictor receiving the public seed and pin that uses $B+O(\log m)$ bits of advice, runs in $\operatorname{polylog}H$ time, and has success probability at least

$$\frac12+\frac1{12}\sqrt{\frac Bm}-\frac{P}{2^{k+1}}.$$

The advice depends on the actual list. No list-reconstruction algorithm within the predictor clock is needed.

Use the first $w=\log_2(16m)$ seed bits as $z$ in a universe of size $U=16m$. Under the uniform component this prefix is exactly uniform. Let $\lambda_z$ be its probability under the list component, including repetitions and prefix collisions, and let $D=\{z:\lambda_z>0\}$, of size at most $m$. Write $d_z=\lambda_z-1/U$. Pick a pairwise independent uniform hash $h$ into $B$ buckets and independently pick four-wise independent fair signs $\sigma_z$. Choose a sign $a_j$ for each bucket equal to the sign of $\sum_{h(z)=j}d_z\sigma_z$, with either choice on zero. The signed classifier $C(z)=a_{h(z)}\sigma_z$ has exact discrepancy

$$\mathbb E_{\rm list} C-\mathbb E_{\rm uniform} C=\sum_j\left|\sum_{h(z)=j}d_z\sigma_z\right|.$$

Conditional on the hash, each bucket sum $X$ has second moment $V=\sum_{h(z)=j}d_z^2$ and fourth moment at most $3V^2$, by expanding the polynomial and using four-wise independence. Hölder's inequality gives $\mathbb E|X|\ge (\mathbb E X^2)^{3/2}/(\mathbb E X^4)^{1/2}\ge\sqrt{V/3}$ when $V>0$. Let $n_j$ count the *distinct* members of $D$ in bucket $j$. For $n_j>0$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\sqrt V\ge\sum_{z\in D:h(z)=j}|d_z|/\sqrt{n_j}$. For each fixed $z\in D$, its expected bucket occupancy is $1+(|D|-1)/B$. Convexity of $x\mapsto x^{-1/2}$ and $\sum_{z\in D}|d_z|\ge1-|D|/U$ now bound the expected discrepancy below by

$$\frac{1-m/U}{\sqrt{3(1+m/B)}}\ \ge\ \frac{15}{16\sqrt6}\sqrt{B/m}\ >\ \frac13\sqrt{B/m}.$$

Thus some parameters attain the claimed discrepancy. For an explicit compact family identify $z$ with an element of $\mathbb F_{2^w}$. Project an independent random affine polynomial's value to $\log_2 B$ coordinates for $h$ and one coordinate of a random degree-at-most-three polynomial's value for $\sigma$. Evaluation at distinct field points gives the requisite independence by interpolation. Store the six coefficients and an irreducible modulus in $O(w)$ bits, together with the $B$ bucket signs. A fixed decoder performs the polynomial arithmetic in $\operatorname{poly}(w)$ bit operations and reads the indicated table entry. The parameter $B$ can also be encoded within the stated overhead. Four-wise signed sums are classical in frequency-moment estimation. [Alon, Matias and Szegedy](https://doi.org/10.1145/237814.237823)

Return $1-b$ when $C(z)=+1$, and $b$ otherwise. The success probabilities on the list and ideal uniform components are $(1+\mathbb E_{\rm list}C)/2$ and $(1-\mathbb E_{\rm uniform}C)/2$. At most $P/2^k$ of the uniform component's seeds have an exceptional truth, so averaging gives the displayed advantage. Choose the least power of two $B\ge144m\eta^2$. For sufficiently large scales $B\le m$, and the advantage is at least $\eta-P/2^{k+1}$. Since $m\eta^2=\Theta(\sqrt L)\gg\log m$, the advice length is $O(m\eta^2)$. In combination with the sampler's lower bound, the least advice plus program length sufficient for advantage at least $3\eta/4$ at clock $T'$ is $\Theta(m\eta^2)$. The construction above provides an attack throughout its stated range of $B$; the matching lower bound was proved at the sampler's chosen $\eta$.

## A single checksum gives smaller equivalent SAT instances

In the small-list regime, fixed length-dependent advice of $k+O(\log P)$ bits gives an ordinary one-query reduction for **all** seeds and both pins. Given this advice and $(H,r,b)$, the reduction writes a 3-CNF of encoded length $PM\operatorname{polylog}H$ whose SAT truth equals that of $F_H(r,b)$. The reduction itself uses no call to the reference SAT solver. It applies as well to the original quadratic and cubic reference hypotheses with their respective $M=\lfloor H^{1/d}/L^K\rfloor$. Those cases give reduced length $H^{1/d}\operatorname{polylog}H$. The generator of the original signed family still has its reference hypothesis. [Original clocks and family](common-solver-seeds.md)

Put $U=2^k$. For each of the $2P$ code–pin pairs $(e,c)$, let $a_{e,c}$ be the least seed rank on which $e$ offers the requested $c$ at pin $c$, assigning sentinel $U$ if there is none. Trust the single integer $S=\sum_{e,c}a_{e,c}$, which uses at most $\lceil\log_2(2PU+1)\rceil$ bits. In every row guess either a genuine offering seed with its timely computation or the unconditional sentinel. Its proposed rank $v_{e,c}$ is at least $a_{e,c}$. Require that all proposed ranks sum to $S$. This forces equality in every row, and a table with these exact ranks has suitable witnesses. No witness of an empty row is needed. [Coordinatewise checksum implication in Lean](../lean/SeedFilterAdvice.lean)

The original flip at $(r,b)$ is now equivalent to existence of a code $e$ with $a_{e,b}=r$ and $r\le a_{e,1-b}$. Equality in the second comparison permits both pins to flip together. After checking the whole table, a bounded nondeterministic computation compares $b$ XOR this flag to the desired verdict. To get an equisatisfiable formula ask for verdict one. Asking for zero gives an ordinary formula for the complementary verdict as well. All simulations use the fixed virtual formulas $F_H(u,c)$ at the *original* scale and enforce the interpreted machines' own clocks. Including $2P$ short ranks, guesses, and virtual-access overhead, the total work is $PM\operatorname{polylog}H$. The usual packed Cook construction gives the stated explicit output size and writing time. This reduction can be generated from the short parameters without scanning all $N$ bits of an independently supplied CNF. For an arbitrary explicit input, an extra test would have to establish that it really is the named formula.

Truncating each nonempty rank to its first $d'$ bits and replacing the sentinel by $2^{d'}$ gives an additional tradeoff. Trust a sum of these truncated minima, using $d'+O(\log P)$ bits. Guess genuine offers or sentinels and compare the truncated sum as above. Every actual exception lies in one of the at most $2P$ nonempty prefix buckets obtained in a passing table. Certify $b$ outside them. With $d'=\lceil\log_2(2P/\delta)\rceil\le k$, the coverage is at least $1-\delta$ and the advice length is $O(\log(P/\delta))$. These larger-clock nondeterministic upper bounds exploit the offer predicates directly. Under near-linear SAT, a deterministic algorithm allowed to run the reference already has an advice-free $PM\operatorname{polylog}H$ query procedure. The checksum gives an existential verification and an ordinary 3-CNF without embedding calls to that reference.

For intermediate exact query clocks, fix $1\le g\le P$. Choose one representative code for each **distinct** nonsentinel first-offer seed (minimum over its two pins). For this seed store the aggregate two-bit flip mask over all codes with the same first seed. Partition the sorted distinct ranks into at most $g$ consecutive groups, each of at most $\lceil P/g\rceil$ ranks. Advice gives the boundaries, the checksum of the representative ranks in each group, and for every code either its representative group and aggregate mask or an inactive mark. For a query, locate its group by the boundaries, guess one valid offer on either pin for each representative in that group, and check its sum. Larger ranks cannot counterfeit the genuine table. Compare $r$ with the recovered ranks and read the appropriate mask, using the default $b$ if all differ. Empty groups and the case of no representatives can be encoded explicitly. This uses $O(P\log(g+1)+g(k+\log P))$ advice bits and $O((P/g)M\operatorname{polylog}H+\operatorname{polylog}H)$ verification work. At the long-advice endpoint an explicit dictionary of exceptional seeds and sign masks permits the simpler polylogarithmic-time query.

**Removing the trusted integer.** For arbitrary offered rows one can uniformly compute $S$ by binary search. The predicate that a table of feasible ranks exists with sum at most $s$ is an ordinary bounded existential query; it holds precisely when $S\le s$. This gives an advice-free preprocessing procedure with $O(k+\log P)$ adaptive SAT queries of the reduced size. Their cost must be charged. The original per-seed algorithm separately uses the shorter current-offer and prior-offer queries.

Merely guessing the sum fails on the actual generator. Taking the sentinel in every row would allow $S=2PU$, falsely certifying the default sign even at the zero seed, where constant first-offer programs flip both pins. A generic one-query reduction with no advice would also face a complexity barrier. Allow one supplied offer machine on two seeds. At the earlier seed it guesses a satisfying assignment to a supplied formula $B_0$, and at the later seed it guesses one for $A_1$. The first offer occurs at the later seed exactly when $A_1$ is satisfiable and $B_0$ is unsatisfiable. This is the standard SAT–UNSAT problem; fixing $A_1$ to true already gives coNP-hardness. Thus an unconditional polynomial-time many-one reduction of *generic* first offers to SAT would imply $\mathrm{NP}=\mathrm{coNP}$. This argument leaves open a faster uniform reduction for the particular fixed generator. The checksum construction assumes the trusted sum is available and gives no unrestricted RAM time separation. [Papadimitriou and Yannakakis on the class DP](https://doi.org/10.1145/800070.802199)
