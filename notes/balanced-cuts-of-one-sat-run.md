# Persistent reads at every balanced disclosure cut of one SAT run

There are ordinary 3-CNFs with $s$ independently specified word positions (the input to the template) and $L=\Theta(s\log s)$ encoded bits on which every correct deterministic SAT solver has the following behavior. On **one rejecting input**, stop just before it first inspects a $(k+1)$-st different position, for *any* $s/3\le k\le2s/3$. In the aligned layout below each coordinate varies in precisely one physical word. Let $R_k$ count distinct data-bearing input words physically read both before this cut and again in its suffix. Let $D_k$ count distinct work addresses whose pre-cut contents are read in the suffix before their first post-cut overwrite. Then
$$
                        R_k+D_k=\Omega(s)
$$
**at every one of these cuts**, regardless of the order the solver chose to inspect the input. Also $R_k+S_k=\Omega(s)$, where $S_k$ counts all nonzero work words at the cut. The first count includes a first old read that returns the default zero. For the second inequality no zero-valued work word contributes to $S_k$. The constants may depend on the fixed program and word-width convention. No a priori time or workspace bound is used.

The estimates below hold with probability $1-2^{-\Omega(s\log s)}$ over an explicitly specified uniform distribution on rejecting inputs, for each fixed correct solver and sufficiently large hard scale. A direct solver takes $O(s)$ word steps on this same family. A word probe can belong to many overlapping suffixes. A conditional calculation below gives an exact ceiling for a natural attempt to replace those suffixes by disjoint sibling problems.

## A linear code inside one 3-CNF

Take a simple 3-vertex-connected cubic graph $G=(V,E)$ with positive constant vertex expansion, $s=|E|$ and $n=|V|=2s/3$. Such graphs exist for all sufficiently large even vertex counts by the random regular graph results on expansion and connectivity. The graph may be fixed publicly at each scale. Existence suffices for this worst-case SAT statement. A uniform but unbounded search could choose the lexicographically first graph satisfying fixed rational expansion and the connectivity condition. [Kolesnik and Wormald on expansion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.6555) · [Wormald on connectivity](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095895681800214)

Set $q=2^{\lfloor(\log_2 s)/2\rfloor}$. Each edge receives a word $x_e\in\mathbb F_q$. Addition is bitwise XOR. A circulation has zero sum of the three incident words at every vertex. Let $C$ denote this linear subspace of $\mathbb F_q^E$.

Use a proper public edge coloring $\chi:E\to\{1,\ldots,5\}$. A greedy coloring suffices, since each edge has at most four neighboring edges. Give each vertex a row-selection variable $z_v$, each edge a flag $t_e$, and each pair consisting of a color $h$ and value $a$ a flag $H_{h,a}$. Require at least one $z_v$ by a fixed linear chain of width-three clauses. Include the binary implications
$$
  z_v \Longrightarrow t_e \quad(e\text{ incident to }v),\qquad
  t_e \Longrightarrow H_{\chi(e),x_e}\quad(e\in E).
$$
The second implication occurs **just once per edge**. For each triple of colors $h_1<h_2<h_3$ and every $a,b\in\mathbb F_q$, include
$$
       \neg H_{h_1,a}\lor\neg H_{h_2,b}\lor\neg H_{h_3,a+b}.
$$
Any satisfying assignment selects a row with nonzero sum, since its three incident colors are distinct. Conversely select one nonzero-sum row, just its three edges and just their three colored flags. Each public clause for a different color triple has a missing flag. Consequently SAT is precisely nonmembership in $C$. There are $O(s+q^2)=O(s)$ clauses. A data implication can have three distinct literal positions by adding a public false variable. Force it false by four clauses with all signs on two fresh auxiliaries. All further short public clauses can use fresh padding with all signs at constant overhead.

For precision, use aligned word loads, the project's width $W=12\lceil\log_2(L+2)\rceil$, and a conventional packed triple serialization with three sign-and-fixed-width identifier fields per record. With dense variable names, the record pitch is $p=3\log_2 s+O(1)$ bits. Align the colored flag banks at multiples of $q$, so only the final $\log_2 q$ bits of one identifier vary. Give each edge a block of a sufficiently large fixed number of equal-pitch records (e.g. 32), independent of the placement of its data clause. Use two consecutive candidate slots per block and public satisfied padding records elsewhere. Their varying-field starts differ by $p$. For large $s$, both $p$ and $W-p$ exceed the varying-field length, so the field cannot straddle a word boundary in both candidates. Choose a fitting slot after computing the public length, which is independent of the choice. Blocks keep different data fields more than a word apart. Thus precisely one input word depends on any given coordinate, and every coordinate has just one such word. Public padding can use repeated copies of a previously imposed public clause. For a serialization that permits fields to straddle words without alignment, the same communication calculation charges revisits to earlier *coordinates* possibly through other word addresses. Changing any one edge value on a circulation makes its endpoint equations fail, so every correct solver must inspect all $s$ data-bearing words on each rejecting input here.

The family has a globally correct SAT fast path. Use disjoint, publicly computable ranges for the vertex, edge, color/value and auxiliary names, with fixed-position blocks. The scanner obtains the vertex count from the initial choice chain, computes the edge count and verifies the header, total length, chain, padding, false-variable gadget and every exclusion clause in public order. Block $e$ must have just the prescribed data implication from $t_e$ to a valid colored flag. Vertex block $v$ must have exactly three implications from $z_v$ to in-range edge names. Fetch those three edge blocks by index, require their colors to be distinct and accumulate whether some row has nonzero XOR. Use the verdict only after all shape checks pass. Fall back to a complete solver on a mismatch. These checks suffice even if an edge identifier is used at arbitrarily many vertices, because the proof of the verdict requires only the three distinct colors in each row. Each fetch, block and exclusion row costs a constant number of word operations, giving $O(s)$ total time and constant writable words on the stated family.

## Information across an arbitrary balanced edge split

Write $\partial$ for the vertex-edge incidence matrix over $\mathbb F_q$. Its rank on $A\subseteq E$ is $n-\kappa(A)$, where $\kappa(A)$ counts components of $(V,A)$ including isolated vertices. For each component the row sum is zero and its spanning tree has independent columns by leaf elimination. If $B=E\setminus A$, the dimension of the common image of the two restricted incidence matrices is
$$
 \lambda(A)=\operatorname{rank}\partial_A+\operatorname{rank}\partial_B-\operatorname{rank}\partial
           = n+1-\kappa(A)-\kappa(B).
$$
For a uniformly random circulation the common boundary value $\partial_A x_A=-\partial_B x_B$ is uniform on this image intersection. Every value is attained and the map from $C$ is linear. In any deterministic two-party transcript that certifies circulation, all accepting data have **one** common boundary value. Otherwise exchanging the two parties' inputs between two accepting pairs violates circulation within the same monochromatic rectangle. The same reasoning applies when a SAT protocol publishes the rejecting bit.

For all balanced splits, $\lambda(A)=\Omega(s)$. Here is a direct proof. Write $b$ for the number of vertices touching both colors of edges and let $c_A,c_B$ count the nonisolated components of the two edge subgraphs. The formula above becomes
$$
                 \lambda(A)=b-c_A-c_B+1 .
$$
There are at least $2|A|/3-b\ge n/3-b$ vertices meeting only $A$-edges, and the same bound holds for $B$. If $b<n/12$, the smaller of the two exclusive sets has size in $[n/4,n/2]$. Its external vertex boundary is contained in the $b$ shared vertices, so expansion forces $b=\Omega(n)$. When $b\ge n/12$ this bound already holds.

Make a bipartite multigraph with one vertex per nonisolated component on either side and one edge per shared original vertex joining its two components. The degree of a component is the number of its shared vertices. For large $s$, both colors have more than one edge. If a component touches at most two shared vertices and has an interior vertex, removing the shared vertices separates its interior from a vertex outside the component. Such an outside vertex exists since more than one opposite-colored edge cannot lie entirely on at most two boundary vertices of a simple graph. This violates 3-vertex-connectivity. A nonisolated component with at most two shared vertices and no interior is therefore a single edge, and has degree two in the multigraph. Two such components on opposite sides cannot meet, since their shared original vertex would have degree two rather than three. Consequently the sum of the degrees of components of degree at least three is at least $b$. For each such degree $d$, $d-2\ge d/3$. The sum of all excess degrees equals $2b-2(c_A+c_B)=2\lambda(A)-2$. Hence $\lambda(A)\ge1+b/6$. This calculation concerns every balanced split. Graph and cycle-matroid branchwidth, which optimize over decompositions, also have a classical equality theorem. [Hicks and McMurray on branchwidth](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0095895606001419)

## One hard run for cuts chosen after inspecting the trace

Fix a candidate split $A,B$. Bob knows the $A$ coordinates, Alice the $B$ coordinates. Bob simulates from time zero until the solver would inspect a word from $B$, or halts. At this boundary he sends its finite controller to Alice. She simulates the rest, asking Bob once per distinct old $A$-dependent input word or pre-overwrite work address and caching replies and all subsequent stores. Bob can reconstruct the entire prefix and every requested value, including default zeros. Use public message-type tags and fixed-width addresses and values. The total communication is at most $K(1+R+D)\log_2 s$ bits, where $R,D$ are the two corresponding counts at this handoff. Early halts publish their verdict. All simulations need to be correct for arbitrary coordinate words, including noncirculations. Alternatively Bob sends every nonzero work cell in a sparse list at the handoff. The communication is at most $K(1+R+S)\log_2 s$, including the controller and a self-delimited end-of-list tag. This protocol can use an arbitrarily long list. [Earlier handoff construction](old-words-after-partial-query-disclosure.md)

By the rectangle calculation, each rejecting transcript has probability at most $q^{-\lambda(A)}$ under the uniform distribution on $C$. There are fewer than $2^{\ell+1}$ binary transcripts of length at most $\ell$. For a small fixed $\eta>0$, the probability of a transcript of length at most $\eta s\log_2 s$ on a given balanced split is $2^{-\Omega(s\log s)}$. There are at most $2^s$ splits and two protocols per split. Outside an event of that same asymptotic probability *all* these transcripts are long on the same input. Now for each $k$ take $A$ to consist of exactly the first $k$ different edge coordinates the real execution inspected. Bob's handoff is exactly the stated physical cut. This is valid even though the split was selected using the entire input, since the assertion already holds for every split. For a polynomial-clock solver the assertion extends to arbitrary instants with a balanced set of disclosed coordinates. Include one protocol per clock instant and split, handing off at the earlier of the designated instant and the first $B$ probe. The polynomial factor in the union bound is harmless.

## Charging actual nonzero values across a cut

Write $h_k$ for the number of distinct pre-cut work addresses holding a nonzero value and genuinely read before overwrite in the suffix. At **every** balanced cut of the same typical run there is also the bound
$$
 (1+R_k+h_k)\log_2 s+
 \log_2\binom{S_k+D_k-2h_k}{S_k-h_k}
                    =\Omega(s\log s).
$$

Use the same two-party handoff, now with a certificate for the published rejection. The certificate specifies $h$ addresses and their nonzero values; Bob checks them in his actual frozen memory. Let $P$ be the remaining nonzero support and $Q$ the remaining distinct pre-overwrite suffix requests. For the honest list these sets are disjoint, with sizes $a=S-h$ and $b=D-h$. Pătraşcu and Demaine's separator lemma supplies a public family from which an index of $O(\log\log u+\log\binom{a+b}{a})$ bits selects a coloring that is zero on $P$ and one on $Q$, over the $u=s^{O(1)}$ possible work addresses. The public families can be fixed by unlimited preprocessing. The certificate includes $a,b,h$ with $0\le a+b\le u$ and $0\le h\le u$, costing $O(\log s)$ bits to give the counts and delimit the list. A coarser version charges $O(1+a+b+\log\log u)$ bits: $O(2^{a+b}(a+b+1)\log(2u))$ independent fair colorings suffice by the union bound over disjoint pairs. [Pătraşcu and Demaine, Lemma 6.2](https://erikdemaine.org/papers/DynamicConnectivity_SICOMP/paper.pdf) · [Many-phase address masks](simultaneous-cuts-on-a-sat-execution.md)

Bob checks that every unlisted nonzero frozen cell has color zero. Alice simulates, caching genuine later writes and using a listed value when one is needed. On an unlisted pre-overwrite request she may use zero only at color one. Any other such read invalidates the certificate. Bob supplies earlier input words as before. Thus a successful certificate always simulates the real run. Honest certificates achieve the displayed communication cost up to a fixed factor, including controllers and answer. For a **fixed** certificate and full transcript, the accepted pairs form a monochromatic rectangle by the local checks. Counting all short strings, with their certificate bits included, gives the same exponential tail. Add this third protocol for every balanced split to the union above.

If a run takes $o(L)$ steps, the binomial term is at most $S_k+D_k=o(L)$, so $R_k+h_k=\Omega(s)$ at every balanced cut. Each of these addresses represents a genuine physical input reread or a value produced before the cut and used afterward. More generally on an $O(L)$-step run, at each such cut either $R_k+h_k=\Omega(s)$ or **both** $S_k$ and $D_k$ are $\Omega(L)$ words. Indeed if $a+b\le C L$ then $\log\binom{a+b}{a}\le t\log(e C L/t)$ for $t=\min(a,b)\ge1$, with value zero at $t=0$. For this cost to be a fixed positive fraction of $L$, $t/L$ must be bounded below. The hidden constants in the dichotomy depend on the fixed running-time constant. The construction imposes no universal sublinear-space restriction.

## An additive memory and rereading bound on that same execution

Summing the sparse-snapshot inequality over the $\Theta(s)$ balanced cuts gives a statement about actual work memory over time. Write $S(t)$ for the number of nonzero working words just before instruction $t$. Let $U$ count probes to a data-bearing input word after the first probe to that word, with multiplicity. For each such probe, count the balanced cuts *after* its preceding same-word probe and *at or before* this probe. Let $g$ be the largest such count, with value zero when $U=0$. Use individual probe times if an instruction performs several probes, changing the cumulative-word bound by at most a fixed factor and an additive constant per selected cut. On the common rejecting run,
$$
                   \sum_t S(t)+gU=\Omega(s^2).
$$
Indeed, each $R_k$ counts the previously seen data words having at least one future probe. For each counted word there is a unique consecutive pair of its probes straddling that cut. Summing $R_k$ therefore counts precisely the total number of straddled balanced cuts across the $U$ revisit intervals. Also the selected cuts are distinct times, so their summed workspace is at most the cumulative workspace.

In particular, reading each data word only once forces $\Omega(s^2)$ word-steps of cumulative memory, even allowing arbitrary input order and unlimited computation. If peak work memory is $o(s)$ and elapsed time is $O(s\log s)$, then $g=\Omega(s/\log s)$: some word is reread only after that many different new edge values were disclosed. The direct per-vertex scanner uses $O(s)$ probes and $O(1)$ work words. At the opposite extreme, a solver can cache every edge datum once and use $O(s)$ work words for $O(s)$ steps on this family. Thus the quadratic tradeoff scale is realized at both ends. It concerns cumulative memory or the temporal separation of genuine input probes and does not force quadratic time.

## The exact budget when sibling intervals are isolated

A natural attempt to sum costs across scales is to fix the true values outside two sibling coordinate groups $A,B$ and prove a separate communication lower bound inside their union. This conditional experiment has an exact answer for any linear code $C$ over $\mathbb F_q$. Write $C[T]$ for the subcode supported on $T$. Given any fixed outside values admitting a completion, the possible pairs on $A,B$ form an affine translate of $C[A\cup B]$. The number of distinguishable shared values is $q^\gamma$, where
$$
     \gamma=\dim C[A\cup B]-\dim C[A]-\dim C[B].
$$
Indeed take the quotient of $C[A\cup B]$ by $C[A]\oplus C[B]$. Each allowable local projection uniquely determines its quotient label, because any two completions with that projection differ on the opposite side alone. Conversely two allowable projections with equal labels can be spliced to a valid pair by adjusting their two single-side subcode elements. An impossible local projection can be rejected immediately. Thus, with unlimited local computation, the parties need only compare the two labels, using at most $\lceil\gamma\log_2 q\rceil+O(1)$ communicated bits and at least $\gamma\log_2 q$ bits by the diagonal rectangles. This includes $\gamma=0$.

For **any** binary tree whose leaves are the distinct input coordinates, these exact exponents telescope over its internal nodes:
$$
               \sum_{\rm nodes}\gamma
                 =\dim C-\sum_{e\in E}\dim C[\{e\}]
                 \le |E|.
$$
For our loopless connected graph this is $s-n+1$, since a nonzero single edge has nonzero boundary. The result holds for a tree chosen after seeing the trace as well. Even adding the constant number of protocol bits per node only gives $O(s\log q)$ bits. In particular, the isolated subproblems do not force more than $O(s)$ word transfers by a communication lower bound. For any distribution of a vector $X_E$, the corresponding identity for conditional mutual information follows by setting $g(T)=H(X_T\mid X_{E\setminus T})$. At a split of $T=A\cup B$, the difference $g(T)-g(A)-g(B)$ is $I(X_A;X_B\mid X_{E\setminus T})$ by the chain rule, so its sum is at most $H(X_E)$. The full-problem suffix lower bounds and the much smaller conditional exponents measure different experiments. An unrestricted superlinear time lower bound still needs an argument charging disjoint native instructions beyond the available scan.
