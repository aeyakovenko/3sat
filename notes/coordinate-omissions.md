# Amplifying witness-count dips across coordinates of one omitted word

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Set $D=n^2$, $k=n$, $M=2^n$ and $U=M^k=2^D$. Read the actual first omitted native $D$-bit output $v$ as $k$ consecutive symbols in $[M]=\{0,\dots,M-1\}$. The machine runs for $U^{20}$ steps on each name shorter than $D$. Replacing one symbol of $v$ by any smaller value while holding the rest fixed gives a genuine output at this same cutoff. Thus every such full-context coordinate line has a genuine first omission. Weak declines in local certificate counts at a positive excess fraction of these symbols would rule out cubic SAT under a quantitative limit on the number of other symbols a verifier needs. ## Adaptive exposure bound

Draw $Z\in[M]^k$ uniformly. For coordinate $i$, a deterministic procedure reads at most $d_i\le k-1$ other symbols, choosing addresses adaptively. Before seeing $Z_i$, it returns a function $F_i:[M]\to[0,\infty)$ whose mean on $[M]$ is at most one for every possible transcript. Different procedures may read the same coordinate arbitrarily often. Then

$$
  \mathbb E_Z\prod_i F_i(Z_i)^{1/(d_i+1)}\le1. \tag{1}
$$

Pad each path to exactly $d_i$ distinct side addresses by adding ignored reads. Draw an independent uniform permutation of the $k$ coordinates. For a fixed tuple, call $i$ ready when all addresses of its actual path precede $i$. Its probability of readiness is $1/(d_i+1)$. For a fixed permutation, expose the independent symbols in that order. Readiness of the current coordinate is determined by the past. Simulate its procedure up to the first request for an unexposed address, or to completion. Multiply by $F_i(Z_i)$ on ready steps and by one elsewhere. Its conditional expected multiplier is at most one, so the expected product is at most one. For each fixed tuple, average over orders and apply the geometric–arithmetic mean inequality to the products. The geometric mean is the left integrand of (1). Zeros follow by using $(F_i+\epsilon)/(1+\epsilon)$ and taking a limit.

Suppose now all $d_i\le d$, and each transcript specifies a hit set of density at most $p\in(0,1)$. Write $X_i$ for its hit indicator at $Z_i$. Pad all paths to $d$. Use $F_i=e^{\lambda X_i}/(1-p+p e^\lambda)$ for $\lambda\ge0$ in (1), apply Markov's inequality and minimize over $\lambda$. For $p<q\le1$,

$$
  \Pr\left[\sum_i X_i\ge qk\right]
    \le \exp\!\left[-\frac{k}{d+1}D_{\rm KL}(q\Vert p)\right], \tag{2}
$$

where $D_{\rm KL}(q\Vert p)=q\ln(q/p)+(1-q)\ln((1-q)/(1-p))$ with the endpoint defined by continuity. Query cycles across procedures are allowed. A procedure choosing its side addresses using its own symbol would defeat the predictability argument. In particular, certifying that a scalar is a dip can require inspecting *several* candidate scalar values. The side transcript must select the entire scalar table. Bounding the side reads separately for each entry does not suffice.

The entire earlier prefix can be read for free in a variant of (1). Allow arbitrary earlier symbols and at most $d$ distinct later symbols on each adaptive path before reading the tested symbol. For $0<\theta<1$, independently mark each coordinate with probability $\theta$. Reveal all unmarked coordinates, then all marked coordinates in increasing order. At a marked coordinate $i$, the test is ready if its path uses only unmarked later coordinates. This is predictable since every earlier coordinate is now exposed. Given a fixed tuple with $r\le d$ later probes at $i$, the joint probability of marking and readiness is $\theta(1-\theta)^r$. On a ready path retain the multiplier with independent probability $(1-\theta)^{d-r}$, now determined by the past. Average over both the mask and these extra coins. Every fixed tuple's $i$-th multiplier is retained with probability $\gamma=\theta(1-\theta)^d$, so the same geometric-mean proof gives $\mathbb E\prod_i F_i^{\gamma}\le1$. Take $\theta=1/(d+1)$ for $d\ge1$. With $d=0$, natural order alone gives $\gamma=1$. Thus arbitrary earlier information and $o(k)$ adaptive later probes still allow amplification at fixed positive excess and zero advice.

Random-order encoding and bounds for local recovery supply relevant precedents. The read-$k$ Chernoff theorem controls how many output functions can use each independent input. Here the hypotheses bound side reads of each procedure and require a uniform fiber-mean bound for its own symbol. No claim of priority for (1) or (2) is established. [Paturi, Pudlák and Zane](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/PaturiPudlakZane_1997_focs.pdf) · [Tamo, Barg and Frolov](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07196) · [Gavinsky, Lovett, Saks and Srinivasan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.1478)

## A condition sufficient against linear or cubic SAT

Fix a uniform side procedure and scalar witness verifier, and allow a shared advice string of $A=A(D)\le D$ bits, with $A$ uniformly computable. For a given transcript let $b(s)=\min(M,W(s))$, where $W(s)$ is the number of fixed-length witnesses that pass at $s$. Let $g(s)$ be the distance to the last earlier position with value at most $b(s)$, counting from $-1$ if none exists. Fix an integer $t\ge2$. A hit means $g(s)\ge t(b(s)+1)$. Capping preserves this test, since values at least $M$ cannot hit, and for smaller values the earlier comparisons are unchanged. The sharp form of the one-dimensional counting lemma gives hit density at most $p=\lfloor M/t\rfloor/M$, positive for all large $n$. [Counting lemma (3)](multiplicity-dips.md)

Suppose ordinary explicit SAT has deterministic time $O(L^\beta)$ at encoded length $L$, with $\beta=1$ or $3$. Let $V$ bound both the uniformly constructible scalar verifier size and witness length, and the time for constructing its context and circuit, up to fixed polynomial factors in $D$. For a threshold $j\le M$, ask whether $j$ passing witness strings exist in strictly increasing lexicographic order. Consecutive comparisons suffice. Copying $j$ circuits and using Tseitin clauses gives length $O(MV\,\mathrm{poly}(D))$ for $V\le U^{O(1)}$. Auxiliary SAT variables are outside the compared strings. Binary search gives $b$ exactly. Compute the gap at the tested position by a backward scan. Across all tuples, advice strings, coordinates and scalar candidates, the decoder therefore takes

$$
  O\!\left(U2^A M (MV)^\beta\,\mathrm{poly}(D)\right)
$$

steps, including ranking by the maximum number of hits over advice. Count-score buckets and lexicographic traversal resolve ties. The simulation on the native interpreter costs a further fixed polynomial in $D$. Since $A\le D$ and $M=U^{1/n}$, for every fixed $\epsilon>0$, **$V\le U^{18-\epsilon}$ for linear SAT and $V\le U^{6-\epsilon}$ for cubic SAT** give total time at most $U^{20-\beta\epsilon+o(1)}$. If $V=M^{O(1)}$, the decoder in fact fits the clock under any fixed polynomial SAT-time hypothesis, since $M^{O(1)}=U^{o(1)}$. Establishing the excess below with verifiers this small would imply $\mathsf P\ne\mathsf{NP}$.

Take $qk$ to be the actual best hit count at $v$. For $q>p$, (2) and the union over advice bound its one-based rank by $U2^A\exp[-kD_{\rm KL}(q\Vert p)/(d+1)]$. A fixed decoder can print a tuple given its rank minus one as a raw binary suffix. Some fixed $c>0$ therefore forces the rank of this actual omission to exceed $cU$ eventually. The decoder never needs $q$ as input. Consequently the stated SAT hypothesis implies

$$
  \frac{kD_{\rm KL}(q\Vert p)}{(d+1)\ln 2}-A\le O(1). \tag{3}
$$

The additive constant depends on the fixed decoder, so a bounded positive surplus gives no contradiction. A qualifying verifier for which the left side is unbounded along actual omissions excludes the corresponding SAT time. For example, fixed $t$, a fixed positive excess $q-p$, $d=o(k)$, and $A=o(k/(d+1))$ suffice. The source definition supplies no verifier with that excess and locality.

With $R$ witness bits, direct enumeration takes $U2^A M2^R V\,\mathrm{poly}(D)$ time for the same table. Whenever this already fits, (3) holds unconditionally. In particular, with no advice, testing all short native names as candidate witnesses, each for $U^{18-\epsilon}$ time or less, gives no qualifying excess on the actual omission. Here there are fewer than $U$ names, and $M=U^{o(1)}$. This tests the early-simulation approach on the original machine even when the tests count false positives. The argument does not cover its full $U^{20}$ cutoff.

**Long shared randomness.** In the $V=M^{O(1)}$ regime a tape of $M^{O(1)}$ random bits can supply common context for all coordinates. It may be conditioned on a nonempty, input-independent live set recognized in deterministic $M^{O(1)}$ time. Fix a uniformly computable rational threshold $q>p$ within this time bound, and write $\mu(z)$ for the fraction of live tapes yielding at least $qk$ hits at $z$. Equation (2), averaged over live tapes, gives $\sum_z\mu(z)\le U\exp[-kD_{\rm KL}(q\Vert p)/(d+1)]$. Under polynomial-time SAT, compute the number of successful live tapes at each tuple within a fixed multiplicative factor, recognizing zero exactly. The test for a given tape is deterministic $M^{O(1)}$ under this hypothesis. Standard approximate counting by affine hashing and the fixed-quantifier shift test then take $M^{O(1)}$ per tuple, including writing the hash seeds. Ranking by approximated numerator suffices because every tuple has the same live denominator. The native rank lower bound gives

$$
  \mu(v)\le C\exp[-kD_{\rm KL}(q\Vert p)/(d+1)]
$$

for some fixed $C$. Thus the penalty for useful random tapes is the negative logarithm of their *relative frequency among live tapes*, even when the tapes are long or survival is extremely rare. [Detailed deterministic counting test](multiplicity-dips.md) · [Stockmeyer](https://doi.org/10.1145/800061.808740) · [Lautemann](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(83)90044-3)

## Many genuine late producers in the prefix tree

At any cutoff $T\le B=U^{20}$, call a child of a prefix unresolved if some completion has no producer by $T$. Take $T=\lfloor U^{19-\epsilon}\rfloor$ for fixed positive rational $\epsilon<1$. Unconditionally, the entire trie of unresolved prefixes can be built within $B$. Simulate all $U-1$ names to $T$, mark their outputs, and traverse the prefix trie. Simulated $D^2$-bit addresses can use a binary trie with $O(D^2)$ cost per access. Along the actual $M$-ary word $v$, let $b_i$ be the number of unresolved children of its length-$(i-1)$ prefix. Its own child is always unresolved. Give each unresolved child score $M/b_i$ and each resolved child zero, using score one everywhere below a prefix with no unresolved child. Under ordered exposure the product has expectation one. Rank all tuples by this product. Each positive product on the unresolved tree is compared using $O(D)$-bit integers. Native incompressibility therefore gives

$$
  \sum_{i=1}^k \ln(M/b_i)=O(1),\qquad \sum_i (M-b_i)=O(M).
$$

Thus across all $k$ prefixes of $v$, only $O(M)$ child blocks combined are already fully covered at $T$. At $B$, every earlier sibling is fully covered. Their number is $\sum_i v_i=(k/2+O(\sqrt k))M$. Indeed, on independent uniform digits the sum has mean $k(M-1)/2$ and two-sided Hoeffding tails $2\exp(-2x^2/[k(M-1)^2])$ at deviation $x$. Rank by absolute deviation and use native incompressibility. Hence at least $(k/2-O(\sqrt{k}))M$ disjoint earlier sibling blocks each contain a genuine output whose first producer takes longer than $T$.

**Near the native cutoff under linear SAT.** Reparse the same $v$ into $D$ binary digits. There are constants $a$ and $\eta>0$ such that, at $T=\lfloor B/D^a\rfloor$, every prefix of $v$ contains at least an $\eta$ fraction of $T$-missing completions. All but $O(1)$ sibling blocks along its binary path have the same property. In particular, at least $D/2-O(\sqrt D)$ *disjoint earlier sibling blocks* each have an $\eta$ fraction of completions for which every producer takes more than $T$ steps, although every completion has a producer by $B$.

For the computation, let $c_T(P)$ count $T$-missing completions of a given prefix $P$. To check $c_T(P)\ge r$, guess $r\le U$ distinct full words in increasing order extending $P$. Universally quantify the short program and its candidate computation transcript. Reject if the transcript proves that this program emitted any word on the list by $T$. The test compares the one claimed output with the entire list and checks one transcript. Its circuit length is $(T+UD)\,\mathrm{poly}(D)$, including the memory-consistency check and the list-order test. Binary search costs $O(D)$ decisions. Fixed-block quantifier elimination under linear SAT computes the count in $(T+U)\,\mathrm{poly}(D)$ time. Polynomially many adaptive prefix queries still finish before $B$ for sufficiently large fixed $a$. The encoding overhead exponent can be chosen independent of $a$ since $\log T\le20D$. [Encoded quantifier elimination](linear-quantifiers.md) · [Bounded native transcripts](native-missing-word.md)

Fix an integer $j$. Follow raw input bits until the first prefix $P$ with $0<c_T(P)\le2^{D-|P|-j}$. At that point, encode the rest of $v$ by its rank among these $c_T(P)$ missing completions. To unrank, use the child counts and descend. The payload uses at most $D-j$ bits including the raw prefix, while a single fixed decoder plus a self-delimiting description of $j$ costs $O(\log(j+2))$ bits. For a sufficiently large fixed $j$ the native omission excludes any such prefix, proving the first claim with $\eta=2^{-j}$. Now enumerate the children whose missing density is at least $\eta$. The actual path always has one. A second fixed decoder reads one raw bit when both qualify and no bits when only one qualifies. More than a constant number of one-child steps would compress $v$, proving the sibling claim. Native incompressibility applied to the fast Hamming-weight ranking gives $D/2+O(\sqrt D)$ ones in $v$. Each one has a strictly earlier sibling, all of whose completions are genuine by $B$. The unknown $\eta$ and the additive constants depend on the decoders.

The source at an earlier sibling is known to have complete coverage at $B$, yet this calculation forces substantial failure of coverage at $T$ in many such siblings. A verifier distinguishing genuine coverage in time below the full simulation budget is still missing.

## Correlated dips at the actual omission

Take fixed $t\ge2$ a power of two dividing $M$. Partition the coordinates into groups of $h=d+1$ when $h\mid k$. Choose a target residue for each group. In a coordinate's scalar table give zero witnesses exactly when the sum of its group, including the candidate scalar, equals the target modulo $t$. Otherwise accept all $M$ binary $n$-bit witnesses. Every coordinate with value at least $t-1$ hits exactly when its group sum has the target residue. Each rare scalar lies $t$ past the previous one, except possibly at the left endpoint. For $h\ge2$, conditioning on the group sums leaves every individual coordinate uniform. All $k$ hit with probability at least $t^{-k/h}(1-k(t-1)/M)$. For $h=1$ choose residue $t-1$ for equality. With $q=1$, (2) gives the matching upper bound $t^{-k/h}$.

For a single group of size $k$, all but $o(U)$ tuples have no coordinate below $t-1$, since the exceptional fraction is at most $k(t-1)/M$. Unconditionally, the native $v$ eventually avoids these exceptions because one fast decoder enumerates the exceptional tuples first. At every sufficiently large length one of the $t$ phases now gives **all $k$ dips at this actual $v$**. A single fixed phase does so infinitely often. Thus even perfect coordinatewise counts in the full context can deliver only one residue constraint on the whole omitted tuple.

What happens when the other coordinates are compressed to a short fingerprint? Consider any uniformly testable sets $S_{i,a,u}\subseteq[M]^k$, with $i\in[k]$, $u\in[M]$, and $a$ an $\ell\le D$-bit string. Assume joint tabulation fits the native clock. This holds for uniformly polynomial-in-$D$ deterministic membership, or under cubic SAT for $M\,\mathrm{poly}(D)$-size existential circuits. Stream the tuples in order, maintaining one counter per set, and rank by the smallest within-set rank among sets containing the tuple. Assign uncovered tuples infinite score. At most $kM2^\ell r$ tuples have score at most $r$, by counting the first $r$ elements of each set. Since $v$'s global rank exceeds $c'U$ for a decoder-specific $c'>0$, **every** such set containing $v$ contains at least $c'M^{k-1}/(k2^\ell)-1$ strictly earlier tuples. Each is a genuine output. An exact uniform multiplicative constant is unavailable. The bound charges both the selection of the coordinate and every bit disclosed about the other coordinates. A short fingerprint shared by many full contexts therefore cannot by itself certify absence of earlier producers in a bucket containing $v$.
