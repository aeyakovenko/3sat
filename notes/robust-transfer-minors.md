# A constant-verdict computation with large memory-transfer minors after prediction

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** The [separator proof](slab-product-tradeoff.md) forces large clique minors on a hypothetical near-linear SAT solver's rejecting runs, even after removing transfers whose values a short policy correctly supplies. Can every linear RAM instead break up its memory-transfer graph by such predictions? There is an unconditional counterexample on ordinary explicit inputs, and a separate unary counterexample under the very fast-SAT hypothesis in question.

One fixed deterministic RAM takes $O(n)$ steps and returns a constant. Fix a policy making $O(\log^d n)$ probes per designated read to the original input, for a fixed $d$. It may use unrestricted local computation in the probe bound below, fresh private scratch, and a common string selected after seeing the whole input. On arbitrarily long inputs, for some fixed $K$, **every** advice string of at most $n/\log^K n$ bits leaves a $K_{\Omega(\sqrt{n/\log n})}$ minor after deleting the transfers whose values it correctly predicts. Here a wrong prediction can simply be retained as an edge. Up to $n/\log^K n$ further edges for loads can be deleted for free. The chronological edges are retained, as in the separator theorem. The controller, address, time, slot and true writing time of each designated load may be given to the policy. The policy has no extra bank containing the precomputed inverses. Under the alternative bounds $n^\theta$ advice bits, $O(n^\sigma)$ probes and $n^\theta$ extra deletions with fixed $\theta,\sigma\ge 0$, $\theta+\sigma<1$, the surviving clique minor has order $\Theta(\sqrt n)$, maximal up to constant factors.

## Reordering a permutation inverse

On the eligible lengths pack a permutation $\pi$ of $[M]$ in $M$ fields of $\log_2 M$ bits, where $M=2^{2j}$ and $n=M\log_2 M$. Use zero-based indices. One input-word probe exposes parts of only a constant number of fields. A scatter pass computes the inverse table in working memory. Put $l=\sqrt M$ and take $d_0$ to be the largest power of two at most $\sqrt{\log_2 M}$. There are $r=d_0l$ producer rows and $r$ consumer columns, so $r^2=\Theta(n)$. At coordinates $(i,j')$ define the key $x=(i\bmod l)l+(j'\bmod l)$.

* In one contiguous phase for each row $i$, write $1+\pi^{-1}(x)$ to the private cell $(i,j')$ for every column $j'$.
* In one subsequent contiguous phase for each column $j'$, read its private cells in increasing row order. Discard each returned word. Halt with a constant verdict.

Use separate banks, including for the scatter, so each designated private cell is written exactly once. Its value is positive. Its sole legal value-supplying anchor is that write, even under the relaxed rule allowing intervening equal writes. Fixed-period loop bodies and clearing scratch registers make the pre-load controller, native slot, actual time and store time computable from the indices and the length. In particular the preceding read's value is gone before the next designated load. Pad loop transitions by a bounded number of steps if needed. Bit extraction, address arithmetic and initialization fit $O(n)$ steps. Inputs of other lengths can return immediately, and out-of-range or repeated fields need no special verdict. The computation is a total constant function. Contract each connected row phase and each connected column phase. Every surviving designated transfer gives the corresponding edge of a bipartite graph on two sides of size $r$.

## How many transfers can the policy supply?

Fix a replica, meaning the two high index parts $\lfloor i/l\rfloor,\lfloor j'/l\rfloor$. This leaves one designated read per permutation key. For any fixed advice, query its policy, interpret an in-range guess as an inverse, and use one more forward probe to test it. Charge a fixed $Q=O(q+1)$ permutation-entry probes per key if the word-probe budget was $q$. The policy's public queried data in this experiment, including the actual producing time, contain no information about the inverse. The same bound works with a guess that is sometimes wrong. We analyze each replica and advice after fixing them, before drawing the permutation. [Earlier contiguous-start inversion test](scatter-inverse-live-reads.md) · [De, Trevisan and Tulsiani on inversion with advice](https://www.ias.edu/sites/default/files/math/De_Trevisan_Tulsiani.pdf)

For $0<\epsilon<1$ put $t=\lfloor\epsilon M/(8Q)\rfloor$, and take $t\ge1$. Draw $t$ distinct keys uniformly in order, independently of $\pi$. For any fixed keys, finding all their preimages with $tQ$ adaptive probes on a uniformly random permutation has probability at most $(tQ/M)^t\le(\epsilon/8)^t$. Exposing fresh forward entries gives a sample without replacement, even if each next address depends on the earlier answers. The extra test probe includes lucky predictions in this bound. Conversely, if at least $\epsilon M$ keys are correctly inverted by one advice string on a given permutation, all sampled keys are correct with probability at least $(\epsilon/2)^t$. Thus the probability over permutations that *some* string of at most $s$ bits succeeds this often on *some* replica is at most

$$d_0^2\,2^{s+1}4^{-t}.$$

One may either union-bound the fixed advice events or choose one good string per successful permutation before sampling keys. For $q=O(\log^d n)$ take $\epsilon=1/\log_2 n$ and $K>d+3$. Then $t\gg s+\log n$ with $s\le n/\log^K n$. Under the polynomial-exponent bounds take $\epsilon=n^{-(1-\theta-\sigma)/2}$, again obtaining $t\gg s+\log n$. There is a permutation on which **all** eligible advice and replicas invert fewer than an $\epsilon$ fraction. The fraction of all edges removed is at most $\epsilon+u/r^2$ for $u$ extra deletions. For polylogarithmic probes the remaining graph has average degree $\Omega(r)$. The Kostochka–Thomason bound gives a clique minor of order $\Omega(r/\sqrt{\log r})$. [Alon, Krivelevich and Sudakov, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.08530)

## A dense bipartite graph after polynomially few missing edges per pair on average

If at most $r^{2-\alpha}$ edges are missing for fixed $\alpha>0$, the graph has a clique minor of order $\Omega_\alpha(r)$. On each side discard vertices with more than $r^{1-\alpha/2}$ missing neighbors. At most $r^{1-\alpha/2}$ vertices per side are discarded. Randomly permute the good left side and use $f=\lfloor r/(4t_0)\rfloor$ disjoint consecutive groups of $t_0>6/\alpha$ vertices each, a fixed integer. For any good right vertex, the chance it misses a particular group is at most $(2r^{-\alpha/2})^{t_0}$ for large $r$. A union bound over at most $r^2$ pairs gives a grouping where every good right vertex touches every group.

Each group also has at least $r-(t_0+1)r^{1-\alpha/2}$ common good right neighbors. Choose a different one as its center, making it a connected branch set. At least $f$ other good right vertices remain as singleton branch sets. This gives a $K_{f,f}$ minor, and pairing its sides gives a $K_f$ minor. In the polynomial-exponent inversion case, $\epsilon+u/r^2$ is inverse polynomial in $r$, as required. A graph with $O(n)$ native edges has clique-minor order $O(\sqrt n)$ by counting edges between branch sets.

The finite branch-set construction and the agreement identity for extending a sound policy are checked in Lean. [Formal statements](../lean/TransferMinorBranches.lean)

## All-zero inputs

There is a version on a single all-zero input at each sufficiently large square power-of-two length $n$. It requires the fast-SAT hypothesis. Generate the [exact hard unary table](output-linear-hard-tables.md) in a separate bank on that input and set $r=\sqrt n$. Use the row and column phases above with the value $1+Y[i r+j']$ at each private cell. Generation plus the two sweeps costs $O(n)$ steps. Only the starting time of the fixed-period sweeps depends on the generating computation, and it uses $O(\log n)$ advice bits. Clear constructor registers and avoid leaving table words in the consumer's pre-load controller. The true producing time for every designated load remains locally known.

Under linear SAT the exact table requires advice plus query time $\Omega(n/\log^J n)$ on a fixed universal RAM evaluator for some fixed $J$. If a fixed polylog-time policy is correct except for $v$ cells (counting abstention among the misses), a sorted dictionary repairs those cells with $O((1+v)\log n)$ bits and polylogarithmic query time. Together with the policy's own string this would give exact table lookup. Choose $K>J+3$. Even after $n/\log^K n$ external deletions and the same budget for advice, $\Omega(n/\log^{J+1}n)$ edges remain. Their bipartite graph has average degree $\Omega(\sqrt n/\log^{J+1}n)$, hence clique-minor order $\sqrt n/\operatorname{polylog}n$. This part permits fallible word predictions.

For any $k=1,2,3$ under an $O(n^k)$ SAT hypothesis, use the [biased-prediction resistant table](biased-hard-table-lookup.md) with fixed slack. With advice plus time at most $n^{1/k^2-\eta}$ for fixed $0<\eta<1/k^2$, a **sound** policy (each supplied answer is correct) covers at most $2n^{-\delta}$ of the pairs, for some $\delta>0$. To see this, run the policy at an indexed read and extend an abstention to the better of two fixed default bits. Agreement is at least one half plus half the coverage. Apply the table theorem with smaller slack to absorb the schedule, default and interpreter overheads. After a further $O(n^{1-\delta'})$ deletions for any fixed $\delta'>0$, the dense graph argument gives a $\Theta(\sqrt n)$ minor. Soundness matters for this last assertion: a constant bit guess can be right at about half the cells with no advice. The construction turns one table produced under the hypothesized solver into a workload with the same value-sensitive graph obstruction, on a simple input and with a constant verdict. A lower-bound argument can still exploit which transfers affect SAT's verdict.
