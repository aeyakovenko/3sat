# How many false two-date events does the SAT argument need to certify?

There is a variant of the native SAT family on which ruling out even a few disjoint pairs would suffice. Let $e$ denote the rank of a designated reporter that really wins with a positive word, and let $i$ be its winning date. An event specifies two **different**, earlier, currently unretired ranks $f,g$: the first wins with zero at $i$ and the second with zero at $i+1$. Give the verifier the true history before $i$, the names $e,f,g$, and the promise that the two named ranks genuinely win with zero on or after $i$. It knows that $e$ is ready; it is never told that $e$ wins today. A certificate must be sound at every eligible start satisfying the promise. At the positive win *every* such event is false.

In the auxiliary enumeration below, the following would exclude unrestricted $O(n^k)$ deterministic time for explicit SAT, $k=1,2,3$. Find one such sound uniform verifier with branch work $2^{(1/k-\epsilon)i}$ for some $0<\epsilon<1/k$. On every sufficiently late designated positive win having at least $e/2$ live earlier ranks which will win with zero, require it to certify exclusions on $\omega(\log^*(e+1))$ **pairwise vertex-disjoint** ordered pairs of those ranks. The precise comparison is **one additional disjoint pair** above an explicit rule. If $z_i$ earlier live ordinary program slots will eventually win with zero, the rule certifies $a_s+z_i$ disjoint honest pairs in bin $s$, where $a_s=\log^*(e+1)$. Achieving $a_s+z_i+1$ at all sufficiently late qualifying wins would suffice. The checker need not know $z_i$; it is used to measure what its accepted witnesses cover. An alternative below gives a threshold computable from the past alone. The pairs and the proofs can be different at each win. Neither an output word nor an actual winner need be identified. Certifying $\omega(e\log^*(e+1))$ distinct ordered events on those wins, without disjointness, would also suffice. The quantifier over sufficiently late qualifying wins matters. One can cheaply certify almost all pairs on an infinite recognizable family of bins. The matching size counts individually certified pairs with no shared endpoint.

These thresholds follow from a stronger counterconstruction under the hypothesized solver. At arbitrarily late genuine positive wins, all but $O(\log^*(e+1))$ earlier ranks form one live set $S$ of eventual zero winners. **No ordered pair of distinct members of $S$ has a timely exclusion.** The conclusion is unconditional for a fixed deterministic verifier at $2^i/(i+1)^B$ and conditional for a nondeterministic verifier at $2^{\lfloor i/k\rfloor}/(i+1)^B$, with sufficiently large fixed $B$. The efficiency measures are on the native computation. The remaining lower-bound question is the existence of a sufficiently successful nondeterministic verifier.

## One pool that changes roles after a win

At the beginning of a finite phase there are $e$ lower ranks, numbered $0$ through $e-1$. A set $P$ of these ranks forms one pool; $h=e-|P|$ lie outside it. A positive tag $e$ offers on each eligible date of the phase and wins by its $(e+1)$st eligible opportunity. In the implementation a date immediately following a win by a lower ordinary raw program during this phase is a cooldown. The entire pool and tag idle on that date. Only finitely many such raw programs exist, each with a single award; the time bound below pays for every cooldown. The responder following a pool win never encounters this condition. Every pool member offers only the all-zero word. On a normal date each live member performs the *same* deterministic search for a timely certified exclusion on an ordered pair of distinct live pool members. It uses the genuine start history and a common public order. If there is a result $(f,g)$, only $f$ offers zero. When a pool member wins in this mode, **every** member switches to response mode on the next date. Only $g$ offers, reconstructing the same choice from the preceding history and actual winner. No member runs a new first-offer rule on a response date. Keep the entire successor available before releasing the group. After the phase, all remaining members offer zero persistently until retirement. This proves the eventual-zero promise regardless of what the verifier or the search did. [Two-group construction and implementation of common searches](negative-winner-rectangles.md)

For a sound test with a correct completed search, the responder $g$ cannot win: the two actual awards would be exactly the pair for which the test certified an exclusion. Some outside rank smaller than $g$ must win in response. Consequently each pool retirement before the tag wins consumes a different outside retirement. There are at most $h$ pool retirements. The tag's actual winning date is normal, since a live $g<e$ would beat it on a response date. An accepted exclusion between any two of the surviving pool members would now produce an earlier offer, contradicting the tag win. These survivors give $S$. Its complement in the prefix consists of the outside ranks and the retired pool ranks and has size at most $2h$. Each accepted off-diagonal pair on this date meets that complement. At most $2h$ pairwise vertex-disjoint pairs and at most $4he$ distinct ordered pairs can meet it. On queries restricted to live ranks every accepted pair must actually touch a live outside rank. Diagonal claims $f=g$ are already excluded by retirement and are omitted throughout. [Lean counting bounds including the diagonal correction](../lean/DenseNegativeWinnerPool.lean)

Rank ordering gives more exact finite information. For $|P|=p\ge2$, let $c$ count the outsiders below the largest pool rank. In the abstract schedule with freely chosen outside offers, omitting raw cooldowns, let $A$ count normal dates on which the search finds a pair before the tag wins, and $l$ count pool retirements. A found pair either loses its first race to an earlier outsider, or wins that race and consumes an outsider at its response. The dates charged this way are distinct. Thus $A\le c$ and $l\le\min(A,p-1)$. All such pairs of counts occur. Keep the largest pool member $z$ live, spend $A-l$ outsiders on immediate preemptions of $z$, and spend the remaining $l$ on responses of $z$ to wins by different first members. No found pair wins both races. This exactness concerns abstract schedules and uses no correctness hypothesis about SAT. ## Putting almost every smaller rank into the pool

Use $t(0)=1$, $t(d+1)=2^{t(d)}$ and $a_0=8$, $a_{s+1}=t(a_s)$. Allocate the rank interval $[a_s,a_{s+1})$ to bin $s$ and use $e=a_{s+1}-1$ for its positive tag. Use $\log^* x$ for the number of iterated base-two logarithms needed to reach at most one. On every bin endpoint $a=a_s$ is **exactly** $\log^*(e+1)$. Initialize the eight ranks below $a_0$ as ordinary constant-zero reporters. They retire after activation and finitely many possible preemptions. Assign the following roles in rank order.

* Rank $a$ simulates ordinary word program number $s$. This reserves one slot for every ordinary word reporter over the entire enumeration.
* Rank $b=a+1$ is a control responder. Its next $b+1$ ranks offer zero persistently throughout the phase, until they win. The responder offers zero only directly after the genuine win of one of these suppliers.
* The next $a$ ranks are guards. They wait through the phase and its final possible response and then persistently offer zero until retirement.
* Every remaining rank before $e$ belongs to $P$. The tag is the last rank in the bin. The cooldown after a lower raw win idles the tag and pool only; the control suppliers and their responder obey their own rules. The entire prefix below $e$ therefore has at most $3a+4$ ranks outside $P$, counting all earlier bins.

The control responder wins immediately after a supplier on some genuine pair of dates before the tag. Otherwise all $b+1$ suppliers would retire before the tag and the response at each of their successor dates would be preempted by a different rank below $b$. There are only $b$ such ranks. Thus the same phase really contains two consecutive zero wins. Knowing its role layout cannot certify that the whole phase avoids double negatives.

Use a fixed computable dispatch on $s$ that visits each finite descriptor infinitely often; for example decode the exponent of two in $s+1$ as an index in a length-ordered list. A descriptor can name a deterministic verifier and its cap exponent, or a nondeterministic verifier, a proposed deterministic SAT solver, $k$, and a cap exponent $B$. Malformed descriptors may idle in the search. In the deterministic case enumerate the candidate pairs and run the common bounded test; in the nondeterministic case compile each bounded existence test to ordinary SAT and run the indicated solver. Keep the lexicographically first acceptance. When a timeout occurs during search, idle without using a partial selection. The response reconstructs the preceding start with exactly the same width, cap and input data. The proofs above concern bins matching the **true** fixed verifier and, when needed, an actually correct solver. Other bins are still finite, clocked RAM computations.

All bin programs except the ordinary raw slot wait for a common public $J_s$. Let $K_e$ be the first stage with the entire bin active and $2^{e+1}\le K_e+2$, and use $J_s=(e+2)^2(K_e+1)$. The $K_e$ are nondecreasing. Consecutive endpoints differ by much more than three, so $J_{s+1}>J_s+4e+7$, using the specified bin endpoints. The start can be recovered by a polynomial-in-stage scan. Write $J=J_s$. The first date is eligible. Thereafter at most $s+1$ phase dates can be cooldowns, since each requires the preceding award to a different lower raw rank. On all other dates the tag offers until it wins. Thus the win occurs no later than $D=J+2e+2$. Maintain the cooldown and pending-response conventions through $D+1$ and from $D+2$ make every still-live non-raw member of this bin offer zero persistently. As long as any such member remains, a different rank at most $e$ wins each date. Within another $e+1$ dates all these members retire. Thus all earlier bins are clear of live non-raw ranks before the next public start, irrespective of the behavior of their dispatched verifier or proposed SAT solver. With a fixed matching descriptor and sufficiently large fixed $B$, each complete normal search and replay at these starts fits inside $O(2^i/(i+1)^2)$ interpreter ticks. There are only polynomially many pairs in $i$. The packed SAT simulation costs $(T_i+\operatorname{poly}(i))^k\operatorname{poly}(i)$, including the genuine prefix and any permitted local physical formula reads. The per-rank allowance is at least a constant times $2^i/(i+2)$. For each fixed tuple all sufficiently late matching bins meet these estimates. An unknown SAT runtime constant is never used as a test for activating a bin. [Compilation on the unrestricted machine](packed-band-transfer.md)

This is a **different auxiliary reporter enumeration** in one fixed uniform native program. Its physical pins are again ordinary explicit 3-CNFs of $2^i\operatorname{poly}(i)$ encoded bits. The word RAM deciding those inputs has the original instruction set and unrestricted workspace. To classify an active rank at most $i$, cap the bin-boundary computation at $i+2$. During a tower calculation, before forming $2^x$, compare $x$ with the bit length of the cap, and stop if the cap is reached. Even the number of tower levels can be truncated on saturation. Incomplete bins idle apart from their ordinary raw slot. Scanning previous stages for the common activation costs polynomial time in $i$; compiled stage code and rank metadata are short. The mask side of the original family can be kept. Each fixed ordinary program $s$ has a fixed finite raw rank and hence a fixed positive share of $2^i$ eventually, sufficient for the genuine-history retirement argument. The actual solver receives the resulting explicit formula with its full length charged. At the actual tag win, every current control supplier and the control responder have also retired. Among live outsiders only the $a_s$ guards and some of the $s+1$ ordinary raw slots from bins $0,\ldots,s$ remain, on every sufficiently late bin. If $u_i$ of those raw slots are still live and $z_i$ of those live slots eventually win with zero, each certified honest pair on the matching positive date has an endpoint among the $a_s$ guards or those $z_i$ raw slots. An honest matching has at most $a_s+z_i$ edges. Without the eventual-zero promise one counts all live endpoints instead; the corresponding bound is $a_s+u_i$. The actual past suffices to compute $u_i$, and $u_i\le s+1$. Thus an unconditionally sound verifier for all live endpoints could use the public success threshold $a_s+u_i+1$. In the promised version $a_s+s+2$ honest pairs always suffice, without calculating $z_i$. The input promise concerns individual candidate pairs on genuine starts and never asserts that $e$ wins today. On other bins there may be very few live pool ranks; the qualifying condition in the statement retains all of the matching hard bins.

## Which bounds can actually be attained?

Use the following fixed deterministic rule on any descriptor. Require a genuine eligible start and a live second rank in $P$. If the live first rank is a current guard, it cannot offer now. If it is a lower raw rank and does win now, the second date is a cooldown, when the entire pool idles. In both cases certify the exclusion. The rule only inspects bin roles, the phase and the past. It costs polynomial time in $i$ and is sound even without any future-outcome promise.

At any sufficiently late qualifying positive win there are at least $e/2-a_s-s-1$ live pool members among the eventual-zero ranks, enough to pair every guard and every live raw rank with a different one. Every remaining pool member will win with zero. Matching just the promised endpoints gives exactly $a_s+z_i$ disjoint certified honest pairs for this rule. Counting all live raw slots instead gives exactly $a_s+u_i$ with the publicly recognizable endpoints. The rule also certifies $\Omega(e\log^*(e+1))$ honest ordered exclusions there. On its own hard matching bins in deterministic mode, the universal upper bounds match those counts of disjoint pairs exactly. The bound for the count of ordered pairs matches in order of growth. Thus the orders of growth for the pair count and the disjoint-pair count are sharp for this enumeration. Padding the bins differently changes the factor; $\log^*e$ expresses the chosen rank allocation, not a time bound on a SAT input.

There are also genuine positive wins where a fixed deterministic verifier certifies at least $(1-O(\log^*(e+1)/e))e(e-1)$ ordered events. Take the infinite bins whose dispatch explicitly simulates an always-abstaining checker in deterministic mode. On those bins no pool member makes a search offer. A new verifier recognizes that descriptor, the phase and the pair membership using just the bin metadata, and soundly excludes all distinct live pool pairs. At the positive win all these ranks still await their later zero awards. More broadly, for a fixed trusted sound checker with correct search, a response to any genuine first pool win must be preempted, giving the same cross-pair rule inside its pool. Applying such a rule to its own matching bins would require the very soundness justification under investigation. [Using arithmetic proofs to certify exclusions](negative-winner-rectangles.md)

The public rule can be combined with a proof for even one specifically named pair wholly inside the live pool at each sufficiently late qualifying win. Reserve those two vertices, then match the outsiders into other pool vertices; there are plenty left. This would beat the exact threshold by one. A certificate that the common search cannot select that particular pair would suffice, provided the proof is sound on all genuine eligible starts. Checking that a different lexicographically earlier pair has a bounded accepting branch can rule out later choices when the search is correctly implemented. At the hard matching positive wins there is no such branch for any live pool pair. The available upper bound for replaying the whole search to establish that absence exceeds the required verifier budget. A single fixed deterministic test can already alternate between near-complete coverage and coverage of order $e\log^* e$ on different subsequences, by combining the guard-and-cooldown rule and the recognized abstainer rule. I have no sound short certificate for even one named pool pair on every sufficiently late qualifying win. These are verifier-dependent resource bounds and a constructive partial verifier on a changed native family. Historical priority for this coverage threshold has not been established.
