# Certifying a positive mass of a private-coin RAM computation

Consider the fixed deterministic native program that computes the bit $b_j$ in $O(M)$ time on the canonical input of length $M=2^j$. For either fixed color, every sound deterministic detector for that color needs $\Omega_D(M)$ time whenever it signals at any sufficiently large scale. The program uses ordinary explicit word operations. Fix an interactive verifier $V$ claiming the color $c$. On every scale with $b_j\ne c$, each prover strategy has acceptance probability at most $1/3$. On the canonical input the verifier can use private fair coin flips, arbitrary conversation length and ordinary random-access workspace. A strategy sees exactly the public messages and frames in its conversation. A machine carrying out an ordinary SAT algorithm can be used as the verifier or inside it. [Pinned program and detector theorem](priority-pinned-sat.md)

A finite certificate specifies a pruned execution tree for one deterministic prover strategy at one scale. At a private coin flip it can explore either or both outcomes, or abandon the branch. It can abandon any other branch as well. It may credit acceptance only after actually reaching an accepting halt. Give an accepting leaf the weight $2^{-d}$ when its path used $d$ coin flips. Let $Q$ count the instructions, message tokens, branch records and leaves in the part explored, sharing the work on a common path prefix. Words have $O(j)$ bits. A certificate succeeds if the sum of its credited weights exceeds $1/3$. Completeness of the protocol is needed only at the scales where a successful certificate is to be deduced.

**Transfer.** If ordinary explicit SAT has a deterministic $O(N^k)$ algorithm, $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, then at every sufficiently large scale *every* successful tree has
$$
 Q=\Omega_{V,k}\!\left(\frac{M^{1/k}}{(j+1)^3}\right).
$$
The constant also depends on the hypothesized solver. This holds even if the omitted random outcomes have unbounded running time. In particular, if more than $1/3$ of the honest random tapes accept within $v$ work and message tokens and at most $r$ coin flips, then $2^r(v+r+1)$ obeys the same lower bound. Truncation keeps precisely the short accepting executions; the complete $r$-coin tree has at most $2^r$ copies of a length-$v+r$ path. Private randomness and the number of rounds require no separate assumptions. This specializes to the usual completeness $2/3$ and worst-case $r,v$ bounds. Logarithmic randomness and polynomial strategy or tree size have classical precedents. The parameter here is the least charged work on a pruned tree that already beats soundness, with the ordinary 3-CNF length accounted for. [Condon and Ladner](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.1995.1040)

For existence of a short proof with a freely chosen verifier, an ordinary nondeterministic witness is itself a one-path tree with no coins. Conversely, the checker below turns any successful tree into an ordinary nondeterministic witness at its charged cost. The transfer becomes informative for a **specified** statistical protocol when its shortest high-mass tree can be bounded independently. A generic existence claim for such trees repackages the short-witness requirement.

## Checking the explored tree in linear native time

Simulate the real verifier depth first. At each explored coin node the checker chooses the subset of its two **different** children. Keep a stack of the pending alternatives, their registers, program counters, depth and position in the public conversation. Log the old word for each guest store. Pop and restore these stores back to the fork before exploring the second child. Every simulated instruction and every undo are charged a constant number of times. Give each branch a forced cutoff at the total public exploration budget. An accepting leaf is a cylinder of full private coin tapes and distinct credited leaves have disjoint cylinders. Use one fair-bit instruction per exposed flip. Any instruction supplying a whole random word may be normalized with the cost of its independent bits counted.

Enforce a single deterministic prover across hidden verifier histories. Intern each public prefix in a trie whose transition key consists of the current prefix ID, the sender or frame tag, and the next public word. Include observable turn requests, message boundaries, and any public timing that the model makes available. On each prover turn choose a token only if that observable history has no binding. Reuse the binding on every later visit. Private coin values, the hidden state, and previously explored siblings are absent from the lookup key. The *strategy table itself* persists across all guest rollbacks. Normalize messages to streams of charged tokens with a charged end marker. Sending an unobserved token influences no earlier history. Thus the table on the explored histories extends to a total strategy; it cannot choose different replies to private states with identical observations. Conversely, fixing any strategy gives permissible bindings whenever a new public history is first explored. Randomized provers may be conditioned on their own tape.

For caps polynomial in $M$, the trie ID, tag and a logarithmic-width guest token fit into a fixed number of native words. One may concatenate them into a sparse direct address on a host with a sufficiently large fixed width coefficient. At most $O(Q)$ trie transitions and bindings are written. Guest state, undo stack, prefix table and counters use separate tagged initially zero banks. On the virtual source the width coefficient can be chosen once for $Q\le M^{O(1)}$, with a fixed positive power of $M$ as the minimum advertised compiler clock. The original fixed-color detector can simulate wider polynomial-range sparse addresses for its first $O(M)$ steps at constant overhead after $o(M)$ setup as in the pinned construction.

The exact threshold comparison takes $O(Q)$ word operations even for a branch with $Q$ private flips. Increment $h_d$ for each credited leaf of depth $d$ and record the maximum depth $D$. Start with $3h_d$ units at every depth. For $d=D,\ldots,1$ add the quotient by two to the next shallower count and keep just the remainder at this depth. Each operation preserves the total dyadic value, since two depth-$d$ units equal one depth-$(d-1)$ unit. All integer counts are at most $3Q$. The remaining deep digits sum to less than one. Accept precisely when the depth-zero count exceeds one, or equals one with some deeper digit set. This implements the strict inequality even when its gap is exponentially small. Prefix-freeness and the single prover strategy show that acceptance witnesses a true color. [Carry identity in Lean](../lean/PrivateCoinTreeMass.lean)

## Turning optimal tree cost into an ordinary SAT bound

Take the cap $B$ for explored nodes and accept iff the nondeterministic checker finds such a tree within it. Its own enforced clock is $O(B)$ on every branch. Construct its ordinary 3-CNF using the packed RAM compiler with a virtual unary length supplying its native width, fixed public metadata $(j,B)$, and free choices for the proof. Throughout $M^\delta\le B\le M^C$ for fixed positive $\delta,C$, its address and word widths are $O(j)$. A suitable dummy length is $\Theta(B)$; the guest verifier still sees its *own* canonical input of length $M$, whose words are locally generated. The formula has $O(B(j+1)^3)$ encoded bits. A packed word is available in constant native time after polynomial-in-$j$ setup. In particular, creating the virtual input incurs no $M$-step initialization. [Packed compiler](packed-band-transfer.md)

A deterministic color detector starts at a dyadic cap within a factor two of $M^{1/(2k)}$, doubles the cap and runs the hypothesized SAT solver on each formula until a YES answer. It may stop at a cap of order $M$ and give no signal there on a NO. For a successful tree of size $Q\le M$, the detector signals in
$$
 O_{V,k}\!\left((\max\{M^{1/(2k)},Q\}(j+1)^3)^k+\operatorname{poly}(j)+M^{1/4}\operatorname{poly}(j)\right)
$$
steps when testing the relevant caps. The sum of the powered caps is geometric. The last additive term allows the native normalization tables of the priority proof. For the lower bound it suffices to simulate the first $O(M)$ steps faithfully, with sufficient headroom in the fixed stopping constant or with the signed signal emitted immediately on success. All queries are sound even at true scales with insufficient cap. An $\Omega_D(M)$ signal bound from the fixed detector gives the assertion, since the initial cap costs $o(M)$. Larger trees already satisfy it. There is no algorithmic requirement to know the shortest successful tree or the lengths where it exists.

There is also an **unconditional** assertion about every ordinary deterministic SAT solver $D$. Fix a single cap schedule $M^\delta\le B_j\le M^C$ computable from $j$ in $o(M)$ time in the stage model, and form the individual formula just described. At every sufficiently large scale at which that formula is satisfiable, $D$ takes $\Omega_{V,B,D}(M)$ time on that very formula. Otherwise the detector using this one scheduled call could signal the color too quickly. If the cap yields a successful tree at *every* sufficiently large true-color scale, the scheduled formula computes the color bit exactly there and on the opposite color. Hardwiring any finite initial exceptions then gives the same $\Omega(M)$ time bound on each verdict. The formula length in this assertion is $O(B_j(j+1)^3)$. An actual protocol giving a suitable power saving in $B_j$ would yield the unrestricted SAT exclusion, even if trees that small occur at only infinitely many true-color scales under the fast-SAT hypothesis. The existence of that protocol remains unresolved.

## A deterministic bound on the private-coin game itself

There is a second unconditional conclusion for the same ordinary priority computation. Fix publicly computable caps $r,p,v$ at scale $j$ for private fair bits, prover action bits and verifier work including communication. Normalize the prover stream to binary choices, including its observable end markers in $p$. Treat any exceeded cap as rejection. If the truncated protocol's best acceptance probability is greater than $1/3$, then at every sufficiently large such scale
$$
 2^{r+p}(v+r+p+1)=\Omega_{V,r,p,v}(M)
$$
provided the caps can be obtained from $j$ in $o(M)$ time. Caps growing so large that the displayed left side already exceeds $M$ need no simulation. This gives a tradeoff about statistical proofs for actual native code independently of the running time of SAT.

For the remaining scales, enumerate all $2^r$ private tapes and all $2^p$ prover bit strings, simulating the verifier with each pair. Build one trie of the resulting public dialogues. On an accepting halt reached after $b$ prover bits, credit the terminal node with one unit for this tape only when the last $p-b$ bits of the enumerated prover string are all zero. This suffix rule counts each public action path compatible with a private tape exactly once. Unused private bits, in contrast, count separately because each padded private tape carries probability $2^{-r}$. Normalize the public framing so a prover node begins after a visible request. A terminal event can be represented as a distinguished verifier event with its accepting count at the leaf. Unseen replies can be assigned weight zero.

Work up the trie. At a prover choice use the maximum child weight, since one public history must determine one reply. At a verifier event use the sum: for a fixed previous dialogue, different next observable verifier events have disjoint private-tape sets. Optimal strategies from these children can be joined because their public histories have diverged. By induction the root weight is exactly $2^r$ times the truncated game's value. Hidden accept and reject bits at the same terminal dialogue contribute their correct accepting tape count. Trie construction and evaluation take $O(2^{r+p}(v+r+p+1))$ word steps, with one sparse child lookup per public token and counters fitting in $O(j)$ bits in the nontrivial range. If the optimum exceeds $1/3$, a deterministic color detector can signal. The dyadic detector lower bound gives the inequality.

For a test of the quantifiers, let a verifier hide one fair bit, ask for one bit, and accept exactly when the answer matches the hidden bit. Its value is $1/2$ and independent replies at the two private successors would spuriously give value one. A verifier can also accept immediately on the zero outcome of its first coin and, on the other outcome, flip arbitrarily many more coins. The single successful leaf has weight $1/2$ regardless of the work on the omitted outcome. The theorem measures the cost of actual high-mass accepting histories, which can be much smaller than the full execution tree. The finite audit checks 256 two-turn private-coin games with an adaptive public challenge, and tests the carry computation on pruned trees including gaps smaller than $2^{-1000}$. The simulated machine remains the explicit priority computation.
