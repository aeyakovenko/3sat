# What do trusted SAT answers buy on the next query?

A single uniform family of ordinary explicit 3-CNFs has the following two properties. The learner gets the **entire sequence of previous answer tables** in compressed form, and can ask for true decisions on current members before it predicts further decisions. Use a logarithmic-word RAM with unrestricted workspace.

* At every sufficiently late stage except overrides obeying the counting bound below, a fixed fast learner with $b$ bits of common advice can correctly predict at most $b+O_D(1)$ successive independent new answer bits before its first error, however it interleaves predictions with calibration queries. An answer counts as new if its linear form lies outside the span of all labels already revealed. Each true answer is revealed after the learner bets or requests calibration. The learner can choose its queries, abstain and stop adaptively. The same stages have unconditional two-sided prediction hardness on a uniform balanced label. The fast clock is $N/(\log N)^C$ for encoded length $N$ and sufficiently large fixed $C$.
* At **every** sufficiently late stage a fast deterministic rule that is uniformly sound with the real teacher can certify only conclusions logically forced by its transcript, even on counterfactual transcripts. Under deterministic $O(N^k)$ SAT, $k=1,2,3$, this extends to nondeterministic rules with clock $2^{\lfloor j/k\rfloor}/(j+1)^B$ at stage $j$, for sufficiently large fixed $B$. A rule with a single sound extrapolation outside these logical consequences at infinitely many stages would therefore exclude the corresponding unrestricted SAT clock. Soundness in this statement covers every allowed guess. The first statement allows even stage-specific advice chosen in hindsight.

The exceptional stages can total at most $R(J)$ through stage $J$, for **any** fixed nondecreasing unbounded polynomial-in-$J$-time computable integer $1\le R(J)\le J$. For a density-one guarantee choose $R(J)=o(J)$, for example an iterated logarithm. Their sparsity conceals a genuine obstruction to using an online estimate of which stages will be easy.

## The native construction

Fix a positive integer $A$, $M_j=2^j$, $b=\lfloor\log_2(j+1)\rfloor$, $h=b+(2A+3)\lceil\log_2(j+2)\rceil$, $Q=2^h$, and $H=j+1$. Select one word $a_j\in\mathbb F_2^h$ at each stage. Every opponent receives the public stage parameters and the already completed history $(a_i,\mathrm{winner}_i)_{i<j}$, including which stages were overrides. This costs polynomially many bits in $j$. It determines every earlier SAT verdict in the family. No opponent receives the current default, the current winner, or future words.

The first $H$ deterministic programs in a fixed length-ordered list get individual budgets of at most $M_j/H$ universal instructions to print masks on the $Q$ possible words. Each mask is an explicitly streamed table of $Q$ bits. The interpreter supplies each mask reporter with its index $i\ge0$ as metadata. It can use two channels, with $b_0=0$ and $b_1=b$. On channel $m$ accept at most $2^{b_m+1}-1$ complete masks per reporter. Keep only masks of density at most $2^{-b_m-i-4}$. Ignore malformed or unfinished records. Summing the union bound over the two channels, the retained masks cover at most half of all words, since $\sum_{i\ge0}2^{-i}=2$. Use the lexicographically first uncovered word as the default. For $i+b_m+4>h$ only an empty mask qualifies. The postprocessing and storage of the retained complete masks are polynomial in $j$. Bounded streaming parsers charge rejected or excess output to its reporter’s simulation budget.

Independently give the first $R(j)$ fixed word reporters geometric budgets $\lfloor M_j/2^{e+1}\rfloor$ in order of index $e\ge0$. Skip reporters that previously won. At the first well-formed timely word take that word as $a_j$ and retire the winner permanently. With no such report take the default. Replay stages in order through $j$ with the genuine prior history available from a read-only bank to both kinds of reporter. All budgets sum to $O(2^j)$ over all stages. The initialized-on-demand interpreter banks and fixed word-width adaptation are as in the earlier native construction. Time to populate and access the prior-history interface, in addition to its reads by the programs, is polynomial per stage. An index can win only after activation and at most once. This proves the asserted bound on overrides. [Native interpreter](priority-pinned-sat.md) · [Earlier one-word priority argument](collision-forecasts-for-sat.md)

Compile the fixed native program once per stage with $h$ output bits, using its fixed $O(M_j)$ clock and the parsimonious computation encoding. For the public label $(x,\tau)\in\mathbb F_2^h\times\{0,1\}$ pin inputs $x$, compute $a_j\cdot x$ by Boolean gates and require that it equal $\tau$. Every label gets a fixed-length ordinary 3-CNF with three distinct literals per clause and $M_j\le N_j\le M_j(j+1)^{O_A(1)}$. Exactly half have a unique satisfying assignment, the rest have none; the auxiliary variables, including padding constants, have fixed values once the inputs are known. A physical input word is generated from the stage, address and label in polynomial-in-$j$ work **without knowing $a_j$ or the past**. Thus a clocked opponent can inspect the same physical inputs even in a simulation giving it hypothetical teacher replies. The short trusted history is an additional input to the learner, and is recomputed from scratch by the native program represented in the formulas. ## Tests under truthful adaptive calibration

Here the masks emitted by a fixed reporter, one per advice string, are the trial words $u$ on which the learner displays a specified behavior. Simulate it with the true prior history fixed, all physical CNFs unchanged, and current teacher answers $u\cdot x$ (remove any stated pin when recording a reply). Treat $u$ as uniform **only to bound mask cardinality**. Conditional on a deterministic transcript, $u$ is uniform on the affine subspace cut out by its revealed labels and bits. All remaining internal state, including physical formula reads, is fixed by that transcript, the history and the chosen advice. A probe outside their span splits this affine space into two equally large fibers. Pair them by a translation annihilating all earlier rows and pairing to one with the new row. Dependent probes reveal no further bit. Selection of the next row, the decision to bet, and its direction must occur before seeing its reply.

For a candidate advice string, include $u$ in the test mask if the learner makes $r=b_m+i+4$ correct bets on new rows before its first incorrect such bet. Skip calibration queries and dependent rows. This event has probability at most $2^{-r}$ even with intervening adaptively chosen truthful calibrations and early stopping. For instance, from a fair node of capital one, double the capital on a correct new bet and lose it on the other branch. Freeze it upon reaching $2^r$. Its expectation cannot increase on the finite tree, so the boundary is reached on a fraction at most $2^{-r}$. A false prediction on a dependent row can simply be ignored, which strengthens the bound. A learner whose claims are sound on a particular stage and advice thus obtains at most $b_m+i+3$ new bets there on a default stage. With $b'$ memorized genuine answers on independent standard basis rows the learner can always output $b'$ correct new bets, for $b'\le b$. A bound that forbade even one correct unadvised bet on every late default would be contradicted by the two constant guesses on the same nonzero row. A chain of redundant true conclusions is unrestricted.

A reporter can simulate the entire capped interaction for every trial word and permissible advice in time $Q2^{b+1}(T+1)\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$, with fixed-program virtual word generation and fresh memory for each run. For sufficiently large fixed $B$, $T=\lfloor M_j/(j+1)^B\rfloor$ fits inside its $M_j/H$ budget with all tables complete. Each learner has a fixed reporter index. For the statement in encoded length, compute the exact fixed-layout length from the stage and cap the simulated learner directly at $\lfloor N_j/\lceil\log_2(N_j+2)\rceil^C\rfloor$. This is within $T$ for sufficiently large fixed $C$. A timed-out forecast uses its specified default at that very cutoff. The no-advice statement uses its separate channel with $b_0=0$. This also proves a more general finite test. Any uniformly computable event on these capped interactions with fair-word probability at most $2^{-b_m-i-4}$, testable within the same budget, fails for the genuine word on all sufficiently late default stages; the index refers to its actual reporter.

For the two-sided forecast, the learner receives the past and **no current calibration** and is evaluated on a uniform label with either fixed timeout bit. For each advice, generate the forecast table once, undo the two pins and take the pin-averaged signed guess $g(x)$. For a trial word $u$ the accuracy gap is $|Q^{-1}\sum_x g(x)(-1)^{u\cdot x}|/2$. Parseval bounds the density of words with gap greater than $\epsilon=(j+1)^{-A}$ by $1/(4Q\epsilon^2)$, eventually at most $2^{-b_m-i-4}$ for the fixed reporter. Its mask is computable within budget, as the number of labels and all Walsh-transform costs are polynomial in $j$. The near-fair accuracy follows on the very same default stages. Without a novelty condition, current calibration would let the learner simply request the verdict it was asked to predict. ## Why a sound rule can never certify a fresh answer cheaply

Allow more general current teacher queries whose answers are fixed deterministic functions of the trial word, question, stage and fixed previous history, as long as each can be simulated in polynomial-in-$j$ time. For example these include arbitrary polynomial-in-$j$-size Boolean circuits on the current $h$ hidden bits. A conclusion can name any similarly testable predicate of the word. When $u=a_j$, the teacher is genuine. Fix a rule sound on every actual stage, including on every branch of any allowed guesses. Finitely many exceptional initial stages may instead be hardwired out.

If, on some simulated trial $u$, the rule outputs a conclusion false of $u$, that word is a sound exclusion $u\ne a_j$. Otherwise the actual computation with the same guesses and true answers would have made precisely the same false claim. A fixed deterministic word reporter that outputs such an exclusion cannot ever win. Eventually it is active and all earlier ranks that will win have already done so. From then on even a single timely exclusion by that reporter would win its own rank. The fixed-rank time budget is a constant fraction of $M_j$; hence no uniformly sound reporter can produce such exclusions in $o(M_j)$ time on infinitely many actual histories.

For a deterministic fixed rule enumerate all $Q=\operatorname{poly}_A(j)$ trial words, clock the interaction, and report any trial falsifying the final claim. This takes $o(M_j)$ at the sublinear clock above with sufficiently large $B$. This proves the all-late-stages conclusion without a SAT hypothesis. For a nondeterministic rule at the rooted clock, assume an unrestricted deterministic $O(N^k)$ ordinary SAT algorithm. A bounded nondeterministic computation guesses $u$ consistent with a prescribed prefix and the rule's choices, simulates its teacher from $u$, and checks that its conclusion is false of $u$. It receives the **actual previous history** from the reporter as explicit public data. The packed bounded-computation encoding and the assumed solver decide this existence question in $T^k(j+1)^{O(1)}$ time, including for $k=1$. Use an existence query and then $h$ prefix queries to recover one actual falsifying trial. For sufficiently large fixed $B$ the sum is $o(M_j)$. Any untrusted advice included among the guesses requires soundness for every such guess. A privately selected correct advice string would invalidate this step. [Bounded-computation elimination](packed-band-transfer.md) · [Quasilinear SAT reductions](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf)

Consequently every conclusion reached within the specified clock at a sufficiently late stage must hold for **every** word that gives the recorded sequence of teacher replies, since each such word reproduces the same path. For parity teachers and a parity conclusion this says exactly that the target row is in the span of the calibration rows and its bit is their corresponding parity. Two slopes differing by a null vector prove necessity. More generally the test applies to nonlinear queries and conclusions on any reachable hypothetical path. Actual formulas can expose information about $a_j$ through their computation constraints. The result bounds how quickly a sound procedure can combine that information with the teacher's bits. An unconditional short nondeterministic extrapolation on infinitely many stages remains a sufficient missing step.

## Why the sparse exceptions cannot all be removed

Take any past-dependent deterministic polynomial-in-$j$ rule selecting infinitely many stages **along this actual history**, and any similarly computable proposed word $f(j,\mathrm{past})$. Infinitely many selected stages have $a_j=f(j,\mathrm{past})$. Otherwise hardwire the last matching selected stage and output $f$ exactly on later selected stages. This is a fast, uniformly sound exclusion on infinitely many stages, contradicting retirement. In particular $a_j=0$ on infinitely many stages selected by any such rule. A predictor that always uses the zero slope gets every label right there. All sufficiently late such hits are overrides, by two-sided hardness on defaults. Thus although overrides can be limited by the chosen arbitrarily slowly growing $R$, a fast past-dependent selector cannot pick an infinite subsequence of all-default stages. This also proves that imposing the unrestricted sound-exclusion property and two-sided near-fairness at *all* late stages of this same family is impossible.

The [trace-product application](trace-product-quadratic-correlation.md#how-many-true-sat-answers-can-certify-a-native-verdict) gives an explicit target and an exact calibration threshold. The two branches incur distinct costs. On the default branch the native program can afford to test many virtual learners because the public label is short. On the override branch it can refute one actual proposed exclusion. Neither operation makes the ordinary formula smaller than the time spent running its interpreter. All three unrestricted worst-case SAT exclusions remain open. The narrow genericity and elementary fair-game estimates used here are diagonalization and counting arguments. Priority for this particular SAT formulation has not been established.
