# The treewidth of a clause-splitting history

Starting with the eight signed clauses on three variables, repeated fresh-variable splits always produce a formula of primal treewidth at most three. One globally correct RAM solver takes linear time in the encoded bit length on every such history, even when the split choices, clause order, signs and variable names are hidden. A different growth rule joins two old edges of a graph. Starting with the same eight clauses, it produces minimally unsatisfiable proper 3-CNFs of unbounded primal treewidth.

## The whole closure of the fresh split

The primal graph has a vertex for every used variable and an edge for every pair of variables sharing a clause. In a tree decomposition every edge is covered by a bag and the bags containing each particular vertex form a connected subtree. The width is one less than the maximum bag size. A clause's variables occur together in some bag. Indeed they are a clique, and their pairwise-intersecting bag subtrees have a common node by the Helly property for subtrees of a tree.

Choose any clause with literals $a,b,c$ and replace it, for fresh $z$, by $(a\lor b\lor z)$ and $(a\lor c\lor\neg z)$. Attach a new leaf bag containing the three old variables and $z$ to an old bag containing the three old variables. This covers all new edges and preserves the connectedness condition. After clause deletions one may remove unused names from every old bag, so a name fresh for the current formula can be reused. The three-variable seed uses a single bag of size three. Induction gives width at most three for *all* choices at *every* step. The bound also holds if the old scopes in each new clause are given arbitrary signs, if clauses are deleted or permuted after a step, or if all variable names are permuted. A disjoint satisfiable filler of bounded width costs at most its own width. The proof needs neither the order of construction nor witnesses for individual clauses. [Original split and minimality argument](minimal-sat-cores-under-one-more-clause.md)

More generally a substitution can replace one clause by a gadget meeting the old formula only in that clause's variables. If the gadget together with a clique on its interface has width at most $b$, attach its decomposition at a bag containing the interface. Width stays at most the maximum of the seed width and $b$ through arbitrarily many substitutions. A finite dictionary of fixed gadgets supplies one constant $b$.

For fixed width bound, Bodlaender's linear-time algorithm constructs a decomposition from the graph or reports that its treewidth exceeds the bound. Process arbitrary decimal variable names in ordinary ASCII DIMACS with a digit trie, interning names by their normalized digits without converting huge identifiers into machine words. Build the primal edges and remove duplicates by counting-sort passes on the dense identifiers. These steps cost $O(n)$ native operations on $n$ encoded bits. For each bag of the fixed-width decomposition, keep a Boolean table of its compatible assignments, combine child tables over shared variables and impose the clauses assigned to the bag. Assign clauses to bags in linear time by listing all triples in each bag, sorting triples and clause scopes by dense identifiers and merging. The table sizes are constant, and the sum of the numbers of children over all bags is linear. At larger treewidth the same program may fall back to exhaustive assignment search. Thus obscuring the substitution history leaves the whole closure, even after arbitrary sign edits, within the fast cases of one total SAT solver. [Bodlaender, *A Linear-Time Algorithm for Finding Tree-Decompositions of Small Treewidth*](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539793251219) · [Samer and Szeider on SAT and tree decompositions](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0610174)

## A two-edge replacement that escapes bounded width

Take a cycle with an even number of vertices and add a perfect matching on those vertices, allowing a matching edge parallel to a cycle edge. Use a separate Boolean variable for each edge. At each vertex with incident edge variables $x,y,z$ impose $x\oplus y\oplus z=q_v$ using the four proper clauses forbidding the four triples of the opposite parity. For the seed, use two vertices joined by three distinct parallel edges, of which two form the initial cycle. Give the two vertices different charges. The resulting formula comprises exactly the eight clauses on three names.

Pick two positions on the cycle. Insert one new vertex at each, subdividing the corresponding cycle edges, and join the new vertices by a matching edge. Both insertions can be in the same old edge, in either order. Preserve the charges on old vertices and give the two new vertices equal charges. Each step adds two vertices, three edge variables and eight clauses on balance. Some old clauses change their edge names as the edges are subdivided. Existential projection onto every old edge assignment is not required here. The rule preserves the decision for arbitrary old charges by preserving the XOR of all charges and connectedness of the graph.

Indeed summing the vertex equations of a component shows that its charge must be even. With even total charge, set edges outside a spanning tree to zero, then solve each leaf equation for its parent edge. The remaining root equation holds. To check *clause* minimality at odd charge, remove a single clause at $v$ and fix the three incident bits to exactly the triple falsifying it. This opposite-parity triple satisfies the three surviving clauses at $v$. The cycle through every vertex leaves the graph connected after removing $v$, including the two-vertex seed. Transfer the fixed contributions to the charges in the remaining graph. Their total is zero because the old total was odd and the fixed triple has parity $1\oplus q_v$. Spanning-tree elimination now satisfies all other clauses. This is the usual minimality property of Tseitin contradictions on 2-connected graphs. [Itsykson, Riazanov and Smirnov, Lemmas 2.12 and 2.18](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol236-sat2022/LIPIcs.SAT.2022.6/LIPIcs.SAT.2022.6.pdf#page=8)

Every cycle with a prescribed perfect matching is reachable in this scheme by choosing one chord for the seed and inserting each remaining matched pair at its final positions in the current cyclic order. Optional global negations of edge variables can move the charges to any vector with the same total. Toggling an edge toggles both of its endpoint charges, and spanning-tree elimination realizes any prescribed even difference.

For an explicit width certificate, take $k\ge3$ consecutive blocks on the cycle, each with $k-1$ vertices. Label positions of block $i$ by the other $k-1$ blocks. Match position $j$ of block $i$ to position $i$ of block $j$. Contract the connected blocks to obtain a $K_k$ minor of the cubic graph. These endpoints and intervals can also be grown in a nested sequence. On passing from $k$ to $k+1$, insert a new block between two old blocks and add one new endpoint to each old block, paired with one endpoint of the new block. The $k$ matched pairs are inserted one at a time. Within each old block the order of the old endpoints is preserved.

The primal graph of the four-clause encoding is the line graph of the cubic graph. It too contains the cubic graph as a minor. Orient the spanning cycle consistently, assign each cycle edge to its head and every chord to either of its endpoints. The edges assigned to each vertex form a nonempty clique of the line graph. For any graph edge assigned to one endpoint, a cycle edge assigned to the other endpoint intersects it. Thus these disjoint cliques give the required branch sets. The resulting proper 3-CNFs have primal treewidth at least $k-1$, using $4k(k-1)$ clauses and $3k(k-1)/2$ variables at the $k$th layout. Ordinary dense decimal serialization takes $O(k^2\log k)$ encoded bits.

Even and odd charges on these high-width layouts are both recognizable and decidable in linear time. Except in the constant-size two-vertex case, group clauses by their sorted scope and check that the four sign patterns forbid exactly one parity. Check that every scope has three distinct variables and that each variable belongs to exactly two scopes. Sorting uses a constant number of counting-sort passes after interning variable names. Sum the charges in each connected component and return SAT exactly when all sums are even. A complete solver falls back on other inputs. High treewidth and inclusion-minimal unsatisfiability therefore survive an unbounded growth sequence whose decisions remain linear-time on the RAM. The exponential lower bound in the cited Tseitin paper concerns particular proof systems.
