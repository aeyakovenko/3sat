# When a canonical hard SAT table forces nonzero retrievals

A communication argument can force an actual nonzero work read on almost every input when the solver uses narrow enough collections of controllers and memory labels. A canonical Arthur–Merlin table supplies the needed average-case hardness. The ordinary RAM word width of this particular construction also gives a counterexample to dropping those qualifications. This distinction matters because the half-read bound for the much shorter expander formulas charges only genuinely nonzero reads in its worst-case version. [Expander layout](expander-cut-signs.md) · [Native half-read proof](half-read-reuse.md)

Let $m$ be the half-length of the raw data address and $V=2^{2m}$. At infinitely many $m$ there is a hard table $g$ and a family of ordinary 3-CNFs obtained by fixing a correct common construction parameter and a public random tape. Uniform tapes give the exact $g$ on **all** addresses, for both verdict pins, except with probability $2^{-6m}$. Fix any such good tape and one polarity. Only the $2m$ private literal signs now vary. The physical length satisfies $2m\le\log_2 N\le m^{O(1)}$ using the padded canonical construction. Space the private signs by enough public words that one physical fetch exposes at most one private bit.

Fix a deterministic SAT solver, a catalog $\mathcal C$ of possible native controllers and a palette $P$ of possible nonzero (work address, word value) labels. These objects may be chosen after seeing the public tape and table. There is no computation budget for constructing them. At the cut immediately after the solver learns its $m$th distinct private bit, call a prefix eligible if its controller lies in $\mathcal C$ and, on **every continuation with the same prefix**, every nonzero incoming work reply has its label in $P$. An incoming read looks up the prefix memory before its first post-cut overwrite at that address. An eligible prefix may have other nonzero words that no such suffix ever reads.

Write $V_0(n,j)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(n,j)}\binom ni$. There is an absolute $\delta>0$ with the following property. If
$$
 \log_2 |\mathcal C|+\log_2 V_0(|P|,k+1)\le m/128,
$$
the probability over uniform private bits that the prefix is eligible, the genuine suffix rereads at most $r=\lfloor\delta m\rfloor$ previously seen private bits, and it accesses at most $k$ distinct **nonzero** incoming work labels is $2^{-\Omega(m)}$. Zero replies have no count or time limit. This is simultaneous as a mathematical estimate for every choice of catalogs; taking their union would cost their number. Each verdict polarity has probability $1/2+o(1)$, so the bound also holds after conditioning on either SAT truth. It is useful to specify the palette by true continuation behavior, since unexpected positives on other continuations of the same prefix would invalidate the feature count below.

## Selecting the hard table

In the dense property of the canonical-table construction use the first $V$ bits of an $M$-bit candidate with $m=\lfloor(\log_2 M)/2\rfloor$. Thus $M/4<V\le M$. For a fixed time exponent $J$, require all interpreter descriptions of lengths $l\le V$ and query time $V^J$ to satisfy
$$
 (2a-V)^2\le 8V(l+2m+2)
$$
where $a$ is the number of agreeing address bits. Also require no monochromatic coordinate subcube of dimension $d=\lceil\delta m\rceil$. Both violations are NP-verifiable in $M$. The same binomial tail as in the canonical-table proof bounds the measure of the first type of violation by $o(1)$. A specified $d$-cube is monochromatic with probability $2^{1-2^d}$ and there are at most $3^{2m}$ cubes. The intersection has density at least one half at all sufficiently large lengths. On small sizes accept everything. Chen, Li and Liang's Theorem 1.3 supplies the infinitely many canonical prefixes with these properties. The two Arthur-first bit tests, amplification on shared public messages and fixed-layout CNFs are as in the canonical construction. The subcube test ensures that every complete deterministic run on a good tape leaves fewer than $d$ private bits unread. The exponent $J$ may be fixed large at the outset. [Chen, Li and Liang](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/182/) · [Construction and advice, errors and pin placement](semantic-public-tapes.md)

The hard table has the following additional property on **every** balanced split into $m$ row bits and $m$ column bits. A threshold of $D\le 2^{m/16}$ arbitrary Boolean row features with column-dependent real coefficients and a bias has agreement at most $1/2+2^{-m/8}$ for large $m$. To prove this, first include a constant Boolean feature and assign zero scores a fixed sign using a small bias perturbation on the finite domain. Every column labeling admitting a strict separator on the actual Boolean row vectors admits integer coefficients of $O((D+1)\log(D+2))$ bits each. Here is an elementary determinant proof. Multiply the rows by their desired signs, and scale a feasible weight vector so every dot product is at least one. Keep only linearly independent columns spanning the same column space, of rank $b\le D+1$. The resulting system still has a feasible point. If its tight rows do not span the full $b$-space, move along a vector orthogonal to them until a new independent constraint becomes tight. Full column rank ensures some constraint varies, and one direction reaches a first bound. Repetition gives $b$ independent tight rows. Cramer's rule followed by clearing the determinant gives signed integer weights of magnitude at most $b^{b/2}$ by Hadamard's inequality. The empty or constant feature cases have immediate representations. [Bounds on threshold weights](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/papers/threshold.pdf)

Store the split, the $2^m$ Boolean feature rows and the integer weight list for each of the $2^m$ columns. Their combined length is $O(2^m(D+1)^2\log(D+2)+\operatorname{poly}(m))<2^{3m/2}$ for large $m$. A fixed plain table interpreter scans the selected data and computes the signed integer dot product in $V^{O(1)}$ time on tapes, within a sufficiently large chosen $J$. This is well below the $\Omega(V 2^{-m/4})$ description length required for the claimed advantage by the dense property. Very long lexicographic coefficients in a native decision tree do not enter this encoding. The resulting prohibition concerns Boolean row features and exact finite threshold weights. The paper of Alman and Williams relates other forms of sign-rank rigidity to threshold circuit lower bounds. [Alman and Williams](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05558)

## Turning a fraction of cheap real runs into a predictor

Let $\lambda$ denote the claimed event's probability. Independently fill all private coordinates uniformly at random; keep the original prefix values on its observed $m$-set $S$ and use the fill on its complement. Both continuations are independent conditional on the entire true prefix. Hence the probability that both are eligible and cheap is at least $\lambda^2$. Fix one public fill attaining this bound. For the companion suffix name its controller and up to $k$ positive labels in $P$, and the at most $r$ old data bits it reads that disagree with the fill. Simulate with those extra data bits flipped and all unnamed incoming work replies zero. Its suffix set differs from $S^c$ by at most $r+d$ positions. A list of those positions recovers $S$ exactly. Thus at most
$$
 M_0=|\mathcal C|\,V_0(|P|,k)\,V_0(2m,r)\,V_0(2m,r+d)
$$
read-set/controller pairs cover all joint successes. Choose a pair covering at least $\lambda^2/M_0$ mass. On its split $x=S$, $y=S^c$, the existence of this native prefix and its palette eligibility depend only on $x$. Let $A$ be these rows. Their true cut memories $C(x)$ are independent of $y$. These functions can be nonuniform and computationally costly. The feature-table construction will store the needed bits explicitly.

On $A$, for every $y$ simulate the actual suffix, returning a fixed default sign when either probe cap is exceeded. Count the first extra positive label in an aborted leaf; every path uses at most $k+1$ distinct positive labels from $P$. An overflow on attempting a new old pin can be detected before fetching its value. On the complement of $A$ return the same default. This gives two total predictors, one for each default. At least one is correct on half the table plus half the mass of eligible cheap executions on these rows. Their non-abstaining answers on those executions are the true bits. Thus its advantage is at least $\lambda^2/(2M_0)$.

For each fixed $y$ the finite tree of truncated executions on rows in $A$ sign-represents its output by a real threshold of positive-label monomials. Order the leaves lexicographically with zero answers first. A leaf contributes its signed power-of-two rank weight times the conjunction of its positive labels. At an actual row its own term is present, every lexicographically greater leaf has a contradictory nonzero answer at its first divergence, and all smaller weights sum to less. The same argument includes the leaves returning the default sign. One Boolean row feature per subset of up to $r$ old pins and up to $k+1$ nonzero labels therefore suffices across every column. Restrict all these features to $A$ and add $1[x\notin A]$ to implement the outside default. The total number is at most
$$
 D=V_0(m,r)V_0(|P|,k+1)+2 .
$$
On eligible rows arbitrary public-tape bits, construction advice, local computations and native memory addresses are allowed. No further storage of them is needed in the plain predictor, since every row feature and every column labeling has already been encoded. Choose $\delta$ sufficiently small that $4H_2(2\delta)<1/128$. The catalog and feature estimates then give $M_0\le 2^{m/32}$ and $D\le 2^{m/16}$ for large $m$. Comparing the two advantages gives $\lambda^2\le 2 M_0 2^{-m/8}$. ## The physical obstruction and a route to the original time question

For the available AM layout $\log_2 N\ge 2m$. A constant number of native registers can hold **all $2m$ data bits**. A globally correct solver can inspect them in their public order, pack them into registers, and read all of them before beginning its usual SAT computation on the virtual formula. Take care to validate the public portions of words containing old pins when they are first read; on any failed format test fall back to a complete solver. A fixed regular header supplies the pin-bank layout at every size, and all other input words can be read from the actual formula. At the half cut the prefix needs no writable store. The ordinary virtual solver can later reconstruct the old words from the packed bits. Both charged operations are then zero on every family input, at the cost of many possible controller values.

Large addresses alone also matter. Before the last prefix pin is read, set one nonzero marker at the address encoding the first $m-1$ private bits and clear that packed register. Read the $m$th bit into a designated register. At the half cut the input-dependent controller data are now only this last bit. After gathering the remaining private pins, enumerate all candidates for the first $m-1$ bits, evaluating the full actual formula with the corresponding old words virtually supplied. Use a workspace bank separate from the prefix marker, initialized by the suffix. For candidates giving verdict one, consult the marker; halt with one precisely on a hit. If none hits, return zero. On every zero input the solver rereads no old pin and receives **no nonzero incoming work word**, regardless of how many zero probes it spends. The virtual evaluation uses the checked template bits from the original pin records, and the rest of the input can be supplied through the actual untouched encoding. This is an ordinary globally sound, potentially very slow, decision algorithm. It violates the small-palette premise on the many possible marker addresses. Both examples apply to the actual RAM width; a bit-probe accounting that omits wide controllers or addresses cannot establish the unqualified conclusion.

There is a different potential consequence if the *canonical table itself* can be represented more compactly. Take any fixed proposed SAT exponent $d_0$ and choose $J$ sufficiently larger than $d_0$ to pay for a tape simulation of a polynomial-time word RAM. Suppose an $N$-bit seed-fixed CNF template computes the canonical hard table with agreement at least $1/2+\nu$, $\nu>0$ fixed, under $2m$ individual variable sign pins. If $N\le V$ and SAT takes $O(N^{d_0})$, store the entire zero-address template and the locations and conventions of the pins. This costs $N+O(m\log V)$ description bits, even for nonuniform templates or unknown advice. Build the pinned instance and run the solver at each query in $V^J$ universal tape steps. The signed agreement inequality then gives
$$
 N\ge \nu^2 V/2-O(m\log V).
$$
Consequently, a sub-$V$-linear template at infinitely many of the hard scales with fixed positive advantage would refute the proposed exponent. Taking $d_0=3$ would settle the requested exclusions. The cited protocol has a larger budget. An output-explicit computation of a $V$-bit table already needs to write $V$ bits; the known upper bound for the canonical construction does not yield such a template. This identifies a sufficient compression task independent of the random-access palette assumptions.

Finally, adjoining many scanned data bits through a few quadratic forms need not repair the width loss. For an arbitrary outer Boolean function of $t$ quadratic forms on $2s$ independent bits, fix **any** balanced split. A form's cross term can be written as the parity of $Z=\sum_{i=1}^b u_i(x)v_i(y)$, where independent invertible changes on the two sides make the $b\le s$ pairs uniform independent bits. Separate row-only and column-only signs account for the remaining terms. Thus $Z$ is binomial $(b,1/4)$. Except on at most $\xi/t$ of the inputs it belongs to an integer interval of $O(1+\sqrt{s\log(2t/\xi)})$ consecutive levels. Interpolate the actual signs $(-1)^Z$ on these levels by an ordinary polynomial of that degree. Its expansion in the Boolean products uses at most $s^{O(1+\sqrt{s\log(2t/\xi)})}$ products of a row and column function. Combine the $t$ approximants using the exact multilinear polynomial of the outer function on sign bits, multiplying the separate row and column phases. Split a signed row indicator into its positive and negative Boolean parts when needed. On simultaneous success the output is exactly correct, and the threshold of the resulting sum has error at most $\xi$. The number of Boolean row features is at most $2^{O(t(1+\sqrt{s\log(2t/\xi)})\log(s+2))}$. For constant $\xi<1/2$ and $t=o(\sqrt{s}/(\log(s+2)\sqrt{\log(2t)}))$ this is subexponential in $s$. This rules out this variable expansion as a source of the required average-case feature hardness. It extends the central-level polynomial reasoning used for the Hadamard matrix to an arbitrary outer table on several quadratic forms. [Alman and Williams](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.05558) · [Bindua, Hatami, Karimi and Robere](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.01038)
