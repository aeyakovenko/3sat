# Avoiding short forbidden lists with finite lookahead

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** A constant-size forbidden list at every prefix length can be avoided by a uniform streaming strategy with constant lookahead. The lookahead is O(log B+log log B) for lists of size B. Applying it to the heavy rate-prefix lists gives a computable individual rate with a quantitative digit clock. The clock it certifies does not close the SAT argument.

## The finite game

Fix ℓ≥3. At each previously constructed prefix u the adversary names at most B forbidden ℓ-bit continuations. The chooser sees this announcement before writing the next bit. A violation occurs if any announced continuation is completed. The chooser has a computable finite-state winning strategy whenever there is a real 1<λ<2 with

    B ≤ (2−λ)λ^(ℓ−1).                                                   (1)

Fix **any** deterministic adversary strategy, including one that changes its announcements according to the bits already played. Each visited prefix uniquely determines the preceding play. Let aₙ count all length-n prefixes that have survived that strategy. For n<ℓ this is 2ⁿ. For n≥ℓ a newly lost extension at length n must be one of the B words announced by its surviving ancestor at length n−ℓ. Thus aₙ≥2aₙ₋₁−B aₙ₋ℓ. Inductively aₙ₋₁≥λ^(ℓ−1)aₙ₋ℓ and (1) gives aₙ≥λaₙ₋₁. The surviving binary tree has an infinite path. Hence no adversary strategy forces a finite loss against every chooser.

The game state consists of the residual forbidden suffixes of length at most ℓ and whose turn is next. After a bit is selected, remove incompatible suffixes and strip that bit from every compatible suffix. An empty suffix loses. This finite safety game is computably determined: repeatedly mark states where the adversary can force a marked successor or every chooser move is marked, starting at losing states. The remaining chooser states have a move that stays in the remainder. Since an adversary win has just been ruled out, this computes a positional winning strategy from the initial state.

Taking λ=2−2/ℓ makes the right side of (1) at least 2^ℓ/(eℓ). Thus 2^ℓ≥4Bℓ suffices. Conversely, if B≥2^ℓ the adversary can announce all continuations. In fact order 2^ℓ/ℓ is necessary in the worst case. Mykkeltveit constructed a set of one ℓ-bit representative per binary necklace meeting every infinite binary stream as a contiguous substring. The adversary may announce that same set as forbidden continuations at each visited prefix. The number of necklaces is 2^ℓ/ℓ+O(2^(ℓ/2)): full orbits have ℓ members, and words with proper period dividing ℓ number O(2^(ℓ/2)). For a fixed online chooser the adversary can publish the corresponding global list only at the actually visited prefix each turn. This defeats the chooser while keeping each published global list within the same size. Thus the worst-case lookahead is log₂B+log₂log₂B+O(1). Exact finite safety-game enumeration gives maximal winning B of 1,1,1,2,3 for ℓ=1,…,5. [Mykkeltveit's construction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0095895672900068) · [Finite-game calculation](../audits/rate_list_game.py)

For a prescribed list of at most B strings per level, announce at u only the forbidden extensions of u at level |u|+ℓ. Computing the resulting prefix of length h accesses no list beyond h+ℓ. The first ℓ−1 levels can be omitted when the goal is eventual avoidance.

## Applying the game to actual rate-prefix masses

Use the computable W_C(σ) from [the individual-rate criterion](exceptional-rate-capacity.md), taking its time allowance 2^(C h2ʰ) and program-length bound C h2ʰ at h=|σ|. Let M_C(h) be its sum over the level. At every h with positive total, forbid the strings with W_C(σ)>M_C(h)/B. There are at most B. Empty levels forbid nothing. The finite game gives a computable path β outside those lists at every late h.

Computing an entire level needs at most 2^(2C h2ʰ)poly(h2ʰ) time by simulating all candidate programs and sorting the outputs. With ℓ fixed by (1), this method computes the first h bits of β within 2^(R h2ʰ) time for some R=O((C+1)2^ℓ). The strategy table can be computed once at a fixed startup cost. This is an upper bound for the actual construction. It supplies no smaller coefficient for the same path.

Suppose a native name within H bits of shortest realizes the clock crossing for x=3+.β with denominator mᵃ. Use C large enough for the recovery compiler in the individual-rate proof. The true cell has signed offset i with |i|≤a+4 from the cell indicated by the name's completion time. If the fixed β algorithm has a prefix description of length b, then K₀(β↾h)≤K₀(h)+b+O(1), while M_C(h)≤C₁2^(−K₀(h)). At that witness the recovery estimate therefore gives

    W_C(β↾h)/M_C(h) ≥ cᵥ 2^(−H−b) / ((a+6)(log₂(a+6))²).

To contradict this by the certified upper ratio 1/B one would need B larger than a constant times 2^(H+b)(a+6)(log₂(a+6))². Yet to target a digit algorithm with certified clock 2^(R h2ʰ), the direct point-mass decoder computes only b'≈log₂m−log₂log₂m−log₂R rate digits before issuing a grant. The width error in the deadline exponent is then O(R log m), so its certified a is of order R. In the explicit finite-game bound R is taken proportional to (C+1)2^ℓ, with 2^ℓ>B necessary even for the game to be winnable. These estimates do not give the two required inequalities simultaneously. Taking a coarser recovery cell reduces the candidate-offset cost but increases the time coefficient required for recovering its prefix.

There is also an unconditional check on the proposed speedup. Suppose a fixed program of header length b computes β↾h on the reference interpreter with enough slack to fit the recovery clock 2^(C h2ʰ) after poly(h) setup, and is short enough to meet its program-length cutoff. Take the injective ordinary halt-record weights wₕ from the [grant construction](one-bit-production.md). On infinitely many h, wₕ≥δ2^(−K₀(h)) for a fixed δ>0. A reference wrapper runs the grant name to recover h and prints β↾h by the assumed routine. It gives W_C(β↾h)≥c2^(−b)wₕ there. Thus the normalized level ratio is at least c'2^(−b) infinitely often. Small forbidden lists can yield computable rates, but the resource bound of the avoiding algorithm enters the very mass table being avoided.
