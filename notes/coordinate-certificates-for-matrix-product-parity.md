# Coordinate certificates for one matrix-product parity

For three independent uniform binary $d$-square matrices let $M(A,B,Z)=\mathrm{tr}(ABZ)$ over $\mathbb F_2$ and $D=d^2$. A coordinate certificate fixes input bits so that every completion has the same verdict. With probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(d))$, the minimum number of fixed bits through the given input is
$$
                     \tfrac52D-\Theta(d^{3/2}).
$$
The constants in the two-sided estimate are absolute. In particular, on almost all triples every exact deterministic algorithm must inspect at least $(5/2-o(1))D$ distinct source bits, even with unrestricted computation between queries. The leading constant is attainable in expectation. Read $A,B$ and then the entries $Z_{ji}$ with $(AB)_{ij}=1$. This uses $(5/2-2^{-d-1})D$ source-bit probes on average. Finding a minimum certificate may take additional work. I have not established priority for the typical-certificate estimate. [Decision-tree and certificate definitions](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030439750100144X)

## A large certificate-free set must follow a directed cut

Think of the coordinates as edges of the cyclically directed complete tripartite graph $I\to K\to J\to I$, each class of size $d$. The parity $M$ counts the directed triangles of ones modulo two. Let $U$ be the free edges of any monochromatic subcube through an input. Every first Boolean difference at such an edge is zero on the input. For $A_{ik}$ it is $(BZ)_{ki}$, with cyclic counterparts for the other two factors. Also, if two free edges meet head to tail, the second difference in those two coordinates is zero. It is precisely the value on the unique edge completing their triangle. These assertions follow by flipping edges within the subcube; they also hold when the third coordinate is free.

Put $h=\lceil\sqrt d\rceil$. The chance any input matrix has an all-zero $h$-by-$h$ rectangle is at most $3\binom dh^2 2^{-h^2}=\exp(-\Omega(d))$. On the complement, at each vertex fewer than $h$ incoming or fewer than $h$ outgoing edges belong to $U$: otherwise their pairs force an entire zero rectangle in the opposite input. Delete the smaller of the two sets at every vertex, losing at most $3dh$ edges. Each vertex now has only outgoing free edges or only incoming free edges. The surviving edges lie in a directed cut. If the source fractions of the three classes are $x,y,z$, the cut contains
$$
 D\big(x(1-y)+y(1-z)+z(1-x)\big)
 =D\big(1-xyz-(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)\big)\le D
$$
possible coordinates. In each matrix the cut is a rectangle fixed by the corresponding vertex sets.

We need a simultaneous bound for every possible choice of this cut. For a random binary matrix, with probability at least $1-\exp(-\Omega(d))$, its rank defect is at most $h$. Indeed, among $h$-dimensional subspaces of the domain there are at most $C2^{h(d-h)}$ possibilities by the ordered-basis formula, each contained in the kernel with probability $2^{-dh}$. Conditional on a left factor of defect at most $h$, select independent rows spanning its row space. On those rows its product with an independent uniform right factor has fully independent uniform entries. For an i.i.d. matrix, Hoeffding's bound and a union bound over at most $4^d$ rectangles show simultaneously that each rectangle has at most half its area plus $2d^{3/2}$ zeros except with probability $\exp(-\Omega(d))$. The at most $h$ omitted rows add at most $hd$ to the discrepancy. Apply this to $AB,BZ,ZA$, reordering or transposing indices as needed. They need not be independent of one another or of the chosen cut. Thus altogether at most $D/2+O(d^{3/2})$ cut coordinates have vanishing first derivative. Restoring the $3dh$ deleted free edges proves the lower bound on certificate size.

## Certificates can save order d to the three halves beyond the leading term

Fix all of $Z$. For each $k$ the contribution is a bilinear form of just the column $A_{:k}$ and row $B_{k:}$. One can free every bit in one of these two vectors having zero derivative, fixing the other vector completely. These choices are independent across $k$. In particular the larger of the two zero counts can be freed for each $k$.

Conditional on $Z$ of defect $j$, the two derivative vectors for a fixed $k$ are independent. Each is uniform on a $(d-j)$-dimensional subspace, and different $k$ use independent vectors. For each derivative vector, keep pivot coordinates and replace the other $j$ coordinates by fresh fair bits. The resulting vectors are i.i.d. uniform, and their zero counts differ from the originals by at most $j$ each. For two independent $\mathrm{Bin}(d,1/2)$ counts $X,Y$, one has $\mathbb E\max(X,Y)=d/2+\tfrac12\mathbb E|X-Y|\ge d/2+c\sqrt d$ with an absolute $c>0$. For example, $X-Y$ is a sum of independent centered differences with variance $d/2$ and fourth moment $O(d^2)$. Paley–Zygmund on the square yields the estimate.

The sum over $k$ of the maxima of the resampled zero counts has bounded differences one in $2D$ independent bits. Its probability of falling below $D/2+(c/2)d^{3/2}$ is $\exp(-\Omega(d))$. The rank-defect estimate above gives $j\le(c/4)\sqrt d$ except with probability $\exp(-\Omega(d))$, with integer rounding absorbed for large $d$. The actual sum is within $dj$ of the coupled sum. The resulting monochromatic subcube frees at least $D/2+(c/4)d^{3/2}$ bits. This proves the other half of the typical minimum-certificate estimate. The deterministic upper bound on the average query count in the introduction uses a single computable order and charges queries only.

## A gap between discovery and certificates for a fixed public mask

For each sufficiently large $d$ there is an invertible matrix $Z$, fixed independently of the solver, such that every exact adaptive coordinate-query algorithm needs at least
$$
     \tfrac32 d^2-O(d\log d)
$$
**expected probes to $A,B$ alone** under independent uniform factors, with $Z$ supplied for free. Reading all of $A$ and then the sensitive positions of $B$ has expected cost exactly $3d^2/2$. On these same inputs the expected minimum certificate size in the two variable factors is $3d^2/2-\Theta(d^{3/2})$. Thus a solver spends $\Theta(d^{3/2})$ more probes in expectation than an omniscient choice of shortest certificates, when the solver is optimal in expectation.

Choose an absolute constant $\alpha>0$ with $2H_2(\alpha)<1$ and put $q=\lfloor\alpha d\rfloor$, $h=\lceil4\log_2 d\rceil$. Require that $Z$ have no all-zero $h$-by-$h$ rectangle and that every nonempty sum of at most $q$ rows, and every such sum of columns, have weight greater than $q$. For uniform $Z$, each fixed nonempty row or column sum is uniform. A union bound gives failure probability at most $2^{1+(2H_2(\alpha)-1)d}$ for the sum property. The zero-rectangle bound above is $o(1)$ with the new $h$, while invertibility has probability bounded below by an absolute constant. These masks therefore exist. The fixed-mask statement concerns ordinary formulas with that mask supplied or pinned. It requires no efficient procedure for constructing a particular good mask.

Write $a=A_{:k},b=B_{k:}$. Fix all input bits in other inner-index groups to arbitrary values, and any private coins of a zero-error algorithm. An execution on $(a,b)$ is now an adaptive deterministic coordinate tree computing $bZa$ up to a known constant. Probes outside this pair have fixed replies, and repeated probes can be simulated from earlier replies. For word probes the exposed bits can be ordered sequentially before using the reply. The expected lower bound here counts distinct coordinates exposed.

Stop when one of $a,b$, called $F$, first has $q$ bits exposed. This must occur: at any leaf the unread coordinates in the two factors force a zero rectangle in $Z$, so one factor has at most $h-1$ unread. At the stopping time, conditioning on any fixed local transcript leaves all unexposed bits independent and fair. The event of reaching that transcript is exactly its cylinder of observed bits, since the other groups and coins are fixed. In particular choosing the first factor and its $q$ positions adaptively does not condition on further hidden bits.

Let $S_F,S_G$ be the counts of sensitive coordinates in $F$ and the other factor $G$ at the actual input. Conditional on the stopping transcript, every sensitivity flag in $G$ is fair, since its row or column of $Z$ has weight greater than $q$ and still involves an unread $F$ bit. Therefore $\mathbb E[S_G\mid\text{transcript}]=d/2$. Moreover the $q$ sensitivity flags at the exposed positions of $F$ are independent fair bits under the conditional distribution. Any nonempty linear combination of their coefficient rows on $G$ has weight greater than $q$ and cannot vanish on the unexposed coordinates of $G$. With probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(d))$, at least $q/3$ of these $q$ early probes are nonsensitive. Unconditionally, each of the two original sides' full sensitivity counts is $\mathrm{Bin}(d,1/2)$ since $Z$ is invertible. With the same high probability both are within $q/8$ of $d/2$, hence $|S_F-S_G|\le q/4$ even after the adaptive designation. This use of concentration is unconditional on the stopping transcript, unlike the independent-flag argument.

At a leaf which reads at least $d-h$ positions of $F$, add all $S_G$ sensitive positions of $G$. At a leaf which instead reads at least $d-h$ of $G$, add all $S_F$ sensitive positions of $F$ and the nonsensitive early probes of $F$. On the good event this second charge is also at least $d-h+S_G$. Losing at most $2d$ on the exponentially rare exceptions and averaging now gives $3d/2-h-O(d e^{-\Omega(d)})$ per inner index. The argument held for every fixing of the other input groups. Undo those conditionings and add expectations over $k$. Interleaving work across different $k$ does not change the bound.

For the certificate comparison, given invertible $Z$ the two derivative counts in each group are independent binomials. The larger zero count can be freed, as above. Conversely in each group one free side has size less than $h$ and the other at most the larger zero count. Thus the expected shortest certificate fixes $3d^2/2-\Theta(d^{3/2})$ coordinates in $A,B$. The gap between this mean and the optimal adaptive mean costs more than a constant number of extra probes per group, but it is smaller than the leading quadratic input-size term.

## What an actual SAT solver must inspect

For an unconditional explicit family, take a fixed Boolean circuit for $M$ with $O(d^3)$ gates, using AND and XOR with constant-size width-three graph constraints per gate. Pin the $3D$ input variables by fixed-position unit signs and assert a true output. The encoded formula has $O(d^3\log d)$ bits using fixed-width binary variable names. Units can instead use constant-size width-three padding, with the literal sign copied. All other layout bits are constant. For any exact deterministic general SAT solver, collect the distinct source coordinates exposed by the input words it reads on this formula. Every completion agreeing on those coordinates yields the identical machine run by induction on the steps, including random-access writes and reads. Its inspected source coordinates therefore form a certificate. The typical bound applies separately to all correct solvers on the very same typical inputs.

Under the additional hypothesis of a fixed deterministic near-linear SAT solver, the construction can use the earlier nested-verification reduction and produce a fixed-layout instance of $\widetilde O(D)$ encoded bits. The inner deterministic tester checks a proposed $C=AB$ by solving a formula that guesses a differing entry. The outer fixed-clock nondeterministic computation guesses $C$, runs this tester on its own work tape or RAM memory, then checks the trace against $Z$. Compile **this entire fixed-clock machine** to a fixed circuit for accepting histories, with external input registers for the $3D$ bits of $A,B,Z$. Those input bits enter the final formula only as fixed-position unit signs, because constructing the varying inner call is part of the machine being simulated. This gives the same certificate consequence even if a different general SAT solver handles the outer instances. The clock and logarithmic instruction assumptions are those of the original reduction. [Construction and time accounting](verifying-matrix-products-from-sat.md) · [Fixed-clock RAM encoding](packed-band-transfer.md)

A word can expose more than one source sign. The universally valid inference for $w$-bit input words is a lower bound of the number of inspected coordinates divided by $w$ on word reads. In a fixed record layout it can be sharpened using the actual number of distinct signs per word. Even exposing all $3D$ coordinates costs at most one pass over the explicit pins. This yields no unrestricted superlinear SAT time lower bound.
