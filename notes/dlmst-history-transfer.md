# Which minimum-spanning-tree histories transfer to RAM and SAT?

Khanna, Putterman and Song give a deterministic linear comparison bound for minimum spanning forest and apply Pettie and Ramachandran's problem-specific comparison-to-time theorem. Their Corollary 4.2 gives uniform deterministic $O(m)$ time for connected graphs with $m$ edges and $O(m+n)$ for a general forest problem. The weight-comparison model measures edges and vertices. The SAT goal measures the explicit bit length of a formula and gives the RAM unrestricted work memory. I checked the fixed-set counting argument and the use of its hypotheses here; this is not an independent verification of the entire minimum-spanning-tree preprint. [Pinned manuscript, Theorem 2.6 and Corollary 4.2](https://github.com/Louiealbp/DLMST/blob/0072335716b145032b418760883daed2a89809db/final.pdf) · [Authors' ECCC report](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/339/)

## The history being counted

Lemma 3.9, attributed to Fredman, fixes $m$ nonempty subsets $S_i$ of an $n$-element set. Change one **common total order** and record each subset's maximizing element. There are at most $\binom{n+m-1}{m}$ resulting vectors. In Lemma 3.10 the authors first condition on the forest and the spokes in each round. The relevant sets of edges are then fixed: they consist of the endpoint spokes and the unique forest path, when the path exists. Their maxima determine the comparison-dependent part of the preferred actions. Accounting also for the choices of forest and spokes in $t^2$ rounds gives $\log |\mathcal H|=O(t^2\sqrt{mn})$. Lemma 3.11 selects a schedule by bounding bad edges simultaneously over this family. Selecting the schedule takes no **weight comparisons**. The final uniform time claim uses their Theorem 2.6. A SAT verifier seeking a short check would still have to account for schedule construction, encoding or validation on its own model. ## Two writers with every possible binary history

Write zero to data cell 0 and one to data cell 1 at two fixed time slots. For $i=1,\ldots,m$ read a fresh explicit input bit $x_i$ and load data cell $x_i$. No later write is needed. The vector of last **data** writers at the $m$ loads realizes all $2^m$ binary strings, even though the two writer times and their order are fixed. A fixed family of subsets of two writers admits at most two maxima vectors as their order varies. The varying eligibility set of a RAM load, here the singleton selected by $x_i$, carries the input information. Counting the data writers alone omits this cost.

This can change satisfiability while keeping even the exact writer-use histogram fixed. Give the program $b\ge1$ blocks of four address bits. Each block is either $1100$ or $1010$, so it reads each writer twice. From the returned bits $a,b',c,d$ in a block compute $(a\land b')\mathbin{\oplus}(c\land d)$. The two block types return one and zero, respectively. XOR the block results together. All $2^b$ histories have identical counts in every block, and exactly $2^{b-1}$ have each verdict. They are genuine last-writer histories on native RAM runs.

For corresponding ordinary 3-CNFs, pin the four input bits of each block to its chosen pattern by unit clauses, encode the two AND gates and the XOR gates by their usual exact truth-table equivalences of width at most three, and assert the final output. The two constant-cell loads in the RAM supply the identity on each address bit, so direct wires implement their exact values in this fixed circuit. The acyclic gate values are uniquely forced by the pins. Satisfiability of the resulting linear-gate formulas is exactly the computed verdict. Their bit length is $O(b\log(b+2))$ with indexed gate names. Width-one or width-two clauses can be expanded into exact three distinct literals by the standard constant-size fresh-variable padding. This construction shows failure of the writer histogram even for real SAT answers; a solver for this family can simply evaluate the pinned circuit.

## A sharp positive bound when read addresses can be reconstructed

Fix $m$ data reads in a memory with a $q$-symbol alphabet, $q\ge2$. Suppose the decoder knows the initial memory and either knows the address sequence, or can compute each next address from a fixed public seed and the preceding **read replies alone**. Write addresses, their timing, and their values can vary freely across the histories. If at most $W$ writes occur, the number of possible read-reply vectors for this decoder and seed is at most
$$
 \sum_{j=0}^{\min(m,W)} \binom{m}{j}(q-1)^j.
$$
The table of last observed values starts at the known initial contents. Mark a read if its reply differs from that address's last observation, and replace the observation by the reply. Each mark requires a write to that address since its preceding observation. The corresponding open-left, closed-right time intervals for different marks at the same address are disjoint; a write is to only one address. Thus at most $W$ marks occur. Given the marks and, for each, the index of the new symbol within the $q-1$ choices different from the last observation, recover the replies in order. At step $i$ the decoder has recovered the preceding replies and hence knows the address, including for an adaptive address rule of the specified type. A family of $H$ specified address rules multiplies the bound by $H$. Computing these addresses can still take time.

Equality is attainable for a known zero memory and the fixed schedule of $m$ *distinct* reads: select any at most $W$ of these cells and write their prescribed nonzero values before the reads. For binary data, when $1\le W\le m/2$, the base-two logarithm of the sharp bound is $O(W\log_2(em/W))$. An unknown initial memory can encode $m$ arbitrary replies with zero writes. Fresh input bits used in address selection can also supply arbitrary replies from just two cells, as above. Conditioning on all such bits may fix the addresses, but those bits must be supplied or regenerated in a proposed certificate. Finally, a compressed vector of replies specifies neither the private writes outside the reads nor the intervening controller trace. A proposed proof of correct RAM execution still needs the checks for both.

## An explicit SAT obstruction to preserving every future pin

A minimum spanning forest preserves connectivity at every weight threshold (Fact 2.5). For unweighted 3-CNF a natural attempt to retain a small summary is to ask that it preserve satisfiability under each of a family of later pins. Even three pins can disclose a cubic number of independent bits.

For arbitrary $H\subseteq[r]^3$ take $3r$ variables in banks $X,Y,Z$ and form
$$
 F_H=\bigwedge_{(a,b,c)\in H}(\neg X_a\lor\neg Y_b\lor Z_c).
$$
For a query $(i,j,k)$ assert $X_i=Y_j=1$ and $Z_k=0$. The resulting formula is unsatisfiable exactly when $(i,j,k)\in H$. If the triple is present its clause is violated. When it is absent set all other $X,Y$ to zero and all other $Z$ to one. This satisfies every clause. Each unpinned formula in the family is satisfiable.

Consequently $2^{r^3}$ distinct vectors of pinned SAT answers occur. Every deterministic summary from which a decoder can answer all these queries without further access to $H$ requires at least $r^3$ bits in the worst case. Even variable-length binary strings of maximum length $r^3-1$ give only $2^{r^3}-1$ possibilities. In particular each clause in the full $r^3$-clause formula is necessary if a summary must itself be a subformula. Releasing that restriction on the representation still leaves the information bound across the family.

The $r^3$-bit membership table attains the summary size and answers a **single** query by one indexed bit lookup. For power-of-two $r$ a log-word RAM forms the offset by concatenating the three fixed-width indices. One explicit fixed-layout 3-CNF encoding also permits constant-word-time queries on this promise: reserve one clause slot per triple and put either the indicated clause or a fixed positive clause on three dummy variables. The dummy clause can always be satisfied and the decoder reads the queried slot directly. A compact unsorted list of present clauses has a different access cost. In particular the summary lower bound is in the number of variables, is compatible with indexed answering, and supplies no superlinear bound in the encoded length for one SAT decision.
