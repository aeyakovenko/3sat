# Which adaptive checksum queries can recover the parity of independent SAT answers?

For a fixed number of independently chosen targets on the padded [ordinary checksum 3-CNFs](distance-between-checksum-queries.md), an exact decision tree needs one auxiliary checksum query per target even if it may assume some true native clauses. At that depth its only strategy is to query the targets themselves in some order. This includes two independent answers for which one seeks a [short certificate of their XOR](certifying-pairs-of-checksum-answers.md).

Use the full table of $V=2^d$ bits, including the variables pinned to zero for padding, and its true assignment $h$. Write $c(p,\lambda)$ for the address-indexed binary selector and $c\cdot x$ for its binary linear checksum on a table $x$. Subtract the known terminal pins from physical verdicts. Let $m\ge1$ be fixed as $d$ grows. Use the field $K=\mathbb F_q$ of the checksum construction, so $q$ is polynomial in $d$, $q/d\to\infty$ and $s=\log_2 q$. The targets are $m$ independent uniform choices of $(p,\lambda)\in K^{d+1}$.

With probability $1-o(1)$ over these public targets, the following holds simultaneously. Choose any subset of fewer than $2^{-m-12}V$ clauses of the native base and any binary decision tree of depth at most $m$. Each node can query **any** checksum of the proposed table over the same field; the next node may depend on its reply. Choose both the tree and the clause subset knowing $h$ and all the targets. Suppose that on **every** table satisfying the selected clauses the tree returns the XOR of the $m$ target answers. Then every root-to-leaf path has length $m$ and queries each of the original selectors once, up to reordering and simultaneous Frobenius within each selector. Each leaf returns the parity of its path answers. In particular the tree's verdict is already correct on every binary table. If depth is at most $m-1$, a stronger bound of $(1/2-o(1))V$ selected clauses is necessary.

This also applies to a sequence of arbitrarily many cheap rewrites of a parity. If each rewrite expresses the current parity as the parity of at most $m$ further answers using fewer than $2^{-m-12}V$ native clauses, its new tuple is an equivalent presentation of the original one. The good-product hypotheses are preserved by component permutations and Frobenius, so induction applies even if each step chooses different clauses.

One additional query permits a genuinely different exact strategy without any clauses. Replace one target by two queries pinning a chosen field coordinate of its point to zero and one. Give them masks $(1-p_i)\lambda$ and $p_i\lambda$. Multiaffinity expresses the target as their XOR. On typical targets all coordinates are interior, and the replacements have Boolean coordinates, so neither is an original descriptor up to Frobenius. The other $m-1$ queries are unchanged. This explains the exact stopping point of this local argument. It gives no shortcut for obtaining the needed answers to the replacement queries.

## The field products in a random tuple

Split a Boolean table address into independent uniform half-addresses $x,y$. For each target split its interpolation product at this cut and put its mask into the first factor. Bundle the $m$ products on the two sides into $A(x),B(y)\in R=K^m$, with componentwise arithmetic. The parity selector is the bit
$$
 H(x,y)=\langle A(x),B(y)\rangle,\qquad
 \langle a,b\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^m\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}(a_k b_k).
$$
This binary bilinear pairing is nondegenerate.

Except with probability $O(m(d+1)/q)$ the masks and all interpolation factors are nonzero. Conditional on that event, changing address bit $i$ from zero to one multiplies the vector of products by a unit $r_i$, whose components are independent uniform in $K^*\setminus\{1\}$. These vectors are independent between address bits. The product on a block is a fixed unit times a random subset product of its ratios. For any nontrivial multiplicative character of $(K^*)^m$ involving $k\ge1$ components, a single ratio has character expectation $(-1/(q-2))^k$. The mean squared magnitude of the Fourier multiplier of the random subset product is at most $\rho=(1+(q-2)^{-2})/2$ per coordinate, for $q\ge4$. Multiplication by the fixed unit preserves distance from uniform even if that unit depends on the ratios. Multiplicative Parseval and Cauchy–Schwarz now give expected squared total variation at most $(q-1)^m\rho^l/4$ from uniform on $(K^*)^m$ for a product with $l$ free bits. Adding the missing zero coordinates costs at most $m/q$ in total variation from uniform on $R$.

Apply Markov's inequality to both half-blocks and after fixing any one bit in either. Each has $l\ge\lfloor d/2\rfloor-1$. With probability $1-o(1)$ all these distributions are within $\nu=o(1)$ of uniform on $R$, for example with $\nu=m/q+1/d$. Fix such a tuple. No distributional assumptions will be made on the competing queries.

## A correlation bound for fewer queries

On this cut, a matrix defined by fewer than $m$ auxiliary selectors and any fixed Boolean function of their address bits has at most $q^{m-1}$ distinct rows. Knowing their vector of first factors determines the entire row. Against the target $H$, any sign matrix with $L$ distinct rows has absolute correlation at most
$$
                       \sqrt{L/q^m}+4\nu.
$$
To see this, lift the nearly uniform $B(y)$ to an exactly uniform symbol by coupling, preserving the conditional distribution of $y$ in each nonempty fiber. For each candidate row $u(y)\in\{-1,1\}$ take its conditional mean in the fiber of $B$, filling empty fibers by zero. The correlation of $u$ with the honest row labeled by $a$ changes by at most $2\nu$ under this reweighting. Against the uniform symbol the correlations of a fixed row over all $a\in R$ have squared sum at most one by additive Parseval. Take a maximum of their absolute values over the $L$ candidate rows, and average over uniform $a$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives at most $\sqrt{L/q^m}$. The varying row may depend on the entire left address. Replacing the uniform label by $A(x)$ costs at most $2\nu$. The same argument applies after fixing either value of a single address bit. In particular put $\eta=q^{-1/2}+4\nu=o(1)$ for fewer than $m$ selectors. The estimate holds for each choice even if the row dictionary was constructed with knowledge of the original targets. This calculation uses the usual inner-product character orthogonality. [Chazelle, Section 10.1](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~chazelle/pubs/book.pdf)

## Rigidity for exactly as many summands

Let $H'$ be the XOR of any $m$ competing selectors. If the relative distance between $H'$ and $H$ is below $1/128$, then the competing descriptors are exactly the original tuple up to permutation and independent Frobenius powers. The distance between $H'$ and the complement of $H$ is always at least $1/128$ for sufficiently large stages of these good tuples.

For either claimed close match, a zero auxiliary mask would leave at most $m-1$ summands. A Boolean coordinate of an auxiliary point would do the same on half the address cube. The previous bound, also conditional on one address bit, gives error at least $(1-\eta)/4$ in the latter case, also for complemented target. Hence all auxiliary products are units. Call them $A',B'$. The honest row words $w_a(y)=\langle a,B(y)\rangle$ separate distinct labels by distances in $[1/2-\nu,1/2+\nu]$. Define $w'_a$ analogously, with no separation assumed.

Let $e<1/128$ be the putative fraction of erroneous bits, and call a row close when its error is at most $1/16$. A fraction at least $1-16e$ have close rows. Near-uniformity of $A$ shows that the corresponding honest symbols occupy more than half of $R$. Since two honest row words cannot both be close to the same alternative row, the corresponding close alternative symbols occupy more than half as well. For a complemented match this is impossible. Three close alternative symbols can be chosen with $u+v=w$ by translating this majority set. Their row words XOR to zero. The three approximating complemented honest words XOR to the complement of a single honest word, which has weight at least $1/2-\nu$. Their total error is at most $3/16$.

For a positive match, each alternative symbol is a sum of two close ones. Its word lies within $2/16$ of a unique honest row, defining $D:R\to R$. It is additive since otherwise two different honest words lie within distance $6/16$ of one another. Its image contains the more-than-half set of matched honest symbols, so it is a binary-linear isomorphism. These arguments are the additive row lemma used for the [one-selector theorem](distance-between-checksum-queries.md). Write $T=D^{-1}$. The bad fraction of first addresses with $A'\ne T(A)$ is at most $e/(1/2-\nu-2/16)$. A random $i$-edge from bit zero has either endpoint bad with probability at most twice this number. On all other such edges $T(r_i A)=r'_i T(A)$. The distribution of $A$ at bit zero is within $\nu$ of uniform, so the two binary-linear functions agree everywhere, as distinct ones agree on at most half of $R$. Propagate from one good address along cube edges, forwards or backwards, to get $A'=T(A)$ everywhere. Exchanging sides gives $B'=U(B)$ everywhere with $U$ another binary-linear isomorphism. Nonidentical bilinear forms $\langle T(a),U(b)\rangle$ and $\langle a,b\rangle$ would incur at least $(1/2-\nu)^2$ distance via the proper left kernel. They are identical.

For completeness, the algebra now used is a product of fields. The unital binary subalgebra generated by the ratios $r_i$ in either block is all $R$. Otherwise all that block's products lie in a unit multiple of a proper binary subspace, contrary to near-uniformity. From $T(r_i a)=r'_i T(a)$ we obtain $T(f(r)a)=f(r')T(a)$ for every binary polynomial $f$ in that block's ratios. Since $T$ is invertible, $f(r)=0$ if and only if $f(r')=0$. Thus $f(r)\mapsto f(r')$ defines a unital algebra automorphism $S$ of $R$. Necessarily $T(a)=k S(a)$ for the unit $k=T(1)$. Similarly $U(b)=l J(b)$. Every automorphism permutes the minimal nonzero idempotents (the coordinate unit vectors) and acts within each field factor by a Frobenius power. It therefore preserves the sum of traces. Setting $b=1$ in equality of the two pairings and applying nondegeneracy gives $kl=1$, then varying $a,b$ gives $J=S$. Since $r'=S(r)$ for every ratio, inverting the odds map gives $p'=S(p)$ at every index of the two blocks. The second block is unmasked, hence $B'=S(B)$ and $l=1$. Consequently $k=1$ and the first block also gives $\lambda'=S(\lambda)$. The claimed permutations and Frobenius operations preserve every selector. Exact identifiability of tensor decompositions has a long literature. Priority of this approximate binary-selector statement is unverified. [Kruskal](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795%2877%2990069-6)

## The adaptive tree on single-coordinate flips

Each base clause true at $h$ can be falsified by flipping at most one coordinate of $h$. Consequently if $t$ clauses are retained, the tree's agreement with the target parity on a uniformly chosen single-coordinate flip $h+e_i$ is at least $1-t/V$. This remains true for the complete auxiliary checksum gates since their values can be recomputed. Regard the desired parity change as the target sign $(-1)^{H(i)}$. At any tree node the new reply is its old reply on $h$ XOR the node's selector bit at $i$. Relabel the branches at each node accordingly, and relabel the output by the old target parity. The tree is now a Boolean function of address-dependent selector bits. The relabeling is only for the proof and can use $h$.

For a path of length $k$ with signed branch values $b_1,\ldots,b_k\in\{-1,1\}$ and selector signs $u_1,\ldots,u_k$, its leaf indicator is $2^{-k}\prod_{a=1}^k(1+b_a u_a)$. Multiply by the leaf sign and sum over leaves. Every term involving fewer than $m$ occurrences has absolute correlation at most $\eta$ with the target, since it uses at most $m-1$ selectors. Their total contribution is bounded by $2^m\eta$ using the sum of absolute coefficients per leaf. This proves the $m-1$ lower bound on clauses. For depth $m$ only the full product on a depth-$m$ path can contribute more. Each has weight $2^{-m}$. Write $\rho_\ell$ for its correlation including its coefficient sign but before multiplying by this weight. There are at most $2^m$ such paths, and each $\rho_\ell\le1$. Thus
$$
  (2^m-\#\{\text{depth-}m\text{ leaves}\})
       +\sum_{\ell:\;|\ell|=m}(1-\rho_\ell)
       \ \le\ 2^m (2t/V+2^m\eta).
$$
For the claimed clause bound and sufficiently large stage the right side is less than $1/128$. All $2^m$ paths therefore reach depth $m$, each with signed correlation greater than $1-1/128$. The preceding rigidity result excludes a complemented match and forces each path's parity selector to equal the target with the stated descriptor classification. In particular each signed correlation is exactly $+1$. In the original tree the coefficient sign compares the leaf output to the XOR of the specified branch replies: the extra XOR offsets from the relabeling cancel because the path selector and the target agree on $h$. Hence each original leaf outputs that parity. This proves the theorem.

A procedure that changes the clause subset depending on the proposed table must account for how the choice itself is validated. The theorem considers one subset whose conjunction certifies a decision tree for all its satisfying tables, on any one tuple of public descriptors. It gives a barrier for nonlinear local certificates and allows correlated auxiliary queries beyond the proven depth. It does not bound the time of an unrestricted solver on the genuine SAT inputs.

## A positive refinement for correlated query grids

A verifier of genuine pair parities with adequate coverage would give a single nondeterministic certificate of the true native output. There is a sharper sufficient coverage than the [two-line bound](certifying-pairs-of-checksum-answers.md). Let $o$ be the public output coordinate, $P$ the multiaffine interpolation of the binary table, and partition the $d$ coordinates into $r$ nonempty blocks of sizes $d_1,\ldots,d_r$. Define
$$
 B=\prod_{i=1}^r(d_i+1),\qquad
 \delta_r=1-\prod_{i=1}^r(1-d_i/q).
$$
If a uniformly sound pair verifier covers a fraction strictly above $(1+\delta_r^2)/2$ of independent uniform query pairs at a stage, there is a sound certificate of $P(o)$ using at most $2s(B-1)+1$ successful pair witnesses at that stage. With per-pair verification time $T$, the total is $O(sBT)+O(sB^2\operatorname{poly}(d,s))$ in the same local-word model. In particular for fixed $r$, the time is the per-pair witness cost times a polynomial in the stage index, exactly as required in the original clock transfer.

Take independent random direction vectors on two copies of the blocks. On the first grid use a common nonzero uniform field parameter $t_i$ for all coordinates in block $i$, evaluating at $o_k+a_kt_i$ in position $k$. Form the second grid with independent directions $b$ and parameters $u_i$. Use independent uniform masks on their respective field outputs. Averaging over directions, the two endpoints are independent uniform, even conditional on all the nonzero parameter values. For fixed directions let $k_i(a)$ count the nonzero directions in block $i$. The polynomial on that grid has individual degree at most $k_i(a)$ in $t_i$; the constant term is $P(o)$. Set
$$
 z(a)=1-\prod_i(1-k_i(a)/(q-1)).
$$
Every polynomial with those individual degrees and nonzero constant vanishes on at most $z(a)$ of the parameter grid $(K^*)^r$. Indeed the coefficient of the largest power in the last variable that occurs is a nonzero polynomial in the preceding ones. Wherever that coefficient is nonzero there are at most $k_r(a)$ roots in the last variable. Induction gives the displayed product bound. Here $d<q-1$ as in the checksum construction.

Consider the linear equations in the coefficients of two hypothetical block polynomials with the same binary constant, using the **actual** degree caps $k_i(a)$ and $k_i(b)$. A candidate with opposite constant differs from the true polynomial on both sides by a polynomial of nonzero constant. On the two grids with directions fixed, its pair parities can agree on at most $(1+z(a)z(b))/2$ of the parameter/mask pairs. Unless both polynomial differences evaluate to zero, uniform independent masks make the parity of the differences a fair bit. The effective degree caps vary with directions. By independence of the blocks and $\mathbb E k_i(a)=d_i(1-1/q)$ we have $\mathbb E z(a)=\delta_r$. Independence of the two direction collections gives $\mathbb E z(a)z(b)=\delta_r^2$. Average the **difference** between actual coverage and this direction-dependent agreement bound. Its expectation is positive under the hypothesis, so some fixed pair of grids has coverage exceeding its own bound. A verifier must impose the correctly reduced degree cap on each such grid; replacing it by $d_i$ at this step would lose the improvement.

On those grids the genuine equations force their common constant. Binary row-span duality expresses the constant-coordinate functional by the XOR of at most $2s(B-1)+1$ accepted rows. Guess these, the directions and their witnesses. Verify the pair labels. For every nonconstant allowed monomial on each grid, check that the sum of its masked evaluations over the selected rows is zero. Check that the trace of the sum of all masks on both grids is one. These polynomial identities make the XOR of the witnessed parities equal the native bit on all stages, also when no coverage guarantee holds. A direct implementation costs $O(sB)$ pair verifications and $O(sB^2\operatorname{poly}(d,s))$ extra word operations by enumerating allowed monomials. Finite-field arithmetic and the metadata fit polynomial work in the stage index per operation.

Already one block gives $d/q$ instead of $d/(q-1)$. Singleton blocks give exactly the global algebraic obstruction $\delta_*=1-(1-1/q)^d$ from the two binary tables differing only at $o$. At that endpoint $B=2^d$ and the witness bound is too large for the targeted speedup. More generally,
$$
 0\le\tfrac12(\delta_r^2-\delta_*^2)
 \le \frac{d}{q^3}\sum_i\binom{d_i}{2}.
$$
For each block the difference between $(1-1/q)^{d_i}$ and $1-d_i/q$ lies between zero and $\binom{d_i}{2}/q^2$ by the first two union bounds on independent hits. Telescope the products and use $\delta_*\le\delta_r\le d/q$. For $r=\lfloor\sqrt d\rfloor$ balanced blocks the number of coefficients is $2^{O(\sqrt d\log d)}$ and the coverage excess over the global obstruction is $O(d^{5/2}/q^3)$. This consumes a subexponential factor in the pair-verification budget. The supporting uniformly sound verifier at the required coverage and cost remains missing.
