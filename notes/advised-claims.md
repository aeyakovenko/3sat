# How many independent parity claims can a few advice bits buy?

**Research journal, 2026-11-24.** The [sparse diagonal](coded-diagonal.md) makes one wrong claim suffice to defeat an unadvised predictor. The construction here permits the verifier to receive advice chosen after the true answers are known. Each advice bit can trivially supply one independent answer. The question is whether a small verifier can turn that information into an unbounded number of additional correct claims. A second choice of parity queries below gives an ordinary language with exactly three yes instances per sufficiently large length and reduces the target to identifying two of them.

## Excluding sound claims beyond the advice budget

Fix an integer budget function A(n)=O(n), computable in polynomial time. The most concrete instance is A(n)=n. As before let N=2ⁿ be the number of entries in a binary truth table, and use claims that are parities of N bits. For the polynomial remainder construction the query specifies a monic irreducible binary polynomial f of degree b=2n and a b-bit mask v. Its value on a coefficient table Y is the dot product of v with the coefficients of Y modulo f. Each proposed signed claim includes a bit. A verifier is sound on a length with a particular advice if every claim it certifies there has the right bit.

The following source J depends on A and the query family, and is constructed **independently of a SAT algorithm**. Enumerate deterministic multitape claim recognizers i=1,…,n and truncate each call after 2^(20n) simulated steps. For each i and every advice of length at most A(n), collect the first

    hᵢ = A(n) + 2⌈log₂(i+1)⌉ + 3

linearly independent claimed normals in canonical claim order, keeping their proposed bits. If fewer than hᵢ occur, issue no constraint for that advice. Otherwise the event to avoid is that all the chosen claims hold. A uniform random table gives the event probability 2^(−hᵢ), even if another batch uses the same normals. The sum over all programs and advice strings is below one, using fewer than 2^(A+1) advice strings and Σ_{i≥1}(i+1)⁻²<1. Thus there is a table that avoids all the events.

For CRT queries one can use a uniform table supported just on the last b·maxᵢ hᵢ positions (or all N positions at small lengths). Indeed any batch of h rows uses at most h moduli. For their product B, of degree d≤bh, restriction of polynomial residues to the final d consecutive powers of X maps onto the entire quotient by B: X is a unit there and the ordinary d power-basis elements span. Thus the batch retains its full row rank on this same common tail for every advice and program. This gives a polynomially sparse table even when the construction considers exponentially many possible advices.

Find the table by assigning its tail bits in order. Conditional on assigned bits, the probability of satisfying a batch is either zero if the remaining system is inconsistent, or 2⁻ʳ with r its remaining rank. Compute both possible sums of event probabilities by Gaussian elimination and fix a bit giving the smaller sum. The initial bound survives, and the final sum is an integer below one. With A=O(n) there are 2^{O(n)} batches and candidates, hᵢ=O(n), and only O(n²) tail bits. All probability sums can use a common denominator 2^{max hᵢ}. Universal simulation and elimination fit 2^{O(n)} deterministic tape time including the work to calculate the query rows. This defines an ordinary source language. Its truth table and allowed support set differ from those of the preceding unadvised construction.

Suppose cubic SAT holds on the unrestricted-workspace word RAM, with size measured in encoded bits. A nondeterministic verifier with clock n^{O(1)}2ⁿ on a claim, proposed bit and advice can be simulated by an ordinary deterministic multitape program i within the cutoff for all sufficiently large n. A quasilinear reduction to explicit SAT and direct RAM-to-tape simulation give 2^(6n+o(n)) on tapes. For this fixed i, *each* advice of length at most A(n) at *every* such length is either unsound or yields rank less than hᵢ. Hence a verifier with unbounded excess of sound rank over A(n), even on an unrecognized subsequence with different good advice at every length, would refute the cubic SAT hypothesis. Supplying the true bits for A publicly indexed independent rows already attains rank A. [RAM simulation](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~ryanw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf)

If advice is measured by its actual length ℓ and all lengths from zero to A are allowed, one must also charge for the length itself. One simultaneous bound replaces A in hᵢ by ℓ+2⌈log₂(ℓ+1)⌉+O(1), summing first over ℓ. Another charges ℓ+⌈log₂(A+1)⌉+O(1) by assigning equal weight to each length. A uniform bound ℓ+Oᵢ(1) across unrestricted raw lengths would be false. For example take A=n and k=⌊log₂(A/2)⌋. Choose the length ℓ in [2ᵏ,2^(k+1)) whose k low bits equal the first k true answers in a public independent sequence. Store the next ℓ answers as ordinary advice bits. The verifier recovers ℓ+k independent true labels on every source, even with a single degree-b modulus since ℓ+k≤b. There is no such free channel for a prescribed fixed length.

One can also tolerate errors. In a batch of h independent claims the probability of at most e wrong labels is 2⁻ʰΣ_{j≤e}binom(h,j). With h=O(n) choose parameters for which h−log₂Σ_{j≤e}binom(h,j) exceeds A+2⌈log₂(i+1)⌉+3 at stage i. The same argument avoids all these events. Compute a conditional probability by enumerating the admissible error patterns and adding their disjoint linear-system probabilities. There are at most 2^h patterns. A batch that is reached then contains at least e+1 independent falsely labeled normals. This is a variant source for the chosen parameters.

## Can the tail be shortened enough?

The common-tail rank-preservation calculation has an exact converse. Assume hb≤N and at least h distinct available moduli. For any coordinate set P of size below hb, use h distinct moduli, each with its b basic residue coordinates. Their hb rows are independent on the full table by the Chinese remainder theorem. Their restrictions to P have a nonzero binary relation. Combine its coefficients per modulus to turn that relation into a sum of at most h single-mask rows. The rows with nonzero masks are independent on the full table since they belong to distinct residue coordinates in the direct product of fields. Their sum vanishes on P. Consequently even an irregular set P preserving independence of **all** groups of at most h masks must have at least hb positions. The construction only needs to avoid the particular events enumerated, which leaves room for other ways to constrain its actual table.

In the opposite direction, a smaller known support can give *genuine additional parity information* using only single-modulus claims. Suppose at a tested length it is guaranteed that all possible one positions of Jₙ are in a publicly generable set P of size m. Take g=⌊m/b⌋+1 distinct moduli. The same linear dependency supplies k≤g distinct queries with an identically zero XOR on P. Supply k−1 of their actual label bits as advice. The verifier recovers the final bit by XOR and can certify all k individually. This produces one extra independent claim. Repeat the calculation in disjoint clusters of moduli, so all recovered normals are jointly independent on the unrestricted table. If A=n and m=o(nb) along the tested lengths, g=o(n) there. Take K=⌊n/max(1,g)⌋ clusters and spend at most K(g−1) advice bits on them. Spend the rest of n on individually advised nonzero masks at fresh moduli. The resulting verifier certifies rank exactly n+K, with K unbounded. At most O(n) distinct moduli were used and their total degree is O(n²)<N eventually. The advice need only be accurate for the actual table. The tested lengths need not be recognizable.

Generating the needed moduli fits the clock. Construct a degree-b binary extension by Shoup's deterministic algorithm. Fewer than 2^(b/2+1) elements belong to proper subfields in total. In a fixed enumeration of field elements, examine the first 2^(b/2+1)+O(nb) elements, find their degrees by Frobenius, and take distinct degree-b conjugacy classes. Each supplies its minimal polynomial and has b elements. This takes 2ⁿ poly(n) time when b=2n. For a coordinate p of P compute the residues of Xᵖ by repeated squaring, obtain the binary relation by elimination, and select its components in a canonical order. Require the producer of P to fit n^{O(1)}2ⁿ time and bound m by O(n²) on every query, abandoning an oversized output. The construction covers subsequences on which its claimed support contains the actual support and is o(n²). [Shoup](https://www.shoup.net/papers/detirred.pdf)

The support need not be described by a list of positions. A known affine space of dimension m containing the table also works when its offset and direction vectors admit all required residue evaluations at the stated clock. Use the differences for the dependency and use the offset to compute its right-hand side. In particular advice can identify a *list* of candidates without identifying the correct one. If an A-bit prefix names at most b candidate tables, there is a nonzero mask common to their answers up to a fixed output bit at each modulus: the differences of at most b residue vectors span dimension at most b−1. The verifier can certify one such fact at arbitrarily many fresh moduli provided it can enumerate and evaluate the named candidate tables within its clock. This saves up to ⌊log₂ b⌋ bits over naming one candidate from a uniformly indexed list. The number b is exact for an arbitrary uncertainty list at a single modulus: the b+1 polynomials 0,1,X,…,X^(b−1) leave no nonzero mask constant at any degree-b modulus.

For unrestricted families of normals, in particular unit-vector queries, extremely sparse known support answers behave differently. Any table with at most w ones leaves an empty block in a public partition into w+1 nonempty blocks. An advice of ⌈log₂(w+1)⌉ bits can name one of them and thereby certify at least ⌊N/(w+1)⌋ independent unit answers as zero. This defeats an attempted extension of the sparse advised-rank bound to arbitrary query families at such advice lengths. For A=O(log n) a general construction can instead keep a pivot set for the span of *all* sampled rows, of polynomial size because the number of batches is polynomial. Coordinate restriction to this set preserves every sampled rank. Its actual size and the applicable advice budget both matter.

For the CRT source, a sufficiently fast *deterministic* generator of a support set of size o(n²) is already excluded unconditionally. Indeed its advised rank-recovery program is a deterministic predictor inside the original simulation cutoff. If support generation needs s bits of fixed-format advice, leave n−s label bits and take K=⌊(n−s)/g⌋ clusters. The recovered rank is n−s+K. The same diagonal excludes an unbounded excess K−s for any fixed generator and decoder within the cutoff. A genuinely additional hypothesis is that a fast *nondeterministic* recognizer singles out a containing set of o(n²) possible positions at infinitely many lengths. Under cubic SAT its membership could be tabulated deterministically on the known O(n²) tail, after which the recovery program would contradict the unconditional bound. The CRT construction controls the tail size, but does not ensure that the true Hamming weight is o(n²).

## A source with exactly three one bits

There is a different family of grouped parity questions for which the advised source can have **exactly three** one positions at every sufficiently large length, while retaining the same bound of n+Oᵢ(1) on sound rank for n advice bits. Use A=n, b=⌊n/2⌋, q=2ᵇ and D=n². Work at lengths with D<q and b>0. Index the first q² table positions by pairs (u,v) in the binary field of order q, and give all query normals value zero at the unused positions. Choose any nonzero binary linear functional L on the field, for instance one coefficient in the polynomial basis. The query with group 0≤j≤D and scalar mask λ has normal

    r(j,λ)[u,v] = L(v λ uʲ).

Use u⁰=1 also at u=0. Combining rows in a group adds their scalar masks. A combination over groups is L(v P(u)) for a polynomial P of degree at most D. For a uniform random pair (u,v), its signed mean, the expectation of (−1) raised to this bit, is exactly the proportion of roots of P in the field. At each nonroot the map v↦vP(u) permutes the field and L is balanced. Hence every nonzero polynomial has signed mean at most D/q. In particular the b basic queries in each group are jointly independent across all groups. This is the powering construction for small-bias spaces in field coordinates. [Alon, Goldreich, Håstad and Peralta](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/aghp4.pdf)

Take the XOR of the indicators of three independently uniform positions (u,v), permitting repeats. Each nontrivial linear combination of independent query rows has signed mean of absolute value at most (D/q)³ on this table, by independence of the three positions. A prescribed outcome for h independent bits therefore has probability at most

    2^(−h) + (1−2^(−h))(D/q)³.

Indeed its indicator is 2^(−h) times the sum of the 2ʰ signed characters on the output, each multiplied by the sign of the prescribed outcome. The trivial character contributes one. Use the same machine/advice batches as in the first construction. The sum of their 2^(−hᵢ) terms is below 1/4. The sum of their extra terms is at most n 2^(n+1)(D/q)³, tending to zero, including odd n. We may also require the three abscissas u to be distinct and nonzero and the three multipliers v to be distinct and nonzero. Each equality or zero condition has probability 1/q, so their combined probability is at most 12/q. Thus some triple avoids all these forbidden events simultaneously. Enumerate the triples in canonical order and use the first that avoids every event. There are at most 2^(3n) triples and 2^{O(n)} query/program/advice work overall before testing triples. Each table evaluation tests three positions. The field can be constructed canonically by searching degree-b irreducibles and applying binary polynomial arithmetic within 2ᵇ poly(n). The entire construction is uniform deterministic 2^{O(n)} tape time. When a short length has no avoiding triple, prescribe the zero table there.

Distinct abscissas already ensure three marks. Even if we omit these extra constraints, the number of marked positions in an XOR of three unit tables is one or three. At weight one, n advice bits naming that position enable a fixed polynomial-overhead predictor (including field construction within 2ᵇ poly(n)) to answer *all* queries, of joint rank b(D+1). This exceeds the stage threshold at every sufficiently large length. Therefore the source actually has weight three eventually. In particular the eventual census is known without computing any of its positions. The support-cluster argument also works for these new groups since they again form a direct sum of binary dimension-b spaces. Enumeration of the group identifiers is now immediate.

### What positive certificates would suffice?

* **Two identifiable marks.** Suppose a fixed nondeterministic 2ⁿ poly(n)-time predicate on ordinary n-bit positions accepts a subset of the three marks, of size at least two, at infinitely many lengths. Under cubic SAT, enumerate all N positions and decide the predicate at each one. Even the direct tape simulation costs only 2^(7n+o(n)) total, below the claim-predictor cutoff. If exactly two are returned, n advice bits name the remaining one. If three are returned, their list is complete. Ignore other counts. On the good lengths there is advice for which every query can be correctly answered. This contradicts the deterministic bound. The predicate need not recognize the good lengths.
* **A small menu containing the marks.** More generally, suppose a 2ⁿ poly(n)-time nondeterministic position predicate accepts some set P containing all three marks at infinitely many lengths, where its size m satisfies binom(m,3)≤b·2ⁿ there. Tabulate P using the same decision procedure. List its unordered triples in a fixed order and group them in buckets of size at most b. At good lengths one n-bit advice string identifies a bucket containing the true triple. In any individual group j, every triple gives a field value Σ v uʲ, and the b or fewer candidate values have differences spanning dimension less than b. Find a nonzero mask λ whose label L(λ·) is constant on the bucket (multiplication against L identifies the field with its binary dual). Certify that label in every group. This yields at least D+1 independent claims. One can generate the specified bucket by integer unranking or scanning the triples in time 2^{3n} poly(n). The bound on m permits roughly (6b·2ⁿ)^(1/3) candidates. The same reasoning applies to any uniformly testable menu of at most b·2ⁿ triples containing the true one, with arbitrary overlaps, by tabulating all candidate triples under cubic SAT in 2^(9n+o(n)) tape time.
* **One mark and a restricted remainder.** If the deterministic tabulation under cubic SAT identifies a single actual mark and a menu of m positions containing the other two, the same proof applies whenever binom(m,2)≤b·2ⁿ. The resulting threshold for m is of order 2^(n/2)√n. As above the identification and containment only need to hold together on infinitely many lengths.

With deterministic menu membership already within those clocks, the same contradiction rules out the corresponding menus unconditionally. The remaining question is how to recognize sufficiently many marks or confine the possible triples nondeterministically in 2ⁿ poly(n) time. The fact that there are exactly three marks by itself gives no fast test that a proposed position is one of them. The nondeterministic hypotheses above concern the constructed language itself.

## How many bits of one abscissa would suffice?

Retune the three-point construction with D=n² and field degree

    b = ⌈(n + 8⌈log₂(n+2)⌉)/3⌉.

The first q²=2^(2b) positions still fit in the 2ⁿ-entry table for large n. The three-impulse error summed over program/advice pairs is at most 2n⁷·2^(n−3b)=O(1/n), and the coordinate-distinctness conditions cost at most 12/q. Hence this new source retains the full n-bit advice obstruction and the three distinct abscissas. The degree adjustment changes which source language is constructed.

For known abscissas, the normalized monomial-span construction gives more than 2n independent full-table rows whose actual answers require at most 192n^(2/3) advice bits at all sufficiently large lengths. In its notation choose the least dyadic L with (L+1)s_L>2n. Then (L+1)³>2n gives L≥n^(1/3) eventually. Minimality and doubling give s_L≤4s_(L/2)≤16n/L. The necessary label rank is at most 3s_(2L)≤12s_L≤192n^(2/3). For this calculation a fixed coefficient functional on F can equally be written as a trace against a fixed nonzero scalar. [Normalized monomial spans](moment-information.md)

Suppose a uniform 2ⁿ poly(n)-time nondeterministic predicate on field elements accepts a set U hitting at least one of the three abscissas at infinitely many lengths, with

    |U| ≤ 2^(b−⌈200 n^(2/3)⌉)

there. Under cubic SAT deterministically decide this predicate on every field element in canonical order, within the existing clock. A good advice string identifies one true member of U by its index, supplies the two remaining abscissas explicitly, and supplies the basis labels for the recovered triple. The cost is at most

    ⌈log₂|U|⌉ + 2b + 192 n^(2/3) ≤ 3b − 8 n^(2/3) ≤ n

eventually. The decoder can fix each block length from n and the tabulated set, reject oversized inputs, sort the three proposed abscissas, and compute the basis before reading its labels. It certifies more than 2n independent true bits on the good lengths, contradicting the advised obstruction under cubic SAT. If U is empty the decoder simply produces no claims.

In particular, one fully identified true abscissa suffices. It even suffices to obtain the first ⌈200 n^(2/3)⌉ bits of one true abscissa, with its identity among the three unspecified. A uniform procedure producing the prefix defines U by prefix agreement. The same counting applies to equally many specified bit positions when those positions and their prescribed values are available in the locator clock. The charge above requires the partial information to come from the locator, independently of the n-bit advice. A uniform deterministic locator already fitting the cutoff is excluded unconditionally. This criterion leaves unresolved how to find the required partial information about any of the actual three abscissas.

## Two untagged marks with four field moments

Shrinking the number of groups changes how much advice two impulses can withstand. Retain j=0,1,2,3, so D=3 and the full rank is 4b. At length n examine s=⌈log₂(n+2)⌉ programs. Let H be the least power of two at least 256s, and take A=n−log₂ H. Use large enough n for A≥0 and q>3. Every fixed program eventually appears. For two random positions the signed mean is at most (3/q)² on every nonzero combination. The total contribution of these biases across all forbidden batches is at most

    s·2^(A+1)·(3/q)² ≤ 36s/H ≤ 36/256.

The other contribution is below 1/4 by the earlier summation of 2^(−hᵢ). Enumerating pairs therefore gives an ordinary E source obeying the advised rank bound. We can again insist on distinct nonzero abscissas and distinct nonzero multipliers at an extra cost of at most 6/q in the union bound. Even when we drop these constraints, the weight is zero or two. A zero table would make a fixed predictor claiming zero on every row sound, while it has rank 4b>A+O(1). Thus eventually the table has exactly two marked positions. The positions use the same untagged domain and the same four query groups.

At a good length suppose a quickly tabulable set P of size m contains **at least one** true mark. Build a list of possible two-element supports by pairing every member of P with every other table position, retaining a canonical copy of each pair. There are at most mN pairs. Divide the list into 2ᴬ consecutive buckets of size at most mH (or use fixed bucket capacity mH). One advice string names a bucket containing the true pair. If its actual size is k, the candidate group values have differences spanning at most k−1 binary dimensions. Hence *all* masks in an annihilator space of dimension at least b−k+1 give reliable bits at each group, with total rank at least 4(b−k+1) across the four groups. With m=o(n/log(n+2)) on infinitely many lengths one has k=o(n) there and hence sound rank 2n−o(n), more than the budget plus any program constant. When the budget is known, a single fixed verifier computes this menu uniformly, including by direct enumeration of all N² pairs.

Consequently a uniform 2ⁿ poly(n)-time nondeterministic position predicate that accepts such a short list *hitting either of the two marks*, even with false positives and even on unrecognized lengths, suffices to refute cubic SAT. Tabulating the position predicate under that hypothesis costs 2^(7n+o(n)) tape time; constructing, scanning, or sorting the pairs can also be done beneath the 2^(20n) cutoff, for example by enumerating all unordered pairs and consulting the stored position table by sequential scans. Identifying a single actual mark is the case m=1. One may substitute any slowly diverging computable stage count s for the logarithm, with the corresponding sufficient list-size condition m·s=o(n), assuming s is cheap to compute and log₂ s=o(n).

For the predicates tested on individual positions, the same cutoff accommodates **one alternation**. Allow two alternating blocks of witnesses of length at most 2ⁿ poly(n) and a deterministic 2ⁿ poly(n)-time verification matrix, starting with either quantifier. Under cubic SAT the inner block can be decided or complemented in 2^(3n+o(n)) RAM time. The outer computation, which includes that decision procedure, has an explicit SAT reduction and takes 2^(9n+o(n)) RAM time to decide. Simulate separately for each position on tapes and retain the answer. The total is 2^(19n+o(n)), beneath the existing cutoff. A predetermined larger number of alternating blocks can be accommodated by increasing the diagonal cutoff and constructing the corresponding new source. The two-block estimate applies to the N individual position checks.

This census offers a direct test of attempted witnesses: a provably short list containing a mark on infinitely many lengths is enough. Guessing addresses alone can generate lists much larger than the bound. The ordinary SAT time lower bound remains open. The field character construction is classical, and priority for these combinations of sparsity, advice and sound rank has not been established.

**Finite and formal checks.** For GF(4) with modulus X²+X+1, L the constant coefficient and j=0,1,2, direct enumeration checked all 63 nonzero characters against the root-count formula. Enumerating the 256 ordered pairs and 4,096 ordered triples then verified the exact signed-power identity. For each of the 1,953 pairs of distinct nonzero binary characters (rank two), test all four prescribed labels. The largest event probabilities were 7/16 for two impulses and 11/32 for three, both equal to the displayed Fourier upper bound with h=2 and D/q=1/2.
