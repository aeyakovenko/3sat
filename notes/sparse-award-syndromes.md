# How many checksum bits can certify the actual rare awards?

Let $z_i=1-b_i$ be the indicator of a zero output of the [fixed priority program](priority-pinned-sat.md). It computes the complete prefix through $h$ in $O(2^h+1)$ native steps, $\sum_{i\le J}z_i\le A_J=a_J=O(\log\log(J+4))$, and a fixed sound deterministic detector of either value at scale $i$ needs at least a program-dependent positive multiple of $2^i$ steps on every sufficiently late success.

Consider the last $L=\lfloor\log_2 J\rfloor$ bits $u_J=(z_{J-L+1},\ldots,z_J)$ and abbreviate $A=A_J$. Explicitly computable binary parity measurements give a syndrome $H_Ju_J$ of $m=2A\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil$ bits which determines the whole window among words of weight at most $A$. For any fixed $k\in\{1,2,3\}$ the following gives a test of an ordinary deterministic $O(n^k)$ SAT algorithm. Suppose a fixed nondeterministic RAM verifier can certify a parity of the syndrome bits, with the queried parity named by an $m$-bit vector. Soundness requires every accepted claimed value to be correct at every stage. There need be no proofs for other queries. At cap
$$
 R_J=\left\lfloor 2^{\lfloor J/k\rfloor}/(J+1)^B\right\rfloor
$$
let $r_J$ be the dimension over $\mathbb F_2$ of the span of all coefficient vectors with an accepted proof of length in machine steps at most $R_J$. For a sufficiently large fixed $B$, under the fast SAT hypothesis as $J$ tends to infinity,
$$
 (m-r_J)/\log_2 L\ \longrightarrow\ \infty.
$$
In particular it would suffice to construct such a sound verifier leaving at most $C\log_2\log_2 J$ independent checksum directions uncertified on infinitely many stages, for any fixed $C$. This permits both the unproved checks and the sparse completions to vary with $J$. The candidate requires information about several real outputs and has a quantified tolerance for missing information.

## Alternative sparse windows compatible with the true past

For a family of admissible windows $S_J\subseteq\{0,1\}^L$, always containing $u_J$, suppose a fixed deterministic algorithm produces their full lists in $O(2^J/(J+1)^4)$ time. It suffices to generate the lists of admissible words of weight at most $A$, and we take $S_J$ to consist only of those words. There exists a constant $d\ge1$ such that for every sufficiently large $J$ and each $J-L+d\le i\le J$ there is $v\in S_J$ which agrees with the true window through $i-d$ yet has $v_i\ne z_i$. Here indices on a window word refer to their original stage numbers.

To prove this, use the same fixed algorithm at target scale $i$ for all potentially relevant $J$. List every $i\le J\le i+2\lceil\log_2(i+2)\rceil$ for which $J-\lfloor\log_2J\rfloor<i$. These include every $J$ whose window contains $i$ at large $i$. For any such $J$, $2^J\le2^i 2^L\le 2^i J$, and the setup to obtain all lists costs $o(2^i)$ even after summing over $J$. Virtual length metadata and fixed-width simulation cost at most a fixed polynomial in $i$ if needed; charge these costs when constructing the list generator so the displayed bound holds on the target-scale host. For the applications below, both hosts have $O(i)$-bit words with bounded coefficient ratios, and the budget has arbitrary fixed polynomial slack.

Start with no known bits. Replay the true priority stages in chronological order. After learning through $h$, for each candidate $J$ retain just list entries consistent with the known true prefix. The real window remains among them. If the retained entries for any one $J$ agree at $i$, their common bit is sound. Stop and signal it. Run two fixed versions if a separate detector is required for each color. In the checksum and sparse-table applications the total time for these filtering passes, even if recomputed from scratch up to $i$ times, is $2^{o(i)}$: there are at most $(L+1)^{A+1}$ words per list, with $A=O(\log\log i)$. Through stage $h$ the remaining cost is $O(2^h+1)$, including consistent simulation of earlier native widths. By bi-immunity choose $d$ such that this last bound at $h=i-d$, plus setup and filtering, is below both detectors' positive lower-bound constants times $2^i$ at large $i$. Thus neither detector can have certified a value by then. This argument fixes the programs before choosing $d$.

Choose $i$ spaced $d$ apart in the last $L$ positions, starting at $J-L+d$. For each $i$ the difference $v\oplus u_J$ has its *first* nonzero coordinate in $[i-d+1,i]$. The chosen intervals are disjoint. These differences are linearly independent: in a nonempty sum take the vector with the earliest first nonzero coordinate. This proves three consequences.

* Every admissible list contains $\Omega(L)$ **distinct** sparse alternatives. They survive despite the exact preceding prefix used in each test.
* If the list is cut out by affine checks, their common binary kernel contains $\Omega(L)$ independent words of weight at most $2A$. Each is the difference between the actual window and an admissible sparse alternative. This is stronger than a dimension count for a system with few rows.
* A global version holds over all $J$ bits. If the lists of sparse candidates can be generated in $2^{o(J)}$ time, the same argument applies to every $i\in[J/2,J]$, trying $i\le J\le2i$. Total filtering time is $2^{o(i)}$ since $\sum_{t\le A}\binom Jt\le(J+1)^{A+1}$. Hence there are $\Omega(J)$ independent sparse differences with first disagreements in disjoint bounded intervals. A polynomial-time list of affine measurements and *their actual values* gives this setting unconditionally. An arbitrary matrix alone provides no such information.

## Why a constant number of exceptional positions does not suffice

Most windows of length $\lfloor\log_2 J\rfloor$ contain no zero award at all: each of the $A_X$ rare stages through $X$ belongs to at most $1+\lfloor\log_2 X\rfloor$ such windows ending through $X$. Nevertheless the numbers of rare awards inside individual windows are unbounded, even though the full sequence is exceptionally sparse.

More precisely take $F(i)=i+g(i)$ with $g(i)=\lceil\log_2\lceil\log_2(i+4)\rceil\rceil$. For every fixed word $w$ of length $s$, there are infinitely many $i$ on which the genuine sequence at $i,F(i),\ldots,F^{s-1}(i)$ spells $w$. For the induction, suppose a prefix of length $s$ occurs infinitely often. On a proposed next endpoint $j$ determine whether $j=F^s(i)$ and the preceding $s$ bits match the prefix. Invert the strictly increasing map by bounded search. The actual prior bits can all be read by replay through $F^{s-1}(i)$, at cost $O(2^{F^{s-1}(i)}+\operatorname{poly}(j))=o(2^j)$ because the last gap diverges. Thus the set of possible next endpoints is infinite and decidable within the short clock. Both possible next bits occur there infinitely often: otherwise after hardwiring the finitely many exceptions this decider would be a sound timely signal for one color. The empty prefix starts the induction. For fixed $s$ these positions occupy $O_s(\log\log J)$ successive stages at endpoint $J$, eventually within the chosen window. Taking words of all ones in the indicator sequence proves the claim about bursts of rare awards. No bound on their frequency or effective location follows from this argument.

## An exact native lower bound for certifying a missing pattern

Fix any nonempty binary word $p$ and any $\operatorname{poly}(J)$-time accessible $\ell(J)\in[1,J]$ with $J-\ell(J)\to\infty$. A fixed deterministic partial tester that signals only if $p$ is absent as a **scattered subsequence** of $z_{\ell(J)},\ldots,z_J$ takes $\Omega_D(2^J)$ steps at every sufficiently late success. For $p=1^{K+1}$ this merely asks to certify that a growing window contains at most $K$ rare awards, for any fixed $K$. At logarithmic window length this weak statement holds on a density-one set. The same lower bound applies to a sound certificate that *some unspecified word* of fixed length $s$ is absent: every length-$s$ word is a subsequence of $(01)^s$, so the purported certificate entails the absence of this one fixed word. Here the deterministic tester has no external proof string. Under $O(n^k)$ SAT, bounded nondeterministic successes at $2^{J/k}/(J+1)^B$ for sufficiently large fixed $B$ give such a tester by one bounded-run SAT query.

Prove the native bound by induction on $|p|$. For a one-letter pattern, absence forces the opposite endpoint bit, so its sound tester is subject to the ordinary one-sign bound. For the induction write $p=p'c$ with $p'$ nonempty. Suppose a tester $D$ has a sequence of late successes of cost $t_D(J)=o(2^J)$ along that sequence. Form a fixed detector for $z_J\ne c$ that first waits for $D(J)$ to signal. It replays the true history until it sees $p'$ as a subsequence between $\ell(J)$ and $J-1$. If it finds one, it signals. The absence guarantee makes this detector sound. If $p'$ already appears by $h<J$, its total work is at most $C(t_D(J)+2^h+1)+o(2^J)$ for a fixed constant $C$, uniformly in $h$. In particular its program does not depend on a chosen gap $J-h$. The additive setup may use the native interpreter's $2^{J/4}\operatorname{poly}(J)$ initialization.

Let the detector's lower-bound constant be $\gamma>0$. Choose a fixed positive integer $d$ and a fixed dyadic $\eta>0$ so that $C(2^{-d}+\eta)<\gamma/2$. At all sufficiently late successes of $D$ within $\eta 2^J$ steps, $p'$ must be absent from $[\ell(J),J-d]$. On input scale $i$ run $D(i+d)$ through that cap, and signal for the absence of $p'$ in $[\ell(i+d),i]$ upon a success. Hardwire a fixed threshold discarding the finite exceptions. This is a sound fixed partial tester for the shorter pattern. Its window length still diverges. If necessary use $\min(i,\ell(i+d))$ at the finitely many short scales. The native word sizes at $i$ and $i+d$ differ by a fixed additive number of bits. The [native fixed-limb and sparse-bank simulation](priority-pinned-sat.md) costs a constant number of host operations per guest operation on this fixed $D$, with $o(2^i)$ setup. On the originally assumed arbitrarily cheap successes, the cap is respected and the shifted tester takes $o(2^i)$ since $2^{i+d}/2^i=2^d$ is fixed. This contradicts the induction hypothesis. All constants in this argument may depend on the tester, pattern and window rule.

A presence certificate has an explicit fast implementation on infinitely many genuine windows. For fixed positive integer $C$, let the window have $C\lfloor\log_2 J\rfloor$ positions. Take infinitely many starts $i$ realizing the chosen fixed word at the spaced stages $i,F(i),\ldots,F^{s-1}(i)$ above. Choose $J=i+C\lfloor\log_2 i\rfloor-1$. The window starts at or before $i$, differing from $i$ by $O_C(1)$ for large $i$. The last required stage is within $O_{C,s}(\log\log J)$ positions of that start. A single deterministic procedure replays stages until it actually finds the pattern, accepting only then. On these windows its cost is $2^J J^{-C+o(1)}$. With left endpoint $\lfloor\beta J\rfloor$ for fixed rational $0<\beta<1/k$, choose $J$ with $\lfloor\beta J\rfloor=i$ instead. The same presence test then succeeds in $2^{\beta J+o(J)}$ on infinitely many real windows. Its successful runs certify presence. The lower bound and the proposed SAT contradiction require a certificate of absence.

## Amplification using the sparse true answers

In fact every last-window list $S_J$ above has $|S_J|=L^{\omega(1)}$. Both the list and the condition that its size is small must be uniform. Here is the proof, which works even for lists described by nonlinear constraints.

Suppose that for some fixed positive integer $c$, infinitely many $J$ have $|S_J|\le L^c$. Scan endpoints in increasing order and keep a small-list window if it is disjoint from all previously kept windows. Infinitely many remain, since their left endpoints tend to infinity. The kept windows and their endpoints in any prescribed finite range can be recognized by a uniform deterministic computation. For a target $i$ simulate the scan through $i+2\lceil\log_2(i+2)\rceil$. Summing the $O(2^J/(J+1)^4)$ costs through this upper endpoint gives $O(2^i/(i+1)^2)+2^{o(i)}$. This includes all possible kept windows containing $i$. Store the last retained right endpoint while scanning. Only the potential target window's list needs to survive after the scan. Sparse list operations cost $2^{o(i)}$.

Choose a fixed integer $t>c+1$. In each kept window set $w=\lfloor L/(2t)\rfloor$ and choose $t$ consecutive pairs of subintervals of width $w$. Use the first subinterval of each pair as a zone and the other as a gap. Discard the final remainder and finitely many short windows. From the current list choose in the first zone a coordinate with the fewest candidate ones, breaking ties deterministically. Keep only candidates with a one there, then proceed to the next zone. On an empty list choose the first coordinate of each remaining zone. All these choices are determined by the public list. If $s$ candidates remain at the start of a zone, summing their weights in that zone gives at most $As$. The chosen coordinate retains at most $As/w$ candidates. Thus after $t$ choices the number remaining is at most $L^c(A/w)^t$, eventually less than one since $A=O(\log L)$.

Yet infinitely many kept windows realize all $t$ chosen ones in the **actual** sequence. Induct on the number of chosen ones already realized. From a target $i$, test whether it is exactly the chosen position in the next zone of a kept window and whether all previous chosen positions have true bit one. The windows are disjoint and the public scan is timely. The true-bit tests use the actual priority computation through at most $i-w$ with $w\to\infty$. Therefore their total cost, including setup, is $o(2^i)$. If there are infinitely many windows with the previous choices realized, this defines an infinite easy set of target stages. It contains infinitely many actual ones by bi-immunity. The induction starts with every kept window. On a window realizing all $t$, its true word survives the entire filtering process, a contradiction. This proves the growth assertion on the full tail, even if small-list exceptions would have extremely low density.

The same argument in the global version gives list size $J^{\omega(1)}$ when list production takes $2^{o(J)}$. Use zones and gaps in the last half of $[1,J]$. Select disjoint **last halves**, rather than disjoint full prefixes. Recognizing these through $2i$ and replaying selected earlier positions remains timely. For list production of cost $2^{k\beta J+o(J)}$ use disjoint last fractions $[\lceil\theta J\rceil,J]$ with $\theta>k\beta$, and scan through $\lfloor i/\theta\rfloor$. The maximum weight is $O(\log\log J)$, and enumeration of these sparse words costs $2^{o(J)}$ time.

In particular let a verifier certify that the true window differs from a proposed sparse word $v$. Soundness requires that it cannot accept when $v=u_J$. Under the hypothesized fast SAT algorithm, test each proposed word for a proof through $R_J$ steps using the ordinary bounded-run CNF. There are at most $2^L\le J$ possible words in total, so a fixed large $B$ makes all tests fit $O(2^J/(J+1)^4)$. The resulting list of unrefuted sparse words contains $u_J$. Consequently on **every sufficiently late window**, more than every fixed power of $L$ sparse alternatives eventually escape that verifier's bounded refutations. Producing a verifier that cuts the list down to *any* fixed power on infinitely many windows is sufficient for the unrestricted time exclusion at the chosen $k$. Moreover the VC dimension of these surviving sparse words must tend to infinity: a family of VC dimension at most $d$ has cardinality at most $\sum_{v=0}^d\binom Lv$. Thus for each fixed $t$, every sufficiently late window contains $t$ positions on which all $2^t$ joint patterns extend to an unrefuted sparse word. [Sauer](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(72)90019-2)

## Computing the span under a SAT hypothesis

For the last-window theorem, find a basis of certified augmented vectors $(q,b)$, where $q\in\mathbb F_2^m$ and the accepted bit is $b=q\cdot H_Ju_J$. Ask whether there exist $q,b$ outside the current augmented span together with an accepting verifier run of at most $R_J$ steps. This is a bounded nondeterministic computation with public input consisting of the scale, the current basis and any prescribed prefix of $(q,b)$. Eliminate the existential guess with the [ordinary, explicitly encoded 3-CNF compiler](packed-band-transfer.md). Its formula length, construction cost and the width adaptation for a solver are bounded by $(R_J+\operatorname{poly}(J))J^{O(1)}$ in this exponential-clock regime. No oracle gates or implicit-input SAT algorithm are assumed. A positive answer lets us recover $(q,b)$ by prefix queries. Repeat until no new vector exists. Soundness implies projection from the augmented span to coefficient vectors is injective. At most $m+1$ insertions and $O((m+1)^2)$ SAT calls suffice. Choosing $B$ sufficiently large bounds the aggregate cost, including all handling of the public basis, by $O(2^J/(J+1)^5)$. The verifier and model are fixed. A constructible floor or dyadic rounding with constant slack gives the same conclusion.

For completeness the syndrome may be constructed over $\mathbb F_{2^t}$, $t=\lceil\log_2(L+1)\rceil$. Choose $L$ different field elements $\alpha_i$ and take the field-valued moments $\sum_i u_i\alpha_i^e$ for $0\le e<2A$, using $\alpha_i^0=1$. Write them in a binary field basis. If two words of weight at most $A$ collide, their nonzero difference has some $s\le 2A$ occupied positions. The first $s$ equations give an invertible square Vandermonde matrix on those distinct field elements, a contradiction. For this small field a uniform implementation can find an irreducible degree-$t$ polynomial by exhaustive polynomial division in $\operatorname{poly}(L+1)$ time. These parity-check and decoding ideas are classical. [Reed and Solomon](https://doi.org/10.1137/0108018)

Enumerate all words of weight at most $A$ and keep those whose syndrome satisfies the recovered basis. This produces the preceding list $S_J$ on budget. By injectivity its $L^{\omega(1)}$ different words give just as many distinct syndromes. All syndromes lie in an affine space of size $2^{m-r_J}$, yielding the claimed codimension. At no point does the detector have the correct list entry as advice.

For a longer window $L=J$ and budgets $2^{\beta J}$ with fixed $0<\beta<1/k$, use only $i\in[\theta J,J]$ for a fixed $k\beta<\theta<1$. The same basis extraction and list filtering cost $2^{k\beta J+o(J)}=o(2^i)$ on the relevant scales. This gives $\Omega(J)$ independent sparse directions and codimension $\omega(\log_2 J)$. More generally equip the length-$J$ syndrome with at least $4At$ field moments for a positive integer $t$ with $4At\le J$. Take sums of up to $t$ of the $g=\Omega(J)$ independent differences. These sums are distinct in the input. Any collision between their syndromes would give a nonzero kernel word of weight at most $4At$, excluded by the Vandermonde argument. All their images annihilate every certified homogeneous check. The uncertified dimension is therefore at least $\log_2\sum_{a=0}^{\min(t,g)}\binom ga$. All setup costs remain polynomial in $J$ apart from sparse-list enumeration and proof search.

The source of the bound is the computation time of the actual sparse word's *parity data*. Ordinary sparse recovery with a supplied exact syndrome already reconstructs it. We have no procedure certifying enough of these actual parity values to violate the bound. The unrestricted SAT lower bound remains open.
