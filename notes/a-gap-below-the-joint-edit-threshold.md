# A gap below the joint edit threshold and a solver when the edits have no common pin

Let $E$ be a proper ternary CNF and change one variable name in each of $r\ge1$ different clause occurrences, keeping the edited clauses proper. Write $F$ for the result, $q=7/8$, $K=(4/7)^4$, and $D(E)=\mu(E)/q^{\tau(E)}$ as in the [one-edit theorem](a-sharp-density-threshold-for-one-physical-sat-edit.md). For edit $i$, let $W_i$ be its four distinct variable names, and let $P_i$ be the assignment to them under which the old clause is true and the new one false. In particular the two shared literals and the new literal are false, and the old replaced literal is true. The measure is uniform on the union of the variable sets. Put $M=Kq^{\tau(E)}$ and $m_i=\Pr[E\land P_i]$.

**Gap.** If $(r-3/49)K<D(E)<rK$, then $F$ is satisfiable. A joint edit at $D(E)=rK$ can still be unsatisfiable, as the [two-edit construction](joint-edits-at-the-sharp-model-density-threshold.md) shows for $r=2$. The gap concerns possible negative outcomes under the promise. A density promise is not supplied to an arbitrary SAT solver.

**Running time.** Suppose the $r$ pin patterns have no common signed pin. For each variable, at least one pattern omits it or at least two patterns assign it different values. For fixed $r$ and $0\le\delta<3/7$, there is a globally correct deterministic solver taking $O_{r,\delta}(N)$ time on $F$ whenever $D(E)\ge(r-\delta)K$. It is given **only $F$**, even when $F$ is unsatisfiable. For $r\ge6$ the same linear-time guarantee holds without any assumption on common pins. At $r=5$ the argument here leaves exactly one common signed pin. A separate [ternary density spectrum theorem](the-first-three-normalized-densities-of-ternary-sat.md) handles that case. At two edits the endpoint $\delta=3/7$ can already encode arbitrary 3-SAT without a common signed pin. The separate five-edit conclusion uses the narrower range stated below.

## Saturation of one edit

Always $m_i\le M$. In fact either $m_i=M$ or $m_i\le(46/49)M$. In the equality case the easy models inside $P_i$ form a product of independently chosen satisfying patterns on vertex-disjoint signed triples and unrestricted values on the remaining unpinned variables.

Let $J$ be the family of full ternaries avoiding $W_i$, so $\tau(J)\ge\tau(E)-4$. Conditional on the four pins, every $J$ clause remains and each other old clause is either satisfied or shortens to at most two literals. If $\tau(J)>\tau(E)-4$, the bound $\mu(J)\le q^{\tau(J)}$ costs a factor at most $q<46/49$ relative to $M$. If $\tau(E)<4$ this case holds automatically. Otherwise suppose $\tau(J)=\tau(E)-4$. If two distinct supports of $J$ intersect, the [intersecting-pair inequality](a-sharp-density-loss-for-intersecting-ternary-clauses.md) gives the factor $46/49$.

For disjoint supports keep one signed clause per block, with a uniform allowed set of seven patterns on that triple. Each other variable is a free singleton. The base measure of these clauses is exactly $q^{\tau(J)}$. Another sign pattern on the same support excludes one of those seven patterns. A nontrivial binary clause on a single triple block fails with probability at least $1/7$. On different blocks its failure probability is at least $(3/7)^2>1/7$, since either value of any single coordinate occurs with probability at least $3/7$. An unsatisfied constant or a unary clause loses at least as much. Consequently every effective additional constraint costs a factor at most $6/7$. If all are ineffective the entire base product is allowed, giving exactly $m_i=M$.

Two saturated model sets with compatible pin patterns have intersection mass at least $M/16$. Indeed every old clause touched by a saturated pin assignment must be satisfied by one of its pins. Otherwise a shortened, falsifiable clause on at most two unpinned variables would exclude positive mass from its base product. Fix both compatible patterns. At most eight variables have now been fixed. The unsatisfied clauses that remain untouched are a subset of the first pattern’s $\tau(E)-4$ disjoint, singly signed blocks. Thus the measure of the joint intersection is at least $2^{-8} q^{\tau(E)-4}=M/16$. Incompatible patterns have disjoint cylinders.

If $F$ is unsatisfiable, all easy models belong to the union of the $E\land P_i$. One unsaturated edit reduces the sum of the masses by at least $3M/49$. If all are saturated and two intersect, the union loses at least $M/16>3M/49$. If they are pairwise disjoint, the union has mass exactly $rM$. Dividing by $q^{\tau(E)}$ proves the gap. For one edit the gap is sharp at the lower endpoint as well: conjoin a disjoint copy of the three-clause, five-variable gadget of density $23/32$ and transversal two to the sharp one-edit construction. This multiplies $D(E)$ by exactly $46/49$ and preserves the hard verdict. [Tight overlap gadget](a-sharp-density-loss-for-intersecting-ternary-clauses.md)

## The case of no common signed pin

First suppose every edit is saturated. Put $W=\bigcup_i W_i$. The clauses of $E$ lying entirely outside $W$ have disjoint distinct supports, since they appear in the untouched triple family of every saturated edit. Consider an old clause with exactly one variable $v$ in $W$, and write it as $\ell(v)\lor L(x)\lor L(y)$. No pattern can pin $\ell$ false: the surviving binary clause on $x,y$ would exclude a positive-mass event in the corresponding base product. Since no signed pin is common to all patterns, some pattern leaves $v$ free. In that pattern this is an untouched ternary, and it is the only such single-$W$ clause incident at $v$, up to identical signed copies. A different sign on the same support would also contradict saturation. Moreover neither $x$ nor $y$ can lie in any all-outside triple, as that would be a distinct intersecting support in the same pattern.

Add one of the two outside vertices from each distinct such single-$W$ clause to $W$, obtaining $Z$. Then $|Z|\le2|W|\le8r$. Every clause of $F$ either avoids $Z$ or contains at least two distinct vertices of $Z$, and the avoiding supports are mutually disjoint. Indeed, none of the outside vertices added to $W$ can touch an all-outside triple, and the replaced clauses use only names in $W$.

A solver can find any such set from $F$ alone. Start with the empty set. If a clause currently has exactly one selected vertex, branch on adding either of its other two vertices. If two distinct avoiding supports intersect, branch on adding any vertex of their union, at most five choices. Each branch rule includes a choice from every valid extension of the current set. Stop at depth $8r$. Check for the two defects by scanning the clauses, grouping duplicate supports and keeping one incident avoiding support per vertex. At a successful node enumerate all assignments of the found variables. The residual has only units, constants, and ternary blocks on disjoint supports. Within each block try its eight patterns, respecting its units and all signed clauses on that support. Check units on the remaining variables for conflict. This decides either verdict. At fixed $r$ the entire search takes $O_r(N)$ time after dense renaming.

If some $m_i$ is unsaturated, the mass of easy models still satisfying all edited clauses is at least $\mu(E)-(r-3/49)M$. Since $\tau(F)\ge\tau(E)-r$, it follows that
$$
D(F)\ \ge\ q^r K (3/49-\delta)>0 .
$$
Run the bounded structural search first. If it finds a set, it already gives a correct decision. If it finds none, run the [globally correct solver for positive normalized density](two-models-from-positive-normalized-density.md). On the stated promise this second case is necessarily within its linear-time guarantee. The all-saturated case allows both verdicts, and the algorithm computes which holds.

## No common signed pin up to the sharp two-edit endpoint

Fix $0\le\delta<3/7$ and put $\eta=(3/7-\delta)/2$. If some $m_i\le(4/7+\eta)M$, the union bound leaves easy-model mass at least $\eta M$ satisfying all new clauses. Then $D(F)\ge q^r K\eta$ and the positive-density solver applies. Assume for the structural argument that every $m_i>(4/7+\eta)M$.

Start with $W$. Among the distinct ternary supports lying entirely outside $W$, choose a maximal packing of intersecting pairs with disjoint unions. Its size $p$ is bounded in terms of $\eta$: for any pin pattern its conditional mass satisfies $m_i/M\le(46/49)^p$ by the untouched-triple argument. Let $A$ consist of $W$ and all vertices of the selected pairs. The ternary supports wholly outside $A$ are mutually disjoint.

Consider each signed literal $\ell$ on $A$ separately. Choose a pin pattern $P_i$ that does not fix $\ell$ true, using the absence of a common signed pin. If it leaves $\ell$ free, the model mass in $P_i$ with $\ell$ true is at most $(4/7)M$ by the five-pin cylinder inequality. If it fixes $\ell$ false, that mass is zero. In either case more than $\eta M$ remains with $\ell$ false.

Every old clause with $\ell$ as its only literal on $A$ leaves a binary clause on outside vertices in this last event. Suppose $k$ of their two-variable supports are disjoint. After the four or five pins, align signs as in the [transversal bound](model-density-and-a-transversal-of-ternary-sat-clauses.md), preserving sizes and weakly increasing the model count. For each selected binary edge expose its first zero; the total surviving branch probability is $3/4$, and each surviving branch uses exactly one zero. The union of the original pins, these $k$ zeros and any transversal of **all** remaining edges (including shortened ones) hits the original ternaries. The residual transversal is thus at least $\tau(E)-j-k$ for $j=4$ or $5$ pins. Applying the rank-at-most-three density bound to the residual gives the upper mass
$$
 2^{-j}(3/4)^k q^{\tau(E)-j-k}
   = M(4/7)^{j-4}(6/7)^k \le M(6/7)^k .
$$
This remains an upper bound if the exponent in the residual bound is negative. Hence $k\le\log(1/\eta)/\log(7/6)$. Select a maximal such matching separately for every signed $\ell$. Add both endpoints of each of its edges. They meet every clause with exactly one vertex in $A$. Finally add all vertices of every formerly all-outside-$A$ ternary that these new endpoints touch. Those ternaries have disjoint distinct supports, so this last enlargement ends in one pass. The resulting set has size bounded by a constant depending only on $r,\delta$. Every edited clause is internal to $W$. The resulting set therefore has precisely the property required by the preceding search on $F$. A valid cap can be computed using $|A|\le4r+5\log(1/(4/7+\eta))/\log(49/46)$ and at most $2|A|$ signed-literal matchings. The same structural search followed by the positive-density solver gives $O_{r,\delta}(N)$ on both verdicts.

For $r=2$ the endpoint $(2-3/7)K$ admits a linear-length reduction of ordinary proper 3-SAT. Begin with the [two-box construction](joint-edits-at-the-sharp-model-density-threshold.md) on $m$ disjoint source occurrence triples, selector $t$ and guard $b$. In the easy file $b=0$ on both boxes. In the joint edited file the hard control pins give $b=1,t=0$. Write $a_0$ for the old truth literal in the first edited clause. Add a disjoint positive triple $y\lor u\lor v$, the clause $b\lor t\lor y$, and hard-only pins forcing $y=0,u=1,v=0$ when $b=1$. These pins can be written in proper ternaries by padding each guarded binary with both signs of $t$. Change the first editable occurrence of $a_0$ into $\neg y$, leaving the second edit as before. In the physical file use a complemented representation of $a_0$ on decimal name 3, and name $y$ by 1, so this first edit is the single physical bit in the tokens `-3` and `-1`. The [ordinary-file audit](../audits/no_common_pin_at_two_edits.py) gives the complete name assignment.

In the first easy box $b=t=0$ now requires $y=1$, retaining half the extra assignments. In the second easy box the positive triple retains $7/8$. The old easy boxes had mass $2^{-4}q^m$ apiece. The original clauses have the same transversal as before, the new disjoint triple adds one, and the old transversal with $y$ meets every new clause. Thus $\tau(E)=m+5$ and
$$
 D(E)=\frac{2^{-4}q^m(1/2+q)}{q^{m+5}}
      =(1+4/7)K=(2-3/7)K.
$$
After both edits, the active first row at $t=0$ requires $y=0$, thereby forcing $b=1$ through $b\lor t\lor y$. At $t=1$ the active second edit requires $b=1$ as in the original construction. Applying just one edit still leaves the other easy box. The joint hard file keeps the original source-model projection, with the three new variables uniquely pinned. Its two loss patterns have opposite values of $t$; the first uses $y$ in place of $b$, leaving no common signed pin. This attains the endpoint on either source verdict.

## Six changed clauses suffice to dispense with the pin hypothesis

If there are $j$ common signed pins, the entire lost union lies in their one subcube. Its easy-model mass is at most $(4/7)^j q^{\tau(E)}$ by the cylinder inequality. Thus $D(F)\ge q^r((r-\delta)K-(4/7)^j)$ whenever the difference is positive. For $r\ge6$, $j\ge1$ and fixed $\delta<3/7$ this lower bound is separated from zero, since $(6-3/7)K>4/7$. The positive-density solver applies. For $j=0$ the structural argument already proves a linear time bound. The solver need not know which case holds. For $r=5$ in the narrower range $\delta<3/49$, $(5-3/49)K>(4/7)^2$ handles $j\ge2$ in the same manner. The remaining five-edit case has exactly one common signed pin and is addressed separately by the [first three normalized densities](the-first-three-normalized-densities-of-ternary-sat.md). The normalization always satisfies $D(E)\le1$, so threshold regimes with $(r-\delta)K>1$ are vacuous.

The [finite audit](../audits/joint_edit_density_gap.py) checks the discrete mass factors and constructs canonical proper files with two differently named edits whose signed pins have empty intersection. It tests both target verdicts in the all-saturated case and performs the structural search using only the edited clause list. The all-length claims and running times are mathematical proofs.
