# Computing the missing SAT label when a RAM reports three signatures

Suppose a fixed deterministic RAM computes a signature $S(X)$ for every legal proper DIMACS file of encoded length $n$ in time $t(n)\ge n$. At each length there are at most three realized signatures and SAT is constant on each. Both the realized signatures and their meanings may vary with $n$. There is a deterministic algorithm which computes a correct decoding rule from $n$ alone, using time
$$
 O\!\left(H\log^3(H+2)+t\bigl(O(H\log^3(H+2))\bigr)\right),\qquad H=O(t(n)+n).
$$
Here $t$ is a known computable monotone clock, with its setup charged within the displayed bound, as for the standard polynomial clocks. A correct rule then answers every query at that length in $O(t(n)+n)$ steps. For $t(n)=O(n^c)$, fixed $c\ge1$, the uniform single-query bound is $O(n^{c^2}\log^{3c}(n+2))$. In particular a linear-time three-signature computation yields deterministic SAT in $O(n\log^3(n+2))$. It takes two uses of the signature time bound at different file lengths; arbitrary constant-bit advice can give more than three signatures.

## Two tests for an entire length

At an admissible byte length $n/8$ pad a fixed proper satisfiable anchor and a fixed proper unsatisfiable anchor to that length with legal body spaces. One clause and the eight clauses excluding all valuations on a triple suffice. Use an ordinary exhaustive algorithm for the finitely many shorter lengths. Evaluate $S$ on both anchors. Their signatures differ. Normalize the observed alphabet by comparison with those two signatures. Call their classes NO and YES and every other signature U. Equality comparisons are charged in the time bound; usual fixed finite output alphabets have constant comparison cost.

Define clocks $B_0,B_1$ that decide a file from its signature, using the calibrated labels and returning $0,1$ respectively on U. At least one is correct on the whole length. If U occurs, it has a single true label, making exactly one of the clocks incorrect. If U is absent, both are correct. Each $B_j$ is a fixed program: it can regenerate and evaluate the anchors from the input length in $O(t(n)+n)$ time, including at every use inside a verifier.

Construct $G_j(n)$ expressing that $B_j$ has a local defect at length $n$. The witness is an arbitrary legal proper $n$-bit file $Z$. The verifier runs the same-length self-reduction on $Z$ and tests the row on the root and its constant number of children, or checks the directly decidable base. Absence of a defect is equivalent to correctness on the entire length, by induction on the number of remaining variable names. Thus $G_j(n)$ is satisfiable exactly when $B_j$ is incorrect. Both queries depend only on the length and the specified signature program. Their construction never tries to guess the truth of a candidate counterexample. [Exact-length self-reduction and defect verifier](same-length-local-defects-for-ordinary-sat.md)

For clarity, the bounded computation to compile is deterministic once the $n$ witness bits are supplied. Give it a public cap $H$ large enough for the verification just described, its input initialization and its constant number of signature computations. Use the fixed logarithmic-word RAM basis, with a fixed host word coefficient large enough to simulate the candidate. Take the witness input wires as free Boolean variables and pin the public length and constants. Sorting the $O(H)$ memory events uses $O(H\log H)$ word comparators, hence $O(H\log^2 H)$ Boolean gates, including the ordinary word arithmetic. Gate equivalences, the enforced acceptance bit, and proper-clause padding use $O(1)$ clauses per gate. Printed decimal variable names cost the final logarithm. The journal's local sorting construction gives both $O(H\log^3(H+2))$ physical output bits and deterministic generation within that many RAM steps. Existential acceptance suffices here. The classical quasilinear completeness reduction underlies this step; the logarithm count quoted here uses the journal's specified ASCII layout and machine basis. [Local RAM-to-DIMACS construction](local-sorting-for-parsimonious-ram-encodings.md) · [Fortnow, Lipton, van Melkebeek and Viglas](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf)

Pad $G_0,G_1$ and the two known anchors to one common larger encoded length $L=O(H\log^3(H+2))$. On these four ordinary files evaluate $S$; this uses the calibration at $L$, independently of its meaning at $n$. For two files with opposite truth values, at least one has a calibrated signature: otherwise they both have the unique residual signature and must agree. If the first has a calibrated signature use its known bit; otherwise negate the known bit of the second. Extend this deterministic rule arbitrarily to pairs where no bit can be inferred. If U occurs at the source length, the pair of defects has opposite labels, and the rule identifies the unique incorrect $B_j$. Use the other. If U is absent, either choice answers all files correctly. This proves the uniform preprocessing and single-file bounds. For $t(n)=O(n\log^a(n+2))$, $a\ge0$ fixed, the same calculation gives $O(n\log^{2a+3}(n+2))$.

The result can also be read as a necessary collision condition. If SAT lacks deterministic time $O(n^{c^2}\log^{3c}(n+2))$, every $O(n^c)$ three-signature program has an opposite-verdict collision at arbitrarily large individual lengths. A finite set of bad lengths can always be handled by a fixed table or bounded brute force. This statement supplies no unconditional exclusion at these clocks. In particular the logarithms still matter to the exact linear milestone.

## What exact-clock advice extraction would separate

Let $D_j$ consist of the unary strings $1^n$ for which $B_j$ has a defect at physical length $n$. Outside the calibrated lengths take both languages empty. These are disjoint unary languages with $O(t(n)+n)$ deterministic verification time and at most $n$ witness bits. A deterministic $O(t(n))$ separator containing $D_0$ and avoiding $D_1$ gives an exact-clock uniform SAT solver: choose $B_1$ when the separator accepts, and $B_0$ otherwise. When neither has a defect either choice works. An example of a three-signature computation that answers SAT up to length-dependent decoding in $O(t(n))$ but admits no uniform SAT algorithm at that clock would therefore yield such a unary pair without a separator at the clock.

There is a converse for *producing a decoding rule in advance*, conditional on an ordinary uniform $O(t)$ SAT baseline. Assume $t$ is nondecreasing, $t(n)\ge n$, and $t(Cn+C)=O_C(t(n))$ for every constant $C$. Take any disjoint unary pair $A_0,A_1$ whose two fixed verifiers on $1^m$ run in $O(t(m))$ time with at most $d m$ guessed bits, for a fixed positive integer $d$. Supply exactly $dm$ bits, ignoring unused choices. Prepare two fixed proper DIMACS templates on variables 7,8,9, one with the eight contradictory sign clauses and the other with eight copies of a satisfiable clause. Equalize their byte lengths to a constant $c$ using fixed interior spaces. They differ from the calibration anchors on variables 1,2,3. Insert a run of exactly $dm$ spaces or tabs between the final zero and final newline, encoding one guessed bit per **byte**. The tested physical length is exactly $n(m)=8(c+dm)$ bits. A signature program recognizes the exact two templates and the injection $m\mapsto n(m)$, then checks the appropriate verifier on the payload. Report U when the negative template contains an $A_0$ witness or the positive template an $A_1$ witness. Otherwise return the baseline SAT bit. Parsing and reconstructing the unary input cost $O(n(m))$. The output is pure by disjointness, and the program runs in $O(t(n))$ on every file.

Any eager $O(t(n))$ generator of a length's full three-color decoding table for this signature would separate $A_0,A_1$: read its value at U, which must be 0 on $A_0$ lengths and 1 on $A_1$ lengths. The direction of the separator can be reversed. If U is absent its table entry can be arbitrary. Computing at $n(m)$ costs $O(t(m))$ by the stated time regularity. This converse concerns the decoding table for a specified signature, even in the presence of an independent fast SAT baseline. It supplies no lower bound for a solver free to choose its own method.

## More signatures with nested possible decoders

At an upper query length, suppose a fixed finite list of possible decoding tables is uniformly enumerable and includes a truthful table. After discarding tables incompatible with a fixed finite list of known labelled anchors, suppose the surviving YES sets are linearly ordered by inclusion on the reports actually attained at that length. An example is a score in a fixed finite ordered range with an unknown length-dependent threshold. Given two reported values $p,q$, we must be able to compute the predictions of each surviving table there; its evaluation, the anchor tests and the enumeration are included in the clock. No search over unobserved inputs to determine the inclusion order is required.

If the underlying files have opposite verdicts, at least one candidate table assigns opposite bits. All candidate tables doing so give the same orientation: two opposite orientations would give one point in each of the two YES-set differences. Enumerate the predicted pairs, choose any that is opposite, and use a fixed default if none is. In the opposite-verdict case this is correct. Conversely, in a fixed calibrated decoder family, two incomparable YES sets give a pair of reported values whose opposite orientation cannot be resolved from those two reports alone. Whether the particular input generator reaches that pair is an additional question.

The upper-length condition suffices to select a correct member of an arbitrary fixed finite source menu with a truthful row. Split the remaining nonempty menu into two nonempty halves. For each half construct a satisfiability query asking whether **every** member has a local defect on the source length. Guess one original-length file per member and check its local rule; the number of rows is fixed. Pad both aggregate tests to a common upper length. If just one half has a correct member, exactly one aggregate test is UNSAT. Use the upper-length decoder comparison to keep that half. If both contain correct members, both tests are UNSAT and either half can be kept. The invariant that the retained menu contains a truthful row ends at a singleton. A menu of size $K$ takes at most $\lceil\log_2 K\rceil$ comparisons. The $c^2$ exponent and the earlier logarithmic estimates apply for fixed source and upper menus under the same computation-cost assumptions. Only the upper-length comparisons require nested YES sets; the source candidates may be incomparable.

## Why four possible reports change this particular comparison

Give an experiment every Boolean predicate $q$ of one hidden bit $b$, including negation and the two constants. A reporter with four colors can return $(q(0),q(1))$. The complete list of observations is independent of $b$, while projection to the first or second coordinate is a valid decoder in the respective world. This also covers adaptive predicate choice, since both worlds give the same replies to every query. Thus adding any further truth functions of just one unknown verdict cannot force identification at four colors on the strength of label consistency alone. This is a query-interface example; the two coordinates need not be efficiently computable from arbitrary SAT files. Their syntax and verified predicates about the actual program are additional sources of information.

The counting extends to several bits. Suppose a list of at most $r$ reports is observed for every Boolean predicate on a finite secret set $U$. A secret $u$ is compatible if some decoder of the reports evaluates every observed predicate correctly at $u$. If $k$ distinct secrets are compatible, choose a compatible decoder for each. For each of the $2^k$ patterns on the chosen secrets there exists a predicate with that pattern. Its report must yield that precise vector across the chosen decoders, so $2^k\le r$. Reporting the vector of values at $k$ candidate secrets attains the bound when $r=2^k$. This is the elementary finite counting behind bounded enumeration of membership vectors. The known Karp–Lipton argument removes logarithmic SAT advice in polynomial time; the calculation here tracks the cost of the complement test for three signatures. Priority for this particular time estimate is unestablished. [Karp and Lipton](https://doi.org/10.1145/800141.804678) · [Amir, Beigel and Gasarch on bounded queries and nonuniform complexity](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/papers/nonuniform.pdf)
