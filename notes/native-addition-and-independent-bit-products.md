# How many independent bit products fit in one word addition?

Fix a word width $w\ge1$. Two $w$-bit operands may be arbitrary affine functions over $\mathbb F_2$ of all the raw input bits. Add them modulo $2^w$. The program may return any affine combination of the sum bits and the original input bits. The greatest $m$ for which it can compute $\sum_{j=1}^m x_jy_j$ on $2m$ independent input bits is **exactly $\lfloor w/2\rfloor$**. The upper bound allows every choice of affine packing into the words. For comparison, one bitwise AND with affine packing and readout handles exactly $w$ independent products. The question arose in attempting to extend the affine triangle-query induction to ordinary RAM instructions. [Earlier induction and the dimension bound for inner product](affine-batches-of-butterfly-queries-and-triangle-parity.md)

Schulte-Geers previously gave a linear change of coordinates for the full graph of modular addition. The classification for observing the whole output below follows from his theorem. The scalar proof uses the additional freedom to cancel the contribution of an unobserved carry. [Schulte-Geers, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-012-9668-4)

## Eliminating two carries with one equation

Use addition in $\mathbb F_2$ in the bit formulas. With input bits $a_i,b_i$ in increasing order of significance, write $p_i=a_i+b_i$. The output bit is $p_i+c_i$, where $c_0=0$ and
$$
                 c_{i+1}=(1+p_i)a_i+p_i c_i.
$$
On any nonempty affine flat of raw inputs, the observed scalar is an affine function plus a fixed linear combination of the carries $c_1,\ldots,c_{w-1}$. Take the highest carry with nonzero coefficient, say $c_{i+1}$. Write $\beta$ for the coefficient of $c_i$, taking $\beta=0$ if $i=0$. If the subflat $p_i=\beta$ is nonempty, restrict to it. On this subflat
$$
              c_{i+1}+\beta c_i=(1+\beta)a_i .
$$
This removes two successive carry levels with at most one independent equation. If the requested subflat is empty, $p_i$ is already constant with the other value. Substitute that value. This removes the top level at zero cost. A top level with zero coefficient also drops for free. Induction on the number $h$ of levels still present gives codimension at most $\lceil h/2\rceil$, including the last level $c_1$. Taking $h=w-1$ proves that the scalar observable is affine on some nonempty subflat of codimension at most $\lfloor w/2\rfloor$. The choice of subflat can depend on the observable.

On $2m$ independent bits the polar form of inner product is nondegenerate. Any flat on which inner product is affine therefore has codimension at least $m$. For attainment put the $j$th input pair in bit $2j$ of the two operands, starting at $j=0$, and put zeros in all odd lanes. Bit $2j+1$ of the sum equals the product of that pair and the carry into the following even lane is zero. XOR these odd sum bits. For AND, fixing one operand's $w$ affine bits suffices to make every output affine; placing the pairs directly in distinct lanes attains the matching bound. The same proof composes for a sequence of additions in which each instruction exposes just one affine bit of its sum. Allow the next operands and the final answer to depend affinely on the raw input and on all previously exposed bits. After each addition, the preceding observations are still affine on the chosen subflat. At width $w\ge2$, exactly $\lceil m/\lfloor w/2\rfloor\rceil$ scalar observations suffice and are necessary in this straight-line model. This also applies to one parallel layer of full-word additions followed by any affine scalar output, since each sum then contributes just one scalar observable. Branching freely on an observation would require fixing its value on the flat. Reusing several bits from an intermediate sum in a later native instruction also changes the model.

## When several bits of the same sum survive

For $k\ge1$ simultaneous affine scalar observations, the carry coefficients are vectors in $\mathbb F_2^k$. Put $D=2^k$ and $h=w-1$. All $k$ outputs are affine together on some nonempty subflat of codimension at most
$$
                         h-\lfloor h/D\rfloor .
$$
Process the levels in descending order in disjoint blocks of $D$. Include in the top coefficient of each block the contribution arriving from earlier reductions. Among the $D+1$ partial sums of the $D$ coefficient vectors, two agree. The intervening nonempty consecutive interval has sum zero. Set the propagate bit immediately above this interval to zero if that level is in the block, and set the propagate bits within the interval to one down to, but excluding, its bottom level. Carry substitution then gives coefficient zero at the bottom level, which can be discarded at zero cost. Levels outside this prescription can be eliminated by fixing any attainable value of their propagate bit. If a prescribed choice is impossible, that bit was already constant on the current flat. Its substitution is free instead, and the remainder of the block can use arbitrary attainable values. Thus every full block saves at least one equation. This is an upper bound on the cost of making the outputs affine; optimality for intermediate $k$ remains open here.

This bound also applies gate by gate to a straight-line word program with arbitrary affine wiring between additions. If the output of addition $j$ is used only through $k_j$ linearly independent combinations of its bits in the next operands and final readout, it suffices to make those combinations affine. Processing the additions in execution order gives
$$
                 m\le \sum_j \bigl(h-\lfloor h/2^{k_j}\rfloor\bigr)
$$
for any such program computing inner product on $m$ independent pairs, with unused gates contributing zero. Here uses include every direct affine dependency of subsequent nonlinear gates on that word. One cannot count only the bits ultimately returned by the whole program. The exact cost for a single scalar observation therefore applies even with reuse of that bit. A full intermediate word can transmit additional independent combinations.

## When every bit of the sum survives

The maximal affine operand flats on which the entire word sum is affine have **dimension $w+1$** in $2w$ input dimensions. There are **$6^{w-1}$** of them. To see this, write
$$
             c_{i+1}=(a_i+c_i)(b_i+c_i)+c_i .
$$
A product of two affine bits is affine on a nonempty flat precisely when one of the bits or their XOR is constant. If their two linear parts were independent, their joint image would be the whole binary plane, where the product has nonzero second difference. Since the sum and its two input words are affine on the given flat, all its carries are affine there. At each $i<w-1$ choose one of $a_i+c_i,b_i+c_i,a_i+b_i$ and its constant value. These are six possible equations. Given the previously chosen equations, the incoming carry is affine on the entire larger flat they define. Each new equation involves a fresh bit at this level. It leaves two choices of the operand pair for every previous assignment and makes the outgoing carry affine. The two top operand bits are free. The resulting extension has dimension $w+1$ and contains the given flat.

Two prescriptions that first differ at level $i$ give distinct extensions. For any shared lower prefix they select different affine lines of the $(a_i,b_i)$ plane, and every point of each line extends through the remaining levels. This proves the count and maximality. Each input belongs to $3^{w-1}$ maximal flats, since at each level its bits fix the value for each of the three equation types. Prescribing only the equation type at every level partitions the whole space into $2^{w-1}$ flats by the resulting constants. A Lean proof verifies the coverage by triangular flats, their affine parametrizations, the bijection with their free bits and the distinctness of their prescriptions. [Boolean word proof](../lean/CarryFlatAddition.lean)

The exact count is also a corollary of Schulte-Geers's linear equivalence between the graph of addition and the graph of shifted partial sums of bitwise products. Taking successive differences gives $w-1$ separate one-bit product graphs; each has four points, six two-point lines and no plane. [Schulte-Geers, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10623-012-9668-4)

With affine operand words on any already restricted input domain, one may choose an attainable constant for $p_i$ successively at cost at most $w-1$ total. With independent operands this uniform bound for **all** sum bits is sharp by the dimension calculation. A useful check on near-agreement also holds. If an affine word prediction agrees with the sum on more than $3/4$ of a finite flat, it agrees everywhere there. The proposed carries, obtained by XORing the predicted sum with both operands, are affine. The initial-carry test and all carry recurrences have degree at most two. Any nonzero multilinear polynomial of degree at most two on a binary cube takes value one on at least one quarter of its points, as seen by fixing other variables around a top-degree monomial. The threshold is sharp on two low input bits with all higher operands zero. The affine XOR prediction misses just the $(1,1)$ case for $w\ge2$.

## The first case with full intermediate reuse

Two full **three-bit** additions, with arbitrary affine wiring from all earlier words and raw input bits, can compute two independent products in parity and cannot compute three. This includes feeding both carries of the first addition to the second. The finite argument below treats one putative circuit on six independent bits; setting extra pairs to zero excludes larger inner products as well.

Write $z=(a,b,x,y)$ for the four low input bits of the first sum. Its new signals are $g=ab$ and $h=xy+(x+y)g$. If $z$ has affine rank at most two, restrict its image to a line or a point at a cost of at most one equation. Both carries then become affine. If its rank is three, its image is a hyperplane $A(a,b)+D(x,y)=e$ with linear $A,D$. When $D=0$, the low pair already ranges over a line and one more equation makes $x+y$ constant. When $D=x+y$, restrict the low pair to a line on which $A$ is constant. In the other cases $D$ is a single bit $x$ or $y$. Among the four low pairs choose two with equal value of $A+g$. Their line makes both $g$ and $D+g$ affine with the latter constant after using the hyperplane equation. In each case both carries are affine on an inhabited slice at one equation's cost. The scalar bound for the second addition costs at most one more. Inner product on three independent pairs requires three.

Now let $z$ have rank four. Complete it to affine coordinates $(z,s,t)$ on the six input bits. Every available wire before the second addition belongs to the span $S$ of the affine functions, $g$ and $h$. Each member of $S$ has constant slopes in the two fresh directions $s,t$. By the carry recurrence, every scalar output of the second addition including direct affine terms has the shape
$$
                         F=L+p(d+uv),\qquad L,p,d,u,v\in S.
$$
For example the upper carry gives $xy+(x+y)uv$, and selecting the lower carry as well adds $uv$. Since $xy=x(1+x+y)$ for bits, all the other linear terms can be absorbed in $L,d$. An output using only the lower carry has this shape with $p=1$.

Suppose $F$ is quadratic with polar rank six. First suppose the fresh slopes of $p,u,v$ span at most one dimension. A nonzero fresh direction $e$ kills all three. The Boolean difference $D_e F$ is a constant plus $p D_e d$. It must be a nonconstant affine function by nondegeneracy. Thus $p$ is nonconstant affine. On the hyperplane $p=0$, $F=L$. The latter is a scalar observable of the first addition and a direct affine term. Its scalar bound costs at most one further equation on this hyperplane. The resulting codimension at most two contradicts polar rank six.

If the fresh slopes span two dimensions, $D_sD_t F$ is constant. For $f\in S$ write $f'$ for its two slopes and $f_0$ for its restriction to $s=t=0$. In $D_sD_t(puv)$, the part depending on $z$ is
$$
     \det(u',v')p_0+\det(p',v')u_0+\det(p',u')v_0 .
$$
This follows by multiplying the three expressions $f_0+s f'_1+t f'_2$ in the Boolean ring. The remaining terms and $D_sD_t(L+pd)$ are constant. The three determinants therefore give a nontrivial constant linear combination of $p,u,v$, since that combination's fresh slopes cancel as well. If $p$ is constant, $F$ is a member of $S$ plus at most one product of members of $S$. Otherwise on $p=1$ the relation makes $u$, $v$ or $u+v$ constant, so $uv$ there equals an affine combination of $1,u,v$. Again $F=L'+UV$ with $L',U,V\in S$.

The fresh slopes of $U,V$ must be independent. Otherwise a nonzero fresh direction kills both and has constant derivative of $F$. The two derivatives $D_s F,D_t F$ are affine and, up to constants, are two independent linear combinations of $U,V$. Therefore $U,V$ themselves are globally affine. The quadratic $L'=F+UV$ has polar rank at most two because it is a scalar from the first addition: it is affine on a subflat of codimension at most one. The polar form of $UV$ has rank at most two. Their sum has rank at most four, the final contradiction. The matching two-pair construction uses one addition per pair. Extending the result to three full additions would require controlling interactions where fewer than two fresh input directions survive the earlier additions.

The independent audit enumerates all 13,888 homogeneous rank-six quadratics on six bits. For each of the 512 possibilities for $p$, it compares the tables of all $puv$ modulo the linear span $S+pS$ with those quadratics modulo the same span. This includes every affine offset. There are no matches. The separate dependent-case audit checks all 30 hyperplanes of the four low operand bits. The two-addition proof is a manuscript proof with these finite checks. ## Consequence for the SAT time question

On a concrete family of proper ASCII DIMACS files, the scalar-addition count is an exact count for the SAT verdict. The existing packed construction has verdict $\sum_{j=1}^m x_jy_jz_j$ and encoded length $N=\Theta(m)$. Fix every $z_j=1$. Each file bit is affine in the remaining independent bits and the encoding is injective, so its image has an affine left inverse returning those bits. Free affine preprocessing on the physical input therefore gives exactly the same optimum $\lceil m/\lfloor w/2\rfloor\rceil$ on this promise for $w\ge2$. Both the restriction on observing intermediate sums and the grant of affine preprocessing are material to this count. [Packed proper SAT encoding and the matching word-RAM algorithm](spanning-trees-for-a-packed-sat-verdict.md) On the binary inner-product slice, a RAM can also use packed ANDs, XORs, and a final parity fold, and evaluate all $m$ products in $O(\lceil m/w\rceil+\log w)$ instructions. Increasing $m$ by one can therefore leave the number of arithmetic instructions unchanged. An unrestricted SAT lower bound still needs inputs forcing repeated work after several independent products have been processed together. The finite audit enumerates all 26,387 nonempty affine operand flats at width three and tests the scalar elimination against every output mask. Priority for the scalar extremal formulation is unestablished.
