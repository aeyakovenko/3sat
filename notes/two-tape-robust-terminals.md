# All-to-all reachability after faults on two planar tapes

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** One fixed deterministic machine with two single-head writable planar tapes has arbitrarily long input-driven trajectories with the following property. Among its $T$ step vertices are two designated sets $I,O$ of the same size $M=\Theta(T)$. For every deletion of $z\le\gamma T$ step vertices, there are surviving subsets $I'\subseteq I$ and $O'\subseteq O$ with
$$
 |I'|,|O'|\ \ge\ M-132z .
$$
Every vertex of $I'$ reaches every vertex of $O'$ by a directed path of length $\Omega(T^{1/4})$. The constant $\gamma>0$ is independent of the trajectory length. The graph has the chronological edges and edges between consecutive visits to the same work cell. Each head moves to a neighboring cell or stays. The third, read-only tape supplies a sequential stream of moves. The designated input and output vertices are hub times in the step graph. [Two-plane construction and graph conventions](two-plane-directed-grates.md)

## Choosing the outer graph and the lane walk

Blocki and Cinkoske's Corollary 17, with their Definitions 1 and 2 and $\epsilon=1/2$, gives an outer DAG with $s$ inputs, $s$ outputs, $O(s)$ vertices and bounded indegree. After $k\le c s$ vertex deletions, some $s-k$ inputs and $s-k$ outputs have a surviving path of length at least $\lfloor\sqrt{s}\rfloor$ between **each** chosen input and **each** chosen output. The depth guarantee concerns those individual pairs. The corollary is an existence result, which suffices for the movement streams. [Blocki and Cinkoske, *A New Connection Between Node and Edge Depth Robust Graphs*, Definitions 1–2 and Corollary 17](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol185-itcs2021/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.64/LIPIcs.ITCS.2021.64.pdf)

For each vertex with large outdegree replace its fanout by a binary tree oriented away from the original vertex. Each formerly outgoing edge leaves its assigned leaf. The root and its tree belong to that original vertex. The new graph has both degrees bounded by a fixed $\Delta$ and still has $O(s)$ vertices, since the original has $O(s)$ edges. Deletions in $k$ replacement vertices affect at most $k$ original owners. Every original path avoiding those owners lifts through intact trees, preserving or increasing its length. Keep the original roots as terminals.

Set $q=\lceil\sqrt{s}\rceil$ and $m=q^2$. Use $m$ lanes at every outer vertex, and use the same symmetric doubly stochastic matrix $P$ between lanes on every directed edge. Take a fixed power of the lazy Margulis–Gabber–Galil walk on $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^2$ so that its operator norm on mean-zero vectors is at most $\lambda=\min(1/128,1/(16\sqrt\Delta))$. Its steps are a fixed number of affine permutation labels counted with multiplicity. The two-plane layout realizes this graph in $T\le Csm$ steps, with distinct hub times for every lane of every outer vertex. Deleting $z$ steps marks at most $2z$ distinct outer-vertex/lane pairs, counting both heads also on incidental visits. Every edge between unmarked lanes lifts to a surviving directed path between their hubs. Choose the $M=sm$ hubs at the outer input roots and the same number at the output roots. [Gabber and Galil](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4) · [Layout and fault charging](two-plane-directed-grates.md)

## Choosing lanes simultaneously for every terminal pair

Put $\delta=1/64$. Discard any outer vertex with more than $\delta m$ marked lanes, and discard the other replacement vertices of the same original owner. This affects at most $k\le 2z/(\delta m)$ original owners. Taking $\gamma\le\min(c\delta/(2C),1/(264C))$ ensures $k\le cs$ and $132z\le M/2$. Choose the promised outer terminal sets and their long connecting paths on the remaining graph. For each remaining vertex $v$, let $d_v$ count its marked lanes.

In reverse topological order define $F_v$ to contain every unmarked lane that sends at least $3/4$ of its transition probability into $F_w$ for **every** remaining edge $v\to w$. At a sink this retains all unmarked lanes. Write $x_v=m-|F_v|$ and $\kappa=\lambda^2/(1/4-2\delta)^2$. For any excluded subset $E$ of a neighbor's lanes of density $u\le2\delta$, the mean-square deviation of $P1_E$ from $u$ is at most $\lambda^2u(1-u)$. Thus at most $\kappa |E|$ lanes send more than $1/4$ of their probability into $E$. Union over the outneighbors gives
$$
 x_v\le d_v+\kappa\sum_{v\to w}x_w .
$$
Since $\kappa\Delta<1/2$ and $d_v\le\delta m$, reverse induction proves $x_v\le2\delta m$, justifying the density hypothesis. Summing the inequality and using the **indegree** bound gives $\sum_v x_v\le2\sum_v d_v\le4z$. Forward topological order and the transpose walk give sets $B_v$ with the same total loss and the analogous $3/4$ guarantee toward every predecessor. These choices depend only on the deletion set.

Take any surviving outer path and a nonempty set $S\subseteq F_v$ of reached lanes on it. Let $Y$ be the neighbors reached in $F_w$ at the next vertex. Spectral mixing, with the ordinary unnormalized inner product, gives
$$
 3|S|/4\le\langle 1_S,P1_Y\rangle
 \le |S||Y|/m+\lambda\sqrt{|S||Y|}.
$$
Consequently $|Y|\ge\min(m/4,2|S|)$, since violating both bounds would make the right side less than $(1/4+\lambda\sqrt2)|S|$. After $r=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ transitions a single starting lane in $F$ therefore reaches at least $m/4$ lanes, and this remains true along further transitions. The same estimate works from a target lane in $B$ going backward. On a path of at least $2r+1$ edges choose a middle edge with at least $r$ steps on either side. For the two frontiers $S,Y$ there, mixing gives
$$
 \langle 1_S,P1_Y\rangle\ge
 \sqrt{|S||Y|}\big(\sqrt{|S||Y|}/m-\lambda\big)>0 .
$$
Its crossing edge joins the two surviving paths. The long outer path can be chosen separately for each pair while the endpoint sets $F$ and $B$ stay fixed.

For all large $s$, the outer guarantee $\lfloor\sqrt s\rfloor$ exceeds $2r+1$. Keep the forward lanes at each selected input and the backward lanes at each selected output. Each side loses at most $km+4z\le132z$ hubs. Along the chosen outer path the lift visits a distinct hub at every layer in order, giving at least $\lfloor\sqrt s\rfloor=\Omega(T^{1/4})$ native edges. More generally the same proof with fixed $0<\epsilon<1$ in Corollary 17 gives path length $\Omega(T^{(1-\epsilon)/2})$, with constants allowed to depend on $\epsilon$.

Klawe previously used expander replacement and pruning along a chosen path of surviving sets. The additional estimate here bounds the total number of lanes pruned simultaneously over all neighboring vertices, allowing a single pair of terminal sets to work for all paths. PPST's grate question concerns robust directed reachability. The fixed controller can execute these trajectories while ignoring the contents of both writable tapes, so this structural result leaves the unrestricted RAM SAT lower bound open. Priority for this planar-tape realization remains unverified. [Klawe, *Shallow Grates*, §3](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/tr/1992/TR-92-03.pdf) · [Paul, Pippenger, Szemerédi and Trotter, §5](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)

## What the surviving paths can transmit

The same movement schedule supports a finite-alphabet experiment with genuine Boolean influence. Supply one independent injection bit at each designated input time and zero elsewhere. At every step write the OR of the two scanned bits and the current injection into both scanned cells. Take the written bit at a designated output time as an observation. Initially both planes contain zeros. A fault at a deleted step forces its two writes to zero. With all injections zero, every output is zero. When only one retained input injects a one, that one reaches every retained output: along a consecutive-visit edge on a plane, the next visit reads precisely the bit left by the earlier visit. Each transfer meeting can copy that signal across planes. The lifting proof uses only these consecutive-visit edges. Thus after the same faults each retained input can individually change every retained output in the same fixed OR computation. On a fault-free schedule the output vector simply repeats the OR of all designated inputs. Its easy computation shows why this influence property alone cannot establish a decision-time lower bound. The native tape reads, OR updates, propagation along surviving paths, and zero baseline are checked in Lean. [OR semantics in Lean](../lean/TwoTapeGratePaths.lean)

There is also a disjoint-path consequence for transmitting independent bits. Fix $k$ designated inputs and $k$ designated outputs. Set $r=\min(\lfloor k/132\rfloor,\lfloor\gamma T\rfloor)$. After $z<r$ faults, every further vertex cut separating these two subsets has size at least $r-z$. Otherwise the combined deletion set has size $j<r$, so $132j<k$. The robustness theorem leaves at least one of these inputs and one of these outputs in its connected terminal sets. The proof constructs their connection using only consecutive-visit edges. The vertex form of Menger's theorem therefore supplies at least $r-z$ vertex-disjoint directed paths in that subgraph, allowing the cut to include endpoints. For a given set of paths, constant-size routing flags on the instruction tape can forward the chosen input bits along their respective paths. The pairing and flags may depend on the faults and chosen terminals. This does not assert that a single routing carries arbitrary prescribed pairings.
