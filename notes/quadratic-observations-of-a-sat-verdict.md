# When quadratic observations of a SAT instance must encode every input bit

There is a fixed-layout family of ordinary 3-CNFs indexed by all $d=3m$ bits of a label. Its encoded length is $\Theta(m\log(m+2))$ using literal signs that are linear functions of the label. Consider storing any affine observations of the physical string together with $h$ arbitrary nonadaptive quadratic Boolean observations, followed by any decoder. **For $h<m$, every exact summary is injective on all $2^{3m}$ labels.** At $h=m$ there is a summary using only $2m$ bits. Even a lossy observation map used for prediction incurs a quantitative error below. The unrestricted solver can perform successive field multiplications on this family.

## Collision flats and the trace product

Let $K=\mathbb F_{2^m}$ and $F(x)=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_2}(uvw)$ on $x=(u,v,w)\in K^3$. The derivative $D_z F(x)=F(x+z)+F(x)$ is quadratic for $z\ne0$. Its polar bilinear form on directions $a,b$ is $\operatorname{Tr}(\det(z,a,b))$, by polarization of the three factors. It has binary rank exactly $2m$. Its radical contains $Kz$. For $a\notin Kz$, the $K$-linear form $b\mapsto\det(z,a,b)$ is nonzero and surjective onto $K$, where the trace takes both binary values. Thus the radical equals $Kz$. The trace product and its derivatives occur in earlier work on Boolean nonlinearity. The observation bound below uses the shape of collisions of a quadratic map; priority for this consequence has not been established. [Carlet and Feukoua, *Three basic questions on Boolean functions*](https://doi.org/10.3934/amc.2017061)

Write the proposed summary as $E(x)=(Lx,Q_1(x),\ldots,Q_h(x))$, allowing an affine offset in $L$. For a fixed $z\ne0$ the set $C_z=\{x:E(x)=E(x+z)\}$ is either empty or an affine flat of codimension at most $h$. Indeed the linear part of $L$ must kill $z$, after which the only conditions are the affine equations $D_zQ_i(x)=0$. Exact decoding of $F$ forces $D_z F=0$ on $C_z$. A quadratic form of polar rank $2m$ restricted to a flat of codimension $c$ has polar rank at least $2m-2c$: write its polar matrix in a basis extending the direction space and delete $c$ rows and columns. This is positive for $c<m$. An identically zero restriction would have zero polar form, so $C_z$ must be empty for all nonzero $z$. In particular $\operatorname{rank}(L)+h\ge 3m$, where rank uses the linear part.

The threshold for the first possible exact compression is sharp. At $h=m$, use the $m$ binary coordinates of $vw$ as quadratic observations and the $m$ coordinates of $u$ as linear observations. The decoder computes $\operatorname{Tr}(u(vw))$. This compresses three field elements to two.

There is also a two-sided error bound. If $E$ has a collision for $z\ne0$ and $h<m$, every decoder's error on the uniform label is at least
$$
                    2^{-h-2}(1-2^{h-m}).
$$
A quadratic of polar rank $s$ on a flat has absolute sign bias at most $2^{-s/2}$. Squaring the bias and averaging the signs of its first derivatives kills every derivative with nonzero linear part, leaving at most the $2^{-s}$ fraction of radical directions. Hence at least $(1-2^{h-m})/2$ of $C_z$ has $D_z F=1$. Translation by $z$ pairs these points, and each pair has equal summaries and opposite verdicts. The bound follows since $|C_z|/2^d\ge2^{-h}$. The same inequality holds for randomized decoders or mixtures of such lossy summaries whose coins are fixed independently of the label.

The error bound is exact over the class of all noninjective summaries with $h<m$ quadratic probes. Fix $z=(a,0,0)$, $a\ne0$, and choose a coordinate $u_1$ along $a$ in a binary basis of the first field factor. Choose dual coordinates of $v,w$ so $\operatorname{Tr}(avw)=\sum_{i=1}^m v_iw_i$. Store all raw coordinates except $u_1$ and add the $h$ quadratics $u_1v_1,\ldots,u_1v_h$. The only possible collisions are $z$-pairs, and these occur exactly on $v_1=\cdots=v_h=0$. There $D_z F=\sum_{i>h}v_iw_i$ has bias $2^{h-m}$, by averaging over $w$ for each $v$. A decoder can be correct on every singleton and every same-verdict pair, making one error per opposite-verdict pair. This attains the stated error exactly. This construction optimizes over noninjective summaries; its displayed number of output positions is $3m-1+h$.

## Allowing an adaptive affine prefix

Before choosing the summary, a deterministic procedure may ask up to $a$ adaptively chosen affine questions about the input. The transcript for a nonempty leaf restricts $x$ to an affine flat of codimension $c\le a$. The final affine and quadratic observations may depend arbitrarily on that transcript. If $a+h<m$, exactness forces the **entire transcript together with its final observations** to be injective on the labels. Within a leaf, a collision can use only a shift in its direction space. The collision flat now has ambient codimension at most $c+h<m$, and the same derivative proof applies. Different leaves already have different transcripts. Thus even that amount of adaptivity cannot merge two inputs. Conversely, after reading the $m$ coordinates of $u$, a single quadratic query selected from those replies returns $\operatorname{Tr}(uvw)$ directly.

This restriction on which observations can branch is significant. Fix a nonzero shift $z=(a,0,0)$. First query the quadratic derivative $D_zF=\operatorname{Tr}(avw)$. On its zero branch, affine coordinates modulo $\langle z\rangle$ suffice to determine the verdict. Every pair differing by $z$ on this branch has equal verdicts. On its one branch retain all coordinates. Thus even one adaptive quadratic used before the choice of final affine coordinates can permit some collisions.

## Exact decision-tree depth with fully adaptive quadratic queries

Allow every new quadratic Boolean question to depend on all earlier answers. The exact worst-case depth on this family is **$m+1$**. For the upper bound read $u$ and ask the one remaining quadratic. For the lower bound put $q=2^m$. The 2011 preprint of Kolokotronis and Limniotis states the second-order nonlinearity value used here. Applying it to field multiplication, or using the [alternate derivation](trace-product-quadratic-correlation.md), gives
$$
   \max_{Q:\deg Q\le2}\left|\mathbb E_x(-1)^{F(x)+Q(x)}\right|
                   =(2q-1)/q^2.
$$
This is the normalized correlation form of their Corollary 1. [Kolokotronis and Limniotis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2011/212)

Every sign-valued tree of depth $t\ge1$ is a real combination of quadratic phases with sum of absolute coefficients at most $2^{t-1}$. At depth one the output is a constant or a single signed query phase. For the induction multiply each child expansion by its branch indicator $(1\pm(-1)^Q)/2$, then add them. Multiplication by that indicator has coefficient-sum norm at most one, since the product of two quadratic phases is another quadratic phase. Thus depth $m$ gives absolute verdict correlation at most $2^{m-1}(2q-1)/q^2=1-1/(2q)<1$. This proves exact depth, including $m=1$. More generally a depth-$t$ randomized tree, with its coins independent of the input, has absolute correlation on the uniform labels at most $2^{t-1}(2q-1)/q^2$ for $t\ge1$. A constant prediction advantage requires $t\ge m-O(1)$.

The asymptotic linear query lower bound also follows from earlier multiparty communication work. Ford and Gál's field multiplication theorem includes this trace of three factors in the number-on-the-forehead model. Three broadcasts suffice to simulate a quadratic query because every monomial touches at most two field factors. Assign each monomial to one player who sees both factors, and have each player broadcast the parity of the assigned terms. This already yields $\Omega(m)$ deterministic query depth. The exact $m+1$ count above uses the precise second-order nonlinearity. I did not locate an earlier statement of this exact query depth or of the injectivity threshold. [Ford and Gál, Theorems 4.4–4.5](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~panni/icalp05.pdf)

## Realization by ordinary 3-CNF

For the shortest construction use the known linear bound on the binary bilinear rank of multiplication in $K$. For each scale one can fix $R=O(m)$ pairs of binary linear forms $\ell_i,k_i$ and field elements $c_i$ such that $uv=\sum_i \ell_i(u)k_i(v)c_i$. Consequently
$$
      F(u,v,w)=\sum_{i=1}^{R}\ell_i(u)k_i(v)\operatorname{Tr}(c_iw)
                 \quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2.
$$
Supply these $3R$ binary linear forms in fixed input-pin slots. An ordinary Boolean circuit needs $O(m)$ gates to AND the triples and XOR the results. Also include pins for the raw $3m$ label bits, with their own variables, so every label is visible and all stated query upper bounds are available. The more compact family supplies the linear forms already evaluated in the signs. The theorem does not charge the cost of computing those forms from the raw label to the formula generator's output length. One can choose the forms computably by finite search at every scale. [Ballet and Pieltant, binary tensor rank of field multiplication](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.1864)

For a presentation in which every variable sign is a raw label bit or its complement, choose a fixed degree-$m$ irreducible binary polynomial for each scale, and express field elements in its polynomial basis. The trace of the quotient-ring residue is a fixed binary linear functional of the coefficients of the ordinary polynomial product $uvw$ of degree at most $3m-3$. Two binary polynomial multiplications and a fixed XOR selection suffice, with $O(m\log(m+2)\log\log(m+4))$ gates by Cantor and Kaltofen over $\mathbb F_2$. No variable divisor or evaluation of the defining polynomial on the input is needed. This size statement allows coefficients fixed for the family at each scale. [Cantor and Kaltofen on polynomial multiplication](https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/91/CaKa91.pdf)

In either construction use ordinary gate equivalences, assert its prescribed input pins, and require output one. In a fixed layout the only changing physical bits are signs of the pins, each affine in the label. Short clauses can be expanded into all sign choices on fresh padding bits to obtain exactly three distinct variables, replacing them by constant-size blocks. Constants and repeated gate inputs can first be simplified or given distinct copy variables. The formula is satisfiable exactly at $F=1$. The number of encoded bits gains one logarithmic factor for dense fixed-width binary variable identifiers. Thus the two encoded lengths are respectively $\Theta(m\log(m+2))$ and $O(m\log^2(m+2)\log\log(m+4))$. In the first family the exact adaptive lower bound has order $N/\log N$ in physical length $N$. An affine or quadratic observation of the physical encoding restricts to one of the same degree on the labels. Conversely the coordinates of $u$ and $vw$ can be observed from the visible pin signs at degree one and two, respectively. This proves both claims for explicit SAT.

These are restrictions on information retained by a fixed observation procedure. An arbitrary RAM can change its future observations after inspecting intermediate values. The argument does not exclude linear, quadratic or cubic RAM time.
