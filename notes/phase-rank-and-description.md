# Which short descriptions can contribute to atoms of the first-time distributions?

**Research journal, 24 November 2026.** Under linear SAT, the high-phase distributions still have all their previously proved atoms after discarding words with sufficiently short time-bounded descriptions. Moreover, within every weak neighborhood of a high-phase cluster point, the remaining distributions have cluster points with arbitrarily large affine dimension. This gives an exclusion criterion that allows an unknown, even noncomputable, finite-dimensional family of time profiles. [Phase distributions and linear SAT transfer](phase-limit-measures.md)

## An upper bound that holds for all time bins together

Use the globally prefix-free native source in the phase experiment. In phase $r$, let $Y_r$ be the target words at the selected lengths of failed windows that have genuine producers by their deadlines. For $y\in Y_r$ put $m=|y|$. The finite measure $\mu_r$ places mass $2^{-m}/P_r$ at the observed first-production rate of $y$. Every word occurs in at most one $Y_r$. Each $Y_r$ is computable by finite simulations after the end of the phase. The positive mask costs $P_r$ are summable and the grant masses $I_r$ sum to the halting probability. [Exact phase ledger](near-clock-credits.md)

Fix an ordinary universal prefix machine with no auxiliary inputs and let $K$ be its unbounded prefix complexity. Define

$$J_r=\sum_{y\in Y_r}2^{-K(y)}.$$

The sequence $J_r$ is uniformly lower semicomputable, and $\sum_r J_r\le1$ by Kraft's inequality on one shortest description of each distinct word. If $\mathbf m$ is the universal discrete lower semicomputable semimeasure on the phase indices, then $J_r\le C\mathbf m(r)$. The term test for the grant series gives infinitely many $r$ with $I_r\ge c\mathbf m(r)$ for one fixed $c>0$. On these same phases $I_r\ge c'P_r$ and eventually $I_r\ge1/r^2$, by universal domination of the computable summable costs and the summable lower bound $1/[r(\log(2+r))^2]$. Under linear SAT, the exact grant lemma gives the bound in the other direction on every sufficiently late phase. One fixed deadline decoder assigns distinct target-word mass $2^{-a}I_r$ on the failed and successful windows together, whose total available target-word mass is at most $2P_r$. Thus $P_r\ge 2^{-a-1}I_r$. [Bienvenu and Shen, random series term test](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1110.5028) · [Grant assignment](fifo-producer-credits.md)

Consequently there is a constant $A$ such that on these infinitely many high phases

$$\sum_{y\in Y_r}\frac{2^{-|y|}}{P_r}\,2^{|y|-K(y)}=\frac{J_r}{P_r}\le A.$$

For every $d\ge0$, the entire normalized weight of words with $K(y)\le |y|-d$ is at most $A2^{-d}$. This includes all words in any chosen time bin with such complexity. Moreover a native producing program $p$ of low ordinary prefix complexity would give $K(y)\le K(p)+O(1)$, by first recovering its exact name and then running it. Therefore a positive lower bound on mass in a bin eventually costs positive mass in words for which **every** producing program has complexity at least $m-O(1)$. The constant depends on the desired positive mass. A long incompressible program can share a simple clock rule with many other programs, so this does not bound the complexity of the rate by the complexity of an individual payload. In fact no infinite computably enumerable set of nonempty phases can satisfy this ordinary-$K$ tail estimate with a common constant. Otherwise take a computable increasing selection $f(i)$ in that set and compute the first $y_i\in Y_{f(i)}$ in length-lexicographic order. Then $K(y_i)\le K(i)+O(1)$. The selected lengths grow faster than $\log i$, so $d_i=|y_i|-K(y_i)$ is eventually nonnegative. The bound at $d_i$, applied to this one word, implies $2^{-K(i)}\le C P_{f(i)}$. But the latter weights form a computable summable sequence. Find increasing indices $i_j$ at which they are below $2^{-2j}$. Since $K(i_j)\le K(j)+O(1)=O(\log(j+2))$, the proposed domination fails. This also explains why mere enumeration of the earliest simple word in each observed phase does not defeat the bound on the special phases.

There is a computable refinement for any specified computable time cap $F(m)$. Let $K_F(y)$ be the shortest length of a description of at most $2m$ bits producing $y$ by $F(m)$ steps on the chosen prefix machine, with value infinity if none exists. Compute $J^F_r$ by the same formula. It is rational, computable and bounded by $J_r$. For a sufficiently large fixed $k$, take the phases satisfying the three exactly decidable tests

$$I_r\ge2^{-k}P_r,\qquad I_r\ge1/r^2,\qquad J^F_r\le2^k I_r.$$

They include all sufficiently late phases of the preceding infinite subsequence. On this computable refinement $J^F_r/P_r$ is bounded. For any computable integer $d(r)\to\infty$, discard every word with $K_F(y)\le |y|-d(r)$ from $\mu_r$. The mass lost is $O(2^{-d(r)})$. The retained finite rational measures therefore have **the same weak cluster points** as the original measures on this refinement. All those limits still have positive atoms at every limit-computable rate in $[3,4]$. The result holds separately for each specified $F$. Unbounded $K$ gives the stronger word estimate on the possibly noncomputable subsequence above.

## Exact finite-dimensional interpolation on late phases

Let $(\eta_i)$ be any computable sequence of finite rational positive measures of bounded mass on a compact interval with rational endpoints. Let $C$ be its weak cluster set. If $\dim\operatorname{aff} C=d<\infty$, the closed convex hull of $C$ contains a measure computable from the halting problem. The measures spanning this affine space need no effective presentations.

For $d=0$ the sequence converges, which proves the claim. Otherwise select rational piecewise-linear functions $f_1,\ldots,f_d$ for which the moment projection $\Phi(\nu)=(\int f_i\,d\nu)_{i=1}^d$ is injective on $\operatorname{aff}C$. Such tests exist because rational piecewise-linear functions are uniformly dense in the continuous functions and their integrals separate finite signed measures. Successively cut down the kernel on the $d$-dimensional direction space. Select $d+1$ affinely independent cluster points. Their projected simplex has interior in $\mathbb R^d$. Fix one rational point $b$ strictly inside it. The tests and $b$ are finite constants in the following algorithm.

At stage $j$, enumerate tuples of $d+1$ distinct indices all at least $j$ until $b$ lies strictly inside the simplex of their projected measures. These are rational determinant and barycentric-coordinate tests. Every sufficiently close tuple to the chosen cluster points passes, so the search terminates. Take the convex combination of its measures using the barycentric coordinates of $b$. Its projection is exactly $b$. Every subsequential limit of these combinations is a combination of at most $d+1$ points in $C$, by compactness of both the measures and the weights and passage to a subsequence of the selected tuples. All such limits lie in $\operatorname{aff}C$ and project to $b$. Injectivity makes them identical. The resulting computable sequence converges to a point of $\operatorname{conv}C$, which has a halting-problem-computable weak name even without a computable rate of convergence.

Apply this to the computable high-phase refinement and the retained measures. The closed convex hull of their cluster set has no halting-problem-computable element under linear SAT: its elements inherit the uniform positive lower mass at each limit-computable atom, whereas such a name constructs a limit-computable nonatom by nested intervals. The same conclusion holds after restricting to the infinitely many visits to any rational basic weak neighborhood with closure in a given open neighborhood of a cluster point. Membership is decidable on the finite rational measures for neighborhoods defined by strict rational moment inequalities. Take the neighborhood small enough that its closure remains in the given open set. The interpolation result therefore gives **infinite affine dimension in every such open set**. In particular, for every fixed $d$, some $d$ rational piecewise-linear probes and a fixed positive determinant threshold distinguish $d+1$ phases there arbitrarily late: select affine-independent cluster points in the open set and then use nearby phase tuples. [Convex-hull atom argument](phase-limit-measures.md)

An actual upper bound placing the local cluster points of the retained first-time distributions in any single finite-dimensional affine family would contradict linear SAT. Ordinary enumeration gives the description-length tail bound above. It does not force such finite-dimensional dependence of the runtime profiles of the remaining nearly incompressible programs. Both the resource transfer and the interpolation proof here are manuscript arguments.
