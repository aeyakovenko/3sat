# Parallel proof attempts of 27 November, round three, and why each stopped

On 27 November, a third round of parallel attempts took up the open steps left by the first two rounds, together with a list of new routes. Each attempt had about one hour and wrote a note. **None bridges the gap; the linear, quadratic and cubic questions remain open.** The results published as separate notes, listed below, were each checked by an independent referee and revised. This note records the other attempts, with the exact reason each stopped, so that later work does not repeat them. None of the attempts recorded here was refereed: their lemmas are attempt history, not verified theorems. [Rounds one and two](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-and-why-each-stopped.md)

## Attempts recorded here

**Mak's unit-cost RAM speedup (dead as a route to the alternating speedup).** The one published speedup of the unit-cost RAM, Mak's "Parallelism Always Helps" (read in its 1993 technical-report version; the SIAM text was not accessible), simulates RAM time T by a CREW PRAM in time O(T^{1/2} log T) with T^{O(T^{1/2})} processors: one processor for each possible trace of a T^{1/2}-step segment. Its polynomial-processor variant, time O(ρ log T + (T log ρ)/ρ) with T^{O(ρ)} processors, gives only constant-factor speedups. Under a conversion to alternation that names the writing processor, as the standard one does, that name is the segment's trace, so one branch writes at least (T/ρ)·ρ log₂ T = T log₂ T bits: word-priced alternating time Ω(T) at every ρ, never o(T). The failing ingredient is what the processors are named by: exhaustive trace search becomes a guessed trace of one word per simulated step, the commitment obstacle again. Mak's RAM also has only addition and subtraction, and the compile from the journal's RAM into it is unchecked. The speedup DTIME_RAM[t] ⊆ ATIME_RAM[o(t)] itself is neither proved nor refuted; a PRAM route would need parallel time o(T) with processor names and words totalling o(T log T) bits, which is Dymond and Tompa's polynomial-processor question for the unit-cost RAM. [Mak, technical report](https://hdl.handle.net/2142/74573) · [Mak, SIAM J. Comput.](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539794265402)

**Equality digests for every run of the diagonal machine (partial).** A positive answer is not empty: if every run of the diagonal machine J has a sound equality digest whose description has o(t) bits and is read by a fixed O(1)-time evaluator, the native milestone follows (a manuscript argument that rests on one reading of the alternation note). The universal player names the digest, so no algorithm has to compute it and only the description length counts; unconditionally, the hypothesis puts J in Π₄TIME_RAM[o(m²)], an alternating speedup of the one square-time language J, which no proof relativizing to the packed interface can give. When the evaluator does not read the input, the answer is no for every description shorter than about m/2 bits, O(1) words included: the journal's common-collision lemma carries over to J (checked at one small size, assuming J's interpretation schedule does not depend on the data). Open: evaluators that read the input, and descriptions between m and o(t) bits. Per-run existence with no bound on the description yields nothing on this route. [Common collision](separate-fingerprint-witnesses.md) · [Alternation criterion](alternating-native-speedup.md)

**Compressing gadgets for the pairwise-XOR family (dead as a source of bounds, in fields without ports).** The round-two escape was a gadget of volume o(r√log q) that routes r ≫ N bits through O(1) copies of the pairwise-XOR predicate P. Such gadgets are not excluded for r up to about kN log₂N/√log q, but in every field without ports they cannot make the uniform-destinations lemma of the matrix-multiplication preprint certify more than O(N log N) per copy. The lemma's proof sees only the network's topology, so each copy can be replaced by one vertex of degree N carrying an arbitrary table, which costs only its N legs in the price inequality and at most about N log₂N in the edge-law entropy. The resulting cap, (17/64)·N log₂N/(1 + log₂d) certified internal edges per copy whatever r, q and k, holds for every tensor with N legs in place of P and lies below the ω(N log³ N) the route needs. It is proved given the preprint statements it uses, with its identities and algebra checked numerically, and it closes for fields without ports the compressing-embedding case that the technique-ceiling note leaves open. It also caps every routing-based certificate for the fixed-B slices, so the other escape needs a technique that sees more than topology; reading single vertex tables through prices does not see more, as [a later note](the-price-inequality-and-its-table-reading-dual-cannot-certify-more-than-n-log-n.md) shows. Open: the ports branch, where nothing yet excludes a compressing Tag gadget unless the batch bound survives black boxes. Sharper evaluations of the lemma's minimax constant cannot help an evaluation that excludes networks only by violations of the price inequality ([the price-inequality note](the-price-inequality-and-its-table-reading-dual-cannot-certify-more-than-n-log-n.md)). [Technique ceiling](the-list-distinctness-technique-cannot-certify-more-than-n-log-n.md)

**The opcode barrier for a constant number of blocks (partial).** Relative to the two-word opcode of the hidden-word note, three cases of the speedup premise are barred for black-box arguments, which treat an instruction as an arbitrary map on two words. With one existential block, every accepting path on a positive table queries more than (1 − η)T genuine keys, on every graph. With one universal block and any number of branches, a branch needs more than q queries to reach a flagged vertex with more than q ancestors; the proof is a deterministic substitution, and it needs a positive table with a decoy of flag 1. On the depth-robust graphs, every two-block verifier whose first proof stays accepted when genuine labels change outside a set of at most cT/log₂T vertices (checkpoint lists, pebble placements, run values at chosen cells) needs Ω(T) queries on some branch. Chains cannot carry a two-block barrier: a two-block verifier decides the chain exactly with O(√T) queries per branch and O(√T) words of proof. The proofs are manuscript arguments, with the relabeling lemma and the chain verifier checked at small sizes. The general two-block case needs every first proof to be closed in this sense on some table, or a counterexample, which would be a non-pebbling game; three or more blocks remain a conjecture. This is a method barrier, not a counter-model: relative to the opcode the native milestone holds, and arguments that use the algebra of the real instructions are not bound. [Hidden-word opcode](hidden-word-opcode-and-local-verification-of-a-gap.md) · [Alternation criterion](alternating-native-speedup.md)

**Tree evaluation and space simulations as games (partial).** For every register program built by the recursion of Cook and Mertz (Lemma 14 of the ECCC version), any game whose moves assert the program's register contents at split points costs at least 2(d+1)·b·h·log₂(d(db+1)) bits on some path. That is more than 2.58 times the top-down tree-evaluation game for every h, d and b, although at d = 1 a midpoint game on true values is far cheaper than both. Through Williams's reduction of time-t machines, assuming h ≥ t/b, such games never cost o(t) bits, which generalizes the journal's remark that the square-root simulation's game costs t log t. At one level, the multiplexer's selector depth is 2, 4 and 5 for 1, 2 and 3 selector bits (the last resting on an unchecked CaDiCaL UNSAT answer), so formulas built by composition need the top-down depth for d ≤ 3 and b = 1; the exact formula depth of TE(2,2,1) is only bracketed between 5 and 8. What remains is a game whose moves are neither register contents nor true node values. For b = 1 and d ≥ 2, such a game costing o(dh) would refute the KRW conjecture as Cook and Mertz state it; for b > 1, the case the RAM needs, no verified statement exists. [Cook–Mertz, ECCC TR23-174](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2023/174/) · [Alternation criterion](alternating-native-speedup.md)

**Truth-claim games on the unit-cost RAM (partial).** For explicit-history machines, in which every step writes only a fresh cell and every load reads the input or an earlier cell, word-priced alternating time O(t/log t) suffices. The existential player plays a Dymond–Tompa pebbling strategy on the claim graph (their Lemma 1), and an induction on time handles edges that depend on claimed values. In bits this costs O(t), so there is no bit-priced gain, and the separation it would feed (a diagonal and a collapse inside that model) was not checked. Whether truth-claim games can beat walking the state chain on the overwrite ladder stays open: exact values for T ≤ 12 (frame game T ≤ 10, with five instances at T = 11–12 bracketed) put the worst ladders for T = 3..8 at T in the frame game and at T − 1, the walk bound, in the node game, but such sizes cannot tell T from T/log T. On the traces of the standing-opponent suite below the node values agree on all 24 traces, and the suite's interval game is the frame game's interval strategy up to one folded claim. Its bisection bound (C+1)⌈log₂ T⌉ makes the conjecture false for ladders with o(T/log T) cells, so it needs Ω(T/log T) cells. A later cone-restricted bisection lowers the frame-game bound to C·log₂(T/C) + O(C), so in the frame game the conjecture needs liminf C(T)/T > 0 ([frame-game upper bound](frame-game-upper-bound-on-overwrite-ladders.md)). A round-four entry proved that the frame value is at least C − 1 on every ladder touching C cells, which settles the conjecture for the frame game on ladders touching a constant fraction of T cells ([frame-game lower bound](frame-game-lower-bound-on-overwrite-ladders.md)). The constant-reader value T − 1 of the round-two QBF entry was an artefact of letting the challenger dispute steps fixed by the program text; without that it is about log₂ T. [Dymond–Tompa, Waterloo CS-83-08](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/research/tr/1983/CS-83-08.pdf) · [Round-two attempts](parallel-proof-attempts-of-27-november-and-why-each-stopped.md)

**Path-parity games on GF(2)-affine runs (partial).** For runs of the journal's RAM whose control is public and whose data steps are GF(2)-affine (XOR, masks, shifts and copies), a halving game on parities of the step-matrix product decides the output with O(log T · log N) bits and blocks and needs no last-writer or live-set claims; plays were enumerated in full on toy runs (T ≤ 10, at most 3 cells). This is ⊕L ⊆ NC² read as a game. With a bitwise AND of data words, a variant with a depth budget is sound for every budget and exact once the budget reaches the nonlinear depth δ, at a cost additive in δ. It is not exactly Valiant–Skyum–Berkowitz–Rackoff, which is priced by the logarithm of the degree, and neither bound dominates. Neither part feeds the alternation criterion. The game uses Θ(log t · log N) blocks (20,880 at n = 2^20 and t = n²) against the criterion's o(log log t); folding it into few blocks brings back the commitment to cut vectors; and on general runs a data-dependent load or the carry of an addition makes δ and the degree Θ(t). The missing step is a counting game with o(log log t) blocks. The construction uses GF(2)-linearity and has no content relative to the packed oracle. [Alternation criterion](alternating-native-speedup.md)

**A packed emitter for the compiler criterion's failure test (partial).** The failure test of the compiler-criterion note can be written in O(1) word steps per clause. A fixed 1,233-instruction program for the journal's RAM emits every record type whose number grows with the witness length W or the source length n (chunk clauses, one-hot decoders, libraries, header and pins); its output is byte-identical to a straightforward emitter's and to an independent reference on 69 parameter sets, and it takes a measured 162–212 steps per clause from W = 2^10 to 2^18. With it the criterion restates for SAT time N·log^a N with a ∈ [−1, 0) (a hand proof): output length O(s log^β s) with β + a < 1 excludes that time, and at a = −1 a length of o(s log² s) excludes SAT in O(N/log N) word steps. That threshold equals the compiler note's Ω(s log² s) bound for count-preserving compilers, so a compiler meeting it would have to destroy witness counts. The missing step is the polylogarithmic check circuit, an XOR placeholder here: serializing its real gate list, which embeds the compiler's local indexer, the scanner and the hash checks, is argued at O(1) steps per gate but not written, so the restated criterion is conditional on it. The criterion's own open step, a compiler of length o(s log² s), is untouched. [Compiler criterion](compact-compilers-and-linear-sat-time.md)

**Rounds against pebbles on explicit graphs (partial).** The graph half of the round-two alternation-depth ladder is done. Every DAG of in-degree at most d with m edges has a winning Kalyanasundaram–Schnitger strategy with R rounds and at most 8m/log₂ R pebbles, for every 2 ≤ R ≤ m/(252d), by a Hopcroft–Paul–Valiant edge-halving split stopped early (proved on paper; the strategy was played against every reply on all 5,910 chain-plus-one-edge DAGs with T = 4..8, and the recurrence iterated to m = 2^20). So the universal gain with R rounds is at least c·log R, which the Kalyanasundaram–Schnitger abstract shows is optimal up to constants on graphs chosen for R; the bound beats pebbling everything only for R > 256, and it is probably in their paper, of which only the abstract was read. The machine half is missing: an alternating program with O(1)-word moves that realizes the strategy on a run's access graph. Combined with the explicit-history simulation of the truth-claim entry, it would give rounds of the ladder for explicit-history machines only, where the access graph is explicit; even there the local check of the split is expected but not written out, and the diagonal and collapse inside that model were not checked. On the mutable-store RAM the crossing edges are last-writer relations, which is the round-two QBF entry's open game. Even with the machine half, R = o(log log t) rounds give gain O(log log log t), far below the log^{1+ε} t that the journal's criterion needs. [Kalyanasundaram–Schnitger](https://doi.org/10.1016/0890-5401%2890%2990002-Y) · [Bounded-round pebbling](mixed-tape-pebble-frontier.md)

**The SAT-target compiler criterion for the deterministic diagonal alone (partial).** Whether a locally indexed compile of the diagonal J's files of length o(s log s) excludes linear SAT, with the compiler hypothesis applied only to J's files, is neither proved nor refuted. The failing step is removing, deterministically in o(T) time, the outer existential block of W = Θ(M/log M) bits. Without a compiler applied to a nondeterministic program, only the usual compile, at a log³ overhead, does this, which gives a diagonal-only criterion at length o(s/log² s) for every polylogarithmic indexer. Alternating J-compiles with the usual compile gives the same threshold as one round (proved for that class of routes), and the journal's construction does not shrink the witness from round to round. Writing the compiled file and running the solver once gives o(s/τ(s)) for an indexer taking τ steps per bit, and o(s) with constant-time packed access. [Black-box routes through the diagonal compile](black-box-routes-through-the-diagonal-compile.md) A J-only criterion at o(s log s) would imply that linear SAT excludes J from nondeterministic time O(T/log^α T) for every α > 2, one logarithm beyond the known α > 3, while excluding J from nondeterministic time o(T) would suffice. The counting branch has no instance, because the proof applies the compiler only to instances whose verdict is J(x). With the unary nondeterministic hierarchy source in place of J, the criterion holds at o(s log s) in one round without compiling the verifier, so the obstruction is J's determinism; but such a compile would have o(t) variables, which by the round-two variable count puts the source in deterministic time 2^{o(t)}. [Compiler criterion](compact-compilers-and-linear-sat-time.md) · [Unary RAM source](a-fixed-unary-ram-source-for-physical-sat-compilers.md)

**A standing-opponent suite for game proposals (tooling).** One interpreter for the journal's native machine runs four standing opponents, each cut off at exactly T work accesses: the constant reader; the inverse-permutation frontier solver; a stand-in for the hard-table writer, built from the 24 three-input truth tables of maximal circuit size, which is periodic and so not hard as a whole (the real writer exists only under linear SAT); and a depth-robust labelling evaluator, plus an eight-cell overwrite ladder as an extra. For each it reports the live interface, last-writer distances, rewrite multiplicity, nonlinear depth (without implicit flow through branches), the store log and the space charge. It also computes exact node-game and interval-game values for T ≤ 14, and exact interval values up to T = 1024; the interval game is sound because a step that loads a cell missing from its claim is rejected. Its interpreter reproduces the round-two node game on the constant reader, graph and value. At the sizes measured, the exact interval value is at least 0.55T words on the frontier solver, the labelling evaluator and the stand-in with all its reads (T = 256 and 1024), so neither game class is cheap on every opponent; on the constant reader the node game costs T − 1 under the round-two convention, while the interval game costs exactly 2⌈log₂ T⌉ for T = 3..14. Its one proved by-product, that bisection in the interval game asserts at most (C+1)⌈log₂ T⌉ words on runs touching C cells, is the bound used in the truth-claim entry. The suite is in the journal's audits and runs in about 15 seconds; a new opponent or game takes a few lines. [Standing-opponent suite](../audits/standing_opponent_suite.py)

## What the routes were missing

The alternation route still lacks its premise, an alternating speedup of the unit-cost RAM. Parallel speedups by trace enumeration give nothing under alternation. Truth-claim games give O(t/log t) words, but only for explicit-history machines, and with R = o(log log t) rounds the universal gain of pebbling strategies is Θ(log R), far below what the criterion needs. Through Williams's reduction, games that assert the register contents of Cook–Mertz programs never cost o(t) bits, and the path-parity game needs Θ(log t · log N) blocks. Relative to a two-word opcode, black-box arguments whose first proof is closed under label changes cannot supply even two blocks. The digest route reduces to the description length of a digest for every run of the diagonal machine, open for evaluators that read the input. The compiler route now has a packed emitter, so its criterion restates down to packed-scan time, conditional on serializing the check circuit; the compiler itself, of length o(s log² s), is missing, and the diagonal-only form is known at o(s/log² s) for polylogarithmic indexers and at o(s) for constant-time packed indexers, where the existential block never arises. In a black-box model with hidden clause signs no route passes o(s). [Black-box routes through the diagonal compile](black-box-routes-through-the-diagonal-compile.md) The network route gains nothing from compressing gadgets: in fields without ports the routing technique certifies at most O(N log N) per copy through any gadget.
