# Nonlinear work after the one-bit SAT cut

Fix a total deterministic aligned word-RAM SAT decider, with unrestricted initially blank workspace, a fixed number of registers and a bounded number of word operands per instruction. Write $N$ for the encoded length in bits, $w=\Theta(\log N)$, with the sufficiently large fixed width coefficient needed for one aligned DIMACS packet. There are arbitrarily large pairs of proper 3-CNFs, one YES and one NO, that differ in one physical bit. Their executions share a prefix up to the request for the packet containing that bit. Among $\Theta(N/\log N)$ data-bearing words, only
$$
 R=\Theta(N\log\log N/\log^2 N)
$$
remain unread. We measure work in each actual continuation, starting immediately before the reply. Give the analysis the entire remaining query, its address map, public syntax, and the bounded controller at the cut. These gifts need never have been available to the solver.

* **Free affine operations.** Even if arbitrary binary-affine combinations of **all** old words are free, a pair exists on which both continuations require $\Omega(R/\log N)$ native steps involving nonlinear processing of old data, an old-data-dependent branch, or an old-data-dependent address. Known-query routing is free. This conclusion places no bound on prefix time or workspace.
* **Free unary operations.** If arbitrary recoding of any one old word and copying are free, as many times and to as many known addresses as desired, a pair exists on which both continuations require $\Omega(R)$ steps combining unresolved sources or using one for control or addressing. For any fixed $K$, a solver running in $O(N\log^K N)$ requires $\Omega(N/\log N)$ such steps in **each** continuation. For an $N^{1+o(1)}$ solver there is a sequence on which the count divided by $R$ tends to infinity on both runs.

The affine bound also strengthens on the very same pair as a function of the total length of intervals covering its nonzero old work cells. A sufficiently compact cut forces $\Omega(N/\log^2 N)$ charged steps on both runs. [Same-pair address-sensitive bound](how-many-old-addresses-can-a-late-sat-query-use.md#an-unrestricted-bound-using-the-memory-on-the-same-pair)

The pairs may depend on the cost convention. The affine convention permits XOR of unrelated words; the unary convention permits arbitrary recoding of one word. Neither result implies the elapsed time of either run is superlinear. [Physical files, aligned RAM and shared-cut construction](near-complete-disclosure-on-one-bit-sat-pairs.md)

## Exact accounting at a cut

An old source is an installed immutable data word or a native work cell as it stood at the cut, including a default zero. Complete fresh input replies and the supplied metadata are public. Model a value in the affine analysis as a vector of affine forms over the old bits. An instruction is free if its control and all effective addresses are determined by the public information and the already resolved transcript, and its new data fields are affine expressions on that branch. A store to a known address can install a new affine expression there. Instructions can mix the query arbitrarily into their *choice* of coefficients. At a charged native instruction, resolve the bounded number of actual operand words and addressing operands, using $O(w)$ binary affine tests, and execute the operation. A newly selected address is then known. Multiple subaccesses in one instruction change the absolute constant. Once a word has been resolved its numerical value is available to all later symbolic computation. Reading an unresolved affine Boolean verdict at termination costs at most one additional bit test.

In the unary convention each unresolved data field is a known function of one old word. Two operands may use the same source, and an operation on them is free when control and addressing are known and the resulting data still depend on just that source. At a charged step reveal the needed old sources and substitute their actual values everywhere. One baseline probe per source suffices, including a check for absence. Arbitrarily many operations on the query, on resolved values, or on copies of one unresolved data word can intervene. Fixed-address reads and writes and query-only gathers incur no charge. Maintain a private map of simulated suffix writes and check it before creating a symbol for an inherited word. No operation needs a charged test merely to identify if a work cell had ever been written in the prefix.

Use a single convention on each standalone continuation. The simulator may recognize constant, unary or affine behavior by exhaustive testing on the finite set of admissible cut states sharing its given metadata and past resolved answers. All free representations or decisions must be valid throughout that conditional set and chosen using disclosed information alone. For example, one can choose the first valid affine form in a fixed order, or a unary recoding on an already represented operand source. A run's particular hidden database cannot supply a free certificate selecting which representation to use. Equally one can use syntactic forms over formal independent source words. The proof works with either convention. During the recursion keep each tag's own simulated cache and apply its standalone convention, including a charge that might have become redundant from the outer simulator's viewpoint.

## One-source recoding and the static query lower bound

Use the balanced database of $k$ rows of $B=\Theta(\log k/\log\log k)$ entries, $M=kB$. Walks select one entry per row in segments of length $d=\Theta(\log k)$. The earlier static lower bound forces
$$
 \Omega\!\left(\min\{M/\log M,\ k/\log(2+S\log M/M)\}\right)
$$
distinct cell probes for some all-good query when the representation occupies $S$ logarithmic-bit cells. Its proof uses cell sampling. It permits arbitrary private computation. At each dyadic interval of $s$ rows down to $\Theta(\sqrt{k})$, preprocess the actual data-only prefix. On a query, run the two half-tag continuations until either they halt or would exceed the cutoff in **charged steps**. Evaluate an unresolved final output with one extra probe. The symbolic simulation above gives $O(1+t_s)$ old-cell probes per tag for cutoff $t_s$, including compact sparse-dictionary lookup when required. No old work or data cell needs to be fetched merely to move its symbolic name.

If a quick simulation reports a miss, the parent contains a miss. If it reports no miss, evaluate the other half if needed. If both time out and no genuinely opposite pair has both expensive, the true half verdicts agree; evaluate the left half. Recurse down just one path. Use fixed tie-breaking and direct tests at the leaf. The compact placement headers and controllers cost $o(k/\log M)$ probes in total along this path, and the leaf costs $O(\sqrt k)$. In the unrestricted case take $t_s=\eta s/\log(sB)$, in the near-linear case $t_s=\eta sB/\log(sB)$. The geometric sum contradicts the corresponding static lower bound for sufficiently small $\eta$. The extracted local file has $N=\Theta(sB)$. For $N^{1+o(1)}$ prefix time, the combined representation has $S=M^{1+o(1)}$ words after summing the levels. The displayed lower bound is then $\omega(k/\log M)$. Every fixed multiple of $s/\log(sB)$ fails as a uniform cutoff on opposite pairs. A diagonal selection gives the stated sequence. [Proof of the static bound and header accounting](near-complete-disclosure-on-one-bit-sat-pairs.md)

## An identity matrix inside the same physical query language

We now allow arbitrary joint affine transformations of the old bits between charged operations. The prefix generating those bits remains unrestricted. Cell sampling for single-address probes would not justify a lower bound in this model.

Use the same cut layouts and dyadic intervals. Number two adjacent columns $0,1$ on the Margulis–Gabber–Galil torus, corresponding to $(0,0)$ and $(1,0)$. A single transition of its powered lazy graph can stay or travel between these columns in either direction: take a labeled move or its inverse and hold for the remaining steps. Thus every binary selector string $y\in\{0,1\}^k$ is realized, with a fixed encoding per segment, including after restriction to a dyadic child. In row $i$ set column $x_i$ to one and the other distinguished column to zero. Fill the other $B-2$ entries with a fixed pattern of weight $B/2-1$. Every $x\in\{0,1\}^k$ is an admissible balanced database. The answer “all selected entries are good” is exactly $[x=y]$. Its matrix in $x,y$ over $\mathbb F_2$ has rank $2^k$. On any subset of database rows its rank is the size of that subset. Appending an independent row tensors the equality matrix with the two-by-two identity.

For each $x$ form one feature vector $Y(x)$, independent of $y$. Include every cut work cell, installed data word and controller bit for every dyadic interval as well as the raw database bits. Pad work storage to the complete native address space for that interval, setting unused entries to zero. Use zero placeholders in pending input slots. Thus the number $U$ of features is $M^{O(1)}$ even for arbitrarily long prefixes: the machine can address only $2^w$ words at each node. Values in this vector can be arbitrary nonlinear functions of $x$. Adding any polynomial number of further database-only features obeys the same dimension estimate.

We need the following rank calculation. For fixed $y$, a decision tree of depth $a$ testing affine forms in $U$ feature bits has a polynomial of degree at most $a$ over $\mathbb F_2$. At a query $L$ with subtrees computing $P_0,P_1$, take $P_0+L(P_1-P_0)$. Reduce repeated powers on binary inputs. The columns obtained for different $y$ belong to the span of the same at most $(U+1)^a$ evaluated monomials. Multiplying those columns by any one fixed row function $b(x)$ changes the spanning functions to $b(x)$ times those monomials. With $J$ different row functions the total dimension is at most $J(U+1)^a$. Neither the feature encoding nor the row functions need be low-degree in $x$. [Parity decision trees and the degree lower bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.08668)

**Accounting for the free address map.** Fix a complete path of the one-child algorithm, possibly ending at an internal node, and specify the free address map and controller at every visited node. Call this a type $t$. For $p=sB/h$ database packets and $R_s=1+s/d$ pending packets, it suffices to specify the pending subset and the tag location: the remaining seed packets go in increasing physical-address order. The base family chooses $h=\Theta(\log k)$. Thus each header uses $O(R_s\log B+\log M)$ bits. There are $O(k)$ spatial paths because a node determines its path from the root. The total number $J$ of types satisfies
$$
 \log_2 J=O((k/d)\log B+\log^2 M)=o(k).
$$
For each type, let $b_t(x)$ be the indicator that its stated headers really occur at its stated nodes. This depends only on $x$. The supply of a full address map *as public information* can affect unboundedly many branch decisions. Its cost in this argument is the factor $J$, rather than the cost of repeatedly fetching individual address bits.

For fixed $t,y$, simulate with these headers forced. Every initial word at a fixed input address is either an old feature word or a public query-dependent constant. Every initial work cell has its own feature bits. Simulate both suffixes with cutoff $t_s=\lfloor\eta s/\log^2(sB)\rfloor$ and stop this path with output zero if its prescribed next child or ending differs from the deterministic algorithm's choice. Return one on an accepting path exactly when the reconstructed root has all entries good. Simulating the cutoff needs at most $O(w(t_s+1))$ affine bit tests per node. Leaf comparison needs $O(\sqrt k)$ raw bit tests. Uniformly over types and queries, the parity decision tree depth is at most
$$
 a \ \le\ C\eta\, k/\log M+O(\log^2 M+\sqrt{k})
$$
for a fixed $C$. On invalid header combinations or unreachable branches of the tree we may return any bit: only its behavior for $b_t(x)=1$ is used. Finite totality on genuine completed files allows each uncharged interval to be contracted. The charge convention for a given child is always based on its own admissible states, even if the root database features would permit a shortcut.

Call a master database easy if there is no node and no binary query giving an opposite-verdict pair on which both continuations exceed the cutoff. For each easy database the dyadic rule computes equality for **every** $y$. Exactly one consistent type follows the executed path including its termination. Consequently the equality matrix restricted to easy rows can be written as a sum, over $\mathbb F_2$, of the simulated type matrices multiplied by $b_t(x)$. A consistent type on a different path contributes zero. The rank bound gives
$$
 \#\{\text{easy databases}\}\ \le\ J(U+1)^a.
$$
Since $\log_2(U+1)=O(\log M)$, choose $\eta>0$ so the right side is at most $2^{k/2}$ for all sufficiently large master sizes. Thus even among just these $2^k$ balanced databases, all but an exponentially small fraction have some dyadic interval and a one-bit opposite pair with **two** expensive continuations. The expensive interval can depend on the database. Since $s\ge\Theta(\sqrt k)$, this supplies arbitrarily large physical lengths, with $t_s=\Omega(R/\log N)$ on the chosen interval. The free layout and the near-complete disclosure hold on these very executions.

There are $O(\sqrt k)$ nodes in the recursion tree above the leaf cutoff. For a uniform master payload, at least one of these nodes admits a hard pair with probability at least $c/\sqrt k$ for a fixed $c>0$, by the union bound and the estimate on easy databases. Its local payload is uniform on $s$ bits. At this *one fixed file length* there are consequently at least $c\,2^s/\sqrt k\ge c\,2^s/s$ distinct local database payloads with such a pair. The logical data packet values recover each payload, so these pairs are disjoint as physical files.

## Sharpness for polynomial prefix space

After arbitrary database preprocessing a direct table can hold, for every segment and every possible seed, a single bit recording whether that segment has a miss. The physical seed library has at most $2^h$ choices per segment, so the whole table uses polynomially many bits and fits the stated fixed width convention. In the asymptotic layout $h\le(\log_2 s)/256$, so even one full word per entry uses at most $s^{257/256}$ words. A globally correct scanner can validate the complete public block and ordered data packets on its canonical data-first cut and build the table before requesting the tag. The first nontrivial multiplexer in the public prefix exposes the index of the first column selector, $5+\log_2 s$, fixing $s$ and the master parameter $L$ (the greatest power of two at most $\log_2 s$). It can then regenerate the expected block. After the cut it checks the tag and every remaining seed packet, including the unselected half, and invokes complete SAT on malformed patterns that are valid alternative formulas. On the promised files, these suffix checks depend only on the now public query and layout. With the query known, gather one such bit per relevant segment at known addresses. Pack groups of $w$ bits using shifts by known constants and XOR, which are binary-affine operations. One zero test and branch per group suffice. This gives $O(1+R/w)$ charged operations, matching the unconditional affine lower bound. This preprocessing may take superlinear time. A linear-time solver for the whole promised family is already given in the construction note.

### What can already be done in linear total time?

The whole powered-walk family has a stronger upper bound at the linear clock. A globally correct SAT program takes $O(N)$ real steps on the canonical data-first files and incurs only $O(1+N/\log^2 N)$ affine-charged steps after the tag, on either verdict. The prefix may spend this linear time computing nonlinear functions of the data. Equivalently the suffix cost is $O(1+R/\log\log N)$. The following tradeoff also attains the unrestricted-prefix optimum with $O_\epsilon(N^{1+\epsilon})$ ordinary steps on these files for **every** fixed $\epsilon>0$.

Write $D=16^{160}$ for the constant number of powered transition labels. Split each $d$-row segment into groups of $r$ rows, with $r$ a power of two dividing $d$. Within a group, $Q=B D^{r-1}$ possible query pieces give the initial column and the next $r-1$ powered labels. The construction uses the classical combination of lookup tables and word-parallel Boolean operations. [Chan, *Speeding up the Four Russians Algorithm by About One More Logarithmic Factor*](https://tmc.web.engr.illinois.edu/bmm9_14.pdf)

Before reading data, build a shared table indexed by a row mask $a\in\{0,1\}^B$ and an offset $j<r$. Its entry is a vector of $Q$ bits. Coordinate $q$ is the bit of $a$ selected at offset $j$ by query piece $q$. Store each vector in $\lceil Q/w\rceil$ words. Direct enumeration of all masks, offsets and pieces takes $O(2^B r^2 Q)$ word operations, using a constant number of elementary torus moves per powered transition. Here $2^B=N^{o(1)}$. The query-piece indices and native addresses fit the specified word width since $r\le d$.

During prefix parsing, decode the actual row masks. For each group take the wordwise AND of the $r$ vectors for its successive masks and offsets. Store the result at a fixed address range for that group. Its $q$ coordinate is one exactly when this group has no miss under $q$. This costs $O(s\lceil Q/w\rceil)$ native steps over the $s$ rows, in addition to ordinary $O(N)$ format validation. At the cut the arrived data have already been checked. After the tag validate every remaining packet and decode each complete segment walk once. Its **actual column at the start of each group** and its next $r-1$ labels give the required index. This uses the transition crossing a group boundary to compute the new start. Because the complete segment seed is supplied by the charge convention, the word address and bit offset in every lookup are known. Extracting that bit and packing it with bits from other groups uses fixed shifts, bit masks and XOR, all affine. Test all-good once per word of $w$ group flags, with a public mask for the short final batch. All suffix work takes $O(N)$ ordinary steps and $O(1+s/(wr))$ charged steps. The scanner uses its general SAT fallback whenever the special encoding fails validation.

For $A\ge1$ take a power of two $r\le d$ with $D^{r-1}\le wA$ and $r=\Theta(\min\{d,1+\log_D(wA)\})$. The shared table construction costs $O(2^B r^2 B w A)=O(NA)$ and the ANDs cost $O(s(1+BA))=O(NA)$. With the indicated choice available to the fixed program, this yields the achievable tradeoff
$$
 \text{ordinary time on the family }O(NA),\qquad
 \text{charged suffix }O\!\left(1+\frac{R}{\min\{\log N,\ \log\log N+\log(2+A)\}}\right).
$$
The choices $A=1$ and $A=N^\epsilon$ require only uniform size arithmetic (a smaller fixed rational exponent suffices for any prescribed $\epsilon>0$). For $A=1$ the ratio between this upper bound and the unrestricted lower bound is of order $\log N/\log\log N$. The stronger linear-clock upper bound is sharp when the possible old addresses fit a common nearly linear bank, or any of a subexponential menu of such banks. Arbitrary scattered preprocessing in linear time on the **full** walk family leaves a gap. [Matching dense-address lower bound and range of issued writes](how-many-old-addresses-can-a-late-sat-query-use.md)

Even a small number of prefix writes can give the equality minor a large ambient feature space. Divide its payload into blocks of $\lfloor c\log M\rfloor$ bits, for small fixed $c>0$. In an initially zero array set one sentinel per block, at the address (block number, actual block value). Given $y$, one query-known address retrieves a block equality bit. Packing and testing $w$ such bits at a time gives $O(1+k/\log^2 M)$ paid operations. The number of writes is only $O(k/\log M)$. This upper bound for the equality restriction explains why counting just the number of nonzero old words as the feature dimension would lead to an incorrect stronger rank claim.

Exactness matters on the whole promised row-query family, too. Store the decoded raw database in prefix memory. For a known query and half, the miss indicators $1+D_{i,y_i}$ are affine in these stored bits. XOR a random subset of them. The result is zero if there are no misses, and a fair bit otherwise. Two independent repetitions and a constant number of tests give one-sided error at most $1/4$ using only $O(1)$ charged operations in the affine convention. A zero-error variant shows a further limit for expected costs. Try $\lceil 2\log_2(k+2)\rceil$ independent parities, return “miss” at the first one, and use an exact scan otherwise. A nonempty miss set yields a geometric stopping time with mean at most two parity trials up to truncation. Its probability of triggering the $O(k)$-charge scan is at most $(k+2)^{-2}$. On every opposite pair the half containing a miss therefore has expected $O(1)$ charged work. This prices the same fixed-query affine operations with input-independent randomness supplied freely; the exact scan on an all-good half can still be expensive.

The unary accounting also stops at a scan: resolving every old data word costs at most one probe per word and suffices for unlimited private computation. An actual SAT computation can still require time after its data have been resolved. Neither kind of accounting imposes another charge for that time. The achievement here is a cost on **both real continuations after a specific late input request**, with the corresponding free operations specified.


## What was verified

Lean checks the simulation of charged steps by old-source probes, and the identity-minor rank bound with arbitrary overlapping row masks and preprocessed features. Lean also checks the decomposition of a powered walk into groups, intersections of packed word masks, and testing a final packed batch. The earlier dyadic formalization checks the two-tag logic. On a four-row balanced instance, the executable now enumerates all 256 database/query pairs and reads both tag choices as proper DIMACS. The recovered all-good matrix is the $16\times16$ identity. All 256 tag edits flip exactly one physical bit, and 96 change the SAT verdict. The shared-table audit exhausts 4,096 mask/path predicates, checks all 256 segments of a four-label powered-walk subalphabet, and compares the two half answers on 258 complete queries. The physical layout, asymptotic parameters, charge simulation for affine RAM instructions, and extraction of the opposite executions use the manuscript arguments above. Priority for the combined result is unestablished.
