# How many affine tests make a matrix-product parity quadratic?

Let $F(A,B,C)=\operatorname{tr}(ABC)$ for three binary $d$-square matrices, and write $D=d^2$. Through **every** input the minimum codimension of an affine subspace on which $F$ is a Boolean quadratic is exactly $D$. Each equation may involve entries of all three matrices, and the quadratic may depend on the input. Fixing the entire first matrix attains the bound. In particular, a decision tree with arbitrary parity queries and free quadratic answers at its leaves needs exactly $D$ queries in the worst case, and every input needs at least $D$.

There is a quantitative version. Let $L$ be the space of directions of an affine subspace of codimension $b$. Take the third Boolean derivative of $F$ on $L$ and let $s$ be its geometric border rank as an ordinary three-mode tensor over $K=\overline{\mathbb F}_2$. Write $M_h$ for the usual square matrix multiplication tensor of side $h$. Then
$$
  s\ \ge\ \underline R(M_h),\qquad h=(d-\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor)_+ .             \tag{1}
$$
Here $M_0$ is zero. For any valid lower bound $\omega_0$ on the formal square multiplication exponent over $K$, (1) gives $b\ge(d-s^{1/\omega_0})_+^2$. The November 2026 preprint states $\omega_0=9/4$. With that additional result, even $d^{2+o(1)}$ free products of three arbitrary affine forms, added to the residual quadratic on a leaf, still require $(1-o(1))d^2$ independent parity answers on **every** input. The exact quadratic case is independent of the stated matrix exponent. ## Degenerating a mixed space of directions

For $x,u,v,w$ in the input space take the sum over $\mathbb F_2$ of the values of $F$ at the eight corners $x+\epsilon_1u+\epsilon_2v+\epsilon_3w$. Expanding the cubic gives the sum of the six assignments of $u,v,w$ to its three matrix arguments. This alternating trilinear form $\Omega(u,v,w)$ is independent of $x$. On pure directions in the order $A,B,C$ it is exactly $\operatorname{tr}(u_Av_Bw_C)$. A quadratic contributes zero. A product of three affine forms contributes a sum of six rank-one trilinear forms. Equality of Boolean functions on a binary affine subspace suffices here. The resulting trilinear coefficient identity extends to $K$ in any binary basis of $L$.

In the Grassmannian of $(3D-b)$-planes in the full direct sum, take the locus on which $\Omega$ has restricted border rank at most $s$. It is nonempty because it contains the scalar extension of $L$. It is closed because on each affine chart one may use a local basis and pull back the closed border-rank locus in a fixed tensor space. The three index-space changes of basis send $(A,B,C)$ to $(g_1Ag_2^{-1},g_2Bg_3^{-1},g_3Cg_1^{-1})$. They preserve the trace polynomial, hence also $\Omega$ and this projective locus. The Borel fixed-point theorem applied to the product of the three upper triangular groups gives a fixed plane $L'$. These groups are connected and solvable over $K$. If the locus is reducible, a connected group preserves its irreducible components.

The diagonal characters of all the matrix units are distinct even across the three blocks. Each character has weight $+1$ at the tagged row index and $-1$ at the tagged column index. Thus $L'$ is spanned by individual matrix units. Within each block, the upper triangular row and inverse column actions imply that a retained entry forces every entry weakly above it and to its right to be retained. Although the action also changes an adjacent matrix space, each individual matrix unit already belongs to $L'$ separately. Denote the three upper-right shapes by $S_{12},S_{23},S_{31}$ and their numbers of holes by $p_{12},p_{23},p_{31}$. Their sum is $b$. This use of symmetry follows the matrix tensor compression method. The single Grassmannian is needed because the original queries can mix the three matrices. [Wigderson and Zuiddam, Part III, §12, in particular Theorems 12.17 and 12.19](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/Wigderson_Zuiddam_2025.pdf)

## Packing three rectangles at once

Fix a positive side $h\le d$, and put $q=d-h+1$. Choose starts $i,j,k$ in $\{1,\ldots,q\}$ for intervals of length $h$ on the three index sets. An interval rectangle on edge $12$ survives exactly when its bottom-left entry $(i+h-1,j)$ survives. Distinct pairs of starts test distinct entries, so at most $p_{12}$ of the $q^2$ pairs fail. The same assertion holds on edges $23$ and $31$. Among the $q^3$ triples at most $q(p_{12}+p_{23}+p_{31})$ fail at least one test. Thus $b<q^2$ guarantees one triple on which all three rectangles survive. With positive $h=d-\lfloor\sqrt b\rfloor$ this strict inequality holds. Restricting the first, second and third direction in $L'$ to the corresponding pure rectangles gives $M_h$. Monotonicity of border rank proves (1). The lower bound $\underline R(M_h)\ge h^{\omega_0}$ for $h\ge2$ follows by tensor powers and interpolation over the infinite field $K$. The cases $h=0,1$ follow directly.

The same argument works for three different lengths $h_i$. Writing $q_i=d-h_i+1$, it suffices that $\sum_{ij}p_{ij}/(q_iq_j)<1$. The uniform threshold for equal sides is sharp for this geometric packing statement. Delete the last $q$ rows times the first $q$ columns in one matrix. Every choice of $h$ rows and $h$ columns hits that hole, even if the indices need not be consecutive. This example establishes sharpness for containing coordinate rectangles. It does not evaluate the border rank of that restriction.

If $b<D$, (1) leaves at least one triangle and $s\ge1$. A quadratic fiber must have $s=0$, proving the exact lower bound. If a fiber carries $k$ products of affine triples, the expansion above gives $s\le6k$. For example, $k=d^{2+o(1)}$ and $\omega_0=9/4$ give $b\ge(d-d^{8/9+o(1)})^2$ for large $d$.

## Sharpening the trilinear predictor bound

Let $U$ be a binary trilinear predictor of border rank $s$ for $M_d$. Write $p$ for the binary slice rank of $M_d-U$. Group the linear factors of its slices by mode and intersect their kernels. This gives a product of three spaces of total codimension at most $p$ on which $M_d$ agrees with $U$. Use the product of three Grassmannians, each with its own fixed dimension. The restricted ordinary tensor has border rank at most $s$ on a nonempty closed invariant locus. The same fixed-point and simultaneous-interval argument gives
$$
    p\ge(d-s^{1/\omega_0})_+^2.
$$
Here one uses $U$ in its original three modes, so the factor six from polarization never enters. The earlier separate-trim estimate had a factor $3/4$ on the right. Applying Lampert's $p\le9a+33$, where $a$ is the analytic rank of the error, bounds the chance of predicting the entire matrix $AB$ by $2^{-\max(0,((d-s^{1/\omega_0})_+^2-33)/9)}$. This last probability identity follows by averaging the scalar error phase over the independent third matrix. ## What a SAT solver would have to supply

The journal's two-call reduction gives a near-quadratic bit-RAM evaluator of $F$ from a near-linear SAT solver. On its fixed-layout SAT query the three source matrices enter through pins. Thus the fiber theorem applies to any proposed explanation through an actual input that certifies the evaluator's verdict on every completion of those affine tests, with the stated residual computation. Simply reading a logarithmic word can provide many affine answers, and later instructions can compose lookups. We have no inference from a linear-time solver to a fiber with $b$ and $s$ violating (1). The primary sources establish earlier compression methods and the imported rank bounds. Priority for the sharp simultaneous-interval bound on mixed affine fibers remains unverified.
