# One scan answers every single matching cut

On $a$ indices, the homogeneous two-layer family with two coincident first-layer matchings and a square or doubled edge at each restored second-layer component has a deterministic $O(a)$-word index. After $O(a)$ preprocessing it answers in $O(1)$ time whether cutting any specified positive edge and making its two endpoints intact gives a satisfiable formula. All $a$ verdicts consequently take $O(a)$ word steps *in total*. By allowing many cuts on one file, the same matching geometry can realize arbitrary proper 3-SAT in linear encoded length. There is also a one-cut SAT reduction if whole intact squares are allowed elsewhere in the second layer. These distinctions concern which other centers carry intact guards. [Definition of the sheet constraints](two-missing-guards-in-a-sheeted-sat-formula.md) · [Single-cut characterization](singleton-relay-matching-cuts.md)

## Constant time per cut

Normalize the first matchings to identity. The logical bit $x_i$ of left sheet $i$ complements its right physical partner. In layer two, let $p,q$ be the restored positive and negative permutations from left to right and let $\tau=p^{-1}q$ be an involution. An ordinary second-layer center uses $P(x,b,c)=\neg x\lor b\lor\neg c$. Define a descent at $i$ by $x_i=1$ and $x_{q(i)}=0$. The ordinary centers require the descent set to be a union of $\tau$-orbits. Say an edge label $i$ is eligible if the two tail indices $\{i,\tau(i)\}$ are disjoint from the two head indices $\{q(i),q(\tau(i))\}$. This depends only on the restored layout.

Traverse each directed $q$-cycle once, recording a cycle identifier, a position, and prefix counts of eligible labels. To cut the positive edge at $u$, write $v=\tau(u)$, so its other endpoint is the right sheet $p(u)=q(v)$. The four pins are $x_u=x_{q(u)}=1$ and $x_v=x_{q(v)}=0$. Different $q$-cycles for $u,v$ admit a constant assignment on each cycle. Coincident opposite pins reject. Otherwise the only directed arc that must contain a descent runs along the $q$-cycle from $q(u)$ up to $v$. Its edge tails form the cyclic half-open interval $[q(u),v)$.

The prefix counts give its number of eligible labels by at most two subtractions and one addition. A prospective descent conflicts with one of the four pins only if its tail is in
$$F=\{q^{-1}(u),u,v,q(v)\}.$$

To check its entire orbit, exclude $F\cup\tau(F)$. Subtract each distinct eligible excluded label inside the interval exactly once. The answer is SAT precisely when the remaining count is positive. Indeed a satisfying assignment has an eligible orbit meeting this arc, and a single such orbit suffices: remove its descent edges from the $q$ cycles, set the tails to one and heads to zero, and extend nondecreasingly on the remaining edges. The only required change from one to zero between consecutive original pins has already been crossed. Both query branches, wrap-around, the four endpoint tests, and the at most eight exclusions take constant time. An eligible-label array per cycle also recovers a qualifying orbit by examining the first nine eligible labels on the arc, or all of them if fewer. A full witness can then be printed with a further scan.

The executable compares the indexed answer with direct evaluation of both projected center constraints for every cut, for every normalized layout through $a=6$. At size six there are 328,320 queries, of which 133,920 are satisfiable. This counts different choices of the cut on a shared input layout. [Index and exhaustive check](all_matching_cuts_audit.py)

## Several cut queries on a preprocessed layout

Suppose a query gives a list of $b$ positive edges to cut. At each selected edge both positive ports become fixed and both guards become intact. All other second-layer centers use $P$. More generally let $t$ be the number of nontrivial first relative positions and $k$ the number of second relative positions on cycles longer than two. There is an $O(a)$ preprocessing pass, followed by deterministic $2^{O(b+t+k+1)}$ time per query, with no scan of the layout for an individual query. This statement uses the sparse edit list as query input. Reading an independently presented ASCII file still costs its parsing time.

Precompute $q$-cycle coordinates, inverse permutations, eligible-label prefix counts and select arrays as above, omitting all $k$ exceptional labels from the eligible lists. Store the $t$ exceptional first positions with their equalities, and both endpoints of the $k$ exceptional edges. A query imposes pins at the two ends of each of its positive and negative blocks. At a cut $u$ its negative block is $v,q(v)$ with $v=q^{-1}p(u)$, valid also when $\tau$ has long cycles. Altogether at most $m=4b+t+2k$ indices are marked. Guess their bits, reject conflicting pins and first-layer equalities, and test both partner requirements of each exceptional second-layer descent. These exceptional requirements involve only marked endpoints. Fixed exceptional descents are now known. Sorting the $m$ pins by their stored cyclic positions gives the directed arcs between consecutive pins. Each required arc starts at one and ends at zero. Remove those covered by fixed descents. There are $r\le m$ left. [Descent realizability with arbitrary pins and exceptional edges](singleton-relay-matching-cuts.md)

For each remaining arc keep up to $4(r-1)+1$ distinct eligible short orbits consistent with the pins, or all if fewer. To produce this list one may inspect the first $2(2m+4r+1)$ eligible labels on the arc. At most $2m$ orbits touch any marked vertex through an entering or leaving edge. Each orbit appears on at most two labels. All other eligible orbits are consistent with the pins and with the fixed descents, since both endpoints of each fixed descent are marked. Cycle prefix counts and select arrays retrieve the specified initial labels in time polynomial in $m+1$. No long arc needs to be scanned. If $r=0$, test the fixed descents alone.

This leaves $O((m+1)^2)$ candidates. To see sufficiency, begin with a solution and designate one of its short orbits for each unmet arc. Different arcs may designate the same orbit. If the candidate for a particular arc is absent from its retained list, replace that slot by a representative from its list. Each of the at most $r-1$ other choices rules out at most four orbits, since a descent only conflicts with a descent immediately next to it on a $q$-cycle. Reusing an identical choice is allowed. The new choice covers the designated arc and is compatible with every other choice, even when two slots previously held the same orbit. Iterate over the slots. Conversely a compatible cover realizes exactly its chosen and fixed descents by the arc test. To find such a cover in single-exponential time, enumerate the explicit colorings used in the square normal form on these $O((m+1)^2)$ candidates. A solution of size at most $r$ can have all its chosen vertices colored red and their at most $4r$ neighbors colored blue. The isolated red candidates are mutually compatible, so their union must cover every required arc for at least one coloring. Construction and enumeration cost $2^{O(m+1)}$ across all pin guesses. The executable uses direct enumeration for the final small kernel. It agrees with direct projection on all 221,184 layout/subset combinations at four positions (all three normalized permutations arbitrary), every smaller size, and 1,800 mixed-defect queries on larger layouts. [Deterministic coloring construction](doubled-relay-square-normal-form.md)

## A fixed layout that selects arbitrary subformulas

A linear reduction shows what can happen when the number of selected edges grows. It uses coincident first-layer matchings, only genuine four-sheet squares in the restored second layer, and $q$-cycles of lengths 1, 4 and 6. Each selectable edge has two permanent intact anchor centers in the positive matching, both when it is cut and when it is crossed. Every other second-layer center is regular. Two designated positive identifier bits select a clause while the guard clauses stay fixed. Alternatively, when generating just the query instance, both guards can be left regular at unselected edges, giving the selected-only convention of the preceding algorithm as well.

Start with clauses of width one to three in which each name occurs at most twice per sign. For each variable make a four-cycle of $q$ whose edges, in cyclic order, stand for the slots $+_1,-_1,+_2,-_2$. Distinct selected sockets can be descents simultaneously exactly when they all have the same sign. Each positive edge is adjacent to each negative edge, and adjacent edges cannot both descend. Conversely mark the tails of any one-sign selection one and leave all other cycle bits zero. Its descent set is exactly the selected slots. For ordinary proper 3-SAT the occurrence limit follows by copying each occurrence and linking the copies of a name by a cyclic chain of binary implications. Each clone is used in one source clause and at most once per sign in the chain. The signed-socket conflict graph is the same literal-conflict idea used for independent transversals in colored interval graphs. Here the matching circuits realize that selection by directed descents. [Tovey on bounded occurrence](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7) · [Bhore et al., Section 2.1](https://www1.pub.informatik.uni-wuerzburg.de/eurocg2020/data/uploads/papers/eurocg20_paper_66.pdf)

For each clause create a six-cycle of $q$ with edge tails, in cyclic order,
$$u,\ c_1,\ c_2,\ c_3,\ v,\ z.$$
Pair $u$ to $v$ in $\tau$. Pair each real $c_i$ to the variable socket assigned to its literal. Pair every still unused edge, including $z$, unused variable sockets and the surplus $c_i$ of shorter clauses, to its own fresh $q$-loop in $\tau$. Since a loop cannot descend, none of these unused edges can descend. All $\tau$-orbits have two distinct elements. Set $p=q\tau$, giving the required matching permutations. The selectable edge for this clause goes from left $u$ to right $q(v)$.

At its two permanent intact anchors, restoring the edge imposes exactly
$$x_v\le x_{q(v)}\le x_u\le x_{q(u)}.$$
Cutting imposes $x_v=x_{q(v)}=0$ and $x_u=x_{q(u)}=1$. Both choices make the ordinary $P$ implications true at the two anchors. Thus any solution obeys descent equality on each $\tau$ pair. In an active clause at least one of $c_1,c_2,c_3$ must descend, since they are precisely the arc between the one-block and the zero-block. The paired literal socket descends as well. Across the selected clauses these chosen literals have compatible signs on every variable, so an assignment satisfies them all. [Lean check of the anchors and sockets](AllMatchingCuts.lean)

Conversely choose one true literal of each selected clause. In the corresponding six-cycle start $u,q(u)$ at one, take the unique descent at that literal's $c_i$, keep the zero-block at zero, and return from zero to one across $z$. Use constant zero on every unselected six-cycle, which satisfies its restored intact anchors. Put the selected socket descents on each variable cycle, and constant zero on each filler loop. The resulting descent set is a union of $\tau$ pairs, with the required pins and restored-anchor inequalities. Local private bits extend independently. This proves that every subset of selectable edges has exactly the SAT verdict of the corresponding subformula. With clone-implication edges always selected, the assertion holds for arbitrary subsets of the original proper clauses as well.

Each occurrence adds a bounded number of sheets and rows. After removing duplicate source clauses by dense-name radix sorting, $s$ distinct clauses occupy $\Omega(s\log(s+2))$ input bits, and the new indices have $O(\log(s+2))$ bits. Thus for the all-selected reduction the encoded output length and deterministic construction time are $O(N+1)$ on an $N$-bit input. Assign the two endpoints of the $j$th selectable edge the decimal identifiers $10j+2$ and $10j+3$, filling the remaining names densely. The two physical identifier substitutions for a cut each change exactly one ASCII bit. Thus distinct cut subsets $A,B$ yield equal-length files at exactly $2|A\mathbin{\triangle} B|$ bits of distance. The zero-clause case may use a constant true file. CaDiCaL agreed with direct source evaluation on 572 emitted DIMACS files, including six unsatisfiable files. These cover all 256 subformulas on both a three-variable sign cube and a seven-variable minimally unsatisfiable tree, plus 60 further clause subsets. The audit checks the physical Hamming distance on every file. [Raw DIMACS compiler and checks](all_matching_cuts_audit.py)

## One cut with intact background squares

If whole intact squares are also permitted in the second-layer background, one designated edit suffices for a linear reduction even with coincident first matchings. Start from the doubled-relay square normal form and split each old sheet into two physical copies. Put the same-side copy in its old second-layer square. Put the opposite-side copy in its old first-layer square, swapping the positive and negative matchings throughout those intact squares. Give the new first layer coincident matching edges between the two copies of each old sheet. These force complementary physical values. Complementing all three physical values and exchanging the partner ports preserves $I(x,a,b)=(x\lor a)\land(\neg x\lor\neg b)$. Thus the split layout has exactly the old projected decision problem. The old two edited centers and their positive entries stay in place on the direct copies. There are twice as many sheets as before and every restored second-layer component is a genuine square. If $h$ background squares have intact guards, guessing their $O(h)$ endpoint values in the index argument gives $2^{O(b+t+k+h+1)}$ query time. This last bound treats all deviations from the regular guard on those squares as additional pinned positions, checking their local constraints directly. [Square normal form and reduction cost](doubled-relay-square-normal-form.md)

The reductions show which search problem would suffice for the unrestricted time target. They give no clock growth on a particular execution. In the homogeneous one-cut case, summing the scan cost over possible cuts overstates the work of one algorithm even when all their verdicts are requested.
