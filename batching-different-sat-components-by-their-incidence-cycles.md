# Batching different SAT components by their incidence cycles

Let $N$ be the bit length of a proper ternary ASCII DIMACS formula and $h=\lfloor\log_2(N+2)\rfloor$. Duplicate signed clauses can be removed. In the variable–clause incidence graph let $B$ range over the biconnected blocks, with a bridge counted as a block. Write $r_B=|E(B)|-|V(B)|+1$, the number of edges outside a spanning tree of the block.

There is one deterministic logarithmic-word RAM that decides the file and returns a satisfying assignment on its occurring variables in $O(N)$ steps whenever
$$
         \max_B r_B\log_2(r_B+2)\le c h
$$
for a sufficiently small absolute $c>0$. The blocks, spanning trees and satisfying assignments are all found by the program. The number of clauses, variables, or cycles in a connected component can be arbitrarily large. The block topologies, all signs and all variable names may differ.

An alternative clock on the same files is
$$
                    O\!\left(N+\sum_B(r_B+1)2^{r_B}\right).                 \tag{1}
$$
In particular, $\max_B r_B\le h+\log_2(h+2)+O(1)$ suffices for $O(N^2)$. Both statements apply to actual native instructions and permit unlimited workspace. The local algorithms use classical conditioning and variable elimination. The point here is the single shared preprocessing allowance and the encoded-length thresholds it gives for batches of arbitrary different kernels. [Dechter, *Bucket elimination*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(99)00059-4) · [Kschischang, Frey and Loeliger, *Factor graphs and the sum-product algorithm*](https://www.isiweb.ee.ethz.ch/papers/arch/aloe-2001-1.pdf)

## Recovering the partition and its constraints

Intern the decimal names by a digit trie, with leading zeros ignored if admitted by the syntax. Use dense indices, sort the three literals of a clause, and deduplicate signed triples by a constant number of stable counting-sort passes. Very long names and header fields can be compared as digit strings. These steps take $O(N)$. With $m$ distinct clauses, $m\log(m+2)=O(N)$: take one physical occurrence per distinct clause and count strings over a fixed alphabet up to any given length. Equivalently, at least half of $m$ distinct signed triples must have a name of $\Omega(\log m)$ digits. This gives $m=O(1+N/h)$, by splitting at $m=\sqrt N$. Build adjacency lists and run the linear-time biconnected decomposition. The total number of vertices and edges over all blocks, counting repeated articulation vertices, is $O(m)$. [Physical normalization](semantic-patches-for-finite-sat-suites.md) · [Tarjan, *Depth-first search and linear graph algorithms*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0201010)

Regard each variable node as having states 0 and 1. A clause node has a three-bit state giving the actual values of its three neighboring variables; its unary domain excludes the single falsifying assignment. Each incidence edge checks equality of the variable state with one specified slot of the clause state. This is a binary constraint problem of domain size at most eight. Every satisfying assignment has exactly one set of compatible clause states.

Root each block-cut tree. A child block sends the set of extendible states of its parent articulation, at most eight bits; intersect all messages at an articulation. Unary constraints on vertices can be repeated in every incident block. For a given state of a block's parent articulation intersect its unary domain with that singleton and solve the block. At most eight such calls per block suffice. In particular an articulation may itself be a clause; its shared three-bit state records the same triple at every incident block. Conditional on the articulation state, the child blocks have independent remaining vertices. A second top-down pass retrieves compatible states and hence a satisfying assignment.

## A small kernel for the cyclic part of one block

Choose a spanning tree and mark both endpoints of each of its $r$ non-tree edges. Keep the non-tree edges separately, with their original slot tests intact. On the tree alone remove an unmarked leaf by projecting its unary domain through its incident binary relation and intersecting the result into the neighboring domain. At an unmarked vertex $x$ of degree two replace the two incident relations by one relation between its neighbors. A pair $p,q$ is permitted precisely when some state $t$ in the current domain of $x$ satisfies both old relations on $p,t,q$. A single isolated unmarked vertex contributes the scalar test that its domain is nonempty. Each deletion preserves the exact set of feasible states on all surviving vertices. Store one local witness per feasible endpoint tuple to reverse the deletions when constructing a model.

A queue of unmarked vertices of current degree at most two, and linked half-edges with back pointers, implement the deletions in linear time. All relation tables have at most 64 entries. The tree never acquires parallel edges. With at least one non-tree edge, the final tree has at most $2r$ marked vertices, every leaf is marked, and every unmarked vertex has degree at least three. A tree with $t\ge2$ leaves has at most $t-2$ vertices of degree at least three. Thus the retained tree has at most $4r-2$ vertices. With $r=0$ the scalar test suffices. The closed kernel consists of this tree with its current domains and relations and the original $r$ slot checks. Unrelated outside components and the order in which their records appear have no role in the reduction.

For (1), enumerate the $2^r$ possibilities for the variable values on the non-tree edges. Each such bit pins the original variable endpoint and the named coordinate of its original clause endpoint. Inconsistent pins simply fail. The remaining tree has $O(r+1)$ nodes and is evaluated for that profile in $O(r+1)$ time. The old edges that were composed into arbitrary binary relations stay in the tree: assigning a single bit to an arbitrary binary relation would be unjustified. Depth-first enumeration and backtracking suffice. The state counters need only polynomial space in the input size even on a file with large $r$. Since $\sum_B(r_B+1)=O(m)$, (1) gives the quadratic statement.

For the linear clock, give the kernel vertices dense local names and encode their unary domains, the tree endpoint pairs with their constant-size relations, and the original slot chords. Use $O((r+1)\log(r+2))$ bits including dimensions and flags. Under the stated cap, there is a fixed format whose padded keys have at most $h/3$ bits, for all sufficiently large lengths after decreasing $c$. Build one direct table for every key at this input scale, check its syntax, and evaluate each well-formed kernel by enumerating profiles. The build takes at most $2^{h/3+O(h/\log h)}\operatorname{poly}(h)=O(N)$ word operations. Store one model on the kernel on successful keys; its $O(r)$ bits fit in a constant number of words. Assemble each actual key by shifts and field writes in $O(r+1)$ operations, skipping zero padding without looping over unused slots. One lookup then answers the call; the top-down pass reverses the stored eliminations. On larger kernels use the exact procedure (1). Thus the program remains globally correct. For disconnected files one may also report every component's verdict and a model for each positive component, even if another component is negative.

The constructive saving for a batch is over paying the same exhaustive conditional search independently on each kernel. For $t$ different kernels of comparable cycle rank $r$, that explicit enumeration budgets $O(tr2^r)$ operations. Once the table has been built, the corresponding evaluations use $O(tr)$. This is a comparison of two specified algorithms; it gives no lower bound on the optimal standalone SAT algorithm. Older component memoization charges at least once per *observed type* in its displayed clock. Here the lookup applies even if every actual kernel differs. ## Why the lossless kernel key costs order $r\log r$

Take three left and three right classes of $j$ named vertices each. For each of the nine class pairs choose an arbitrary perfect matching, independently. The resulting simple 3-regular bipartite graph has $6j$ vertices and $9j$ edges. There are $(j!)^9$ distinct labeled graphs, and at least $(j!)^9/(6j)!=2^{\Omega(j\log j)}$ types up to arbitrary vertex renaming.

Almost all these graph choices are connected. In a component, every class contributes the same number of vertices, since the matching at every pair of adjacent classes induces a bijection between their contributions. Disconnection therefore requires six $k$-subsets of the respective classes, for some $1\le k\le j/2$, that all nine matchings preserve. The probability for a fixed six-tuple is $\binom jk^{-9}$, while there are $\binom jk^6$ tuples. Summing gives failure probability $O(j^{-2})$. There are still $2^{\Omega(j\log j)}$ connected types after arbitrary vertex renaming.

Introduce anchors $U,V,W$ forced to $0,0,0$ by the seven proper clauses excluding the other assignments. For every graph edge $xy$ use the clause $(\neg x,\neg y,W)$. All graph variables, edge clauses and $W$ form one biconnected incidence block. Removing $W$ leaves the connected subdivision of the original graph. Removing a graph variable or edge clause leaves every surviving edge clause joined to $W$ and every surviving graph variable joined to at least two edge clauses. This block has $27j$ incidence edges, $15j+1$ vertices, and cycle rank $12j$. Every vertex has block degree at least three. An unmarked vertex has all its original edges in the spanning tree and thus has tree degree at least three. Consequently there is no initial tree reduction, regardless of the spanning tree. The block retains its graph, which can also be recovered from the truth table under paired vertex pins: pinning two graph variables true is infeasible exactly on its edges, because every other graph variable can be false.

Thus lossless keys for these individual block graphs, or keys that answer all such pair pins up to vertex renaming, require $\Omega(r\log r)$ bits on some inputs. The unpinned formulas are all satisfiable. This establishes sharpness for the indicated representations; a decision-only summary is a different target.

## A separate dense lookup for components with few variables

For a component on $v$ locally indexed variables let $M=\binom v3$. A normalized formula is a mask of $8M$ possible signed clauses. A fixed assignment satisfies exactly $7M$ of them. Generate all submasks of these $7M$ clauses for every assignment, storing the mask and the assignment that generated it. This constructs a dictionary of just the positive masks and may generate the same mask more than once. If $K$ is the largest integer with
$$
                         K+7\binom K3\le h,
$$
one uniform $O(N)$ algorithm returns the verdict of *every* connected component with at most $K$ variables and one assignment on each positive component. Arbitrarily many signed clauses per component and arbitrary interleaving are allowed.

Generate the dictionary for each size $v\le K$. For fixed $v,z$, list the permitted clauses in polynomial time in $v$ and recursively include or omit each of them. Generate the submasks in constant amortized word operations and emit a positive record per leaf, including the all-zero mask. The total number of records including repetitions is $O(2^{K+7\binom K3})=O(N)$, summing a geometrically increasing bound. Assignment initialization over all $z,v$ costs $2^{O(K)}\operatorname{poly}(K)=o(N)$. A mask uses at most $8\binom K3=O(h)$ bits and occupies a constant number of machine words. Renumber the actual variables locally in each component, rank their signed triples (or use a precomputed index for triples), and set one bit per occurring clause. Build indices on touched variable names only. Combining the program-generated dictionary and the actual component records in a deterministic radix sort by $(v,\mathrm{mask})$ answers every request. A constant number of passes on digits of at most half $h$ bits costs $O(N)$. One present positive record suffices for a model; absence is an exact negative answer. This avoids a binary search or a scan of a $v^3$-bit string for every small component. A general solver handles components outside the cap.

The exponent is sharp for a dictionary that explicitly records each *positive labeled mask*. Two different assignments disagree on at least one variable. The intersection of their allowed clauses has size at most $7M-\binom{v-1}{2}$, because on every triple containing that variable they forbid different sign patterns. Inclusion–exclusion through pairs therefore bounds the count $D_v$ by
$$
 2^{v+7M}-\binom{2^v}{2}2^{7M-\binom{v-1}{2}}
 \ \le D_v\ \le 2^{v+7M}.
$$
The lower and upper bounds have ratio tending to one. By comparison a full literal clause-mask table has $2^{8M}$ entries. The savings can be exponential in $v^3$ while admitting every labeled component, with no restriction on graph structure. This counting statement applies to the described dictionary. It does not exclude a more concise representation of the SAT verdict.
