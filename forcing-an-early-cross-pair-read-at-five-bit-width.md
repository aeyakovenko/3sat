# A five-step residual clock at the first wider selector lengths

At 18 independent selector bits there are nine variables. In a **word-aligned sign-plane layout**, the four five-bit words are positive selectors 1–5, positive selectors 6–9, negative selectors 1–5 and negative selectors 6–9. Bit 4 of each short word is fixed zero. There are 18 selector bits and 20 physical bits. At 20 selectors there are ten variables and all four five-bit words are full. These word-aligned and tightly concatenated layouts coincide at 20. The machine uses five-bit words, 32 mutable cells, arbitrary fixed-label code, and the same 20 word operations and binary comparisons as in the four-bit clock. Shift counts with wrapping are reduced modulo five. [Four-bit predecessor](exact-nine-step-clock-for-four-sat-words.md)

The nine-instruction program for four words still applies. Replace the test for the first negative mask being all ones by a test against 31. The second all-ones value is 15 for nine variables and 31 for ten. Verification over all 262,144 and 1,048,576 files gives respectively 19,682 and 59,048 satisfiable files. For a verdict-preserving injection of the eight-variable problem, put the new variable in position 5 and force it false by the two fixed unit selectors $(0,1)$. At ten variables insert a second forced-false variable in position 10. The resulting physical words are $(P,Q,16+M,N)$ or $(P,Q,16+M,16+N)$ for old nibbles $P,Q,M,N$. No bit of the padding depends on the verdict. The exact nine-step optimum at length 16 and the nine-step upper bound at these successor lengths exclude a strictly increasing worst-case clock for this extension.

The tight 18-bit encoding parses differently. Its four physical words hold variables 1–5 positive, 6–9 positive followed by negative 1, negative 2–6, and negative 7–9. Thus the first negative selector crosses a physical boundary relative to word alignment. Appending a fixed zero bit to the old 16-bit selector string gives a 17-bit instance with 16 effective input bits, using a third layout. For the two tight encodings there is a 15-step direct upper bound. Write $W_0,W_1,W_2,W_3$ for the physical words and set $v=8$ for the padded 17-bit case and $v=9$ for the full 18-bit case. After four loads, use two shifts and an OR per negative mask to form $M=(W_2\ll(10-v))\mathbin{\vert}(W_1\gg(v-5))$ and $N=(W_3\ll(10-v))\mathbin{\vert}(W_2\gg(v-5))$ in five-bit arithmetic. The low $v-5$ bits of $W_1$ are the second positive mask. Extra bits there vanish when taking its AND with $N$. The same remaining five operations suffice. The audit checks all 65,536 files of the padded 17-bit layout and all 262,144 tightly packed 18-bit files. Their eight-step feasibility remains unproved.

## One live pair with arbitrary fixed advice

**Theorem.** Let two distinct addresses among $0,1,2,3$ hold $a,b$. Every other memory cell and every initial register may hold an arbitrary constant independent of these two words. Over all such initializations and ordinary five-bit native programs, the minimum worst-case time to decide the following two-word predicate is exactly **five instructions**, for each displayed domain.

| Encoded pair | Physical words | Acceptance predicate |
|---|---|---|
| Four raw bits per mask | $a=p,\ b=m$ | $p\,\&\,m=0$ and $m\ne15$ |
| Four raw bits and a forced-false variable | $a=p,\ b=16+m$ | $p\,\&\,m=0$ and $m\ne15$ |
| Five raw bits per mask | $a=p,\ b=m$ | $p\,\&\,m=0$ and $m\ne31$ |

In each case a five-step algorithm loads $b$, rejects at its maximum physical value, loads $a$, computes $a\,\&\,b$ and accepts exactly when the result is zero. For the lower bound, take the eight pivotal positive inputs with $4\le p\le11$ and $m=15-p$. Changing either live cell can reverse the answer. On each of these inputs each live cell must be read before it is first overwritten.

Suppose the first binary comparison occurs before instruction four in a four-step solver. On every accepting path containing a selected positive there are at most two comparisons. When there are two, both other instructions must be fresh reads. When there is just one comparison, the positive's two fresh reads must both precede that comparison. A verdict fixed at one comparison cannot depend on later data computations if it reaches no further comparison. Thus the relevant comparisons in both cases use only the two original words and literals. The reads on these paths have literal addresses. A first raw value in $4,\ldots,11$ or $20,\ldots,27$ cannot address the other cell. On the high band of the two inputs these same word tables describe the whole path. Across all signed and unsigned raw comparisons with the 32 constants, every intersection of two branch outcomes avoiding the high-band negatives covers at most one of the eight targets, including the case of a repeated guard. At most four leaves of the binary tree at comparison depth at most two can contain targets. This misses at least four required positives.

Suppose instead that the first comparison occurs at step four. The same three earlier instruction labels execute on every file. Its outcome must determine the complete predicate, since all files finish within four instructions. At least two data instructions must deliver the two fresh original loads on each pivotal positive. Constant computations, moves and literal scratch reads only provide public constants. Check the other possibilities.

* With one arithmetic operation and two direct live reads, grant the ALU both inputs and all constants, and compare its result to any raw word or constant in both signed and unsigned orders. The exhaustive scalar test covers every permitted operation, operand choice and branch outcome. There are respectively 943, 866 and 1,241 distinct result tables on the three full pair domains, and no exact predicate. Comparing two raw values is included by an identity operation. If an arithmetic result supplies the second fresh address, that result is a function only of the first input. For every first raw value on the high band its complementary positive requires the other cell. Hence the computed address is constant there, and the final comparison reduces to raw values on that band.
* With one write and two loads, both reads on the selected positives are literal originals. On the entire high band a previous store address is either fixed or a high raw value. A high value misses both candidate input addresses. A fixed address cannot hit a necessary load before its first read on a positive. The final high-band predicate again uses raw values.
* With three loads, a constant scratch read yields only an immediate. When the extra load reads through a high original value it may return an arbitrary unary function of that word because fixed scratch initialization is free. If that value supplies the address of the missing original, pivotality forces it to be the same required original address throughout the high band. Otherwise the final comparison may use a unary table of one original against the other original. For first input 5, the acceptance row on the second raw band $4,\ldots,11$ is exactly $\{8,10\}$ (translated by 16 in the padded domain). For second raw input 5 the acceptance column has the same two separated values. Neither set can be obtained by equality, disequality or a signed or unsigned initial or final interval against a single threshold. Ignoring either variable also fails.

This exhausts the three instructions before the first comparison and proves the lower bound. An arbitrary number of fixed scratch words can encode a table of a single input, which is why the row and column argument is necessary. **Consequence for eight-step solvers.** Fix either mask pair at the exceptional value having zero positive mask and all-one negative mask. On that fiber the other mask pair must decide the two-word predicate above. If a globally eight-step solver took its first four instructions without reading either of the remaining two physical words, the instruction path and the resulting advice would be independent of both. It cannot have overwritten an unread remaining word, since both are essential on this fiber. The remaining four steps would solve the displayed predicate from an arbitrary fixed initialization, a contradiction. Thus every putative eight-step solver must already read the other pair by instruction four on each exceptional fiber. This condition applies both on the full word-aligned catalogs and on the specified forced-false subcatalogs.

**Shared last comparison.** If the first comparison on any target occurs at the time limit, all earlier instruction labels are shared across all inputs. If both successors contain targets, both must accept immediately to meet the limit, contradicting the existence of an input that must reject. Hence all targets must take a single accepting outcome. This replaces a per-leaf support cutoff by a requirement to cover every target for the special cases where the first comparison exhausts the full clock.
