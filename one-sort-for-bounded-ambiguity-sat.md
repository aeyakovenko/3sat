# One sort for SAT with boundedly many assignments

Suppose a fixed-width binary proper 3-CNF has at most two satisfying assignments on its occurring variables. Its exact SAT bit has a trivalent binary-wire tensor network over **every field** costing one sort of the literal occurrences with two payload bits per record, plus linear overhead in the input bits. Over the integers the contraction is already the decision bit. Requiring a unique accepting certificate from a verifier with only membership access to the set of assignments has a very different cost. On a universe of size $M$ with at most two marked elements, it needs exactly $\lfloor M/2\rfloor+1$ membership queries per certificate ($M\ge2$). The exact bound for all ambiguity promises follows below.

## Routing two assignments together

Let $L$ be the number of literal occurrences and $b$ the number of bits in an identifier. Guess two value bits at each occurrence. Before sorting, test that each of the two columns satisfies each clause, using its three signs. Sort all records by identifier, carrying **both** bits along the very same compare-exchanges. Equal keys need no tie-break field: a fixed comparator chooses a deterministic order on a tie. Adjacent records of equal name must agree in both payloads. This makes the surviving columns exactly the satisfying assignments on the occurring names, each with one consistent pattern of guesses. Unused declared names can be ignored for decision, and a promise on all declared assignments also bounds their number after projection.

Read the columns in sorted order. Equal columns have weight $+1$, a strictly lexicographically increasing pair has weight $-1$, and a descending pair has weight zero. Repetitions of a name within a column do not affect the comparison. With $m$ satisfying assignments the contraction is
$$m-\binom m2.$$
At $m=0,1,2$ its values are $0,1,1$. At $m=3$ it is zero. The minus sign and the plus sign coincide over $\mathbb F_2$; the positive instance with two solutions has three contributing configurations in that field. This gives an exact decision without choosing a unique assignment.

More generally, for a public cap $K\ge1$ use $K$ payload columns, one prefix of $j$ active columns, $1\le j\le K$, and constrain each inactive occurrence bit to zero. Test active columns as above and require strict increase between successive active assignments. Give the configuration weight $(-1)^{j+1}$. Each nonempty subset of at most $K$ satisfying assignments has exactly one encoding. By the binomial theorem the result is $\sum_{j=1}^K(-1)^{j+1}\binom mj=[m>0]$ whenever $m\le K$. At $K=1$ this also covers unambiguous SAT. Counting ordered witnesses and passing to modular acceptance are established techniques. The size observation here is that all columns share the key comparisons and the physical routes. [Beigel and Gill](https://cis.temple.edu/~beigel/papers/bg-mods-tcs.html) · [Earlier sorted-subset calculation](matrix-witness-divisibility.md)

For a sorting network with $S(L)$ comparators the trivalent volume is $O((b+K)(S(L)+L))$. A comparator computes its swap decision in $O(b)$ Boolean gates and moves both keys and all payload bits with $O(b+K)$ gates and copies. Clause tests, adjacency tests and streaming lexicographic comparisons fit the remaining cost. Use Boolean gate and equality tensors with unique internal evaluations, and a scalar weight on the selected length. Guess bits occur exactly once as summed indices, with explicit copies for multiple uses. Padding to the next power of two can use a dedicated dummy tag. This analysis fixes the binary field boundaries of the input; an arbitrary ASCII parser would require separate accounting. The finite audit also uses an original-position tie breaker for ease of inspection. [Network convention and circuit translation](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md) · [Two-column sorting and contraction audit](bounded_ambiguity_sort_audit.py)

This use of $K$ witness checks is sharp for an **exact scalar contraction with only membership access**. Fix any $K$ possible assignments and allow an arbitrary subset to satisfy. On these independent membership bits the required output is OR. A signed sum of adaptive terminating branches testing at most $r$ memberships per branch has multilinear degree at most $r$, since every transcript contributes a product of at most $r$ literals. OR has degree $K$ over every field. This proves $r\ge K$ in that oracle model. The $K$-column construction attains this witness-check count when clause satisfaction is treated as the membership operation. Its explicit CNF implementation shares one sort.

## Exact cost of unambiguous membership certificates

Take a universe $[M]=\{0,\ldots,M-1\}$ with $M\ge1$ and a hidden set $W$, promised $|W|\le K\le M$. A deterministic verifier receives an arbitrary certificate and can ask adaptive questions of the form $i\in W$. Require zero accepting certificates on the empty set and **exactly one** on every nonempty promised set. Local computation and certificate length are free. Then the optimal worst-case number of queries is
$$
q(M,1)=1,\qquad
q(M,K)=\max\left\{K,\left\lceil\frac{M+2\lfloor K/2\rfloor-1}{2}\right\rceil\right\}
\quad(2\le K\le M).
$$
The lower bound holds already for a cap on accepting transcripts. It also holds for nonnegative weighted sums of accepting transcript indicators computing the promised decision bit exactly. This last model is the conical junta or nonnegative literal degree model. These are black-box costs; a verifier given a CNF can inspect its clauses. [Unambiguous certificate complexity](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2015/200/revision/1/download/) · [Nonnegative literal degree and computation in expectation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.7280)

**Upper bound.** In ascending order scan the elements of $W$, initially expecting either even or odd parity. Keep a matching element and switch the expected parity after each keep. If there are $r$ successive parity runs in a nonempty $W$, starting with the first element's parity keeps the first element of all $r$ runs. The other start skips the first run and keeps the first element of the remaining $r-1$. Exactly one kept list has odd length. Use that sorted list $S$ as the sole certificate, with one canonical encoding of each list.

Verify that $S$ is nonempty, odd, of size $s\le K$, and its element parities alternate. Query all of $S$ positively. With $a$ the parity of its first element, forbid an index $i\notin S$ precisely when
$$i\bmod2=a\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}\bigl(|\{x\in S:x<i\}|\bmod2\bigr).$$
Query the forbidden indices negatively. If they all pass, scanning $W$ with initial expectation $a$ keeps precisely $S$: each marked element outside $S$ has the wrong parity at its turn. Conversely the genuine kept list passes all tests. On the complement of $S$ the forbidden indices occupy every other position, since $i-|\{x\in S:x<i\}|$ is their zero-based rank there. At most $s+\lceil(M-s)/2\rceil$ queries suffice. If $s=K$, positive answers already imply $W=S$ and the negative queries can be omitted. For $K\ge2$ the largest possible odd $s<K$ is $2\lfloor K/2\rfloor-1$. For $K=1$ only the single positive query is needed.

There is explicit prior art for this upper construction. Biró, Howard, Keller, Trotter and Young partitioned the nonempty Boolean lattice in odd dimension into intervals with exactly this profile. Their Theorem 2.1 gives odd lower endpoints of size $s$ and upper endpoints of size $(M+s)/2$. Their cyclic-gap rule in Lemma 4.6 gives the kept set after reversing the cyclic order. The truncation at $s=K$ uses the promise. Priority for the matching lower bound in this oracle formulation has not been established. [Biró et al., Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 4.6](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/121.pdf)

**Lower bound.** A terminating accepting transcript is a conjunction of queried literals. Discard transcripts which never accept any promised input. There are finitely many relevant accepting transcripts for an unambiguous verifier, since each is witnessed by one of finitely many promised inputs. Remove repeated queries. Their indicator sum is exactly OR on the Hamming ball of weights $0$ through $K$. Restrict to $K$ free coordinates: OR has multilinear degree $K$ there, by the nonzero full monomial in $1-\prod_i(1-x_i)$. Therefore $q\ge K$.

Assume $q\le M$ and pad each conjunction to exactly $q$ distinct literals by splitting on unqueried coordinates. Average this polynomial over permutations of the universe. If $m$ bits are marked and a random $q$-subset sees $J$ of them, the average is $\mathbb E[c_J]$ with some coefficients $c_0,\ldots,c_q\ge0$. Indeed the average of a width-$q$ conjunction with $j$ positives is $\Pr[J=j]/\binom qj$. The conditions at $m=0,\ldots,K$ determine $c_0,\ldots,c_K$ uniquely by triangularity, since $\Pr[J=m]>0$ there.

For $h\le K$, $\mathbb E\binom Jh=\binom mh\binom qh/\binom Mh$, by counting marked $h$-subsets in the sample. Expanding OR in the binomial basis consequently gives
$$
 c_j=\sum_{h=1}^j(-1)^{h+1}\binom jh\frac{\binom Mh}{\binom qh},\qquad j\le K.
$$
Multiply $1-c_j$ by $\binom qj$. The result is the coefficient of $z^j$ in $(1+z)^q(1-z/(1+z))^M=(1+z)^{q-M}$, by expanding the second factor before multiplication. For even $j\ge2$ and $q<M$, nonnegativity of $c_j$ requires $\binom{M-q+j-1}{j}\le\binom qj$, hence $2q\ge M+j-1$. For $q=M$ this inequality is automatic. Use $j=2\lfloor K/2\rfloor$ and combine with $q\ge K$. All inequalities hold with arbitrary nonnegative weights on conjunctions. The deterministic upper gives a weight-one partition attaining the same degree.

For $K=2$ there is also a short proof. The accepting run for singleton $i$ must query $i$. For each pair $i,j$, at least one of the two singleton accepting transcripts must query the other index; otherwise both transcripts survive on the pair. They cannot be the same transcript, because each detected a different positive index. Counting gives $M(q-1)\ge\binom M2$. Orienting the complete graph with maximum indegree $\lfloor M/2\rfloor$ attains the bound: certificate $i$ checks that it is marked and that none of its in-neighbors is marked. Lean checks the adaptive replay, double count and tournament construction. For general $K$ Lean checks uniqueness of the odd greedy scan and its replay from membership and forbidden-position checks, and the audit tests the certificate partition and symmetrized lower-bound computation on all 24,564 promised oracles through $M=11$, counting each $(M,K)$ separately. The general asymptotic-free equality above is a manuscript proof. For $v$ assignment bits and $K=2$, exact signed contraction used two guessed assignments; guaranteeing a single accepting certificate through membership alone costs $2^{v-1}+1$ tests when $v\ge1$. SAT circuits and word-RAM programs read formula syntax directly. Extending either lower bound to their unrestricted decision time requires another argument.
