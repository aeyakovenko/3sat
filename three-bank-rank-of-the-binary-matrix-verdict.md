# Three-bank tensor rank of the binary matrix verdict

For $d$-square binary matrices put $D=d^2$, $G(A,B,C)=[AB=C]$, and $g(A,B,Z)=\langle AB,Z\rangle\bmod 2$. For a coefficient field $F$, group the entire bit string in each input bank into one tensor leg. Its ordinary rank $R_F^{(3)}(f)$ is the minimum number of terms in an exact sum $\sum_s u_s(A)v_s(B)w_s(Z)$ of arbitrary field-valued functions. Write $R_F(M_n)$ for the ordinary rank of the formal side-$n$ matrix multiplication tensor over $F$.

* Over every fixed field $F$ of characteristic different from two, both $G$ and the bit $g$ have $\log_2 R_F^{(3)}=(\omega_F/2+o(1))D=(9/8+o(1))D$.
* Over every characteristic-two field, $R_F^{(3)}(g)=R_F(M_d)$ and an unrestricted exact binary-wire network for $g$ has volume $\Theta(d^2)$.
* For every field with $2\ne0$, a three-bank homogeneous trilinear Boolean function with an ordinary **linear-size circuit** attains $\log_2 R_F^{(3)}=(\omega_F/2+o(1))L$, where $L$ is the number of input bits per bank. Its explicit Walsh restriction gives an ordinary matrix multiplication tensor of side exponential in $L$.

The first bound applies to algorithms which separate the actual matrix inputs into three factors in each summand. The third statement tests using that bound as a network-volume lower bound. The upstream numerical exponent concerns formal matrix multiplication. Pospelov already proves the exponent $\omega/2$ for the invertible matrix group algebra over algebraically closed fields of characteristic zero. The argument here includes singular binary matrices and works over every fixed odd-characteristic coefficient field, including primes dividing the order of the group. Priority for that extension is unestablished. ## Every coefficient field of characteristic different from two

For $S=(-1)^g$, the Walsh transform of $G$ on its third bank is exactly $S$. This transform is invertible since $2\ne0$ in $F$. As $S=1-2g$ and the all-ones tensor is pure, the three-bank ranks of $G$ and $g$ differ by at most one. The entries $G(A,B,C)$ are the multiplication structure constants in the standard basis of $F[\operatorname{Mat}_d(\mathbb F_2)]$, the algebra of the multiplicative monoid of all binary matrices. A linear restriction of the tensor of a larger algebra implements a subalgebra's multiplication by including the two inputs and projecting the output back onto the subalgebra. Changes of basis carry no cost in this rank measure. The usual rank characterization gives $R_F(M_n)\le C_\eta n^{\omega_F+\eta}$ for every $\eta>0$ with a field-dependent constant, and $R_F(M_n)\ge n^{\omega_F}$ for $n\ge2$ by powering any fixed decomposition. [Strassen on asymptotic rank](https://doi.org/10.1145/2442829.2442832) · [Cohn and Umans on group algebra multiplication](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307321)

Write $H_t=\mathrm{GL}_t(2)$ and let $P_t$ be its subgroup fixing the last row covector. Its matrices have block form $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}g&v\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Thus $P_t=V\rtimes H_{t-1}$ with $V=\mathbb F_2^{t-1}$ and $[H_t:P_t]=2^t-1$. For $t\ge2$ there is an $F$-algebra isomorphism
$$
 F[P_t]\ \simeq\ F[H_{t-1}]\ \oplus\
 M_{\,2^{t-1}-1}(F[P_{t-1}]).
$$
To see this, form the orthogonal idempotents $e_u=|V|^{-1}\sum_{v\in V}(-1)^{u(v)}[v]$ for covectors $u\in V^*$. These sum to one, by binary character orthogonality. Left conjugation by $h\in H_{t-1}$ takes $e_u$ to $e_{h u}$ for the dual action. The basis elements $[h]e_u$ therefore multiply like arrows $u\longrightarrow hu$ with composition when endpoints agree and zero otherwise. There are two orbits. The zero orbit gives $F[H_{t-1}]$. For the nonzero orbit choose one base covector $u_0$ (the last row) and transports $s_u u_0=u$. Send an arrow $h:u\to v$ to the matrix unit $(v,u)$ carrying the group coefficient $s_v^{-1}hs_u\in P_{t-1}$. This maps the basis bijectively and preserves multiplication. The argument uses no division by the order of $H_{t-1}$.

Repeatedly taking the second component down to $P_1=\{1\}$ exhibits a full matrix algebra of order $\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(2^i-1)=2^{d(d-1)/2-O(1)}$ as a direct factor of $F[P_d]$. Restricting $G$ to three banks of $P_d$ gives the tensor of this group algebra. Its matrix factor yields the lower bound $\log_2 R_F^{(3)}(G)\ge\omega_F(d^2/2-O(d))$.

For the upper bound we need a sufficiently small faithful representation even when the group algebra has a nonzero radical. Left multiplication embeds $F[H_j]$ in $M_{2^j-1}(F[P_j])$, since the group algebra is a free right $F[P_j]$-module on the left coset representatives, and the action on the unit recovers each element. Inductively $F[P_j]$ embeds in a product of at most $2^j$ full $F$-matrix algebras, each of order at most $2^{j(j-1)/2}$. Then $F[H_j]$ embeds in the same number with orders at most $2^{j(j+1)/2}$. Indeed the $F[H_{j-1}]$ branch of the displayed decomposition for $P_j$ already has the required order bound. The second branch multiplies the preceding $P_{j-1}$ bound by at most $2^{j-1}$, giving the same exponent. The numbers of factors add across the two branches, starting with one at $j=1$.

Kovács's full-matrix-monoid algebra theorem, valid when the characteristic of the base finite field is invertible in the coefficient ring, now gives
$$
 F[\operatorname{Mat}_d(\mathbb F_2)]\ \simeq\
 \prod_{j=0}^d M_{\left[{d\atop j}\right]_2}\big(F[H_j]\big).
$$
Here $\left[{d\atop j}\right]_2$ counts the $j$-subspaces of $\mathbb F_2^d$. Its product formula gives $\left[{d\atop j}\right]_2\le C2^{j(d-j)}$ for a uniform constant $C$, using $\prod_{i\ge1}(1-2^{-i})>0$. Take $H_0$ trivial. Applying the preceding faithful embeddings gives at most $(d+1)2^d$ full matrix factors. Each has order at most $C 2^{j(d-j)+j(j+1)/2}\le C2^{d(d+1)/2}$. Input inclusion and an $F$-linear retraction on the image reduce monoid multiplication to these ordinary products. Summing their rank upper bounds and taking $\eta\downarrow0$ proves the claimed asymptotic upper exponent. In characteristic zero over an algebraically closed field, semisimplicity gives the upper bound directly by decomposing the monoid algebra into matrix algebras whose orders have sum of squares $2^D$. The embeddings above also cover fixed finite coefficient fields such as $\mathbb F_3$. [Kovács, *Semigroup algebras of the full matrix semigroup over a finite field*](https://archives.maths.anu.edu.au/people/Kovacs/K081.pdf) · [Steinberg, alternative proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.02166)

In particular, if deleting $b$ binary wires from an exact network over such a field leaves each connected component incident to external bits in at most one input bank, then $b\ge(\omega_F/2-o(1))D$ for either matrix predicate. Sum over the $2^b$ wire assignments. For each assignment the component contractions multiply into a function of each bank separately, including any scalar components. This argument also permits cutting an external leg by introducing its equality to the guessed bit.

For a nonnegative network with the same three-bank cut, the support of every separated term is a rectangle contained in $G$. On invertible input and proposal banks every nonempty such rectangle contains at most one triple. Its third bank must be a singleton because a product is unique. Fixing an invertible factor and the product then determines the other factor on either side. Thus at least $|\mathrm{GL}_d(2)|^2=\Theta(2^{2D})$ terms are necessary, and $2^{2D}$ terms always suffice by enumerating both factors. The sharp leading cut bound in this support-only version is $2D-O(1)$. The graph $[C=A\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}} B]$ attains $2^{2D}$ required rectangles exactly while admitting an ordinary linear-size circuit.

## In characteristic two

The bit-valued table $g$ is exactly the field-valued sum $\sum_{ijk}A_{ik}B_{kj}Z_{ij}$. Thus its grouped tensor is obtained by separately mapping the $d^2$ coefficient coordinates in each leg of $M_d$ to all evaluations on binary vectors. Each of the three linear maps has a left inverse given by restriction to the $d^2$ unit vectors. This proves equality of grouped ranks even when $F$ properly extends $\mathbb F_2$.

For the whole graph $G$, the grouped rank remains at least $2^{\omega_F d(d-1)/2}$ in characteristic two ($d\ge2$). Restrict all exterior banks to $H_d$. Its defining-characteristic Steinberg module is absolutely irreducible over $\mathbb F_2$ of dimension $n=2^{d(d-1)/2}$. Its scalar extension is absolutely irreducible over $F$, so the image of the group algebra is the whole matrix algebra $M_n(F)$. For instance, after extending to an algebraic closure this is Burnside's theorem, and the dimension of the span of the representing matrices is preserved by scalar extension. A linear section of the surjection on each input and the quotient map on the output give the matrix multiplication tensor as a restriction. Thus $G$ has grouped rank exponential in $d^2$ even though the grouped scalar parity has rank $d^{\omega_F+o(1)}$. The matching upper estimate for $G$ above used an invertible $2$ in the coefficient field. [Steinberg module dimension and absolute irreducibility](https://numdam.org/item/10.5802/crmath.359.pdf)

One explicit trivalent network chooses three internal binary indices $i,k,j$, reads $A_{ik},B_{kj},Z_{ij}$ and accepts if all three bits are one. For each triple of in-range indices the Boolean multiplexer and AND gates have unique internal evaluations. Pad data to a power-of-two side with zeros; out-of-range triples make zero contribution. A multiplexer has linear size in its table bits, including the copies of each address bit needed at each level. Summing the internal guesses therefore gives exactly $g$ in characteristic two at volume $O(d^2)$. Every one of the $3d^2$ exterior bits is essential, so trivalence gives the matching order. In normalized external weights $(1-x,x)$, the scalar contracts to the actual formal trilinear polynomial, by independence in the three banks and characteristic-two arithmetic. Converting that formal contraction into an arithmetic circuit is precisely where network volume ceases to price evaluation.

In the special form $\sum_{u\in\{0,1\}^b} f_u(A)h_u(B)j_u(Z)$ with Boolean outputs from each bank and characteristic-two counting, $b\ge\log_2 R_{\mathbb F_2}(M_d)\ge\omega_{\mathbb F_2}\log_2 d$. Conversely, for every $\epsilon>0$ this form can be realized with $b\le(\omega_{\mathbb F_2}+\epsilon)\log_2 d+O_\epsilon(1)$ and total network volume $O_\epsilon(d^2)$, even charging for all selectors. Choose a sufficiently large constant base $m$ whose binary rank decomposition has $R$ terms with $\lceil\log_2 R\rceil/\log_2 m\le\omega_{\mathbb F_2}+\epsilon$. Pad to side $m^k$ with $k=\lceil\log_m d\rceil$. The $k$-fold product of the base decomposition has one term per $k$-tuple of base indices. Guess the digits separately and give an invalid code zero weight. In each bank compute the one chosen linear form on its $(m^2)^k$ data bits. At the first stage, partition into groups of $m^2$ and take inner products against the same base coefficient vector selected by digit one. Repeat using the next digit on the shorter list of partial sums. The lookup of each constant-size coefficient vector and the fanout of its individual bits incur in total $O_m(\sum_{i=0}^k(m^2)^i)=O_m(d^2)$ sites. Matrix index labels undergo only a fixed permutation between block-recursive and physical order. The three bank results multiply to give the selected term. This establishes a sharp infimum witness-length exponent subject to the three-factor requirement and linear-in-input volume. It imposes no bound on witnesses of a general network whose factors mix the three banks. ## A linear-size trilinear circuit with the same leading rank in odd characteristic and characteristic zero

Use three banks $(a,u,v),(b,x,y),(c,z,w)$, with one tag bit and two $r$-bit strings apiece. Its Boolean verdict is the binary trilinear form
$$
 h=a\langle y,z\rangle+b\langle u,w\rangle+c\langle v,x\rangle\pmod2.
$$
An ordinary Boolean circuit takes three binary inner products, gates each by the remaining tag, and XORs the three answers. This costs $O(r)$ gates even including copies, and yields a linear-volume, uniquely evaluated exact network in every field.

Write $m=2^r$, $L=2r+1$, and take any coefficient field of characteristic different from two. For all three tags equal to one the sign tensor $(-1)^h$ is a product of three Walsh matrices, linking the pairs $(y,z),(u,w),(v,x)$. Each $m$-square Walsh matrix is invertible, since it squares to $mI$. By changing the basis on one vector coordinate at one endpoint of each pair, the three kernels become Kronecker deltas. These are legal separate transformations of the three grouped input spaces because the two vector coordinates in each bank are distinct. The resulting tensor has entry one exactly when $y=z,\ u=w,\ v=x$. Its three banks now carry the index-pair labels for three edges of a triangle. The equality tensor is exactly $M_m$ up to relabeling.

Conversely, in every tag slice other than $(1,1,1)$, at most two Walsh pairs occur. Expanding each active pair matrix as $m$ pure terms between its two endpoints yields at most $m^p$ grouped pure terms when $p$ tags are set. Pad each of these terms by indicators for this tag triple to embed it back into the total tensor. The all-one slice costs $R_F(M_m)$ terms. Hence
$$
 R_F(M_m)\ \le R_F^{(3)}((-1)^h)
 \ \le R_F(M_m)+3m^2+3m+1 .
$$
Again the bit $h$ changes grouped rank by at most one. It follows that $\log_2 R_F^{(3)}(h)=(\omega_F/2+o(1))L$. In particular, the genuine matrix bit and this linear-circuit homogeneous trilinear bit have exactly the same limiting ratio of log grouped rank to bank length over each fixed field $F$ of characteristic different from two. Extracting a superlinear unrestricted lower bound from the leading grouped rank alone is consequently impossible. A proof could still use a finer feature of how a single matrix product's indices occur across its external wires.
