# Checking an arbitrarily high binary matrix power with a constant number of matrix-product checks

Let $G_F(d)$ be the minimum volume of an exact trivalent binary-wire tensor network over a field $F$ for the **Boolean graph predicate** $[AB=C]$, where the matrices are binary of side $d$ and their product is over $\mathbb F_2$. Let $V_F(d,b)$ be the analogous volume for $[A^k=Y]$ with an external $b$-bit nonnegative exponent. For $d,b\ge1$,
$$
 V_F(d,b)\le 6G_F(d)+\widetilde O(d^2+b),\qquad
 G_F(d)\le V_F(3d,2)+O(d^2).
$$
Fixing any longer exponent input to two gives the same reverse reduction. Over every coefficient field of characteristic two, the entire power graph thus has volume $\widetilde O(d^2+b)$ for arbitrary $b$. A bit-graph lower bound of polynomial strength over an odd-characteristic field for the powered operation already requires such a bound for one product, up to the displayed size and dimension changes. This applies to all output entries simultaneously. The two types of field in this statement have separate roles.

The same comparison holds for deciding similarity of two binary matrices. The product lower direction even uses a square-zero matrix and a fixed, public similarity class. These conclusions concern an exact finite-field contraction or an unambiguous verification procedure given product checks. Computing the canonical witnesses on a deterministic RAM is a separate operation.

Fast deterministic computation of Krylov bases, Frobenius forms and powers by matrix arithmetic is classical. The question here is how to use just a constant number of **decision predicates** for products, with total ordinary verification cost close to the input length. The main detail is choosing generators that leave exactly one accepting certificate in every coefficient characteristic. Priority for this verification bound is unestablished. ## Canonical generators from two subspaces

View $\mathbb F_2^d$ as a module over $\mathbb F_2[x]$ with $x$ acting by $A$. Guess its sorted list of elementary divisors $f^r$, with $f$ monic irreducible of degree $e$ and $r\ge1$, in a fixed format with zero padding. Its total degree must be $d$. In each block one generator $g$ gives the standard columns $g,Ag,\ldots,A^{er-1}g$. Concatenate these columns into $P$, and let $D$ be the direct sum of the companion matrices for $f^r$, in that ordering. Checking $P$ invertible and $AP=PD$ identifies the elementary divisors uniquely. Many $P$ can pass these two checks.

Treat each $f$-primary component separately, with $T=f(A)$ on that component. For every length $r$ in increasing order write
$$
 H_r=\ker T^r,\qquad W_r=\ker T^{r-1}+T\ker T^{r+1}.
$$
The quotient $H_r/W_r$ is a vector space over $K_f=\mathbb F_2[x]/(f)$ of dimension equal to the number of blocks of length $r$. In the block of length $l$, take the basis $x^j f^t g$ for $0\le t<l$, $0\le j<e$. A column with height $l-t$ is in $H_r$ iff its height is at most $r$. At height $r$ precisely the columns with $t>0$ come from $T\ker T^{r+1}$. The remaining columns at that height, one group of $e$ per length-$r$ block, give the field basis just described.

Before choosing generator $i$ of length $r$, enlarge $W_r$ by the span of $x^j g_{i'}$ for all previous generators $i'$ of this length and $0\le j<e$. Call the enlarged space $W$. While $W\ne H_r$ choose a vector $g\in H_r$ by the following deterministic rule in the original ambient coordinates, with rows ordered from first to last.

1. Reduce $H_r$ modulo $W$ by requiring zeros on the row pivots of $W$. Every coset has exactly one such representative since projection from $W$ to its pivot rows is bijective. This gives a nonzero residual subspace $U$.
2. Choose the vector in $U$ whose first nonzero coordinate is as late as possible. It is unique over $\mathbb F_2$. Two different choices would have a nonzero sum whose leading coordinate is later still. Its leading row is the largest row in $\operatorname{piv}(H_r)\setminus\operatorname{piv}(W)$. Indeed, a subspace's pivot rows are exactly the possible first nonzero rows of its vectors. Adjoining a basis of $U$ after one of $W$ gives the missing pivots, since its columns are zero on every pivot of $W$.

This generator adds a new nonzero direction over $K_f$ in the quotient, since $W/W_r$ is $K_f$-linear. Iterating selects a basis of each $H_r/W_r$. The resulting cyclic blocks span. To see this, take any elementary-divisor decomposition of the component and reduce the chosen generators modulo $T$. A generator in $H_r$ has zero top coefficient in every block of length greater than $r$, since there its coordinates are divisible by $f$. In blocks of length $r$ the top coefficients form an invertible matrix over $K_f$ by the quotient basis property. The resulting block triangular matrix on the component modulo $T$ is invertible. If $S$ is the generated submodule, this gives $S+T M=M$ on the primary component $M$. Iteration gives $S+T^jM=M$ for all $j$, and nilpotence gives $S=M$. The chosen generators are killed by their indicated $f^r$, and their block dimensions sum to $d$, so the proposed monomial columns form a basis. Existence and uniqueness of the normalized $P$ follow, including for repeated elementary divisors.

## Checking all the pivots together

For each primary component sort its $f$-adic columns by increasing height. At each height place all descended columns ($t>0$) first, then the tops grouped by generator order and increasing $j$. Fix all remaining ties by block order and $j$. In this list $H_r$ is a prefix. So is $W$ immediately before each proposed generator. Form each $f$-adic column as the monomial-basis coefficient vector of $x^jf^t$. Their total change of basis is one known $d$-square matrix $E$ depending only on the proposed shape. Successive schoolbook multiplications by $f$ build the coefficient arrays within $O((er)^2)$ bit operations per block, in addition to writing $E$. One product determines $M_*=PE$.

For a primary component with $h$ columns, guess the factorization $M_f=L_f U_f$ with $L_f$ of size $d$ by $h$ in ambient row order and $U_f$ unit upper triangular. In column $j$ of $L_f$ require zeros on earlier pivot rows and require the declared pivot to be its first nonzero row. This determines a unique factorization and pivot list. Induct on the column number: eliminate the previous pivot coordinates of the next column in their recorded order. The multipliers determine that column of $U_f$ above the diagonal, and the residual determines the new column of $L_f$. Conversely this elimination always works because the columns are independent. For every prefix the set of recorded pivots is its row rank profile. One way to see this is that picking the smallest nonzero pivot in each residual gives distinct leading rows. Sorted by these leading rows, arbitrary linear combinations have their first one at the earliest leading row among the selected columns. [Related rank-profile factorizations of Dumas, Pernet and Sultan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01798)

Concatenate the $L_f$ and put the $U_f$ on a block diagonal. One square product verifies **all** the factorizations. For the actual raw column $g$ at each generator, check zeros on the pivots of its $W$ prefix and check that its first one is the maximum pivot between that prefix and the end of $H_r$. Each scan costs $O(d)$ row operations and there are at most $d$ generators. To check the earlier-pivot zeros for all the elimination columns one may iterate over pairs of columns in each component. Their total count is at most $d^2$. The pivots and all the matrices in this check are unique given $P$. In particular, repeated Jordan blocks do not introduce even multiplicities into the contraction.

## Processing an arbitrarily long exponent

Check irreducibility of each distinct $f$, for example by repeated modular squaring, testing $x^{2^e}=x$ modulo $f$ and taking gcds with $x^{2^{e/p}}-x$ for prime divisors $p$ of $e$. Linear factors are included. These tests cost $\widetilde O(e^2)$ bits per distinct factor with fast binary polynomial multiplication and division. The factorization data are already specified by the witness and its similarity equation. Ordinary polynomial products of degree $n$ take $\widetilde O(n)$ bit operations here; division with remainder by a monic divisor follows by truncated inverse of its monic reversal. [Cantor and Kaltofen](https://kaltofen.math.ncsu.edu/bibliography/91/CaKa91.pdf)

Put
$$
 L=2^{\lceil\log_2(d+1)\rceil}\prod_{f\ne x\text{ distinct}}(2^{\deg f}-1).
$$
Its bit length is $O(d)$. For $f\ne x$, a root of $f$ has order dividing $2^e-1$. Raising to the indicated power of two makes $x^L-1$ divisible by $f^r$ for all used lengths $r$. The factor $x$, if present, is nilpotent of index at most $d$. Hence set $k'=k$ for $k<d$, and otherwise use $k'=d+((k-d)\bmod L)$. Powers $k,k'$ agree in every block. One may guess the unique binary quotient and remainder with fixed widths, verify their equality by fast integer multiplication, and check the remainder range. This costs $\widetilde O(b+d)$ bits. When $k<d$ set unused fields to zero. [Harvey and van der Hoeven, integer multiplication](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2021/193-2/p04)

In each block compute $x^{k'}\bmod f^r$ by repeated squaring. The $O(d)$-bit exponent costs $\widetilde O(d\,er)$ per block. Repeated shifts and reductions of this residue by the monic $f^r$ give the $er$ columns of the multiplication operator for $x^{k'}$, in $O((er)^2)$ additional bits. These blocks make up $D_k$. Altogether the ordinary part of the verifier uses $\widetilde O(d^2+b)$ bit or logarithmic-word operations.

## The six product predicates and the reverse reductions

Check $P P^{-1}=I$, $AP=PD$, $PE=M_*$, the concatenated $LU=M_*$, and $YP=PD_k$. The second equation needs just **one** product predicate since forming $PD$ uses shifts and, per block, one linear combination of its columns, with $O(d^2)$ total bit operations. The last equation uses two predicates and one shared guessed product. This makes six. Except for the internals of these predicates, a valid external input has exactly one accepting witness. Fix widths and pin all unused storage or padding in guessed arrays to zero. The ordinary deterministic verifier can be compiled within polylogarithmic overhead: guess its sequential execution, check word operations locally, and sort the address/time records to check each read against the latest write. Its deterministic run supplies a unique history. Fan-out to every use is charged via binary copy trees. Composing exact graph predicates now gives the desired exact scalar in any coefficient field; each internal predicate sums to its own zero or one given its boundary. This argument supplies an unambiguous nondeterministic RAM verification overhead as well if the product checks are deterministic subroutines. It does not give a deterministic near-linear RAM method for finding $P$.

For the reverse direction square the $3d$-square strictly upper block matrix with $A$ and $B$ in consecutive positions $(1,2),(2,3)$ and zeros elsewhere. The only possible nonzero block of its square is $AB$. All extra wiring is $O(d^2)$.

Certify similarity of $A,A'$ with the same normalized primary descriptions, checking both decompositions and comparing their elementary divisors. The two accepted representations are individually unique and their equality characterizes similarity. Conversely, for proposed factors and product $A,B,C$ let
$$
 S=\begin{pmatrix}I&B\\A&C\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Z=\begin{pmatrix}0&S\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
The rank of $S$ is $d+\operatorname{rank}(C+AB)$ by block elimination. $Z$ is square-zero, and a square-zero matrix is classified up to similarity by its rank at fixed dimension. Thus $Z$ is similar to the public square-zero matrix with exactly $d$ length-two blocks and $2d$ length-one blocks iff $C=AB$. The transformation uses input wires and constants. This also gives a fixed similarity-class formulation of the unresolved odd-characteristic binary product lower-bound route. The exponents in the comparison refer to Boolean matrix graphs; an arithmetic-circuit bound for formal matrix multiplication does not supply the needed lower bound.

## A native clock for the proposed SAT witness family

There is also a deterministic $\widetilde O(d^3+b)$ word-RAM algorithm for the **actual output** for an arbitrary $b$-bit exponent, independently of any new matrix-exponent lower bound. First compute $\chi_A$ by the deterministic characteristic-polynomial algorithm of Keller-Gehrig. For $j=1,\ldots,d$, maintain $x^{2^j}\bmod\chi_A$ by squaring. The degree of $\gcd(\chi_A,x^{2^j}-x)$ is $\sum_{i\mid j} i n_i$, where $n_i$ is the number of distinct degree-$i$ factors of $\chi_A$. This holds because $x^{2^j}-x$ is squarefree and contains exactly the irreducibles whose degrees divide $j$. Subtract earlier divisors' contributions to learn which degrees occur. Even schoolbook polynomial squaring, reduction and Euclidean gcd cost $O(d^2)$ bit operations per $j$ (in Euclid the quotient degree costs telescope). Use one factor $2^i-1$ for each occurring degree in the preceding period bound. Its $O(d)$-bit size follows from $\sum i n_i\le d$. Standard fast integer division reduces the external clock to $O(d)$ bits in $\widetilde O(b+d)$ bit operations. The deterministic powering result of Neiger, Pernet and Villard applies to this reduced clock, for instance with Strassen's feasible exponent $\log_2 7$ and any sufficiently small fixed positive slack. This takes less than $O(d^3)$ field operations for large $d$. Over $\mathbb F_2$ these routines fit the indicated RAM bound, including logarithmic bookkeeping. [Characteristic polynomial](https://www.wlkl.ch/myPdfs/fastCharacteristicPolynomial.pdf) · [Deterministic power algorithm and clock-length condition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07345)

Consequently, consider an explicit SAT family on which a uniform recognizer checks the whole template and recovers $(A,k,Y)$ in $\widetilde O(N)$ RAM time, where $N=\Omega(d^2+b)$ is the encoded file length. If its verdict is determined by power equality, a globally correct SAT solver decides **every file of that family** in $\widetilde O(N^{3/2})$ time, switching to ordinary SAT search when recognition fails. This rules out that family as a source of quadratic-time witnesses against all programs, even if the algebraic iteration count is exponential. The argument leaves the unrestricted linear milestone open.

The finite audit exhausts all conjugators for all primary shapes through side four and checks that every matrix occurs under exactly one normalized witness. It also tests exponent reduction and the square-zero projection. Lean checks the leading-row uniqueness step, which columns survive the height quotient, and lifting generation through the nilpotent filtration.
