# Finding a defect in a proposed pushdown invariant

Fix a positive intersection instance consisting of one visibly pushdown automaton and $k-1$ finite automata over a common constant input alphabet. Let $B$ be the bit length of their explicit sparse transition lists, with fixed-width binary state names. A verifier knows all the automata and may query a proposed relation $R(u,v)$ on pairs of product control states. The candidate promises that the designated initial/final pair is absent. The verifier must return one violated rule of balanced reachability. The rules add a diagonal, add a neutral edge, concatenate two pairs, or wrap a pair between a matching push and pop. Positive instances admit no closed root-excluding relation. This is the search version of the invariant checks used for CFL reachability. [Chistikov, Majumdar and Schepper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.13095) · [Earlier journal note](negative-invariants-of-pushdown-intersections.md)

* For fixed $k$, a common list of rule instances that hits a defect of every candidate of polylogarithmic circuit size can require $\Omega_k((B/\log B)^{2k})$ entries. The same instances have an optimal adaptive search using only $\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$ membership queries, where $M$ is the minimum length of the common list.
* On another family in the same format, fully adaptive deterministic search takes $\Theta(H)$ membership queries, where $H=\Omega_k((B/\log B)^{k/2})$. The lower bound extends to parity queries and allows larger candidate representations.
* Already for one explicitly listed PDA, there are instances requiring $\Omega(B/\log^2 B)$ ordinary adaptive queries. These bounds charge for probes to the proposed relation. The automata and their positive answers can be given to the verifier in advance.

## A unique violation at each cut

Choose distinct primes $p_1,\ldots,p_k,q_1,\ldots,q_k$ between $L$ and $C_k L$, possible by iterating Bertrand's postulate. In component $i$, the left states form an $a$-cycle of length $p_i$, the right states a $b$-cycle of length $q_i$. There is exactly one neutral transition on $\#$, from left $p_i-1$ to right 0. Initially each component is at left 0, and it accepts at right $q_i-1$. In the first component $a$ pushes and $b$ pops the same symbol. Acceptance requires a balanced stack. The lists occupy $B=\Theta_k(L\log L)$ bits. Set $P=\prod p_i$, $Q=\prod q_i$, and $M=PQ$.

Write $f_t$ for the ordered pair whose left residues are $(-1-t\bmod p_i)_i$ and whose right residues are $(t\bmod q_i)_i$. The Chinese remainder theorem enumerates every left-to-right pair exactly once as $0\le t<M$. Its balanced witness is $a^t\#b^t$. The root is $f_{M-1}$. The neutral edge produces $f_0$. Wrapping $f_t$ in one matching pair gives precisely $f_{t+1\bmod M}$, since the individual cycles each have unique successors and predecessors. Same-phase balanced paths have length zero. Mixed-phase product states have no transitions, and no path moves from right to left.

For each $0\le j<M$ let $I_j$ include all diagonals and exactly the cross pairs $f_t$ with $t<j$. A Boolean circuit on the binary endpoint names tests equality and phases, reconstructs $t$ by fixed-modulus CRT arithmetic, and compares it with the hardwired $j$. For fixed $k$ elementary binary arithmetic on $O_k(\log B)$-bit integers uses $\operatorname{polylog} B$ gates. Every $I_j$ excludes the root. Two cross pairs cannot compose. The only violation for $j=0$ is the neutral edge. For $j>0$ it is the unique wrap from $f_{j-1}$ to $f_j$. The wrap around the end of the cycle cannot fail, since its inner pair is the excluded root.

Each of the $M$ candidates therefore requires a different rule in any common list. The $M$ displayed rules also suffice for every root-excluding relation, by induction from $f_0$. More generally, any positive instance with $S$ product states admits a list of at most $S^2$ rules. Select a first-discovery derivation for the root in balanced saturation, retaining one rule per required fact and recursively retaining its earlier premises. Any candidate satisfying the list must contain the root. Here $S=\Theta_k(L^k)$, so the construction reaches that counting bound up to constants. Its shortest accepted word has a binary straight-line grammar of size $O(\log M)$ by repeated doubling. Coprime push/pop cycles yielding quadratic shortest paths already appear in the one-counter literature. The application here concerns simultaneous testing of small invariants. [Chistikov et al., Example 2.5](https://lmcs.episciences.org/5251)

## Ordered membership search

Give a virtual position $-1$ value one and use the promised zero at $M-1$. Between endpoints of opposite values, query an intermediate $f_t$ and retain a subinterval with left endpoint one and right endpoint zero. The readings between the endpoints can occur in any order. At distance one, the right endpoint names a failed neutral or wrap rule. Bisection takes at most $\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$ queries. The unique violations on the $I_j$ require that many binary queries in the worst case.

In $r\ge1$ adaptive batches with total worst-case budget $q$, the exact capacity in possible cut locations is

$$
 C(r,q)=\max_{q_1+\cdots+q_r=q}\prod_{i=1}^r(q_i+1),\qquad q_i\ge0.
$$

For the lower bound, a batch of $b$ queries divides threshold candidates into at most $b+1$ classes. Each nonconstant query compares the same hidden cut $j$ with one fixed index. Even if subsequent budgets depend on the answers, the capacity is at most $\max_{b\le q}(b+1)C(r-1,q-b)$, with initial value $C(0,q)=1$. For the matching upper bound, choose optimal batch budgets and partition the current interval into at most $q_i+1$ subintervals whose lengths fit the remaining capacity. Query the split points and retain a subinterval of opposite endpoint values. The least budget is the first $q$ with $C(r,q)\ge M$. One batch therefore requires $M-1$ membership queries. A common list of rule violations and a batch of membership queries count different primitives.

## Branching procedures embed pebbling search

For a binary fan-in DAG $G$, let $x_v$ be its pebbling variable. Source variables must be true, every nonsource with both predecessors true must be true, and the sink must be false. The search problem asks for one violated clause. One may promise a false sink at a cost of at most one query, since a true sink gives its violation immediately. The deterministic and parity decision-tree depths equal the visiting reversible pebbling price. Bounded-degree $N$-vertex single-sink families attain $\Omega(N/\log N)$. [de Rezende et al., Corollary 1.5 and Theorem 4.6](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/186/revision/4/download/)

Construct one PDA procedure per vertex $v$. For a source, join entry $l_v$ to exit $r_v$ by a neutral transition. For a vertex with predecessors $u,w$, make two calls in sequence from $l_v$ to $m_v$ and then to $r_v$. A call from $a$ to $b$ using child $u$ has a push $a\to l_u$ and a matching pop $r_u\to b$. Assign distinct tags to the call sites using each particular child, reusing tags freely across distinct children. Bounded fanout gives a fixed stack alphabet. Input letters mark neutral, push and pop transitions. The designated root is the sink's entry/exit pair. Every procedure admits a balanced run by induction along the DAG. Unary gates can call the same child twice.

For a binary gate set $R_x(l_v,r_v)=x_v$, $R_x(l_v,m_v)=x_u$, and $R_x(m_v,r_v)=x_w$. Use $x_v$ for a source's full pair. Include all diagonals and exclude all remaining pairs. A full pair belongs to just one procedure, so any wrap with a present premise reaches the intended return site and copies precisely the same bit. A diagonal cannot serve as an inner pair for a wrap, since entries and exits are distinct. Only concatenating the two stages can expose an internal gate violation. A missing neutral edge is a false source. Every relation probe is either a constant or an input-variable probe. The same substitution converts an arbitrary parity of relation memberships into a parity of input variables plus a constant.

For a constant fan-in $d>2$, use $d$ successive calls. Assign each proper forward interval of stages the conjunction of the corresponding consecutive input bits and use $x_v$ for the full interval. Any failed concatenation still requires all the inputs true and the output false. A membership probe costs $O(d)$ ordinary queries. This extends the ordinary-probe transfer to the constant-degree theorem above and gives the single-PDA bound $\Omega(B/\log^2 B)$ since the construction has $B=O(N\log N)$. This argument uses ordinary probes for general $d$. The parity transfer below uses binary gates. For fixed degree the assignment, hardwired predecessor tables and their bounded-fan-in multiplexers can be represented in $\operatorname{poly}(B)$ circuit bits.

## A compressed pyramid in the synchronized product

Let $H=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i$ for distinct comparable primes. Name procedures by $(t,j)\in(\mathbb Z/H)^2$. Component $i$ stores both coordinates modulo $p_i$. The PDA also stores one of the three stages $l,m,r$. Use separate shared input letters for first push, first pop, second push, second pop, and neutral. From entry, the first push calls the entry of $(t-1,j)$. Its matching pop returns from that exit to the original middle stage. From middle, the second push calls $(t-1,j+1)$. Its matching pop returns to the original exit. Each pop applies the inverse coordinate translation selected by its stack tag. The only neutral edges go from entry to exit at $t=0$, checked locally in every component. The root is the full pair for $(H-1,0)$. This costs $O(p_i^2)$ states and transitions per component and $B=O_k(L^2\log L)$ bits for $p_i=\Theta_k(L)$.

The triangle $0\le t<H,\ 0\le j<H-t$ is the usual height-$(H-1)$ binary pyramid under the two predecessor maps for $t>0$. The neutral edges supply its sources, so the root is positively reachable. Extend any pyramid assignment with false sink by true outside the triangle. Construct $R_x$ with the binary stage rules, using periodic coordinates at every vertex, including sources. Missing neutral edges are false pyramid sources. Any failing concat at $t=0$ also exhibits a false source, regardless of its two periodic predecessors. For $t>0$ a false parent lies inside the triangle and both children are inside, so a failing concat yields a pyramid gate violation. Both coordinate translations are bijections, ensuring correct returns. Each procedure membership reads at most one pyramid bit, and each parity costs at most one parity query.

The $H$-row pyramid has ordinary black pebbling price $\Omega(H)$, hence at least this reversible price. The decision-tree correspondence gives $\Omega_k((B/\log B)^{k/2})$ adaptive deterministic membership queries, even for a verifier allowed to query parities. The construction uses arbitrary assignments to $\Theta(H^2)$ pyramid variables. The following halving argument quantifies the effect of a circuit-size promise. [Pyramid bounds, Göös, chapter 7](https://ic-people.epfl.ch/~goos/thesis.pdf) · [Parity decision-tree correspondence](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/186/revision/4/download/)

Conversely there is a search using $O(H)$ ordinary probes for every candidate, including candidates outside the encoded family. Start at the missing full pair at the root. Query its two stage pairs. If both are present, report the concatenation. If a stage is absent, query its child's full pair. A present child exhibits a wrap failure. Otherwise continue from the missing child. Each step lowers $t$, and at a source its missing full pair violates a neutral edge. This matches the lower bound on the entire oracle search problem for these inputs.

## A circuit-size promise caps adaptive probe complexity

For any positive instance and any known finite family $\mathcal F$ of candidate relations, a deterministic verifier can find a defect of every root-excluding member in at most $3(1+\lfloor\log_2|\mathcal F|\rfloor)$ adaptive membership queries. Computation between queries is free here. Keep only root-excluding candidates consistent with the answers so far. Their pointwise majority also excludes the root and therefore violates some ground rule. Query the at most three membership bits occurring in that rule. If all match the majority, return this defect. Otherwise one disagreeing bit alone discards at least half of the surviving candidates, irrespective of tie breaking. The singleton case succeeds immediately. This applies the classical halving algorithm for a finite hypothesis class to the local closure rules. [Angluin and Dohrn, §2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v76/angluin17a.html)

In particular, for candidates described by at most $s$ bits the bound is $O(s+1)$, and for $s$ bounded-fan-in gates on the binary endpoint names it is $O(1+s\log(s+\log B+2))$ for a one-output membership circuit with the usual gate encoding (allow an additional $O(\log\log B)$ bits for a direct input output). Polylogarithmic-size candidates therefore admit polylogarithmic adaptive query complexity on every instance, even where a common list must have nearly $B^{2k}$ tests. The unrestricted pyramid lower bound requires allowing a sufficiently large family. The halving calculation grants the verifier arbitrary computation to determine majority bits and locate a violated rule of that majority. Obtaining those operations cheaply in deterministic RAM time would be a separate algorithmic result.

## Algebraic refutation degree over every field

Make the proposed relation itself the input variables of a CNF. Give each control-state pair a variable, include a Horn clause for every ground balanced-reachability rule, and add the unit clause excluding the root. Encode a clause as the polynomial vanishing on its satisfying Boolean assignments, taking the product of its falsifying literal indicators. Include $Y^2-Y=0$ for each variable. For the compressed pyramids this inconsistent system has **Nullstellensatz refutation degree $\Theta(H)$ over every field**, even allowing arbitrary polynomials as multipliers. The degree of an identity $1=\sum_i p_i f_i$ is $\max_i\deg(p_i f_i)$.

For the lower bound use the substitution $Y_{a,b}=R_x(a,b)$ above, now leaving the pyramid sink variable free and mapping the root-exclusion clause to the ordinary pebbling sink axiom. Each replacement is a constant or one variable. A projected source edge gives a source axiom, and a nontautological internal concat in the triangle gives its pebbling clause. A failing concat at a periodic source yields a polynomial divisible by the source polynomial $1-x_v$. A projected Boolean tautology is either the zero polynomial or contains complementary literal factors $x(1-x)$ and is therefore a multiple of a Boolean axiom. Projected Boolean axioms themselves are either zero or Boolean axioms. Thus each nonzero term in a projected identity can be written as a multiple of one pyramid axiom without increasing its degree. This would refute the pyramid in the same degree. The Nullstellensatz degree of the pebbling contradiction equals its reversible pebbling price over every field. [de Rezende et al., Lemma 1.4](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/186/revision/4/download/)

For the upper bound first query the root, reporting its unit clause if present, and otherwise use the $O(H)$-query descent above. In a deterministic decision tree erase repeated queries on any branch. The indicators of all root-to-leaf paths sum to the constant polynomial one, by the identity $Y+(1-Y)=1$ at each branching. Each reachable leaf forces its reported clause false, so its indicator is divisible by the clause's falsifying literal product (use the usual CNF convention that duplicate literals are merged). This gives a degree-$O(H)$ refutation. With unsimplified repeated literals the Boolean axioms give the same bound up to a constant. The statement concerns the expanded Horn formula on candidate relations. Its number of variables and clauses can be much greater than the encoding length $B$ of the automata.

## A succinct certificate that succeeds on both families

Let $D$ be a Boolean circuit taking a line number and returning a purported row of a balanced-reachability proof. Each row names a state pair, one rule, its earlier premise line numbers, and any product-transition witnesses by component record numbers. The final row must prove the root. For $S$ product states one can use a public table length $2^{\lceil\log_2(S^2+1)\rceil}$, repeating the final inference as needed. Every positive input has such a table by first discovery. The table certifies positivity by induction if every row passes its local checks. Circuit descriptions of proof lines are a standard form of implicit proof. [Krajíček](https://www.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~krajicek/implicit.pdf)

Both families above have a proof-index circuit of $\operatorname{polylog} B$ gates. For the cyclic family line $t$ gives the neutral or wrap deriving $f_t$ and names its predecessor $t-1$ if needed. For the pyramid list the sources first. At each higher level list two wraps and their concat for each vertex, using the two full pairs from the previous level. One can enumerate the entire periodic row for every $t>0$, since the two translated children always lie in the previous row. Append or repeat the root inference at the end. All row numbers, endpoint residues, and local transition numbers can be computed from the index by arithmetic on $O_k(\log B)$ bits when the explicit component tables are listed in coordinate order. Thus the large refutation degree of the invariant encoding coexists with a small circuit describing a positive derivation.

The unrestricted RAM SAT question remains open. A solver can inspect the circuit description of a proposed invariant, certify several tests jointly, or use a different proof of nonemptiness. Both positive families have short arithmetic or inductive proofs. The hierarchy lower bound for deciding arbitrary product intersection still needs a suitable reduction to SAT.
