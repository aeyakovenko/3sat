# How many fixed filters can isolate an equal pair?

In the dense list projection to ordinary 3-SAT, a positive file encodes a repeated record. An existential circuit guesses two row indices and checks whether their records agree. Each unordered equal pair gives one witness. Consider a filter chosen before reading any record values. It retains an arbitrary subset of the possible index pairs. How many such filters ensure that at least one retains an odd number of witnesses on **every** positive list? With at most two witnesses, an odd count is already a unique witness. The resource measured here is the number of filters, irrespective of their circuit sizes. [List verifier and projection to proper DIMACS](multiplicity-and-size-of-fixed-ram-verifiers.md#an-unambiguous-verifier-lower-bound-for-ordinary-3-sat)

Let $s_k(t)$ be the minimum for $t$ rows and at most $k$ equal pairs, and $s(t)$ the minimum without the multiplicity promise. Assume the alphabet contains at least $t$ values. All logs below are base two.

* For $t\ge3$, $s_2(t)=\lceil\log(t-1)\rceil$. For $t=2$ it is one. For every fixed $k\ge2$, $s_k(t)=\lfloor k/2\rfloor\log t+O_k(1)$.
* For unrestricted collisions and $m=\lfloor t/2\rfloor$,
  $$
  s(t)\ \ge\ m+\tfrac12\log\log m-\tfrac12\log\log\log m-O(1).
  $$
  On six matched pairs the proof uses only three explicit partitions, each consisting of two triangles. A separate nine-pair argument strengthens the additive term to $\Omega(\log m)$, even when each record value occurs at most three times. [Stronger bound and counterexample extraction](logarithmic-surplus-for-collision-filters.md)
* For $t=2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9$, the exact unrestricted minima are respectively $1,1,2,2,3,4,5,5$. In general $s(t)\le t-1-\lfloor\log(t-1)\rfloor$ for $t\ge2$. With an explicit 15-position block one also obtains $s(t)\le t-5\lfloor\log_{16}(t+1)\rfloor$.

These results concern the pair verifier and require no upstream circuit lower bound. A RAM that reads the record values can find an equal pair in linear word time by radix sorting. For generic existential verification circuits, the previous journal entry bounds the sum of their gate counts using the upstream network theorem. The number of filters for this particular verifier and the gate cost of a general verifier are different resources. There is no unrestricted RAM time lower bound from counting these filters. ## Reading some of the rows first

Allow a deterministic decision algorithm to interleave two operations adaptively: it may learn an entire row value, or it may choose any subset of pairs and obtain the parity of its equal pairs. Between operations it can compute any function of the transcript. On **each all-distinct input**, if it reads $r$ distinct rows and makes $s$ parity queries, then
$$
             s\ge s_k(t-r)
$$
under the promise of at most $k$ equal pairs; use $s(t-r)$ without that promise. Here a domain of size zero or one needs no filters.

Fix its complete transcript on the given input. Exclude the $r$ queried rows, and restrict its $s$ chosen masks to pairs of remaining indices. If those restricted masks miss a permitted collision pattern, assign a separate fresh value to each of its classes, avoiding the values of the $r$ rows. An alphabet of size $t$ suffices. No equal pair crosses into the queried rows. Every recorded value and every parity reply is the same, so the machine follows the same path on this positive list and gives the wrong answer. For unbounded collisions the nine-pair theorem consequently gives $2s+r\ge t+\Omega(\log(t-r))$ as $t-r$ grows. This tradeoff allows input-dependent choices of which filters to ask. It measures the two specified input operations on the list projection.

## At most two witnesses

Assign each edge $ij$ of the complete graph a vector $v_{ij}\in\mathbb F_2^s$ recording which filters retain it. An equality partition has syndrome equal to the XOR of all edges inside its blocks. The requirement is precisely that every allowed nondiscrete partition has nonzero syndrome. Every such partition is realized by a list. An adaptive algorithm whose only input-dependent replies are these parity bits has the same lower bound: run it on the all-distinct list, and use its all-zero transcript as the fixed filter list. An undetected positive follows the same transcript.

One equal pair requires a nonzero label on each edge. Exactly two equal pairs must be disjoint, and any two disjoint edges are realizable. Thus disjoint edges must have distinct labels. The chromatic number of this disjointness graph $KG(t,2)$ is $t-2$ for $t\ge3$. An elementary proof assigns one color to each of $t-3$ successive stars and one to the final triangle. Conversely each class of pairwise intersecting edges lies in a star or a triangle: a third edge missing the intersection of two meeting edges must close their triangle, and no other edge meets all three. Remove the centers of $a$ star classes. If $b$ triangle classes remain and $r\ge t-a$ vertices survive, then $\binom r2\le3b$. Whenever $a+b\le t-3$, this would give $\binom{b+3}2\le3b$, impossible. Encoding the colors as nonzero binary words gives the asserted ceiling. The optimal chromatic number is classical. The parity-mask characterization and the counting inequality are checked in Lean. [Lovász, *Kneser's conjecture, chromatic number, and homotopy*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(78)90022-5) · [Lean proofs](CollisionPairColors.lean)

There is also an exact adaptive tradeoff for this promise. A solver allowed at most $r$ full-row reads on $t\ge3$ rows needs exactly
$$
\left\lceil\log_2(1+\min(t-r,t-2))\right\rceil
$$
parity queries in the worst case. Along an all-distinct transcript, let $r'$ be the actual number of distinct rows inspected. All pairs contained in the $r'$ fixed rows can be discarded. Every remaining single edge or pair of disjoint edges can be realized while keeping the read values fixed: use each pinned value only in its own equality class, and assign fresh values to the other classes. Thus the mask labels must properly color the disjointness graph on the edges of $G=K_t-E(K_{r'})$.

Write $u=t-r'$. The $u$ stars at the unread rows or the $t-2$ complete-graph colors give the upper bound $\min(u,t-2)$ on colors. If $r'\le1$, the graph is $K_t$ itself. For $r'\ge2$, a purported coloring with $a$ star classes and $b$ triangle classes using at most $u-1$ colors leaves at least $\binom v2+r'v$ edges after deleting the star centers, where $v=u-a\ge1$. Indeed deleting $a$ unread vertices minimizes the surviving edge count: there are at least $t-a$ surviving vertices and at most $r'$ pinned vertices, whose mutual pairs are the only possible missing edges. The triangle classes cover at most $3b\le3(v-1)$ edges, whereas $\binom v2+2v>3(v-1)$ for every $v\ge1$. For $u=0$ there is nothing to color. This proves the lower bound for each all-distinct transcript, and $r'\le r$. To attain it, first read any $r$ rows and return YES if two agree. Otherwise encode the colors of this graph as nonzero mask labels and query their coordinates. Thus parity queries interleaved with adaptive raw reads confer no further savings in this two-witness decision model. The finite audit also checks the color count for all pin sizes through eight rows. [Audit](collision_filter_small_audit.py)

## A fixed number of witnesses

Take $d=\lfloor t/2\rfloor$ pairwise disjoint edges. Every submatching of size at most $k$ has nonzero XOR unless empty. For $h=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$, all sums of at most $h$ of these labels are therefore distinct: two equal sums would cancel to a forbidden nonempty submatching. This proves
$$
2^{s_k(t)}\ge\sum_{i=0}^{\min(h,d)}\binom di.
$$

For the upper bound let $b_j$ be the parity of the number of earlier rows equal to row $j$. The coordinate at the earliest repeated row is one. The number of nonzero coordinates is at most the number of equal pairs. Label each edge by a function of its larger endpoint $j$. The XOR of the pair labels is the syndrome of the support of $b$. On the $t-1$ possible repeat endpoints choose distinct nonzero elements $a_j$ of $\mathbb F_{2^w}$, $w=\lceil\log t\rceil$. Use the binary coordinates of
$$
(a_j,a_j^3,\ldots,a_j^{2h-1}).
$$
If a support of size $e\le2h$ has zero syndrome, repeated squaring makes all power sums of orders $1,\ldots,e$ zero. The resulting Vandermonde system on the distinct nonzero elements is invertible, contradicting all coefficients being one. For odd $k=2h+1$ add the all-edges filter: if there are precisely $2h+1$ witnesses its count is odd. This costs at most $h\lceil\log t\rceil+[k\text{ odd}]$ filters. Together with the matching count it proves the fixed-$k$ asymptotic. For $k=1$ the all-edges filter alone suffices. This use of the binary BCH check only needs the support bound on the endpoint vector. [Bose and Ray-Chaudhuri](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(60)90287-4) · [Binary syndrome proof in the RAM journal](local-edits-against-fingerprints.md)

## An unbounded additive term above a full matching

Use $2m$ rows and fix the canonical matching joining the two rows in each of $m$ ordered blocks. Its $m$ labels are independent since every nonempty submatching is an allowable partition. Suppose all edge labels lie in $\mathbb F_2^{m+k}$. Extend the matching labels to a basis. For a selected set of blocks, quotient out the matching basis vectors for **all other blocks**. A nondiscrete partition with zero projected syndrome gives an actual counterexample: its full syndrome is a subset sum of those outside matching vectors, so append those outside pairs on fresh, mutually distinct values. The $k$ extra coordinates survive the quotient.

Index the two rows of every block by $a=0,1$. Only edges joining two rows with the same $a$ will be needed in the selected blocks. Color a pair of blocks $i<j$ by the following data for both of its same-row-type edges: the $k$ extra coordinates, and its two basis coefficients at $i$ and $j$. This takes at most $q=2^{2k+4}$ colors. Color each ordered triple of distinct blocks (in increasing order) by six further bits. For each of the two row types and each of the three block pairs, record the coefficient of its edge at the third block. There are at most 64 triple colors. Let $U$ be the fixed multicolor triple Ramsey number for a monochromatic six-set in 64 colors. If the multicolor pair Ramsey number $R_q(U)$ is at most $m$, take $U$ blocks homogeneous for pairs and then six homogeneous for triples. Relabel the six in increasing order by $0,\ldots,5$.

In the quotient each edge between positions $i<j$ on a fixed row type has a constant extra vector. Its six remaining bits take five fixed values $L,A,M,B,R$ on $x<i$, $x=i$, $i<x<j$, $x=j$, $j<x$, respectively. These five bits may differ between the two row types. For a triangle with median $j$, the XOR of its edges has value $L$ at all $x<j$, value $H=A+M+B$ at $j$, and value $R$ at $x>j$. This follows by adding the three five-region vectors, including at the outer two vertices. Its extra vector is the row type's constant extra vector. For two disjoint triangles the extra vectors cancel. If their medians are $u<v$, the remaining syndrome has values $L+H$ at $u$, $L+R$ in the open interval, $H+R$ at $v$, and zero elsewhere.

There are just four outcomes. When $L=H=R$ the syndrome is zero. When $L=R\ne H$ it is $e_u+e_v$; choose the triangles $(024),(135)$ with medians $2,3$. When $H=R\ne L$ it is the interval $[u,v)$; choose $(023),(145)$ with medians $2,4$. When $H=L\ne R$ it is the interval $(u,v]$; choose $(014),(235)$ with medians $1,3$. Each of the last three choices has the same syndrome $e_2+e_3$. Consequently one row type can vanish on its own, or both row types can realize $e_2+e_3$ and cancel each other. Assign a different record value to each triangle and singleton. The result is a nondiscrete equality partition with zero projected syndrome. This contradicts the asserted filters by the outside matching argument. The finite six-block computation is also proved from the actual edge sets in Lean with kernel reduction. [Six-block proof](CollisionFilterSixBlocks.lean)

Therefore $m<R_q(U)$. The elementary multicolor Ramsey bound $R_q(U)\le q^{qU}$ suffices here. For instance, greedily retain a largest monochromatic neighbor class at each chosen vertex of an ordered sequence, then take $U$ chosen vertices of one outgoing color. Taking logs twice gives $\log\log m\le 2k+\log(k+2)+O(1)$. If $k\ge\frac12\log\log m$ the stated lower bound already holds. Otherwise substitute that upper estimate for $k$ inside $\log(k+2)$. This proves the additive lower bound, including odd $t$ by restriction to $2\lfloor t/2\rfloor$ rows. The unspecified constant depends on the fixed $U$. The asymptotic argument uses no computer search.

## Finite exhaustion and endpoint upper bounds

Deleting a disjoint external pair and quotienting out its nonzero label gives $s(t+2)\ge s(t)+1$. This is the one-pair case of the outside matching argument. On seven rows the three-bit case already fails for matchings; the exhaustive backtracking has 1,357 nodes. On eight rows the four-bit search uses all 4,139 nondiscrete partitions, normalizes the four disjoint matching labels to a basis, and exhausts 4,305,327 nodes. The same procedure finds a feasible labeling on six rows. The audit supplies explicit successful witnesses through eight rows. For nine rows the seven-row case yields $s(9)\ge5$ and the following upper yields five. [Reproducible search and witnesses](collision_filter_small_audit.py)

The endpoint vector $b$ defined above is exactly a word with at most as many ones as zeros in every prefix. In the forward direction, record the current number of equality classes with odd occupancy. A one consumes one odd class and a zero creates one odd class, from initial count zero. Conversely, at a zero create a new singleton; at a one pair with a singleton still waiting. This realizes every such word. Only the discrete partition has $b=0$.

The coordinate $b_1=0$ is fixed, so work in dimension $t-1$. Put $d=\lfloor\log(t-1)\rfloor$. On the first $2^d$ positions write the evaluations $\langle y,i\rangle$ for binary $i=0,\ldots,2^d-1$; on the next position repeat the last evaluation and use zeros thereafter. As $y$ varies this gives a $d$-dimensional subspace of the ambient $b_1=0$ hyperplane. Every nonzero $y$ gives exactly half ones in the first $2^d$ positions. If the last of those bits is zero, truncating it gives a strict majority of ones; if it is one, the appended bit gives a strict majority. Thus no nonzero attainable $b$ lies in this subspace. Projecting onto the quotient uses $t-1-d$ bits. For $d=0$ the subspace is zero and the same bound holds. Turning the projected unit vectors into edge labels realizes the filters.

For a larger asymptotic saving, use all $t$ endpoint positions and the following 15 probes of a five-bit word, in decimal notation:
$$
1,2,2,4,8,16,28,12,20,12,20,24,24,24,24.
$$
For each of the 31 nonzero five-bit words, its dot products with an initial segment of these probes contain a strict majority of ones. The audit tests this finite property directly. Concatenate such blocks on independent five-bit inputs. After a prefix of length $D$, repeat every probe of the next block $D+1$ times. If that block's input is the first nonzero input, its prefix majority offsets the entire previous prefix of zeros. The new length is $16(D+1)-1$. Starting with $D=0$, the kernel obtained with $j$ blocks has dimension $5j$ and length $16^j-1$; append zero positions as necessary. The quotient therefore gives the second general upper bound. This is an upper bound for odd detection; three or more accepted pairs can give an odd count.

On partitions consisting only of disjoint pairs, the label condition is a linearly independent coloring of the matching complex over the binary field: every face must map to an independent set. This is a standard real Buchstaber invariant formulation. Additional triangle classes enter the unrestricted logarithmic-surplus proof. [Ayzenberg, generalized chromatic invariants](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3663)

The chromatic and coding ingredients are classical. Priority for these particular filter bounds has not been established. The six-block lemma and the two-witness characterization have Lean proofs. The link from partitions to the RAM projection, the Ramsey argument, and the asymptotic estimates are manuscript arguments.
