# Seven AND gates for a two-by-two binary matrix product

**Theorem.** Let two independent binary matrices of order two be given by their eight entries. Computing all four entries of their product over the two-element field requires exactly seven binary AND gates. The circuit can use arbitrary affine Boolean operations, constants and fanout for free. Each AND may take affine combinations of any preceding wires. The lower bound allows intermediate functions of arbitrary Boolean degree.

Strassen supplies the seven gates. Below is a finite proof of the lower bound with a reproducible enumeration. A September 2026 research note reported the interval from six to seven in precisely this Boolean model. Earlier results for bilinear or quadratic arithmetic circuits use different hypotheses. The search found no earlier proof of the exact Boolean result, and priority remains unestablished. Morse used the dimension surplus of intermediate functions for a related binary polynomial convolution. [Strassen](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02165411) · [MF-180, including the model and six-gate question](https://slopdealer.com/research/papers/mf-180/) · [Shpilka, model specifications](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0201001) · [Boyar and Find, vectorial Boolean multiplicative complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.6169) · [Morse, binary polynomial multiplication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30238)

## Eliminate one gate on a hyperplane

Before the first AND both factors are affine on the eight input bits. A constant factor makes that gate free. Otherwise take the kernel of the linear part of one factor. On that seven-dimensional linear hyperplane the factor is constant, so all four restricted outputs would have a circuit with one fewer AND. A circuit with at most five gates on the full space would already restrict to at most five on every hyperplane. It therefore suffices to exclude five gates on each of the 255 linear input hyperplanes. This implication uses only one fixed gate of a hypothetical circuit, with no assumption on subsequent products.

## An exact search when there is room for one extra direction

Work on any nonempty finite input set. Use the vector space of Boolean truth tables and identify functions differing by a constant. Concretely store each table after subtracting its value at a distinguished input. AND uses these representatives. Complementing either operand changes their product only by a constant and a linear combination of the operands, which are already available. Equivalently one may keep both constants in the free space and work with ordinary truth tables throughout.

Let $U$ be the space spanned by the free inputs and $W$ the span after adjoining the desired output tables. Write $m=\dim W-\dim U$. Each AND enlarges the accessible space by at most one dimension. A gate whose value is already accessible can be removed by substitution throughout the remaining circuit.

For an ambient space $S$ containing $U$, repeatedly adjoin a product of accessible factors whose value lies in $S$ and outside the accessible span. Denote the final space by $R(U,S)$. Every computed direction is realizable, at one AND per new dimension. Conversely the final space is closed under all such products, so induction in circuit order places every wire of any computation staying in $S$ in this space. This also proves that every order of additions has the same result. Thus $m$ gates suffice exactly when $R(U,W)=W$.

Suppose $m$ gates do not suffice but $m+1$ do. The final space must be $W+\langle h\rangle$ for a product $h=ab\notin W$. Take the **first** gate outside $W$. Everything preceding it lies in $P=R(U,W)$, so $a,b\in P$. Enumerating these candidates and testing
$$
W\subseteq R(U,W+\langle ab\rangle)
$$
is exact. Sufficiency follows since this ambient space has dimension only $m+1$ beyond $U$. Adding all reachable directions can use at most that many independent gates.

A sieve makes this enumeration small. For each nonconstant $f\in P$, restrict to its two fibers separately. On a fiber $E$, compute the reachable space from $P|_E$ within $W|_E$, taking constants as free again. Retain $f$ only if this closure contains all restricted outputs on **both** fibers. For a possible hidden product $ab$, each of $a,b,a+b$ must pass. On either fiber of $a$ or $b$, the product becomes zero or the other operand. On the equal-input fiber of $a+b$ it becomes $a$, and on the unequal-input fiber it vanishes. Consequently the whole hypothetical circuit restricts to $W|_E$, where the sieve gives it the entire restricted prefix for free. Failure rules out that factor. A hidden product has three distinct nonzero factors in this test. The three ways to choose a pair among them give products differing by an accessible summand, by $a^2=a$. Only one product per such triple, and one closure per distinct coset modulo $W$, is required.

## Exhaustion

Index a matrix row-major, using bit positions 0 through 3 for the first matrix and 4 through 7 for the second. A mask $j=1,\ldots,255$ specifies the inputs with even parity in the marked positions. For each mask the checker forms the four output truth tables by the definition of matrix multiplication, evaluates the ranks, runs the sieve and tests every surviving ambient space. Each domain has 128 points, seven free dimensions modulo constants, and four independent new output dimensions. The budget is therefore $m+1=5$ throughout.

The default checker visits all 255 normals individually, so orbit completeness is unnecessary. It also evaluates Strassen's upper bound on all 256 inputs. As a cross-check on the procedure, it compares against unrestricted breadth-first gate synthesis on all 67 output spaces modulo the three free inputs on a three-bit cube. A separately written checker keeps the constant-one truth table explicitly and computes allowed products by Gaussian elimination. For each accessible factor $a$, it finds the kernel of the linear map $b\mapsto ab\bmod S$ and takes products with a basis of that kernel. Both its six-orbit run and its direct run on all 255 hyperplanes obtain the same surviving spaces. Across the 18 exceptional hyperplanes all 36 candidate extensions fail. **Every single affine parity restriction has optimum six.** The six linear orbit representatives have explicit circuits with six AND gates each. Invertible changes $(A,B)\mapsto(PAQ,Q^{-1}BR)$ and interchange with transposition preserve cost. Translating the inputs changes the product by an affine function, since $(A+X)(B+Y)=AB+AY+XB+XY$ for fixed $X,Y$. The checker actually transports the circuits, evaluating all four outputs on all 128 points of all 510 affine hyperplanes. The lower bound transports to each parallel translate by the same identity. [Six circuits and all 65,280 checks](mm2_six_gate_restrictions.py)

**Any six-AND circuit gets at least eight of the 256 input pairs wrong.** This follows by projecting away the output contribution of the last AND. Requesting any three independent linear combinations of the four outputs costs exactly six ANDs on the full domain, and five ANDs make at least eight errors on every such projection. The proof tests the only two first-gate planes that survive and checks the 192-point complement of one quarter of the cube in each case. Thus even deleting seven arbitrary input pairs leaves the exact full-product cost at seven. The minimum actual number of errors for six ANDs remains undetermined. ## What happens when the number of blocks grows?

There is an additive lower bound for a different quantity that can be proved without enumeration. For $r$ independent products of order $d$, put $D=d^2$ and $h=\lfloor D/4\rfloor+1$. Computing the $rD$ output bits needs at least $r(2D-h)$ ANDs. Deciding the **single bit** that says all $r$ supplied products are correct needs at least $r(3D-h)-1$ ANDs. The decision inequality also holds for a Boolean circuit with an arbitrary finite existential witness and one acceptance bit.

To see this, fix any two inputs to a circuit with $t$ ANDs. Successively impose at most one affine input equation per AND while keeping both inputs. Once all earlier wires are affine on the current flat, the operand pairs at the two points lie on a common affine line in the binary plane. Impose its defining equation on the operands. Their product is affine on this line because it contains just two points. Every wire is now affine on a flat of codimension at most $t$. For equal scalar verdicts impose at most one more equation to keep the verdict constant. If there are witnesses, first choose honest lifts of the two points. Projecting the flat to the public variables loses at most the number of witness dimensions, leaving at most $t+1$ affine equations on the public inputs. Lean checks the circuit induction and the elimination of the witnesses. [Lean proof](TwoPointGateRestriction.lean)

Choose the two graph points with all blocks $(0,0,0)$ and all blocks $(I,I,I)$. Any graph flat containing both is linear. In each block a point $(A,B,C)$ on the flat satisfies both $C=AB$ and $I+C=(I+A)(I+B)$, giving $A=B$. Closure under addition forces the first coordinates of any two points to commute within each block. The Schur–Jacobson bound on a commutative matrix algebra gives dimension at most $h$ per projected block, hence at most $rh$ in total. For the vector problem the affine output on the input flat lifts it to a graph flat of the same dimension, at least $2rD-t$. For the scalar problem the accepting public flat has dimension at least $3rD-t-1$. This gives both bounds. The blockwise graph-flat estimate itself is attained by taking products of the one-block extremizers. [Matrix-space proof and extremizers](binary-witness-cost-of-matrix-products.md) · [Jacobson](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9904-1944-08169-X)

For $d=2$ this gives $6r$ for generating the products and $10r-1$ for checking all of them. Separate Strassen computations give $7r$ for generation and at most $11r-1$ for verification, including the conjunction of all $4r$ affine equality bits. The finite seven-gate theorem settles generation at $r=1$. Its proof uses the fact that a five-gate restriction has only one spare direction. With many copies, a circuit can mix directions across blocks, and the same exhaustion does not establish $7r$.

There is a successor for which the exact cost does propagate. If the outputs are the four bits of the two-by-two product and a separate independent dot product of $s$ bit pairs, the exact cost is $7+s$. This follows from a parity decision-tree bound that applies to adjoining a separate output to any Boolean vector function. Each fresh dot-product pair raises the optimum by one even when the circuit may mix all its inputs. For a second complete matrix-product block that decision-tree argument guarantees only four additional gates. [Exact direct sum with an independent dot product](adding-an-independent-dot-product-to-a-boolean-circuit.md)

## Proper 3-SAT and the native clock

The scalar test has a bit projection to actual proper ternary ASCII DIMACS with length $N=\Theta(r)$. Use dummy variables 1, 2 and 3 pinned to zero by the seven clauses leaving only the all-zero assignment. For each twelve-bit string $j$, give $T_j$ the five-digit decimal identifier consisting of a leading digit 1 and the four octal digits of $j$. Use one clause for each identifier from 4 through 17777, padded with the two false dummies. It pins that variable to the truth of the matrix graph on its tuple, or to zero for unused identifiers. All names from 1 through 17777 occur. This fixed prefix has 17,781 clauses. For each input tuple print one further clause containing its positive table variable and the two false dummies. Declare 17777 variables and the correct clause count in the header. Every changing ASCII bit is one of the three input bits determining its octal digit. The remaining physical bits are constant for fixed $r$. The scalar gate lower bound therefore transfers even to circuits allowed existential witness bits. The input convention permits repeating clauses. The audit checks all 4,096 records and all 49,152 individual bit changes. [DIMACS audit](mm2_dimacs_projection.py)

One globally correct RAM program recognizes and decides all these files in $O(N/\log N)$ steps. Check the fixed header layout and the table, and infer the claimed record count from the header and the length. Apart from the header the construction is a fixed prefix followed by fixed-length records from a finite alphabet. Both valid records and accepting records have fixed finite dictionaries. A fixed finite automaton can therefore check the record syntax and accumulate whether any tuple fails. For each input length tabulate its transition on chunks of at most half a logarithmic word, shortened if needed so the chunk length is at most half $\log_2 N$ bits. The table construction takes $O(\sqrt N\,\operatorname{polylog}N)$ steps, and the packed scan takes $O(N/\log N)$. One can cap header integer parsing at the expected length and test all length and syntax conditions before returning a verdict. Use a complete SAT solver on files outside the recognized family. On an all-correct input, changing one supplied output digit in any record makes the file unsatisfiable. Every record therefore has to be touched, which costs $\Omega(N/\log N)$ word probes as $r$ grows.

This yields an actual fast solver on the same files to which the gate-count lower bound applies. To deduce a general RAM lower bound by the matrix-product proposal still requires a growing lower bound in the appropriate computation-history model and an encoded-length-preserving transfer. For a single growing square matrix, a bit-network lower bound of order $d^{2+\delta-o(1)}$ would exclude SAT clocks $O(N^k)$ for $k<1+\delta/2$ under the journal’s transfer. Even matching the stated arithmetic exponent $9/4$ at the bit-network level would reach only $k<9/8$. Repeating the two-by-two obstruction gives no such speed penalty.
