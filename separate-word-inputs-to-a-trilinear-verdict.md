# Extracting rank from a Boolean computation with separate word inputs

Suppose an XOR of Boolean computations agrees on every binary triple $(A,B,Z)$ with a trilinear form $T$. A summand may have arbitrarily complicated logic and take arbitrary functions of each block as its three inputs. Let the three inputs to summand $j$ have at most $2^{a_j},2^{b_j},2^{c_j}$ possible values. No linearity is required of these encodings. In each summand sort the widths as $p_j\le q_j\le r_j$. Then the ordinary binary tensor rank satisfies
$$
                  R(T)\le\sum_j(2^{p_j}-1)(2^{q_j}-1).
$$
There is no factor for the largest view. A summand reading only two blocks costs zero. A scalar matrix-product evaluator obtained from near-linear SAT would contradict the cited exponent if it admitted such a representation with $d^{2+o(1)}$ summands and $p_j+q_j\le(1/4-\epsilon)\log_2 d$ throughout. This gives a sufficient condition on two input widths, independent of all precomputation done separately on individual blocks. [SAT to trilinear evaluation](verifying-matrix-products-from-sat.md) · [Formal matrix exponent](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/lean/docs/107.md)

Evaluate the alternating cube of each summand at $0$ and one unit vector per block. Over the binary field the signs are all plus. The result for $T$ is its coefficient tensor. Consider a summand for which the two cheapest blocks are $B,Z$. Classify each unit vector in $B$ by the value of the summand's $B$-input on it, with one distinguished class for its value on zero. Do the same in $Z$. If either class is distinguished the cube sum vanishes. On every fixed pair of nondistinguished classes the full tensor slice is a single vector indexed by the unit vector of $A$. Multiply that vector by the two class-indicator vectors. Summing these pure tensors over class pairs reconstructs the cube sum at every coefficient. The new linear forms can involve coordinates outside the spans used by the original word inputs. In fact a three-bit example precludes enforcing that constraint gate by gate. [Local obstruction](bilinearization-with-prescribed-word-inputs.md)

A related version allows summand $j$ to be given by a deterministic protocol with arbitrary interaction, provided each party sees just one of the three blocks and the public transcript. If there are $K_j$ accepting transcripts, its bit is the sum over their disjoint input rectangles. Each rectangle indicator factors into three arbitrary Boolean functions, one per block. Its cube sum is a pure tensor, since the eight terms factor into three first differences. This gives $R(T)\le\sum_j K_j$. Choosing one of several summands based on an input-dependent branch requires accounting for the branch predicate in this representation.

## How accurate must such a conversion be?

The same extraction works on translated unit cubes. If the XOR only approximates $T$ with error $\eta$ on uniform triples, then
$$
 R(T)\le \sum_j(2^{p_j}-1)(2^{q_j}-1) +8\eta\,D_A D_B D_Z,
$$
where the $D$'s denote the bit dimensions of the three blocks. Choose a uniform anchor triple, and for each coordinate triple take the cube formed by toggling or retaining the three unit directions at that anchor. Every one of its eight inputs is marginally uniform. The expected number of erroneous coefficients is at most $8\eta D_A D_B D_Z$ by a union bound per coefficient. Some anchor meets this average. The translated class-factorization proof has the same cost and each erroneous coefficient takes one pure tensor to correct. For the $d$-square contraction the guarantee is enough when $\eta=o(d^{-15/4})$ and the rank budget is $o(d^{9/4})$. This accuracy requirement applies to this particular correction argument.

The two cheap views must come from *separate* source blocks. The coefficient of matrix multiplication at three unit matrices is just the product of three equality checks on indices, each check comparing indices from two different blocks. It therefore has a short description with overlapping pair inputs even though its rank is superquadratic in $d$ under the cited exponent. A word RAM can build joint views and carry them across arbitrarily many steps. Neither the number of required summands nor the two small widths follow from a native RAM clock. The usual rectangle-to-rank method and Boolean coefficient extraction are classical; priority of this width-specific formulation is unverified.
