# Three local choices preserve the full SAT clock

Fix a finite graph with an ordered list of at most three ports per vertex and an explicitly named coordinate for each edge. Each coordinate joins ports of two distinct vertices. A vertex supplies a list of at most three allowed binary words, one bit per port. Pick one word at each vertex. Its bits define a vector in $\mathbb F_2^E$, supported on the incident coordinates. Ask whether the sum of the chosen vectors equals a specified binary target. Equivalently, the two bits at an edge must agree when its target bit is zero and disagree when it is one. Use an ordinary fixed-alphabet encoding of port lists, edge names, words and target bits. This is a Boolean edge CSP, or the feasibility version of a Boolean Holant instance. [Cai, Lu and Xia on Holant and equality constraints](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~jyc/880/holant-SIAMJComp.pdf) · [Kazda, Kolmogorov and Rolínek on edge CSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03124)

**Clock equivalence.** This problem and explicit ASCII DIMACS 3-SAT reduce to each other with $O(N)$ output bits and $O(N)$ deterministic logarithmic-word RAM time for an $N$-bit input. Consequently they have the same $O(N^j)$ solvability for each fixed $j\ge1$, including the linear, quadratic and cubic targets. The SAT-to-graph direction uses only the two ternary lists
$$
       \operatorname{COPY}=\{000,111\},\qquad
       \operatorname{EXACTONE}=\{100,010,001\}.
$$
Allowing four options per vertex includes these instances. With at most two options per vertex there is a deterministic linear-time algorithm.

## The two reductions

Give each clause of three literals a selector with the three EXACTONE alternatives $s_1,s_2,s_3$. For each position $j$, enforce $s_j\Rightarrow b_j$ where $b_j$ is that literal's truth value. Also enforce $s_j\Rightarrow\neg b_i$ for $i<j$. Thus a satisfying assignment forces selection of exactly its first true literal. One implication $s\Rightarrow b$ is implemented by $\operatorname{EXACTONE}(s,1-b,u)$ with a free terminal $u$. There is precisely one possible $u$ on each allowed pair. Negating a port just toggles its edge target. A short nonempty clause can repeat its last literal to reach width three. A zero-clause formula and an empty clause can be handled by fixed yes and no instances.

Use a connected network of COPY vertices for each variable that appears and for each selector output requiring multiple copies. A tree with $r$ external ports and $r-2$ ternary vertices suffices for $r\ge3$. A one-port free terminal is obtained by subdividing one edge of $K_4$ and attaching the external wire at the new degree-two vertex. A binary copy is obtained by deleting one edge of $K_4$ and attaching two external wires at its endpoints. All internal edges of these networks have target zero. Once their common external value is fixed, all their internal words are unique. Ordinary wires suffice where a value has just one destination. The number of vertices and edges is $O(m)$ for $m$ retained clauses. The choices are in bijection with satisfying assignments on the occurring variables. If a DIMACS header declares unused variables, ignoring them preserves the decision verdict.

To bound encoded length, discard duplicate ordered literal triples after normalizing their numerical tokens. A byte trie interns the names and a separate trie recognizes duplicate triples in linear total work, even with very long decimal identifiers. Of $m$ distinct normalized triples, at least half require $\Omega(\log(m+1))$ bits by fixed-alphabet string counting. Their total length is bounded by a constant times the input length. Thus $m\log(m+1)=O(N)$. Fresh consecutive edge identifiers cost $O(\log(m+1))$ bits each. Building the constant number of vertices per retained literal, linking occurrences by their interned names and emitting the graph all fit $O(N)$ native steps. [Related DIMACS deduplication argument](one-edit-cubic-sat-reduction.md)

Conversely use one SAT variable $x_e$ for each edge. Its two endpoint bits are $x_e$ and $x_e\oplus t_e$ for the given target $t_e$. At each vertex forbid every missing local word by a clause of at most three literals. There are at most eight clauses there. Isolated vertices may be checked and dropped. With $e$ distinct coordinate names there are at most $2e$ nonisolated vertices, and $e\log(e+1)=O(N)$ by the same distinct-string bound. Interning the input names and emitting consecutive DIMACS identifiers therefore take linear time and space in words and produce $O(N)$ bits. If a dense proper three-literal output is wanted, force three private variables false by the seven proper clauses excluding all other settings and pad short clauses with distinct false variables. Edge variables omitted from the emitted clauses are unconstrained. They can be made to occur by adding a proper clause containing that variable and a fixed true literal. For the decision variant, a single encountered zero-arity contradiction can give a fixed unsatisfiable output.

## Two unsigned tables of exactly three words suffice

The same native decision clocks hold on simple cubic graphs using only ordinary unsigned wires, with each vertex of one of two types. The first is $E=\mathrm{EXACTONE}$. The second has two designated through ports and a tap, with $R(a,b;c)$ requiring $a=b$ and $c\le a$. Its three possibilities are $000,110,111$. A triangle of $R$ vertices simulates a ternary COPY: at vertex $i$ equate its exterior bit $x_i$ to its outgoing triangle wire and take the incoming wire as tap. This imposes $x_{i-1}\le x_i$ cyclically. All three exterior bits are equal and their common value fixes all three internal wires.

Use the two-terminal diamond with five internal edges $a,b,c,d,e$ and constraints $R(x,b;a)$, $R(y,d;c)$, $R(c,e;a)$, and $E(b,d,e)$. Its only boundary choices are $(x,y)=(0,1),(1,0)$, each with a unique filling. It can therefore replace every signed negation. To ensure simplicity even when earlier gadgets had repeated adjacencies, first insert a private COPY identity buffer ($K_4$ minus an edge as above) along every logical wire, replacing negations by diamonds as required. Expand the COPY nodes into $R$ triangles. All external endpoints of each buffer are distinct. The resulting graph is simple and cubic, with the same number of solutions on occurring-variable sources. There is constant overhead per wire. In the reverse direction a vertex forbids five of its eight settings; each setting is forbidden by one proper ternary clause over its distinct incident edges. Graph input can be validated by interning identifiers and checking reciprocal port assignments and distinct neighbors in linear input time. [Local Lean proofs](UnsignedCubicGadgets.lean) · [Exhaustive gadget audit](unsigned_cubic_gadgets_audit.py)

Every such graph has a feasible three-sheet cover. At each $E$ use its three unit vectors once each across the sheets; at each $R$ use $000,000,111$. Every endpoint uses its one bit once and its zero bit twice. Pair equal bits over each edge to obtain the lift. Three is sharp for a uniform guarantee: take $K_4$ with one $E$ in the center and an $R$ triangle as the rim above. In a cover of degree $d$, let $t_i$ be the number of selected exterior bits over rim vertex $i$. The tap inequalities imply $t_{i-1}\le t_i$ cyclically, so all three counts agree. Summing over the center gives $3t_i=d$. Only degrees divisible by three can be feasible on this base. The freely chosen covers in this argument need not decide feasibility of the base graph. [Graph covers and local marginals](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.0065)

## Which local choices carry the difficulty?

With one or two choices at each vertex, index them by a bit $x_v$, treating a singleton as a constant. The contribution at a port is an affine Boolean function of $x_v$. Every edge gives a constant, a unary constraint, or an equation $x_v\oplus x_w=b$. A graph traversal propagates along each component and checks for contradictory cycles or unary conditions. Parsing and traversal take $O(N)$ time. If all vertices have at most two *ports*, arbitrary local lists also give linear time by a traversal of each path or cycle with a two-state transition table.

There is a more precise distinction for the three-option, three-port case. A relation on at most three coordinates is expressible in 2-CNF if it is closed under coordinatewise majority. Indeed, if all pair projections of a missing ternary word occurred, take one witness for each designated pair. Their majority would be the missing word. Clauses excluding the absent pair and unary patterns therefore suffice. Conversely all binary clauses are closed under majority. For at most three allowed words the only way closure fails is that their majority is absent. Translating the majority to $000$ leaves three disjoint nonempty supports in three positions, hence exactly the three unit vectors. If there are $t$ such vertices, enumerate their $3^t$ choices and solve the remaining 2-SAT instances in $O(3^t N)$ time.

A second algorithm applies if no allowed local table contains a pair at Hamming distance three. Besides translated EXACTONE, a three-word table that depends on all three port labels can be analyzed by translating its majority to zero. When the majority belongs to the table, its two other words have disjoint nonempty supports. Their distance is at most two, so both supports have size one. This gives a signed AT-MOST-ONE constraint on two ports and pins any third port. Two distinct words either pin extras and leave one free bit, or pin extras and enforce a signed equality on two bits. The latter is EXACTONE on the pair after a possible sign change. A singleton fixes every port. These descriptions include all tables of smaller arity. All lists of uniform local parity satisfy the no-distance-three condition.

Propagate pins and reject contradictions. An exact-one constraint already containing a selected fixed port forces its other ports to zero. The corresponding rule holds for at-most-one. A bounded-degree queue processes this simplification in linear time. The remaining vertices require either exactly one selected port or at most one. A free one-port terminal is of the latter kind. An equal-bit edge is an ordinary candidate matching edge. For an opposite-bit edge insert a fresh midpoint requiring exactly one of its two incident edges. The task is now to find a matching covering all mandatory vertices, with the at-most-one vertices optional. Take two disjoint copies of the graph and join each optional vertex to its twin. A matching covering the mandatory vertices extends to a perfect matching on the double by matching the unused optional twins. A perfect matching restricts to a matching covering the original mandatory vertices in the first copy. The reduction gives deterministic time $O(N+e\sqrt e)$. [Micali–Vazirani matching algorithm and proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.03582) · [More general even delta-matroid edge CSP](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03124)

Together these tests classify the fixed libraries under arbitrary signed wires and port permutations. Suppose one table has a missing majority, and a possibly different table contains a distance-three pair. The first implements EXACTONE. If the second table has two words, it is a translated COPY. If it has three words, translate its middle word to zero. The other two words then have disjoint supports of sizes one and two, so the table is a translated permutation of $R=\{000,110,111\}$. This relation $R(a,b;c)$ enforces $a=b$ and $c\le a$. Put $R(x_i,f_i;f_{i-1})$ on the three vertices of a triangle, with subscripts modulo three. Every internal $f_i=x_i$, and the cyclic inequalities force the three external bits equal. Each legal assignment extends uniquely. Consequently both conditions again give the full SAT clock via COPY and EXACTONE. When either condition fails, one of the preceding two algorithms applies. These are upper bounds and reductions. They do not require separating unrestricted SAT from polynomial time. General fanout classifications for Boolean constraint systems predate this small-table analysis. [Feder, *Fanout Limitations on Constraint Systems*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-3975(99)00288-1)

## A fixed four-choice affine set for all formulas

There is also a formulation with two *succinctly described* vector families, one of them independent of the clauses. On a formula with $m$ nonempty clauses of width three, list their $3m$ literal-falsity bits as $A(x)$, where $x$ assigns the occurring names. Each coordinate is one signed selector bit. The other vector belongs to the affine space
$$
 U_m=\{u\in\mathbb F_2^{3m}:u_{3j}+u_{3j+1}+u_{3j+2}=1\text{ for }0\le j<m\}
     =\{100,010,001,111\}^m.
$$
The formula is satisfiable exactly when $A(x)\odot u=0$ for some $x$ and $u\in U_m$, with $\odot$ denoting coordinatewise multiplication. A true literal supplies a permitted unit vector in its clause. A false clause would require the impossible local vector $000$. Thus both transformations between signed-projection files for this relation and DIMACS copy a constant number of records per clause. The repeated-name conventions and byte bounds above apply. This map preserves existence. A clause with several true literals can admit several different $u$ choices.

More generally, fix a finite collection of allowed Boolean masks $V\subseteq\{0,1\}^b$ of constant width $b$ and use $V$ independently in every block. A set $T$ of positions *hits* $V$ if it intersects the support of every mask. A block with falsity bits $a_i$ rejects exactly when the positions with $a_i=1$ contain an inclusion-minimal hitting set. Equivalently its constraint is the conjunction, over minimal hitting sets $T$, of the clauses $\bigvee_{i\in T}\neg a_i$. If every such $T$ has at most two positions, signed-projection instances reduce to 2-SAT in linear time. If some $T$ has at least three positions, fix all coordinates outside $T$ to zero and all but three inside $T$ to one. Minimality then yields exactly one ternary clause on the three remaining falsity bits. These fixed block instances have the full 3-SAT clock. In the opposite direction the constant-width clauses for $V$ reduce to 3-CNF with a constant number of gates per clause, after deduplicating repeated records as above. This classification permits constant selector coordinates. The empty palette rejects every block and a palette containing the zero mask accepts every block.

For an affine mask family $u_i=t_i+g_i\cdot y$, a minimal hitting set is exactly a minimally dependent set of the rows $g_i$ whose $t_i$ sum to one in $\mathbb F_2$. Indeed $a_i=1$ requires $g_i\cdot y=t_i$ there. Inconsistency gives a zero row sum with odd right-hand side. A strictly smaller dependency in a minimal inconsistent set either already has odd right-hand side or can be subtracted to obtain a smaller odd dependency. In $U_1$ the three rows may be written as $p,q,1+p+q$, so all three form the unique minimal hitting set. Any block with at most two alternatives is easier even when its width varies: use one Boolean choice bit and one binary implication per nonzero mask entry requiring that an activated entry have $a_i=0$. This makes local affine dimension two the first possible dimension that retains the full SAT clock. The setting is related to satisfiability of products of affine forms over the binary field. [Arvind and Guruswami, *CNF Satisfiability in a Subspace and Related Problems*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.05914)

## Fixing the total number of groups

There is a separate restriction under which the desired lower clocks are already upper bounds. Consider $k$ **total** groups, each listing arbitrarily many explicit sparse vectors in $\mathbb F_2^d$. Use sorted nonzero coordinate lists with canonical fixed-alphabet names and delimiters. A fixed $k$ can be solved deterministically in $O_k(N^{\lceil k/2\rceil})$ word operations. Thus four total groups admit quadratic time and six admit cubic time. This concerns addition of the vectors. In orthogonal vectors the coordinate condition instead uses a product. [Horowitz and Sahni on meeting in the middle](https://cise.ufl.edu/~sahni/papers/computingPartitions.pdf)

For target zero, split the groups into two halves and enumerate each half's sums by merging the coordinate lists and canceling equal names. Intern the names and assign lexicographic ranks by a trie traversal for constant-time comparisons. Serialize a resulting support with the original canonical names. Its length is bounded by the sum of the lengths of its selected inputs plus constant overhead. For a half with $h$ groups, each input vector is used in at most $N^{h-1}$ tuples. Hence all enumerations, merges and serializations together cost $O_k(N^h)$ per half. Put the first half's strings into a trie over the fixed alphabet and query the second half's strings there. A terminal flag handles the empty vector. The time and space fit the stated bounds on a RAM with the fixed polynomial address range for that $k$.

For a nonzero target $T$, simply attaching $T$ to every enumerated tuple can overcharge its length. Give each coordinate weight equal to its canonical name length plus a delimiter, and let $\mu$ be the sum of those weights on a vector's support. The triangle inequality holds because the support of a sum lies in the union. At least one vector in a solution has $\mu(v)\ge\mu(T)/k$. Branch on its group, restrict that group to such vectors, and add $T$ to each retained vector there. There are at most $O(kN/\mu(T))$ retained candidates, so the transformed input still has size $O_k(N)$. The reduced target is zero. This proves the bound without allocating a full dimension-length array for each candidate sum.

There is a separate witness-count obstruction even for arbitrary predicates on the tuple. Take $m$ disjoint positive clauses on $3m$ different variables in proper dense DIMACS. Their encoded length is $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ and they have $7^m$ satisfying assignments. If every assignment must give a different tuple, $k$ explicitly listed groups of total encoded length $L$ must have $(L/k)^k\ge7^m$, by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality on their cardinalities. Each option here occupies at least one encoded bit, including a delimiter for an empty option. For fixed $k$ even the output length must then be $2^{\Omega(N/\log N)}$. With $L=O(N)$ this requires $k=\Omega(N/(\log N\log\log N))$: writing $z=N/k$, the inequality gives $z=O(\log N\log(2+z))$, hence $z=O(\log N\log\log N)$. This applies to parsimonious reductions and leaves open reductions that preserve only the existence of a witness.

The edge-CSP equivalence gives an algebraic statement with exactly the original RAM clock. A superlinear lower bound on unrestricted mixing of COPY and EXACTONE, including their wiring input, is still needed to exclude a linear SAT solver. The encoding reductions use established constraint-satisfaction gadgets and matching methods. Priority for this particular encoded-length formulation is unverified.
