# XOR hashes on the seven-tile compiler

The construction below uses the free-boundary signed-interval system of the single-lane reduction. The physical anchored permutation also has external boundary conditions. [Compiler and signed system](one-permuted-lane-and-the-sat-clock.md) · [Local port lemma](SevenTrianglePorts.lean)

Use the free-boundary signed system with first source colour zero. There are \(n\ge2\) source vertices, in red order, and \(n\) seven-tip tiles. Write \(H\) for the number of source colourings with that first colour fixed, and \(J=H/2\) for the number after also fixing the second red colour to one. The red edge guarantees distinctness; reflection about zero pairs the two orientations.

**Exact local coordinates.** Write \(a\) for a tile's first base, \(\theta\in\{1,-1\}\) for its first red increment and \(\delta\in\{1,-1\}\) for its final base minus \(a\), all in \(\mathbb F_3\). The local tip order is \(1,4,3,5,6,0,2\). Its four legal sign strings are precisely
\[
(\theta,\theta,-\theta,\theta,\theta,-\delta,-\delta).
\]
After subtracting \(a\), its eight base colours are \((0,\theta,-\theta,\theta,-\theta,0,-\delta,\delta)\) and its seven tip colours are \((-\theta,0,0,0,\theta,\delta,0)\). Direct finite evaluation checks these identities; it is enough to test the 128 possible sign strings. The two exposed tips both have colour \(a\). Thus the first \(n-1\) terminal differences are exactly the source red increments, and the last terminal difference is free.

**Signed witness hashes.** Code each positive sign by zero and each negative sign by one. Over \(\mathbb F_2\), toggling the first five bits in any tile changes its independent \(\theta_i\). Toggling the last two bits of the last tile changes its free \(\delta_n\). These \(n+1\) disjoint masks span a space \(D_0\), common to all source colourings. Signed target solutions are disjoint translates of \(D_0\), one per fixed-first-colour source witness. Adjoining the all-ones mask reflects the whole configuration and gives a space \(D\) of dimension \(n+2\) since \(n\ge2\). Its solution cosets are indexed by the \(J\) doubly normalized source witnesses.

For any binary affine hash \(Mx=w\), write \(r=\operatorname{rank}(M|_D)\). Each source orbit contributes either zero or exactly \(2^{n+2-r}\) solutions to that bucket. In particular no hash of restricted rank below \(n+2\) can isolate a unique signed witness. With exactly \(n+2\) rows of full restricted rank every syndrome has exactly \(J\) solutions. Concrete coordinate pins attaining this in every instance are the first sign in all tiles and the sixth signs in the first and last tiles (one-based numbering). Dropping the first tile's sixth-sign pin gives exactly \(H\) on every syndrome with the remaining \(n+1\) pins. If the source has a unique colouring modulo palette symmetries, the stronger pin set isolates. With several source orbits, additional independent information can select among them. For \(n=1\) the final-endpoint flip and the early flip already span reflection, and there are just four local sign strings.

There is an exact reduced hash even when the number of rows exceeds the rank. Let \(d\) be the \(n-1\) binary source sign bits and \(u=(\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_n,\delta_n)\) their independent binary tile coordinates. The four-string description gives an injective affine map \(x=L_u u+L_d d+c\) independent of the source order. Eliminate the \(u\)-columns in \(M L_u u+M L_d d=w+Mc\) by Gaussian elimination. If \(r_0=\operatorname{rank}(M L_u)\), the resulting residual system \(Bd=t\) obeys the exact integer identity
\[
\#\text{target bucket}=2^{n+1-r_0}\#\{\text{legal source signs }d:Bd=t\}.
\]
One can similarly eliminate the global reflection bit after using \(d_i+d_1\) as normalized source signs. This describes the fate of affine hashes in this compiler. Randomized isolation of SAT itself is classical. [Valiant and Vazirani](https://ics.uci.edu/~vazirani/ValVaz.pdf)

**Raw colour flags.** For each fixed labelled source colouring, the one-hot flags of all target colours form an affine binary cube of dimension \(n+1\). In tile \(i\), the early choice affects only bases 1,2,3,4 and tips 0,4. At each of these six sites, changing it toggles the flags of the two colours other than \(a_i\). The final tile's free endpoint choice independently affects bases 6,7 and tip 5. All these sets of positions are disjoint; both label choices at every affected position are distinct. Here the subspace directions depend on \(a_i\). A raw affine hash has a possibly different restriction rank on each source fibre, with the corresponding rank-nullity count. Imposing \(y_1=y_7\) within every tile by two flag XOR equalities (equality of colours 0 and 1 suffices for one-hot vectors) selects \(\theta_i=\delta_i\). Two further equalities fix the terminal colour of the last tile to the preceding *source* vertex's colour, which differs from the last source colour. The zero bucket of these fixed tests has exactly \(H\) colourings, and pinning the second source colour to one gives exactly \(J\). These statements concern actual colour flags and signed witnesses. A CNF encoding with additional free auxiliaries requires its own normalization.

**Bounded-degree binary hashes.** Let a binary hash bucket be cut out by polynomial equations of degrees at most \(d_1,\ldots,d_s\), and \(D_*=\max_j d_j\ge1\). On any affine witness fibre of dimension \(h\) its population is divisible by
\[
2^{\max(0,\lceil(h-\sum_j d_j)/D_*\rceil)}.
\]
Use \(h=n+2\) on signed source-orbit fibres, or \(h=n+1\) on raw labelled fibres. The estimate applies to their union by summation. In particular total degree below \(h\) cannot produce an odd bucket. On signed fibres this total-degree threshold is sharp per source orbit. Partition the \(h\) independent pins above into blocks and for each impose the single equation \(1+\prod_{i\text{ in block}}(1+x_i+w_i)=0\). Its degree is the block size, and the tests together pick exactly one point per orbit.

For a direct proof, restrict the equations to a fibre and absorb the requested output into the constant terms. Write each as an XOR of squarefree Boolean monomials. The integer indicator of its zero set is \((1+\prod_{m}(1-2m))/2\). Expanding the product of these indicators, a term selecting \(t_j\) monomials from polynomial \(j\) has coefficient divisible by \(2^E\) where \(E=\sum_j\max(t_j-1,0)\). If its union involves \(v\) variables, then \(v\le\sum_j d_j+D_*E\); its sum on the cube contributes another \(2^{h-v}\). Hence \(D_*(h-v+E)\ge h-\sum_jd_j\). This is the binary Ax–Katz divisibility bound, specialized to the compiler's affine fibres. [Ax](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/goren/SeminarOnCohomology/Ax.pdf) · [Katz, Theorem 1.0](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/goren/SeminarOnCohomology/Katz.pdf)

**Verification.** The Lean proof enumerates all 128 local sign strings and verifies the displayed raw colors and both independent bit switches. The executable audit checks all source orders of sizes two through four and eight deterministic samples of size five. It reconstructs 5,152 legal sign strings from the physical tip inequalities, checks the two pin projections, and compares actual signed and one-hot color bucket counts with affine-rank predictions for 200 pairs of hash matrices. The rank-nullity and nonlinear divisibility arguments above are manuscript proofs. Run `lean SevenTileIsolation.lean` and `python3 seven_tile_hash_audit.py` from the checkout.
