# The cost of deciding existence when opposite siblings must yield a timely answer

A timed SAT solver can be used on two restrictions of a formula. Suppose that whenever the restrictions have different verdicts, at least one of the runs returns a sound answer by its deadline. Could a constant number of cleverly chosen pairs then decide whether the source is satisfiable? In a model granting arbitrary filters on the assignment set, there is an exact answer. Even **decision** on $2^v$ possibilities requires $v$ pair calls if only one direct assignment check is allowed. This remains true on the promise of at most $v+1$ witnesses. With at most one witness the corresponding number is $\lceil\log_3(2^v)\rceil$, and overlapping filters attain it. These are costs of a black-box use of timed answers. Actual programs can inspect the clauses or exploit structure in their own executions. [Single-bit DIMACS restrictions and the timed search](one-bit-hard-pairs-at-consecutive-sat-lengths.md)

## Exact game

There is an unknown set $W$ of marked points in a public universe of size $M\ge1$. The task is to decide $W\ne\varnothing$. A point test at $x$ returns the bit $[x\in W]$. A pair call specifies any subsets $A,B$ and gets two optional Boolean replies. Each reported bit must equal the truth of intersection with $W$ on its side. If those two truths differ, at least one bit must be reported. With equal truths both replies may time out. The pair is the context of the two replies. Repeating the same call may be required to return the recorded replies; the bounds are the same. Filters may overlap, computation is free and descriptions can have arbitrary length.

For deterministic adaptive algorithms with a budget of at most $q$ pair calls and $u$ point tests on every run, the exact condition for success is
$$
       M\le b^q u,\qquad
 b=2\quad\hbox{for general }W,\qquad b=3\quad\hbox{for }|W|\le1 .
$$
For costs $A_0,B_0>0$ per call and per point test respectively, the exact worst-case cost is
$$
 \min_{0\le q\le\lceil\log_b M\rceil}
 \left(A_0q+B_0\left\lceil M/b^q\right\rceil\right).
$$
The cost formula permits different numbers of calls on different runs. It does not charge their maximum counts separately.

For the binary upper bound retain a set $R$ that contains a witness whenever the original does. Split $R$ into two balanced disjoint parts and submit them as the filters. A timely YES decides existence. A timely NO on a part allows retaining its complement in $R$. If both replies time out then the two parts have the same truth; when a witness exists in $R$, each part contains one. Choose either part. After $q$ pairs at most $\lceil M/2^q\rceil$ points need direct testing.

Under the uniqueness promise partition $R$ into balanced disjoint parts $L,D,H$ and query $(L\cup D,D\cup H)$. Following a timely NO retain its complement in $R$. Both timeouts force the labels equal. A unique witness in $R$ would then have to lie in $D$. A timely YES already decides existence. This gives the corresponding upper bound with radix three. Without the uniqueness promise, a witness in $L$ and another in $H$ make both labels true even with $D$ empty.

## Why merging surviving witness sets matters

Consider an all-negative point-test transcript for the empty input. For pair calls use only three sorts of replies: NO on $A$ alone, NO on $B$ alone, or both time out. These are always legal for the empty set. A candidate witness set remains possible after such a transcript if it avoids all negatively tested points and filters, and hits both sides or neither side of every both-timeout pair. This condition is closed under union. Thus disjoint alternatives that conflict individually with a *new* both-timeout response can sometimes be merged into an alternative consistent with all replies.

Initially keep the $M$ singleton sets as disjoint nonempty alternatives. At a pair call write $z,a,b',c$ for the numbers of current alternatives whose intersection indicators with the two filters are $00,01,10,11$. NO on $A$ leaves $z+a$, and NO on $B$ leaves $z+b'$. Two timeouts keep the $z+c$ alternatives of equal type and allow merging $\min(a,b')$ disjoint $01,10$ pairs into new alternatives of type $11$. All resulting alternatives are disjoint and each is compatible with every previous response. If $a\ge b'$, the counts for NO on $A$ and for both timeouts sum to at least $z+a+b'+c$; the other case uses NO on $B$. Choose a reply retaining at least half the current count, rounded up. On a point test the answer NO removes at most one disjoint alternative. A previously asked pair can simply be replayed, keeping all current alternatives. All selected replies therefore extend to a valid deterministic table keyed by pairs for each surviving hidden input.

For the singleton promise, keep separate points. NO on $A$, NO on $B$, and both timeouts respectively keep $z+a,z+b',z+c$. The largest is at least a third of the total. A point test again removes at most one. A singleton lower bound with only disjoint submitted filters already has radix two, since the $11$ class is empty there.

Write $r$ for the number of surviving nonempty alternatives, and let $q',u'$ count the calls and point tests so far. Then $M\le b^{q'}(r+u')$. A pair loses a factor at most $b$, and a point loses at most one; the inequality holds in every order. A correct algorithm can declare the empty verdict only at $r=0$, since any surviving alternative produces the same complete transcript. Applying the inequality to that run proves both lower bounds, including the weighted bound with its actual $q',u'$.

**Sparse witnesses suffice for the binary bound.** After a transcript with $q'$ pairs every compatible nonempty alternative $S$ contains a compatible nonempty subset of size at most $q'+1$. Start with any point of $S$. If a both-timeout pair currently hits only one side, $S$ contains a point on its missing side. Add one such point. That pair is now hit on both sides forever. Each repair permanently resolves a distinct pair, so at most $q'$ points are added. The subset still avoids every negatively tested point or filter. Apply this separately to the disjoint alternatives after the final transcript. Thus for any fixed $q$ the exact budget condition $M\le2^q u$ still holds on the promise $|W|\le q+1$. For example, the claimed $v$-call lower bound with one point test follows by excluding any budget $q<v$ even if the overall promise allows $v+1$ witnesses. The $q'+1$ bound on trimming a given transcript is sharp: on $q'+1$ points query the singleton sets at each two consecutive points and suppose both replies always time out. The resulting equality constraints force a witness set containing any point to contain all points. This establishes sharpness for the trimming lemma. The argument is an oracle adversary and supplies no short explicit CNF describing its chosen set.

## Implementing the trisection filters on proper one-bit SAT pairs

For a dense source with $v$ occurring variables and at most one satisfying assignment, view assignments in numeric order. Retain one interval and trisect it into consecutive intervals. The unions in the trisection procedure are intervals. Each can be computed from binary endpoint comparisons by a Boolean circuit with $O(v)$ gates. Use shared source variables and a Tseitin encoding whose auxiliary outputs equal their prescribed Boolean values on every assignment. Enlarge one- or two-literal clauses to proper clauses using fixed fresh false variables or the four sign cases. Create two fresh named selector variables whose values equal the two interval predicates and whose names have identical decimal prefixes and terminal digits 2 and 3. The sole edited clause consists of two dedicated false variables and the positive name of one selector. Keep every other clause from using **both** dedicated names together (introduce separate false guards for the circuit). Hence the selector row stays in place on the sorted files and toggling its last digit changes a single bit. If necessary pin unused intermediate names between the fresh identifiers. Every assignment satisfying either target file projects to a source witness inside the corresponding interval, and each source witness there extends. [One-bit selector and exact common-length padding](one-bit-hard-pairs-at-consecutive-sat-lengths.md) · [Threshold comparator construction](majority-thresholds-for-fallible-sat-programs.md)

This construction has $O(n)$ encoded bits and costs $O(n/\log n)$ native steps per query after normalization in the journal RAM convention. There are $O(n/\log n)$ distinct source clauses and this also bounds the number $v$ of variables. Thus $O(v)$ new gates and dense names cost $O(n)$ serialized bits by $v\log(v+2)=O(n)$. The two interval endpoints, as $v$-bit integers, can be computed in $O(v)$ single-bit operations, including trisection by the constant three. Under the timely-opposite guarantee, a fixed globally sound portfolio using timeout $t$ and $h$ common padded lengths can decide this promised source with $\lceil v/\log_2 3\rceil$ paired queries and one final assignment check. The same materialization and reset costs as in the binary sibling search apply. The selector result is confined to the unique-witness promise. Establishing an unrestricted lower bound from an actual SAT solver would require more than the reply interface analyzed here.
