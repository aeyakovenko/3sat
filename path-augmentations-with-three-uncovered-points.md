# Every successful repair can be a single path

Given two permutations on $q$ points, take the $q$ triples $\{i,P(i),Q(i)\}$ (or their inverse-permutation convention). An incumbent packing is a supplied disjoint set of triples leaving precisely three points uncovered. For a perfect cover discard the triples common to it and the incumbent. Its **simple conflict graph** has one vertex per remaining old or new triple, and one edge for each old–new pair that intersects.

**Theorem.** Explicit 3-SAT reduces, in deterministic $O(N)$ native steps with $O(N)$ output bits, to instances whose every perfect cover has a simple conflict graph that is a single path. The perfect covers are in bijection with the satisfying assignments on occurring variables. Each formula variable has a designated distinct column selected precisely on its true assignments. The construction also supplies a signed exact cover in $\{-1,0,1\}$ with exactly one negative coordinate and an incumbent packing missing three points. These assertions hold simultaneously. Thus deciding even whether these path repairs exist has the same linear, quadratic and cubic solvability thresholds as unrestricted explicit SAT. The lower clocks themselves remain unproved.

## A path with incompatible pairs

Construct a directed acyclic graph with a source $s$, a sink $t$, and colors on the other vertices. A path may visit at most one vertex of each color. The forbidden-pairs path problem is classical; the incidence construction below uses the degree bounds and first-true choice explicitly. [Kolman and Pangrác, *On the Complexity of Paths Avoiding Forbidden Pairs*](https://kam.mff.cuni.cz/~kolman/papers/fpath3.pdf)

In its formula branch the graph first has one stage per occurring variable. Two branches select its value, each with a private singleton marker. Then put one stage per clause, with an option for each literal position $j$. The option demands that literal $j$ be true and every preceding literal be false. Add one vertex to that option's chain for each demand. Pair its color with a fresh blocker on the variable branch that would violate the demand. Each demand occurrence has its own color, even for a repeated literal. The blocker and the clause test each have in-degree and out-degree one. At most six such pairs are required per three-literal clause. All remaining vertices have private colors.

Implement three alternatives with nested binary forks and merges. Successive stages meet at an unconditional junction. Every variable value has its own marker including on empty tracks, and each clause option includes its own test vertex. There are no parallel arcs. Both in-degree and out-degree are at most two. The two vertices of a repeated color are never adjacent: a blocker and its clause test are separated by a stage junction. Add a second, bypass branch directly from the source to the common sink, on which two vertices of one new color are separated by a singleton. Use separate entry and final links for the formula branch so the source and sink retain degree at most two in the usable direction.

Every color-respecting source–sink path must use the formula branch. Its variable choices specify an assignment and only its first true literal can be chosen at any satisfied clause. Conversely that path exists for every satisfying assignment. The incoming arc at the true marker of a variable records that variable's value. Empty conjunctions have a single formula route; an input containing an empty clause may use any fixed contradiction with the same zero count. Compact names and discard exact duplicate clauses as in the [linear encoded SAT compiler](two-permutations-and-the-explicit-sat-clock.md). Neither operation changes the assignment count on occurring variables.

## From a colored path to a packing

For each color $c$ create three distinct points $O_c$ and include $O_c$ as an incumbent triple. Give each vertex of color $c$ a different point in $O_c$. There are at most two such vertices; the vacant slots are dummy points. The source is instead an uncovered point $s$, given no color. Reserve two other uncovered points $h_2,h_3$. For each directed arc $u\to v$ introduce a new column
$$
            \{u\}\ \cup\ (O_{c(v)}\setminus\{v\}),
$$
where a vertex name now means its point. Finally include the terminal column $\{t,h_2,h_3\}$. There is no arc into $s$ or out of $t$. Every arc goes between distinct groups or starts at $s$, so these are proper triples. No duplicate column arises: an arc column identifies its target group by the two points it retains, its target by the point it omits, and its source by the remaining point.

The degree of an ordinary point $v\in O_c$ is
$$
 1+\operatorname{outdeg}(v)+[v=t]+
       \sum_{w\in O_c\setminus\{v\}}\operatorname{indeg}(w).
$$
A dummy contributes zero in-degree and out-degree. At a paired color both real points have in-degree and out-degree one, so each has degree three and its dummy also has degree three. At a singleton the real point has degree at most three (the sink has no outgoing arc), and each dummy has degree at most three by the in-degree bound. The three holes also have degree at most three.

Fill the missing incidences in batches of three; their total is three times the number of points minus three times the number of columns. For each batch add external filler columns $a,b,c$ with one slot apiece at its three deficient points and add three fresh internal points whose exact-one rows are
$$
                  (a,b,f),\qquad (a,c,f),\qquad(b,c,f).
$$
The fourth new column $f$ consists of the three internal points and is added to the incumbent. Subtracting the row equations gives $a=b=c$. Over Boolean assignments this forces $a=b=c=0$ and $f=1$. Each filler has its two distinct internal points in addition to its one external point. A batch can have two or three deficient slots on the same old point: they use different filler columns. All points and columns now have degree three; the only uncovered points are the three original holes. No perfect cover ever changes a regularizing column from the new incumbent.

## Exact decoding and the shape of every repair

In a perfect cover, all alternatives targeting the same group use two of its three points. Any two intersect. The old triple excludes all of them. At least one group column (old or one incoming alternative) must be selected because all groups have a dummy point, which cannot be captured from outside and the regularizing fillers are zero. Thus exactly one is selected. Write $a(v)$ for the number of selected arcs into $v$, and $b(v)$ for the number of selected arcs out of $v$. At point $v$ the old-or-incoming column already contributes $1-a(v)$. Exactness gives
$$
                      a(v)=b(v)+[v=t].
$$
The terminal is forced by the two slack holes. Exactly one arc leaves $s$, and at most one arc enters each group. Following that arc therefore yields an $s$–$t$ path. Any additional selected arcs would form a directed cycle by conservation away from the endpoints, excluded by acyclicity. The path uses at most one vertex of each color. Conversely, choose the arc columns of a color-respecting path, the terminal, the incumbents of unvisited groups and all regularizer incumbents. The same row calculation gives an exact cover. There is one cover per path, without independent choices elsewhere.

Let the path be $s=v_0,v_1,\ldots,v_j=t$. Its $j$ removed triples are $O_{c(v_1)},\ldots,O_{c(v_j)}$. The new column for arc $i$ uses two points of $O_{c(v_i)}$ and one point of $O_{c(v_{i-1})}$ for $i>1$; the first uses the source hole instead. The terminal uses just one point of $O_{c(t)}$ and both remaining holes. Therefore the **entire** difference, including any components that could have been neutral, is the path
$$
 A_1, O_{c(v_1)}, A_2, O_{c(v_2)}, \ldots, A_j,O_{c(t)}, T .
$$
Here $A_i$ denotes the new arc column and $T$ the terminal. All these vertices are distinct and there are no other intersections. The simple conflict graph has pathwidth one and cycle rank zero. The ambient instance can have arbitrarily many unrelated arcs. The proof uses doubles of point intersections in the new–old pairs; it does not establish this hardness for linear hypergraphs where two triples may meet at most once.

The regular bipartite incidence graph splits into three perfect matchings. Identify the first matching to relabel columns by rows; the other two become permutation arrays. Invert them if the triples are encoded as the images of a selected column rather than the incidences of a row. Carry all packing, signed and marker labels through this public relabeling. Cole, Ost and Schirra's deterministic bipartite edge-coloring algorithm uses $O(E\log \Delta)=O(E)$ ordinary word operations at degree three. Even $O(\log E)$ native work per arithmetic operation fits the required encoded clock. With $m$ distinct normalized clauses there are $O(m)$ vertices and incidences; the names and auxiliary lists cost $O(m\log(m+1))=O(N)$ bits by the fixed-alphabet counting argument. The reverse reduction to explicit SAT uses one variable per column and a constant number of constraints per point, as for the ordinary permutation problem. [Cole, Ost and Schirra](https://cs.nyu.edu/~cole/papers/COS00_ab.html) · [Encoding and reverse reduction](two-permutations-and-the-explicit-sat-clock.md)

## A supplied signed solution and local data for arbitrary assignments

Even a path with a repeated color gives a signed solution. Give its arc columns and the terminal weight one. Set the old column of group $c$ to $1-\nu_c$, where $\nu_c$ is the number of visits to that group. Use $a=b=c=0,f=1$ in each regularizer. At a real or dummy point of $O_c$ the old column and incoming alternatives together contribute $1-a(v)$, since exactly $\nu_c$ incoming alternatives are used and $a(v)$ of them omit that point. The outgoing arcs and the terminal supply its complement. Apply this rule to the specified bypass, which visits exactly one color twice. The resulting public $y$ has precisely one $-1$ and all other entries in $\{0,1\}$. The complementary integer vector $\mathbf1-y$ supplies a satisfiable double cover by the [fiber criterion](smallest-false-two-sheet-permutation-cover.md).

For **any** Boolean source assignment, take the selected variable tracks and then the first true option at each clause, defaulting to option one if the clause is false. This always gives a directed path. A satisfied clause causes no color collision. A false clause causes exactly one collision, at the single demanded positive truth test for its default literal (the literal itself may have either sign). Replace the signed old value $1-\nu_c$ by the Boolean bit $[\nu_c=0]$. This changes an old coefficient only on repeated colors, increasing it from $-1$ to zero. The exact integer residual at a point is therefore one precisely on the three points of the default-test color of each false clause, and zero elsewhere. Each such group is specific to its clause, so the row residual is identically zero or exactly the falsity indicator of one known source clause.

Each tentative column bit depends on at most three source variables. An arc internal to a variable stage depends on its variable, an arc internal to a clause stage on that clause's variables, and connecting edges are unconditional or permanently unused. The singleton old bits have the same bounds. A paired old bit ORs visits of one clause option and a blocker for one variable of that very clause. Regularizer and bypass choices are constants. Thus the bits have multilinear representatives of degree at most three over every field by truth-table interpolation. The local residual identity holds pointwise on the Boolean cube over every field. It does not rely on an order or a positivity test in that field. The structural and signed promises above concern actual Boolean/integer instance data.

## Where a bound on the size of a repair does give a fast clock

For **any** three-hole instance of this degree, consider the connected augmenting component of an actual repair. If it removes $j$ old triples, it adds $j+1$ new ones. Counting a separate edge per shared point gives $3j$ edges on $2j+1$ vertices, hence multigraph cycle rank exactly $j$. A three-point overlap forms a separate neutral pair and cannot occur in this component. Two-point overlaps form a matching by disjointness within each packing. If there are $p$ such overlaps, the simple conflict graph instead has cycle rank $j-p$.

One can find a repair with $j$ removed triples in $O(N+2^j)$ native work when it exists, with no hypothesis on the rest of the ambient graph. Preprocess the incumbent and the constant-length incidences of each point. Keep some nonincumbent triples irrevocably committed on the current branch, the surviving incumbents, and a list of holes. Choose an uncovered point and try a nonincumbent triple through it. Reject overlap with a committed triple, remove all old triples hit by the new one, and commit the new triple. Only a constant number of point entries, triple entries and hole-list links change; retain an undo record at each depth. A perfect extension containing the chosen columns always supplies a trial through the next hole. Stop when every point is covered, or when the limit of $j+1$ committed triples is exhausted. On the branch following the desired repair only its old triples are removed.

Give priority to the initial holes. Each has at most three trials, can be chosen at most once on a branch, and never reopens. Every other newly uncovered point initially belonged to an excluded incumbent, so at most two nonincumbent choices remain. The search tree has $O(2^j)$ vertices, including the at most three initial ternary levels. A depth-first traversal costs constant work per vertex after preprocessing. Iterative deepening, with preprocessing retained, gives the stated positive clock when $j$ is unknown. In particular repairs with $j\le\log_2N+O(1)$ yield a linear-time positive fast path. Cygan's bounded-pathwidth local-search algorithm also depends exponentially on the size of the swap. Here the path hardness and the elementary size-parameter algorithm give opposite conclusions for two distinct graph parameters on the same supplied-packing problem. [Cygan, *Improved Approximation for 3-Dimensional Matching via Bounded Pathwidth Local Search*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1304.1424)

The [independent finite audit](path_augmentation_audit.py) normalizes the actual graphs to permutation rows and enumerates every full Boolean cover there with PicoSAT. It checked 169 formulas including short clauses, repeated literals, tautologies, contradictions and empty conjunctions. It verified all 409 covers against the explicit assignment-to-path map, showed their simple conflicts are paths, and checked the three holes, bounded degree, forced fillers and signed solution. All 601 tentative source assignments satisfied the local residual identity. The largest tested permutations had 177 labels. The asymptotic reductions and search clock above are manuscript results; priority for this conjunction of restrictions has not been established.
