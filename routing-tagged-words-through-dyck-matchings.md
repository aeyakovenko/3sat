# Routing tagged records through stacks, and what cancellation changes

Consider $m$ records of $h$ payload bits to be placed at their specified distinct destinations. An unrestricted RAM performs this scatter in $O(m)$ word steps when $h=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$. A proposed tape simulation might use bracket matchings to carry these bits. If every bit physically follows a path to its destination, any arrangement of stack matchings, fixed wires and bounded-degree switches needs
$$
 T=\Omega\left(\frac{mh\log(m+1)}{\log(h+2)}\right)
$$
charged ports. The bit positions of one record may separate, pass through different stacks and be intermixed with bits of other records. The topology can have cycles. At $h=\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ this is $\Omega(B\log B/\log\log B)$ for $B=mh$ payload bits. Arbitrary finite-field tensor networks can use cancellations that have no such physical realization.

## A counting proof on one shared bit position

Fix a topology. A stack has any fixed number of ports in a fixed linear order. Each setting can pair them by a noncrossing partial matching, even one chosen with knowledge of the entire target permutation. These are the pairs available from a well-formed bracket word. A local switch may pair its ports arbitrarily and has degree bounded by a fixed constant. Fixed wires connect ports or terminals. Count all incidences and terminals in $T$, including each port on a stack. On every permutation $\pi$ require simultaneous paths from every bit $(i,j)$ to $(\pi(i),j)$ through the chosen pairings, with no two paths using the same port. It is enough that such a routing exists. Fix one for each $\pi$ and take the paths simple.

For a bit position $j\in[h]$, let $K_j$ be the number of ports in its $m$ paths. Since $\sum_j K_j\le T$, at least half the bit positions use at most $2T/h$ ports. Double-counting over permutations gives one **fixed** bit position $j$ with this cost on at least $m!/2$ permutations. Describe one of them by the subset of its used ports, followed by their pairings. At each stack the restriction to the chosen ports is a noncrossing matching and uses at most one parenthesis bit per chosen endpoint to describe. This holds even when unchosen data paths run between or around the retained pairs. Local switches have at most a constant number of choices per used port. Starting at the labeled input bits, following the recovered pairings and the known fixed wires reconstructs every $\pi(i)$.

For a constant $C$ determined by the local degree the number of descriptions of size at most $u$ visited ports is at most $\sum_{v\le u}\binom{T}{v}C^v$. For $h\ge4$ use $u=\lfloor 2T/h\rfloor$ and the binomial bound to get $\log(m!/2)=O((T/h)\log(2+h))$. For $h\le3$, describing the entire setting uses $O(T)$ bits and yields the same order. The physical number of terminals also gives $T\ge mh$. The calculation applies whenever the representation supports all permutations, regardless of how it is computed or certified.

The bound permits a constant fraction of missing routes. Suppose at least $\theta mh$ correct, mutually port-disjoint bit routes exist for every $\pi$, where $0<\theta\le1$ is fixed. At least $\theta h/2$ bit positions deliver $\theta m/2$ or more bits. At most $\theta h/4$ positions cost more than $4T/(\theta h)$. Some fixed position is therefore cheap and productive for at least $\theta m!/4$ permutations. A description determining $r\ge\theta m/2$ routes extends to at most $(m-r)!$ full permutations, because it specifies the labeled sources as well as their destinations. The logarithm of the ratio between the required number of permutations and this multiplicity is $\Omega_\theta(m\log(m+1))$. The same estimate follows up to $\theta$-dependent constants.

A rearrangeable Beneš network with $O(m\log m)$ switches yields $O(mh\log m)$ ports by moving each payload bit through its record's switches. The two legal positions of a $2\times2$ switch can themselves be represented by noncrossing pairings with alternating input and output terminals. The remaining $\log(2+h)$ gap is real for the subset-counting step in isolation. Take a fixed slot for each input $i$, bit $j$, and base-$h$ digit of $\pi(i)$. At a digit of value $d$, assign bit $j$ to the column $(j+d)\bmod h$. These column assignments are disjoint across $j$, and the subset assigned to any fixed $j$ encodes all the permutation digits. This construction assigns informative subsets only. Realizing the corresponding connections still requires a routing. Ordered tape simulations, oblivious sorting and circuit coding lower bounds place other limits on data movement. Priority of the stated model-specific count is unestablished. ## The sharp boundary between two and three payload symbols

Place $m$ source terminals and $m$ destination terminals alternately around one circle. For a permutation $\pi$, let $\Gamma_\pi(x,y)$ be the product of the equality indicators for its wires. Over a **binary** payload alphabet, the Catalan many noncrossing $\Gamma$ tensors form a basis for the span of *all* $\Gamma_\pi$. The coefficients are integral and the basis remains independent over every field. This is the two-dimensional Temperley–Lieb web straightening rule in the alternating-port convention. [Rhoades, the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ web basis and crossing resolution](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/0B73F34B82335C51BF7253E0104CA7CF/S2050509419000227a.pdf)

To verify the statement over the integers, write $\epsilon_{01}=1,\epsilon_{10}=-1,\epsilon_{00}=\epsilon_{11}=0$. Apply the same invertible matrix $\epsilon$ on each destination leg. This changes equality along each wire into $\epsilon$, up to its orientation sign. Write all chords with their smaller endpoint first. For $i<j<k<l$ two crossing chords resolve by the two-dimensional determinant identity
$$
 \epsilon_{z_i z_k}\epsilon_{z_j z_l}
  =\epsilon_{z_i z_j}\epsilon_{z_k z_l}
    +\epsilon_{z_i z_l}\epsilon_{z_j z_k}.
$$
Each replacement strictly decreases the total number of crossings. For a third chord its endpoints split the circle into two arcs. The four selected endpoints on either arc are consecutive in their cyclic order. For zero, one, three or four of them on the arc its crossings with the selected pairings stay the same. For two adjacent ones on the arc the crossing resolution starts with two intersections and can only decrease that count. Once all crossings are gone, every chord joins opposite parities of terminal because the number of enclosed points is even. Undoing the invertible transform gives only noncrossing source-to-destination equality tensors.

For a product of $\epsilon$ tensors, the lexicographically first binary word where it is nonzero places zero at each smaller endpoint and one at its mate. The coefficient there is one. For noncrossing matchings these words are exactly the distinct Dyck words. The evaluation matrix on these leading words is triangular with diagonal one. This proves independence in every characteristic.

With **three or more** payload symbols, every crossing permutation has an entry at which all noncrossing tensors vanish and $\Gamma_\pi=1$. Choose two crossing wires and give each its own private symbol at both endpoints. Set all remaining entries to a third symbol. Any permutation compatible with these data must include both chosen crossing wires. This rules out every linear combination of noncrossing *whole-record* matches in this one-stack geometry. Two independent bit lanes each allowed their own signed expansion are different from one common whole-record match.

Even on single bits, few explicit summands can fail exponentially. With three sources, the only missing permutation from the five noncrossing pairings joins the three opposite pairs. The signed sum $\sum_{\tau\in S_3}\operatorname{sgn}(\tau)\Gamma_\tau$ is zero: for each binary source there are two equal bits, so swapping them pairs cancelling terms. The missing permutation therefore has five nonzero basis coefficients, all $\pm1$. Put $r$ disjoint copies in consecutive groups of six terminals. The product expansion consists of $5^r$ distinct noncrossing global matchings, and independence proves all $5^r$ are necessary, in every field. Its coefficient rule factors across the groups. Thus an exponential number of summands can have a short implicit description.

## Coefficients exposed by one boundary assignment

For a noncrossing matching $M$, color the endpoints so that its paired terminals agree and every *other* noncrossing matching fails. The least alphabet size with such an assignment is exactly the maximum degree of the tree dual to its disjoint chords. Its vertices are the regions of the disk, with one edge between the two regions separated by each chord. For even alphabet size this is the saturated-structure degree criterion of Haleš, Héliou, Maňuch, Ponty and Stacho: complementing every even-position symbol interchanges their complementary-pair convention and equality, since every noncrossing perfect chord joins opposite parities. [Haleš et al., Theorem 4](https://inria.hal.science/hal-01285499/preview/CombinatorialRNADesign-Hales-Manuch-Ponty-Stacho-Algorithmica-2015.pdf)

Here is a direct proof for any alphabet size. Two chords incident to the same face cannot have the same color: join their midpoints across that face and re-pair their ends along the two sides of a thin strip, keeping all other chords. This gives a distinct noncrossing compatible matching. Conversely, properly edge-color the dual tree using its maximum degree many colors, working from the root and giving each vertex's children different colors distinct from the parent edge. The boundary order traverses every tree edge twice. Map the root to the identity of the free product of one order-two group per color, and map each tree vertex to the word of colors on the path from the root. This embeds the tree in the Cayley tree: a simple path has no successive equal colors, and therefore has nonempty reduced word whenever the path itself is nonempty. For any proposed compatible matching, the word strictly between two paired endpoints reduces to the identity by successively removing its innermost equal-colored pairs. The boundary walk at the pair therefore takes the same undirected Cayley-tree edge out and back. The embedding and the two traversals of each original edge force the proposed pair to be an original pair.

At alphabet size two the dual must be a path. With the cut of the linear token sequence fixed, a path rooted at the initial face has either one chain of $m$ edges or two chains with lengths $k,m-k$. Their bracket directions are exactly the $m$ two-mountain patterns $+^k-^k+^{m-k}-^{m-k}$ for $0\le k<m$. These are precisely the cyclic reflections in the earlier tape note. On each of these matchings evaluating a wire tensor at one suitable binary boundary assignment reads off its basis coefficient. For every other basis matching, no single binary boundary assignment has this property for all tensors in the span. The audit checks the minimal color count and the exact two-mountain list for every noncrossing matching through five source ports. ## Consequence for the tape and RAM question

The preprint uses tagged record routing to obtain its list lower bound, including the case of finite fields with ports. The port count excludes a subpower-logarithmic-overhead implementation of that routing by literal stack paths on logarithmic-width records. Finite-field contraction is a stronger operation. Given a true permutation $\pi$, write $c(\pi,d)$ for the unique coefficient of its binary wire tensor at the noncrossing matching described by Dyck directions $d$. Set it to zero for invalid directions or a nonpermutation tag list. For independent directions $d_1,\ldots,d_h$, the signed contraction of
$$
 \prod_{j=1}^h c(\pi,d_j)
$$
with one typed Dyck predicate per bit position and a sum over the directions is exactly the tagged-permutation predicate. On nonpermutations take the product to be zero, with $h\ge1$. This identity follows from the basis expansion at each bit position and holds on all payloads. The cost of representing that coefficient function on arbitrary tag lists is unresolved here. Consequently neither the path count nor the signed expansion supplies a multitape or unrestricted RAM time lower bound for SAT.
