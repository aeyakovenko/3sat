# Two units of code weight and linear sum-of-squares degree

Let $A$ be the incidence matrix of a simple cubic bipartite exact-cover instance on $q$ rows and $q$ columns. Each row and column contains three ones. The input includes $y\in\{-1,0,1\}^q$ with exactly one negative entry and $Ay=\mathbf1$ over the integers. Write $C=\ker_{\mathbb F_2}A$ and $w=y\bmod2$. The Hamming distance from $w$ to $C$ has just two possible values:
$$
 d_H(w,C)=\begin{cases}q/3&\text{if the instance has an exact cover},\\ q/3+2&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}
$$
There are infeasible instances of unbounded size on which refuting distance $q/3$ requires real sum-of-squares degree $\Omega(q)$. The same lower bound holds for refuting the original Boolean exact-cover equations. These instances come with a disjoint packing missing three rows, the signed solution $y$, and a satisfiable double cover. The reduction to these promises takes linear encoded size and time. [SAT-to-cover reduction and supplied witnesses](two-permutations-and-the-explicit-sat-clock.md)

## The two possible distances

Summing $Ay=\mathbf1$ gives $3\sum_i y_i=q$. As a Boolean vector, $w$ agrees with $y$ except for an increase by two in its unique negative coordinate. Thus $w$ has weight $q/3+2$. Its integer row sums are one outside the three rows incident to that coordinate and three on those rows. The syndrome-$\mathbf1$ words are exactly $w+C$. For any such word $u$, let $t$ count its rows of integer weight three. The other rows have weight one, giving $3|u|=q+2t$. As $3\mid q$, we have $3\mid t$. Taking $u=w$ gives $t=3$, and attaining $t=0$ is exactly an exact cover. The maximum weight in $C$ is correspondingly $2q/3$ or $2q/3-2$, since $A\mathbf1=\mathbf1\bmod2$ and complementation exchanges $C$ with $w+C$. The classical exact-cover reduction to finding a low-weight syndrome preimage is due to Berlekamp, McEliece and van Tilborg. Here the additive gap, regular row and column weights, and a word at the larger distance threshold all hold at once. [Berlekamp, McEliece and van Tilborg](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1978.1055873)

Lean 4.19 checks the double count for arbitrary two-permutation rows, the equivalence between the lower weight and exact coverage, the two-unit gap for all other odd words, and the weight of the supplied odd word obtained by reducing the signed solution. The degree lower bound below is a manuscript argument. [Lean proof](OneNegativeCosetWeight.lean)

## Real sum-of-squares degree on the promised instances

Use the exact-cover equations $L_r(x)=\sum_{i:A_{ri}=1}x_i-1=0$ and $x_i^2=x_i$. A real sum-of-squares refutation is an identity $-1=\sum_j p_j^2+\sum_s h_s F_s$ with $F_s$ drawn from the equations. Its degree bounds every square and every product in that identity.

Start with a fixed-degree expander on $n$ vertices with an odd Tseitin charge. With edge signs $X_e\in\{-1,1\}$, the equations are $X_e^2=1$ and $g_v=u_v\prod_{e\ni v}X_e-1=0$, where $u_v\in\{-1,1\}$ and $\prod_vu_v=-1$. Grigoriev's Corollary 1 proves degree $\Omega(n)$ for the stronger dynamic real Positivstellensatz calculus on these equations. His Definitions 2 and 3 include the static sum-of-squares certificates considered here, and his Lemma 9 gives the degree transfer under bounded-degree substitutions. [Grigoriev, *Linear lower bound on degrees of Positivstellensatz calculus proofs for the parity*, §3](https://logic.pdmi.ras.ru/~grigorev/pub/square_journal.pdf)

Encode each vertex check with its constant truth table of forbidden assignments and give each resulting bounded-width clause its own bounded-size 3-CNF conversion. For example, a chain of partial disjunctions can use definitional gates and assert the final bit. The number of clauses is $O(n)$ since the expander degree $r$ is constant. One may also take a disjunction with a second unsatisfiable bounded-width formula of size $O(n)$ using selector-gated clauses. In the substitution below fix that selector to activate the Tseitin branch, fix the original variables of the other branch to constants, and evaluate its local gates. Apply the one-negative reduction to the resulting 3-CNF. Its output has $q=O(n)$ columns.

Assign each output variable a Boolean function of the original edge signs, including on source assignments that violate checks. Use $(1-X_e)/2$ for the original bit associated to $e$. Evaluate source clause gates at their inputs. In the signed-solution construction set its separate guard $g=1$ and set each $h=g\land\neg a$ as defined there. Complement bits use their actual complements. Each first-true selector is the indicator that its position is the first true literal, and all selectors are zero on a false clause. For a positive row $E(s,1-b,u)$ take $u=(1-s)b$. In the cubic regularization take the common underlying value for the occurrence copies $x_i,w_i$, put $a_i=0$, $b_i=1-x_i$, and use one Boolean cover of the six-column patch. Both inputs exchanged by the incidence swap are now zero. These choices are fixed functions on the whole source cube. They need satisfy the target rows only on the relevant locally valid source inputs.

Every assigned function depends on at most $r$ signs of a single source vertex, or is constant. Each individual output row has one of two properties. Its equation holds on the entire cube, as for copy and complement equations, occurrence comparisons, and the six-column patch. Otherwise its substituted equation involves only the signs of one source vertex $v$ and holds whenever that vertex's parity check holds. This follows by following a source clause and its own auxiliary variables through the construction. Occurrence comparisons across different clauses reuse the same underlying variable function. No comparison combines two differently computed local predicates.

Represent each assigned function by its multilinear interpolation on the sign cube. These polynomials have degree at most $r$. For the second kind of row, let $f$ be its substituted residual and put $p_v=u_v\prod_{e\ni v}X_e$. On the cube $f=((1-p_v)/2)f$, pointwise, because $f$ vanishes on $p_v=1$. Therefore $f$ has an ideal expression in the source equations of degree $O(r)$: subtract $-g_v f/2$ and reduce the difference with $X_e^2-1$. Multilinear reduction never increases degree. The identical-zero rows and substituted Boolean axioms require only the Boolean equations, again in bounded degree. Substitution into a target degree-$D$ static certificate keeps its squares squares and replaces each axiom occurrence by this bounded-degree expression, giving a source refutation of degree at most $rD+O(r)$. Grigoriev's lower bound implies $D=\Omega(n)=\Omega(q)$. The explicit packing, signed assignment and lift are constants verifying the instance promise. The proof system here has the displayed Boolean and exact-cover equations as axioms.

For the distance question use Boolean $x_i$, the local odd-parity equations $1+\prod_{i:A_{ri}=1}(1-2x_i)=0$, and the weight equation $\sum_i x_i=q/3$. These ask whether $w+C$ contains a word at the lower threshold. The weight equation follows by summing the exact-cover rows and dividing by three. On the three-bit cube each parity residual vanishes where $L_r=0$, so it equals a bounded-degree multiple of $L_r$ modulo the Boolean equations. To see this directly, interpolate a multiplier giving the ratio where $L_r\ne0$ and zero otherwise. Thus a low-degree certificate for the parity-plus-weight system would yield a certificate of degree at most $D+O(1)$ for exact coverage. The same applies if the weight threshold is an inequality, since its defining slack polynomial vanishes under the stronger exact-cover equations. Affinely replacing each $x_i$ by the bit $c_i\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}w_i$ gives the equivalent nearest-codeword system on $c\in C$. Prior work established linear-round Lasserre bounds for decoding other low-density parity-check code families. Priority for the regular two-unit promise has not been established. [Ghazi and Lee, *LP/SDP Hierarchy Lower Bounds for Decoding Random LDPC Codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.4241)

## When the supplied signed solution already guarantees a cover

Suppose the two permutations commute on the orbit containing the negative coordinate. All other orbits are already covered by $y$. On the distinguished orbit the real system $Az=\mathbf1$ has the constant solution $\mathbf1/3$. To describe the kernel, simultaneously diagonalize the two commuting permutation operators over $\mathbb C$. On the kernel their joint eigenvalues satisfy $1+\alpha+\beta=0$ and $|\alpha|=|\beta|=1$. Hence $(\alpha,\beta)$ is $(\omega,\omega^2)$ or its conjugate, where $\omega$ is a primitive cube root of unity. On a transitive orbit an eigenfunction is determined by its value at one vertex. Either no such character exists, or the two eigenspaces consist of functions constant on the three equal-sized fibers of the phase labeling. Every real solution in the latter case is constant on each fiber, with its three values summing to one. The supplied integer solution excludes the first case. Its unique negative entry occupies an entire fiber, forcing this orbit to have exactly three points and its three values to be $(-1,1,1)$. Selecting any single point there completes the cover. The result applies even when the permutations fail to commute on other orbits. [Phase labeling and small-defect algorithm](commutation-defects-in-two-permutation-covers.md)

On a general instance, if $q>3$ and $\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker A=1$, it is infeasible. All three values $-1,0,1$ occur in $y$ because it has $q/3+1$ positive entries. Every real solution then lies on the line through $y$ and $\mathbf1/3$. The constant point has fractional entries, and each other point has three different values.

## Degree in terms of the number of commutation failures

Every infeasible two-permutation instance has a real Nullstellensatz refutation of degree $O(d+1)$, where $d=|\{u:PQu\ne QPu\}|$. This upper bound applies to the exact-cover system even without a supplied signed solution. Together with the preceding lower bound it implies $d=\Omega(q)$ on the sum-of-squares-hard instances, and the linear dependence on $d$ in the worst case is sharp.

Use $U=P^{-1},V=Q^{-1}$ and let $H$ be the at most $3d$ exceptional points from the [local rotation proof](commutation-defects-in-two-permutation-covers.md). At each $u\notin H$, that proof uses only a bounded number of nearby equations to show that $r(u)=(x_u,x_{Uu},x_{U^2u})$ rotates by one position at $Uu$ and the other way at $Vu$. Each coordinate equality has a real ideal certificate of absolute constant degree. Indeed, for a fixed neighborhood on $s=O(1)$ bits, any polynomial vanishing on all legal Boolean assignments has such a certificate: at each illegal assignment choose a violated row, multiply it by the assignment indicator times the appropriate scalar, sum, and reduce modulo the Boolean equations. The constructed polynomial and the target agree on every assignment. The multilinear reductions take degree $O(s)$ for the linear equalities here.

Restrict to one unsatisfiable orbit of the two permutations. If this orbit contains $b>0$ exceptional points, deleting them leaves at most $4b$ connected components by the degree bound. If $b=0$, there is a single component. On a spanning tree of each component, sum the degree-bounded coordinate equalities along a path, with the appropriate rotations at each step. This gives an expression of constant degree for the difference between every $x_u$ in the component and one of the three actual bits in its root triple. Summation leaves the degree independent of path length. Retain all three actual root bits per component and the $b$ exceptional bits. There are at most $13b$ distinct retained bits for $b>0$ and at most three for $b=0$.

For every variable fix its resulting representative among those bits, using the variable itself if it is retained. All original variables are thus identified with retained bits by constant-degree certificates. Substitute the representatives in every row. If an assignment to the retained bits satisfied all these new rows, extending it by those same representatives would satisfy the original orbit. Thus for every assignment $s$ to the $h$ retained bits some substituted row $L_s$ is nonzero at $s$. Its degree-bounded certificate follows from the original row and the variable identifications. Modulo Booleanity, the indicator $I_s$ equals $I_s L_s/L_s(s)$. Sum over assignments to obtain $1$ in the original ideal in degree at most $h+O(1)$. This proof may have exponentially many terms. The upper and lower degree bounds concern an algebraic proof system; the unrestricted RAM clocks remain open.
