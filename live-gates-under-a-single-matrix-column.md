# How many gates of a SAT circuit stay live on one column?

Take any bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit deciding SAT on the journal's proper, dense DIMACS files for binary matrix mismatch, of length $N=\Theta(d^2)$. For all but a $2^{-\Omega(d^2)}$ fraction of left matrices $A$, **every choice of one column** $j$ has the following property. Pin every other column of $B,C$ to arbitrary values satisfying $C_\ell=AB_\ell$. Leave just the $2d$ bits of $B_j,C_j$ free. At least $\Omega(d^2)$ original gates in the output's dependency graph remain nonconstant after these pins. The ambient circuit can have arbitrary size, share any gates and have any depth. The quantifier covers **all valid pins of the other columns**, even if chosen after $A$ and the circuit. The compact projection puts each matrix bit in one physical ASCII bit. [Encoding and verdict](compact-bit-projection-of-matrix-mismatch.md)

The size being measured is that of an original circuit under partial evaluation. A newly synthesized circuit with wiring tailored to a known $A$ can compute the residual function in $O(d^2/\log d)$ gates. The construction below identifies the information supplied by its new wires.

Counting the functions obtained by pinning all but one input block goes back to Nečiporuk’s 1966 paper *A Boolean function*. Here the fixed predecessor wiring encodes a residual gate without listing its site number; the argument measures individual restricted circuits with shared gates.

## Encoding a restricted output from its fixed topology

Let $b$ bound the fan-in, with no restriction on fan-out. Treat the circuit and its ordered predecessor lists as public. Fix a subset of varying input sites and the variables carried at those sites. Any context pins all other inputs to constants. For every site decide mathematically whether the function it computes under those pins is constant. Start at a nonconstant output and recursively visit its nonconstant predecessors, skipping gates already visited. Call the visited internal gates live. A gate need not be constructively recognizable as constant for this definition.

If $s$ gates are visited, a depth-first traversal uses at most $bs$ trits. At each input port write zero or one if the predecessor is constant, otherwise write “visit.” On “visit” the decoder knows the actual predecessor site from the public wiring. It follows the same traversal, memoizing previously decoded sites. At a varying input site its variable is known. Each recovered gate thus has precisely its original restricted function. For a constant output encode its value instead. There are at most $O(3^{bs})$ possible nonconstant residual functions of cost at most $s$. No gate position has to be sent. An unused nonconstant predecessor that the traversal happens to keep only increases the measured cost. One may also omit an irrelevant port by supplying a constant there whenever the resulting gate function stays correct on its retained predecessors.

The residual output on column $j$ is the predicate $[Ax\ne y]$ for $x=B_j$, $y=C_j$. The functions distinguish all $2^{d^2}$ choices of $A$: as $y$ varies, the unique $y$ for which mismatch is false at $x=e_i$ is the $i$th column of $A$. The exterior site list is fixed by $d,j$ and independent of $A$ and all the other matrices. Hence at most $O(d\,3^{bs})$ left matrices admit even one exceptional $j$ and one setting of the other columns with live cost at most $s$. Taking $s$ a sufficiently small constant fraction of $d^2$ proves the claim. The identical argument applies directly to any ordinary Boolean circuit for the equality graph $[AB=C]$.

At the common background $B=C=0$ take the $d$ restrictions in turn. They produce $\Omega(d^3)$ gate-column incidences. For a circuit with $T$ original gates, some gate belongs to $\Omega(d^3/T)$ of the restricted output cones. More quantitatively at least $\Omega(d^2)$ gates occur in $\Omega(d^3/T)$ cones: discard incidences at gates below a sufficiently small constant times that threshold, then use the bound of $d$ incidences per surviving gate. The same reasoning works around any common background $C=AB$. A construction with disjoint column computations requires $\Omega(d^3)$ gates. Ordinary schoolbook multiplication and a final conjunction give $O(d^3)$ gates and $O(d^2)$ live gates per column on each fixed valid background. A circuit may reuse sites between different restrictions. Adding the cone sizes gives a count of incidences, not a lower bound of that size on ordinary circuits.

There is also a single $O(d^2)$-gate circuit that passes **every one** of these one-column tests for every $A$ and every valid background: compute the column sums $s=B\mathbf1$, $t=C\mathbf1$ over $\mathbb F_2$, and test $As=t$. If the only possibly erroneous column is $j$, the syndrome is exactly that column of $AB-C$. Consequently the same gate-count argument applies to this small circuit and exhibits $\Omega(d^3)$ live incidences on its $O(d^2)$ sites. Two equal nonzero errors in distinct columns cancel, so the circuit fails the unrestricted matrix graph. For fixed $r$, the journal's rank-$r$ promise circuit similarly agrees with the graph when any $r$ columns are varied around a valid background, with $O_r(d^2)$ total gates. Checking all such restrictions simultaneously cannot establish a superquadratic volume bound. [Rank-promise circuits](sparse-product-verification-bit-circuits.md)

The argument also applies when only logarithmically many selector bits change. In the journal’s compact proper DIMACS lookup family, $M=2^t$ table bits and $t$ selectors produce SAT value $T(u)$, with physical bit length $N=\Theta(M)$ at digit-block width $\lfloor t/8\rfloor$. Only the fixed selector sign slots vary once the table is set. For almost every table the $t$ selectors alone keep $\Omega(M)=\Omega(N)$ original gates live, independently of the size of the original SAT circuit. Each changing physical bit in a sign slot is a prescribed unary function of the same selector. The known linear-time RAM fast path on these files remains consistent with this circuit statement. [Lookup family and verification](balanced-selector-probes-on-compact-sat-files.md)

## Cost of substituting nonlocal wires

Allow $r$ input ports in a reduced circuit to take a value from a different gate or input site of the ambient graph, with no cycles in the reduced computation. If there are $P$ ambient sites, add a constant-sized flag at each visited port and $\lceil\log_2(P+1)\rceil$ bits for each new target. One can also change each visited gate to any bounded-arity truth table at constant cost for fixed $b$. The number of residual functions with at most $s$ gates and $r$ such wires is at most $2^{O_b(1+s+r\log(P+1))}$. It follows under the same matrix quantifiers that
$$
     s+r\log_2(P+1)=\Omega(d^2).
$$
For the joint tradeoff use a single code budget, allowing all pairs whose total description length is below the threshold. All counted codes together still fit the number of bit strings of that length. This expressly charges a chain of pinned multiplexers when it is bypassed and its data-dependent wire is installed.

For polynomial ambient size this budget is attained in order while keeping both $s$ and $r$ at $O(d^2/\log d)$. Choose $w=\max(1,\lfloor(\log_2 d)/2\rfloor)$ and split $x$ into $\lceil d/w\rceil$ chunks. On each chunk compute every subset parity by adding one bit to a previously computed parity, with $O(2^w)$ XOR gates. For each row and chunk the relevant substring of $A$ selects one of these parities. The ambient verifier implements the selection by a binary multiplexer tree. After $A$ is fixed use one new predecessor choice to bypass this tree. XOR the chunk parities for each row and compare the resulting words to $y$. There are $O(d^2/w)$ row-chunk pairs and $O((d/w)2^w)$ menu gates. The other already-valid columns can be replaced by their verdict one at the final conjunction. All constructions use bounded fan-in. This realizes the claimed gated size and rewiring budget in a polynomial-size ambient matrix verifier. The tabulation is the standard Four Russians method. [Arlazarov, Dinic, Kronrod and Faradžev](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=dan&option_lang=eng&paperid=35675&wshow=paper)

For a fixed bounded-degree finite-field tensor topology an analogous counting statement covers literal subnetwork simplification. Specify retained vertices, allow any replacement local table of the same bounded arity, and keep the topology's own incident edges, with deletions and local pins. Each retained component carrying a varying external bit can be explored from its known external site by a bounded-degree depth-first walk. Components carrying no such bit contribute a single combined field scalar. The encoding costs $O_F(s+d)$ bits with $2d$ varying ports, or $O_F(s+d+r\log(P+1))$ with $r$ charged nonlocal connections. A low-cost scalar does not specify an arbitrary tensor imported along a wide internal boundary. This applies to exact history networks built from a RAM solver if one tries to simplify them locally. It gives no unconditional lower bound on the elapsed time of that solver.

Lean proves that the address-free flag stream recovers the site-annotated traversal on a fixed binary DAG, that memoization records each visited gate at most once, and that equal port explanations and equal public leaves force equal values at a retained root. The counting bounds are in prose. [Lean lemmas](RootedResidualCode.lean)
