# What every local rank perturbation can miss about a binary product

For $d$-square binary matrices put $E=AB+C$. Allow one edit to add a rank-one matrix to any one of $A,B,C$. The minimum number of such edits from $(A,B,C)$ to a true product is **exactly** $\operatorname{rank}E$. Editing $A$ by $uv^\mathsf T$ changes the residual by $u(v^\mathsf TB)$; editing $B$ similarly changes it by $(Au)v^\mathsf T$. Thus residual rank changes by at most one each time even on a path that mixes edits to both factors. Conversely decompose $E$ into $\operatorname{rank}E$ rank-one summands and change just $C$.

Consequently every Boolean circuit correct on residuals of rank at most $r$ agrees with true verification on all length-$r$ mixed perturbations of **every** true triple, including at dense factors. With ordinary bit flips this includes, around every true triple, all coefficients of total degree at most $r$ in the multilinear interpolation translated to that Boolean vertex. Each coefficient is an inclusion–exclusion sum of values from that ball, over any coefficient field. These local equalities admit quadratic-size false verifiers with large prescribed sets of fully invertible errors.

## A quadratic circuit hiding preassigned corruptions

Let $1\le r<d$. Prescribe a binary linear subspace $W$ of dimension $s\le d$, all of whose nonzero matrices have rank greater than $r$. There is a nonuniform Boolean circuit of size $O(rd^2)$ whose verdict on $(A,B,C)$ is $[E\in K]$ for a linear $K\supseteq W$ with

$$dr\le\operatorname{codim}K\le O(dr),\qquad 0\ne H\in K\implies\operatorname{rank}H>r.$$

Recall the constant-rate binary linear code $G$ of constant relative distance $\delta$ and linear encoder size. For each nonzero $D$, a $\delta^2$ fraction of the coordinates of $GDG^\mathsf T$ are nonzero. The candidates $D=E+w$ for $0<\operatorname{rank}E\le r$ and $w\in W$ are all nonzero and number at most $2^{2dr+s}$, by padding a rank factorization to width $r$. Some fixed $t=O(dr+s)$ sampled coordinates hit every candidate by the union bound. Let $F$ consist of those binary coordinate measurements and set $K=F^{-1}(F(W))$. Every nonzero rank-$\le r$ matrix lies outside $K$. Its codimension is at most $t$. Matrices supported on the first $r$ rows form a $dr$-dimensional space disjoint from $K$ except at zero, proving the lower bound. [Spielman, *Linear-Time Encodable and Decodable Error-Correcting Codes*](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/Research/ITsuperc.pdf) · [Computing the encoded product checks](sparse-product-verification-bit-circuits.md)

The circuits for $GA,BG^\mathsf T,GCG^\mathsf T$ cost $O(d^2)$ gates. At each sampled coordinate one dot product of length $d$ suffices. Membership of the resulting $t$ bits in the fixed subspace $F(W)$ costs $O((s+1)t)$ gates. Select at most $s$ pivot coordinates and compare each other coordinate with its prescribed linear combination. Because $s\le d$, the total is $O(rd^2)$. The verdict is invariant under every translation $C\mapsto C+H$ with $H\in K$, on every input. For example take $W$ to consist of the matrices of multiplication by the elements of $\mathbb F_{2^d}$ in any chosen basis. Every nonzero member is invertible. For constant $r$ this gives an $O(d^2)$-gate circuit with the true degree-$r$ interpolation coefficients at all valid triples, yet the circuit accepts *every* translate of *every* valid triple by these $2^d-1$ predetermined invertible errors.

## Exact codimension with an explicit set of checks

There is also a family with exactly $dr$ independent scalar checks, at $\widetilde O(rd^2)$ Boolean gates. The invisible subspace is a classical maximum-rank-distance code. Here one can compute its syndrome on a **factored** product without constructing $AB$ or changing the basis of an input array. [Delsarte, *Bilinear forms over a finite field*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(78)90015-8) · [Gabidulin, *Theory of Codes with Maximum Rank Distance*](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=ppi&option_lang=eng&paperid=967&wshow=paper)

In $F=\mathbb F_{2^d}$ write $e_i=\alpha^i$ for a polynomial basis and $e_k^*$ for its trace-dual basis. Interpret a binary matrix $E$ as a linear map $L:F\to F$ sending $e_k^*$ to $\sum_i E_{ik}e_i$. Since $x=\sum_k\operatorname{Tr}(e_k x)e_k^*$, expanding the absolute trace yields

$$L(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}c_j(E)x^{2^j},\qquad c_j(E)=\sum_{i,k}E_{ik}e_i e_k^{2^j}.$$

These $d$ coefficients give independent coordinates on binary matrices. A nonzero polynomial of degree at most $2^{d-1}$ cannot vanish on all of $F$, and the source and target spaces have equal dimension $d^2$. Test $c_0(E),\ldots,c_{r-1}(E)$ for zero. This kernel has codimension $dr$. When the tests all vanish, $L(x)=Q(x^{2^r})$ with $Q$ a linearized polynomial of degree at most $2^{d-r-1}$ for $r<d$. Nonzero $L$ has nullity at most $d-r-1$ since Frobenius is bijective, hence rank at least $r+1$. For $r=d$ the kernel is zero. If $r<d$ the kernel contains all maps $x\mapsto a x^{2^r}$; these are invertible for every $a\ne0$. Any linear kernel avoiding positive ranks through $r$ has codimension at least $dr$ by the first-$r$-rows argument, so this size is optimal.

Pack column $h$ of $A$ as $a_h=\sum_i A_{ih} e_i$ and row $h$ of $B$ as $b_h=\sum_k B_{hk}e_k$. By bilinearity,

$$c_j(AB)=\sum_h a_h b_h^{2^j}.$$

The $c_j(C)$ have the same dot-product formula with packed columns of $C$ and the constants $e_h$. Computing successive $j$ uses $O(rd)$ extension-field additions, multiplications and squarings. Binary polynomial multiplication and reduction by the fixed field modulus cost $\widetilde O(d)$ bit gates each. In particular the reduction can obtain the quotient by multiplying the reversed dividend by the precomputed truncated inverse of the reversed monic modulus, reversing back, and subtracting its product with the modulus. The polynomial-basis packing uses the physical input bits as wires; the trace-dual basis explains the syndrome and requires no input conversion. [Cantor and Kaltofen](https://users.cs.duke.edu/~elk27/bibliography/91/CaKa91.pdf)

Rank-neighborhood agreement alone cannot give a superquadratic bound for any fixed radius. These constructions leave global exact decision open. The packed-coefficient audit confirms all kernel sizes, all rank thresholds, and the trace-dual interpolants at sides two and three. It independently verifies the factored formulas on all 262,400 ordered factor pairs at those sides. The related folded checks have a sharper batched upper bound for polynomially growing rank and a matching formal cost for their full output vector. The exact-codimension construction and the preassigned-corruption construction impose different requirements on the hidden subspace. Priority for the application of these classical codes to the local product-graph argument is unestablished.
