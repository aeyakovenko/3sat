# Tight nondeterministic syntactic read-k size of encoded SAT

Fix an integer $k\ge1$. At every sufficiently large byte-aligned encoded length $N$, a nondeterministic syntactic read-$k$ Boolean branching program deciding SAT on well-formed ASCII DIMACS 3-CNFs needs size
$$
                 2^{\Theta_k(N/\log N)}.
$$
The lower bound holds on a single fixed template at each length, even when all names occur, the three variables in each clause are distinct, and the entire declared name range has at most one satisfying assignment. On the same template the lower bound also holds for recognizing UNSAT. The matching upper bound asserted here concerns SAT. One sequence of hard templates works simultaneously for every fixed $k$, with the asymptotic constants allowed to depend on $k$.

The program is a nonuniform directed acyclic graph with literal tests on **physical input bits** and optional free nondeterministic choices. Every path from start to acceptance, including paths with conflicting tests, contains at most $k$ tests of any given position. Restricting every root-to-sink path gives the same lower bound. We use labeled-edge size in the proof. For ordinary binary query vertices and free choice vertices, the number of labeled edges is at most twice the number of vertices. Even in the edge-labeled multigraph convention there are only $O(N)$ distinct labels per ordered pair of vertices after removing duplicates, so the exponential orders also hold for vertex size. The [read-once result](read-once-size-of-sat-and-unsat.md) proves the upper bound already in physical file order, including sparse, arbitrarily long decimal names.

## A sparse binary bilinear form

Put $\alpha=1/(2\cdot4^k)$ and take $m$ a sufficiently large multiple of $4k$. There is a simple bipartite graph on two copies of $[m]$, of maximum degree $D=\lceil2(2\ln2+1)/\alpha\rceil$, with an edge in every $S\times T$ satisfying $|S||T|\ge\alpha m^2/2$. To see this, take the support of $D$ independent uniformly random permutation matchings. A single matching misses $S\times T$ with probability at most $(1-|T|/m)^{|S|}$, by sampling without replacement. The probability all matchings miss a specified rectangle above the threshold is at most $\exp(-D\alpha m/2)$. There are at most $4^m$ pairs of subsets. Their union has failure probability at most $e^{-m}$. Collapse parallel edges before forming the binary adjacency matrix $A$.

Every submatrix with row set $S$, column set $T$ and $|S||T|\ge\alpha m^2$ contains an identity minor of order at least $\lfloor\alpha m/(4D)\rfloor$. Choose any remaining edge $(u,v)$ and remove from the current row set every neighbor of $v$ and from the current column set every neighbor of $u$. At most $D$ rows and $D$ columns disappear, decreasing the area by at most $2Dm$. The hitting property continues supplying edges until the stated number have been chosen. Their crossed endpoints are nonadjacent, so the selected minor is an identity after reordering. Write $r=\lfloor\alpha m/(4D)\rfloor=\Omega_k(m)$.

For $x,y\in\{0,1\}^m$, put $b_A(x,y)=x^TAy$ over $\mathbb F_2$. This is the parity of at most $Dm$ two-bit products. The same matrix has a linear-size Boolean circuit for fixed $k$ and rank at least $r$ on every block of the required area. The graph may be chosen nonuniformly, as is sufficient for lower-bounding even a nonuniform representation. All the subsequent statements hold for each such graph.

## Why a syntactic program gives enough rectangles

The decomposition theorem of Borodin, Razborov and Smolensky applies directly to the $2m$ Boolean variables. The theorem uses an arbitrary finite alphabet. It bounds a syntactic read-$k$ nondeterministic program with $B$ labeled edges by an OR of at most $(2B)^{2ka}$ terms, each an AND of at most $ka$ factors on at most $\lceil2m/a\rceil$ variables, with each variable in at most $k$ factor supports of that term. We take $a=4k$, which divides $2m$. The argument below invokes the decomposition itself and specializes their large-rank submatrix argument to the sparse binary matrix above. [Borodin, Razborov and Smolensky, Theorems 1 and 6, *Computational Complexity* 3 (1993)](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/Papers/lower-bounds-read-k-times-branching.pdf)

In one term call a row-variable/column-variable pair good if no factor contains both. A factor on $h\le m/(2k)$ variables can cover at most $h^2/4$ pairs. There are at most $4k^2$ factors, so at least $m^2/2$ pairs are good. Color each factor independently red or blue with equal probability. Retain in $S$ the row variables appearing in no red factor, and in $T$ the column variables appearing in no blue factor. For a good pair, the requirements for retaining its endpoints constrain disjoint collections of colors, totaling at most $2k$. Its retention probability is at least $4^{-k}$. Some coloring therefore gives $|S||T|\ge\alpha m^2$.

Fix arbitrarily all bits outside $S,T$. In this fiber the AND of the blue factors depends only on $x_S$, the AND of the red factors only on $y_T$. The term is an ordinary rectangle $L\times R$. If the program recognizes the value $c$ of $b_A$, every point in this rectangle has that value. The restricted polynomial has bilinear matrix $M=A_{S,T}$ and possible affine terms. If the rectangle is nonempty, form the vector spaces $U=\operatorname{span}(L-L)$ and $V=\operatorname{span}(R-R)$. The four-point difference of the polynomial gives $U^TMV=0$. Hence
$$
 \dim U+\dim V\le |S|+|T|-\operatorname{rank}M.
$$
Indeed $M(V)\subseteq U^\perp$ while $\dim M(V)\ge\dim V-(|T|-\operatorname{rank}M)$. The rectangle lies in a product of translates of $U,V$, so its conditional uniform mass is at most $2^{-r}$. Averaging over the fixed bits gives the same bound for the whole term. The calculation holds for $c=0$ and $c=1$.

The original form has $\Pr[b_A=1]=(1-2^{-\operatorname{rank}A})/2\ge1/4$, since conditional on $Ay\ne0$ a uniform $x$ gives a fair bit. Both verdicts have mass at least $1/4$. The covering number is at least $2^{r-2}$, whence
$$
       (2B)^{8k^2}\ge 2^{r-2},\qquad \log_2 B=\Omega_k(m).
$$
This uses the syntactic read restriction precisely in the imported decomposition, which can concatenate subpaths through the same successive vertices even when their variable values disagree.

## One changing DIMACS bit per source bit

Use two rails $10i,10i+1$ for the $i$th of the $2m$ input bits, numbered from $i=1$. Enforce that the rails have opposite truth values using the two binary clauses for exclusive-or. A positive unit clause names the rail $10i+z_i$. Its last ASCII decimal digit is 0 or 1, so it changes exactly one physical bit, it has the same byte length for either value, and it occurs once as a data-dependent digit. The second rail now has value $z_i$.

For each graph edge make an AND of its two rails carrying the actual bits. Sum the edge values by a chain of two-input XOR gates and assert zero for the final parity. Encode each gate by a full equivalence using clauses of width at most three with distinct signal variables. For example, a fresh accumulator initially forced false avoids a special first parity gate. For proper triples reserve three names $1,2,3$ forced jointly false by the seven clauses excluding each other assignment to these three. Pad each short signal clause using distinct false names. Allocate gate signals consecutively above the rail range and force every vacant identifier in the reserved range false by a padded unit. This makes every declared identifier occur and gives each file exactly one model if $b_A=0$, and none if $b_A=1$. The polarity of a forced-false padded unit is chosen in the usual way: the filler variable occurs negatively and the two padding names positively.

There are $O(Dm)$ gates, variables and clauses, each of $O(\log(Dm))$ bits. Distinct rail names require $\Omega(m\log m)$ bits. For fixed $k$, the encoded length is $\Theta_k(m\log m)$. For any large prescribed byte-aligned $N$, choose $m$ from $4k\cdot2^j$ using the uniform upper bound $C_km\log(2m)$, then pad the file with fixed legal whitespace. This gives $m=\Theta_k(N/\log N)$ independently of the selected graph.

Given a branching program for the file verdict, fix all its public bit tests. Retain free transitions for satisfied fixed literals and remove those for falsified ones. Replace the private tests by their source variable tests. Each physical changing bit corresponds to exactly one source bit and vice versa. Every remaining path lifts to an original path, so no source variable can acquire more tests than the syntactic cap. This is an ordinary bit restriction with no word-alphabet conversion. The two file verdicts give the two values of $b_A$. The preceding lower bound and the read-once SAT upper bound prove the claim. For growing $k$, the intermediate unpadded estimates are $r=\Omega(m/16^k)$ whenever $m/16^k$ is sufficiently large, and file length $O(4^k m\log(4^k m))$.

## One template sequence for every cap

A fixed bounded-degree graph on its own has a small syntactic read-$D$ program: process one edge at a time, rereading its endpoints and retaining a parity bit. A direct sum removes the dependence of the hard file family on the chosen cap. For scale $M=2^h$ allocate, for each integer $j\ge1$, a block of side $m_j$ equal to the largest multiple of $4j$ no greater than $M/(4^j j^2)$. Omit zero-sized blocks. Take for that block a hitting graph as above for cap $j$ (for example the first successful tuple of permutations in lexicographic order). Form the block-diagonal adjacency on the disjoint unions of their two vertex classes. Its bilinear form is the XOR of the block forms. There are $O(M)$ edges in all, since $D_j=O(4^j)$ and $\sum_j D_jm_j=O(M)\sum_j j^{-2}$. The first block already has $\Theta(M)$ vertices. The resulting template therefore has length $\Theta(M\log M)$ with absolute constants. Pad to prescribed byte lengths as before using a uniform upper bound.

For any fixed $k$ and large $M$, the $k$th block has $m_k=\Theta_k(M)$. Set all inputs of every other block to zero. The remaining function is the original hard $k$th block, with both possible verdicts. Further bit restrictions preserve the syntactic cap. This proves the simultaneous assertion. More quantitatively, once rounding and the condition $r\gg k^2$ hold in the retained block, the calculation gives $\log_2 B=\Omega(M/(64^k k^4))$ on this one sequence. For example $k\le(1/6-\varepsilon)\log_2 N$ still gives a lower bound of the form $2^{N^{\Omega(\varepsilon)}}$ for each fixed $0<\varepsilon<1/6$. Lexicographic selection only specifies the family; the solver fast path reads its actual gates.

## Average-case hardness for unambiguous programs

Under the uniform distribution on all private digits of the simultaneous template, any deterministic syntactic read-$k$ program of size at most $2^{c_k N/\log N}$ has success probability at most $1/2+2^{-\Omega_k(N/\log N)}$, for a sufficiently small constant $c_k>0$. This extends to programs having at most one accepting path per promised file and randomized mixtures of such programs satisfying the same individual size bound. Here success means that the Boolean acceptance verdict equals SAT.

First consider the single hard block for $k$. In equation (3) of Borodin, Razborov and Smolensky, each term records one trace of the accepting path. The cut edges of a path are determined uniquely by the first support-size threshold crossing after each previous cut. A satisfied term supplies a consistent accepting path containing its indicated trace, by concatenating its segment paths. On an input with at most one consistent accepting path, at most one term can therefore be true. This argument can work on the original graph with $E$ total edges, bounding the number of traces by $(2E)^{4k^2+1}$. Equivalently one can use the paper's free-edge elimination: deleting vertices incident solely to free edges just contracts portions of paths, and merging identical free edges preserves unambiguity. The paper then gives $T\le (2B)^{8k^2}$ as above. Promised-file unambiguity suffices because every assignment of the pins produces a promised file.

For any term, use its coloring and outside assignment as before. The matrix $K_{x,y}=(-1)^{x^TMy}$ on the remaining $u=|S|$, $v=|T|$ bits has operator norm $2^{(u+v-\operatorname{rank}M)/2}$. Indeed $KK^T$ has entries $2^v$ for two rows in the same coset of $\ker M^T$ and zero otherwise. Affine terms only change row and column signs. Applying this norm bound to the two indicator vectors of an arbitrary rectangle and dividing by $2^{u+v}$ gives signed mass at most $2^{-r/2}$ in absolute value. This estimate does not require the term to be monochromatic. After averaging over the fixed variables the bound still holds.

The acceptance indicator $P$ is the sum of its disjoint trace indicators. The correlation between its sign and the target sign is therefore at most $2T2^{-r/2}+2^{-\operatorname{rank}A}$ in absolute value, using $|\mathbb E(-1)^{b_A}|=2^{-\operatorname{rank} A}$. Taking $\log_2(2B)\le r/(32k^2)$ makes the correlation exponentially small in $r$. On the full direct sum, condition on every pin outside the $k$th block. These values may complement the target sign but the absolute bound is uniform. Averaging over those pins and over any independent random choice of program proves the assertion. Bollig, Sauerhoff and Wegener already combined a trace partition with bilinear discrepancy over $\mathbb F_2$ for an explicit Hankel-matrix predicate (Theorem 21 and Lemmas 24–27). Here sparsity pays for the DIMACS length, and the same disjointness argument permits unambiguous programs on the file promise. [BRS trace construction, pp. 5–6](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~bor/Papers/lower-bounds-read-k-times-branching.pdf) · [Bollig, Sauerhoff and Wegener, *Information and Computation* (2002)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0890540102931743)

An independent finite audit enumerated every binary matrix of order one, two and three, all affine terms on the two sides and both verdicts (66,064 combinations). It maximized monochromatic rectangles over every row subset by intersecting the permitted columns. It also parsed 116 generated dense files, checked 584 directed single-bit edits and used PicoSAT to verify zero or one complete models against the source parity. The combinatorial bounds for unbounded dimensions are proved above. [Reproduce the finite audit](syntactic_read_k_projection_audit.py)

The hard part imported here is the published branching-program decomposition. Jukna used high-distance codes to obtain a separate family with easy nondeterministic read-once complements. The sparse bilinear form gives comparable lower bounds for both values on one template. Priority for this exact fixed-$k$ statement in DIMACS bit length has not been established. [Jukna, *A Note on Read-k Times Branching Programs*](https://www.numdam.org/item/ITA_1995__29_1_75_0/)

## Executed RAM time on the hard templates

A single globally correct SAT solver can evaluate all of these promised files in $O(N)$ native steps, including a nonuniform choice of graph at each dimension. Serialize the forcing gadget, rail and filler records, then the successive AND/XOR gate records and the final unit in a publicly specified order. The general fast path accepts a broader grammar of forward Boolean circuits of this form. It verifies the gadget and input pairs, checks the complete list of filler pins, accepts successive fresh gate outputs only when their predecessor values are already assigned and the clauses give exactly the expected truth-table equivalence, and matches the header, final output assertion and end of file. It stores the derived bit of each name in a word array. The graph edges are supplied by the gate records themselves. Unrecognized files fall back to ordinary complete SAT on the original input. On a template, all identifier values are $O_k(m)$ and all gate counts and the dense work table fit within the standard address range. A monotone byte scan, decimal accumulation by shifts and additions, and constant work per literal and gate suffice. The upper bound thus concerns an actual solver on the hard files.

The same distinction is visible directly on read-only input with unrestricted private memory. A RAM can copy each of its original packed words to private memory once in $O(N/w)$ steps and use the copies thereafter. The syntactic size lower bound counts the states needed to represent what has been retained. It gives no superlinear unrestricted RAM clock. Semantic and syntactic read caps also diverge even for polynomial-size branching programs. [Beame, Jayram and Saks, Section 1.2 and Corollary 36](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~beame/papers/branch.pdf)
