# How many single-edit SAT answers can one order encode?

A family of ordinary 3-CNFs on three variables realizes every binary vector of length $m$ as the satisfiability answers to $m$ specified single-bit edits. A fixed collection of queries that report the color of the largest label in a subset requires at least $m+1$ ordered labels to represent those answers. This threshold is exact. Allowing $R$ successive batches increases the capacity to order $u\min(R,\log u)$ bits with $u$ labels. The individual SAT answers on the example take constant time given the edit location.

The motivation is the preference estimate in the minimum spanning forest algorithm of Khanna, Putterman and Song. Once its forest and spokes have been fixed, each preference is determined by whether a maximum on a prescribed set is a spoke. The authors bound the possible profiles, then sample preferences to distinguish profiles far apart in Hamming distance. Their final time bound uses Pettie and Ramachandran's theorem about minimum spanning forest. [DLMST, §§3.4–3.5 and Corollary 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31187v1) · [Pettie and Ramachandran](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~pettie/papers/jacm-optmsf.pdf)

## Static queries

Let $U$ be a set of $u\ge1$ labels. A query specifies a nonempty subset $S_j$ and a map $c_j:S_j\to\{-1,+1\}$. Its reply is $c_j(\max_{\pi} S_j)$ for an unknown total order $\pi$ common to all queries. Both the subset and the coloring may vary across queries. Define $B(m,k)=\sum_{i=0}^{\min(k,m)}\binom mi$ for $k\ge0$.

**Theorem.** For any $m$ fixed queries at most $B(m,u-1)$ reply vectors occur. The largest number of queries on which every vector occurs is exactly $u-1$.

For the upper bound, number the ranks from $0$ to $u-1$ and set $w_a=2^{\pi(a)}/\sum_b 2^{\pi(b)}$. Give the vector $z_j$ coefficient $c_j(a)$ on $S_j$ and zero elsewhere. The sign of $z_j\cdot w$ equals the reply because the largest power of two exceeds the sum of all smaller ones. All the weights lie on the affine plane $\sum_a w_a=1$ of dimension $u-1$. Thus at most the number of regions of $m$ hyperplanes in that plane can occur. Adding one hyperplane splits at most as many old regions as the older hyperplanes cut out on the new one. Induction with Pascal's identity bounds the number by $B(m,u-1)$, also for coincident hyperplanes and constant queries. Every order point avoids all the nonconstant query hyperplanes. In particular $m=u$ cannot be shattered. For equality compare each of $u-1$ labels to one anchor. Any chosen subset may be placed above the anchor and its complement below. The region count and the exponentially separated weight representation have classical precedents. [Cover on hyperplane counts](https://isl.stanford.edu/~cover/papers/paper2.pdf) · [Decision lists as linear thresholds, Lemma 3](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~rocco/Public/nips06.pdf)

## A smaller sample for the MST preference step

For any $m$ fixed queries there is a set of $O(tu\log(t+1))$ positions such that any two orders agreeing there disagree on at most $m/t$ positions, for $t\ge1$. This bound is independent of the number of queries. In Claim 3.12 of DLMST the forest and spokes are conditioned upon, $u\le2v$ for $v$ vertices, and the paper gives $O(t\sqrt{m v})$ sampled positions. The new estimate improves that step when $m/v$ grows faster than $\log^2(t+1)$. The comparison algorithm may enumerate a suitable sample from the public forest and spokes. The complexity of the complete MST algorithm is already linear. [DLMST, Claim 3.12](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31187v1#S3.SS5)

To prove this, let $\mathcal D$ be the sets of query positions on which two orders disagree. On $d$ prescribed positions, at most $B(d,u-1)^2$ restrictions of these sets occur. For $s=u-1\ge1$ and $d=10s$ the bound is at most $(10\mathrm e)^{2s}<2^{10s}$. Thus $\mathcal D$ has VC dimension less than $10s$. For $u=1$ every disagreement set is empty. Apply the epsilon-net theorem with the uniform measure on query positions and $\epsilon=1/t$ to hit every disagreement set larger than $m/t$. A nonempty sample is unnecessary at $t=1$. [Haussler and Welzl, *Epsilon-nets and simplex range queries*](https://people.inf.ethz.ch/emo/PublFiles/EpsNets_DCG2_87.orig.pdf)

**The sample scale is sharp for pair comparisons.** Give $k$ left labels one color and $k$ right labels the other, and query all $k^2$ left-right pairs. For $t\ge2$, the minimum number of sampled pairs needed to leave at most $k^2/t$ differences between any two consistent orders is
$$
\Theta\bigl(\min\{k^2,kt\log(t+1)\}\bigr).
$$
The upper bound follows by using the epsilon-net sample or taking all positions. For the lower bound let $kD$ pairs be sampled. These pairs are the edges of a bipartite graph on the two label sets. Assume $k$ is sufficiently large. If $D+1> k/1024$ this already costs $\Omega(k^2)$. Otherwise put
$$
h=\left\lfloor\frac{k\ln(D+2)}{128(D+1)}\right\rfloor.
$$
Here $h\le k/128$ and $h\ge k\ln(D+2)/(256(D+1))$. While at least $v\ge k/2$ labels remain on each side, at least $v/2$ right labels have current degree at most $4D$. Pick $h$ remaining left labels uniformly without replacement. Each of these low-degree right labels has probability at least
$$
\left(1-\frac{4(D+1)}{v-h+1}\right)^h
\ \ge\ \exp(-18(D+1)h/k)\ \ge\ (D+2)^{-1/4}
$$
of having no neighbor in the pick. Here $4(D+1)/(v-h+1)\le9(D+1)/k<1/2$ and $1-z\ge e^{-2z}$ on that interval. The expected number of such right labels is at least $(k/4)(D+2)^{-1/4}\ge h$, using $\ln(D+2)\le\sqrt{D+2}$. Thus there are two $h$-sets, one on each side, with no sampled edge between them. Remove both and repeat. This is the elementary random-subset argument for balanced independent sets. [Prior work on balanced independent sets in bipartite graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.02506)

After removing more than $k/2$ labels on each side, concatenate the extracted pairs of sets as adjacent blocks, then append the unremoved labels. Make a second total order by interchanging the two blocks in each extracted pair while preserving both internal orders. Every sampled answer agrees. Each swap reverses $h^2$ queried pairs, so at least $kh/2$ true answers differ. A tolerance of $k^2/t$ implies $D+1\ge c t\ln(D+2)$ for an absolute $c>0$, hence $D+1=\Omega(t\log(t+1))$. For $t\ge2$ also $D=\Omega(1)$. With fewer than $k/8$ sampled edges there are at least $7k/8$ isolated labels on each side, whose two blocks can be interchanged to reverse more than $k^2/2$ pairs. This proves the claimed lower bound. Classical one-round approximate sorting also studies what can be inferred from sampled pairs. The guarantee proved here concerns the maximum Hamming distance between two consistent *entire* reply profiles. [Alon and Azar on sorting and approximate sorting in rounds](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0401028)

The bipartite comparisons even occur on a fixed forest with all spokes threshold-supported. Make one portal $p$ and $k$ leaves $l_i$, each joined to $p$ by its spoke of weight $s_i$. For each $j$, add a distinct portal $q_j$ joined to $p$ by an edge of weight $x_j$, and a right endpoint $r_j$ joined to $q_j$ by a spoke of low weight $w_j$. The $2k$ weights $s_i,x_j$ can occur in any total order, all above the distinct $w_j$. The tree path from $l_i$ to $r_j$ has weights $s_i,x_j,w_j$. Its endpoints' heavier spoke has weight $s_i$, so the preferred action is find exactly when $s_i>x_j$. All designated spokes are in the tree. This realizes the sample lower bound for the conditional forest-and-spokes rule on $3k+1$ vertices. The tree uses $k+1$ portals. Reaching this state in the full recursion with its per-call portal budget requires a further argument.

## Several rounds

Let the queries be divided into $R\ge1$ batches of predetermined lengths $m_1,\dots,m_R$ totaling $m$. The complete list of queries in a batch may be any deterministic function of all preceding replies. On any branch the number of extensions in batch $i$ is at most $B(m_i,u-1)$. The number of possible transcripts is therefore at most
$$
N_R\;=\;\min\left\{u!,\ \prod_{i=1}^{R} B(m_i,u-1),\ B(m,R(u-1))\right\}.
$$
The factorial bound counts orders directly. For the last bound, a choice of at most $u-1$ positions in each batch gives a subset of at most $R(u-1)$ positions in the entire transcript. This counts the terms of the product. In particular every fully shattered transcript has $m\le\min(R(u-1),\lfloor\log_2(u!)\rfloor)$.

For a lower construction choose $1\le r\le R$ with $2^r\le u$. Reserve $2^r-1$ anchors in a fixed relative order and leave $u-2^r+1$ payload labels. Each payload can be put independently into any one of the $2^r$ gaps among the anchors, with an arbitrary fixed tie order among payloads in the same gap. In each of $r$ rounds compare every payload to the midpoint anchor of its current range of gaps. Its replies specify the successive bits of its gap index, realizing every one of the $2^{r(u-2^r+1)}$ transcripts. For $u\ge4$ take $r=\min(R,\lfloor\log_2(u/2)\rfloor)$. Together with the static construction for small $u$, this gives shattering capacity $\Theta(u\min(R,\log_2 u))$ for all $u\ge2$. For fixed $R$ the upper and lower bounds differ by at most $R(2^R-2)$ bits as soon as $u\ge2^R$.

With $K$ public templates and up to $e$ incorrect coordinates, at most $K N_R B(m,e)$ true vectors are covered. This permits choosing both the template and the entire order separately for each input at unlimited computational cost. If lengths vary by template, the coarser bound $N_R\le\min(u!,B(m,R(u-1)))$ still applies. For any fixed $\delta>0$ and $0\le e\le(1/2-\delta)m$, the covered fraction is $2^{-\Omega_\delta(m)}$ if $\log_2 K=o(m)$ and either $R(u-1)=o(m)$ or $\log_2(u!)=o(m)$. This follows from $B(m,o(m))=2^{o(m)}$ and the binary entropy estimate for the Hamming ball.

## Ordinary 3-SAT edits

Fix variables $a,b,c$. Include seven width-three clauses, one falsified by each of the seven assignments other than $(a,b,c)=(1,0,1)$. Together they force this assignment. For $x\in\{0,1\}^m$ append
$$
 C_j=(a\lor b\lor \ell_j),\qquad \ell_j=\begin{cases}c& x_j=1,\\ \neg c&x_j=0.\end{cases}
$$
Call the resulting satisfiable formula $F_x$. Edit $j$ flips just the sign bit of $a$ in the $j$-th appended clause. Every unedited appended clause still holds. The edited clause now evaluates to $x_j$ under the only candidate assignment. Consequently
$$
 \bigl(\operatorname{SAT}(\operatorname{edit}_j F_x)\bigr)_{j=1}^m=x.
$$
In the journal's explicit binary literal encoding these are $\Theta(m)$-bit formulas with predetermined edit positions. Each clause contains three distinct variables. The list of clauses can contain repeated records, as ordinary explicit 3-CNF permits. Lean 4.19 checks the seven-clause forcing property and the satisfiability equivalence for every length and edit position. The profile and sampling bounds have paper proofs above. [Lean proof](OrderedMaximaSatEdits.lean)

It follows that the bounds above apply to the exact and approximate representation of these genuine SAT answers. For a single static template, $m+1$ labels are necessary and sufficient. A coordinate sample wanted for the stronger purpose in DLMST Claim 3.12, that any two completions agreeing on the sample differ in at most $e$ positions, must contain at least $m-e$ positions on this family. Indeed two completions can differ on every unsampled coordinate, and $m-e$ probes suffice. On this promise, looking up the sign of the third literal in the edited record answers the question. An unrestricted solver deciding one edited formula need never encode the whole profile in an order.

There is also a native SAT family attaining the tight pair-comparison sample bound. Given an arbitrary ordering $\pi$ of $2k$ named variables, use the $2k-1$ clauses $\neg X_{\pi(i)}\lor X_{\pi(i+1)}$. Its satisfying assignments are exactly the sequences of zeroes followed by ones in order $\pi$. Pin $X_a=1$ and $X_b=0$ for a left-right pair $(a,b)$. The pinned formula is satisfiable exactly when $b$ precedes $a$, so all $k^2$ answers are the bipartite comparison profile above. The base has $O(k\log k)$ bits, and two fixed unit-clause slots can specify a query by changing $O(\log k)$ bits. Clauses of width below three can be padded with distinct fixed-false variables. If one sign flip per query is required, allocate a fresh variable $g_{ab}$ to each pair. Include the unit clause $\neg g_{ab}$ and both implications $g_{ab}\to X_a$ and $g_{ab}\to\neg X_b$. Flipping the unit's polarity activates precisely this pair. This version has $O(k^2\log k)$ encoded bits. For either version, a fixed sample of SAT verdicts on the indicated queries must have size $\Theta(\min(k^2, kt\log(t+1)))$ to bound the pairwise ambiguity of all the remaining answers by $k^2/t$. Reading the chain of implications gives the full order directly. The result charges acquisition of other SAT verdicts in the stated sampling model.

There is a trace-level counterpart using two writable cells, apart from the loop and input registers. For each $x_j$, reset cell zero, write one to cell zero when $x_j=1$ and to cell one otherwise, then read cell zero. Each read's last writer is either its reset or its second store. All $2^m$ binary profiles occur in $O(m)$ steps even though all reads use a fixed cell. The underlying candidate writers are dynamic events. Counting only distinct addresses loses the distinction.

The earlier journal result for fixed writer menus recovers the *names* of winners from aggregate multiplicities and explains what changes under adaptive menus. Here the observable is a query-specific *color*, and the resource being bounded is the number of priority labels across several batches. The earlier single-edit results charge changes between time-aligned traces. Here only SAT verdicts on the edited formulas are needed. The proof combines classical region counting, binary search and an explicit encoding. Historical priority for their exact combination is unverified.
