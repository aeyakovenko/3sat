# A bilinear answer with no gate-by-gate bilinearization

A possible way to use a matrix-product lower bound on a RAM computation is to preserve the linear measurements entering each small word operation, then replace the operation by its bilinear contribution. This can fail even on three input bits per side, with seven nonadaptive gates and only XOR after those gates. There is a one-dimensional obstruction one can compute exactly. Across disjoint copies it costs exactly one unrestricted rank-one correction per copy to repair.

Let $A,B\in\mathbb F_2^3$. For each nonzero $n\in\mathbb F_2^3$, gate $n$ sees two independent parities spanning $n^\perp$ of $A$, and the single parity $n\cdot B$. It can tell from its two left inputs whether $A\in\{0,n\}$. Output
$$
        [A\in\{0,n\}]\,(n\cdot B).
$$
XOR the seven gate outputs. For $A\ne0$ exactly one active term has $n=A$. For $A=0$ all seven terms are active and their sum vanishes, since the sum of the seven nonzero vectors is zero. The computed function is exactly $A\cdot B$.

Any *bilinear* gate with these inputs has coefficient matrix $\ell n^\top$ for some $\ell\perp n$. Its trace vanishes. The same is true for any sum of such gates, whereas the identity matrix in odd dimension has trace one over $\mathbb F_2$. Arbitrary formal polynomials over a characteristic-two extension of the base field at these gates cannot fix this if the final sum must equal the dot product as a polynomial identity: extract the bidegree $(1,1)$ component of the sum. Each local term still contributes only a matrix with zero trace. Thus even the option of a large replacement polynomial at each gate fails when it must preserve its input forms.

In fact, the span of the available local bilinear matrices is precisely the space of trace-zero matrices. All $E_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$ are allowed. Taking $\ell=n=e_i+e_j$ and removing the off-diagonal entries gives $E_{ii}+E_{jj}$. These generate the diagonal trace-zero subspace. This argument and the nonlinear construction work in every odd binary dimension $h\ge3$, with $2^h-1$ local terms and $h-1$ left parities each.

## Why the seven directions occur together

In the three-dimensional example one can characterize the obstruction for arbitrary Boolean gate tables, and any proper subset of the seven gates is free of it. Normalize a gate by taking the XOR of its outputs with its right bit set to zero and one. This is its slope as a function of the right bit. It is a Boolean function of the two left parities $u_n,v_n$, hence has form affine $+\,d_n u_nv_n$, $d_n\in\mathbb F_2$. The part of $u_nv_n$ of squarefree degree two in $A$ has exterior coefficient $u_n\wedge v_n$. Under the standard volume pairing in dimension three this is the vector $n$: it annihilates precisely the plane spanned by the two rows and has the unique possible nonzero scale.

If the XOR is bilinear, its degree-$(2,1)$ squarefree coefficient vanishes, so $\sum_n d_n nn^\top=0$. These seven symmetric matrices have exactly one relation. Those for $n=e_i$ and $n=e_i+e_j$ with $i<j$ are independent by looking first at off-diagonal entries, then at diagonal entries. All seven sum to zero since exactly two vectors contribute to each off-diagonal and four to each diagonal. Thus the nonlinear slopes either all vanish or are present at all seven gates. In the first case the local linear parts compute the bilinear result. In the second, subtract the seven-gate identity above, whose zero test on two bits has quadratic coefficient one. What remains has affine slopes and a trace-zero coefficient matrix. Consequently trace classifies the obstruction completely on this wiring. The inputs of a mixed gate of total arity two permit only one parity from each side and its bilinear term is already local. The exhibited failure first appears at total arity three, with these separated inputs.

## Exact repair cost and average-case error

Use $b$ disjoint three-bit blocks and sum their dot products. A gate still sees only the indicated projections of its own block. For every sum of local bilinear replacements the error matrix is block diagonal, with trace one on each diagonal block, and has rank at least $b$. Each block can attain a rank-one error by the trace-zero span calculation. Exactly $b$ extra rank-one bilinear gates with freely chosen inputs are necessary and sufficient for an exact result, even if they can mix different blocks. With only $s$ such corrections the residual rank is at least $(b-s)_+$. A binary bilinear form of rank $r$ has sign bias $2^{-r}$ on independent uniform inputs, by averaging one block first. The best sign bias achievable with these replacements and $s$ corrections is exactly $2^{-(b-s)_+}$. Offsets depending arbitrarily on just $A$ and just $B$ can raise the absolute bias only to $2^{-(b-s)_+/2}$ by the character matrix operator norm. The exact prediction above by nonlinear three-bit gates still has bias one.

This rules out a conversion that preserves each gate's local input spans and expects cancellation between high-degree gate functions to preserve the formally extracted bilinear part. A globally rewired algorithm just uses the ordinary three coordinate products per block. The example consequently does not exclude a globally size-controlled conversion for full matrix multiplication. Nonlinear versus linear computation with restricted access has a substantial prior literature in index and network coding. Historical priority for this particular local obstruction is unverified.
