# Wiring one fixed native SAT clock across lengths

There is one fixed deterministic RAM program $Q$ deciding a conjunction of selector records such that, for every signed 3-CNF $\Phi$ with $m$ clauses and every assignment $a$, a uniformly constructed explicit positive input $X_\Phi(a)$ has the exact uninterrupted clock
$$
 T_Q(X_\Phi(a))=5+14m+4\,|\{C\in\Phi:a\models C\}|.
$$
Finding an assignment attaining $5+18m$ on a signed wiring is therefore 3-SAT. Every explicit input in the image is accepted and runs in linear time in the number of records. The symbolic source assignment is materialized before this execution. This distinguishes an actual shared clock from the task of finding an assignment maximizing it.

Even many-wise independence of the record inputs does not force an improvement above their marginal late frequency. In one fixed program the two clocks on the four-word target are 7 and 15, with late probability $7/8$. There are bounded-fan-in affine wirings with $\Omega(\sqrt m)$-wise independent complete records and with late proportion at most $7/8+O(m^{-1/8})$ for **every** source. The statement applies across unbounded record counts with unchanged program text.

## Native clock on a signed wiring

Use the four-nibble catalog from the finite census. A vector $(p,q,M,N)$ encodes positive and negative unit masks on eight variables and one disjunction of all eight. It is SAT exactly when $p\mathbin{\&} M=q\mathbin{\&}N=0$ and $(M,N)\ne(15,15)$. A positive target has $p,q\in\{4,\ldots,11\}$ and $M=15-p,\ N=15-q$. The 64 such inputs are the census cohort. [Seven-step target classification](which-four-word-sat-targets-finish-in-seven-steps.md)

In the four-bit RAM let $P$ take the seven-step shortcut testing $M=11$ and $\operatorname{mem}[q\mathbin{\&}N]=4$. A failed test starts the general nine-step algorithm, whose loads overwrite its working registers before use. The shortcut is sound on the full catalog: at address one or three the returned $q$ or $N$ is odd, at two it is $M=11$, and beyond three the initial memory is zero. Success therefore requires address zero and $p=4$. On the cohort, $p=4$ takes seven instructions and each other row fails the first test at six and takes nine more. All 65,536 verdicts were checked. [General nine-step solver](exact-nine-step-clock-for-four-sat-words.md)

For the uninterrupted program, use a canonical array with header $m$ and $4m$ succeeding nibble-valued words. The recognized predicate is conjunction on disjoint selector records. Let the word width be at least $\max(4,\lceil\log_2(4m+2)\rceil)$, so the one-past-last address fits. The same fixed-label code works at every such width. Four setup instructions read $m$ and compute the end pointer and first address. The loop reads $q,M,N$, computes $j=q\mathbin{\&}N$, rejects if $j\ge4$ and otherwise reads the record at offset $j$. On a failed shortcut the generic branch loads $p$, tests the two conflicts and handles $(15,15)$. Reaching a terminal is free; comparisons, arithmetic and loads each cost one. The attached program includes every address addition and label. Each positive shortcut record costs 14 steps and each other target costs 18, including the pointer increment. One final loop test follows. The input format is explicitly the nibble-valued array; the theorem charges no conversion from DIMACS to this array.

For a source clause take the three signed literal truth values as bits of a label $u\in\{0,\dots,7\}$. Set $p=4+u$ and choose any $q\in\{4,\ldots,11\}$, then complement both nibbles for the negative masks. The shortcut fires exactly when the clause is false. This proves the clock identity. A signed description contains three literal references per record, so the reduction from the explicit source formula has linear encoded size. For clauses on distinct source variables, drawing source bits independently and giving $q$ three independent bits makes each individual record exactly uniform on the 64 targets. Håstad's theorem therefore transfers the usual E3-SAT gap: for any fixed $\epsilon>0$ it is NP-hard to distinguish maximum clock $5+18m$ from maximum clock at most $5+(35/2+4\epsilon)m$ on symbolic descriptions. [Håstad, Theorem 6.5 of *Some Optimal Inapproximability Results*](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/BLOGPAPERS/max3satl.pdf)

## Small affine wirings and many independent marginals

Index nine records by the eight elements of $\mathbb F_8$ and an infinity point. With independent uniform $a,b\in\mathbb F_8$, use $a+tb$ for the free $p$ label at $t$, and $b$ at infinity. Use an independent pair for the $q$ labels. Any two complete physical records are independent uniform targets, since any two of the distinct projective forms have rank two over $\mathbb F_8$. Every assignment gives at least one fast record. Every nonzero $(a,b)$ gives exactly one. In a binary basis each output bit uses at most four source bits.

For growing $m$, put $r=\lceil m^{3/4}\rceil$. Create $6m$ binary affine forms for the six free bits of the records, independently choosing four source positions in $[r]$ with replacement per form, and independent uniform constant terms. A sum of $s$ different linear parts can vanish only if its $4s$ incidences hit at most $2s$ positions. For $2s\le r$, the probability of any such dependency is at most
$$
 \binom{6m}{s}\binom r{2s}(2s/r)^{4s}
 \le (24 e^3 ms/r^2)^s.
$$
For all $1\le s\le c r^2/m$ simultaneously the failure probability is $o(1)$ for a sufficiently small absolute $c$. Indeed each fixed term tends to zero and all terms are dominated by $2^{-s}$. Thus arbitrary sets of $\lfloor cr^2/(6m)\rfloor=\Omega(\sqrt m)$ full records are exactly independent under a uniform source.

For each *fixed* source, the independent constant terms make the number of zero $p$ labels binomial with mean $m/8$, where probability at this step is over the wiring. Hoeffding's inequality and a union bound over $2^r$ source strings put every one of their counts within $m^{7/8}$ of $m/8$, except with probability at most $2^{r+1}\exp(-2m^{3/4})=o(1)$. The two properties therefore hold on the same wiring. These descriptions occupy $O(m)$ native words, and have linear expected generation work with uniform random words. An exhaustive lexicographic choice gives one computably uniform family; efficient deterministic generation of these particular sparse descriptions is unproved here. For any fixed $\epsilon>0$ the same construction with $r=\Theta_\epsilon(m)$ sufficiently small gives linear-wise independence and additive error at most $\epsilon m$ simultaneously for all sources. [Hoeffding, *Probability Inequalities for Sums of Bounded Random Variables*](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1963.10500830)

There is also a polynomial-time uniform dense affine construction. For $m=8^e$ take an odd $D$ of order $m^{1/4}$, field $K=\mathbb F_m$, and independent random coefficient polynomials $f,g\in K[T]$ of degree $<D$. At site $t\in K$ the two labels are $\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_8}(t^D+f(t))$ and $\mathrm{Tr}_{K/\mathbb F_8}(g(t))$. Interpolation on any $D$ distinct sites and the surjective trace give independent uniform records. For every coefficient choice, additive character orthogonality and Weil's polynomial bound give
$$
 \left|\#\{t:\mathrm{Tr}(t^D+f(t))=0\}-m/8\right|
 \le \tfrac78(D-1)\sqrt m.
$$
Each of the seven nontrivial characters lifts to a nontrivial character on $K$, and the leading degree $D$ is odd in characteristic two. In particular every uninterrupted clock lies within $O(m^{3/4})$ of $5+(35/2)m$. One may construct the field by enumerating monic polynomials over $\mathbb F_2$ of degree $3e$, testing irreducibility, and using the eight roots of $x^8=x$ for its subfield. The generation time is separate from the native clock on the explicit records. [Wan and Wang, equation (1) of *Index Bounds for Character Sums with Polynomials over Finite Fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.00988)

## Exact high-order marginals with no all-late input

A short construction gives as many as $\lfloor m/7\rfloor-1$ independent records if only exclusion of the all-late tuple is required. Let $s=t+1\le m/7$, let $\pi_j=\binom mj (1/8)^j(7/8)^{m-j}$, and replace these masses for $0\le j\le s$ by $\pi_j-(-1)^j\binom sj\pi_0$. The new distribution on the number of fast records gives zero mass to zero. Its masses are nonnegative because $\binom mj/7^j\ge\binom sj$. The $s$-th finite difference annihilates all polynomials of degree at most $t$, preserving total mass and all factorial moments through order $t$.

Sample the indicated number of fast sites uniformly without replacement. Use label zero on the fast sites and independent uniform nonzero $p$ labels elsewhere; take independent uniform $q$ labels. For each $d\le t$, the probability that any $d$ prescribed sites are all fast is the preserved moment $\mathbb E[\binom Kd]/\binom md=8^{-d}$. Inclusion–exclusion supplies the mixed patterns, hence the complete physical seeds at any $t$ sites are independent uniform cohort members. The construction is an ordinary finite distribution, with an exact rational sampler computable from $m,t$. It need not be an affine wiring. The identification of exchangeable bounded independence with binomial moments is classical. [Peled, Yadin and Yehudayoff, *The maximal probability that k-wise independent bits are all 1*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0059)
