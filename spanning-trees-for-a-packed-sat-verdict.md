# Spanning trees for one SAT verdict at full encoded-bit density

There is an explicit fixed layout of ordinary width-three formulas of length $L$ for which the following holds. A constant-advantage representation of SAT by a minimum spanning tree on a fixed simple graph of polynomial size requires $\Omega(L/\log L)$ vertices. Each edge key may be an arbitrary real-valued function of **any two** of three independent source blocks. The owner pair of each edge is fixed in advance. The decoder may use arbitrary functions of pairs after seeing the tree, with their signs multiplied. It can even receive a tree-dependent sketch of one whole block of up to $(1-\epsilon)$ times that block's bit length, for fixed $\epsilon>0$, and use that sketch in all its pair functions. Without an advantage requirement, success $1/2$ is always attainable.

The lower bound is tight in order for this representation. It concerns one ordinary SAT decision on each input, with no workspace restriction on the local functions, and uses neither the matrix multiplication exponent nor a general circuit lower bound. The restriction is how the three blocks can interact.

## Encoding the generalized inner product in literal identifiers

Take $h\ge1$, $r=2^{3h}$ and $D=rh$. Give each of three parties $r$ strings of $h$ bits, collectively $A,B,Z\in\{0,1\}^D$. Reserve one public Boolean variable $T_{a,b,z}$ for each of the $r$ triples of $h$-bit strings, and pin it to $\sum_{j=1}^h a_j b_j z_j\bmod2$. Introduce $r+1$ separate parity variables $p_0,\ldots,p_r$. Pin $p_0=0,p_r=1$. For each $i=1,\ldots,r$ impose the four three-literal clauses for
$$
          p_i=p_{i-1}\mathbin\oplus T_{A_i,B_i,Z_i}.
$$
Each pin expands to four width-three clauses using two additional distinct dummy variables and all sign choices on them. All clauses then use three distinct variable names, and the same two dummy variables suffice for all pins. A fixed bank tag followed by the concatenation $a,b,z$ is the literal index for the chosen table entry. The only varying input bits are copies of source bits in these identifier fields. There are $\Theta(r)$ clauses with $\Theta(\log r)$-bit names, so $L=\Theta(D)$. The formula is satisfiable precisely when the generalized inner product $\sum_{j=1}^D A_jB_jZ_j$ is one. In particular, all auxiliaries apart from the irrelevant dummies are determined by the data when the endpoint constraints are consistent.

## Information after a proposed tree is named

Let $F=(-1)^{\sum_j A_j B_j Z_j}$. For a nonempty set $X\subseteq\{0,1\}^D$ restrict $A$ to be uniform on $X$, leaving the other two blocks independent and uniform. For arbitrary bounded complex pair functions $P_{AB},P_{BZ},P_{ZA}$, each of modulus at most one, three applications of Cauchy–Schwarz on these product measures give
$$
 \left|\mathbb E_{X,B,Z} F P_{AB}P_{BZ}P_{ZA}\right|^8
 \le \mathbb E_{a,a'\in X} 2^{-\operatorname{dist}(a,a')}.
$$
Indeed, the eight corners give the sign evaluated on $(a+a',B+B',Z+Z')$. Each differing bit of $a,a'$ contributes a factor $1/2$ on averaging over the other two differences. For $X$ the entire cube this is $(3/4)^D$. This is the classical generalized-inner-product discrepancy method. [Babai, Nisan and Szegedy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/002200009290047M)

For a sketch $S_T(A)$ of at most $(1-\epsilon)D$ bits defined for each proposed tree $T$, put $X$ equal to each sketch cell in turn. Cells of size less than $2^{\epsilon D/2}$ have total uniform mass at most $2^{-\epsilon D/2}$ (a fixed output alphabet of size $2^{(1-\epsilon)D}$). In a larger cell, choose $\eta>0$ with binary entropy $H_2(\eta)<\epsilon/4$ and $\eta<1/2$. For each $a$, at most $2^{H_2(\eta)D}$ points of the whole cube are within distance $\eta D$. Therefore the expectation on the right is at most $2^{-\epsilon D/4}+2^{-\eta D}$. Summing the conditional estimates with cell probabilities gives $2^{-\Omega_\epsilon(D)}$ correlation for a product of pair functions, even when those functions depend on the sketch. The sketch is an information allowance and its own computation has arbitrary cost. For linear sketches of rank $b$ there is a sharper bound $(3/4)^{(D-b)/8}$ since $a+a'$ is uniform on a space with at least $D-b$ independent pivot bits.

Let $\tau$ denote the number of spanning trees. For each fixed tree, split its fundamental-cut replacement tests by owner of the replacement. Checking its optimality then costs at most $2(v-1)$ cross-owner comparisons, the other tests being pair functions. Each comparison of $w$-bit keys is exactly a sum of products of pair functions with total absolute coefficient $O(w+1)$. This remains true within every sketch cell. Break ties with public IDs and globally rank all possible keys. There are at most $m\,2^{2D}$ such labeled keys across the whole input domain, so $w=O(D+\log(m+1))$ even for arbitrary real weights. Sum the correlation estimate, including the decoder for each tree, over the partition by the unique minimum tree. For constant advantage the result is
$$
            \log_2\tau + O\big(v\log_2(D+\log(m+1)+1)\big)
               =\Omega_\epsilon(D).
$$
For a fixed simple graph of polynomial size, $\log\tau=O(v\log D)$. This proves the vertex bound. If every key uses one source block only, the indicator for each proposed tree is just a product of pair functions, regardless of precision. In that case $\log_2\tau=\Omega_\epsilon(D)$ directly. [Comparison factorization and proof](forest-certificates-for-matrix-product-parity.md)

On a complete graph with $\Theta(D/\log D)$ suitably many vertices, there are at least $2^D$ spanning trees. Encode the first block injectively in a tree, give its edges lower keys than all other edges, and decode the remaining parity from $B,Z$ and the encoded first block. No sketch is needed for this upper bound. The local encoder and decoder have no resource budget.

## Checking what a real solver can do

A log-word RAM can evaluate the queried table entries and parity along this layout in $O(r)$ word operations using fixed-width records. Each lookup key combines pieces from **all three** blocks. A correct general solver can recognize the layout, verify the fixed parity-chain clauses and the consistency of repeated index fields, accept arbitrary Boolean pins in the table, and evaluate the chain with those actual pins. It can use any full SAT solver on other inputs. All layout checks take $O(r)$ word operations on this family. Thus this family itself admits $O(L/\log L)$ native time even though converting its decision to the prescribed forest form requires the stated number of vertices. To reach a general SAT lower bound by this approach one has to account for jointly formed addresses or provide a distinct representation theorem. Every exact general solver also needs $\Omega(r)$ word probes on **every** input of this layout. For any one row replace its query label everywhere in that row by a public table position of opposite pinned parity. The other rows are unchanged and the SAT verdict flips. The original run must meet that row's varying fields, and a word intersects only constantly many row regions. Thus the native running time achievable by a correct general solver on this family is sharply $\Theta(L/\log L)$.

With single-bit input probes the optimal mean under uniform source bits is $7D/4$ if fixed bits and wiring are free. Each underlying triple must either expose a zero or expose all three ones. Otherwise two completions consistent with the transcript flip just this triple's contribution. Under independent fair bits the first fresh probe from each triple is necessary, a second is necessary when the first bit is one, and a third when both are one. Interleaving queries to other triples preserves these probabilities. Querying each triple until its contribution is known attains the bound. Multiple copies of a field confer no additional information. [Three-block forest analysis](forest-certificates-for-matrix-product-parity.md) · [Small-instance checks](packed_sat_forest_audit.py)

There is also an optimal scanner whose addresses are public throughout this family. Validate the prescribed table at public positions, combine each row's three fields by bitwise AND and XOR the results into an accumulator, then take its parity. Thus even the formation of a data-dependent lookup address is avoidable here. A separate theorem bounds the number of quadratic tests needed to explain the values on an actual run.
