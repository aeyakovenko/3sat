# A smaller strip with the same colorings

Use the 18-point anchor with a consecutive red strip and two blue lanes, each allowed to reorder its own parity. The anchor stays fixed throughout. [Anchor, file format and simplicity proof](one-point-growth-with-mandatory-ram-reads.md)

Number the red strip points $1,\ldots,m$, with $m\ge2$. Call $\{i,i+2\}$ a missing skip when it is absent from the actual blue lane, and let $b$ count these pairs. There is a deterministic $O(m)$-word-time transformation back to the same 18-point anchored family with at most $20b+49$ remaining strip points. It preserves the direction of a fixed increasing lane, the number of missing skips, and the number of proper colorings extending each fixed anchor coloring. Its packed output uses $O((b+1)\log(b+2))$ bits regardless of the starting $m$.

Mark the positions $1,2,m-1,m$ and both endpoints of each missing skip. There are at most $s=2b+4$ marks. An unmarked position has both natural skip neighbors within its lane. Those two incidences exhaust its blue degree, so it cannot touch an exceptional chord or a lane attachment. In each maximal unmarked red interval of length $e$, retain the entire interval if $e<4$. Otherwise retain the first and last two positions and enough other positions to leave length $4+((e-4)\bmod6)$, deleting one contiguous subinterval. This leaves at most nine points per unmarked interval. There are at most $s+1$ such intervals. Delete the chosen positions from both blue lists and relabel by increasing retained red rank. Computing marks and assigning new red ranks each take one direct-address scan. Counting the retained points gives $s+9(s+1)\le 20b+49$.

On the two red positions directly before and after any deleted block, and throughout that block, every internal natural skip is present. Within each parity lane the deleted positions are consecutive between the corresponding two surviving neighbors, possibly in reverse blue order, since each internal lane vertex has exactly the two expected neighbors. The deletion therefore splices a natural skip on each parity and a consecutive edge in red. No endpoint of an exceptional chord or lane attachment is deleted. Each original missing skip stays missing. Its two endpoints are marked, and deletion leaves the one intervening position intact. The endpoints of every new blue adjacency across a deletion lie among its unmarked guard points, so filtering the list keeps these marked pairs absent. Every exceptional chord keeps both endpoints and still spans more than two red ranks. Indeed, a chord crossing a deletion spans the four surviving guard points because its endpoints are marked. Every other skip on the shorter strip is an original retained skip or a replacement across one deleted block. Hence $b$ is unchanged. The retained parities agree with the old ones because the deletions have even length. A prescribed monotone lane stays monotone, and the anchor and parity separation still imply simplicity of the full order.

In any proper coloring, consecutive red edges surrounding a present skip form a triangle with a unique choice for the third color. On two guard points and an adjoining deleted block this yields an oriented period-three sequence. Erasing $6j$ positions preserves every splice inequality. Conversely, the two splice skips and three red edges force all four guard colors to be consecutive phases in the shortened graph. Its first two guard colors determine the orientation. Reinsert the omitted points by continuing this orientation for $6j$ positions. This is the unique extension, agrees with the right guard colors, and creates no exceptional incidences. Disjoint guarded deletions can be processed independently. This proves the claimed bijection. Kim and coauthors develop protrusion replacement for graph kernels with bounded interfaces. ## What a vanishing fraction of breaks on negative outputs would imply

Fix $j\ge1$. Suppose a deterministic $O(n^j)$-time reduction, of encoded output length $O(n)$, takes every explicit 3-SAT input of $n$ bits to this strip family, preserving its verdict. If on its negative outputs $b=o(m)$ uniformly as $m\to\infty$, then 3-SAT has a deterministic $O(n^j)$-time unrestricted-workspace RAM algorithm. No rate of convergence in the little-o estimate is required.

On an input of length $n$, form the strip, compute the kernel and render the usual dense 3-CNF for its path coloring. The rendered formula has $O(b+1)$ variables and $O((b+1)\log(b+2))$ physical bits. The original packed order has $\Theta((m+1)\log(m+2))=O(n)$ bits. Thus all sufficiently long negative inputs have a rendered kernel of length at most $n/2$, since bounded $m$ also gives bounded kernel length. Above a fixed finite cutoff, accept immediately when the kernel exceeds $n/2$. Otherwise continue on that ordinary 3-CNF. At bounded lengths use exhaustive search. The time sum over geometrically shrinking inputs is $O(n^j)$. A fixed polynomial-time reducer similarly gives $\mathrm P=\mathrm{NP}$. Conversely an $O(n^j)$ SAT decider supplies such a reducer by deciding the source and choosing a fixed positive or negative representative, with the negative representative chosen from the increasing-lane family, which has zero missing skips at every strip length. Thus producing a native reducer with a vanishing negative break fraction is equivalent to the respective fast SAT upper bound. This equivalence leaves open each of the three unrestricted SAT running-time questions.
