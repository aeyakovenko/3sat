# A near-quadratic exact network for the graph of binary matrix multiplication

The tensor-network attack on SAT asks for a $d^{2+\delta}$ volume lower bound for the bit predicate $[AB=C]$, where the matrices are binary and the product is over $\mathbb F_2$. **Over every coefficient field of characteristic two this is impossible.** There is an exact binary-wire network with $O(d^2\log^2(d+2))$ sites of arity at most three. It takes the actual factors and proposed product as external inputs. Its volume exponent in $d$ is exactly two. This conclusion uses no conjectured or preprint lower bound. In odd characteristic the graph exponent is now at most $11/5$; a lower exponent strictly above two remains open. ## A witness for every proposed entry at once

In characteristic two use the identity
$$
  [AB=C]=\prod_{i,j}\left(1+C_{ij}+\sum_{k=1}^d A_{ik}B_{kj}\right).
$$
Give every output position one independent selector. Its alternatives are the constant one, its own proposed bit, and the $d$ inner indices. A selection of $k$ contributes one exactly when both of its requested input bits are one. Each joint selection of alternatives therefore contributes its chosen product, and summing the accepting selections gives the displayed expression. The joint selection is allowed to have many accepting completions. Their **parity** is the required external tensor value.

Encoding each index costs $O(\log(d+2))$ bits. Directly supplying a separate multiplexer of size $d$ to every position would cost cubic volume. Instead form one batch of table requests per input bank. Each record contains an address and one claimed value. Include one initialization record per input cell with its actual external value. Sort the records by address, then require adjacent records with the same address to have the same value. Since every address has an initialization record, precisely one assignment to the claimed bits is consistent. Use a fixed dummy address for inactive alternatives, enforce canonical encodings of the two special alternatives, and reject unused selector codes. Every comparator and consistency gate is deterministic. Ties can be resolved by the same fixed rule in every comparator. A sorted record need not be routed back: the original claimed bits already enter the local checks.

There are $O(d^2)$ records of width $O(\log(d+2))$. A fixed sorting network with $O(m\log m)$ comparators on $m$ records therefore uses $O(d^2\log^2(d+2))$ bounded-fan-in gates and copy sites. A Boolean gate has one allowed output per input, so converting this verification circuit to an exact network adds no summation multiplicity. Explicit bounded-degree copy trees charge linear cost in the number of uses. The table cells are read through one external bit each. The matching exponent lower bound $\Omega(d^2)$ already follows from the named exterior bits in the upstream volume convention. ## Many factors and any fixed input field

Let $K$ be a fixed finite field of characteristic $p$, let $F$ be **any** coefficient field of characteristic $p$, and supply $\ell\ge1$ square matrices over $K$ together with a proposed product $C$. Use any fixed canonical binary representation of $K$, for example coordinates in a basis over $\mathbb F_p$. The exact graph has volume
$$
                  O_K\bigl(\ell d^2\log^2(d+2)\bigr).
$$
Each coordinate of each product entry is a sum of contributions of paths of length $\ell$ in the layered matrices. A path specifies one inner index per intermediate layer, and contributes the indicated $\mathbb F_p$-coordinate of the product of its $\ell$ entries in $K$. For one coordinate let $y$ be the proposed coordinate minus this path sum. Its equality bit is $1-y^{p-1}$ in the prime subfield of $F$. Multiply these factors over all coordinates and positions. In expanding each factor, either choose the constant one, or choose the other term and take $p-1$ independently ordered choices from the discrepancy sum. A choice there is the proposed coordinate or a signed path contribution. The order accounts for the multiplicities in the ordinary power. Require canonical unused and dummy bits. Each path product and its coordinate can be computed by a deterministic constant-width circuit per step since $K$ is fixed. Small field weights are realized by constant-size local tables over $F$. If invalid bit codes need a verdict, a deterministic validity check can set their weight to zero.

At any given layer there are $O_K(d^2)$ requests, addressed by the two consecutive path indices. Verify them against that layer's table by the preceding batch construction. The layers have separate sorts, so the logarithm depends on $d$ even for growing $\ell$. For fixed $K$ this approaches linear volume in the total input length up to logarithms. Modular path counting and closure under products have classical complexity-class formulations. This argument needs their explicit volume and uniqueness of the lookup verification. ## The exact width of a product-preserving change of field

A possible way to carry this algorithm to odd characteristic would be to encode the two binary factors separately so that their ordinary matrix product in the new field is an encoding of $AB$. For any $d\ge1$, the **minimum intermediate width of such an encoding is exactly $2^d-1$** when the coefficient field has characteristic different from two. This applies even with different nonlinear encoders for the two factors, arbitrary outer dimensions, and an output encoding required to distinguish only $0$ and $E_{11}$.

More precisely suppose $L(A)R(B)=O(AB)$ for all binary matrices, where $L$ has $h$ columns, $R$ has $h$ rows and $O(E_{11})\ne O(0)$. Fix a coordinate of the output matrices where the two encodings differ by $c\ne0$. For $A=e_1v^\mathsf T$ and $B=xe_1^\mathsf T$ their binary product is $(v^\mathsf T x)E_{11}$. Subtract the chosen row of $L(0)$ from the chosen row of each $L(e_1v^\mathsf T)$ and pair it with the chosen column of $R(xe_1^\mathsf T)$. This gives a rank-at-most-$h$ factorization of $c M$, where
$$
                        M_{v,x}=[v^\mathsf T x=1],\qquad v,x\ne0 .
$$
For $d\ge2$, $MM^\mathsf T=2^{d-2}(I+J)$, since equal nonzero linear forms have $2^{d-1}$ common ones and distinct ones have $2^{d-2}$. Here $I+J$ is invertible: its inverse is $I-2^{-d}J$ at size $2^d-1$. At $d=1$ the matrix is $[1]$. Thus $h\ge2^d-1$. In characteristic two the same bit matrix has rank $d$, by factoring it into its two bit-vector banks and restricting to unit vectors. The identity encoding attains that width there.

For the matching cross-characteristic upper bound use formal basis vectors labelled by nonzero binary $d$-vectors. Represent $A$ by the linear operator sending the basis label $x$ to the label $Ax$ when $Ax\ne0$, and to the zero vector otherwise. Composition gives the binary product exactly, and the operators distinguish every pair of binary matrices. This proves sharpness even when faithful encoding is required. The rank calculation is the usual inner-product matrix argument. Lean verifies the width lower bound for separate encoders over any field in which two is nonzero. ## Nonlinear decoding of several measured products

Now permit a **completely arbitrary nonlinear decoder** of $m\ge1$ Boolean measurement results. The measurements are chosen in advance, and each one, on the full two input banks, is a bit given by the ordinary dot product of $h$ features of $A$ with $h$ features of $B$ over a field of characteristic different from two. The features themselves may be nonlinear. Even to decode a single specified binary product entry on all inputs one must have
$$
                              (h+1)^m\ge 2^d .
$$
When $m$ divides $d$, the minimum possible maximum width is **exactly $2^{d/m}-1$**. In general the width $2^{\lceil d/m\rceil}-1$ suffices.

To prove the bound, retain one free row $v$ of $A$ and one free column $x$ of $B$, fixing other entries. The desired sign is $(-1)^{v^\mathsf T x}$. Its matrix over all $v,x$ has rank $2^d$, since its square is $2^d I$. Every function of the $m$ measurement bits has a multilinear representation on their cube, including the sign of the decoder output. Expand this polynomial after substituting the $h$ separated terms in each measurement. Any resulting row factor is a product choosing one of $h$ features or the constant from each measurement. There are at most $(h+1)^m$ such row factors, independently of the coefficients, so the sign matrix has rank at most that value. To attain the upper bound, split the $d$ coordinates into $m$ nearly equal blocks. On $b$ bits, the binary inner-product truth matrix has rank $2^b-1$, as proved above, hence is one width-$(2^b-1)$ measurement. The parity of the $m$ measured block bits gives the entry. This is an exact bound on width even though the final decoder is unlimited. Lean verifies the nonadaptive lower bound including arbitrary truth-table decoders. [Formal Walsh-rank and measurement proof](upstream-checks/CrossCharacteristicProductWidth.lean)

The same counting idea gives a bound for arbitrary answers in a fixed *prime* field $\mathbb F_p$ of odd characteristic, without a Boolean range promise. Interpolate the output sign in the $m$ answers to individual degree at most $p-1$. Per measurement there are at most $\binom{h+p-1}{p-1}$ row-feature monomials, giving
$$
                     \binom{h+p-1}{p-1}^{m}\ge 2^d .
$$
For adaptive prime-field measurements, expand into at most $p^m$ transcripts, padding any short branch by dummy measurements. A path is the product of its answer indicators, each of individual degree at most $p-1$ in the fixed measurement on that path. This gives $(p\binom{h+p-1}{p-1})^m\ge2^d$. These versions bound one decoder tree; a sum over arbitrarily many adaptive trees introduces additional rank.

These lower bounds determine the optimal exponential rate even when a channel can return an **arbitrary symbol of a fixed odd finite field** $F=\mathbb F_{p^a}$. For either adaptive or nonadaptive decoding of one product bit with $m$ queries, the optimal width satisfies
$$
                  \log_2(h+1)=\frac{d}{a(p-1)m}+O_{F}(1).
$$
To obtain the lower bound, expand one channel in $a$ prime-field coordinates. Its $a$ answers are bilinear functions of $ah$ prime-field features on each side, since field multiplication is bilinear in the basis coordinates. Any answer indicator, interpolated in these $a$ coordinates, has degree at most $a(p-1)$ in the row features. It has at most $B=\binom{ah+a(p-1)}{a(p-1)}$ distinct row monomials. For a nonadaptive decoder interpolate jointly on all $am$ answer coordinates, giving ordinary rank of the exact signed table at most $B^m$ over $\mathbb F_p$. An adaptive tree has at most $|F|^m$ transcripts and the same per-transcript bound; its exact sign table has rank at most $(|F|B)^m$. Since the Walsh table has rank $2^d$ over $\mathbb F_p$, this gives the claimed lower estimate on $\log_2(h+1)$.

For the upper bound split the $d$ coordinates into $am(p-1)$ nearly equal blocks. Use one rank-$2^b-1$ measurement for the parity on each block of length $b$ as above. Group at most $p-1$ such bits in each of the $a$ coordinates of a field element. Multiply the terms for each coordinate by its field basis vector and concatenate all their features to obtain *one* ordinary field dot product per query. Its coordinate is the integer number of ones in that group, between zero and $p-1$, with no modular wrap. Recover all the coordinate parities and take their XOR. This nonadaptive construction uses width at most $a(p-1)(2^{\lceil d/(am(p-1))\rceil}-1)$ per query. Thus adaptivity cannot improve the leading logarithmic width for a fixed alphabet. The fixed-field assumption matters: on an infinite field a single channel of unbounded range can encode arbitrary data into one symbol for an unrestricted decoder.

In particular, a conversion that supplies a separate decoder for each of the $d^2$ binary output entries, each fed only Boolean width-$h$ product measurements, needs at least $d^3/\log_2(h+1)$ measurement-to-decoder incidences. The same channel may serve multiple entries, and an incidence is counted at each decoder it feeds. The block construction attains this incidence bound at $h=2^b-1$ for $b\mid d$. For polynomial width this rules out a near-quadratic number of incidences in this conversion. A single joint decision on the proposed product can bypass the separate-output requirement.

The upper bound relies essentially on many internal choices summing in matching characteristic. Already at side one, with $A=B=0$ and $C=1$, two selectors accept and their contributions add to zero. Existence of a selector therefore cannot serve as a SAT-style certificate of equality. Enumerating the selectors to compute parity loses the volume bound.
