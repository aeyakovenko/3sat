# One not-all-equal triple suffices for unrestricted SAT

Ordinary proper 3-SAT has a deterministic linear-time, linear-output-length RAM reduction to the following class of dense, sorted, duplicate-free DIMACS formulas. Every variable occurs **exactly four times**. Exactly two clauses are homogeneous. They use the **same three variables** and have opposite signs.
$$
  (r_1\lor r_2\lor r_3),\qquad (\neg r_1\lor\neg r_2\lor\neg r_3).
$$
Together they require the three bits to be not all equal (NAE). Every other clause contains both a positive and a negative literal. They can always be put on names 1, 2 and 3. Every clause has exactly three distinct variables. If either exceptional clause is deleted, an explicitly known constant assignment satisfies the resulting file. In an unsatisfiable output the maximum possible number of satisfied clauses is exactly one less than the total. Every unsatisfiable subset contains both exceptional clauses.

Consequently a deterministic $O(n^d)$ solver for this restricted class would decide unrestricted proper 3-SAT in $O(n^d)$, for $d=1,2,3$ and encoded bit length $n$. Conversely every unrestricted solver already handles the class. The construction preserves the question at each exponent with unrestricted workspace and any sufficiently large fixed logarithmic word-width coefficient.

Tovey proved NP-completeness at occurrence four and satisfiability of every proper instance at occurrence three. The extra restriction here is on the signs of *every* clause, with the two exceptions sharing their variables, together with the encoded-size and time bounds. I have not established priority for this combination. [Tovey, Theorems 2.3 and 2.4](https://cedric.cnam.fr/~bentzc/INITREC/Files/CB11.pdf) · [Further results on restricted 3-SAT by Berman, Karpinski and Scott](https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/scott/Papers/restricted3sat.pdf)

## Generating forced constants with bounded occurrences

Use the two exceptional clauses once. For each root $r_i$ add the mixed clauses $(\neg r_i,A_i,B_i)$ and $(r_i,\neg C_i,\neg D_i)$, using fresh variables. A nonconstant root assignment forces at least one of the six positive candidates $A_i,B_i$ true and at least one of the six negative candidates $C_i,D_i$ false. Each candidate has appeared only once and each root has now appeared four times. It is unnecessary to know which candidate is forced.

For each positive candidate $a_0$ create $a_1$ and $z$ and add $(\neg a_0,a_1,z)$ and $(\neg a_0,a_1,\neg z)$. Then add $(\neg a_i,\neg a_{i+1},a_{i+2})$ for consecutive $i$ starting at zero. If $a_0$ is true, induction forces all descendants true. The initial $a_0$ uses one incoming occurrence, two for initialization and one for the first recurrence. The next variable $a_1$ uses two initialization occurrences and up to two later ones. Each $a_j$ with $j\ge2$ occurs in at most three recurrence clauses, permitting one further use. Complement all chain literals to obtain the corresponding propagation of falsity from a false candidate. Both constructions use mixed clauses. Supply linearly many descendants, sufficient for the following uses.

To obtain a fresh variable $t$ that is forced true, divide the six positive chains into two groups of three. In each group create two intermediate variables $u,v$. For each of the three chains take two fresh descendants $a,b$ and append $(\neg a,\neg b,u)$. Do the same for $v$ with two *other* descendants of each chain. Append $(\neg u,\neg v,t)$ for each group. A group containing an active chain forces $u=v=1$, hence $t=1$. Each intermediate occurs four times and $t$ occurs twice. Each chosen descendant is used only once. Dualize this construction on the six negative chains to obtain a forced-false $f$. Repeat with fresh descendants and intermediate variables to get all the constants needed. For the extension direction one may set all positive candidates, their chains and their output variables true, and the whole negative side false, for **any** fixed nonconstant root triple. The initialization variables $z$ can then be assigned arbitrarily.

## Compiling an arbitrary source

Dense renaming and duplicate removal can be implemented in $O(n)$ time even by scanning one byte at a time. Intern significant decimal digits of each variable name in a radix-ten trie with one node per distinct prefix, identify equal names by their terminal nodes, and counting-sort the triples of identifiers to remove duplicate clauses. On the normalized source $G$, make a fresh copy of each literal occurrence, preserving its sign. For a source clause on these signed copies $A,B,C$, use distinct new padding variables $f,t$ of the kinds just constructed and fresh $u,v$. Append the three mixed clauses
$$
 (f,A,\neg u),\qquad (u,B,\neg v),\qquad (v,C,\neg t).
$$
For $f=0,t=1$ the projection onto $A,B,C$ is their ordinary disjunction. If all three are false, the first two clauses successively force $u=0,v=0$, which violates the third. If at least one is true, take $u=A$, $v=A\lor B$, which satisfies all three. All comparisons here concern the truth values of the signed occurrences.

For each source variable with at least two copies, put the copies in a directed cycle. Each edge $x\to y$ contributes $(\neg x,y,f)$ with its own forced-false padding variable. Thus all copies have the same value. Each occurrence copy is used once in its source clause and at most twice in links. The ordinary fresh variables and padding bits also stay within four occurrences. Private gate names, distinct root names and fresh padding ensure proper clauses without duplicates. Number all allocated names consecutively and sort the output.

For *every* nonconstant setting of the three roots the resulting clauses extend to a model precisely when $G$ has a satisfying assignment. Conversely, on removing both exceptional clauses, the two constant assignments are models of the mixed body, independently of $G$. Its projection onto the three designated bits is therefore exactly $\{000,111\}$ when $G$ is unsatisfiable and the entire three-bit cube when $G$ is satisfiable. This also proves a time-preserving equivalence with asking whether two designated bits can differ in an all-mixed, always-satisfiable proper formula. One prescribed bit by itself can take either value by choosing the corresponding constant assignment. The same two roots can be named in every instance.

If $m$ distinct source clauses remain after normalization, the construction uses $O(m+1)$ variables and clauses. In particular there is one compiled copy of the logical source. The constant-supply chains and gates have total length linear in the number of occurrences and equality links. Minimal decimal names need $O(\log(m+2))$ bits each. Distinct source records give $m\log(m+2)=O(n+1)$ by counting strings over a fixed byte alphabet. Allocate names densely and build occurrence buckets, then use counting-sort passes on the constant-size tuples of signed integers. Precompute the decimal representations of identifiers before printing. This gives $O(n+1)$ native work and output bits including normalization of arbitrary proper input names. The empty source can also be sent to the two root clauses with the six initial mixed clauses. [Dense normalization on the RAM](dense-sorted-cores-for-ram-exhaustion.md)

## Making every occurrence count exactly four

For every missing occurrence at an old variable $x$, take five fresh variables $a,b,c,d,e$. Add $(x,a,\neg b)$. On the five auxiliaries consider the five-cycle $a,c,b,d,e,a$ and its extra edge $ab$. For each of these six different edges take the complementary triple of names, in numerical order, and add the row with signs $+,-,+$ on that triple. In these six triples $a,b$ occur three times each and the other three names occur four times each, by counting omitted edges. The boundary clause completes $a,b$ to four and adds exactly one occurrence of $x$. Both uniform settings on the five auxiliaries extend either value of $x$. The seven rows are distinct and mixed. This costs only a constant number of new rows per missing incidence and leaves the root projection unchanged. Each root already occurs four times in the original construction.

On the resulting exactly regular instances the root restriction also preserves resolution hardness. There are unsatisfiable files of $N$ bits requiring $2^{\Omega(N/\log N)}$ lines in general resolution despite either constant assignment violating just one clause. The new clauses admit a literal substitution to the source. [Proof projection and resolution bounds](literal-projection-of-the-nae-core.md)

## How far the decisive sign changes can be from the root triple

The root values can depend on signs at graph distance $\Omega(N/\log N)$ in the mixed body. In each chain skip its first $h$ available descendants before allocating any to the padding construction. Every path from one of the three roots to the source clauses must first leave a chain through a used descendant. A recurrence clause connects variables whose chain indices differ by at most two. Crossing from one variable to another costs two edges of the clause-variable incidence graph. Thus even reaching the first used descendant from a root costs at least $2+2\lceil(h+2)/2\rceil$ incidence edges. Each regularization gadget attaches at just one old vertex. Taking $h$ of the order of the source clause count retains linear encoded length and gives the displayed distance.

There are opposite-verdict pairs with the *same entire unsigned incidence graph*, byte length and byte histogram, with just two different signed incidences outside this radius. Start with an odd-charge Tseitin contradiction on a connected simple cubic graph. Pick a vertex whose removal leaves a connected graph, for example a leaf of a spanning tree. Its four local clauses exclude the four patterns of the wrong parity. Choose two excluded patterns taking opposite values at one common coordinate and flip that literal in their two clauses. The four clauses after the change have distinct forbidden patterns. They now allow the first formerly forbidden pattern. Fix it at the chosen vertex. On the remaining connected graph the residual vertex charges sum to zero, so the remaining parity constraints extend by solving on a spanning tree. This gives a satisfiable companion.

Use the same clause and variable-occurrence order to construct the two outputs. Only the signs of the two designated copies change, once in each direction. Allocated names, clause and variable counts, all unsigned incidence lists and all degree completions agree. Sorting the output records preserves the byte counts. The two changes count signed incidences. Canonical sorting can move bytes within the serialized files. On cubic expanders the negative member retains the exponential resolution bound by assigning the extra descendants their chain polarity in the literal projection. On the $K_4$ example with $h=64$, the exactly regular files have 195,988 bytes each. The nearer of the changed signed incidences has an endpoint at distance 73 from the root set in the mixed body. ## How much satisfiability the mixed body already exhibits

Every all-mixed proper formula on $v$ occurring variables with maximum occurrence four has a constructible satisfying Boolean subcube of dimension at least $\lceil v/12\rceil$ with one of the constant assignments as a corner. Call a variable safe at one constant if flipping that variable alone still gives a model. Each mixed clause prevents just one single flip across the two constants, at its minority signed literal. If $S$ counts the safe coordinate copies across the two centers, then $S\ge2v-m$, where $m\le4v/3$ counts clauses.

Make a separate conflict graph on the safe coordinates at each center. When exactly one literal of a clause is true, the variable of that literal is unsafe. When two are true, put an edge between their variables if both are safe. An independent set of safe coordinates can be varied arbitrarily. Each clause either retains its unique true literal or retains at least one of its two true literals. Across the disjoint union of the two graphs the maximum degree is four. A vertex of degree four used every occurrence of its physical variable on the two-true side, with the same literal polarity. Its counterpart at the other center is safe and isolated because none of those occurrences is a unique true literal or a member of a true pair there. Reserve those distinct isolated counterparts. If there were $k$ degree-four vertices, ignoring those $k$ and reserving their $k$ counterparts leaves a graph of maximum degree three. A greedy four-coloring supplies an independent set of size at least one quarter of the remaining vertices. Together with the reserved vertices this gives at least $S/4$ vertices, and one center accounts for at least $S/8\ge v/12$. The graph construction and coloring take $O(v+m)$ word operations. Fixing the three roots at their original value costs at most three dimensions.

The constant is optimal for cubes required to have a *uniform corner*. Take three disjoint sets $Y,Z,W$ of four variables each. A row $a,b\to c$ denotes $(\neg a,\neg b,c)$. Start with the eight rows
$$
 y_1,y_2\to y_3;\quad y_1,y_3\to y_4;\quad y_1,y_4\to y_2;\quad y_2,y_3\to y_1;\quad
 y_2,y_4\to w_1;\quad y_3,y_4\to w_2;\quad w_1,w_3\to w_4;\quad w_2,w_4\to w_3 .
$$
Add their sign-complements after replacing every $y_i$ by $z_i$ and keeping $W$ fixed. Each variable occurs four times. At zero only the four $Y$ variables can individually flip, since each $Z$ and $W$ variable is the unique negative literal of some row. Every pair of $Y$ variables occurs as two negative inputs in a row and its simultaneous flip violates that row with the output left at zero. Thus the largest satisfying cube with zero as a corner has dimension one. Sign symmetry with $Y,Z$ interchanged gives the same answer at one. Disjoint copies attain the bound for all positive multiples of twelve.

The largest of the 256 two-exception files has 24,831 bytes before regularization and 44,375 bytes with every occurrence count equal to four. For both versions the audit also checks all 2,048 root settings on the mixed bodies. It checks both exceptional clauses, their common triple, the occurrence bound and the SAT verdict, verifying positive assignments on the parsed clauses.
