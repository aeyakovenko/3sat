# Implicit affine batches and the cost of recovering triangle parity

Can one aggregate many expensive continuations into one SAT input? Consider a public butterfly subgraph and an implicitly named affine subspace $H$ of its source–sink pairs. There are two existential questions about $H$. Finding whether **some pair is unreachable** takes one scan of the possible edges. Finding whether **some pair is reachable** already includes tripartite triangle detection when only three vertex layers can be disabled. The asymmetry also gives an algebraic test of the proposed matrix-multiplication route. Recovering the parity of the number of triangles needs $\Theta(n^2)$ adaptive triangle-existence queries on $n$-by-$n$-by-$n$ input tables if the inputs to each query may be arbitrary affine functions of the original table bits. The lower bound counts queries, regardless of the size of the queried graphs.

## Projecting an affine family instead of enumerating its paths

Take a depth-$d$, degree-$B$ butterfly with $B$ a power of two. Its labels consist of $d$ base-$B$ digits. On the unique path for $(s,t)$, the edge at layer $i$ is specified by the first $i+1$ digits of $t$ and the last $d-i$ digits of $s$, for $0\le i<d$. This is a coordinate projection $\pi_i$ of the combined binary endpoint label. Assume $H$ is specified by an offset and a basis; the empty family can be handled separately. A pair in $H$ is unreachable exactly when some missing edge is in $\pi_i H$ at its layer. Row-reduce the projected basis over $\mathbb F_2$ and enumerate the resulting coset, for example by depth-first XORs of its independent generators. Each layer has at most $B^{d+1}$ distinct edges. With $E=dB^{d+1}$ possible edges, the total time is $O(E+\operatorname{poly}(\log E))$ word operations, even when $H$ has $B^{2d}$ pairs. A descriptor fits a constant number of logarithmic words. Gaussian elimination over the $O(\log E)$ coordinates and $O(\log E)$ layers contributes the polynomial term. Reporting all pairs is unnecessary. A mere explicit matching of $L=B^d$ queries costs only $O(Ld)$ path checks even for the other verdict. [Butterfly definition and previous single-run lower bound](three-address-shares-on-one-cold-sat-run.md)

For the other polarity take $B=2$, depth $2h$, and $n=2^h$. Fix the affine endpoint relation
$$
                         s=(j,i),\qquad t=(k,j)
$$
with $i,j,k$ unrestricted $h$-bit words. The midpoint is $(k,i)$. Mark enabled source, midpoint and sink vertices using three arbitrary $n$-square Boolean tables $A_{ji},C_{ki},B_{kj}$, respectively. Delete every edge leaving a disabled source or midpoint and every edge entering a disabled sink. An endpoint pair in $H$ is reachable precisely when its three corresponding bits are one. Thus the aggregate is exactly triangle existence. Reading or writing these three layer masks costs $O(n^2)$ entries. Expanding the whole butterfly costs $O(n^2\log n)$ edges. Directly freeing the three index selectors in the journal's existing proper-3-CNF three-table lookup construction gives a $\Theta(n^2)$-bit ordinary SAT encoding with one physical bit per data entry. Triangle detection is a classical Boolean matrix multiplication application. These reductions give no superquadratic RAM lower bound for it. ## An exact query lower bound on the identity slice

The input consists of independent binary entries in three $n$-square matrices. The desired output is the parity of the triangle count, $\sum_{i,j,k} U_{ij} V_{jk} W_{ki}$ over $\mathbb F_2$. After any transcript, a deterministic query algorithm can submit a new graph of any size, with each edge indicator an arbitrary affine function over $\mathbb F_2$ of all original input bits. It learns whether some triangle is present. Its choice of affine functions and the computation on its old answers are unrestricted. It can even use an affine function of the original bits as its final output, chosen at the leaf. Any additional *observation* of a fresh affine bit counts as a one-bit test. This is a restriction on how input data reach the computation; arbitrary nonlinear preprocessing of all input bits would solve the problem without any oracle call.

Set $W$ to identity. The target is then $Q(x,y)=\sum_{a=1}^{M} x_a y_a$ on $M=n^2$ independent pairs. Maintain a nonempty affine flat on which the transcript is fixed. If no triangle in the current query can appear anywhere on the flat, answer NO and keep the flat. Otherwise pick a feasible triangle, impose its three affine edge equations, and answer YES. This costs at most three in codimension. A direct affine bit observation costs at most one. Thus after $q$ triangle queries and $r$ bit observations the remaining affine flat has codimension at most $3q+r$.

If $Q$ agrees with an affine function on $v_0+D$, its second differences on $D$ vanish. Its polar form is
$$
       ((x,y),(x',y'))\longmapsto x\cdot y'+x'\cdot y.
$$
It is nondegenerate. Therefore $D\subseteq D^\perp$ implies $2\dim D\le 2M$. This proves **$3q+r\ge M$ on some single execution** of every correct deterministic query algorithm. Adding one independent product $x_{M+1}y_{M+1}$ appends a nondegenerate plane, increasing the required codimension by exactly one: $x=0$ still gives a constant flat of codimension $M+1$. At graph size $n+1$, the identity slice gains $2n+1$ independent product terms. The adversary chooses each restriction after seeing the submitted query, so adaptivity and affine re-encoding at the successor size do not bypass the bound.

With only triangle queries the optimum on the identity slice is **exactly $\lceil M/3\rceil$**. Split the pairs into blocks of three, padding with zero pairs as needed. For one block over $\mathbb F_2^3$,
$$
            x\cdot y=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
            x=0\quad\text{or}\quad y=z\times x\text{ for some }z\in\mathbb F_2^3.
$$
Here $\times$ is the ordinary coordinate cross product reduced modulo two. For $x\ne0$ the map $z\mapsto z\times x$ has kernel $\{0,x\}$ and its image is the plane orthogonal to $x$. Each of the nine alternatives is three affine equalities. Make nine vertex-disjoint triangles with the edge indicators testing those equalities. One query gives the complement of the block inner product. XOR the recovered block answers. On the full three-table problem $O(n^2)$ queries also suffice by reading all entries via isolated triangles, so the exponent two is exact there. [Lean verification of the three-pair oracle](AffineTriangleOracle.lean)

This rules out a constant or polylogarithmic number of adaptive affine triangle-existence calls to compute even one binary trace parity on these inputs. The obstruction survives arbitrary dimensions for the queried graphs, and restriction to $W=I$ already proves it. On that same identity slice a word RAM computes the parity by reading and combining packed words. An unrestricted SAT oracle can accept formulas describing those operations directly. Thus the exact $M$-to-$M+1$ induction prices a concrete algebraic recovery strategy and leaves the unrestricted SAT clock open. Lean checks the adaptive adversary, the nondegenerate polar form, the dimension bound, and their composition for triangle queries with an arbitrary affine output at each leaf. The theorem `adaptive_query_count` proves $M\le 3\operatorname{depth}$ in this model. The mixed $3q+r$ charge for additional bit tests is proved in the text.
