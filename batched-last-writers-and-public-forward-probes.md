# Predicting several last writers from one batch of forward probes

Write the one-based time $i$ to memory address $\pi(i)$ for $i=1,\ldots,m$, where the input supplies a permutation. Then read addresses $1,\ldots,m$ in that order. The source timestamps and the returned words are both $\pi^{-1}(y)$. The entire run takes $O(m)$ unrestricted word-RAM steps. Consider a decoder that can probe the raw forward permutation and is asked to reproduce a consecutive block of these read replies.

Partition the targets into $g=m/k$ blocks of $k$ each, with $g$ even. One $S$-bit advice string may depend on the entire permutation. Every block receives **all genuine inverse answers in preceding blocks for free**, and a fixed-width $b$-bit local message chosen after seeing the permutation. Short messages use a fixed padding convention. Earlier private probe results, if retained, must be represented in those $b$ bits or in the common advice. Each block has a public family of at most $M$ sets of forward positions, each of size at most $q$. Arbitrary computation and choice among those sets is allowed. In fact the proof grants access to their entire union.

Suppose $k\le q$ and $Mq\le m/256$, and put $d=Mq/k$. For all sufficiently large $m$, an exact deterministic decoder for every permutation requires

$$
  S=\Omega\!\left(\frac{m\log(2+d)}{d}\log m\right)
  \qquad\text{if } b\le c k\log_2 m
$$

for a sufficiently small absolute $c>0$. In particular a single public batch with $O(k)$ forward probes requires $\Omega(m\log m)$ bits of common advice. These bounds concern retrieving complete last-writer answers. A supplied inverse $i$ on this permutation promise can be checked by one forward probe to $i$.

## The counting argument with preceding answers

Only use the first $h=g/2$ blocks to select challenges. A block is adjacent to the union of all its possible probe positions. The mean number of incident blocks at a forward position is at most $hMq/m=d/2$. At least half the forward positions have degree at most $d$. Choose uniformly

$$
  \ell=\left\lfloor h\,\ln(2+d)/(64d)\right\rfloor
$$

of these $h$ blocks. Here $h\ge128d$ and $1\le\ell\le h/2$. A position of degree at most $d$ is missed with probability at least $(1-2d/h)^\ell\ge\exp(-4d\ell/h)\ge(2+d)^{-1/16}$. This follows by drawing blocks without replacement, conditional on missing the position so far. Thus some selection of blocks misses at least $m/(2(2+d)^{1/16})$ forward positions.

Let $E$ be the $s=k\ell$ target labels of the selected blocks. Among the missed forward positions reserve a set $V$ of size $v$ equal to the smaller of their number and $m/2$. Then $v\ge m/(2(2+d)^{1/16})$ and $s\le v/2$. For the latter, use $s\le m\ln(2+d)/(128d)$, $\ln(2+d)\le2\sqrt d$ and $(2+d)^{1/16}\le2d^{1/16}$ for $d\ge1$. In particular $\log(v-s)=\Omega(\log m)$ even when $d$ grows polynomially.

Fix the forward table **everywhere outside $V$**. Place all first-half labels outside $E$ there in fixed positions and fill the other outside positions with fixed later labels. This is possible because $v\le m/2$ and $s\le v$. The remaining labels consist of $E$ and $v-s$ later labels. Any injection of $E$ into $V$ extends to a permutation, using fixed ordered placement for the remaining later labels.

Given the common advice and the $\ell$ local strings for selected blocks, recover the inverse answers to all first-half labels in order. For unselected blocks their answers were fixed. For each selected block all permitted raw probe replies were fixed outside $V$, and preceding inverse answers have already been recovered. Hence one choice of these advice bits determines the injection, giving the finite inequality

$$
  S+\ell b\ \ge\ \log_2\big(v(v-1)\cdots(v-s+1)\big).
$$

Using $s=k\ell=\Theta(m\log(2+d)/d)$ proves the result. This is a worst-case theorem. It also holds if each block may return up to $\lfloor k/4\rfloor$ incorrect indices on every input: encode which answers need correction in a $k$-bit mask plus at most $\lfloor k/4\rfloor\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ value bits per selected block. Supply the corrected past to the next block. The lower logarithm per selected key is at least $(15/16)\log_2 m-O(1)$.

The bound also survives randomized preprocessing and decoding, provided every coin outcome uses forward positions in its block's stated public union. It suffices that for each permutation and block the expected fraction of incorrect coordinates is at most $\varepsilon\le1/2$, with genuine earlier answers supplied. Choose the hidden injection uniformly. Let $R$ contain all random tapes, independent of that injection, and $C$ the common advice and selected local messages. Conditional on $R,C$ and the true preceding selected blocks, one can run the actual block decoder since every other required value is fixed. For one answer coordinate with error probability $p$, its remaining conditional entropy is at most $H_2(p)+p\log_2 m$: specify whether the decoder erred and, if so, the true value. Summing over coordinates, applying concavity of binary entropy, and expanding entropy in block order gives
$$
  S+\ell b\ \ge\ \log_2((v)_s)-s\big(H_2(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\log_2 m\big).
$$
Indeed, $C$ contributes at most $S+\ell b$ bits conditional on $R$, and the genuine selected output vector determines the injection. Even $\varepsilon=1/2$ leaves a positive constant fraction of $s\log m$ on the right for large $m$. If instead an entire block is wrong with probability at most $\varepsilon$, one error flag per block suffices, replacing the loss by $\ell H_2(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon s\log_2 m$.

Gajulapalli, Golovnev and King give the public-probe bound for one inverse target per query and exhibit the adaptive separation. Their neighborhood and counting argument is the starting point here. The block statement uses joint probes for $k$ targets, local bits, and recovery in the presence of the entire preceding inverse stream. Priority of these refinements is unestablished. [Gajulapalli, Golovnev and King, Theorems 1–2](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITC.2024.5)

## A bound for single-pin SAT verdicts

Use zero-based positions and labels here. Give one common formula core a signed unit for each position $i$. It requires $x_{\pi(i)}$ if $i$ is even and $\neg x_{\pi(i)}$ if $i$ is odd. Variant $y$ appends a positive unit for $x_y$. Its SAT verdict is exactly the evenness of $\pi^{-1}(y)$. Every unit $l$ becomes four proper ternary clauses, one $(l,\pm a,\pm b)$ for each sign pair. Use different pairs of auxiliary names for the core and final pin, so no clauses repeat. To obtain dense one-space ASCII DIMACS with fixed record offsets, let $L=10^{\lceil\log_{10}(m+3)\rceil}$ and give the $m$ data variables the consecutive names $L,\ldots,L+m-1$. Take anchors $1,2$ and $1,3$. For every unused name $4\le u<L$ add the public clause $(u,1,2)$, which any consistent data assignment can satisfy by setting its filler variables true. Only the decimal digits encoding data names depend on the permutation. Record lengths, all signs and all delimiters are fixed. The encoded length is $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ bits and each packed input word depends on $O(1)$ forward entries. In this encoding four consecutive clauses constitute one data record.

Consider batched decisions on variants of this same core in the stated public-probe model. Every block receives the **full inverse indices** of all previous variants. If the expected fraction of wrong verdicts in every block of every input is at most a fixed $\varepsilon<1/2$, then
$$
  S=\Omega\big(m\log(2+d)/d\big)
  \quad\text{for } b\le c_{\varepsilon} k.
$$
A batch of $O(k)$ public forward probes therefore requires $\Theta(m)$ common bits, since all $m$ verdicts fit in $m$ bits. The same conclusion holds for public packed-word probes to the formulas by charging a constant number of parallel forward-entry probes for each. The bound applies to a preprocessing procedure receiving a common permutation and exporting a single reusable message to the separate block decoders. Unrestricted RAM decision on a single variant admits a linear scan.

To prove the lower bound, color forward positions by parity. Each color has $m/2$ positions of average incident degree at most $d$, so each has at least $m/4$ positions of degree at most $2d$. Choose $\ell=\lfloor h\ln(2+d)/(128d)\rfloor$ blocks from the first $h$. For a fixed pair consisting of a low-degree even and a low-degree odd position, at most $4d$ blocks touch either. The probability both are missed is at least $(1-8d/h)^\ell\ge (2+d)^{-1/8}$. Thus for some selection the product of the numbers of missed even and missed odd positions is at least $m^2/(16(2+d)^{1/8})$. Each count is at most $m/2$, so each is at least $m/(8(2+d)^{1/8})\ge s=k\ell$. The last inequality follows from $s\le m\ln(2+d)/(256d)$, $\ln(2+d)\le2\sqrt d$, and $(2+d)^{1/8}\le2d^{1/8}$. Also $2s\le m/2$.

Reserve exactly $s$ missed positions of each color and fix the whole table outside those $2s$ positions as before. Given any $s$-bit pattern, process the selected target labels in order, assigning each the first unused reserved position of its requested color. Fill the remaining $s$ positions with the remaining later labels. The full inverse index of each selected label is recoverable from the pattern **up through that label**, so earlier true inverse indices disclose no future pattern bits. Every unselected early label still has fixed inverse. With a uniform pattern the $s$ hidden bits are independent. Conditional on public coins, the common string and $\ell$ selected messages must therefore supply at least $s(1-H_2(\varepsilon))$ bits: given the preceding pattern and these messages, run the true decoder, and one binary error flag determines each actual verdict. The entropy bound follows by summation and concavity, including for correlated errors. Hence $S+\ell b\ge s(1-H_2(\varepsilon))$. The proof works with any prescribed balanced binary coloring of forward positions. The balanced single-target inverse predicate itself is classical. Up to relabeling the positions, it is the predicate whose randomized query complexity Aaronson lower-bounded by a linear function. [Aaronson, Section 4](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/pls.pdf) The parity verdict asks for membership in the image of the even half of the table. Golovnev, Guo, Peters and Stephens-Davidowitz previously studied image membership with preprocessing and proved search-to-decision reductions. Here the decoder can also see the complementary half of the permutation and receives true earlier inverse indices. [Golovnev et al., Sections 1.1 and 2.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/145/revision/1/download/)

With fully adaptive raw probes and unlimited retained private state, $q=O(k)=o(m)$ still requires $\Theta(m)$ common advice bits for the SAT pin verdicts at any fixed expected error below $1/2$, when $b\le c_\varepsilon k$. A uniform-permutation exposure argument gives a general $\Omega_\varepsilon(mk/q)$ tradeoff under its probe cap. [Adaptive proof and optimal-policy audit](adaptive-single-pin-sat-verdicts.md)

## Adaptive reconstruction and the SAT clock

With unrestricted sequential probes let $t=\lfloor q/(2k)\rfloor$ when $q/k\ge4$. On each permutation cycle longer than $t$, mark positions at gaps at most $t$ using at most $\lceil L/t\rceil$ marks on a cycle of length $L$. Store each marked point and its preceding marked point. From a target walk forward to its first mark, then from the preceding mark to the target, retaining its immediate predecessor. A short unmarked cycle closes within $t$ steps. This uses at most $2t$ probes per inverse, hence at most $q$ per block, and $O((mk/q)\log m)$ common bits. At smaller $q/k$ store the inverse outright. Thus for $q/k\to\infty$ and $M=o(\log(q/k))$ the lower bound for the prescribed menus exceeds this adaptive upper bound by an unbounded factor. Giving each block $k\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ local bits to name every inverse also suffices.

These writes and reads can be executed on a family of explicit negative SAT formulas with the permutation stored in literal names, as in the journal's inverse-scan program. The time after a scatter write can be arbitrarily long without affecting this counting argument. This describes the requirements of a shared decoder for a particular family of genuine RAM transfers. It gives no lower bound on unrestricted SAT decision time. A general decoder can build the inverse by a single $O(m)$-step scatter if it can keep the resulting workspace, and an arbitrary SAT solver need not perform these transfers. [Related native trace and inverse-bit bounds](long-gap-inverse-bits.md)

For comparison, verifying an *explicit* general history by tensor contraction already has a short reduction to one read-only gather. Supply one index per chronological event and gather its address, its public time label, its operation, and its data. Require the selected $(\mathrm{address},\mathrm{time})$ pairs to increase strictly. Every time occurs once, so there is a unique ordering that passes. Along a same-address run compare every load to the carried word and update it on a store, resetting the carried value to the initial value at group boundaries. Source timestamps, if required, are carried in parallel. With zero-initialized work memory this uses $O(Tw)$ additional bounded-fan-in gates and a constant-word payload gather on $T$ rows. The hidden order and carried words have exactly one completion on each legal history. A constant number of word fields can be packed into a single conventional batch by padding the row count to a power of two, giving each field its own bank with public bank bits and sharing query indices. The reverse embedding initializes a table by writes and then issues the prescribed batch reads. This equivalence is for exact projected networks; computing the hidden order by an ordinary deterministic circuit incurs an additional cost. [Single accepting lookup order](circuits-for-read-only-batched-word-lookups.md) · [Thaler, Section 6.5, pp. 93–94](https://people.cs.georgetown.edu/jthaler/ProofsArgsAndZK.pdf)

## Verification

The Lean file proves that the common advice and the selected local messages are injective on any family distinguished by its first-half answers, assuming the selected raw replies and the unselected answers are fixed. It also checks that extending the binary pattern preserves the full positions of every earlier target, and proves the proper three-CNF pin semantics for any bijection. The analytic neighborhood and entropy bounds are proved above. The Python audit constructs 2,874 finite families with 25,922 members in total and checks the first differing block on 186,462 pairs. It also runs the adaptive inverse decoder on all 5,913 permutations through size seven, for every positive gap up to the size and every target, giving 276,327 checked queries. The parity check adds 387 two-color families and 1,827 pairwise previous-index comparisons. Truth-table enumeration counts all satisfying assignments of 118 small proper three-CNF pin variants before the optional public filler clauses. A dynamic program checks the optimal no-advice success at all 440 even-size/probe pairs through size 40.
