# Signed clock failures after adding a solver that recognizes the previous witnesses

Start with any fixed satisfiable proper DIMACS formula $B$. At each of the following three budgets there are ordinary, strictly longer files extending $B$ on which every included program takes more than its specified $a(N+1)^k$ executed steps. One file is satisfiable and one is unsatisfiable. Here $N$ is the length in physical bits, $a>0$ and $k$ are fixed, and the programs below are globally correct deterministic RAM algorithms with unrestricted workspace. A new program at each of the first two transitions solves the previous example family in linear time. The transformation changes the clauses that hide the next assignment.

The budgets use a stated universal description encoding. Include ascending and descending full-assignment enumerators at all budgets. Reserve the entire text `0` for the pin recognizer and `10` for the pin-and-parity recognizer below, each with clock multiplier one. All other ordinary clocked programs, including arbitrary positive multipliers, enter as `11` followed by a nonempty raw description. All remaining strings are invalid. This gives a complete classification of the eligible descriptions of lengths zero, one and two **for this encoding**. It amounts to choosing which genuine solvers enter first. The lengths are code bits, independent of stored instruction count or worst-case executed steps. The constructions work equally as explicit portfolio extensions without the code convention. ## Programs and the first two transformations

Every named program works on ordinary CNF. On input outside its recognized preprocessing format it can use complete enumeration. The two basic programs test each assignment of the occurring names in binary order, starting at opposite ends, and stop on the first model. The recognizers group original clauses by unordered triple and represent the allowed local assignments by an eight-bit mask. The pin recognizer records any variable fixed throughout a bucket. The pin-and-parity recognizer also records any constant three-bit parity on the allowed local assignments. It solves connected components of these parity equations of up to four variables, emitting every forced value. An arbitrary larger parity component can be skipped. Both reject a certified contradiction, substitute all found pins, propagate ordinary units to a fixed point, discard satisfied clauses, and then try **all complete assignments of the remaining occurring variables**, one at a time from zero. They use no component decomposition for this final enumeration. All simplifications are sound independently of whether their recognizers found every forced value.

* **Budget zero.** On fresh names force $m$ bits of each sign. Encode each pin $l$ by $l\lor(\pm u)\lor(\pm v)$ with all four choices, on two different fresh anchors. Append an opposite pin to one variable for the negative member. Any model is at least $2^m-1$ places from either endpoint in every variable order. At budget one the pin recognizer handles all these new records in one grouping and substitution. On the negative member it detects a conflicting bucket or an empty reduced clause. The old seed has fixed size.
* **Budget one.** On four distinct names use the four parity rows obtained by omitting one coordinate at a time. Choose the right-hand sides from any prescribed model with two zeros and two ones. The row matrix over $\mathbb F_2$ is $I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^T$; its square is $I$, so the full block has just that model. Encode each row by the four proper clauses forbidding the wrong parity. Every pair of coordinates has all four settings in each row. No pin can be read off a bucket. Repeat on $m$ disjoint blocks. Append one clause falsified by the unique fresh assignment, on a previously unused cross-block support (take $m\ge2$), for the negative member. Both basic programs and the pin recognizer try at least $2^{2m}$ assignments on the positive member. On the negative member they exhaust the fresh choices. At budget two the parity recognizer recovers all the fresh coordinates, solves the positive member and detects the extra false clause on the negative one.
* **Budget two.** Use complementary copies of the five-variable, ten-clause gadget below. In each pair exactly five fresh values are one and five are zero. Put a single false clause on an unused mixed triple for the negative member. Every fresh bucket consists of one clause. Its seven allowed values span the full affine cube, so neither recognizer finds a fresh pin or an affine row. No new ordinary unit appears in the disjoint fresh part. With $m$ pairs, each program tries at least $2^{5m}$ assignments on the positive member. The negative member requires all the fresh choices. A smaller four-variable eight-clause gadget works as well when distinct clauses may reuse a support. Its individual support buckets also have full affine span. [Lean proof of the two four-variable constructions and eight-clause optimality on four names](SeedRankSuccessors.lean)

Rebuild the header, copy every old clause-body byte, and append the fresh records. Each extension preserves every old model on the positive member and eliminates all models on the negative one. The analogous files in the image of the earlier seed reduction are obtained by adding its fixed neutral triple and using its fresh namespace. For fixed $B$ each family uses $O_B(m\log(m+2))$ physical bits. The least integer with $q$ ones is $2^q-1$. Complementing an assignment gives the identical count from the other endpoint. The bounds survive any permutation of bit positions, even chosen after inspecting the file. One iteration of these particular fallback loops costs at least one executed instruction. For each fixed finite set of multipliers and each fixed power, taking $m$ sufficiently large beats every listed deadline on both signs.

The claimed fast paths admit linear implementations on the standard logarithmic-word RAM. Intern decimal names in a fixed-alphabet trie while reading their bytes. Sort the constant-arity tuples of interned names by radix/counting passes on the resulting dense integer IDs, and then aggregate each bucket. Index literal occurrences to propagate each pin once. Construct the parity-component lists in time linear in their incidence count. A four-variable component costs bounded work, and the residual search on the previous positive family uses only the fixed old portion (and at most the neutral triple). Clause ordering and injective renaming do not change this analysis. The [physical-file experiment](recognizing_seed_successors_audit.py) runs the named methods on both signs across twelve layouts of each family. It records actual fallback assignment counts and exercises a control solver operating on connected components.

## The minimum under one clause per variable triple

Each triple below is a signed DIMACS clause, with the terminating zero suppressed. The unique assignment is all zero.
```
-1  2  3       1 -2  4      -1 -2  5       1  3 -4       1  3 -5
-1  4 -5       2 -3  4       2 -3 -5      -2 -4 -5      -3 -4  5
```

Among formulas with at least one occurring variable, proper triples and at most one clause on each unordered triple, five is the smallest number of variables permitting a unique model. Ten is the minimum number of clauses over **all** numbers of occurring variables. To prove the lower bound, complement variable signs to plant zero. Each support forbids one of seven nonzero patterns. With at most four variables all the available supports together cover too few words. For five and six variables the [exact covering search](single_support_seed_census.py) rules out a cover of the other words in nine clauses. It branches on an uncovered word and tries every remaining support on which that word has nonzero projection, using that precise projection as the forbidden pattern. Variable symmetry puts a required all-ones pattern on the first triple. Bounding remaining capacity by the sum of the best individual support gains is safe even if their gains overlap.

With $t$ variables the $t$ words of weight one require $t$ separate forbidden patterns of weight one, and all ones needs an additional weight-three pattern. This rules out $t\ge9$. At eight variables the one remaining pattern misses some word with one zero. At seven variables the two remaining patterns must both be weight three to cover the seven one-zero words. Their supports must be disjoint. The nine words with one zero in each of those two triples escape both. Each of the seven weight-one patterns catches at most one of the nine, since its two zero positions must be that precise pair. This exhausts all cases. The script independently checks the witness on all 32 assignments, all coordinate permutations and all sign translations. Pairwise intersection of two different supports may be two here. Scheder studies the stricter linear-CNF condition that the intersection has size at most one. Priority for the finite minimum above is unestablished. [Scheder, *Unsatisfiable Linear CNF Formulas Are Large and Complex*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.1587)

## What happens when the next program arrives

A correct component solver decides every family in the first two transformations by exhaustive search on each connected component, with a qualification for the pin family. Its shared two anchors make its fresh component large, so first run the pin pass. On the parity and five-variable families each fresh component has bounded size, including the pair or triple joined by the negative clause. The old component is fixed. This combined solver decides all three families in $O_B$N$$, including arbitrarily permuted clauses. It defeats any induction that simply repeats one fixed block. An explicit directed cycle raises by exactly one the minimum number of original clauses needed for any affine consequence involving a fresh variable. Its positive files have a linear-time recognition algorithm. A growing-block construction handles a sequence of increasing resolution-width and component-size budgets. Arbitrary programs enter the universal encoding as well, and there is no transformation here for an arbitrary incoming text.
