# Which errors can force large exact networks for binary matrix multiplication?

Let $E=AB-C$ for three $d\times d$ binary matrices, using arithmetic over $\mathbb F_2$. Assume $0\le K\le d$. The desired output is the zero-one indicator of $E=0$. A trivalent binary-wire tensor network may use arbitrary field-valued local tables and sum over its internal wires. Its coefficient field need not be the field of the matrix product.

* On the promise $\operatorname{rank}(E)\le K$, the exact cost in a signed row-or-column query model over **any** coefficient field is $\min(d,2K)$.
* On that same rank promise the optimum with arbitrary binary vector probes in either direction is $K$ queries in characteristic different from two and $\min(d,2K)$ in characteristic two.
* If an entire binary linear combination of the rows or columns can be read in one query, then over **characteristic zero** the exact cost is $|S|$ when the allowed ranks are $\{0\}\cup S$, for any $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,d\}$. In particular a single known nonzero rank, however high, costs one signed query.
* For an actual bit-network, the first argument costs $O(Kd^2)$ for $K\ge1$. Batching improves this to $d^{2+o(1)}$ for $K\le\sqrt d$ using the November 24 rectangular multiplication upper bound. The second argument gives the same volume over characteristic zero for **any** list of at most $\sqrt d$ nonzero ranks.
* A linear-length proper ASCII 3-CNF can select the first error in both a row and a column. Its number of satisfying assignments is positive on every mismatch and at most the rank of the error, even for a dense error. [Physical projection](compact-first-error-matrix-projection.md)

A signed query representation is a finite linear combination over the coefficient field of fixed transcripts. A transcript imposes specified answers to at most $q$ allowed queries. This includes arbitrary adaptive decision trees of depth $q$ with scalar leaf weights, and arbitrary sums of such trees, by expanding into paths. There is no bound on the number of terms. This distinction matters even at rank one. A single deterministic run in general cannot certify zero from one random Freivalds test.

## Coordinate lines over every field

Call $(i,j)$ a witness when $E_{ij}=1$ is the first one in row $i$ and in column $j$, using increasing indices. The first one in the first nonzero row is such a witness. Different witnesses use distinct rows and columns. Order them by increasing row, and take their columns in the matched order. Each column is zero in earlier selected rows. The resulting lower triangular submatrix has diagonal ones, so there are at most $\operatorname{rank}(E)$ witnesses. The underlying support criterion is the triangular minor used in the literature on visible rank. [Alrabiah and Guruswami, *Visible Rank and Codes with Locality*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX/RANDOM.2021.57)

For every subset of at most $K$ positions, test whether they are all witnesses using at most two lines per position. Include the empty subset. Assign the subset sign $(-1)^{|U|}$. With $m\le K$ witnesses the sum is $(1-1)^m=[E=0]$, over every field. Querying all $d$ rows is another upper bound. The inclusion-exclusion argument works on the larger promise that the *number of witnesses in the given order* is at most $K$. For example a matrix with first row and first column all ones has just one witness, and such matrices can be invertible (subtract the first row from the others and prescribe the remaining lower-right block to be the identity).

For the lower bound take a $2\times2$ block. Assign weight $+1$ to the zero matrix. Each of the other nine binary matrices of rank one is uniquely $1_U1_V^{\mathsf T}$ for nonempty row and column subsets $U,V\subseteq\{1,2\}$. Its weight is $-a_Ua_V$, with $a_{\{1\}}=a_{\{2\}}=1$, $a_{\{1,2\}}=-1$. The total weight is zero. For a particular row $i$ and any nonzero row answer $1_V$, the weight of its fiber is $-a_V\sum_{U\ni i}a_U=0$. The zero-answer fiber then has weight zero, and the column calculation is identical. A scalar $1\times1$ block with weights $+1,-1$ has total weight zero. Its zero entry has weight one.

For $0\le K\le d$ use $s=\min(K,d-K)$ square blocks and $u=K-s$ scalar blocks down the diagonal, padding the remaining rows and columns with zeros. There are $s+u=K$ blocks, every supported matrix has rank at most $K$, and $2s+u=\min(d,2K)$. Take the product of their signed integer weights. The all-zero matrix has weight one. Each queried line constrains at most one block. Any fixed path using fewer than $2s+u$ queries either has inconsistent answers or leaves a square with at most one query or a scalar untouched. Its weight therefore factors with a zero marginal. Consequently any signed sum of such paths integrates to zero. The target indicator integrates to one. The calculation survives reduction into every field. Together the bounds give the claimed exact cost, also for the larger witness-count promise.

## Arbitrary vector queries over characteristic zero

One query chooses $x\in\mathbb F_2^d$ and obtains $Ex$ or $E^{\mathsf T}x$. On a matrix of rank $r$, the chance that $Ex=0$ for uniform $x$ is $2^{-r}$. For each $j\in S$ independently choose a uniform vector $x_j$. Then the signed expectation
$$
  \mathbb E\prod_{j\in S}\frac{[Ex_j=0]-2^{-j}}{1-2^{-j}}
   = \prod_{j\in S}\frac{2^{-r}-2^{-j}}{1-2^{-j}}
$$
is one for $r=0$ and zero for $r\in S$. This is a finite linear combination, with no error probability. The same formula works in odd characteristic when all its denominators are nonzero.

Here is a matching characteristic-zero lower bound. Functions on the additive group of binary matrices have the Walsh basis $\chi_H(E)=(-1)^{\langle H,E\rangle}$, where the exponent is the binary entrywise dot product. A prescribed answer to $Ex=y$ is a linear combination of characters indexed by $ux^{\mathsf T}$ as $u$ varies, by orthogonality on the answer bits. Left queries have the same rank-one support bound. Multiplying transcript indicators adds the indexing matrices, so $q$ adaptive queries have Fourier support on ranks at most $q$.

Average the computed function over invertible binary row and column operations. Characteristic zero permits division by the group size. The average still has the required values on the promised ranks. Its Fourier coefficients are constant on each rank orbit and vanish outside ranks $0,\ldots,q$, taking $q\le d$. This space of radial functions has dimension at most $q+1$. For each $0\le j\le q$, the function $2^{-j\operatorname{rank}(E)}$ belongs to the space by $j$ independent zero-answer tests. These $q+1$ functions are independent by evaluating at ranks $0,\ldots,q$ and using Vandermonde. Thus the average is a polynomial of degree at most $q$ in $2^{-r}$. It is one at $1$ and vanishes at $|S|$ distinct points. This proves $q\ge |S|$. Rank-polynomial symmetrization for finite-field matrix-vector oracles is established prior work. We make no priority claim for the polynomial method or the Freivalds identity. [Childs, Hung and Li, *Quantum Query Complexity with Matrix-Vector Products*](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2021.55)

For comparison, a single ordinary deterministic oracle computation on this promise has exact worst-case cost $d-\min S+1$ for nonempty $S$. On the zero transcript, $a$ independent left and $b$ independent right queries still admit matrices up to rank $\min(d-a,d-b)$, by factoring through the quotient of the right span and into the common left kernel. With at most $d-\min S$ probes a permitted nonzero matrix survives. Conversely, testing $d-\min S+1$ independent columns leaves possible rank less than $\min S$ if they all vanish. In particular, the rank-one promise takes $d$ deterministic probes. This explains why summing the signed tests has a different cost from executing a single decision path.

The characteristic matters. Over a field of characteristic two, restrict a one-sided right-query algorithm to matrices whose first row is any binary string $z$ and whose other rows vanish. Every prescribed answer now tests at most one binary linear form of $z$. A $q$-query signed representation has Boolean multilinear degree at most $q$. The zero indicator $\prod_i(1-z_i)$ has degree $d$, even though every allowed matrix has rank at most one. Hence the one-sided cost there is exactly $d$. Two-sided **coordinate** queries already yield a two-query signed representation on all rank-one errors.

## Exact vector-query complexity in each characteristic

For the full promise $0\le\operatorname{rank}(E)\le K\le d$, the exact cost of adaptive signed two-sided vector queries is
$$
   K\quad(\operatorname{char} F\ne2),\qquad
   \min(d,2K)\quad(\operatorname{char} F=2).
$$
The matrices and query vectors are binary throughout. The first answer already holds using only right queries. In the second case both directions can matter.

**Odd characteristic and characteristic zero.** Restrict $E$ to a free $K\times K$ block. The zero indicator on this unrestricted smaller block has a nonzero Walsh coefficient at every mask, in particular the identity of rank $K$. Fourier expansion only requires that 2 be invertible. Since any transcript of $q$ vector queries has Fourier support on ranks at most $q$, this gives $q\ge K$.

For the upper bound let $g(n,j)$ be the number of $j$-dimensional binary subspaces of an $n$-dimensional space, with $g(n,0)=1$. On a matrix of rank $r$, testing one fixed basis for each $j$-dimensional subspace and adding the zero-answer transcripts gives $g(d-r,j)$ using $j$ queries per term. Set $D=d-K$. The integer matrix $M_{ij}=g(D+i,j)$ for $0\le i,j\le K$ has determinant $2^{DK(K+1)/2}$. To see this, initially work over the rationals. Counting ordered independent bases gives
$$
   g(n,j)=\prod_{a=0}^{j-1}\frac{2^n-2^a}{2^j-2^a}.
$$
This is a polynomial of degree $j$ in $2^n$, also giving zero when $n<j$. Its evaluation determinant on $2^D,2^{D+1},\ldots,2^{D+K}$ is the product of the leading coefficients and the Vandermonde determinant. Compared with $D=0$, each of the $K(K+1)/2$ Vandermonde factors has been scaled by $2^D$. At $D=0$, the matrix is unit lower triangular. This proves the stated integer identity. Thus over *any* field where 2 is invertible, a linear combination of the $K+1$ subspace counts takes the values $[r=0]$ on $0\le r\le K$. This includes small odd primes for which two ordinary Freivalds probabilities can coincide.

**Characteristic two.** Give weight one to every binary matrix of rank at most $K$. Its pairing with the desired zero indicator is one. We show that each fixed transcript of fewer than $\min(d,2K)$ arbitrary vector queries has even weight. Drop inconsistent transcripts, discard linearly dependent queries on each side, and use invertible binary basis changes to put the independent left and right queries on the first $a$ rows and $b$ columns. This fixes three blocks of a matrix
$$
 \begin{pmatrix} X&Y\\ Z&H\end{pmatrix}
$$
and leaves $H$ of size $\ell\times m=(d-a)\times(d-b)$ free. Both $\ell,m$ are positive. Write $t=\operatorname{rank}X$, $p=\operatorname{rank}[X\ Y]-t$ and $s=\operatorname{rank}[X;Z]-t$, where $[X;Z]$ denotes vertical stacking.

For completeness, clear $t$ pivots in $X$ by elementary operations. This translates $H$ by a fixed matrix and leaves upper-right and lower-left strips of respective ranks $p,s$. Use row operations within each row block and column operations within each column block to put these strips into pivot form. They occupy disjoint row and column sets. Clearing their pivots discards $s$ rows and $p$ columns of the translated $H$. The total rank is $t+p+s$ plus the rank of its remaining $(\ell-s)\times(m-p)$ subblock. The discarded $\ell m-(\ell-s)(m-p)$ bits are unconstrained and each completion of the remaining subblock has that power of two as multiplicity. If $p+s>0$ this exponent is positive, giving an even count of legal completions, also when there are none.

If $p=s=0$, then $t\le\min(a,b)<K$ since $a+b<2K$. We need the parity of the number of $\ell\times m$ binary matrices of rank at most $K-t\ge1$. Rank zero contributes one. Rank one contributes $(2^\ell-1)(2^m-1)$, an odd number. For rank $h\ge2$, fix the kernel and image. The isomorphisms between the quotient by the kernel and the image are counted by $|\mathrm{GL}_h(2)|$, which is even. The total is even. This proves the annihilation for every adaptive transcript, since its queries and replies are fixed once the path is selected. The coordinate-line upper bound attains the exact value. The case $K=0$ is constant.

## Compiling the probes into a single bounded-degree network

For $K\$ bounded-rank witnesses the direct implementation guesses an increasing lexicographic list of at most $K$ positions. Use a unique zero encoding for the unused slots and weight the list by its length parity. Validate each active row and column by a multiplexer for the selected row of $A,C$ and column of $B,C$, followed by matrix-vector products over the binary field and the two first-one tests. This costs $O(Kd^2)$ Boolean gates for $K\ge1$. Gate truth tables have a unique internal evaluation on fixed guesses. Split every wire with multiple uses by explicit copy trees of total size linear in the gate and input count.

For the batched construction let $1\le K\le d$. Validate that active row indices are distinct and active column indices are distinct, which always holds for true witnesses. One can gather $K$ labeled rows of a $d\times d$ matrix in $O(d^2\log^2(2d))$ gates. Sort records for the $d$ original rows and the $K$ requests together by index, originals preceding requests at ties. Use an index check and a deterministic adjacent transfer to copy the payload from each requested original. Sort back by request slot. Inactive requests use unique out-of-range keys and zero payload. All records have $O(d+\log d)$ bits, and a fixed bitonic sorter uses $O(d\log^2(2d))$ comparisons. The procedure for columns uses the transposed input wiring. With repeated active indices one may reject immediately after sorting. Use the resulting matrices to compute the $K\times d$ selected rows of $AB-C$ and the $d\times K$ selected columns. Check all first-one conditions and the sorted-list convention in $O(dK\log(2d))$ additional gates, for example by comparing each constant line position with its selected binary index. Each subset has exactly one encoding and contributes its sign. This construction permits signed cancellation in the contraction.

In the characteristic-zero rank-list construction, guess a $d\times |S|$ binary matrix $X$. Compute $BX,A(BX),CX$ in three rectangular products, then test each residual column for zero and multiply the indicated scalar weights. One scalar normalization $2^{-d|S|}$ accounts for uniform guesses. A binary XOR is always implemented by its Boolean truth table, regardless of the network's coefficient field. The normalization factors are legal entries in the local tables.

Write $K\le d^t$, $0<t\le1$, using $|S|$ for $K$ in the rank-list construction. Both volume exponents are at most $\omega_{\mathbb F_2}(1,1,t)$, with $d^{o(1)}$ slack. An arithmetic circuit over the binary field evaluates by Boolean AND and XOR, and copy trees cover fan-out. The earlier rectangular exponent bounds already yield volume $d^{2+o(1)}$ for $t=0.32$. The November 24 rectangular theorem gives $2+(\max(0,2t-1))^2/(4t)$, hence includes $t=1/2$. This endpoint uses the separate rectangular upper bound, not the square lower bound. [Vassilevska Williams, Xu, Xu and Zhou](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07970) · [November 24 rectangular theorem and formalization scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/lean/docs/107.md)

For example, to obtain a $d^{2+\delta-o(1)}$ rational-network lower bound on any input subfamily, for fixed $0<\delta\le1/4$, the number of different positive residual ranks on that subfamily must be at least $d^{t_\delta-o(1)}$ along the lower-bound sequence, where
$$
   t_\delta=\tfrac12(1+\delta+\sqrt{\delta(2+\delta)}).
$$
This follows by inverting the increasing branch of the rectangular upper exponent. Over every coefficient field the same necessary bound applies to the *maximum number of first-in-row-and-column witnesses* on that family. Even meeting both numerical conditions can be easy. On the promise that $E=0$ or its first row is nonzero, every positive rank occurs, all invertible matrices are admitted, and $I_d$ has $d$ witnesses. Computing that one row from the physical factors takes $O(d^2)$ Boolean gates. A large set of ranks or many witnesses is therefore only a necessary condition for the proposed lower-bound strategy. A lower bound on unrestricted SAT running time remains open.
