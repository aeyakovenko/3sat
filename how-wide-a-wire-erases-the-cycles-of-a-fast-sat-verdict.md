# How wide a wire erases the cycles of a fast SAT verdict

There are explicit proper DIMACS families where the SAT verdict on $k$ records of $t=\Theta(\log k)$ editable bits needs $(1-o(1))kt$ cycles with binary wires. If each internal wire may carry $a$ bits, all cycles can be eliminated precisely at a threshold $a_*$ satisfying
$$
                              t/3-5\ \le\ a_*\ \le\ t/3 .
$$
A single globally correct deterministic RAM can decide every file in this family in a packed scan, including generating its lookup tables. All three claims apply to the **same** family. The parameter $a_*$ minimizes width over arbitrary ternary local tables and arbitrary trees. A table on three word ports can be very large; the fast RAM algorithm is proved separately.

## A code with a wide cut in every tree

Take $t=6s$, $r=3s$, and an $r\times t$ binary matrix $H$. Its code is $C=\{z:Hz=0\}$. On a bipartition $A,B$ of the external coordinates the Boolean rectangle cover number of its acceptance matrix is exactly $2^{\lambda(A)}$, where
$$
          \lambda(A)=\operatorname{rank}H_A+\operatorname{rank}H_B-\operatorname{rank}H .
$$
The two partial syndromes must coincide. Every syndrome in the intersection of the two column spans gives one rectangle; rows and columns indexed by different such syndromes cannot share an accepting rectangle. Cutting a single $a$-bit wire in any representation on a tree gives at most $2^a$ rectangles by fixing its value. This lower bound permits arbitrary nonlinear tables and existential assignments.

On a fixed tree the same bound on its worst edge suffices for the internal widths. On an edge display the common partial syndrome in a basis of the intersection of its two column spans. At a ternary internal vertex require that the three decoded syndromes sum to zero. Match a leaf to the contribution of its coordinate. Induction from the leaves recovers the intended partial syndromes, so this realizes exactly $C$. Trivial singleton edges may be suppressed or realized with unary leaf constraints. This is the usual code realization on a tree; depending on convention, matroid branch-width adds one to the displayed cut function. [Forney, normal realizations](https://doi.org/10.1109/18.910573) · [Kashyap, minimal tree realizations](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.1383) · [Jeong, Kim and Oum, branch-decompositions](https://doi.org/10.1137/19M1285895)

Every subcubic tree with $6s$ marked leaves has an edge separating at least $2s$ leaves on each side. After pruning unmarked branches, take a centroid by terminal weight: move toward a component containing more than half until none does. One of at most three components there contains at least a third and at most a half. Splitting or suppressing a degree-two vertex preserves the available cut. A realizing forest may be joined by ignored wires before applying the argument, leaving its width unchanged for $a\ge1$.

For a uniform random binary $H$ and a fixed cut, let the smaller side have size $x=2s+j$, $0\le j\le s$. Put $u=x-\operatorname{rank}H_A$ and $v=r-\operatorname{rank}H_B$. If $\lambda(A)<2s-5$ then $u+v\ge j+6$, because $\operatorname{rank}H\le r$. A uniform rectangular matrix with dimension gap $g$ has probability at most $4\cdot2^{-L(g+L)}$ of rank deficit at least $L$ from the smaller dimension. Indeed such a deficit requires an $L$-dimensional subspace of the kernel on the smaller side (transpose if necessary). The Gaussian binomial coefficient counting the subspaces is at most $4\cdot2^{L(m-L)}$ for a smaller dimension $m$, by the product formula, and each one has probability $2^{-L(m+g)}$. The constant four suffices since $\prod_{i\ge1}(1-2^{-i})>1/4$; for example use the first three factors and the sum bound for the tail.

Both submatrices have gap $s-j$ and use independent columns. Put $D=j+6$. For the bad cut there are nonnegative $p,q$ with $p+q=D$, $u\ge p$ and $v\ge q$. The rank bounds multiply for each such pair. Summing gives probability at most
$$
          16(D+1)2^{-D(s-j)-D^2/2},
$$
using $p^2+q^2\ge D^2/2$. The exponent in the denominator is $6s+18+j(s-j/2)$. For $1\le j\le s$ it is at least $7s+17$, enough to sum over all $2^{6s}$ cuts with a vanishing bound. At $j=0$ the number of cuts is only $\binom{6s}{2s}\le 2^{6s H_2(1/3)}$, with binary entropy $H_2(1/3)<1$, so that contribution vanishes too. The union bound stays below one even including all events that a column is zero or that two columns agree, for sufficiently large $s$. The extra events have total probability at most $(t+\binom t2)2^{-r}$. Thus there is an $H$ with binary code distance at least three and $\lambda(A)\ge t/3-5$ on every balanced cut. The rank-width of dense random graphs is known to be within an additive constant of a third of their order. The argument here is a random-matrix specialization to the code realization. Random matrices as column matroids and the relation between matroid connectivity and bipartite graph cut rank have also been studied. Priority for the matrix estimate is unestablished. [Kelly and Oxley](https://doi.org/10.1002/sapm1984713181) · [Oum, Proposition 3.1](https://mathsci.kaist.ac.kr/~sangil/pdf/vertexminor.pdf) · [Lee, Lee and Oum](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.0461)

## Making the construction fit the RAM clock

Use the method of conditional expectations for the sum of the indicators of the joint events $u\ge p,v\ge q$ for all the cuts and pairs just used, together with the bad-column indicators. Its initial expectation is less than one. Set entries of $H$ one bit at a time, column by column, choosing the value that minimizes the conditional expectation. For either side of a cut let $S$ be the span of its fully set columns. If the currently exposed column is on this side, its remaining choices form an affine coordinate slice $V$. Gaussian elimination gives the exact probability $|V\cap S|/|V|$ that the rank stays fixed, with the empty intersection counted as zero. Otherwise it rises by one. All remaining columns on that side are uniform. Their exact final rank distribution follows by the same two-outcome recurrence: with ambient dimension $r$ and current rank $j$, one uniform column leaves the rank fixed with probability $2^{j-r}$. The two sides depend on disjoint unrevealed columns, so their conditional tail probabilities multiply. A forbidden column equality or a zero column is also a set of linear equations on still-unset bits and has an exactly computable conditional probability. All denominators divide a power of two of exponent $O(t^2)$, so the sums and comparisons take $\operatorname{poly}(t)$ bit operations per cut and candidate bit value. Enumerating the cuts at each bit costs $2^t\operatorname{poly}(t)$ overall. At the end the integer event count is zero. Finite small widths can use any fixed code since the claim is asymptotic.

For the physical file use the two-guard, octal-digit-in-a-decimal-name construction. Choose $t=6\max(1,\lfloor\log_2(k+1)/96\rfloor)$. Use the code $\ker H$ for the first record and the high-rate greedy distance code for the other $k-1$ records. Two flag tables with disjoint fixed leading decimal digits suffice. Building them, verifying the guarded prefix, generating $H$, and computing all suffix-spelling lookups take $o(k)$ time. The file has $N=8k(t/3+O(1))+o(k)$ bits. Each actual record then costs $O(1)$ word operations on a logarithmic-word RAM. On other inputs the checked recognizer falls back to a general solver. Flipping any editable data bit of an accepted file rejects by minimum distance, giving the matching scan lower bound on the positive file and many adjacent negative files. [Physical reduction, greedy code and word-probe proof](hamming-cycles-in-the-physical-sat-verdict.md)

For the binary-wire bound, pin the first record to the zero word. The remaining data realize the product of $k-1$ high-rate greedy codes, requiring $(1-o(1))kt$ cycles by the colored cut proof. For the word-width bound, pin all those other records to zero, which the greedy code includes. A tree for the full decision relation then gives a tree at the same width for $\ker H$. The balanced-cut bound gives $a_*\ge t/3-5$. Conversely partition each record into three groups of exactly $t/3$ coordinates. Pack each group into one $t/3$-bit edge by a binary tree. A single arbitrary ternary table on the three packed groups recognizes that record's code, and the disjoint union recognizes the product by existential feasibility in every component. This proves $a_*\le t/3$. If a connected graph is desired, join the components with ignored ports by subdividing an edge in each. This choice keeps the tables ternary and the graph acyclic.

Thus on files taking precisely a packed scan it can take an internal alphabet of $2^{t/3-O(1)}$ states before a tree becomes possible. The restricted verdict remains easy for the unrestricted RAM even below that representation threshold, by the explicit native algorithm. The rank bounds and polynomial-time arithmetic of the conditional expectation are manuscript proofs. [Exact small-width search and cut checks](tree_word_width_audit.py)

## A physical verdict whose required tree state exceeds a native word

A second family gives a larger absolute separation. Its exact SAT verdict on independently editable physical data bits needs an edge carrying $\Omega(N/\log N)$ bits in every ternary tree. Even a cyclic network with native-word ports needs $\Omega(N/\log^2 N)$ independent cycles. There is a globally correct RAM program that decides these files in $O(N/\log N)$ steps including recognition. This concerns arbitrary word-valued constraint tables and arbitrary graph topologies.

Use an asymptotically good binary code $E$ with message length $m$, block length $L=\Theta(m)$, distance $\Omega(m)$ and a bounded-fanin encoding circuit with $O(m)$ gates. Spielman constructs codes with these properties. Consider the graph of the encoder as a code on $m+L$ coordinates, with words $(x,E(x))$. It has $2^m$ words and minimum distance $d=\Omega(m)$. [Spielman, *Linear-time encodable and decodable error-correcting codes*](https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/spielman/PAPERS/linearTimeIT.pdf)

For an exact representation of this relation with cycle rank $b$ and at most $a$ bits per internal edge, discard the $b$ nonforest edges. Make at most $\lfloor(m+L)/\lceil d/2\rceil\rfloor=O(1)$ further cuts using the one-color case of the tree partition above, so every piece has fewer than $d$ real terminals. Fixing all cut states leaves a Cartesian product containing at most one codeword. Therefore
$$
                                 a(b+O(1))\ \ge\ m .
$$
Lean checks the general tree partition for every $d\ge2$, including the exact terminal accounting at each detached component. Passing from the graph to this tree and interpreting fixed edge states as independent factors are manuscript arguments. [Core Lean tree partition](GluedCodeCut.lean)

This also applies to a forest, for which $b=0$. The asymptotic lower bounds follow once the relation is put into $N=\Theta(m\log(m+2))$ physical bits.

Use a proper 3-CNF with one data pin each for the message and the proposed output, one fresh bit for each gate, local gate truth tables in topological order, and equalities comparing the final outputs to the proposed ones. Three fixed false guards pad short clauses. Represent each data bit by the pair of complementary names ending in the digits 2 and 3, with a padded unit selecting the true name. Exactly one physical bit per coordinate changes; it occurs only in that unit. All other bytes can be fixed. The resulting SAT verdict on those bits is exactly the stated code.

One uniform program recognizes and handles *arbitrary* supplied feed-forward bounded-fanin circuits in this layout. The preamble can include the gate types, parent indices and outputs in comments. Verify the entire body against the descriptor and the pin format, range-checking backward indices, then evaluate each gate once on the pinned message and compare the outputs. Outside that format use a general SAT algorithm. The descriptor and body have $O(m)$ fixed-width records. There are $\Theta(m)$ distinct names and fields use $\Theta(\log m)$ bits. A constant number of word loads per field suffices. For example precompute a decimal parser for chunks of at most $\lfloor(\log_2 m)/16\rfloor$ bytes; all its spellings, including invalid chunks, occupy $O(\sqrt m)$ entries and can be decoded in $o(m)$ setup time. A fixed-width field takes a constant number of chunks. The program checks actual clauses before trusting any circuit description. It need not reconstruct a particular encoder or certify its distance. On the prescribed files all checks together cost $O(m)$. The pin fields are spaced by $\Theta(\log m)$ bits. On every positive file the distance condition makes all $m+L$ pins pivotal, so its time lower bound is $\Omega(m)$ and the last-probed pin gives an equally costly negative neighbor. Thus the worst-case time is a packed scan on each verdict. A physical test substitutes the seven-bit Hamming encoder and checks every one of the 2,048 combinations of a pinned four-bit message and seven-bit output. Exactly 16 have a satisfying extension. [Hamming circuit and DIMACS audit](hamming_cycle_sat_audit.py)

An alternative positive predicate is the binary cycle space of a bounded-degree expander with the balanced cut ranks proved earlier in the journal. Pin the edge bits and impose the four proper even-parity clauses at each cubic vertex. Here a balanced edge of a spanning tree together with at most $b$ cut nonforest edges separates the terminals. The rank lower bound implies $a(b+1)=\Omega(m)$ directly. The orientation of the SAT predicate matters since a witness to a violated parity would accept the complementary relation. [Balanced cycle-space cuts](balanced-cuts-of-one-sat-run.md)

The wide-edge example and the dense physical binary-cycle example can also occur on the same files. Adjoin $k=\Theta(m)$ of the high-rate greedy records on disjoint variable names. Use the physical lookup component unaltered, and assign the gate component a disjoint numerical name interval above it. The combined length is $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ and the independently editable inputs number $h=\Theta(N)$. All but $O(m)$ of these edit positions belong to the greedy records, so pinning the gate data to a valid word forces $(1-o(1))h$ binary-wire cycles in the remaining predicate. Pinning the greedy records to zero retains $a(b+O(1))=\Omega(N/\log N)$. A single recognizer checks the combined header and both bodies before using the two packed evaluations. Hence the word-width and binary-cycle lower bounds can be simultaneously attained by a family with the same scan-time upper bound.

**Finite width checks.** The Hamming $[7,4,3]$ code has tree width two bits. Ten is the smallest number of terminals on which a binary linear code can require three bits per internal edge. To check the lower threshold, matroid duality reduces nine columns to rank at most four and preserves $\lambda$. Remove zero columns and merge parallel copies. Extend the remaining simple set to nine distinct nonzero spanning columns of $\mathbb F_2^4$ and choose a basis as the four unit columns. All $\binom{11}{5}=462$ choices for the rest have a width-two decomposition. Deleting columns can only decrease span-intersection dimensions in the restricted tree. Restore deleted zero columns freely and parallel copies as siblings with a one-bit local constraint. The reduced incidence matrix of $K_5$ attains three bits at ten terminals. The audit evaluates the exact subset recurrence and the matching syndrome realizations, and compares the recurrence with literal tree enumeration on the smaller examples. No priority claim is made for these matroid calculations. [Reproducible width audit](tree_word_width_audit.py)
