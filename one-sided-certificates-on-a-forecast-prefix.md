# One-sided certificates and simultaneous program exhaustion on a forecast prefix

Let $V(W)$ be the [single-delay forecast](one-variable-delay-and-one-binary-product.md) and $n=|\operatorname{bin}W|$. In its two diagonal modes the designated home verdict track begins at zero. It stays zero throughout initialization, simulation, timeout and any return trip to the home cell. The sole write to this track latches the complement of a returned guest verdict. The fixed diagonal header selects the program and word-width convention; the rest of $\operatorname{bin}W$ can vary. A wide guest with words of $O(n)$ bits completing at most a sufficiently small constant times $W/n$ instructions fits in the interval and latches before observation. For a fixed native guest with words of $O(\log n)$ bits the corresponding budget is $cW^4/n^b$, with program-dependent $b,c>0$. These are the two different simulation bounds proved in the linked note.

## A real SAT execution or a missed negative

Fix a uniform, possibly partial compiler $F$ of the width into ordinary explicit SAT. The only semantic promise is that whenever it produces a satisfiable formula, $V(W)=0$. It may miss negatives, return unsatisfiable formulas or run indefinitely. Take **any** correct deterministic SAT program $S$. A wide guest runs $F$ and then $S$ on its emitted input. On a satisfiable answer it returns zero. Otherwise it continues forever. The compiler and solver must admit sequential simulation with constant instruction overhead in a sufficiently large $O(n)$-bit word convention on the runs in question, including writing the formula and setting up the solver's input.

Every width on this guest's own header has $V(W)=0$: if a verdict had been latched by observation, it would have been one, while the certified satisfiable answer implies zero. Hence there was no write by the deadline. On every sufficiently large width of this prefix, either $F$ misses this true negative or the actual combined work to its satisfiable answer is at least $c_{F,S}W/n$. In particular, on any such widths where $F$ produces a satisfiable formula $D$ in $o(W/n)$ work, **the actual run of $S$ on $D$ takes $\Omega_{F,S}(W/n)$ instructions**. This conclusion assumes no running-time guarantee for $S$ and imposes no space bound on its actual SAT run. Soundness only on the guest's own header would suffice for that guest. Global soundness makes the promise independent of the choice of $S$. [Lean latch and deadline lemmas](SilentLatchForecast.lean)

For $k=1,2,3$, a compiler meeting both the $o(W/n)$ construction bound and $|D|=o((W/n)^{1/k})$ on infinitely many satisfiable outputs of **each** candidate solver's own prefix would therefore exclude $O(|D|^k)$ for SAT. For one solver with a stated clock constant, a single hit late enough for both inequalities gives the contradiction. Arbitrary missed widths, and every forecast-positive width, may be left untreated. A sufficient hypothesis that avoids knowing the header is that along an infinite sequence of lengths, the fraction of all $n$-bit widths which are negatives and miss these cheap, short, satisfiable outputs tends to zero, using uniform little-oh bounds. Every fixed header occupies a positive fraction at every sufficiently large length; all its inputs here are negative, so the hits must enter it. Positive coverage concentrated elsewhere does not imply this intersection. No compiler with the required hits has been constructed.

## What arbitrary proofs can certify without a SAT-time assumption

Let $R(W,p)$ be any fixed deterministic native verifier such that accepting a proof $p$ implies $V(W)=0$. It may be partial. Use logarithmic words in the encoded argument length and standard effective arithmetic. There are constants $b,c>0$ and a fixed header depending on $R$ such that every sufficiently large width on that header is a genuine negative, and **every** accepting proof there has
$$
                  2^{|p|}(1+t_R(W,p))\ \ge\ cW^4/n^b .
$$
Here $t_R$ counts the verifying execution, with an explicit binary proof representation and standard self-delimiting framing. For a polynomial-time verifier this rules out proofs shorter than $4\log_2 W-O_R(\log n)$ at every width on that prefix. If $R$ is complete on negatives, each of these widths does have an accepting proof.

The narrow guest performs the following finite search and otherwise keeps running. At stage $j\le4n+2$, for each $h\le j$ it enumerates all $h$-bit proofs and simulates the verifier for up to $2^{j-h}$ steps on each, returning zero at first success. Total verifier steps at that stage are at most $(j+1)2^j$. Widths for arguments with $h=O(n)$ are $O(\log n)$; the guest can simulate even a long verifying execution using a polynomial-size address space. Proof generation, clearing the verifier's memory, input framing and the $O(n)$-bit counters cost at most polynomial factors in $n$. An accepting proof with $j=|p|+\lceil\log_2(1+t_R)\rceil$ within range would thus be discovered in $2^{|p|}(1+t_R)n^{O_R(1)}$ narrow instructions, or earlier if another proof succeeds. The narrow diagonal budget and the untouched zero track give the bound. Beyond the cutoff the product already exceeds $W^4$ up to a constant. For the length consequence it suffices to consider $|p|\le4n$; larger proofs already meet it. The polynomial factors include the tape simulation of this particular narrow search. Lean checks the implication from timely discovery and soundness to the exclusion of a cheap certificate; the search clock is a manuscript argument. ## Every bounded portfolio on a single actual tally input

Define a total language on binary strings. Only all-zero strings of length $M=2^j$ can be accepted. Form the width $W_j$ by writing $\operatorname{bin}(j)$ and appending zero bits to total length $n=4j$, and take the answer to be $V(W_j)$. Fix an arbitrary convention for finitely many small $j$. Then $M^4/2\le W_j<M^4$. A native deterministic RAM can check the syntax and decide this language in $O(M^{20})$ time: simulate the physical recurrence with a circular buffer of $O(W_j)$ bits and a counter through its $O(W_j^5)$ ticks. A sufficiently large fixed logarithmic word coefficient covers both buffer addresses and the counter. At each length a nonuniform Boolean circuit of at most $M$ fan-in-two gates suffices, taking an OR of all input bits and inverting it if that length is accepting, or outputting the constant zero. One advice bit per length also suffices.

Fix a finite program size budget $C$ in a fixed standard native ISA. There is a computably specified wide guest header $H_C$ such that, for every sufficiently large $j$ beginning with that header in binary, **one actual negative input** $0^{2^j}$ takes $\Omega_C(M^4/\log M)$ time on every correct program of at most $C$ description bits. The header explicitly specifies a set of hard lengths. The eventual threshold need not be effective. For $C$ large enough the class contains the circular-buffer decider above.

Here is the guest. For all programs of size at most $C$, dovetail computations on canonical all-zero strings against the exact decider, marking a program as soon as it returns a wrong verdict on any such string. This is an ordinary infinite search. On the current input run only the first $n$ transitions of a fixed tape implementation of that search from its initial state. This prefix test costs $\operatorname{poly}_C(n)$ resource. Every candidate that ever returns a wrong answer on a canonical string has a finite refutation, and there are finitely many candidates. Thus on every sufficiently long current width all surviving programs give correct answers whenever they return on these strings. Programs that sometimes diverge are harmless.

Check that the current width really has the stated form $W_j$, and race the survivors on $0^M$, returning the first verdict. The complete header $H_C$ occurs as a prefix of $\operatorname{bin}(j)$ for infinitely many $j$ and hence as a prefix of each corresponding $W_j$. On these sufficiently late inputs an observed complemented verdict would contradict correctness of the first return, so the untouched track gives $V(W_j)=0$. If any correct candidate halted in fewer than a sufficiently small constant times $W_j/n$ steps, the race would have latched its answer. Indeed, $O_C(M)$ work prepares all inputs; each round of the finite race costs $O_C(1)$ wide guest steps. Separate guest work banks can be represented with disjoint address prefixes in $O_C(n)$-bit words. Only touched words have to fit in the interval simulation. The small-constant wide budget therefore suffices. Off the canonical inputs this guest may diverge. Alternatively, for existence alone one can hardwire the finite set of correct candidates and omit the initial filtering.

There is an algebraic length successor. Starting at an old hard index $j$, take any sufficiently large multiple $j'=ej$ whose binary expansion begins with $H_{C+1}$. Such multiples exist at every sufficiently large chosen bit length, since the interval selected by the prefix eventually contains $j$ consecutive integers. Then $M'=M^e$. The new string extends the old all-zero string, preserves its negative verdict and obeys the time bound anew against the larger portfolio. The exponent $e$ and the threshold need not be bounded in terms of $C$ effectively by this proof. The execution on the shorter string need not occur inside the new execution.

This gives simultaneous exhaustion, unrestricted workspace and a preserved clock lower bound for a concrete language in deterministic polynomial time. Its threshold transfers to ordinary SAT only with a sufficiently efficient uniform reduction or the one-sided hits above. A single hardwired verdict per length already removes the difficulty for nonuniform tally circuits.
