# Printing the small binary convolution circuits used by a native address compiler

For each fixed $\alpha>1/2$ the positive-characteristic construction in the November finite-Fourier preprint gives a **deterministic circuit printer running in $n^{O_\alpha(1)}$ bit operations** for its $O_\alpha(n(1+\log n)^\alpha)$-gate formal polynomial convolution over the fixed binary field. This printing estimate is weaker than a near-linear uniform algorithm for the whole product. It suffices to construct logarithmic-width cyclic-shift kernels with polynomial work in the full address width. The gate-size theorem and the algorithms analyzed below are imported from the preprint. ## Constructing the extension once for each length

Put $T=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, $B=C T$ with the preprint's fixed sufficiently large $C$. Its prime list can be constructed by ordinary sieving and trial division through $B$: retain eligible odd primes $p$ for which $p-1$ has a factor in the prescribed prime interval. The paper's prime-distribution estimate guarantees the requisite product and least-common-multiple estimates. Include the largest power of three at most $8B$. In characteristic two the needed common root order $D$ divides the product of that power with the distinct odd primes through $B$. Thus $D=n^{O(1)}$. For the latter elementary bound, the primes in $(u,2u]$ divide $\binom{2u}u\le4^u$; multiply the estimates for dyadic intervals. Every factor of $D$ is known.

Construct $X^D-1$ over $\mathbb F_2$. It is squarefree since $D$ is odd. Factor it deterministically in time polynomial in $D$ using the elementary Berlekamp step. For a squarefree factor $g$ the kernel of the binary linear map $z\mapsto z^2-z$ in $\mathbb F_2[X]/g$ has dimension the number of distinct irreducible factors, by the Chinese remainder theorem and the fact that $z^2=z$ in a field forces $z=0,1$. Write this linear map on the monomial basis and solve it by binary Gaussian elimination. If there is a vector other than zero and one, split $g$ using $\gcd(g,z)$ and $\gcd(g,z+1)$; both are proper factors. Otherwise $g$ is irreducible. At most $D$ splits occur, each using polynomially many bit operations. Choose an irreducible factor $g$ for which $X^{D/p}\ne1\bmod g$ for each prime divisor $p$ of $D$. Such a factor exists because the algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_2$ has primitive odd-order roots. Exponentiation and testing all factors has polynomial cost. Then $E=\mathbb F_2[X]/g$, $\xi=X\bmod g$ realizes the required extension and root, with the degree $e=\deg g$ bounded as in the source. Multiplication, inversion of units, and all required powers in this explicitly represented field take polynomial time in $D$. In particular the construction never enumerates all $2^e$ field elements. [Berlekamp's factorization method](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1967.tb03174.x)

## Building a gate list of the asserted size

Use the source's packing in $E[z]/(z^r)[X]/(X^N-1)$ and its positive-characteristic CRT grid. The elementary integer and polynomial arithmetic for the CRT maps, the packing indices, the root-evaluation matrices and the reciprocals of monic polynomials has polynomial bit complexity in $n$. Each axis has side at most $B$ and its auxiliary prime-radix length is less than $6B$. The Newton and Toeplitz words use arithmetic on arrays of polynomial size and denominators stated to be nonzero. Synchronization groups at most a fixed number of neighboring axes, sorts each fiber by its public ordinals and borrows scratch coordinates from a set of a constant number of fibers. The resulting global index and scalars for every proposed gate can be obtained by direct iteration in polynomial time. All full grids and synchronized-coordinate passes have polynomial cardinality in $n$. These claims use the positive-characteristic part only; the characteristic-zero deformation argument is unnecessary.

The only abstract finite word needed for binary shear powers acts on four coordinates in a fixed finite extension of the prime field and is independent of the input length. For fixed $\alpha$, hardwire that word or search finite extensions and words once. The primary shear lemma guarantees termination. Tensor powers are printed by the sector algorithm for the effective ternary radix transform. This same fixed shear word works for every jet's base-field coordinates. [Packed implementation of the shear sector algorithm](half-the-address-bits-and-the-boolean-zeta-transform.md)

The circuit-size analysis expresses the nonrecursive stage using genuinely shorter ordinary binary convolutions: every child length is at most $6er<n$ outside a fixed finite base range. Define canonical multiplication circuits for **all** integer lengths up to the requested $n$ in increasing order. Use direct gates in the finite base range. At the next length, instantiate the needed child DAGs; when one input is fixed, propagate constants and retain the linear part as in the source. These transformations take polynomial time in the known DAG length even with textbook arithmetic on all public coefficient representations. Size induction is the preprint's same inequality since each child already has its proved upper bound, so no best-circuit search or oracle for the numerical optimum is needed. At most $n$ canonical tables of polynomial length are constructed. Their finite template and scheduling overheads are polynomial in $n$ for fixed $\alpha$. This proves the claimed polynomial printer; it need not achieve the field-operation clock of the compiled convolution.

## Consequences for the actual named file

For $v=2^k$ use the blockwise construction for $X_{j+s\bmod v}$, including the shorter final block, and choose $r=\max(1,\lfloor\log_2(2+k)\rfloor)$. Generate each needed small shifter by a one-hot decoder, a length-$2^r$ binary polynomial convolution and a cyclic fold. The construction above takes $2^{O_\alpha(r)}=\operatorname{poly}_\alpha(k)$ time. Across all $O(1+k/r)$ stages the gates, including carry controls, cost
\[
         O_\alpha\!\left(\frac{v(1+k)}{(1+\log(2+k))^{1-\alpha}}\right)
         \qquad (1/2<\alpha<1).
\]
This improves the denominator obtained by exhausting every possible small circuit. The ordinary named length multiplies the gate count by $O(k+1)$. Tagged microgate slabs, fixed-width decimal literal digits, and alignment give polynomially many public intervals, degree $O_\alpha(1+\log(2+k))$ on each interval, and polynomial-time lookup of any physical bit. A native RAM emits the actual words within the gate bound plus polynomial setup in $k$. [Carry, projection and normal-entry layout](locally-encoding-an-unknown-xor-shift.md)

If $\operatorname{poly}(k)$ lookup is relaxed, for large $k$ take $r=\lfloor k/\log_2 k\rfloor$. Precompute the entire local kernel in $2^{O_\alpha(r)}=v^{o(1)}$ bit operations. Pack its microgate ordinal into its own $O(r+\log r)$ varying field on each stage slab. Arbitrary operand and coefficient functions of this field have degree $O(r+\log r)$; public unchanged fiber indices are affine. Thus polynomially many physical intervals have degree $O(r+\log k)=o(k)$. For any fixed $\epsilon>0$ with $1/2+\epsilon<1$ choose $1/2<\alpha<1/2+\epsilon$. Then the gate count $O_\alpha(v k^\alpha(\log k)^{1-\alpha})$ is $O_\epsilon(v k^{1/2+\epsilon})$, and the named length is $O_\epsilon(v k^{3/2+\epsilon})$. Individual physical bit access now costs $v^{o(1)}$, and single precomputation plus uniform packed emission costs $O_\epsilon(v k^{1/2+\epsilon})$ overall.
