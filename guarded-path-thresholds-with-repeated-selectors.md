# Exact threshold certificates for guarded implication paths

Take internally vertex-disjoint directed paths from $s$ to $t$, each of length one, two or three. A path of length one may be a parallel edge. Each edge $u\to v$ has a weight and one selector literal $g$. At threshold $\tau$ it imposes $u\to v$ if it is available, its weight is at most $\tau$, and $g$ is true. Pin $s$ true and $t$ false. Selectors and path vertices are separate Boolean variables. This is ordinary width-three SAT. An available edge at or below threshold has clause $\neg g\vee\neg u\vee v$.

The terminal pins extend to the path vertices exactly when no fully activated path goes from $s$ to $t$. Assigning true precisely to reachable vertices proves sufficiency. For each path $P$, put $w_P=\max_{e\in P} w_e$ and let $C_P$ be the disjunction of the **negated** guards of its edges. The problem on selectors is the CNF of those $C_P$ for which every edge is available and $w_P\le\tau$. Deleting any edge deactivates its entire path in this CNF. In the original implication system the surviving fragments may still constrain internal vertices, which are existentially quantified here.

## Projecting all selectors that occur at most twice

Choose a set $H$ of selectors to retain, with $|H|=r$. Every other selector occurs at most twice, counted across the entire graph before thresholding or deletions. There are no occurrence restrictions on $H$. Build a fixed undirected multigraph whose vertices are the paths. For a selector outside $H$ occurring in $C_P$ and $C_Q$ with opposite signs, add an edge between $P$ and $Q$, including a loop if $P=Q$. For each remaining selector outside $H$ that occurs, mark all vertices where it occurs. Such a pure selector admits one setting satisfying all its occurrences. A multigraph tree has neither a loop nor a parallel-edge cycle.

For every **unmarked tree component** $K$, form $D_K$ by taking the disjunction of all $H$-literals in its path clauses, possibly the empty disjunction. Give it weight $W_K=\max_{P\in K}w_P$. With all edges available, the weighted CNF of the $D_K$ is the exact existential projection onto $H$ at every threshold. More generally, for arbitrary edge deletions and threshold $\tau$, include $D_K$ precisely when **every original edge of every path in $K$ survives** and $W_K\le\tau$. In particular, any further constraint solely on $H$ can be conjoined before or after the projection.

**Proof.** Fix the retained bits. An opposite-sign selector can satisfy either endpoint of its edge by orienting the edge toward that endpoint. A loop always satisfies its one vertex. The assignments of these selectors are independent. Each active path clause needs one incoming edge unless a retained literal or a pure selector already satisfies it. In a connected component containing a cycle, orient a cycle cyclically and a spanning forest outward from it. This supplies every vertex. In a tree with a marked, inactive or already satisfied vertex, root the tree there and orient its edges outward. Every other vertex then has an incoming edge. In a tree with every vertex demanding an incoming edge, $|K|-1$ edges cannot cover $|K|$ vertices. This last event is exactly failure of the retained clause $D_K$ under the stated availability and weight conditions. The argument includes isolated vertices and repeated literals.

There is a linear-time construction in the number of edges and paths on a word RAM with dense selector names. Use occurrence lists, one component traversal, counts of vertices and edges, and comparisons for maxima. Each retained literal occurrence contributes to at most one output clause. When $r=0$, the first unsatisfiable threshold is $\min_K W_K$ over unmarked trees with all paths intact, with an empty minimum denoting no failure. For a fixed threshold, after preprocessing, toggling any edge's availability updates the verdict in $O(1)$ time. Maintain each path's number of unavailable or over-threshold edges, each relevant component's number of inactive paths, and a global count of components with zero inactive paths.

The projection also preserves a minimum cost. Give each active guarded implication a nonnegative violation cost $c_e$, and keep the terminal pins hard. For fixed retained bits, threshold and availability, the minimum cost is the sum of $\min_{P\in K,\,e\in P}c_e$ over the failing unmarked tree components. On a fully enabled path some implication must fail, and placing a single true-to-false cut at a cheapest edge realizes its least possible cost. All other paths can avoid violations. Every failing tree must leave at least one path uncovered, and rooting at a path of minimum cost attains that cost alone. The orientation choices in different components are independent. Without costs, one may also obtain a tree's boundary clause by successively eliminating its opposite-sign variables with Davis–Putnam resolution. [Davis and Putnam](https://doi.org/10.1145/321033.321034)

The known linear-time SAT decision for two-occurrence variables and its occurrence-three hardness boundary are due to Tovey. The component calculation above records the entire threshold-dependent projected relation with exceptional selectors and path deletions. Khanna, Putterman and Song use preservation of undirected threshold connectivity by a forest in their MST algorithm. Here the forest is in the incidence multigraph of complementary selector occurrences. [Tovey, *A simplified NP-complete satisfiability problem*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7) · [Khanna, Putterman and Song, Fact 2.5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/339/download/)

## Exactly how many threshold clauses can be needed?

With all edges available, **at most $2^r$** original projected clauses $D_K$ suffice at all thresholds simultaneously. For each assignment to $H$, retain a minimum-weight clause it falsifies, if there is one. That single clause becomes active exactly when the assignment first ceases to extend. This compression need not support future deletions. The full component representation above does.

The bound is sharp for each $r\ge1$, even for a replacement allowed to use arbitrary weighted clauses on the $r$ variables. For every $a\in\{0,1\}^r$, take the full-width clause false precisely on $a$, and give these clauses distinct weights. Their feasible assignment sets change at $2^r$ different thresholds. Each change requires a newly activated clause in any threshold CNF with fixed clause weights. To realize the family by the promised paths, split each wide clause into a width-three chain on private auxiliaries, for example

$$
 (\ell_1\vee\ell_2\vee z_1)\;\wedge\;
 (\neg z_1\vee\ell_3\vee z_2)\;\wedge\;\cdots\;\wedge\;
 (\neg z_{r-3}\vee\ell_{r-1}\vee\ell_r).
$$

Use this for $r\ge4$ and the clause itself for smaller $r$. The auxiliary occurrences form one unmarked tree, a path, for each $a$. Use one separate guarded $s,t$ path for each clause in its chain, giving its edges the weight of the original wide clause. The projection theorem returns exactly the intended single clause at exactly that weight. This example measures the number of changes in the full extension relation.

## Three occurrences already carry general 3-SAT

Starting with an ordinary width-at-most-three CNF, give each literal occurrence a separate copy of its variable. For a variable with $d\ge2$ copies impose the $d$ binary implications around a directed cycle on its copies. A satisfying assignment sets all of them equal. Each copy occurs once in an original clause and twice in its cycle. A variable occurring just once needs no cycle. Turn every resulting clause into a separate directed path guarded by the complements of its literals. The answer with all paths available is the original SAT answer. For a weighted input, keep the consistency paths active from the start and give each edge of an original clause's path that clause's weight. Tovey's reduction uses the same variable-copy cycle and width-two consistency clauses. The bound of three occurrences would behave differently if every clause had to use three *distinct* variables. [Tovey, Theorems 2.1 and 2.4](https://cedric.cnam.fr/~bentzc/INITREC/Files/CB11.pdf)

For unweighted 3-SAT the construction has linear **encoded** overhead under the usual dense fixed-width convention. Let the input list $M$ clauses on $V$ densely numbered variables with identifiers of $\Theta(\log(V+2))$ bits. Simplify repeated literals, remove tautologies and keep one copy of each distinct clause. An empty clause or an empty conjunction can be sent to a fixed instance of the corresponding sign. The remaining $M'$ clauses satisfy $M'\le O((V+1)^3)$ and $M'\le M$. They can be deduplicated in $O(M+V)$ word steps for dense names, for example by stable counting-sort passes on the constantly many signed fields after sorting each clause internally. Unused names may instead be compacted on first use by direct addressing. The output has $O(M'+1)$ edges, vertices, and selectors, so its identifiers need $O(\log(V+2))$ bits. This gives $O(n)$ output bits and construction time for an $n$-bit input with $V$ counting used names. Conversely each promised path immediately yields its width-at-most-three selector clause. Thus an $O(n^c)$ solver for either problem gives one for the other at the same exponent for $c=1,2,3$ under these encodings. For other presentations of unrestricted Boolean formula SAT, the cost of their conversion to 3-CNF has to be charged separately. Every solver for unrestricted SAT must also handle the 3-CNF instances.
