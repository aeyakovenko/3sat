# How often can the same word occur in a degree-four SAT core?

Consider proper ASCII DIMACS 3-CNFs in the [dense, sorted minimal spelling](dense-sorted-cores-for-ram-exhaustion.md), with exactly three distinct variables per clause. Write $v$ for the number of variables, $n$ for the encoded length in bits, $w=16\lceil D\log_2(n+2)/16\rceil$ for the word width and $W=w/8$ for its byte length. Use the separate-operation RAM and a fixed positive integer $D$. The following three properties hold.

1. To exclude any one of the clocks $O(n)$, $O(n^2)$ or $O(n^3)$ on the RAM class allowing a sufficiently large fixed logarithmic width coefficient per program, it suffices to exclude the clock on these cores with **at most four occurrences of each variable**. With at most three occurrences every proper formula is satisfiable.
2. For any fixed maximum occurrence count, each raw packed word appears on at most $v^{\max(0,1-D\log_2(10)/16)+o(1)}$ physical sites in such a core. At $D=1$ a set of words covering a fixed positive fraction of the file must contain at least $v^{(\log_2 10)/16-o(1)}$ distinct words. The exponent in the single-word bound is attained up to $o(1)$ on an infinite degree-four family at $D=1$. Thus the previous sparse-edit argument using one common numerical center at every marked site cannot cover that fraction.
3. At $D=1$ and infinitely many lengths there are degree-four cores with $m=\Theta(n/\log n)$ marked words and five choices per site. Although their centers can differ, the all-center core forces at least $2m-\lfloor m/(k+1)\rfloor$ executed instructions on every solver correct on the promise of at most $k$ noncenter sites. This gives the exact optimum $2m$ at $k=m$. There is an additive error of at most $m^{(\log_2 10)/8+o(1)}$ between that lower bound and the best clock at other $k$. For fixed $k$ the optimum can be made exact by retaining a positive density of marked sites.

## The threshold at four occurrences

First normalize the input and discard repeated clauses. If $c$ distinct clauses remain then $c=O(1+n/\log(n+2))$ by the [packed normalization theorem](packed-normalization-of-dimacs-clauses.md). Give each original occurrence its own copy of the variable. Link all copies of a given variable by successive directed implications on a cycle, omitting the cycle on a singleton. The constraints force equality of the copies. Enlarge each binary implication to a proper three-variable clause with a fresh false padding literal. For the padding use Tovey's constant-size gadget. Its distinguished literal $s$ is forced true by 13 proper clauses using nine private variables. In each of three groups use $(s\lor a_j\lor b_j)$ and, for each nonzero pair $(u,t)$ of bits, a clause forcing $d_j$ true if $(a_j,b_j)=(u,t)$. Add $(\neg d_1\lor\neg d_2\lor\neg d_3)$. At some $d_j=0$ both $a_j,b_j$ must be zero, forcing $s$. Conversely when $s$ is true all auxiliaries may be false. Each auxiliary occurs at most four times. The variable of $s$ occurs three times and can also supply a padding literal $\neg s$ to one implication. The original occurrence copies occur at most three times each. At bound three, each set of $t$ clauses has at least $t$ neighboring variables by counting its $3t$ occurrences. A matching assigns a distinct satisfying literal to each clause. The reduction, pin gadget, and degree-three boundary are classical. [Tovey, 1984, Theorems 2.1, 2.3 and 2.4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-218X(84)90081-7)

The new cost accounting concerns encoded lengths and native clocks. The construction uses $O(c)$ cells of word-sized names and a constant number of integer radix sorts for grouping, dense numbering and sorting the triples. Its dense ASCII has $O(c\log(c+2))=O(n)$ bits and takes $O(1+n/\log(n+2))$ native steps to emit by the earlier packed spelling algorithm. Every introduced identifier occurs. Empty cases can be decided separately. The constant relative size increase may enlarge the next word width; a host with a sufficiently large fixed coefficient can implement the guest's masked operations and keep its uncovered initial memory zero. This gives the same fixed clock exponent including one. It does not give the earlier same-coefficient *nonexpanding* compiler.

## Raw words in canonical files

If the occurrence bound is $d$, with $d$ fixed, then $n=\Theta(v\log(v+2))$. All $v$ names occur, and the sum of their decimal lengths is $\Theta(v\log(v+2))$. The reverse inequality follows from at most $dv$ occurrences of length at most $g=1+\lfloor\log_{10}v\rfloor$. Header and partial final words cost only $O(1)$ sites.

Fix the contents of one $W$-byte word. Among its occurrences in the body, first count aligned blocks containing the entire decimal digit string of at least one literal occurrence. The name and its offsets form one of at most $W(W+1)/2$ nonempty substrings of the proposed word. It occurs at most $d$ times as a literal identifier. Nonoverlapping RAM words containing that entire physical occurrence cannot be distinct. This contributes at most $O(dW^2)$.

In any remaining block there can be at most two incomplete identifier strings. Three intersecting strings in their file order would place the middle one entirely inside the block. Between consecutive variable-name strings there are at most four other bytes, allowing the minus sign, zero terminator and newline. Even permitting two partial outside gaps leaves at most twelve other bytes in the block. Hence there is a run of at least $e=\lfloor(W-12)/2\rfloor$ decimal digits belonging to a single name, once $W$ is large. The proposed word has at most $W$ candidates for this $e$-digit piece. For $e\le g$, each fixed piece occurs in at most $O(g v10^{-e})$ different names: sum the possible starting positions and remaining digits over lengths $e$ through $g$, using $10^g\le 10v$. Each such name occurs at most $d$ times and offers at most $g$ offsets. This gives $O(dW^2+dWg^2v10^{-e})$ aligned sites, also valid with the second term omitted for $e>g$. Substituting the width and $n=\Theta(v\log v)$ gives the exponent claimed above.

## A word attaining the frequency exponent

Here is a matching construction for $D=1$. Take $V=10^q-1$ primary names, give each its own nine-auxiliary pin as below, and add $M=\lfloor V/32\rfloor$ rows $(c_i,u_i,v_i)$ with $c_i=q+i$ for $1\le i\le M$. The second names have $q$ digits beginning with 3 and the third have $q$ digits beginning with 8. No name is used twice within either list. Pin all $c_i$ true. Of the primary names $1,\ldots,q$, pin the first $t$ true and the others false. Compensate by pinning $q-t$ primaries true in a reserved set of $q$ names strictly between the largest $c_i$ and the first available $u_i$. Pin all other primaries false. Every file is satisfiable, uses all $10V$ names and has maximum occurrence four. The file length is independent of $t$ and of both lists because precisely $M+q$ primaries are true and the two large fields have fixed decimal lengths. Again $n(q)=(K'+o(1))10^q q$ for some $K'>0$, so infinitely many native widths are powers of two. Then $W$ is a power of two, $W\le q$ eventually, and 3 is invertible modulo $W$.

Let $s=\lfloor(W-1)/2\rfloor$, $t'=W-1-s$. In the sorted serialization the byte offset of the space between $u_i$ and $v_i$ is its offset at $t=0$ minus $3t$. Each newly true preamble pin removes a minus from three preceding clauses; its compensated pin comes later. Each added row precedes the pin clauses with the same positive first literal since their second names are all auxiliaries. Therefore the offsets do not depend on the large identifiers chosen within the indicated ranges. As $t$ ranges from 0 through $W-1$, each row puts its middle separator at residue $s$ exactly once. At some $t$ at least $M/W$ rows do so.

On as many of these rows as possible choose distinct $u_i$ ending in $s$ copies of digit 5, and distinct $v_i$ beginning with $t'$ copies of digit 8. The capacities are $10^{q-1-s}$ and $10^{q-t'}$. The other rows can be filled injectively within their larger digit ranges since $M<10^{q-1}$. On every chosen row an aligned word is the same string of $s$ fives, one space and $t'$ eights. Its number of occurrences is at least
$$
\min\{\lfloor M/W\rfloor,10^{q-1-s},10^{q-t'}\}
   = V^{1-(\log_2 10)/16+o(1)}.
$$
Replacing $V$ by the actual variable count $10V$ does not change the exponent. These repeated words straddle two incompletely visible identifiers, explaining why the two half-word contributions in the upper bound cannot be replaced by one whole-name estimate.

## Different centers with a common editable byte

Take $V=10^q-1$ primary names and $m=\lfloor V/8192\rfloor$. For each primary $j$ use a disjoint Tovey pin with consecutive auxiliaries $V+9(j-1)+1$ through $V+9j$. Pin exactly $m$ primaries true and the rest false, as specified below. The ultimate variable range is $1,\ldots,10V$. Add the $m$ clauses
$$
               (i\lor (2m+i)\lor A_i),\qquad 1\le i\le m.
$$
Each $A_i$ is a $q$-digit name starting with 8. Sort literals by identifier, positive before negative, sort the clauses and use minimum separators. The pins occupy $13V$ distinct clauses. Within the added clauses every first pair is different, in the given order. Inside the pin gadget a clause containing a primary has its auxiliaries in its other two positions. Thus every added row has a permanent place in the sorting order.

The target length $n(q)$ is known before the $A_i$ are chosen. The fields $A_i$ all have the same digit length, every auxiliary has $q+1$ digits, and changing exactly $m$ primary pins from false to true removes exactly $3m$ minus bytes. In particular $n(q)=(K+o(1))10^q q$ for a fixed $K>0$ by summing digit lengths. The ratio $n(q+1)/n(q)$ tends to ten. From some $q$ onwards the widths $w_1(n(q))$ cannot increase by more than 16 in a step and tend to infinity. Hence infinitely many of them are powers of two. Use this subsequence. Since $W/q\to(\log_2 10)/8<1/2$, the suffix of each $A_i$ past its first digit has a whole aligned RAM word, regardless of its byte offset. Those offsets are fixed too: all affected primary pins begin at much larger identifiers than the first field $i$ of every added row. Any earlier pin on a small primary is negative in all choices. The word in each row may be chosen before filling any center name.

For a given row choose its $A_i$ to have digit 5 at the first and last byte of its chosen word. There are $10^{q-3}$ candidates, because the other fixed digit is the leading 8. Associate five primary names to each candidate by changing its last marked byte to 3, 4, 5, 6 or 7. For this row, each name used previously as a center or any alternative forbids at most one candidate: restore the selected digit to 5. Since $5m<10^{q-3}$, choose pairwise disjoint five-element sets greedily. Pin their centers true and all other primaries false. Each primary now occurs at most three times in its pin and once in the new clauses, even after simultaneous edits, and all nine auxiliary roles in every gadget obey the occurrence cap. Every edit preserves density, ordering, length and clause count. The edited row can be satisfied exactly at its center because both smaller primaries are pinned false. Thus the full formula has verdict SAT exactly when all edits are central.

## Replay with heterogeneous center words

Let $B_i$ be the unedited numerical word at site $i$, $Q=2^w$, and $\Delta=2^{w-8}$. Its choices are $B_i+t_i\Delta$ for $t_i\in\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}$. The low and high bytes of every $B_i$ are both 53. The intervening bytes can vary arbitrarily among digits. In particular $B_i$ exceeds every physical input address. There is no wraparound, no sign-bit crossing, $\Delta^2=0$ modulo $Q$ for large $w$, and $w\mid\Delta$ at the chosen widths.

In the symbolic replay on the all-center file, require the original addresses, branches and numerical results in the [sparse-edit forest proof](exact-executed-clocks-for-sparse-word-edits.md). The deviations $t_i$ now serve as the common vertex alphabet. For a numerical operation or branch on two different private raw operands the binary relation induced by fixing its center result has the same rooted extension property as in that proof. Addition is preserved by opposite deviations and subtraction by equal deviations. For multiplication the opposite deviations give
$$
 (B_i+t\Delta)(B_j-t\Delta)-B_i B_j
     = t\Delta(B_j-B_i)-t^2\Delta^2=0\pmod Q
$$
because $B_j-B_i$ is a multiple of 256. Equal deviations preserve every signed or unsigned comparison, even when the centers are unequal. For bitwise gates only the highest byte varies, where both centers are the same. Complementary tables and the submask and supermask cases of digit 5 are unchanged. A shift or rotation can vary the count operand alone: its residue modulo $w$ stays fixed, and a saturating count always exceeds $w$. This also handles numerical copies and constant operations. In either orientation each relation allows a noncenter child at a central parent or admits a child extension for every parent deviation.

Each of the $m$ compulsory original LOADs imposes no address constraint: a raw marked tag has center value greater than every marked address. All remaining instructions impose at most one fixed-center constraint on at most two private tags. Unrestricted stores forward the entire raw value until another instruction processes it. A tree component carrying no unary constraint can be perturbed, using the old rooted extension lemma on the common deviation alphabet. Correctness under at most $k$ edits forces all such components to have more than $k$ vertices. Counting components gives the stated lower bound on the actual halting center run, irrespective of the static program length.

For the upper bound group marked words by their original $B_i$. There are $H\le10^{W-2}=m^{(\log_2 10)/8+o(1)}$ groups. In each group use the previous equality-block test on blocks of $k+1$ words, and literal tests for the remainder. This saves $\sum_b\lfloor m_b/(k+1)\rfloor$ from $2m$ steps, where $m_b$ are the group sizes. The difference from the lower bound is at most $H$. For exact equality at an individual $k$, keep only full blocks in each group; the removed at most $kH$ rows stay fixed at center as public words. For $kH=o(m)$ the remaining number $m'$ of editable rows is still $\Theta(n/\log n)$, and the optimum for this reduced promise is precisely $2m'-m'/(k+1)$. At $k=m$ individually comparing every word against its center already gives the matching upper bound on the original promise.

The [Lean proof](HeterogeneousCoreEdits.lean) verifies the pin semantics and the heterogeneous modular product identity. The [finite audit](bounded_occurrence_edits_audit.py) checks literal occurrences, sorting, actual bytes and small pin assignments, then uses exact digit counts for layouts too large to materialize. The operation replay and cost accounting are manuscript proofs. The result identifies a degree-four test class equivalent for the time question and preserves a packed-word lower bound on it. It gives no unbounded factor over $n$ bits.
