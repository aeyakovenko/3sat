# Which finite fields test a matrix-product verifier?

Fix a division-free arithmetic circuit over $\mathbb F_q$ with a tuple of outputs $g(A,B,C)$, $d$-square matrix inputs and $s$ nonscalar multiplication gates. Its verdict is equality when every output is zero. Use the **same circuit and constants** over all extensions. Put

$$
 D=3\cdot 2^s,\qquad L=D\lceil\log_q(2D+1)\rceil .
$$

If the verdict is correct on $\mathbb F_{q^m}$ for each $1\le m\le L$, its zero set over $\overline{\mathbb F}_q$ is exactly $C=AB$. An exponential allowance in $s$ is necessary in general, even to force isolation of the correct product at the generic pair $A,B$. On the bit-encoded matrix family, any verifier can be modified at an additional $O(d)$ operations to exhibit this failure in a natural arithmetic interpretation of its checks.

## Two checks can hide a component through every correct product

In characteristic two define the homogeneous polynomial

$$
 Q_u(X,Y)=X^{2\cdot3^u}+X^{3^u}Y^{3^u}+Y^{2\cdot3^u},\qquad u\ge0 .
$$

It costs at most $4u+3$ multiplications by repeated cubing of the two entries. A nontrivial zero has $Y\ne0$, and $(X/Y)^{3^u}$ must have multiplicative order three. Consequently $X/Y$ has order exactly $3^{u+1}$. Such an element exists in $\mathbb F_{2^m}$ exactly when $2\cdot3^u\mid m$. Indeed $\operatorname{ord}_{3^{u+1}}(2)=2\cdot3^u$. An odd exponent gives no factor of three in $2^m-1$, and $v_3(4^a-1)=1+v_3(a)$. The latter identity follows by factoring $4^{3b}-1$ and observing that cubing a number congruent to one modulo three adds exactly one to the valuation. Exponents coprime to three leave it unchanged.

Over all the other extension degrees, $Q_u(X,Y)=0$ enforces $X=Y=0$. Over the algebraic closure it defines a union of lines through the origin, so the origin fails to be isolated. This discrepancy applies at a generic actual graph point. For $d\ge2$, compute two entries of $AB$ by dot products and substitute the correct values for those two entries of the proposed $C$ before calling a verifier. Also require $Q_u(E_1,E_2)=0$ on the two original residuals. On the tested fields the new test has exactly the original correctness guarantee. Whenever the original test accepts the generic genuine product, varying just those residuals along one of the lines leaves the modified test accepting. Computing the dot products and the extra polynomial costs $O(d+u)$ arithmetic operations. Already with $u=d$, a first detecting extension requires $2\cdot3^d$ bits **per unrestricted field element** in binary representation.

In particular the [near-linear SAT reduction](verifying-matrix-products-from-sat.md) gives a deterministic verifier on binary matrices under its stated time hypothesis. Appending the modification there costs $O(d)$ ordinary bit or word operations, even if one performs every cubing on the two bits. The appended checks have the stated extension-field interpretation. The SAT hypothesis itself supplies the underlying verifier only on bit inputs. It shows why finite tests do not make an arbitrary verification program generically faithful. A method producing a particular faithful verifier would require a separate argument.

## An extension-degree bound for any counterexample

Introduce one coordinate per multiplication gate. All remaining computations are affine forms of input coordinates, gate coordinates and constants. Each gate definition is quadratic and each output equation is linear. For a false positive with $C_{ij}\ne(AB)_{ij}$ introduce one variable $y$ and impose

$$
 y\left(C_{ij}-\sum_{\ell=1}^d A_{i\ell}B_{\ell j}\right)=1 .
$$

This is one cubic. The sum of the degrees of the irreducible components of this lifted counterexample variety is at most $D$. Intersect successively with the defining hypersurfaces. A component contained in the next hypersurface is retained, and a proper intersection costs at most the hypersurface degree times the old component's degree by Bézout. Linear equations can therefore be imposed without growing this bound. [Geometric degree and affine Bézout](https://www.maia.ub.edu/~sombra/publications/intrinseco/arn.pdf)

Any nonempty affine variety over $\mathbb F_q$ of geometric degree at most $D$ has a point in an extension of degree at most $D\lceil\log_q(2D+1)\rceil$. To see this, take the union of the projective closures of its top-dimensional geometric components. This union is Frobenius-stable and has degree at most $D$, as does its boundary cut out by the hyperplane at infinity. Set $a=\lceil\log_q(2D+1)\rceil$. Choose successive hyperplanes over $\mathbb F_{q^a}$ avoiding containment of each current component of the union and each current component of the boundary, including point components. At most $2D$ proper linear conditions must be avoided at each step. A proper linear subspace defined over the algebraic closure contains at most a $q^{-a}$ fraction of the rational coefficient vectors, by solving one nonzero linear equation for one coordinate. After as many steps as the dimension of the union, its intersection is finite, nonempty by projective dimension, and disjoint from infinity. Its degree is at most $D$. Frobenius over $\mathbb F_{q^a}$ acts on these at most $D$ points, so one orbit of length $e\le D$ supplies a point over $\mathbb F_{q^{ae}}$. This argument includes a zero-dimensional starting variety.

A false negative is easier. Some $g_i(A,B,AB)$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $2^{s+1}$. It cannot vanish at every point over any extension of cardinality exceeding that degree, by the elementary polynomial identity bound. These two arguments prove the stated cutoff. Over $\mathbb F_5$ the global cutoff is tight up to a logarithmic factor. Multiplying a scalar graph equation by $X^{2^u}-2$ uses $u+O(1)$ nonlinear gates for a free input $X$, and the factor has a root in $\mathbb F_{5^m}$ exactly when $2^u\mid m$. This uses $v_2(5^m-1)=2+v_2(m)$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{5}(2)=4$. The binary homogeneous example above is stronger concerning the local graph fiber.

## Transferring one integral circuit between characteristics

Suppose the same circuit has integer constants. There is an explicit height-dependent test using algebraic closures of prime fields. Form all the false-positive systems above. For a false negative of output $i$, use the quadratic gate equations, all equations $C=AB$, and $y g_i=1$ using its affine output form. Every lifted system is in at most $v=3d^2+s+1$ variables and has degree at most three. Let $H\ge1$ bound the absolute values of the coefficients of all these explicit local equations. Define

$$
 b=3^v,\qquad R=\binom{v+b+3}{v},\qquad M=(\sqrt R\,H)^R.
$$

For a fixed system $f_j=0$, form the integer matrix with columns the coefficient vectors of $X^\alpha f_j$ for $|\alpha|\le b$, in the monomials through degree $b+3$. Over an algebraically closed field the system is empty exactly when the constant vector $1$ is in the column space. Indeed a representation of $1$ is sufficient, and the degree bound $\max(3,\deg f_j)^v\le b$ for the effective Nullstellensatz ensures the included multipliers suffice when the system is empty. A linear system over a field has the same solvability after extending scalars. [Kollár's effective Nullstellensatz](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~deloera/MISC/LA-BIBLIO/trunk/Kollar/kollarnullstellen.pdf) · [Sombra's statement over general fields](https://www.maia.ub.edu/~sombra/publications/sparse-nss/sparse4.pdf)

For the coefficient matrix and its augmentation by $1$, choose one nonzero minor attaining each rational rank, with the order-zero minor equal to one. Each has absolute value at most $M$ by Hadamard's inequality. At primes not dividing their product both ranks are preserved. Thus **one prime $p>M$** at which the circuit is correct over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ suffices for correctness over $\mathbb C$. Also sufficient is any set of tested geometric characteristics whose distinct primes have product exceeding $M^2$. Indeed a fixed characteristic-zero counterexample system would stay nonempty outside the divisors of its two minors. The extension-degree test above can replace geometric correctness separately for each selected prime. The height estimate is deliberately coarse. Arithmetic Nullstellensatz and elimination bounds have a substantial earlier literature. [Krick, Pardo and Sombra](https://www.maia.ub.edu/~sombra/publications/arith-nss/maino.pdf)

For one fixed integral circuit, correctness on all sufficiently large **prime fields alone** also implies correctness over $\mathbb C$, without an effective prime cutoff asserted here. A nonempty counterexample system over $\mathbb C$ has a point over $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. Write its coordinates as polynomials in one integral primitive element $\theta$ with a common nonzero integer denominator. Infinitely many rational primes outside that denominator give a root of the minimal polynomial of $\theta$ modulo $p$. Indeed, for an integer polynomial $F$ of positive degree, any finite product of excluded primes $P$ and an integer $b$ with $F(b)\ne0$, the integers $F(b+PF(b)t)/F(b)$ are $1$ modulo $P$ and have unbounded absolute value. A new prime divisor gives a root of $F$ modulo that prime. Evaluation at this root reduces all coordinates of the counterexample, including the coordinate certifying a required nonzero value. The resulting point is a counterexample in the prime field itself. This specialization also follows from the stronger theorem of Vu, Wood and Wood preserving specified algebraic incidences at infinitely many primes. Related modular reductions occur in work on Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz. [Vu, Wood and Wood](https://arxiv.org/abs/0711.4407) · [Koiran](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~r5olivei/courses/2025-spring-cs860/K96-HN-PH.pdf)

There is no bound depending only on the number of nonscalar gates for the largest characteristic one must inspect **if only the prime fields themselves are tested**. Fix any prime cutoff $P$. By the Chinese remainder theorem choose an integer $c$ such that $T^2+T+c$ is irreducible over every $\mathbb F_p$ with $p\le P$. At odd $p$ prescribe a nonsquare discriminant $1-4c$. At $2$ prescribe $c=1\bmod 2$. One can take $0\le c<\prod_{p\le P}p$. The fixed-size test $X^2+XY+cY^2=0$ enforces both residuals zero in those fields and has lines through zero over $\mathbb C$. All these examples use ordinary finite matrices and one common verifier per stated range of fields.

## When the positive-characteristic deformation is tame

There is also a direct geometric consequence over $k=\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ if $p>2^s$. For a graph-isolating circuit as above, the proof of the [characteristic-zero comparison](set-theoretic-matrix-graph-checks.md) then gives $\underline R_k(M_d)\le2s$. Work over a **separable closure** $K$ of $k(A,B)$ so that entrywise derivations extend uniquely. Use $d^2$ constant linear combinations of the output equations to obtain an isolated parameter ideal at $C=AB$. Its local length $\mu$ is at most $2^s$. In the lifted gate graph this length is exactly the isolated intersection multiplicity of the $s$ quadratic gate equations and $d^2$ linear combinations with the $d^2+s$ unknowns. Gate elimination identifies the local algebras, whose length is bounded by the product of these degrees by the isolated-point Bézout bound with multiplicities. One can extend scalars to an algebraic closure to measure this length since the central point is rational and isolated. [Classical Bézout bound for isolated affine zeros with multiplicities](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-662-03817-8)

In the finite free map of completed local rings in that proof the rank is $\mu<p$. The fraction-field extension is separable because its inseparable degree is a power of $p$. The Jacobian determinant is nonzero. Otherwise a nonzero linear combination over the local ring of the coordinate derivations would kill all the chosen parameters. By continuity it would kill every formal series in the parameters and hence give a nonzero derivation of a separable algebraic field extension over its base. Choosing a constant perturbation line avoiding the norm of the Jacobian is now valid as before.

A branch of this line has degree at most $\mu$ over $K((\epsilon))$. Its ramification index $e$ and residue extension degree are both less than $p$. The latter is one since $K$ is separably closed. The branch field is complete, with the embedded coefficient field $K$ mapping isomorphically to its residue field. Successively lifting residues expresses each integral element as a series in a uniformizer $\pi$ over $K$. Write $\epsilon=\pi^e u$ for a unit $u$. Its residue has an $e$th root in $K$ and Hensel's lemma lifts that root since $e<p$. Thus an $e$th root of $\epsilon$ is also a uniformizer. Coefficientwise differentiation and specialization now work in $K[[\epsilon^{1/e}]]$. The two rank-one terms per product, followed by projection to the output, prove the claim. More generally this local argument needs only $\mu<p$. For example, an equation $E^p=0$ is set-theoretically faithful in characteristic $p$ while its ordinary derivative vanishes everywhere. The bound on $\mu$ is a sufficient condition for this deformation argument.

For $p>2^s$, even encoding a general prime-field element already takes more than $s$ bits. The arithmetic and finite-extension bounds here do not turn a time bound for SAT on bit encodings into an equally small arithmetic circuit correct on the required fields.
