# A lower bound for sparse writes computed from fixed input neighborhoods

On the balanced-row SAT family, a sparse address chosen from one raw word can encode exponentially many possible equality flags with just one write. The full walk queries give a different bound. Let $N$ denote encoded bits and $w=\Theta(\log N)$ the native word width. If all old bit values at the cut have fixed degree in functions of nearly linearly many word-sized input neighborhoods, then some actual opposite one-bit pair incurs $\Omega(N/\log^2 N)$ charged word instructions on **both** continuations. Work addresses can range throughout the native address space. The unrestricted bound without the degree assumption is $\Omega(N\log\log N/\log^3 N)$. [Charge convention, general case and matching algorithm](nonlinear-work-after-the-one-bit-sat-cut.md)

The proof varies the two distinguished columns in every row while preserving the full identity matrix of binary queries. Every union of a small number of the proposed neighborhoods then meets only a small fraction of the independent binary coordinates. This also gives an input degree bound on the exact all-good flag of one segment without a chronology or memory model.

## Assumption on the common prefix

Use the dyadic family of the physical cut construction. A master database has $k$ rows of $B=\Theta(\log k/\log\log k)$ bits. A node has $s$ consecutive rows, $N_s=\Theta(sB)$ bits in the file and $\Theta(s/\log N_s)$ pending packets at the cut. The distinct segments of $d=\Theta(\log k)$ rows are aligned down to the leaves of $\Theta(\sqrt k)$ rows. The width and logarithms on the whole tree differ by at most constant factors. [Physical layout and the two-tag recursion](near-complete-disclosure-on-one-bit-sat-pairs.md)

At each node, take a list of at most $O(N_s\log^K N_s)$ subsets of the raw database bits of that node, for fixed $K\ge0$. Each subset contains at most $b=O(\log k)$ bits. A **local function** is any Boolean function of one such subset. Allow all of them for free. After fixing the database-dependent physical layout header, controller and an optional choice among $2^{o(s)}$ prescribed lists, assume **each bit** of the cut work memory and of the installed immutable input at its physical address agrees with an $\mathbb F_2$ polynomial of fixed degree $e\ge1$ in these functions on the databases consistent with this metadata. The functions and coefficients may vary with the address, header and chosen list. Analyses granting different lists still use one fixed list for every address on the same cut. The $o(s)$ is uniform over the dyadic sizes. Another arbitrary $o(s)$ bits of database-only summary may be incorporated into the list index, even when every cell refers to that summary. The raw database for leaf queries is available separately.

For example, on the canonical data-first scan each physical data packet depends on a fixed chunk of $O(w)$ raw bits. A scatter contribution whose address and datum both depend on one local input neighborhood contributes just one local function to each possible destination bit, even if its address ranges over the whole native space. XOR accumulation of arbitrarily many such contributions has $e=1$, including writes to distinct address ranges. The assumption also allows bounded-degree products of these contributions. There is a useful native overwrite case. Suppose prefix events have a fixed order, and the presence, destination and datum of each event depend on its own neighborhood. If at most $e$ events actually hit any one address on every database in the chosen fiber, the final contents have degree at most $e$ in the local functions. For address $a$, write $h_i$ for its hit flag and $b_i$ for its hit flag times a chosen data bit. Both are single local functions. The final bit from a zero initial cell is $\sum_i b_i\prod_{j>i}(1+h_j)$. In its expansion every term with more than $e$ distinct events vanishes throughout the fiber, since it requires more than $e$ simultaneous hits. This permits unboundedly many *possible* writers. With unrestricted actual overwrite multiplicity the selector of the last writer can have high degree. General RAM preprocessing and a permutation of installed data packets chosen adaptively can also violate the assumption. The input-word condition applies at the actual physical slots, even for such a permutation.

## A legal moving pair of columns

View the $B=u^2$ columns as $(x,y)\in(\mathbb Z/u)^2$. Put $t(x,y)=(x+1,y)$. Two labeled Margulis–Gabber–Galil steps realize $t$: first add $2y+1$ to $x$, then subtract $2y$. Reversing these steps realizes $t^{-1}$. A hold is also a single legal label.

Choose independently for each segment a uniform initial center and a walk of centers $v_i$ on the 156-step lazy MGG graph. Distinguish columns $v_i,t(v_i)$ in its $i$th row. From either distinguished column one can reach either next distinguished column in exactly 160 labeled steps. Undo the optional shift, follow the 156 center steps, and apply the new optional shift. Fill unused slots with holds. Since the first column is freely chosen, **every** binary selector string is a legal powered walk on these columns, with the same property after dyadic restriction.

On the resulting binary database cube put a one at the distinguished position specified by a payload bit $x_i$, a zero at the other, and $B/2-1$ constant ones elsewhere. The two columns are distinct for $u\ge2$. Each row is balanced. For the query specified by $y$, all selected entries are good exactly when $x=y$, independently of how the centers were chosen.

## How frequently an input neighborhood meets the moving cube

Let $V$ be any fixed set of raw positions across the master database. Let $X(V)$ count the rows whose pair hits $V$. For row $i$, write $V_i$ for its chosen columns and $A_i=V_i\cup t^{-1}(V_i)$. The event counted is $v_i\in A_i$, and $\sum_i |A_i|/B\le2|V|/B$. For these nonidentical row sets,
$$
 \mathbb E\,2^{X(V)}\le \exp(4|V|/B).
$$

Here is the moment calculation. Let $P$ be the 156-step transition on the uniform-measure real Hilbert space. It is positive semidefinite, fixes constants and has nonconstant operator norm at most $((1+5\sqrt{2}/8)/2)^{156}<1/3$ by the MGG eigenvalue bound; denote this norm by $\lambda$. Let $E_A$ multiply by the indicator of a set of density $\alpha$. For $f=a\mathbf1+v$, with $v$ centered, put $z=P^{1/2}v$. Then $z$ is centered and $\|z\|^2\le\lambda\|v\|^2$. Consequently
$$
 \langle f,P^{1/2}(I+E_A)P^{1/2}f\rangle
 \le (1+2\alpha)a^2+3\lambda\|v\|^2
 \le (1+2\alpha)\|f\|^2.
$$
The first inequality uses $\|E_A(a\mathbf1+z)\|^2\le2\alpha a^2+2\|z\|^2$. These operators are positive, so their norm obeys the same upper bound. The expectation for one segment is the inner product of $\mathbf1$ with the product of these operators applied to $\mathbf1$, since adjacent square roots multiply to $P$ and the outer ones fix $\mathbf1$. Multiply the norms, use $1+2\alpha\le e^{2\alpha}$, and multiply independent segments. This is an expander-walk moment argument of the type used for varying observables. ## Selecting one cube for all tests, layouts and menus

Fix a small constant $\delta>0$ with $H_2(\delta)<1/2$, where $H_2$ is binary entropy. A completed descent type includes the spatial path, all layout headers and controllers on it, and the choices of neighborhood lists. The earlier physical headers cost $o(k)$ bits along a path. The additional choices also cost $o(k)$ bits because the row counts decrease geometrically and the smallest tends to infinity. There are $J=2^{o(k)}$ types. These menus and headers are specified on the full balanced family **before** the random center walks are chosen.

The pooled neighborhoods for each fixed type number at most $T=k\log^{O(1)} k$. Include singleton sets for the raw leaf bits. Let $a=\lfloor\eta k/\log\log k\rfloor$, with a sufficiently small fixed $\eta>0$. For any one union of at most $a$ neighborhoods its total size is at most $ab$. The probability that **any** such union in any type intersects more than $\delta k$ independently varying rows is at most
$$
 J\left(\sum_{i\le a}\binom Ti\right)2^{-\delta k}\exp(4ab/B).
$$
Pad $T$ to at least $k$ when using the binomial estimate. The logarithm of the binomial sum is at most $a\log(eT/a)=O(\eta k)+o(k)$ for fixed $\eta$. Also $ab/B=O(\eta k)$. The displayed probability is less than one for large $k$. Fix center walks outside this bad event. Now **every** product of at most $a$ local functions from one type depends, on the moving binary cube, on at most $\delta k$ binary payload bits. Each such function has a multilinear polynomial over $\mathbb F_2$ in just the bits on which it depends.

At a node truncate both tag continuations at $\lfloor\gamma s/(w_s\log\log k)\rfloor$ charged instructions, for small fixed $\gamma$. A charged instruction needs $O(w_s)$ affine tests on the actual node's old bits. With a type and query fixed, each test has degree at most $e$ in the local functions of that type. The type simulation checks its next-child and stopping guards; tests for the leaf include $O(\sqrt k)$ raw bits. On every path in the truncated decision tree the sum of these test degrees is at most $C e\gamma k/\log\log k+O(e\log^2 k+\sqrt k)\le a$ for suitably small $\gamma$. Expand an accepting path as a product of the tests or their complements, and sum those products. Extend polynomial representations arbitrarily off the valid header fiber using the respective fixed polynomials. On impossible branches one may stop with zero. The resulting column, before applying the type's row-only consistency indicator, has ordinary multilinear degree at most $\lfloor\delta k\rfloor$ in the moving binary payload. The number of its spanning monomials is at most $2^{H_2(\delta)k}$.

The masked sum over all types therefore has rank at most $J2^{H_2(\delta)k}<2^{k/2}$ for large $k$. On any database on which every genuinely opposite pair anywhere in the dyadic tree has at least one fast side, the two-tag recursion computes all-good for every legal binary selector. The resulting equality submatrix on those databases has rank equal to their number. Thus all but at most $2^{k/2}$ of the $2^k$ payloads on the chosen moving cube have some opposite pair with **both** suffixes spending more than the local cutoff. The chosen node has unbounded size and its bound is $\Omega(N_s/\log^2 N_s)$. The same node-pigeonhole argument as in the cut construction gives $\Omega(2^s/s)$ distinct local payloads with a hard pair at one length. [Type masks and rank](AffineCutRank.lean) · [Two-tag logic](DyadicHalfTimeout.lean)

The earlier packed algorithm attains this order with $O(N)$ actual time on the canonical files. Choose its subgroup length $r_0=\Theta(\log w)$ with a sufficiently small fixed coefficient, so $r_0B=O(w)$. Every stored subgroup flag then depends on one raw neighborhood of that size. The uniform public table is data-independent. A scanner can use statically separated scratch ranges and clear or overwrite data-dependent scratch before the tag, keeping the decoded raw masks and the completed subgroup table. This establishes sharpness for this locality hypothesis even though other valid algorithms in the class may use sparse memory.

## Exact degree needed to materialize one full segment flag

There is a quantitative statement about a common pool of $T$ fixed $b$-bit neighborhoods with no header or simulator. Divide $s$ rows into $G=s/r$ groups of $r$ consecutive rows within the segments. Suppose every group's all-good flag for every compatible walk can be represented by a degree-$c$ polynomial in arbitrary functions of these neighborhoods, $c\ge1$. Pair the raw columns inside each row into fixed disjoint pairs and restrict their two bits to complementary values, with one independent variable per pair. For a fixed query its group response contains the nonzero degree-$r$ monomial using the $r$ selected pair variables. By uniqueness of Boolean multilinear expansion, at least one term of the neighborhood polynomial must involve all those variables. Hence one of at most $c$ neighborhoods in that term must hit at least $r/c$ of the selected pair coordinates.

For one group choose the uniform 160-step walk. The moment bound applies to the union of each neighborhood's paired columns in that row. A neighborhood hits at least $r/c$ selected pair coordinates with probability at most $2^{-r/c}\exp(4b/B)$. To have positive probability it must contain positions in at least $r/c$ rows of the group. Each neighborhood can qualify in at most $bc/r$ groups. Summing the required coverage of all $G$ groups gives
$$
 T\ \ge\ \frac{s}{bc}\,2^{r/c}\exp(-4b/B).
$$
In particular $b=O(w)$ and $T=O(sB\log^K N)$ force $c=\Omega(r/\log w)$ (for $c>r$ this is immediate). The packed table with subgroups of $\Theta(\log w)$ rows attains degree $O(1+r/\log w)$ by multiplying subgroup flags, with shorter pieces at an endpoint if needed. For $c=1$ and $r=d=\Theta(w)$ the required pool has size $N^{1+\Omega(1)}$. The equality restriction with a stationary distinguished pair admits a single sparse sentinel per group. The moving-pair and segment arguments use the additional walks needed to exclude that method on the full family.
