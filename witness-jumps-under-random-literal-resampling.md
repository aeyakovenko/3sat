# A random literal edit can force an exact witness selector to change linearly many bits

Let $\kappa>\kappa^*\simeq4.911$ be fixed. For every sufficiently large fixed clause width $k$, let $m=\lfloor\kappa 2^k(\log k)n/k\rfloor$, with natural logarithms. Draw the $km$ signed literals of an ordered $k$-CNF $F$ independently and uniformly from the $2n$ possibilities. Choose a uniform occurrence and replace it with a fresh independent literal to obtain $F'$. Let $A$ be any fixed deterministic function returning a satisfying assignment on every satisfiable input; it can be arbitrary elsewhere and can depend on $n$.

There are constants $c_k,C_k>0$ such that, for all sufficiently large $n$,

$$
 \Pr\big[F,F'\text{ have a common satisfying assignment and }
       d_H(A(F),A(F'))\ge c_k n\big]\ \ge\ C_k/n .
$$

Indeed a random pair of this kind has a common satisfying assignment with probability $1-o(1)$. The $\Omega_k(1/n)$ lower bound is for the joint event including the jump. The proof gives a high-probability statement for a path of $O_k(n)$ whole-clause refreshes, then passes to individual literal edits. It makes no running-time assumption on the selector. The constants also depend on the fixed $\kappa$.

For worst-case files there is an elementary example already in 2-SAT. Enforce $x_1=\cdots=x_n$ and add the redundant clause $(x_1\lor\neg x_2)$. If the selector returns all zeros, replace the second literal by $x_2$. If it returns all ones, replace the first literal by $\neg x_1$. In either case the edited formula forces the opposite assignment, which was also a model of the original. All $n$ selected bits change after one sign edit. The work below is needed to obtain a probability bound under independent uniform random literals.

## Whole-clause endpoints of the interpolation

Take Bresler and Huang's interpolation from Definition 4.2. It refreshes the $km$ signed literals in clause-major order, independently, for $k$ complete sweeps. Retain the endpoints after each whole-clause refresh, including the initial formula. There are $km$ coarse edges. The old and fresh formulas of each single sweep are independent; their union is an iid $2m$-clause formula. For sufficiently large fixed $k$, its density lies below the known satisfiable density for even proper $(k-1)$-SAT, since $2\kappa 2^k(\log k)/k=o_k(2^{k-1})$. To check the model convention, with probability $1-O_k(1/n)$ each clause has at least $k-1$ distinct variable names: two collisions in one $k$-tuple have probability $O_k(n^{-2})$. Conditional on this event, selecting the first $k-1$ distinct names with their signs gives independent uniform proper $(k-1)$-clauses. A model of the shortened formula satisfies the union. A union bound over the fixed $k$ sweeps gives, with probability $1-o(1)$, a model for each sweep shared by **all its coarse endpoints**. [Bresler–Huang, §4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02129#page=14) · [Achlioptas–Peres, random SAT threshold lower bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0305009)

Use the constants $\beta_-<\beta_+$ of Bresler–Huang §4.4 and set $\theta=(\beta_+-\beta_-)/(2k)$. Two events from Proposition 4.7 suffice. Part (c) excludes, except with probability $\exp(-\Omega_k(n))$, the forbidden tuple of $k+1$ near-solutions anywhere on the full path. Part (b) gives the same failure bound for the event that a fresh formula a full sweep later admits a near-solution of low conditional overlap entropy relative to $k$ earlier selected outputs. Although the proposition states a low-degree hypothesis, the proof of (b) only conditions on the earlier outputs, uses independence of the fresh formula, and takes a union bound over the time tuples. It applies to any fixed selector. Part (c) concerns the formulas alone. Exact solutions are near-solutions for the positive error tolerance in the paper. [Bresler–Huang, Propositions 4.6 and 4.7 and proof of (b)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02129#page=16)

On the events just described, suppose all neighboring coarse outputs have normalized Hamming distance at most $\theta$. Start at the first endpoint. Relative to the selected outputs so far, the conditional overlap entropy of the current selected output starts at zero. One full sweep later it exceeds $\beta_+(\log k)/k$ by (b), with the last previous output repeated among the conditioning assignments if needed. On each coarse step Lemma 4.8 bounds the absolute change by the binary entropy $H(\theta)\le(\beta_+-\beta_-)(\log k)/k$ for sufficiently large $k$. Hence an intermediate coarse endpoint has conditional entropy in the closed interval from $\beta_-(\log k)/k$ to $\beta_+(\log k)/k$. Repeat $k$ times, using at most one sweep per selection. The resulting $k+1$ selected exact solutions form the tuple forbidden by (c). This is the argument of Proposition 4.6 on the coarse grid; a full sweep still has exactly $m$ grid steps. Thus some coarse edge has Hamming jump greater than $\theta n$ with probability $1-o(1)$, and its two endpoints have a common model. [Bresler–Huang, Lemma 4.8 and proof of Proposition 4.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.02129#page=17)

## A single random literal

On such a coarse edge, take its common model. Replace first a position whose **new** literal the model makes true, then replace the other positions in any order. Initially the model satisfies the old clause, and after the first replacement that new true literal is present throughout. Thus the model satisfies all $k+1$ intermediate formulas. The triangle inequality gives a literal refresh with jump greater than $\theta n/k$. The Lean theorem checks the single-position path, preservation of the common model and the Hamming jump for any selector with sufficiently distant endpoint outputs. The interpolation and probabilistic estimates above are manuscript arguments. [Lean proof](CommonWitnessLiteralPath.lean)

The order in this argument depends on the common model. To recover the prescribed random-edit law, consider all directed edges of the cube mixing the $k$ old and $k$ fresh literals in that clause. There are $2^{k-1}$ for each position. For any fixed edge of this cube, the unchanged literals are independent uniform samples, the old literal at the refreshed position is uniform, and its new literal is independently fresh. This is exactly the random-edit law at that specific occurrence. Write $p_{j,b}$ for the probability of the joint common-model and large-jump event at occurrence $b$ of clause $j$, with $c_k=\theta/k$. Each clause is refreshed $k$ times on the path. A union bound over the cube edges on all coarse steps gives

$$
 1-o(1)\ \le\ k\,2^{k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{b=1}^{k}p_{j,b}.
$$

Dividing by $km$ and the prefactor yields the claim, without any symmetry assumption on $A$. Separately, a typical edited pair shares a model: delete position $b$ in every clause of the initial formula. This iid $(k-1)$-literal formula lies below the satisfiable density at width $k-2$ by the same shortening argument. Any solution tolerates arbitrary edits in position $b$, including the specified refresh.

## Transfer to proper 3-CNF

Introduce private, distinct chain variables $z_0,\ldots,z_k$ for each source clause. Pin $z_0=1$, $z_k=0$ and include $(\neg z_{i-1}\lor\ell_i\lor z_i)$ for $1\le i\le k$. The pins can be padded to proper ternaries using two false anchors from a fixed proper three-variable gadget. Every literal occurrence appears once and every row has three distinct variables, even when the source repeats names. The endpoints of a chain extend exactly when the source clause is satisfied: drop from one to zero at a true position. For a one-literal pair with a common source model, the edited clause has a common true position, since either an unchanged occurrence is true or both old and fresh literals at the edited index are true. The two files therefore have a common chain extension.

Apply the theorem to the original-variable projection of any selector on these 3-CNFs. With equal-width space-padded signed decimal fields, a source refresh changes one literal field, occupying $O(1)$ aligned logarithmic RAM words. The files have $N=\Theta_k(n\log n)$ bits. The conclusion concerns their selected witnesses, under this induced distribution; their SAT decision is the same on the event. A linear-time RAM can change $\Theta(n)$ output bits. The witness jump alone gives no superlinear decision-time bound.

For a native search trace, suppose the final assignment occupies the same designated, initially common output words on both runs, using explicit coordinate bits. In any insertion/deletion alignment of the two traces, a differing final output word requires at least one unmatched store to that address. Otherwise its matched writes have identical data and order. Each word contains at most $w$ output bits and an instruction changes only constantly many words. The same event therefore gives trace edit distance $\Omega_k(n/w)=\Omega_k(N/\log^2 N)$ for the fixed-width 3-CNF files. A decision bit agrees on this event. The probability bound also extends to zero-error selectors run with a shared random tape, by first fixing the tape and then averaging. The argument does not give a lower bound against every possible coupling of two output distributions. [Native trace convention](realigning-a-ram-execution-after-an-input-edit.md)

Yoshida and Zhang prove sensitivity bounds for near-optimal satisfiable constraint problems using locally testable codes, including an averaged influence consequence for Max E3SAT. The distribution here is the independent random-literal distribution and the event requires a common exact witness. Priority for this particular corollary of Bresler–Huang is unestablished. [Yoshida and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31657)
