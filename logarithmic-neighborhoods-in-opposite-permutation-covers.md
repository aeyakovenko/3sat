# An algebraic successor for opposite exact-cover verdicts

The [two-permutation problem](two-permutations-and-the-explicit-sat-clock.md) asks whether $S,P(S),Q(S)$ partition a finite set. Represent the instance by its bipartite incidence graph, with variable and clause sides, and separately colored edges for the identity, $P$ and $Q$. We construct **equal-size connected pairs of opposite verdict with identical multisets of rooted colored neighborhoods out to radius $c\log q$**, where $q$ is the size of either permutation and $c>0$ is absolute. All graphs are simple at sufficiently large stages. Reduction modulo the preceding power of two gives explicit covering maps from each stage to its predecessor. One globally correct RAM program decides every instance in both sequences in linear input time, even after a common renaming of the points.

## The three-point obstruction

The smallest positive cardinality that can have a yes instance is three, since $3|S|=q$. On $\mathbb Z/3$, use shifts $(+1,+2)$ for the positive pair and $(0,+1)$ for the negative pair. One point suffices on the positive side. The negative three-point base has a repeated incidence. The separate [nine-point minimum](smallest-false-two-sheet-permutation-cover.md) assumes distinct incidences in each row and requires a feasible double cover. Consider covers with $d$ copies of each residue and permutations sending all fibers to their indicated successor fibers bijectively. On a negative cover let $u_i$ count chosen points in fiber $i$. Exact coverage requires
$$
                    2u_i+u_{i-1}=d\quad(i\bmod 3).
$$
Substitution yields $9u_i=3d$, hence $3u_i=d$. No cover of this kind can be satisfiable when $3\nmid d$. Every positive cover is satisfiable by selecting a whole residue fiber. This gives a condition that survives every successor having degree a power of two, with no need to enumerate the assignments on the successor. [Lean arithmetic checks](CubicCoverCounts.lean)

## A tower with logarithmic girth

Take the integer matrices
$$
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
For $k\ge1$ let $H_k$ be the group generated by their reductions modulo $2^k$, and $d_k=|H_k|$. This group has power-of-two order. To see this, each kernel in reduction from modulus $2^{j+1}$ to $2^j$ for $j\ge1$ consists of elements $I+2^jD$, with the multiplication of their matrices $D$ reducing to addition modulo two. The image of $H_{j+1}$ is $H_j$ and $H_1$ is trivial. Also $2^{k-1}\le d_k\le 2^{4k}$ by the order of $A$ and the number of matrices. The reduction maps have power-of-two degree.

On $\Omega_k=\mathbb Z/3\times H_k$, set
$$
 P(i,g)=(i+a,gA),\qquad Q(i,g)=(i+b,gB).
$$
Use $(a,b)=(1,2)$ and $(0,1)$ as above. The degree of the cover of its three-point instance is $d_k$. The order obstruction proves the negative verdict and the residue-zero selection proves the positive verdict at every stage. Both incidence graphs are connected. After contracting the identity matching, reachable vertices from the identity form the subgroup generated by $(a,A),(b,B)$. It projects onto both factors. Its order is a multiple of both 3 and $d_k$, and these are coprime, forcing the whole product. A covering map of pairs at successive $k$ reduces the matrix coordinate only.

For completeness, $A,B$ freely generate a group over the integers. For nonzero powers, $A^t$ sends the cone $|x|<|y|$ into $|x|>|y|$ and $B^t$ sends the latter into the former. A reduced alternating word beginning and ending in the same generator moves the opposite cone disjointly from itself. Conjugating a cyclically reduced word involving both generators by a suitable power of its first generator gives this form, choosing the power to avoid cancellation of the first syllable. Nonzero pure powers are visibly nonidentity. This is the elementary ping-pong argument behind the congruence construction. [Margulis's large-girth construction](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579283) · [Related free-matrix congruence constructions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09229)

Every simple cycle of length $L$ in our incidence graph projects to a cyclically nonbacktracking closed walk in the two-vertex base with three parallel, differently colored edges. Contracting its identity edge (a spanning tree) gives a nontrivial free word in the two remaining colors of length at most $L$. One can also see noncancellation directly: between two nonidentity edges there can be at most one identity edge, and inserting one identity edge makes the two nonidentity edges point in the same direction. Adjacent inverse letters would require immediate backtracking in the original walk. The product of the corresponding $A^{\pm1},B^{\pm1}$ differs from the identity over the integers and equals the identity modulo $2^k$. Each factor has maximum absolute row-sum norm 3. Some nonzero entry of the difference has absolute value at least $2^k$ and at most $3^L+1$. Hence $L\ge\log_3(2^k-1)$. For $q=3d_k$, this is $\Omega(\log q)$. In particular parallel edges disappear at large stages. Every radius $r$ with $2r+1$ below this bound sees the colored ball in the same bipartite cubic tree, rooted on the indicated side. Each graph has $q$ vertices on each side. The entire histograms agree even if the observer inspects the ball around every vertex and then applies an arbitrary function to the histogram. Vertex identifiers are excluded from these unlabeled isomorphism types.

## The global RAM test

For either choice of $(a,b)$, try to solve
$$
             z(Pu)=z(u)+a,\qquad z(Qu)=z(u)+b\pmod 3
$$
by propagating labels through permutations and inverses on each component. If the positive template fits, take all label-zero points; their two images have labels one and two and fill those fibers. If the negative template fits on a component, the three class sizes there coincide since $Q$ cycles them. A common size indivisible by three certifies infeasibility of that component. Feasibility factors over components; on unrecognized components a complete algorithm can use exhaustive search. The two propagation passes suffice on all displayed instances, which are connected. This takes $O(q)$ steps on array inputs after parsing. For the canonical inverse reduction to actual DIMACS files a globally correct SAT program can check the generated record format and recover the arrays in $O(N)$ or use complete SAT otherwise. If the reverse emitter simplifies or reorders clauses, use an equivalent canonical layout with one fixed, ordered record per unsimplified occurrence triple (including its order); for the simple graphs in the tail all three bits in each triple are distinct.

This is an induction on covered inputs. Its slow-observer conclusion applies to neighborhood histograms. The explicit modular-label algorithm prevents it from being an induction against arbitrary RAM programs or their time multipliers. The general two-permutation instances are still equivalent to explicit 3-SAT at each of the three target clocks. [Reproducible lift audit](permutation_cover_tower_audit.py)

The audit exhausted all 36 pairs at three points. At levels $k=4,5,6$ it constructed permutation pairs on 768, 6,144 and 49,152 points, respectively. On both verdicts their measured incidence girths were 12, 16 and 20. The logarithmic estimate for all levels follows from the matrix proof.
