# How much source computation can a short model predictor avoid?

There are linear-size native XOR computations whose compiled proper 3-CNFs have this property. For **any fixed partition** into small clause chunks, a positive fraction of the named output bits occur in more than one chunk. Predicting even three quarters of those crossing model bits on every satisfiable source requires
$$
                     b=\Omega\!\left(\frac{n\log(2+D)}{D}\right)
$$
bits of source-dependent advice when a prediction may read at most $1\le D=o(n)$ source bits adaptively. Here $n$ is the number of source bits and the physical CNF takes $\Theta(n\log n)$ bits. The entire advice is available free for each lookup and local computation is unlimited. $D$ counts the underlying bits of any probed word. The bound concerns a prediction from a fresh lookup, without source-dependent state carried between names beyond the charged advice. A separate two-block alternating algorithm decides the same promised source computations in $O(\sqrt n/W+\log n)$ logarithmic-word instructions.

## Adaptive prediction on overlapping parity supports

For a uniform source $x\in\{0,1\}^n$ let $P_i$ be its parity on a fixed set $S_i$ of positions. With advice fixed to a value $s$, the $i$th predictor is any deterministic decision tree making at most $D_i$ queries to the entire source. Put $E_i=P_i\mathbin\oplus f_{i,s}(x)$. For every nonempty $J$ satisfying
$$
              \left|\mathop{\triangle}_{i\in J}S_i\right|>\sum_{i\in J}D_i,
$$
the parity of $(E_i)_{i\in J}$ is an unbiased bit. Run the trees for the indices in $J$ in succession. Each leaf is a subcube on which all their predictions are fixed. Its transcript leaves at least one member of the symmetric difference unqueried. Flipping that bit preserves the entire adaptive transcript and pairs the two possible error parities. This proves the assertion on every reachable leaf. If the condition holds for every nonempty $J$ of size at most $k$, Fourier inversion on a $k$-coordinate Boolean cube makes the error bits $k$-wise independent and uniform for this fixed $s$.

For $g$ disjoint $d$-bit blocks and every $D_i<d$, the *entire* $g$-bit error vector is uniform for each fixed advice. With a source-dependent choice of $b$ advice bits, the fraction of inputs on which at most $h$ errors occur is therefore at most
$$
         2^{b-g}\sum_{j=0}^h\binom gj.
$$
In particular, exact success is at most $2^{b-g}$. Conversely one can store $b\le g$ actual parities and answer zero on the other $g-b$ to attain that exact success fraction. For the average error fraction $\varepsilon\le1/2$, the selected error vector has no atom of mass above $2^{b-g}$, hence entropy at least $g-b$. Subadditivity and concavity bound its entropy by $gH_2(\varepsilon)$. Conditioning on the total source parity or supplying one source-dependent sign bit costs at most one further bit in the fraction and entropy bounds. The disjoint exact case is classical. These arguments extend it to error tolerance and to the stated symmetric-difference condition. [Nisan, Rudich and Saks, *Products and Help Bits in Decision Trees*](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0097539795282444) · [Gál and Miltersen, systematic data structures](https://brics.dk/RS/03/44/BRICS-RS-03-44.pdf) · [Query-with-sketch lower bounds, CCC 2026](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2026.41)

## Linear-size computation with many hard local predictions

Use a binary linear encoder $G:\mathbb F_2^v\to\mathbb F_2^l$ of constant rate and relative distance, with a linear-size XOR and fanout circuit. Such encoders and linear-size circuits for their transposes are established. If needed, restrict the message to a prefix of its coordinates, fixing the rest to zero, so the dimension plus one has the form $q=2s^2$ and remains $\Theta(l)$. Let $n=2l$. For $u\in\mathbb F_2^q$, put $b'=u_q$, $z_i=u_i+b'$ for $i<q$, and define
$$
                   H(u)=(Gz,\ Gz+b'\mathbf 1_l).
$$
This uses $O(n)$ XORs, retains distance at least $\min(2\,\mathrm{dist}(G),l)=\delta n$ for fixed $\delta>0$, and has $H(\mathbf 1_q)=(0,\mathbf 1_l)$. Its transpose computes $q=\Theta(n)$ parity labels $y=H^T x$ in $O(n)$ XOR and fanout gates. For every nonempty $J$, the symmetric difference of the supports of its $y_i$ is the support of $H(\mathbf 1_J)$ and has size at least $\delta n$. [Ishai, Kushilevitz, Ostrovsky and Sahai, Theorem 3.2 and circuit transposition](https://web.cs.ucla.edu/~sahai/work/web/2008%20Publications/STOC2008.pdf#page=5)

Make a public loop-free 17-regular edge expander on these $q$ output names. Start with the eight Margulis–Gabber–Galil shifts on $\mathbb Z_s^2$, add eight identity edges for laziness, and take the bipartite double cover. Its adjacency eigenvalues are the positive and negative eigenvalues of the lazy walk. The eigenvalue $-1$ permitted by bipartiteness does not affect the upper bound on the second-largest eigenvalue used for edge expansion. Adding the identity matching between the two layers gives odd degree 17 and preserves the unnormalized edge-boundary lower bound $|\partial U|\ge h|U|$ for $|U|\le q/2$, $h>0$. Parallel edges are distinct gate inputs with separate output names. [Gabber and Galil](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4) · [Earlier use of the lazy double cover](scan-time-on-a-counting-width-hard-sat-pair.md)

For each edge $e=uv$ compute a fresh $z_e=y_u\oplus y_v$. XOR all $z_e$ and compare with the requested sign. An ordinary native execution reads the gate list in topological order, stores each output, loads each edge's two endpoints and accumulates the result in $O(n)$ word instructions given the indexed public list. Compile these actual operations as circuit equations. Pin three false anchors by seven proper clauses, pin each source variable with a signed ternary clause, use four proper clauses for each XOR, and use a false anchor for each equality to a fresh wire. Tied or degenerate linear inputs in the public encoder can first be simplified or given fresh equal aliases. Relate the final bit to two complementary names numbered 4 and 5 and choose which to pin with the final clause. The sign changes one physical ASCII bit. Pad a missing sign in each data pin with a space, so source values do not move the offsets of any later field. Every coordinate of $y$ is forced in every model on a satisfying orientation. The public circuit can be supplied separately or fixed at a length. No polylogarithmic gate-index uniformity of the imported code is needed for the model-prediction lower bound.

Fix *any* partition of the clause positions into parts containing at most $q/4$ clauses, independent of the source values. Mark one of the four XOR clauses for each edge, which contains both its endpoints. If the marked clauses incident to $v$ occupy different parts, then $y_v$ is a genuine cross-chunk name. Let $C$ be the set of such vertices. The other vertices fall into groups by their common part; each group has at most $2(q/4)$ vertices since each designated row contains only two endpoint names. No edge connects two different groups. Summing the expansion bound over groups yields $h(q-|C|)\le17|C|$. Thus $M=|C|=\Omega(n)$, for each fixed partition even if its parts are noncontiguous. A partition encoding source-dependent information would have to be budgeted separately.

Fix any $b$-bit advice value. On these $M$ names the errors are $k$-wise uniform whenever $kD<\delta n$. For even $k\le M$, the $k$th moment of the signed error sum $Z=\sum_{i\in C}(-1)^{E_i}$ equals the independent moment. In its expansion, tuples with any odd multiplicity contribute zero. Pairing the remaining positions gives the elementary bound $\mathbb E Z^k\le (k-1)!! M^{k/2}\le(kM)^{k/2}$. For a fixed $0\le\varepsilon<1/2$,
$$
 \Pr[\text{at most }\varepsilon M\text{ errors with any of the advice values}]
 \;\le\; 2^b\left(\frac{k}{(1-2\varepsilon)^2 M}\right)^{k/2}.
$$
This union bound permits arbitrary source-dependent selection of the advice. For fixed $\varepsilon$ choose an even $k$ a sufficiently small constant times $n/(D+1)$, so $kD<\delta n$ and $k/((1-2\varepsilon)^2M)$ is bounded below one even for small $D$. For $1\le D=o(n)$ the exponent saved is $\Omega(n\log(2+D)/D)$. Conditioning on either fixed satisfying sign costs at most a factor two since the final sign is balanced. The stated advice lower bound follows, even to succeed on a fixed positive fraction of those files. It applies in particular with $\varepsilon=1/4$. A logarithmic-word probe can reveal as many as $W$ source bits; the bound then uses $D=W$ times the number of word probes.

## An explicit faster simulation on the identical source family

In the XOR over edges, each $y_v$ occurs an odd number of times, so the aggregate equals $\bigoplus_v y_v$. By $H(\mathbf 1_q)=(0,\mathbf 1_l)$ this is precisely the parity of the **second half of the source**. The native computation therefore has an alternative two-block verifier which never predicts a crossing model label. Given the source and requested sign, existentially guess the parity of every consecutive block of $\lceil\sqrt n\rceil$ bits in its second half. Check the XOR of the proposed bits against the sign. Universally select one block and recompute its parity from the actual source. The two tests are sound and complete on both signs. Guess and store packed words, XOR them, then fold the final word with shifts in $O(\log W)$ steps. The same calculation for the one selected block, including masks for its boundary words, gives $O(\sqrt n/W+\log n)$ steps on a path. This is a uniform verifier of the promised source language, including when the fixed code lists vary: the algebraic identity holds for every such list. It presumes the source presentation or the specified canonical compiler. An arbitrary externally supplied circuit cannot be taken on trust to be one of those lists. A complete explicit-SAT solver can validate the family and fall back on general SAT, which requires accounting for the physical file.

There is also a uniformly locally indexed, decision-preserving **replacement compiler** on these source computations. For any fixed positive integer $a$, divide the relevant source half into blocks of $B=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil^{a+1}$ bits. Emit one proper ternary pin for each block parity, check their XOR by a chain and pin its output to the requested sign. A small fixed guard gadget handles empty or one-block cases. Give each public clause a predetermined padded stride of $O(\log n)$ bits, with explicit sign columns. A requested byte uses arithmetic on its offset and at most one block of source bits, costing $O(B+\operatorname{polylog}n)$ time, including formatting its names. The output has $O((1+n/B)\log(n+2))=O(n/\log^a n)$ bits for large $n$. This compiler is uniform in the source bits and bypasses both the original gate list and its difficult-to-predict shared model. A generic observation bounds its possible scaling: if length and each bit of a decision-preserving output on source parity require at most $D$ source-bit queries, reading an output of length $M$ and solving it with unlimited local computation takes at most $(M+1)D$ queries. Correctness forces every one of the $n/2$ relevant input bits to be inspected on each source, giving $(M+1)D\ge n/2$. The restriction of the source program to this algebraic family is essential. The original compact-compiler speedup for general bounded RAM acceptance still lacks such a compiler. [General compact-compiler threshold](compact-compilers-and-linear-sat-time.md)

A simpler fully indexed instance uses $q=2s^2$ vertices for odd $s$, with $2s$ independent blocks of $s$ bits and $s$ vertex copies of each block parity. Its edge aggregate is total source parity, whose same alternating verifier applies. At least a constant fraction of the source groups have a constant fraction of their copies crossing, by the expansion count. On any one selected copy per such group, the disjoint-block error theorem holds for all $D<s$. Choosing a copy uniformly in each such group transfers small aggregate crossing error to these distinct source groups. This simpler circuit already prevents accurate lookup with $o(s)$ advice. The coded construction strengthens the bound at polylogarithmic probe budgets.

The computation supplies an unconditional counterexample to a proposed compilation shortcut based on locally predicting almost every cross-chunk name with very short advice. Its semantic speedup also explains why a lower bound on reconstructing the model cannot by itself price every SAT decider on these files. In particular the near-linear advice bound above measures source information needed at a local lookup budget; it makes no native SAT time separation. Priority for this combination of known ingredients has not been established.
