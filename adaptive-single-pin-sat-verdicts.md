# Adaptive SAT verdict queries with persistent workspace

Let $m$ be even and let $k$ divide $m$. A common proper three-CNF core contains the sign-fixed unit gadgets requiring $x_{\pi(i)}$ at each even position and $\neg x_{\pi(i)}$ at each odd position of a permutation $\pi$. Variant $y$ adds the positive pin $x_y$. Its SAT verdict is the parity test $[\pi^{-1}(y)\text{ is even}]$. Four proper clauses implement a unit, and three shared auxiliary names suffice to keep all clauses distinct. There is a dense fixed-offset ASCII encoding of $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ bits in which one packed word depends on $O(1)$ forward entries. [Encoding and pin-equivalence proof](batched-last-writers-and-public-forward-probes.md#a-bound-for-single-pin-sat-verdicts)

The decoder receives one $S$-bit common message after preprocessing the core. Its targets are the consecutive keys partitioned into blocks of $k$. Each block may also receive a fixed-width $b$-bit message chosen with knowledge of the entire input. Messages and guesses may use independent random tapes shared with preprocessing. The decoder is granted every genuine full inverse index from earlier blocks. The linear-advice lower bound at constant probes per target holds with **fully adaptive reads and unlimited retained private state**. In this section each block may make $q\ge k$ arbitrary forward-entry probes, whose addresses depend on previous answers and advice. Only input access is charged. All computation and private storage are free, and the decoder starts without any permutation-dependent state beyond its messages. Let $\Delta=1/2-\varepsilon>0$, where the expected verdict error fraction of every block on every permutation is at most $\varepsilon$. If $q\le\delta m$ with $\delta=\min(1/256,\Delta/32)$, put $\ell=\lfloor\delta m/q\rfloor$ and $s=k\ell$. Then
$$
  S+\ell b\ \ge\ \frac{9\Delta^2}{128\ln 2}s-\log_2(4/\Delta),\qquad
  s=\Theta_{\Delta}(mk/q).
$$
For $q=O(k)$ and $q=o(m)$ this requires $\Omega(m)$ preprocessing bits when $b\le c'_{\Delta}k$. At error $1/3$ the explicit condition $q\le m/256$ suffices. The same asymptotic consequence holds for $O(k)$ arbitrary packed-word probes to the proper DIMACS core, with the word-to-entry constant counted in $q$. This conclusion permits arbitrary probes from arbitrarily large public menus and private probe outcomes carried forward. For growing $q/k$ the fully adaptive argument gives no extra factor $\log(2+q/k)$.

## Adaptive chronological exposure

Choose a uniformly random permutation and collect the first $\ell$ blocks. Fix all random tapes and a particular common string and local-message tuple, granting all the messages at the start. The resulting deterministic process receives at most $J=q\ell\le\delta m$ raw probes and predicts $s\le J$ verdicts. Allow its guesses to be scored in key order and reveal each true full index immediately after its guess. This already simulates an algorithm that makes its joint predictions at the end of each block. If the procedure exceeds its budget on an incorrect message tuple, truncate that run and complete it with fixed guesses. This leaves its fully correct-message behavior available. Represent every revelation by a pair $(i,\pi(i))$ and keep the partial bijection $D$ of distinct revealed pairs. At **every** reachable event transcript the undisclosed permutation is uniform over completions of $D$. At the root this is true by construction. Given any fixed transcript, the next action and its address or guess are determined. A new forward probe chooses an unused row and exposes a uniform unused label. A genuine inverse reveal chooses a known label and, if needed, exposes a uniform unused row. Recording either new pair leaves all completions equiprobable. Previously exposed pairs give deterministic replies. This induction applies at stopping transcripts chosen using past values, including when a controller elects to stop probing only after discovering that a future target is already known. At most $s+J$ distinct pairs have been exposed.

Say a predicted key is *hit* if its image was already exposed by a forward probe. At any one forward probe the probability of a new hit on an unscored key among the first $s$ is at most $s/(m-J-s)$. A union bound over subsets of probe times gives, for $a=\lceil\Delta s/4\rceil$,
$$
  \Pr[\#\mathrm{hits}\ge a]\le {J\choose a}
    \left(\frac{s}{m-J-s}\right)^a
  \le \left(\frac{4e\delta}{\Delta(1-2\delta)}\right)^a.
$$
For this estimate index times by raw-probe number, padding with no-op probes when needed. Condition just before each raw probe, after all intervening predictions and inverse revelations. For any fixed subset of $a$ probe numbers, successive conditioning bounds its probability of all new hits by $(s/(m-J-s))^a$. A key previously revealed by the scoring oracle, even if later probed, contributes no hit. Each scored key already known in advance consumes a different forward hit. Repeated or unused probes add none.

For guess $j$, let $\mathcal F_{j-1}$ be the transcript just after the preceding scored index reveal, $\mathcal H_j$ the transcript just before the genuine index of $j$ is revealed, including the prediction itself, and $\mathcal F_j$ the transcript immediately after that reveal. Thus $\mathcal F_{j-1}\subseteq\mathcal H_j\subseteq\mathcal F_j$. The transcript between scores can contain any allowed adaptive probes. If the original decoder emitted a whole block of guesses at once, keep its vector fixed during that block's score reveals. If its joint output was computed using probes made late in the block, include those probes before the first score. Granting interleaved scoring and new probes only strengthens it. On an unhit key, conditional on $\mathcal H_j$, the target index is uniform among at least $m-J-s$ unused positions. Its predicted parity succeeds with probability at most $p=1/(2(1-2\delta))$, since there are at most $m/2$ positions of either color. Let $I_j$ be the indicator that key $j$ is unhit and guessed correctly. Its success probability given $\mathcal H_j$ is at most $p$, with probability zero for an already-hit key. The tower property gives the same bound conditional on $\mathcal F_{j-1}$. Iterating the conditional bound $\mathbf E[e^{t I_j}\mid\mathrm{past}]\le1-p+pe^t$ for $t>0$ gives the usual binomial upper tail, even with intervening adaptive probes. Since $p\le1/2+\Delta/16$, the chance $\sum_j I_j\ge(1/2+\Delta/4)s$ is $\exp(-\Omega_{\Delta}(s))$. Hoeffding’s bound from the displayed moment estimate gives at most $\exp(-2(3\Delta/16)^2s)$. The hit bound has base at most $e/[8(1-2\delta)]<1/2$, so it is at most $\exp(-(\ln 2)\Delta s/4)$. For $0<\Delta\le1/2$, the probability of at least $(1/2+\Delta/2)s$ correct verdicts with this **fixed** message tuple is therefore at most $2\exp(-9\Delta^2s/128)$.

Under the asserted decoder, the expected number correct is at least $(1/2+\Delta)s$, so it reaches the lower threshold with probability at least $\Delta/2$ over the input and coins. For each fixed random tape, take the union over all $2^{S+\ell b}$ tuples, then average over the tapes. The claimed lower bound follows. It already suffices to require the expected error promise on uniformly random cores on average over these early blocks. The initially fixed messages in this calculation are potential values, not conditioning events; conditioning on the actual input-dependent advice would invalidate deferred decisions.

For a single target and no advice under the uniform permutation, the exact optimal success is $\min(1,1/2+q/m)$. With $q\le m/2$, probing $q$ even positions and guessing odd on a miss attains this. On any path with no hit, at least $m/2-q$ of the remaining $m-q$ positions have each color, even after observing the actual other labels. Padding to $q$ distinct probes makes the no-hit probability exactly $(m-q)/m$, giving the converse. Thus an adaptive lower bound for advantage $\Delta$ must account for the ratio $q/m$. The balanced single-target inverse predicate itself is classical. Up to relabeling the positions, it is the predicate whose randomized query complexity Aaronson lower-bounded by a linear function. [Aaronson, Section 4](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/pls.pdf)

## Finite verification

An exact dynamic program maximizes the probability of reaching each requested number of correct predictions, with arbitrary adaptive probes before or between predictions and all true past indices supplied. Its state keeps unused row counts of both parities, the subset of upcoming keys not yet discovered, remaining budget and remaining required successes. It compares guessing either parity now against probing a fresh row of either parity now. A separate Bellman search keeps the literal set of compatible permutations and branches on every distinct possible probe reply and inverse reveal. The two optimal probabilities agree in 59 parameter settings. An additional 315 comparisons test the two-binomial tail bound against the optimal causal policy through size 64, including 225 upper bounds below one. [Optimal-policy audit](adaptive_pin_verdict_audit.py)

The bound applies to batched decision under the stated response deadlines. A driver with enough time to read the common permutation before its first answer computes all verdicts in one scatter. The native single-formula problem therefore has a scan algorithm on this family. Prior work studied decision function inversion as image membership with preprocessing and search-to-decision reductions. [Golovnev et al., Sections 1.1 and 2.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/145/revision/1/download/)
