# Deterministically estimating MST preferences by portal bottlenecks

In a round of Khanna, Putterman and Song's certificate procedure, let $F$ be the current forest on $v$ vertices, $k$ the number of distinct portals referenced by the current spokes and $a$ the number of active edges. For integer $t\ge1$, all $a$ preferred actions can be estimated with at most $a/t$ mistakes using
$$
                  O\big(v+k\log(k+1)+v\log(t+1)\big)
$$
**weight comparisons**, deterministically. Exact actions cost $O(v\log(k+2))$ comparisons. The first bound can improve the training cost on a dense round with few portals. It makes no claim about an improved overall MST bound or the cost of explicitly outputting $a$ answers. The construction can use comparisons of a spoke to a selected bottleneck threshold, a stronger operation than reading only the preferred action at selected edges. [DLMST, Algorithm 1 and Claim 3.12](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.31187v1#S3.SS5)

## Why one threshold per portal pair suffices

Every defined spoke $\sigma(u)$ joining $u$ to $\pi(u)$ is supported by $F$: the two endpoints are connected in $F$ at threshold $s(u)=w(\sigma(u))$. At creation it is a selected edge in the declared set $D$ in §3.2. The update replaces $F\cup D$ by its minimum spanning forest, preserving every threshold component. Subsequent updates likewise take an MSF of a superset of the previous forest. For two endpoints with defined spokes, put $p=\max(s(u),s(v))$. At threshold $p$ each endpoint is in the component of its portal. Therefore, for distinct portals in the same forest component, the true preferred action is find exactly when
$$
 b(\pi(u),\pi(v))\le s(u)\quad\text{or}\quad b(\pi(u),\pi(v))\le s(v),
$$
where $b$ is the maximum weight on the path **between the portals**. Missing spokes, identical portals and disconnected components have the constant actions given by the paper.

Prune all nonportal leaves from $F$, then suppress degree-two nonportal vertices, giving each compressed edge the maximum weight on its chain. Components with at most one portal have no pair to process. A tree with $j\ge2$ marked vertices has at most $j-2$ additional branching vertices, so there are $O(k)$ compressed edges in total. Computing their keys uses $O(v)$ comparisons because the chains are edge-disjoint. Sort those keys using $O(k\log(k+1))$ comparisons. The bottleneck for any pair now has a known **integer rank** obtained from the compressed path. All the following grouping and sorting of these ranks is on public indices and the outcomes already obtained; it needs no additional edge-weight comparisons.

For each vertex $u$, collect the bottleneck ranks of its still-undetermined incident active edges, retaining multiplicities. Let $N_u$ be their number. In the sorted list mark positions at every $\lfloor N_u/(2t)\rfloor+1$ steps. There are at most $2t$ marked positions. Binary search among the marked keys using the weight $s(u)$. This locates its threshold cut up to a run of at most $\lfloor N_u/(2t)\rfloor$ unmarked occurrences, including at the two ends. Equal keys cause no difficulty because the predicate is the weak comparison and binary search can take the last passing mark. Round its cut down to this mark, or to position zero. This rounds down a true endpoint comparison on at most that many occurrences. An endpoint with $N_u=0$ needs no estimate. The total error in the disjunctions is at most $\sum_u N_u/(2t)\le a/t$. Replacing the marked lists by binary search among all $O(k)$ distinct compressed ranks gives the exact bound instead.

## A limit for exact comparison savings

Consider one portal $p$ with $b$ left leaves. Their spokes to $p$ carry independent keys $s_i$. Join $p$ to $k-1$ other portals by edges of ordered keys $x_1<\cdots<x_{k-1}$. At each other portal attach a right leaf with a spoke lighter than all those keys and all $s_i$. Take as active edges all left-right pairs. Their true preferences give the comparisons $s_i\ge x_j$. Each left leaf independently realizes any of $k$ gaps among the known keys, producing $k^b$ distinct full preference vectors on this fixed forest. A binary weight-comparison algorithm recovering all actions needs at least $b\log_2 k$ comparisons on some such input, even when given the order of the $x_j$. Unique keys can be chosen throughout. This lower bound is for conditioned, threshold-supported snapshots. Reachability of every such snapshot under the recursive schedule and portal-selection rules of Algorithm 1 is a separate assertion.

The change of endpoints to portals uses support of every named spoke at its own threshold. This is a specific property a proposed SAT or memory-history analogue would have to provide. For unrestricted RAM addresses, knowing the final name of a cell does not identify the values or overwrites through which its contents were obtained.
