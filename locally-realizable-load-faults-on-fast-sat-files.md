# A false SAT trace can pass every half-bank test by genuine input edits

For every power of two $m=2^r\ge4$, there is a fixed layout of ordinary, proper DIMACS 3-CNFs of bit length $N=\Theta(m\log m)$ and one fixed, globally correct deterministic RAM solver taking $O(N)$ time on every layout member. Its last phase reads two banks of $m$ work words at fixed addresses and accepts if any pair differs. Every word is loaded once in that phase. Its replies are data-bearing: for each word all $m$ field values occur on genuine inputs.

On **each** unsatisfiable member, changing just one of these $2m$ work replies to any different field value gives a false accepting candidate with a singleton last-write conflict. On a candidate's own chronological trace, every other work reply equals the actual latest store or the initialized zero. Nevertheless:

* Holding the entire opposite bank fixed, every prescription on any $s\le m/2$ positions of the altered bank occurs on a genuine file reached with at most $s$ edits to input fields. For $b$ prescribed comparison bits in such a set of words, at most $b$ single-bit edits to the physical ASCII file suffice. If the prescription includes the distinct altered value, the completing file is necessarily satisfiable.
* Exactly $m/2+1$ selected word values in the altered bank suffice, in the best case, to refute any genuine completion of the full one-fault vector. This is a certificate count for observations of that bank, with the other bank known.
* The closest genuine **satisfiable** comparison transcript differs from the fabricated one at exactly $m/2$ word replies. Editing a single source field attains this minimum. For a fabricated low-bit toggle at **any** of the $2m$ addresses, toggling **one physical input bit** already attains the minimum and reproduces the changed low bit. No number of input edits makes the complete fabricated comparison vector genuine.
* The minimum set of work addresses whose consistency forbids every false accepting candidate on the unsatisfiable member is $\Theta(m)$. In particular the $2m$ one-fault candidates all have distinct singleton conflict sets.

These statements concern the fixed address schedule and reply vector of the final comparison. The earlier controller, reads and field-computation trace may change under an input edit. Requiring the entire preceding native trace to remain identical would already force the same next work reply by determinism and the last-write rule. The new obstruction persists after discarding that entire preceding history. It does not require constant-valued sentinel words. ## Ordinary SAT instance and genuine comparison

Let $q=m$, $K=\mathbb F_q$, and $k=m/2$. The two private lists $u,v\in K^k$ are represented by the field elements' distinct $r$-bit integer names. The SAT bit is $[u\ne v]$. For a direct encoding introduce Boolean names $X_s$ for the field symbols. Require that at least one selector $h_i$ is true, using an OR chain and its output pin. At each position add exactly the two data clauses $(\neg h_i\lor X_{u_i})$ and $(\neg h_i\lor\neg X_{v_i})$. Equal lists preclude selecting any index. At a differing index select just that one and set the two distinct $X$ variables as required. Pad width-two and unit clauses with fixed false variables to three distinct variables per clause, forcing a fresh triple of false variables with the seven clauses excluding every other triple. Choose disjoint selector, gate and data names.

The sole occurrence of the source field $s$ in its list slot uses a positive decimal identifier consisting of a leading digit 1 followed by its $r$ binary digits, each written as an ASCII decimal 0 or 1. The number is at most $2\cdot 10^r=m^{O(1)}$. Other name banks and the header can use polynomially many labels as a numeric range, most of them unused. Every single *bit* of $s$ now changes exactly one low bit in the file, at the last bit of its ASCII digit. The positions, width and total length of the entire valid formula are unchanged. This is a bitwise projection of the $2kr$ independent source bits. [Related explicit inequality layout](sparse-cut-memory-and-the-price-of-zero-reads.md)

Use the same public field presentation on the entire layout at a given $m$. Label the elements $t_0,\ldots,t_{m-1}$ in binary basis order. Given $u$, let $f_u$ be the unique polynomial of degree less than $k$ with $f_u(t_i)=u_i$ for $0\le i<k$. Use a publicly scaled evaluation $C(u)_i=g_i f_u(t_i)$ at all $m$ points, and define $C(v)$ the same way. Put $g_i=1$ at the systematic points. Elsewhere use $g_i=1/\lambda_0(t_i)$, with $\lambda_0$ the Lagrange polynomial for systematic slot zero. Its only roots are the other $k-1$ systematic points, so these factors exist. This coordinate scaling preserves all distances and interpolation properties. The last phase reads the two entire evaluations. Accumulate a bitwise OR of the XORs in a register and test for nonzero only after the last pair. The comparison has fixed control and address order up to its final verdict. Its result is $[u\ne v]$ by systematicity. [Reed and Solomon, *Polynomial Codes Over Certain Finite Fields*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0108018)

For the unedited diagonal $u=v$, write $c=C(u)$. Replace one loaded word $c_i$ on the left by a distinct value $w$. The registers downstream of that load compute a nonzero comparison, while all later work addresses and their replies stay the same. Its only inconsistent address is the one from which $c_i$ was just loaded. The other bank gives the other $m$ singleton conflicts. Any set hitting every false acceptance must contain all $2m$ addresses. Conversely the solver described below uses only $O(m)$ distinct work addresses in its entire honest run on these inputs. Enforcing exact reads at every such address forces its genuine rejection through the halt, even if the public cutoff is longer.

## Sharp completion and distances

For any $s\le k$ selected evaluation points, the restriction of $C$ to them is surjective by undoing the nonzero scalings and interpolating a polynomial of degree less than $s$. Viewed in systematic coordinates, this is a linear map of rank $s$ from $K^k$. Select $s$ independent columns. Any demanded change of its $s$ outputs can be implemented by editing only the associated $s$ source symbols. Thus fixing other, previously inspected values *among these selected points* is allowed. In binary coordinates the same map is a surjective linear map from $kr$ bits to $sr$ bits. Restricting again to $b$ queried output bits yields rank $b$. Choose $b$ independent binary columns and edit at most those $b$ physical input bits. This includes all tests of at most $k$ arbitrary comparison bits. This argument holds separately in each bank and also for adaptively selected points once their addresses and proposed replies are fixed. For $s<k$ an unchanged local prescription always has a distinct completing left polynomial if desired; a nonzero polynomial vanishing on those $s$ points has degree at most $s$, and a support of at most $s+1$ systematic edits can be obtained from a linear dependence among $s+1$ columns.

Now select the false coordinate $i$ and $k$ other positions. A polynomial agreeing with $c$ at the other $k$ points must equal $f_u$, contradicting the prescribed $w$ at $i$. Conversely any $k$ observed values have a genuine completion by the preceding paragraph. The threshold $k+1$ concerns proving incompatibility with every genuine file. If only satisfiable files are allowed, observing $k$ *unaltered* positions can already force equality to the known right bank.

Distinct codewords differ on at least $m-k+1$ symbols because a nonzero degree-$<k$ polynomial has at most $k-1$ roots. For any genuine positive pair $(c',d')$ from this public layout, $c'\ne d'$, so at least one differs from $c$ in $m-k+1$ or more positions. Passing from $(c,c)$ to the one-fault vector saves at most one word of distance. This gives a lower bound of $m-k$. Equivalently, every genuine positive comparison even under a different valid field presentation at the same $m$ differs between the two banks at at least $m-k+1$ indices, while the fabricated comparison differs at just one. This pairwise mismatch count also proves the same lower bound if the public field parameter is edited.

For the upper bound choose a systematic point $j$ whose Lagrange basis polynomial $\lambda_j$ is nonzero at $t_i$. If $i$ is systematic take $j=i$. Otherwise every $j$ works, since the $k-1$ roots of $\lambda_j$ are precisely the other systematic points. Change $u_j$ by $(w-c_i)/(g_i\lambda_j(t_i))$ and keep $v$ fixed. The resulting evaluation matches the altered word at $i$ and differs from the original in exactly $m-k+1$ places. Its distance from the fabricated left bank is exactly $m-k$. For the low-bit fault $w=c_i+1$, choose $j=i$ at systematic positions or $j=0$ outside them. In either case $g_i\lambda_j(t_i)=1$, so the source edit is exactly one low-bit toggle at $u_j$, changing precisely one bit in the ASCII file. It changes SAT to positive and realizes the exact false reply there. Any real such completion must still differ from the fabricated comparison vector at at least $m-k$ other work words.

## Retaining part of the actual prefix

Suppose the stitching test also retains $d<k$ fixed field-linear observations of the original left input, of rank $d$. These can include raw source fields or intermediate linear forms of the transform. The support of the right bank stays fixed. Let $s+d\le k$. Among the $\binom ms$ possible sets of $s$ later left-bank reads, at least $\binom{m-d}s$ permit **every** assignment of their answers while retaining all $d$ prefix values. Each assignment can be realized with at most $s+d$ changed left source fields. The count is exact in the worst case over linear summaries.

Indeed the evaluation vectors have the property that every $k$ of them are independent. Start with the span of the $d$ observed linear forms. After choosing $j<s$ new independent evaluation vectors the span has dimension $d+j<k$ and contains at most $d+j$ of the $m$ native evaluation vectors. There are therefore at least $m-d-j$ choices for the next new read. Multiply and divide by $s!$. For every qualifying set the combined restriction map has rank $d+s$, so $d+s$ independent source columns suffice to meet the old and new conditions. If the prefix observations are $d$ actual systematic entries, any disjoint $s$ positions work and no set hitting one of those $d$ positions allows every assignment. Keeping these systematic entries as frozen coordinates, the remaining columns already span the $s$ new outputs, so at most $s$ *unfrozen* source fields need to change. In particular, retaining $d$ observed raw words together with $s$ later observations still cannot diagnose a known singleton error when $d+s\le k$, provided the new faulty coordinate is not one of the frozen ones.

There is also a distributional statement for a fixed, arbitrary nonlinear $b$-bit summary $Z(U)$ of the **left source alone**. Take $U$ uniform in $K^k$, and take a uniformly random set $J$ of $s\le k$ comparison positions independently. Mark one position by any rule depending only on $J$. Retain its high bits and the other $s-1$ whole words, and flip its low bit. The probability that no genuine left input both realizes these local replies and preserves $Z(U)$ is at most
$$
 \min(1,sb/k).
$$
The right bank may then be fixed at its original value, on the diagonal if desired. Whenever a completion exists its altered comparison bit ensures that this edited file is satisfiable on the diagonal. There is no bound here on the number of input edits for a nonlinear summary. If the summary has beforehand seen both sources, a single equality bit can isolate an entire diagonal when the right bank is held fixed.

For fixed $J$ the $sr$ comparison bits are independent uniform bits. Write them as $(W,D)$ with $D$ the targeted bit. Conditioned on $(W,Z)$, a missing opposite bit means $D$ is deterministic, so its failure probability is at most $1-H(D\mid W,Z)\le I(C(U)_J;Z)$ in bits. To average the right side, take a uniform $k$-set $K_0$, then a uniform $s$-subset $J$ of it. By the conditional entropy subset inequality,
$\mathbb E_{J\subseteq K_0} H(C(U)_J\mid Z)\ge(s/k)H(C(U)_{K_0}\mid Z)$.
For example, order $K_0$ and expand in conditional entropies; a selected coordinate has at least the entropy obtained by conditioning on *all* preceding coordinates, and each index is selected with probability $s/k$. The $k$ coordinates determine the whole uniform source, so their changed entropy is at most $H(Z)\le b$. This proves the bound. It concerns a uniformly selected observation set, and gives no worst-case guarantee for a set chosen after viewing the nonlinear summary.

## Uniform cost of computing both evaluation banks

The fast path validates the entire template and its parameters. A public DIMACS comment may supply the degree-$r$ polynomial specifying a primitive binary field representation. For size checking use the physical file length before allocating a proposed table. Reject a malformed fast-path layout into an ordinary complete SAT fallback. For a proposed monic polynomial with constant term one, iterate multiplication by $x$ modulo it using a shift and conditional XOR. Require that starting from one it first returns to one after exactly $2^r-1$ steps. Multiplication by $x$ is a permutation in this quotient, so this condition visits every nonzero residue and proves the quotient is a field. The loop builds its logarithm and antilogarithm tables in $O(m)$ work. Primitive polynomials exist at every degree. The comment can be chosen independently for each public scale and a single uniform program validates it. All layout members share it. An input reaching this particular comparison must pass the same validation. When a file is outside the fast path the separate fallback does not execute this comparison phase.

In the field's binary vector basis $\beta_0,\ldots,\beta_{r-1}$ let $V_j$ be the span of the first $j$ vectors, and $s_j(x)=\prod_{t\in V_j}(x-t)$. The identity $s_{j+1}=s_j^2+s_j(\beta_j)s_j$ shows inductively that these polynomials are additive, nonzero at $\beta_j$ and of degree $2^j$. Normalize $a_j=s_j/s_j(\beta_j)$ and use the degree-ordered basis $\phi_i=\prod_{j:\operatorname{bit}_j(i)=1}a_j$. A degree-$<2^j$ polynomial splits as $f_0+a_{j-1}f_1$ with each half represented by $2^{j-1}$ coefficients. On the two half-cosets of an evaluation domain $t+V_j$ the multiplier has fixed values $d$ and $d+1$. The coefficient pairs therefore transform to $f_0+d f_1$ and $f_0+(d+1)f_1$ and evaluation recurses independently. The pair map is invertible by adding its two outputs to recover $f_1$ and substituting to recover $f_0$. A transform or its inverse costs $O(m\log m)$ field operations. The $s_j(\beta_i)$ are precomputed for all basis elements in $O(r^2)$ operations by the same recurrence. Node offsets $d$ take at most $O(r)$ further operations each by additivity. Log tables make every field operation constant word cost. This is the established additive FFT in the degree-ordered polynomial basis. [Lin, Chung and Han, FOCS 2014](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2014-Papers/6517a316.pdf)

Invert the transform on $V_{r-1}$ to interpolate each systematic message, append zero coefficients up to length $m$, and apply the forward transform on $V_r$. In-place pair updates followed by recursion on disjoint halves use $O(m)$ work words, including field tables and auxiliary buffers. An additional encoding of the first systematic unit vector produces all $\lambda_0(t_i)$ for the scale factors. Apply them in $O(m)$ further operations before the final scan. The ASCII parsing, with constant work per byte, and the two encodings together take $O(m\log m)=O(N)$ native steps on this promise.

There is also a version taking $O(N/\log N)$ word steps on all its members. Increase the physical size to $N=\Theta(m\log^2 m)$ by a fixed comment row of repeated printable bytes. Validate its body against a repeated-byte word constant in packed blocks, handling the prefix, final fragment and newline separately. The older $O(m\log m)$ computation and the $O(N/\log N)$ padding scan fit the same time budget. The $\Theta(m)$ certificate is then $\Theta(N/\log^2 N)$. This calibration shows why large certificates and even linear-wise realizability of their individual faults do not by themselves yield superlinear time on arbitrary SAT programs.
