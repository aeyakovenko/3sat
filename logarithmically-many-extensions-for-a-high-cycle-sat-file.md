# Logarithmically many extensions suffice for a SAT file that needs many cycles

For an explicit SAT source of encoded length $n$, there is a deterministic $O(n)$-time reduction to proper ternary DIMACS of encoded length $N=\Theta(n+1)$ with the following properties. Every satisfying source assignment has exactly $s=\Theta(\log(N+2))$ full extensions and every other source assignment has none. Fix **any** satisfying source assignment and existentially hide all auxiliary variables except a designated set of terminals. Representing the resulting terminal relation requires $\Omega(N/\log(N+2))$ independent cycles, even with arbitrary ternary Boolean tables and unlimited internal vertices. The output's variable scope graph can be connected. The conclusions also hold when the source has a unique satisfying assignment.

Here a network has one external port per named terminal, Boolean internal edges, vertices of degree at most three, and arbitrary local relations. A terminal word is accepted when it has an internal extension. The cycle cost $b(R)$ is the least feedback-edge count $|E|-|V|+c$ of such a network realizing the relation $R$ exactly. Fixing a terminal to a constant or existentially hiding it replaces its external leaf by a fixed or unrestricted unary constraint and cannot increase this minimum. Thus the cycle bound on a pinned, projected relation also holds for the entire predicate on all output variables. ## How model count limits cycle cost

Any nonempty binary relation with $s$ distinct words satisfies $b(R)=O(2^s)$, independently of its number of terminals. Label the words $1,\ldots,s$. Each terminal reads a subset of these labels. Up to complement, choose the subset omitting $s$. Generate one of the $s$ modes with $s-1$ existential bits constrained to have at most one set bit. The all-zero choice denotes mode $s$. For every nonempty subset $T\subseteq [s-1]$, form the OR of its mode bits, building it from $T$ with one member removed. This costs $O(2^s)$ gates and wires, including the at-most-one checks. Attach each actual terminal to its subset signal, using complement tables when needed. Duplicating or complementing a signal into any number of external leaves through a tree costs zero additional cycles. Constant columns can have their own unary constraints. This proves the upper bound even with arbitrarily many repeated columns. Empty and singleton relations have cycle cost zero.

Consequently a source with a unique model cannot have a parsimonious image with positive cycle cost on its full assignment relation. After fixing one source assignment, requiring $B$ cycles on a projection forces at least $\log_2 B-O(1)$ **distinct full extensions**. This follows since the number of distinct projected words is at most the number of full extensions.

## A matching relation with few words

Choose $s\ge6$. There are $h=2^{s-1}-1$ terminal coordinates indexed by the nonempty subsets $T\subseteq[s-1]$. The $s$ words of $R_s$ are
$$
                            y^{(i)}_T=[i\in T],\qquad 1\le i\le s .
$$
The final word is zero. Every two distinct words differ in $2^{s-2}$ positions. Thus the coordinates outside any set of at most eight terminals identify each entire word.

On a set of $k$ distinct terminals, suppose the mode words show $r$ row patterns. One of the patterns is all zero. Each of the $k$ columns is a different nonzero binary function of the $r$ patterns that vanishes on the zero pattern. Therefore $k\le 2^{r-1}-1$. In particular, two selected terminals have at least three distinct row patterns and eight selected terminals have at least five. Each of these patterns needs its own accepting rectangle across the selected-versus-remaining cut. Indeed take one whole word of each pattern. Crossing the inside of one with the outside of a different pattern is rejected, since that outside already identifies the original mode.

In any realizing network, cutting $u$ edges between two vertex sets covers its accepted rows by at most $2^u$ rectangles. Condition on the values of the cut edges and choose internal witnesses separately on each side. Either side can have multiple components. Hence **no one-edge cut isolates exactly two terminals, and no two-edge cut isolates exactly eight**. Cut arguments and removal of tree branches also occur in graphical realizations of codes. The rectangle proof here permits nonlinear local tables. [Forney, *Codes on Graphs: Fundamentals*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6264) · [Kashyap, *Constraint Complexity of Realizations of Linear Codes on Arbitrary Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.2199)

Draw every external port as a terminal leaf. Since $R_s$ is nonempty, remove terminal-free components. To work with a connected graph, subdivide an edge of each of two components by an equality-through vertex and join the two new vertices by an edge whose value both tables ignore. Repeat until connected. This operation preserves the relation and the number of independent cycles. Terminal-free hanging trees may be evaluated and removed, restricting their attachment port to its feasible values. Degree-two chains can be composed as binary relations. None of these reductions increases cycle cost.

If the entire graph is a tree with its leaves at the terminals, suppress degree-two chains. Two leaves then form a cherry whose parent has just one remaining edge to the rest of the tree, contradicting the one-edge cut bound. Here $h\ge31$, so there are other terminals. Otherwise repeatedly strip leaves to obtain the connected nonempty 2-core. Consider any hanging tree rooted at this core. If it has at least two terminals, a deepest branching point in its pruned part has exactly two terminal descendants after suppressing paths. Its edge toward the core isolates those two terminals. The core attachment itself has at least two core neighbors counting multiplicity, so it has at most one port available for a hanging tree. It follows that each terminal attaches singly at a degree-two site of the core. Degree-three core vertices have no spare port. This argument also covers an attachment with a long degree-two stem.

For cycle cost $b=1$ the connected core is a cycle. Eight consecutive terminal sites are isolated by two edges, which is impossible. For $b\ge2$, the core has $2(b-1)$ degree-three vertices by the degree sum. Suppressing degree-two paths gives a cubic multigraph with $3(b-1)$ edges. On any of the corresponding paths, eight consecutive terminal sites could be isolated by cutting immediately before and after their segment. This works for a loop returning to the same branch vertex, for parallel paths, and for a path joining the two sides of a bridge. The latter cut can leave a disconnected outside, which is permitted by the rectangle proof. Each path therefore carries at most seven terminals. We have proved
$$
                       b(R_s)\ \ge\ 1+\lceil h/21\rceil .
$$
The subset-signal network gives $b(R_s)=O(h)$. Taken with the general upper bound, the maximum possible cycle cost of an $s$-model relation over any number of terminals grows as $\Theta(2^s)$.

In fact $R_s$ attains the largest possible minimum cycle count among all relations with at most $s$ words, with no bound on their terminal count. For a nonempty relation assign the $s$ modes onto its words surjectively. Up to complementation, every nonconstant column is one of the nonempty subsets indexing $R_s$. Delete unused columns existentially, distribute each retained column to the needed leaves using equality trees, undo column complements and adjoin constant leaves. None of these operations increases cycle count. The empty relation has a zero-cycle presentation. In particular every relation with at most three words is representable by a forest: up to constants and complements there are at most three distinct nonconstant columns, so their joint relation fits in a single ternary table. Four words can already require a cycle. The four rows of $R_4$ on seven coordinates have pairwise distance four, every pair of columns has at least three patterns, and a two-leaf cherry in any tree would permit only two rectangles.

## A proper ternary compiler with exact multiplicity

Choose $s\ge6$ so that $h=2^{s-1}-1=\Theta(1+n/\log(n+2))$, rounding up to the next available size. Reserve a reference source variable $x$ when one occurs. For a source with no variables use a fixed false anchor as the reference.

1. Add $s-1$ mode bits. Enforce at most one with the pairwise negative binary clauses. The mode bit for a singleton subset is its signal. For every larger mask $T$, use a fresh output and the three clauses defining its OR from the highest mode bit in $T$ and the already built signal for the remaining mask. Generate sets in increasing binary-mask order.
2. For each $T$ introduce a fresh named terminal $y_T$ and the four proper clauses for $y_T=x\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}\mathrm{OR}_T$. Fix three disjoint false anchors by the seven ternary clauses excluding their nonzero assignments. Pad each short clause with distinct false anchors.
3. To connect a source component even when it does not contain $x$, for each source variable $z$ include $z\lor\neg f_0\lor f_1$ with two of the false anchors. This clause is always satisfied. It makes every source variable adjacent to the anchors in the variable scope graph. The code is attached through its gates and modes. The XOR translation additionally gives a value-dependent attachment to $x$ whenever that variable exists.

Once the source and mode are fixed, every gate and anchor is determined. Distinct modes have distinct terminal words. A fixed source assignment therefore has exactly $s$ extensions if it satisfies the source and zero otherwise. The visible relation is $R_s$ or its bitwise complement according to $x$. Complementing terminal bits can be absorbed in unary or binary tables without changing $b$. The graph lower bound and its multiplicity converse apply to each individual satisfying fibre.

For an input already in proper ternary DIMACS, dense renaming need not enlarge its short identifiers. Intern the normalized decimal spellings in a ten-way trie and visit by increasing length then lexicographic order to assign numeric ranks to the occurring positive identifiers. A new rank is at most the original identifier, so each emitted source token fits its original byte budget. Precomputing the spellings of $v$ new ranks and printing the $v$ connection clauses costs $O(1+v\log(v+1))=O(n+1)$, because the source contains at least one spelling of each distinct name. The code has $O(h+s^2)$ rows and names, and their spellings need $O(\log(n+2))$ bits apiece. Generating masks, recording their output names, and printing the actual dense proper file take $O(n+1)$ RAM steps on logarithmic words. The $h$ distinct terminal names alone require $\Omega(h\log(h+1))$ bits. Thus $N=\Theta(n+1)$. For general explicit CNF, first use the journal's linear-time, linear-encoded-length proper conversion that preserves one extension per satisfying assignment on the **occurring source variables**. Choose the code size using the original input length and connect all variables of the normalized proper formula, including its gate variables. Models on exponentially many unused names merely permitted by a sparse header are outside this counting convention. [Proper normalization, unique auxiliary values and linear RAM rendering](exact-network-ceiling-for-ordinary-sat.md)

The physical audit covers an empty source, a uniquely satisfiable source, a source with seven assignments, an unsatisfiable source, and a disconnected source with 49 assignments. At three, six and seven modes it parses the emitted proper DIMACS, checks scope connectivity, and checks every predicted complete assignment and its terminal bits. After blocking all predicted full assignments, CaDiCaL returned UNSAT on all fifteen files. At six modes the cut census gives 270 two-coordinate sets with three visible patterns and 195 with four. Among eight-coordinate sets, 96,525 have five visible patterns and 7,792,200 have six. [Reproducible audit](sparse_model_cycle_audit.py)

The compiler transfers the SAT decision problem to a promise in which every positive instance has high semantic cycle cost. It supplies no lower bound on the time a RAM needs to decide those instances. On the compiler family the extra code always has a mode, so an algorithm that recognizes the construction can discard the extra constraints and decide the source. The graph proof and compiler accounting are manuscript arguments. Priority for the extremal statement and its application to witness multiplicity has not been established.

## Testing every partial pinning in one packed scan

For any relation $R$ on $h$ bits, let $P_R(e,v)$ accept when some $x\in R$ agrees with $v_i$ at every coordinate with $e_i=1$. This total predicate has $2h$ input bits and satisfies
$$
                                b(P_R)=b(R).
$$
Replace each terminal leaf of an optimal network for $R$ with a ternary table on $(x_i,e_i,v_i)$ enforcing $e_i\Rightarrow x_i=v_i$. The old wire carries the existential value $x_i$ and the two new leaves carry $e_i,v_i$. Replacing a leaf by this tree preserves the cycle count. Conversely, pinning all enables to one in a network for $P_R$ realizes $R$ on the $v$ terminals.

For $R_s$ above this gives an explicit total predicate with $b(P_{R_s})=\Theta(h)$ whose worst-case deterministic RAM decision time on each verdict is $\Theta(h/\log h)$. Here the input consists of two packed $h$-bit arrays and the word width is $w=\Theta(\log h)$ with enough bits to address the input. The algorithm is uniform and its running time includes building its table.

Number the coordinates by their nonzero $(s-1)$-bit subset masks $j$. Keep an $(s-1)$-bit register of surviving nonzero modes, initially all ones. An enabled one intersects this register with $j$ and an enabled zero intersects it with the bitwise complement of $j$ within $s-1$ bits. The all-zero mode survives exactly when there are no enabled ones. Use a dummy disabled position at index zero. For sufficiently large $h$, choose a power of two $L$ between $(\log_2(h+1))/16$ and $(\log_2(h+1))/8$. Each aligned block contains masks $j=A+u$ with common high part $A$ and offsets $0\le u<L$. For each of the $4^L$ possible pairs of enable and value blocks, precompute the bitwise AND of offsets of enabled ones, the bitwise OR of offsets of enabled zeros, and whether either collection is empty. Take the AND's empty value to be $L-1$ and the OR's empty value to be zero. The table costs $O(L4^L)$ word operations to build. In each nonempty collection the high part is common, so the AND of the full one-masks or OR of the full zero-masks is $A$ bitwise-OR the corresponding table value. Intersect the live register with the first mask and with the complement of the second, skipping an empty collection. A block needs a constant number of word reads, shifts and Boolean operations, including extraction across word boundaries. The elapsed time is $O(h/L+L4^L)=O(h/\log h)$. The final register also gives the exact number of compatible rows and the least compatible row label, if one exists, in $O(s)$ additional steps.

For the lower bound, enable all coordinates and set all values to zero. This is an accepting input. Flipping any one value bit makes it rejecting, since the nonzero rows have weight $2^{s-2}>1$. Hence the accepting run must read the input word containing each value bit. If a step reads at most $k$ words, at least $\lceil h/(kw)\rceil$ steps are necessary. Choose a value bit whose word is first read last. The rejecting input that flips this bit follows the same computation up to that read and satisfies the same time lower bound. In particular, a scan-time algorithm for every pinning, including one that returns the least compatible mode, can coexist with a linear minimum cycle count on the full relation. This conclusion concerns the explicit packed predicate; the proper DIMACS reduction above uses its own encoded length $N$.
