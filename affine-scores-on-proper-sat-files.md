# Exhausting affine assignments on ordinary SAT files

There are two ordinary proper 3-CNFs on four variables with eight distinct clauses each, one satisfiable and one unsatisfiable, for which every affine assignment violates exactly one clause. Four variables and eight clauses are both minimal for a positive proper 3-CNF to escape this assignment search. A different construction extends indistinguishable positive and negative files to arbitrarily many variables, even when a score query may reorder all variables. Allowing one-bit changes to the permuted queries suffices to determine every 3-CNF score as a function. These statements describe precisely what this combinatorial exhaustion detects.

## A linear-time search on a standard RAM

Number the $v$ occurring variables by first appearance, starting at zero. An affine model has bits $x_i=b\oplus\langle u,i\rangle$, where the dot product is over the bits of the fixed-width index in $\mathbb F_2$. A uniform deterministic solver can inspect **every** such model and find one if present in $O(N)$ standard word-RAM steps, for $N$ physical input bits. A complete solver can run this test first and fall back to any complete decision algorithm. A procedure refuting that solver on a positive file has to account for its witness search.

Here is the calculation. Write $\chi_a(u)=(-1)^{\langle a,u\rangle}$. For fixed bias $b$, the indicator that a literal on $i$ is false has the form $(1+e\chi_i)/2$, where $e=(-1)^b$ for a positive literal and its negative for a negative literal. Multiply the three indicators of a clause, and expand. Eight times its violation indicator is a signed sum of at most eight characters, including multiplicities. Their indices are subset XORs of the three names. Accumulate the signed coefficients across the actual clauses in an array of $K=2^{\lceil\log_2 v\rceil}$ slots and take one Walsh–Hadamard transform. Its entries are exactly eight times the respective counts of violated clauses. Repeat for the other bias. For a shorter clause multiply by the compensating power of two. Repeated variables and opposite signs satisfy the same product identity. Zero or one occurring variable can also be handled directly. This is a restriction and frequency folding of the known Walsh expansion for MAX-SAT. [Rana, Heckendorn and Whitley, AAAI 1998](https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/1998/AAAI98-206.pdf)

The decimal trie for interning even very long identifiers takes $O(N)$ operations. There are $O(N)$ clauses and $K<2v$ for $v>1$. The distinct input identifiers cost $\Omega(v\log(v+2))$ bits. Thus $O(K\log K)$ butterfly operations fit in the same linear bound. Every intermediate sum has magnitude at most the initial coefficient $\ell_1$-norm, bounded by eight times the clause count, and fits in a constant number of logarithmic words. The same argument works with any assigned public labels in $\mathbb F_2^\ell$ and per-name offsets constructed in $O(N)$ steps, when $2^\ell(\ell+1)=O(N)$. Recover a minimizing mask directly from the resulting table. The tests checked 30,232 individual scores against direct evaluation. [Finite ordinary-file checks](automatic_assignment_front_audit.py)

## Where exhaustion first misses

In the positive formula, seven clauses on $x_0,x_1,x_2$ forbid all settings other than $000$. Append $(x_0\lor x_1\lor x_3)$. Its unique model is $0001$ in index order. An affine assignment on the four binary indices has even parity. Its first three bits choose one of eight cases. The first seven clauses account for seven of these cases and the last clause fails in the remaining case. For a negative mate, branch on $x_0$. Conditional on $x_0=0$, forbid each of the four settings of $x_1,x_2$ by one proper clause. Conditional on $x_0=1$, do the same for $x_1,x_3$. Every full assignment violates exactly one clause. Both formulas introduce the names in the same order. [Lean proof of the files, scores, model and four-variable minimality](AffineClauseProfiles.lean)

For any three distinct indices, the three augmented vectors $(1,i)$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb F_2$. No pair is equal, and a sum of three has leading coordinate one. Uniform random $b,u$ therefore gives independent fair values on these indices. Each proper clause is violated with probability $1/8$. Up to seven clauses have expected violations less than one, so an affine satisfying assignment exists. On at most three occurring variables all assignments are representable. These arguments give minimality over all variable counts and clause counts separately. They use the hypothesis of three distinct variables per clause.

## A pair that survives every ordering

Let $v=2^\ell\ge4$, $k=v/2$, and write $H(x)=x_0(1-x_1)$. In both files force $x_0=1,\ x_1=0$ using common proper clauses. For each of the three other settings of $x_0,x_1$, forbid both extensions on a third distinct variable. Common clauses implied by $x_0=1$ can introduce all names in the same order. Add these clauses:

* In the positive file, for every $i\ge2$ add the clause whose violation indicator is $H(x)x_i$.
* In the negative file, for each of $k-1$ distinct indices $i\ge2$, add both clauses with violation indicators $H(x)x_i$ and $H(x)(1-x_i)$.

The first file has exactly one model of weight one. The second has none. Both add $v-2$ clauses. Subtracting their total violation scores gives the identity on the entire cube

$$
S_+(x)-S_-(x)=H(x)(|x|-k).
$$

In particular the scores agree at the two constant assignments and every balanced assignment. Taking the union over all permutations of the names gives exactly these three weight layers from affine functions on a complete binary index cube. An adaptive algorithm whose only feedback about the file consists of such scalar scores receives the same answers on both files, however many queries it makes. The pair has equal physical lengths in the executable examples. An ordinary RAM can inspect the clauses directly. ## What one more query operation reveals

Suppose the permitted query may now flip one bit of any permuted affine assignment. The available assignments include all three layers of weights $k-1,k,k+1$, as well as zero. If two width-three formulas have identical scores on these assignments, they have the same scores everywhere, and therefore the same SAT verdict.

For a proof, their score difference $P$ is a real multilinear polynomial of degree at most three on the Boolean cube. A multilinear polynomial of degree at most $r$ vanishing on $r+1$ consecutive weight layers vanishes everywhere. For $r=0$ this is immediate. For each coordinate the difference obtained by setting it to one and zero has degree at most $r-1$. It vanishes on the first $r$ of the specified layers of the reduced cube. Induction makes every coordinate difference zero.

For distinct $i,j$, compare $P$ at $x_i,x_j=1,0$ and $0,1$ with the other coordinates free. Their difference has degree at most two and vanishes on the three consecutive layers $k-2,k-1,k$ of the remaining $v-2$ coordinates. These layers exist already for $v=4$. Hence every such swap preserves $P$, so $P$ is symmetric. Its coefficient on a multilinear monomial depends only on the monomial's size. Consequently $P$ is a polynomial of degree at most three in $|x|$, in the basis $\binom{|x|}{j}$. Its values at the four different weights $0,k-1,k,k+1$ force it to vanish identically. This is a determination result. Exhausting the middle layers can take exponentially many queries. Related work treats general polynomial representations on Boolean slices. [Filmus and Mossel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.02713)

Even two-state automata suffice for determination under arbitrary order, without an extra flip, for $v=2^\ell\ge4$. One automaton accepts binary words with an odd trailing run of ones. Its accepted count obeys $a_0=0,\ a_{\ell+1}=2^\ell-a_\ell$, so $a_\ell=(2^\ell-(-1)^\ell)/3$. Output complementation supplies $v-a_\ell$; a last-bit test supplies $k$. For $v\ge8$ all three are distinct and lie in $[3,v-3]$. At $v=4$ the same tests and the constants already give all five weights. For the preceding swap argument one uses the following variant. A degree-$r$ multilinear polynomial vanishing on $r+1$ distinct layers in $[r,n-r]$ is zero. Induct on $r$, applying the hypothesis to a swap difference on $n-2$ coordinates and any $r$ shifted layers. This forces symmetry, and the $r+1$ roots finish the proof. Apply the variant at degree two to the three shifted layers in $v-2$ variables, then use the zero assignment as the fourth root of the symmetric cubic. Exact score equality against every permuted two-state probe is therefore too strong a proposed inductive invariant for an opposite-verdict pair.

## With the variable order fixed

If the order is fixed, the smallest number of occurring variables for which opposite SAT verdicts can have identical scores at every affine center **and every one-bit flip** is ten. At radius two the scores determine the whole cubic function for every variable count.

To see this, write the score in its real Walsh basis on the original variables. Every support $S$ has size at most three. On the affine centers its character depends only on $|S|$ modulo two and on the XOR $c$ of its variable indices. Transforming in the center parameters recovers the separate sum of coefficients in each such class. After a flip at position $i$, the sign changes exactly for supports containing $i$. Thus radius one also gives, separately in each class, every coordinate-incidence sum.

For even classes the pairs of a fixed nonzero XOR form a matching, and the empty support stands alone. In an odd class the possible singleton at $c$ meets none of its triples. With indices in $0,\ldots,7$, XOR-translation by $c$ turns every triple in the class into a line on the seven nonzero vectors of $\mathbb F_2^3$. The seven line-incidence columns are independent over the reals. Their Gram matrix is $2I+J$ since each line has three points and each pair of distinct lines meets once. Deleting unused lines and points outside all remaining lines preserves independence. For nine variables the new index is 8. A low XOR class still has only old triples. In a high XOR class every triple uses 8 and a pair from a single matching among the low labels. Their columns remain independent. This proves determination at every size through nine.

For ten variables, partition the eight low labels into $U=\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $Z=\{4,5,6,7\}$. In the first formula take triples consisting of label 8 together with an XOR-2 pair in $U$, and label 9 with an XOR-3 pair in $Z$. In the second interchange these choices between $U$ and $Z$. All eight triples have XOR 10. Each collection of four triples has the same point incidences. Require even parity on each of its triples by the four proper clauses forbidding the odd patterns. The score of each check is $(1-\chi_S)/2$. Thus the full scores agree on all affine centers and all one-bit flips. In both files add the same proper clauses pinning precisely $x_0,x_2$ true and all other variables false. This point satisfies the first set of checks and violates two checks of the second. The files each have 309 bytes and 30 distinct proper clauses. Their scores agree at all 320 distinct queried assignments. Exact rational ranks at nine and ten variables are respectively $130/130$ and $170/176$ in the degree-three space. [Exact rank and ordinary-file audit](affine_radius_scores_audit.py)

At radius two, inclusion–exclusion on the scores after flipping neither, either, or both of $i,j$ gives each class's sum over supports containing both. In an even class this isolates the pair itself. In an odd class at most one triple contains that pair, since its third index must equal $c\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}} i\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}} j$. This recovers every coefficient of size at least two. The remaining singleton and constant coefficients follow from the unperturbed classes. Thus extending the ten-variable collision while preserving radius-two score equality is impossible even at a fixed order. The number and cost of actual RAM instructions needed to find a model remain separate.

## A solver that can read the clauses and repair a model

For each fixed $r$, an ordinary deterministic RAM can find a model within $r$ flips of any fixed-order affine assignment, if one exists, in $N(\log(N+2))^{O(r+1)}$ time. This permits unbounded variable occurrence. It explains why the ten-variable score collision would fail as a claim of hardness against ordinary algorithms.

At a node let $S$ be the set of already flipped indices. Each clause's failure indicator on the affine assignment with those flips is the product, over $\mathbb F_2$, of at most three affine forms in the seed bits $(b,u)$. As a multilinear polynomial on the seed cube its coefficients occupy $D=O((1+\log(v+2))^3)$ positions. Scan the file and keep an independent basis chosen from the original coefficient rows by Gaussian elimination. The basis contains at most $D$ actual clauses. A seed satisfies them all exactly when it satisfies the entire file with the flips $S$. Test all seeds at this node in $O(N)$ by the original Walsh method with the changed signs.

If a solution with seed $u$ and flip set $S^*\supseteq S$ exists but $u$ currently fails, at least one retained clause fails at $u$. It contains some variable in $S^*\setminus S$, since otherwise its truth would be unchanged by all remaining flips. Branch on every as-yet-unflipped variable in those basis clauses. There are at most $3D$ children. Induction gives a path to a solution by depth $r$. Forming rows and checking independence has polynomial cost in $D$ per clause. A depth-first search through these nodes gives the stated bound; allowing a complete fallback supplies a globally correct solver. This uses classical unsatisfied-clause branching with a simultaneous set of candidate clauses valid for all affine seeds. [Dantsin and coauthors on deterministic local search for SAT](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/CourseWeb/Math268_2007WS/DantsinEtal02TCS.pdf)

There is still a linear-size parsimonious successor that evades every small automaton as a *model*. It follows by fixing a weight that none of the automata attain. The score determination result explains why requiring all their numerical scores to agree would lose that successor. No argument here constrains the elapsed time of an unrestricted solver on the extended file.
