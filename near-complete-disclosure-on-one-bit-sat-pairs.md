# Work remaining on opposite SAT runs after almost every packet has been read

Let $N$ be the number of physical input bits. Fix an aligned deterministic word RAM with immutable input, zero-initialized work memory, a fixed number of registers, a bounded number of word accesses per instruction and word width $w=C\lceil\log_2(N+2)\rceil$, with a fixed sufficiently large byte-compatible coefficient. Split compound instructions into their constituent accesses. A packet is one aligned word of actual DIMACS text. All other input bytes are common to the family at a given length.

**Theorem.** For every correct SAT decider there are arbitrarily large pairs of proper 3-CNFs of the same length, one satisfiable and one unsatisfiable, differing in one physical bit. Their executions have a common prefix ending when the machine requests the word containing that bit. Among $\Theta(N/\log N)$ packets, only $R=\Theta(N\log\log N/\log^2 N)$ remain unread at that moment. Each continuation makes $\Omega(R)$ distinct old-address reads, with no bound on prefix time or workspace. If the decider runs in $O(N\log^K N)$ time for any fixed $K$, there are such pairs on which **both** continuations make $\Omega(N/\log N)$ such reads. A near-linear bound on the number of prefix-written words suffices for the stronger conclusion. Constants may depend on the model, the solver and the fixed exponent.

An old-address read means a reread of a previously exposed input packet, or the first read of a work address before any suffix write to it. Distinct addresses are counted once. A work read testing an untouched zero word counts. Registers, public syntax, new input packets, repeated probes, and values written in the continuation can all be supplied free to the simulation. This definition prices actual instructions, including a query for absence in a sparse table. It does not assert every counted work address was previously written. No input changes during execution.

## A short query with large row projections

Start with $k$ balanced rows of $B$ bits each. Let $M=kB$, with $B$ a power of four of order $\log k/\log\log k$. A query selects one column in each row. The static problem asks whether any selected bit is zero. Consecutive groups of $d=\Theta(\log k)$ rows receive independent walk seeds. Each seed selects a uniform first column and $d-1$ uniform transitions from a fixed alphabet. Put $B=u^2$. Use the 8-labelled Margulis–Gabber–Galil graph on $(\mathbb Z/u)^2$, add eight hold labels and take 160 successive lazy steps per transition. The normalized nonconstant spectral radius of this powered transition $P$ is at most $\lambda=((1+5\sqrt2/8)/2)^{160}<2^{-12}$. Laziness also controls negative eigenvalues. The powered transition has $D=16^{160}$ equally likely labels. [Gabber and Galil](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4) · [Formal MGG spectral theorem](https://isa-afp.org/browser_info/current/AFP/Expander_Graphs/Expander_Graphs_MGG.html)

Two estimates use the uniform distribution on seeds, counting distinct labels even when they lead to the same vertex.

1. For every balanced database, the probability of selecting only ones is at least $8^{-k}$. In each segment work backwards from the final set of good columns. If the retained next set $G$ has density $\alpha\ge1/4$, the normalized squared deviation of $P1_G$ from $\alpha$ is at most $\lambda^2\alpha(1-\alpha)$. At most a $16\lambda^2$ fraction of vertices have transition probability into $G$ below $1/8$. Intersect the remaining vertices with the current row's ones. At least a quarter of the columns survive, and every retained vertex has probability at least $1/8$ of reaching the next retained set. Multiply along each segment and then across independent segments.
2. For any family of query seeds of mass at least $2^{-4k}$, the sum of its row projection sizes exceeds $M/1024$. If $U_i$ is the projection in row $i$, put $\alpha_i=|U_i|/B$ and $\bar\alpha=k^{-1}\sum_i\alpha_i$. Write $D_i$ for the projection onto $U_i$ and $J$ for uniform averaging. From $\|P-J\|\le\lambda$ one gets $\|D_i P D_{i+1}\|\le\sqrt{\alpha_i\alpha_{i+1}}+\lambda\le(\alpha_i+\alpha_{i+1})/2+\lambda$. A segment's box probability is bounded by the product of these norms, since its endpoint vectors have norm at most one and $D_i^2=D_i$. There are $k-k/d\ge k/2$ edges total. Their mean bound is at most $2\bar\alpha+\lambda$, counting each endpoint weight at most twice. If this base is below one, the box probability is at most $(2\bar\alpha+\lambda)^{k/2}$ by AM–GM. Mass $2^{-4k}$ gives $\bar\alpha\ge(2^{-8}-\lambda)/2>1/1024$. A base of at least one gives the claim directly.

## A static cell-probe bound

Allow arbitrary preprocessing of the database into $S$ words of $W=\Theta(\log M)$ bits. Queries get the entire seed for free and can do unlimited computation while reading the representation. Pad the address range to at least $M/W$. For every fixed polynomial upper bound on $S$, some all-ones query on some balanced database requires at least

$$
 c\min\left\{M/W,\ k/\log_2(2+SW/M)\right\}
$$

distinct probes. The constant may depend on the degree of the space bound and the word-width constants.

Suppose every all-ones answer can be certified by at most $t$ probes. For a deterministic implementation take its actual query transcript. Sample $\Delta=\lfloor\delta M/W\rfloor$ cells uniformly without replacement. For $t\le\Delta/2$ the chance the sample covers any fixed certificate is at least $(\Delta/(2S))^t$. Choose $\delta>0$ small enough to encode the sample's addresses and contents in at most $M/4096$ bits, using $\log_2 S=O(W)$. Choose $c>0$ small enough that $t\log_2(2S/\Delta)\le k$. By the first walk estimate some sample certifies all-ones queries of total mass at least $2^{-4k}$.

From the sample alone, enumerate all seeds whose answer is forced to be all-ones by the sampled addresses and values. This can be defined by exhaustive comparison over valid balanced databases and their fixed representations. In particular it includes the covered certificates. Every projected coordinate of these seeds is a one in the actual database. The second estimate recovers at least $M/1024$ known bits. Append the other raw bits in fixed order to the sampled cells and pad to a fixed length. The resulting injective description saves a constant fraction of $M$ bits. Yet there are $\binom B{B/2}^k\ge2^M/(B+1)^k=2^{M-o(M)}$ balanced databases. This proves the bound. For $S=M\log^{O(1)}M$ it gives $\Omega(M/\log M)$. For $S=M^{O(1)}$ it gives $\Omega(k/\log M)$.

The uncompressed row query is blocked lopsided set disjointness. Cell sampling and certificates are established techniques. The walk estimate here addresses the smaller collection of physical seeds used in the reduction. Priority for the combined SAT statement is unestablished. [Pătraşcu](https://people.csail.mit.edu/mip/papers/structures/paper.pdf) · [Panigrahy, Talwar and Wieder](https://theory.stanford.edu/~rinap/papers/anns.pdf) · [Wang and Yin](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5743)

## A literal physical bit chooses which half to test

Choose master sizes $k=2^{2L-1}$ for growing powers of two $L$. Keep $B$ the largest power of four at most $L/\log_2 L$, $h$ the largest power of two at most $L/256$, and $d$ the largest power of two at most $h/1280$. Start sufficiently large that $B\le h$, $d\ge2$, and $\log_2 B+640(d-1)\le h$. All three parameters stay fixed when passing to dyadic intervals of $s$ rows down to $2^L$. Each interval splits between complete walk segments. For every such $s$, $h,d=\Theta(\log s)$ and $B=\Theta(\log s/\log\log s)$.

On an interval of $s$ rows the SAT formula guesses a row and a column. It requires that the database bit there be zero, that the seed of the corresponding segment select that column in that row, and that the row belong to the half selected by a tag. Three distinct pinned variables give false padding and a true constant for proper clauses. A public library of multiplexers decodes an $h$-bit database mask on the low address bits. Another public library tests every possible segment seed against the low row bits and guessed column. Allocate a public selector flag for every database chunk and every segment. Its packet, a padded pair of proper clauses, equates it to the library name for the chosen value. Public high-address multiplexers then select the appropriate flags. The selector checks choose a single missing entry, so any number of misses is allowed.

The tag is a padded positive unit encoded as a triple. Two aliases pinned to complementary values of the high row bit have consecutive decimal names ending in 2 and 3. Their ASCII spellings differ in one bit. All other bytes, including the header and tag packet length, agree. Each packet consists of complete clauses and may go at any packet slot. There are $p=sB/h$ data packets, $s/d$ seed packets and one tag. The public circuit and packet count are $O(p+s/d+d2^h+\operatorname{poly}(\log s))$ clauses. The public libraries are shared across all chunks and segments. Every identifier uses $O(\log s)$ bits. The physical length, including padding for the stated word width, is therefore $N=\Theta(sB)$. To see the padding bound, choose the width exponent near $\log_2(sB)+O(1)$ large enough for the raw file at that width, then add public words up to the lower endpoint of its length interval if needed. The interval has room for a complete word. The $p$ full data words alone give the matching lower bound.

For each database, simulate the actual solver from reset. Answer public reads normally. When a packet address is requested for the first time, place the next data packet there, in a fixed logical order, until all $p$ packets are placed. Repeated reads agree. Stop before giving the reply to the next new packet request. This request must occur. At any earlier point the remaining data can be completed in the same canonical order, one half can have all its selected entries equal to one by estimate 1, and the other can have a miss by choosing the starting column of a segment. Swapping the tag gives opposite answers. All completions agree on every previous reply, and a correct decider halts on both. Put the tag at the requested address. Assign the seed packets to the remaining positions in increasing physical-address order. This defines ordinary immutable files. Their common prefix includes the issue of the tag request itself.

## Why both continuations must be expensive

Preprocess a root database for the static query problem. For every dyadic interval down to the stated leaf size, save the query-independent cut state from the separate simulation on that interval. In polynomial space one can reserve the entire native word-address universe for each state since $w=O(\log M)$. With a near-linear prefix bound, store its sparse work memory and installed input packets in static dictionaries with linear space and constant-probe lookup, including default-zero queries. Store the constant controller and offset information, with fixed bank offsets or a separately charged directory. Summing over a level and then $O(\log k)$ levels costs $M\log^{O(1)}M$ cells in the near-linear case. Include a packed copy of the original database for the leaves. Preprocessing need only terminate. [Static dictionary theorem](https://www.rasmuspagh.net/papers/det-jour.pdf)

The seed-to-address map is part of the database-dependent state. Let $R_s=1+s/d$. Encode the subset of $R_s$ remaining positions by its rank among subsets of the $p+R_s$ packet slots, and identify the designated tag address within it. Sorting the other positions recovers their logical seed order. The entire header costs $O(R_s\log(e(p+R_s)/R_s)+\log M)=O(R_s\log B+\log M)$ bits. Pack it in a fixed-size field for that node. Along one descent, reading the headers and directories costs $O(\log k+(k/d)\log B/W)=o(k/\log M)$ probes. Granting all nodes' placements to the query algorithm for free would invalidate the encoding argument.

Set a cutoff $t_s=\eta sB/\log(sB)$ in the near-linear case, and $t_s=\eta s/\log(sB)$ with unrestricted prefixes. Suppose every genuinely opposite pair submitted at the nodes has at least one continuation using at most $t_s$ old addresses. At a node simulate both tags, stopping each at halt or just before its $(\lfloor t_s\rfloor+1)$-st distinct old-address read. All new input replies follow from the seed and the decoded header. Simulate writes and cache replies privately. At most a constant number of compact probes is needed per newly charged native address. The simulators may spend unlimited arithmetic time. If a simulation halts its verdict is exact.

* If either returns YES, there is a miss in the parent.
* If a simulation returns NO, that half has no miss. Evaluate the other half unless its answer is already available.
* If both time out, their true half verdicts agree by the supposition. Evaluate either child.

Exactly one recursive call suffices whenever a recursive call is needed. The segments restricted to the chosen half are precisely its query. At a leaf $s=2^L=\sqrt{2k}$, direct lookup uses $O(\sqrt k)$ probes. Along the path the two cutoffs per node sum geometrically to $O(\eta M/\log M)$ or $O(\eta k/\log M)$, respectively, plus the lower-order metadata and leaf costs. For small enough fixed $\eta$ this contradicts the corresponding static lower bound. Some opposite pair consequently has two expensive continuations. Its interval size tends to infinity with the master size. Substituting its own $N=\Theta(sB)$ proves both statements.

## Tightness, failure of amplification and verification

At these cuts a packed copy of the raw database gives a matching $O(N/\log N)$ static-probe upper bound by reading it once. With polynomial prefix space one may precompute whether each possible seed hits a zero in each segment. Then one old lookup per seed answers either half in $O(R)$ probes. A sequential packet scanner on the canonical data-first inputs can finish this polynomial preprocessing before asking for the tag. The seed table has at most $2^h$ entries per segment and fits in the stated address space. There is also a uniform $O(N)$-time algorithm on the entire promised family with arbitrary packet permutations. Decode the flags and their decimal names, restore packet order, expand the selected walks in $O(s)$ word steps and inspect their selected bits. Public clauses can be regenerated and validated in linear bit-length time on the RAM, with a complete SAT fallback. There are only a bounded number of candidate powers of two $L$ consistent with $\log_2 N\in[L,2L+O(\log L)]$ up to fixed additive constants. For each, $B$ is known and the fixed bounds $N=\Theta(sB)$ leave only a bounded number of dyadic $s$. Thus the two suffix bounds locate mandatory work on genuine executions. Different cuts may charge the same reads, and the examples admit a linear total clock.
