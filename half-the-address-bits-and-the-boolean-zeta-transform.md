# Dense recovery of a compact physical file and the Boolean zeta transform

Let $H$ be the fixed unary nondeterministic hierarchy language with clock $t=Cn^2$. Fix $0<\eta<1/2$. Suppose a uniform indexer supplies in $o(t)$ time the length $M=O(t(\log t)^{1/2-\eta})$ of a proper ordinary DIMACS file equivalent to $H(1^n)$ and computes each raw bit in $(\log t)^{O(1)}$ native work. Put $\ell=\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$. On infinitely many lengths with $M\ge\sqrt t$ the minimum Boolean extension degree of the file bit in its physical address exceeds
\[
                 \ell/2-O(\sqrt{\ell\log(2+\ell)}).
\]
The implicit constant may depend on the fixed index-time polynomial. The result includes linear physical length. It concerns the complexity of locally presenting the hierarchy language as SAT. Whether ordinary SAT itself requires superlinear time on an unrestricted deterministic RAM remains open here. [Fixed source and earlier constant-fraction degree bound](algebraic-degree-of-a-compact-physical-sat-indexer.md)

The algebraic input is the November finite-Fourier paper's **Tensor powers of a shear** lemma, which expressly covers every field, including characteristic two. The proof uses radix three in that characteristic. Over $\mathbb F_2$, exchanging both binary address labels turns its upper shear into the one-coordinate lower subset-zeta matrix $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. Thus the gate statement already gives $O_\beta(2^r(1+r)^\beta)$ binary XOR gates for $Z_r$ for every $\beta>1/2$. The uniform native bound below uses the construction of the lemma and the paper's explicitly effective prime-radix kernel. This theorem imports those results from the preprint. Jain's October manuscript had already stated $O(2^r r^\theta)$ for a fixed $\theta<1$, via a different network and an explicit four-coordinate transfer word. That manuscript was the starting point for the small-block search below. The later primary shear lemma gives the sharper exponent directly. ## Running the shear circuit on whole packed native words

Let $\beta>1/2$ be fixed. There is a uniform $O_\beta(2^m(1+m)^\beta)$-instruction native algorithm for $Z_m$ on $2^m$ words of independent Boolean lanes, with one XOR acting on all lanes. Here are the indexing and representation details to get this bound from the primary result.

For $m=2q$, the proof of the shear lemma supplies a fixed word of monomial matrices and Fourier blocks $D_3\oplus1$ on four coordinates implementing the two-axis shear. All its coefficients are in a fixed finite extension $K$ of $\mathbb F_2$ containing the third roots of unity. One can hardwire the word, or enumerate finite extensions and finite words once, since the stated existence and finite equality test guarantee termination. Taking $q$ tensor powers gives a constant number of letters. For a monomial letter, traverse the full quaternary digit tree, accumulating the output address after the fixed digit permutation and the scalar product of its one-digit diagonal values. The tree has $O(4^q)$ nodes. This gives $O(2^m)$ native indexing and arithmetic, including a scratch array for a permutation.

For one Fourier letter, classify each four-valued digit as active when it lies in the three-coordinate Fourier block. Its inactive value is unique. A sector mask $B\subseteq[q]$ has exactly $3^{|B|}$ coordinates. Compute all sector sizes and their prefix offsets in $O(2^q)$ native operations; the powers of three and offsets fit in $O(1)$ address words. Traverse the quaternary digit tree again, accumulating $B$ and the rank of its active digits in base three. At a leaf copy its value to the sector offset plus that rank. Visit the same tree to scatter the outputs. Thus gathering and scattering cost $O(4^q)$ over **all** sectors, without an address scan per output. The prime-radix algorithm applies $D_3^{\otimes |B|}$ in $O_\beta(3^{|B|}(1+|B|)^\beta)$ operations and index work on that sector. Sum over masks using $\sum_B3^{|B|}=4^q$. Scalar identities specialize under $\mathbb Z[1/3,\zeta_3]\longrightarrow K$ since three is a unit in characteristic two. The template uses the same predetermined scalar operations on every packed lane. Represent one packed $K$-valued vector by a fixed number of native words in an $\mathbb F_2$ basis. Each fixed-field linear gate, including scalar multiplication by a prepared constant, becomes a constant number of word XORs. Initialize all nonbase components to zero and read the base output component. Literal zero arrays require linear initialization, and sectors can be processed sequentially. On odd $m$ use the last binary shear directly. This establishes the native implementation claimed above.

## Recovering almost half the address degree at superlinear physical lengths

When $M<\sqrt t$ direct bit evaluation and packed verification cost $o(t)$. Otherwise use a power-of-two number $L=2^s=\Theta(\log t)$ of consecutive low lanes fitting in one native word. The remaining $m=\ell-s=\Theta(\log t)$ high coordinates give $2^m=O(M/\log t)$ packed words. At sufficiently large $m$ put
\[
       d=\left\lfloor m/2-A\sqrt{m\log(2+m)}\right\rfloor .
\]
Use a sufficiently large fixed $A$ depending on the bit-query polynomial; rounding the square-root cutoff constructibly within one does not affect the estimate. Sample every physical low lane at high masks of weight at most $d$. The fraction of such high masks is at most $\exp(-2A^2\log(2+m))$ by the binomial tail bound. Enumerating masks, querying their valid lanes and Möbius-inverting on this downward-closed set cost $o(t)$, even at the stated larger length, since $A$ absorbs every fixed polynomial in $m$. Assign zero to invalid addresses. Fill the dense high table with these coefficient words and zero outside the cutoff. For any valid physical bit the submasks on its lane are valid, so if a degree-$d$ extension exists these coefficients give the correct value there. Invalid high masks cannot affect smaller physical outputs.

Apply the packed transform just proved, choosing $1/2<\beta<1/2+\eta$. Its word time is
\[
 O_\beta\!\left(\frac{M}{\log t}(\log t)^\beta\right)
          =O_\beta\!\left(\frac{t}{(\log t)^{1/2+\eta-\beta}}\right)=o(t).
\]
The packed ordinary verifier costs $O(1+M/\log(2+M))=o(t)$ as well. On files without a suitable extension the same resource cap applies to the reconstructed bytes, using a clocked verifier on malformed output if necessary. If all sufficiently large lengths had a degree-$d$ extension this would decide $H$ nondeterministically in $o(t)$. Hardwire any finite exceptions, or apply the fixed-source interval contradiction directly to the new opponent. Infinitely many lengths therefore fail the cutoff and $m=\ell-O(\log\ell)$ there. ## A uniform route from only a qualitative gate saving

The rest of the note considers $M=O(t)$, including elementary variants independent of the finite Fourier result. For the Boolean zeta XOR gate count $c_r$, the following construction would already suffice under the weaker premise $\liminf_{r\to\infty}c_r/(r2^r)=0$. It is useful when the only input is a circuit-size statement with no efficient printer.

## Choosing an entire gate list uniformly

On $M<\sqrt t$, direct bit evaluation and packed verification cost $o(t)$. Otherwise choose a power-of-two group of $L=2^s=\Theta(\log t)$ successive low address lanes fitting a machine word. Write $m=\ell-s=\Theta(\log t)$ for the number of high coordinates. For $1\le r\le R=\max(1,\lfloor(\log_2 m)/4\rfloor)$, enumerate binary XOR straight-line circuits with $2^r$ inputs and at most $r2^{r-1}$ gates. That bound is attained by the ordinary $r$ butterfly sweeps. Copies are free, and the constant zero can be included. To verify a proposed circuit attach a length-$2^r$ coefficient bitset to each wire. Start with the unit vectors and XOR these bitsets gate by gate. All $2^r$ required row masks of $Z_r$ must occur. Their positions supply the output wire list. The linear-form bitsets fit in a native word at these dimensions. Exhaustive enumeration uses $2^{O(r^2 2^r)}$ trials times polynomial work in $r2^r$, since each gate specifies two prior wires. Summing across $r\le R$ costs $2^{o(m)}=o(t)$. It produces a minimum circuit for each tested $r$ without requiring a rate of convergence, a known size threshold, or uniform access to any larger circuit family.

Choose the tested $r$ minimizing $(c_r+2^r)/(r2^r)$, retaining its gate and output lists. The normalized cost tends to zero under the stated liminf bound: take a subsequence of unbounded $r$ along which both $c_r/(r2^r)$ and $1/r$ tend to zero. The prefix minimum eventually sees any fixed member of that subsequence.

## Tensoring over actual packed lanes

Fix a sufficiently large constant $A$. Take

$$
 d=\left\lfloor m/2-A\sqrt{m\log(2+m)}\right\rfloor
$$

at large $m$, or use an integer ceiling on the square root computed by comparisons. On each high address of weight at most $d$, gather its $L$ original file bits into a word. Assign arbitrary zeros outside the physical file. On this downward-closed family compute the coefficient words by the standard coordinate sweeps. Its size $S$ satisfies $S/2^m\le\exp(-2A^2\log(2+m))$ by the binomial tail bound. Both physical querying and the sparse transform therefore cost $o(t)$ for sufficiently large fixed $A$, absorbing all polynomial factors in $m$, address enumeration and lookup time. Fill a dense array of $2^m$ words with these coefficients and zeros above the degree cutoff. The initialization cost $O(2^m)$ fits the budget.

Apply $Z_m$ to these words. Partition the high coordinates into consecutive groups of $r$, with a final block shorter than $r$. For each full block and every setting of the other high coordinates, gather its $2^r$ packed words into the circuit's input wires. Evaluate the chosen XOR list wordwise and store all $2^r$ output words back into their original fiber. Within a fiber, defer the writes until the old inputs have been collected. A contiguous group of coordinate positions permits each address to be formed by masks and shifts in a constant number of native operations. The whole low address lane remains in place. Applying a block transform sums over its submasks and successive disjoint transforms compose to $Z_m$. Handle the final block by its ordinary coordinate sweeps. Hence the bound is

$$
 O\left(\left\lfloor\frac mr\right\rfloor 2^{m-r}(c_r+2^r)+r2^m+2^{o(m)}\right)
 =o(m2^m)+o(t)=o(t).
$$

This uses the single shared circuit across fibers. It does not charge a free large transpose or require a gate-per-byte interpreter. If a physical file has an extension of degree at most $d$, the output words agree with the whole file. Indeed for a valid address every submask in that low lane is physically earlier or equal. The zero padding at invalid addresses cannot influence its recovered coefficients. The same timed algorithm runs within budget when the degree cap fails; use a capped packed verifier on its potentially malformed output.

The source lies outside nondeterministic $o(t)$ time on this word RAM. If every sufficiently large length met the degree cutoff, the native opponent could run the convolution above and the packed nondeterministic verifier in $o(t)$, deciding $H$ on all sufficiently large lengths. Finite exceptions can be hardwired in a fixed decider, or one can apply the interval lemma directly on late intervals of this opponent. Thus infinitely many lengths fail, necessarily with $M\ge\sqrt t$ since the other branch uses the exact file. Finally $m=\ell-O(\log\ell)$ on those lengths. ## An elementary version with one group of coordinates

There is an upstream-independent saving if the indexer has a public contiguous group of $b$ high address coordinates with $b=o(m)$ and $b/\log m\to\infty$. Suppose all restrictions obtained by fixing the other high coordinates and any physical low lane have degree at most $(1/2-\epsilon)b$ in that group, for fixed $\epsilon>0$. For each fixing query only the within-group high masks of weight up to the cap and recover their coefficients. The query fraction is at most $\exp(-2\epsilon^2 b)=m^{-\omega(1)}$. No truth table shared between different fixings is assumed. A full ordinary $b$-sweep on each fiber expands all its remaining words in $O(b2^m)=o(t)$ steps, with no dependence on $c_r$. Consequently even a public middle-sized coordinate group with this uniform degree property is impossible at all sufficiently large lengths for an indexer for $H$. Equivalently there are infinitely many hard lengths having some fixing with group degree above $(1/2-\epsilon)b$, provided the local degree on a truncated file is interpreted by existence of a consistent extension on that fiber. A polynomial of globally bounded degree $d>(b+1)$ can contain all $b$ variables of every such small group, so the group assertion alone does not give the preceding global near-half bound.

For a public catalog of groups the reconstruction algorithm need not be told which guaranteed group to use. When the total sizes of the groups are $o(m)$ and the sum of the sparse query bounds is $o(t)$, generate each candidate. To resolve conflicting candidates, find a valid physical bit where two survivors differ by comparing their packed words, query the true bit there, and eliminate one. Each elimination costs $O(2^m+\operatorname{poly}(m))$, using shifts to locate a differing bit within one word. Identical survivors can be merged at the same cost. At most one pass per eliminated candidate is necessary. If any candidate was exact, the final candidate is exact. There are consequently infinitely many lengths on which none of the catalog's group-degree guarantees hold.

## A sparse alternative at globally bounded degree

Even without a fast dense transform, each nonzero high-monomial coefficient word at support $S$ can be added directly to its $2^{m-|S|}$ supersets. List those supermasks in constant amortized native work per output by incrementing through binary submasks of the complement of $S$, or use a depth-first binary choice tree on its free positions. The latter has at most twice as many nodes as output words, after $O(m)$ setup for each actual nonzero coefficient. All these setup costs fit the $S(m,d)\operatorname{poly}(m)=o(t)$ interpolation work. For an integer cutoff $b$ evaluate the degree-$\le b$ part together by packed coefficient descent, then add the remaining nonzero high-degree words directly. Let $k_j$ count nonzero coefficient words with $j$ high positions. The full bound after taking the same lower half-ball samples is

$$
 O\!\left(o(t)+2^m\left(1+b+\sum_{j>b} k_j 2^{-j}\right)\right).
$$

For a truncated physical file discard high monomial words beyond the final high address, and mask invalid lanes in the last high word. Such entries do not contribute to any larger valid address. On every valid lane submask, interpolation agrees with an honest degree-bounded extension. Thus every retained nonzero coefficient word requires at least one monomial of that extension with its specified high support: evaluation over the physical low lanes is a Boolean function, and a nonzero value on some lane guarantees a nonzero low monomial in its full extension.

Here is a quantitative unconditional consequence for the *same* hierarchy input on infinitely many lengths. Either the file has no extension of degree $\le d$, or every such extension has exponentially many monomials with high support greater than $m/p$, where $p$ is a sufficiently large fixed integer depending on the indexer and source interpreter. Indeed use the quota opponent, set $b=\lfloor m/p\rfloor$, and compute the weighted sum in the displayed bound after interpolation. If it exceeds $m/p$, return a fixed fallback. Otherwise generate the whole predicted file and run its capped packed verifier. This costs $O(t/p)+o(t)$ with the constant uniform in $p$. On assuming the degree promise, failure of the weighted-sum test is the only route to disagreement. Pick $p$ large enough for the diagonal interval and sufficiently late intervals assigned to this opponent. At a disagreement with true degree $\le d$, the retained nonzero words satisfy $\sum_{j>b}k_j2^{-j}>m/p$. Consequently the count at high supports above $b$ exceeds $(m/p)2^{b+1}$. The weighted sum can be compared exactly using integer multiples of $2^{-m}$; boundedly many native limbs suffice since the numerator is at most $2^{2m}$.

In particular the source cannot always have representatives combining degree $\ell/2-O(\sqrt{\ell\log\ell})$ (with the stated sufficiently large constant) and $2^{o(\ell)}$ total monomials. A sparse high-degree tail and a dense low-degree part are both allowed. This trade uses neither the external transform claim nor an assumption about SAT solvers.

A single two-way split of a globally degree-$d$ high polynomial gave no improvement here: assign each monomial to a half in which its degree is at most half the global cap, clipping ties in an agreed order. The remaining half can be transformed cheaply while the low-degree half is still compressed; the two final restricted-half expansions together still contain $O(d2^m)$ word work. The mixed monomials must be present in one of the two expansions. This bookkeeping bounds that implementation only. ## What a general packed XOR evaluator must pay

The same primary Fourier paper's **batched lower bound** gives the converse $c_m=\Omega(2^m\sqrt m)$ for the full binary zeta transform. Combined with the cited upper bound the logarithmic exponent for this transform is $1/2$. This investment in evaluating every unrestricted coefficient table differs from the sparse interpolation step above.

Take $m=2q$, work over $\mathbb F_4$, and write $B=Z_2$. Any binary XOR circuit for $Z_m$ computes the same matrix on $\mathbb F_4$ data. We first express a ternary Fourier block $F_3\oplus 1$ as a fixed word in $B$ and invertible monomial four-coordinate matrices. Number coordinates 1 through 4 and let $D_i(a)$ scale coordinate $i$ by $a$. Since $B^2=I$, $Be_4=e_4$ and $e_4^{\mathsf T}B=(1,1,1,1)$,
\[
 B D_4(a) B D_4(a)^{-1}
   =I+(a-1)e_4(e_1+e_2+e_3)^{\mathsf T}=L .
\]
The commutator $L D_1(b)L^{-1}D_1(b)^{-1}$ is an elementary shear with coefficient $(a-1)(1-b^{-1})$ at $(4,1)$. Take $a,b$ nonzero and different from one. Diagonal rescaling and permutations now provide every elementary shear, and Gaussian elimination provides $F_3\oplus1$. All inverses used here are constant words in the same alphabet. The finite audit supplies a concrete word using 24 occurrences of $B$.

Apply that word in $q$ tensor factors simultaneously. Since $B^{\otimes q}=Z_{2q}$, the result $(F_3\oplus1)^{\otimes q}$ costs $O(c_{2q}+4^q)$ gates over $\mathbb F_4$, allowing a linear number of scalar multiplications for each tensor monomial. Coordinates with $r$ active ternary digits form $\binom qr$ independent $F_3^{\otimes r}$ sectors. We need many transforms **of one common order**, as required by the lower theorem. Put $r_0=\lfloor3q/4\rfloor$ and retain only sectors with $r_0\le r\le r_0+\delta\sqrt q$, for a sufficiently small fixed $\delta>0$. Their total coordinate volume $V_\delta$ is at least $c_\delta4^q$ for all large $q$: after multiplying by $3^r/4^q$ these binomial coefficients are probabilities for $\mathrm{Bin}(q,3/4)$, and Stirling's formula gives $\Omega_\delta(q^{-1/2})$ for every included point. Unneeded inputs may be set to zero and unneeded outputs discarded.

In each retained sector undo the transform on $r-r_0$ ternary axes by ordinary fixed-size inverse butterflies. This additional work is at most $C\delta\sqrt q\,V_\delta$ with an absolute $C$. The final map is exactly $V_\delta/3^{r_0}$ independent transforms on the common group $(\mathbb Z/3)^{r_0}$. Their order is invertible in $\mathbb F_4$, which contains a primitive cube root. The primary batched theorem gives a lower bound $c V_\delta\sqrt{r_0\log_2 3}$. For sufficiently small fixed $\delta$ the extra work is at most half this. Hence $c_{2q}=\Omega(4^q\sqrt q)$. For odd $m$ use $c_{m+1}\le2c_m+2^m$, by applying the binary shear on the last axis directly. This proof only uses the primary batched Fourier lower bound and elementary fixed matrices. [Equal-order batched Fourier lower theorem](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/build/intro.tex) · [Fixed word, sector packing and truncation checks](zeta_ternary_sector_audit.py)

Even an evaluator given coefficients only through degree $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, with higher ones known zero, obeys this lower bound in binary XOR circuits. Let $P$ keep indices of weight through $\lfloor m/2\rfloor$, and let $J$ complement each index. Put $R=Z_m P$. On these low-weight output rows $Rx=Z_mx$ for every $x$, since all contributing submasks are low. The identity $JZ_m^{\mathsf T}J=Z_m$ gives $JR^{\mathsf T}J=(JPJ)Z_m$, supplying every row whose complement is low. The two sets of output rows cover the cube. Transpose a linear XOR circuit by initializing adjoints from its output wires and sending a gate's accumulated adjoint to both predecessors in reverse order. This costs at most twice its gate count plus linear input/output overhead. Combining the two circuits thus produces full $Z_m$ with $O(\operatorname{size}(R)+2^m)$ gates, which proves the assertion. This argument stops short of the lower interpolation cutoff $m/2-A\sqrt{m\log m}$ used in the hierarchy theorem. The circuit lower bounds concern XOR operations on unrestricted coefficient words and do not price native instructions in an arbitrary SAT decider. Priority of these transfers has not been established.
