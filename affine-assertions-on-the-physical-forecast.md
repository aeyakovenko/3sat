# Affine assertions on the physical forecast

At every sufficiently large width, the full physical register is the only time-independent inductive affine flat containing the initialized state. Allowing arbitrary elapsed-time offsets, but keeping one common direction space, gives exactly two possibilities: the full register at every time or the singleton actual state at every time. On a zero width, a valid assertion of the second kind needs a probe of every register bit to recognize the actual state if memory is supplied by individual bit probes.

Use the binary register of length $D$ equal to the greatest physical lag. Immediately before tick $t$ its entry $j$ is $z_{t-1-j}$. Write $S e_j=e_{j+1}$ for $j<D-1$ and $S e_{D-1}=0$. The two tap-bank parities are linear functionals $a,b$. At least one of $a,b$ takes value one on $e_{D-1}$, since the two tap sets are disjoint and one contains lag $D$. The autonomous transition is
$$
                 F(x)=Sx+e_0a(x)b(x).
$$
Let $x_0$ be the initialized register and $x_t=F^t(x_0)$. If the observed stream bit is at tick $H-1$, the physical verdict is $x_H[0]$. The forecast uses $H=PkW^5+s+1$. ## Proper fixed affine flats

Consider a nonempty flat $A=v+U$ with $F(A)\subseteq A$. If $a|_U,b|_U$ are independent, choose $u,u'\in U$ with respective tap pairs $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. The four images under $F$ of $v,v+u,v+u',v+u+u'$ have sum in $U$. Their shift contributions cancel. The second difference of the quadratic feedback is $a(u)b(u')+a(u')b(u)=1$, so their sum is $e_0$. For each $w\in U$, the difference $F(v+w)+F(v)$ is $Sw$ plus a multiple of $e_0$. It follows that $SU\subseteq U$. Iterating on $e_0$ gives every standard basis vector in $U$.

Thus a proper inductive flat has dependent restricted tap forms. The image of $A$ under $(a,b)$ lies in an affine line in $\mathbb F_2^2$, where one of $a,b,a+b$ is constant. On each such line $ab$ agrees with one of $0,a,b$: if $a$ or $b$ is fixed this follows by substitution; if $a+b=0$ then $ab=a$, and if $a+b=1$ then $ab=0$. This gives an exact test for a proposed proper flat. Require such a line, take a corresponding linear form $\ell\in\{0,a,b\}$, and set $T=S+e_0\ell$. The two remaining conditions are $Tv+v\in U$ and $TU\subseteq U$, since $F=T$ on $A$.

## Three tap pairs on the initialized trajectory

In the explicit slot construction, the following patterns occur before $H$ at every sufficiently large width. They concern the two tap **inputs**, so an off-slot zero output by itself would not establish the final pattern.

- The two seeded copies of $K_1$ feed a $K_0$ slot. Both bank inputs there are one, giving $(1,1)$. The constant-copy construction repeats this in subsequent blocks.
- The dedicated import slot multiplies the old output in the left bank by a known local one in the right bank. Take a fixed startup-complete block, for instance block $1$ after the first constant copies are produced. The imported output is from block $1-k(W-2)<0$. Both negative seeds occupy $K_1$ slots distinct from the output slot, so this import is zero and the tap pair is $(0,1)$. At each designated slot the two-sum isolation property of its marks allows only intended arrivals.
- A bit that is one occurs at a slot mark, including both historical seeds. Every delay has residue $s_j-s_i$ for an edge between two marks. Any arrival at all must therefore have residue $s_h+s_j-s_i\bmod P$ for some triple of marks. For $m$ marks there are at most $m^3$ possible residues. The explicit parameters use $q>m$, $B=3q$, $s_m\ge B^2m$, and $P>3\max_i s_i>m^3$. A residue outside the arrival set has pair $(0,0)$ in every nonnegative block. The assertion that ones occur only at marks follows from the construction's three-sum isolation and the two seed locations. [Controller compilation and import slot](one-variable-delay-and-one-binary-product.md#compiling-a-fixed-controller-to-one-product)

These three pairs span the affine plane, whereas each proper inductive flat has tap image contained in a line. Induction would include the three actual states in any fixed inductive flat containing $x_0$. Hence that flat must be the whole register. It contains registers with first bit one even on widths where the actual verdict is zero. This argument concerns closure under $F$ on the entire offered flat.

## A common direction and arbitrary time-dependent offsets

Suppose $A_t=v_t+U$ for $0\le t\le H$, with arbitrary offsets and one subspace $U$. Require $x_0\in A_0$ and $F(A_t)\subseteq A_{t+1}$ for each $t<H$. Then $x_t\in A_t$ throughout, so we may use $x_t$ as the offset when taking differences. Each such difference of outputs is in the same $U$.

If $a|_U,b|_U$ have rank two, the four-point argument on one slice again yields $e_0\in U$, then $SU\subseteq U$ and $U=\mathbb F_2^D$. If the restrictions have rank one, there is a unique nonzero formal combination $p\in\{a,b,a+b\}$ vanishing on $U$. There is also a nonzero linear functional $d$ on $U$ such that, for every $w\in U$,
$$
           F(x_t+w)+F(x_t)=Sw+c_t e_0 d(w).
$$
For $p=a$ take $d=b|_U$ and $c_t=a(x_t)$. For $p=b$ take $d=a|_U$ and $c_t=b(x_t)$. For $p=a+b$, the two restrictions coincide; take their common value as $d$ and use $c_t=1+a(x_t)+b(x_t)$, by $d(w)^2=d(w)$. Each nonzero linear form on the tap pair takes both values among the three observed pairs. Thus $c_t$ takes both values at times strictly below $H$. Fix $w$ with $d(w)=1$ and add the two displayed differences for these times. This gives $e_0\in U$, again implying $SU\subseteq U$ and $U=\mathbb F_2^D$. In particular rank one cannot occur here, since the actual tap forms are independent on the full register.

In rank zero we have $SU\subseteq U$ directly and both tap forms vanish throughout $U$. If $0\ne w\in U$ has first nonzero coordinate $j$, then $S^{D-1-j}w=e_{D-1}\in U$. At least one tap reads this last coordinate, which rules out such $w$. Hence $U=0$.

Both possible families satisfy initialization and preservation: the full spaces do so, and the singleton slices are necessarily $\{x_t\}$ with $x_{t+1}=F(x_t)$. To prove a zero verdict we additionally require $A_H\subseteq\{x:x[0]=0\}$. The full spaces always fail this last requirement. In the remaining family, fix any $t$ and consider a deterministic exact membership test with elapsed time and nonuniform coefficients supplied for free. On the actual $x_t$, each of the $D$ bit coordinates must be probed. Otherwise flipping one unprobed bit preserves every answer along that adaptive transcript while changing membership. The cost statement counts individual bit probes to an arbitrary offered register, as in the journal's assertion verifier. Varying the direction space with time changes the preservation condition used in the proof.

## One affine equation with a different normal at every time

Even without any common direction, **one parity equation on the whole physical word at each elapsed time is insufficient**. Its coefficients may be entirely nonuniform and it may inspect all $D$ bits. There is an exact finite-horizon criterion. Identify a parity normal $p$ with the polynomial whose coefficient at $z^j$ is its coefficient on $X_j$. Let $a$ denote the bank with the one long tap and $b$ the other bank. Write $\nu(p)$ for the smallest exponent in the nonzero normal $p$. For all sufficiently large $W$, and every $H\ge1$, a chain of nonempty affine hyperplanes containing the initial state at time zero, preserved at each step and certifying $X_0=0$ at time $H$, exists exactly when at least one of the following tests succeeds:
$$
  G\in\{a,b,a+b\},\qquad H-1\le\nu(G),\qquad
           (z^{-(H-1)}G)(x_0)=c_G,\qquad
  (c_a,c_b,c_{a+b})=(0,0,1).
$$
An arbitrary Boolean function of one parity per slice can also accept the full space or the empty set. Containment of the genuine trajectory rules out empty slices. A full slice cannot precede any of the hyperplanes required by the proof.

To see this, the terminal normal must be $1$ with offset zero, since its whole flat has to lie in $X_0=0$. When a target normal $p$ has constant coefficient zero, its preimage under $F$ is the hyperplane with normal $p/z$ and the same offset. Inclusion of another nonempty hyperplane of the same dimension forces equality. When the coefficient is one, put $w=(p-1)/z$. The preimage is a level set of $a(X)b(X)+w(X)$. If this is constant on a hyperplane with normal $u$, the polarization $a(v)b(v')+a(v')b(v)$ vanishes on all $v,v'\in\ker u$. Unless $u\in\mathrm{span}(a,b)$, the two tap functionals are independent on $\ker u$, giving a contradiction. Translating inside the proposed hyperplane by vectors of $\ker a\cap\ker b$ now shows that $w$ vanishes there too. Thus $w\in\mathrm{span}(a,b)$. The degree of $w$ is at most $D-2$. Since $a$ alone has the long tap at degree $D-1$, the only possibilities are $w=0,b$. Hence a nonlinear backward step is possible only from the normals $1$ and $1+zb$.

At the very first backward step from the target, the zero set of $ab$ contains precisely the three hyperplanes $a=0$, $b=0$, $a+b=1$. Their tap-pair images are the three lines avoiding $(1,1)$. Pick one of these normals $G$. Subsequent steps are forced linear shifts until the constant coefficient of $G/z^{\nu(G)}$ is reached. None of these stripped normals permits a second nonlinear backward step. The construction has a bounded tap and a long tap in $a$, at least two fixed taps in $b$, and disjoint banks. Both $a$ and $a+b$ retain a bounded lowest degree and the growing highest degree $D-1$. Their stripped degrees eventually exceed $1+\deg b$. The stripped $b$ has at least two terms and has degree strictly less than $1+\deg b$. This rules out both allowed target normals. The finite test above now follows in both directions by the forced shifts. The horizon $PkW^5+s+1$ eventually exceeds $1+\max_G\nu(G)$, a fixed constant. The obstruction holds on every true negative of the actual forecast irrespective of the solver being tested. The largest possible horizon in this class is attained for a suitable initialization, for instance the zero seed and the later-starting of $a$ and $b$. ## One SAT query for a public pool of coordinate pins

Let the physical shift register have $L=O(W)$ bits, $n=|\operatorname{bin}W|$, transition $F$, and observation $X_H[0]$ after $H=PkW^5+s_0+1$ steps. A **public** circuit $Q(t,a)$ takes a binary elapsed time $0\le t\le H$ and an address $0\le a<L$. It returns nothing or one pair $(i,v)$ with $1\le i\le m$ and $v\in\{0,1\}$. Type $i$ requires $X[a]=v$ at every address receiving its label at time $t$. A proposed invariant retains any fixed subset of the $m$ types at all times. Each time slice is a coordinate affine flat. No two types prescribe the same cell at the same time. There can be $\Theta(W)$ pins specified by one circuit, and the retained subset need not be generated. A valid subset contains the initialized state in its time-zero flat, is preserved for $t<H$, and forces $X_H[0]=0$.

A single ordinary formula is satisfiable exactly when **no** subset is valid. Guess deletion ranks $d_i\in\{1,\ldots,m,\infty\}$, with $\infty$ denoting a survivor. Each finite-ranked type $i$ supplies one of two witnesses.

* An address $a$ where $Q(0,a)=(i,v)$ disagrees with the known two-seed register.
* A time $t<H$ and an address $a$ with $Q(t+1,a)=(i,v)$, together with old bits that make $F(X)[a]\ne v$. For $a>0$ only $X[a-1]$ is needed. For $a=0$ the two fixed-size tap sets suffice. Equal old addresses must receive equal bits. At each such old address $b$, if $Q(t,b)=(j,u)$ with $d_j\ge d_i$, require the old bit to equal $u$.

Finally require that $Q(H,0)$ is absent, prescribes one, or has finite rank. Every row's unqueried cells extend to a full source register satisfying all types with rank at least its row's rank: set every prescribed cell to its unique required value and fill the rest freely. Thus by induction on finite ranks, no valid subset can contain a deleted type. Ties can be deleted together because the source already satisfies every type with the same rank. The final check gives a register with $X[0]=1$ satisfying all survivors. Conversely, start with all types. Whenever initialization fails, or a state satisfying the current types can violate one of them on its next step, delete a violated type and use the offending address as its witness. After at most $m$ deletions the survivors satisfy initialization and closure. If no valid subset exists, these survivors permit the final bad bit, giving the final check. For an empty pool the unconditional final bad state witnesses failure. This specializes the earlier [Houdini deletion test](guarded-covers.md); disjoint coordinate prescriptions eliminate the cost of evaluating the entire surviving conjunction on each counterexample state.

If the public circuit has $s$ bounded-fan-in gates, including its output interface, the failure test costs $g=O((m+1)(s+n+\log^2(m+2)))$ gates. Each row calls $Q$ a constant number of times. Dynamic reads of $d_j$ need not use $m$ multiplexers per read. Add one table record carrying each type and its guessed rank, attach the $O(m)$ lookup records, and guess a Beneš permutation sorting the records by type. Check adjacent sorted records with the same type for rank agreement and check all queried types are in range. Every lookup shares its group with its table record. Inactive records can be given a separate activity class. This uses $O(m\log^2(m+2))$ gates on the $O(\log(m+2))$-bit records. The elapsed-time and old-address arithmetic costs $O(n)$ per row after specializing the horizon and lag constants. The proper decimal 3-CNF occupies $O(g\log(g+2))$ bits. These costs include the guessed ranks, list and witness bits. With one type, a circuit that supplies the genuine register bit at every $(t,a)$ represents the singleton trajectory; no small circuit for that function on the learned prefix is known.

For a fixed uniform pool generator and exponent $c\ge1$, use the [learned clock construction](one-fixed-forecast-prefix-for-clocked-sat.md) with this one query, reporting success precisely on UNSAT. Under an $O(N^c)$ SAT hypothesis every sufficiently large width on **this** generator-and-emitter header has forecast zero. At most finitely many widths there can admit a valid subpool along any sequence with generation and explicit emission cost $o(W/n)$ and $(g\log(g+2)+1)^c=o(W/n)$. The subpool itself is chosen nonuniformly at no extra SAT exponent. Allowing the whole circuit $Q$ to vary nonuniformly would change that conclusion. Changing the public generator can change the header.

A calibration illustrates actual coverage at the native guest level. On another fixed wide-guest prefix, parse a list of legal CNFs $F_i$ from the width suffix, each with at most $n/2$ occurring variables. The guest enumerates every assignment to every $F_i$, setting mark $i$ on a satisfying assignment, and returns zero exactly when all marks are set. Invalid encodings may idle. There are at most $n$ files, so this complete search takes $2^{n/2}\operatorname{poly}(n)=o(W/n)$ guest steps. The actual forecast on every sufficiently large legal width is one exactly when every $F_i$ is satisfiable. At an assignment-test macrostep the feature for $i$ requires mark $i$ to stay zero. If some $F_i$ is UNSAT this feature is inductive even with an arbitrary assignment at that test. If every $F_i$ is SAT the actual run sets every mark, precluding a safe subpool. Here the failure CNF simplifies to the conjunction of the $F_i$ on disjoint variable sets. This covers every negative in the calibration family without identifying an unsatisfiable member. Its features concern the guest's marks; coverage on the physical self-referential header requires a separate argument.
