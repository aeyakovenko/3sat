# Opposite SAT deadlines on dense binary systems

A fixed finite family of correct SAT programs can be run on two ordinary files of equal encoded length $N$. Give program $i$ its own deadline $C_iN$ on each file, for a fixed positive integer $C_i$. If exactly one file is satisfiable, **any timely answer, of either sign, identifies the satisfiable file**. Trying all the fixed clocks costs $O(N)$ native logarithmic-word RAM steps, including reset of the cells written. We give two concrete sources of such pairs. The second produces an explicit distribution of pairs simultaneously slow for every member of the family under a stated average-case assumption. All workspace is unrestricted.

## Dense binary feasibility

Given a $d\times d$ binary matrix $A$ and a vector $b$, form the two existential tests
$$
              Ax=b,\qquad A^\top y=0\ \land\ b^\top y=1.
$$
If $b$ is in the column space the second test fails. Otherwise extend a basis of the column space by $b$ and choose a linear functional vanishing on the basis and taking value one on $b$. Its representing vector $y$ witnesses the second test.

Both tests compile to proper three-literal DIMACS in $O(d^2)$ bits and $O(d^2)$ native construction steps. Split the unknown vector into blocks of $s=\max(1,\lfloor(\log_2 d)/2\rfloor)$ bits. In each block compute every subset parity, extending a previous mask by one bit. An output row XORs at most one selected wire per block. For $O(d)$ rows this uses $O((d/s)2^s+d^2/s)=O(d^2/\log(d+2))$ bounded fan-in gates. Read the explicit coefficient bits to form the table indices in $O(d^2)$ steps. Transposition costs the same. This block construction also occurs in the journal's single-column verifier. [Earlier construction](live-gates-under-a-single-matrix-column.md) · [Arlazarov, Dinic, Kronrod and Faradžev](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=dan&option_lang=eng&paperid=35675&wshow=paper)

Give each gate a fresh dense output name and enforce its truth table by a constant number of clauses. Simplify repeated literals and constants. Three separate anchors pinned to zero by seven proper clauses supply two distinct false literals for padding unit and binary constraints on non-anchor names. A false constant after simplification can be imposed by excluding the anchors' remaining row. Each printed name needs $O(\log(d+2))$ digits. Precompute the decimal spellings of the identifiers with a digit counter, and copy each spelling when writing the rows. A sufficiently large fixed integer $K$ permits padding every file at dimension $d$ to exactly $K(d+1)^2$ bytes, by spacing the final zero token in its clause. Use the same length even when the gate count depends on the coefficients. Thus timely coverage of every such pair gives deterministic $O(d^2)$ column-space membership for arbitrary square binary matrices. One uniformly random right-hand side gives a one-sided nonsingularity test, since a proper column space contains at most half the possible right-hand sides. This last test has a random error probability.

## Decoding when there is one possible message

Fix $m=\lceil Cd\rceil$ for a positive constant $C$. Given $A\in\mathbb F_2^{m\times d}$, $y\in\mathbb F_2^m$, and a radius $k$, suppose exactly one message $x_*$ satisfies $\mathrm{wt}(Ax_*-y)\le k$. For each $u\in\mathbb F_2^d$ and $p\in\{0,1\}$ make the file
$$
     F_{u,p}:\quad \exists x\quad \mathrm{wt}(Ax-y)\le k
                  \quad\land\quad u^\top x=p .
$$
On this promise $F_{u,p}$ is SAT exactly when $p=u^\top x_*$. The block circuit above computes $Ax$. Summing the residual bits with binary adders and comparing with $k$ costs $O(m\log(m+2))$ gates even with a simple running counter. Add $O(d)$ gates to compute the masked parity. Enforce the indicated constraints and pad to the same $K(d+1)^2$ bytes, increasing $K$ depending on $C$. This gives deterministic $O(d^2)$ parity queries under coverage of every pair, and $O(d^3)$ recovery of the whole unknown message by choosing unit masks. It applies in particular to decoding within half the minimum distance of a full-column-rank code when a message is promised within radius $k$.

For fixed $(A,y,k)$ all $2^{d+1}$ files can be physical vertices of one cube. Use a selector and its complement for each mask bit and for the polarity, with their complement relation present in every file. Give each pair distinct positive identifiers ending in 2 and 3 in the same decade. A single positive pin with the two false anchors chooses a member of the pair by changing just the low bit of that last ASCII digit. The rest of the file computes the parity of the ANDs of selectors and message bits, and equates it with the polarity selector. All pins precede or follow a common body, and all their byte positions are distinct. Reserved unused identifiers require at most $O(d)$ further pins if a dense occurring-name convention is desired. The gate and byte bounds persist.

## Recovering the message when most deadlines expire

Fix a promised unique input. Suppose the portfolio gives a timely response on either file for a fraction at least $\rho$ of uniform masks. A successful race yields one exact linear equation in $x_*$. Until the rank reaches
$$
 r=\max\{0,d-\lceil\log_2(2/\rho)\rceil\},
$$
the span of the stored rows contains at most $(\rho/2)2^d$ masks. Each independently sampled mask therefore increases the rank with conditional probability at least $\rho/2$. The expected number of trials to rank $r$ is at most $2d/\rho$. Stop after $\lceil4d/\rho\rceil$ trials, giving success probability at least $1/2$ by Markov's inequality. At rank $r$ the number of consistent candidate messages is at most $4/\rho$, including when $r=0$. Enumerate this affine space, check $\mathrm{wt}(Ax-y)\le k$ directly, and recover its unique passing member. Elementary elimination and the linear races take $O(d^3/\rho)$ steps in total. This proof exploits the absence of erroneous equations. An earlier journal construction bounded the same codimension for a finite small-bias ensemble of negative votes. The broader use of random mask inner products as hard-core predicates goes back to Goldreich and Levin. [Earlier rank argument](isolating-a-proof-with-negative-sat-votes.md) · [Goldreich and Levin, STOC 1989](https://doi.org/10.1145/73007.73010)

## Conditional density of simultaneous misses

Fix efficiently samplable constants $0<\epsilon<\eta<1/2$ with efficiently computable $k=\lfloor\eta m\rfloor$, and an integer $C>1/(1-H_2(\eta))$, where $H_2$ denotes binary entropy. Sample the matrix $A$ and secret $x_*$ uniformly and independently, draw independent Bernoulli-$\epsilon$ noise bits $e$, and publish $A,y=Ax_*+e$. With probability $1-\exp(-\Omega(d))$, the secret is the unique qualifying message. In fact the upper tail $\mathrm{wt}(e)>k$ is exponentially small. For each fixed $z\ne0$, $Az+e$ is uniform over $m$ bits conditional on $e$, so the chance of a competing message is at most $(2^d-1)2^{-m(1-H_2(\eta))}$. Uniform sampling of a further mask and construction of either file use polynomial time. Dyadic noise rates permit the whole sampler to use $O(d^2)$ steps. The sampler that retains the secret can label both files correctly on the uniqueness event. [Blum, Kalai and Wasserman on the noisy-parity problem](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0010022)

**Hypothesis.** Every uniform randomized polynomial-time algorithm given $(A,y)$ in this experiment recovers the planted $x_*$ with negligible probability in $d$. Here negligible means smaller eventually than each inverse polynomial. This is the search learning parity with noise (search-LPN) hardness assumption for the indicated parameters.

**Consequence.** For every fixed finite family of correct SAT programs with fixed linear deadlines, the probability that the sampled mask gives a complementary pair on which **all programs miss both deadlines** is $1-\mathrm{negl}(d)$. Indeed, if the mean timely-reply fraction on good inputs, counted as zero on other inputs, is at least $d^{-a}$ along infinitely many dimensions, then at least $d^{-a}/2$ of inputs are good and have timely-reply fraction at least $d^{-a}/2$. Run the preceding recovery algorithm with that lower threshold. It takes polynomial time and recovers $x_*$ with probability at least $d^{-a}/4$ along those dimensions. This contradicts the hypothesis. The exceptional uniqueness probability is negligible as well.

The distribution is independent of the program descriptions. Appending any fixed additional correct program just enlarges the family in the statement. The same density conclusion holds for fixed polynomial deadlines, since the recovery procedure still runs in polynomial time. For any fixed $a>0$ choose a uniform base mask and vary the first $\lceil a\log_2 d\rceil$ mask coordinates freely, together with polarity. Each mask in this random translate has uniform marginal distribution. A union bound over polynomially many masks gives probability $1-\mathrm{negl}(d)$ that every file on this entire physical face overruns every clock. Within the face each polarity pair has opposite answers on the uniqueness event.

The race and reconstruction are unconditional reductions. A lower bound on ordinary unrestricted RAM-SAT time still requires establishing computational hardness. Priority for this specific packaging into same-length proper files and physical faces is unestablished.
