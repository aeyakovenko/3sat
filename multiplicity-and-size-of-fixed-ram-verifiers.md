# How many witnesses a short fixed CNF for list distinctness must have

Write $t=2^w$, $B=2tw$, and let the $B$ Boolean variables $x$ give $t$ unrestricted records of $2w$ bits. A proper CNF $H_w(x,y)$ has public variables $x$ and private variables $y$. Its clauses and their literal names are fixed at this width. On every fixed $x$ there is a private satisfying assignment exactly when the records are distinct. Write $M(x)$ for the number of such assignments and $m$ for the number of **distinct clauses**. The assertions below permit different templates at each width and impose no uniformity or workspace restriction on their construction.

1. If every positive $M(x)$ is odd, then $m=\Omega(Bw^\sigma)$ for a fixed $\sigma>0$. In ordinary ASCII DIMACS the fixed core requires **$\Omega(t w^{2+\sigma})$ bits**. The same holds when every positive input has exactly one witness. A deterministic logarithmic-word RAM decides this list predicate in $O(t)$ instructions by radix sorting.
2. More generally, for each fixed prime $p$, if every positive $M(x)$ is nonzero modulo $p^s$, then $p^s m=\Omega_p(Bw^{\sigma_p})$, for all large $w$ and any integer $s\ge1$. If positive inputs have at most $k$ witnesses, taking $2^s$ between $k+1$ and $2(k+1)$ yields $(k+1)m=\Omega(Bw^\sigma)$. Unused private variables can contribute to the multiplicity.
3. Suppose $m=O(B)$. For each fixed $p$ there is a power $p^{s(w)}=\Omega_p(w^{\sigma_p}/\log(w+2))$ such that **at least $2^{B-o(B)}$ positive inputs have $p^{s(w)}\mid M(x)$**. There need not be a single input serving every prime. The estimate is a logarithmic counting rate; the fraction of positive inputs supplied by the proof can tend to zero.

These are consequences of OpenAI's list-distinctness network theorem over a fixed finite field. The contributions here are the dependence on witness multiplicity, the extra name-length factor for fixed explicit clauses, and the number of positive inputs forced to have divisible counts. The upstream list bound is the external lower bound used in all three conclusions. ## An exact finite-field test for divisibility

For the moment retain only the occurring private variables. Their number is at most $3m$. The count with all declared private variables is the resulting count times a nonnegative power of two. If the full count is nonzero modulo $p^s$, so is the reduced count. Conversely divisibility of the reduced count implies divisibility of the full one. Every public list bit is essential to distinctness: duplicate one pair of rows, then change that particular bit in one of them and choose all other records to avoid the pair before and after the flip. Thus $B\le3m$. Discard duplicate clauses, which change no satisfying assignment.

Over $\mathbb F_p$ put $K=p^s-1$. Build a binary-wire tensor network with $K$ ordered slots for private assignments. Active slots form an initial segment of positive length; the inactive slots are forced to a single dummy bit string. In each active slot check all $m$ clauses on the common $x$ and that private assignment. For consecutive active slots require strictly increasing lexicographic order on their private strings. For an initial segment of length $j$, multiply its contribution by $(-1)^{j+1}$, using one $-1$ weight at each active slot after the first. All comparison auxiliaries and Boolean gates are functional. Copying each input to its clause occurrences and the required slots costs $O(Km)$ trivalent sites; the strict-order tests also cost $O(Km)$. Wires for inactive assignments are pinned conditional on the slot flag. This counts each unordered $j$-subset of the satisfying assignments once, with precisely the displayed weight. For reduced multiplicity $a$ the contraction is therefore
$$
          \sum_{j=1}^{K}(-1)^{j+1}\binom aj
          = [a\not\equiv0\pmod {p^s}]\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_p .
$$
At $a=0$ the sum vanishes. For $a>0$ Pascal's identity gives $\sum_{j=0}^{K}(-1)^j\binom aj=(-1)^K\binom{a-1}K$. In $\mathbb F_p$ the sign factor equals one. In the expansion of $(1+z)^{a-1}$ use the base-$p$ digits and $(1+z)^{p^i}=1+z^{p^i}$. The coefficient of $z^{p^s-1}$ is one exactly when all the bottom $s$ digits of $a-1$ equal $p-1$, and otherwise zero. This proves the identity for arbitrarily large multiplicities. It extends the journal's sorted-witness inclusion–exclusion for bounded ambiguity. [Earlier one-sort construction](one-sort-for-bounded-ambiguity-sat.md)

The number $K$ of witness slots is exact for a black-box membership test of this modular predicate when the witness universe has at least $K$ elements. Restrict all but $K$ candidate witnesses to be absent. On the free $K$ membership bits the predicate becomes OR, of multilinear degree $K$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Any weighted sum of adaptive branches making fewer than $K$ membership queries has degree less than $K$: the indicator of one branch is a product of its queried literals. The sorted-subset construction attains the degree bound. This argument concerns black-box access to the witness set; reading the clauses permits additional methods.

Under the stated nondivisibility promise the network is exactly the distinctness tensor, so its volume is at least $Bw^{\sigma_p}$ by the list theorem. This proves the clause–multiplicity inequality, with a constant independent of $s$. For the ASCII claim, the literal encodings of $m$ distinct clauses give $m$ distinct strings over a fixed alphabet. At least half have length $\Omega(\log(m+1))$, by counting all shorter strings. Omitting garbage variables and removing duplicates only makes this estimate smaller. Hence a serialization retaining this core needs $\Omega(m\log(m+1))$ bits. With $p=2,s=1$, combine this with $m=\Omega(Bw^\sigma)$ and $\log B=\Theta(w)$.

## An unambiguous verifier lower bound for ordinary 3-SAT

A nondeterministic bounded-fan-in Boolean circuit has ordinary file-input wires, extra Boolean witness wires, and one output. Its verdict is YES if some witness makes the output one. It is unambiguous when exactly one witness accepts each satisfiable file. **Every unambiguous circuit for ordinary 3-SAT at encoded length $N$ needs $\Omega(N(\log N)^\sigma)$ gates.** This also holds for circuits where acceptance on all files is the parity of the witness count, even if some negative files have a nonzero even count. It holds at every sufficiently large byte-aligned length for the dense proper ASCII encoding.

Use the journal's bit projection from the list to an ordinary length-$N$ file satisfiable exactly when two records agree. Here $N=\Theta(B)$, with the same bounded-ratio padding to intervening lengths. For each witness string a circuit has a unique gate evaluation. Represent each gate by its truth table, and supply COPY trees for fanout. A single contraction over $\mathbb F_2$ therefore gives the parity of the witness count, which for an unambiguous verifier is its decision. Unused witness bits can be discarded for unambiguous circuits (a positive input rules out such free ambiguity); a parity verifier with an unused witness bit would output zero identically. To get the distinctness tensor instead of duplication, complement by including the empty slot choice and using the weights $(-1)^j$ in the modular formula. For a single parity copy one may equivalently use a control bit with a constant-one branch and a negative copy of the constrained verifier branch; pin the disabled branch's internal bits to a unique dummy configuration. This adds only a constant factor to the number of sites. Alternatively, replace the accepting gate-output condition by a fixed XOR with an extra constant contribution via the same control. The number of sites is $O(G+B)$ for $G$ verifier gates, since all actually used witness bits feed gate inputs. The preprint's list bound gives the claim.

The difference between unrestricted and unambiguous witnesses is attained on a concrete promise. Given the promise of one of the projected list files, an ordinary nondeterministic $O(N)$-gate circuit guesses two $w$-bit indices $i<j$. Multiplexer trees read their $2w$ bits from the fixed digit positions of the input, and the circuit accepts exactly on equality. It has one accepting witness per colliding pair. A parity or unambiguous circuit even on this promise has the superlinear lower bound above. Equivalently, on the unrestricted $B$ list-input bits themselves, duplication is one explicit total function with linear nondeterministic circuit size and superlinear unambiguous circuit size. The linear upper bound on SAT files uses the stated promise. [Encoding, injective occurrence of data digits, and complemented list verdict](checking-the-sat-circuit-bound.md)

A general existential verifier with at most $k$ accepting witnesses on positive files obeys $(k+1)(G+B)=\Omega(Bw^\sigma)$ by the same sorted-slot test. In fact $G=\Omega(B)$ on the projection: each of the $B$ independent list bits must influence the composed circuit through some literal input of a gate, and each gate has bounded fan-in. Thus $(k+1)G=\Omega(N(\log N)^\sigma)$. The bound covers any depth, with arbitrary distinct circuits for distinct file lengths.

## Many positive inputs must have divisible counts

Take $j=\lfloor\log_2\log_2(w+2)\rfloor$ (for large $w$) and $u=t/2^j$. Fix the first $t-u$ records to **any** ordered list of distinct $2w$-bit words starting with zero. In the remaining $u$ records pin the leading $2j$ bits to ones. The free bits form the original hard list at width $w-j$ with $B'=2u(w-j)=\Theta(B/\log(w+2))$ exterior bits. The frozen records do not collide with the free ones. The template after pinning has at most $m$ distinct residual clauses; short or constant clauses need only constant local network size. Apply the preceding argument at this width **for each separate choice of the frozen list**. One may keep the same original private variable set, or use the reduced-count implication above.

Since $m=O(B)$ and $w-j=\Theta(w)$, choose the same power $p^{s(w)}$ for all these restrictions just below a sufficiently small constant times $w^{\sigma_p}/\log(w+2)$. If every positive in a restriction had count nonzero modulo this power, the resulting exact network would have volume strictly below the list lower bound at $w-j$. So there is a positive completion with divisible count for each frozen list. There are at least $(2^{2w-2})^{t-u}=2^{B-o(B)}$ frozen lists for large $w$, even after requiring distinctness. Positive completions from different lists are different.

The same counting argument applies to linear-size existential circuits for **duplication** by including the empty choice in the complementing sum. On each restricted subproblem every positive collision must have some accepting witness. Unless one positive count is divisible by the stated power, the complementing sum is exactly distinctness. The ASCII projection is injective because every row digit occurs at predetermined positions. Consequently any $O(N)$-gate nondeterministic verifier on the projected SAT files must have positive counts divisible by a growing power of $p$ on at least $2^{B-o(B)}$ distinct satisfiable files of size $N=\Theta(B)$. The linear pair-guessing verifier above shows this statement has actual examples.

A portfolio of different isolation attempts can be defeated on the **same** file at a fixed prime. Give attempt $i$ a circuit with $G_i$ gates and a modulus $p^{s_i}$. Require that it has no accepting witness on a negative file, and permit it to miss positives. For each attempt build its complementing subset network; multiply these scalar contractions with public inputs shared by COPY trees. The product is one precisely when every count vanishes at its chosen modulus. If every duplicate list produced a nonvanishing count for at least one attempt, this would compute distinctness. Size is $O(B+\sum_i p^{s_i}(G_i+1))$: pay shared fanout by the number of gate inputs that actually read the exterior bits, omitting unused wires in individual copies. Discard unused witness bits first; if they can change divisibility, use the correspondingly smaller threshold for $p=2$, or the constant complement one when the free bits force divisibility automatically. Thus on the full list a portfolio guaranteeing a hit everywhere requires $\sum_i p^{s_i}(G_i+1)=\Omega_p(Bw^{\sigma_p})$. On the disjoint restricted lists the same bound uses $B'(w-j)^{\sigma_p}$ and only the free exterior wires are needed. For example $o(w^\sigma/\log(w+2))$ attempts of $O(N)$ gates have simultaneously even accepting counts on at least $2^{B-o(B)}$ satisfiable files. On the whole list, guaranteeing an odd count somewhere with $O(N)$-gate attempts needs $\Omega((\log N)^\sigma)$ attempts, hence at least $\sigma\log_2\log_2 N-O(1)$ seed bits when indexing the attempts by a fixed-length binary seed. The counts on a positive may be zero if that attempt abstains. A simple promise of a short satisfying assignment does not imply isolation.

This lower bound applies to verification using a fixed clause incidence structure with public data variables subsequently pinned, including the locally readable deterministic trace encoding. It gives a range between $\Omega(t(\log t)^{2+\sigma})$ for such a parsimonious encoding on general data and $O(t(\log t)^3)$ for the construction. The hard native bit used in the signed successor has a fixed initialized data pattern, so a specialized compressor need not work on every list. Allowing variable identifiers themselves to depend on the input is another distinct format; the SAT circuit projection exploiting that freedom still applies. None of these compiler lower bounds is an elapsed-time lower bound for deciding general SAT.
