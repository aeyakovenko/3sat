# A linear-time SAT family with quadratic clipped address travel up to logarithms

There are dense proper ASCII DIMACS files of encoded length $N=\Theta(m)$, with $m=s^2$, on which a globally correct unrestricted RAM SAT program takes $O(N)$ steps. Yet every globally correct deterministic logarithmic-word RAM solver has UNSAT runs on the family with total linear address displacement $\Omega(N^2/\log^2 N)$, even after clipping all addresses to the physical input range. The same geometry therefore occurs on instances with a direct linear-time decision algorithm.

## An expander consistency check

On $U=(\mathbb Z/s\mathbb Z)^2$ take the eight Margulis–Gabber–Galil permutations
$$
(x\pm2y,y),\quad(x\pm(2y+1),y),\quad
(x,y\pm2x),\quad(x,y\pm(2x+1)).
$$
Use these as eight labeled matchings from a left copy of $U$ to a right copy and add eight labeled identity matchings. Parallel edges retain separate labels. If $G$ is the symmetric eight-neighbor adjacency matrix, the bipartite adjacency has diagonal blocks zero and off-diagonal blocks $H=8I+G$. The eight-neighbor graph has a constant spectral gap below 8. Since $H$ is positive semidefinite, the bipartite adjacency has second **largest** eigenvalue at most $16-\eta$ for an absolute $\eta>0$. Its negative eigenvalue $-16$ causes no problem for edge expansion. In particular a set of $t\le m$ of the $2m$ vertices has at least $\eta t(1-t/(2m))$ outgoing edges: apply the Laplacian bound to its indicator minus its mean. [Gabber and Galil](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0000(81)90040-4) · [Spectral bound for these eight maps](https://isa-afp.org/browser_info/current/AFP/Expander_Graphs/Expander_Graphs_MGG.html)

There are $m$ bits $X_u$, $m$ bits $Y_v$ and $16m$ bits $Z_e$. Correct tables satisfy $Z_e=X_u\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}Y_v$ for every labeled edge $e=(u,v)$. Sample $X,Y$ independently uniformly and fill in all $Z$. Fix any partition of the $18m$ bits with at least $6m$ on each side. Call a check mixed if its three bits occupy both sides. Choose the side containing the smaller number $t$ of vertex bits. For $t\ge m/8$, all graph edges crossing this vertex cut are mixed, giving $\Omega(m)$ checks. For $t<m/8$, the chosen side has at least $6m-t$ edge bits; at most $16t$ can have a vertex endpoint there. This gives at least $6m-17t=\Omega(m)$ mixed checks with both endpoints on the opposite side.

A greedy matching among the mixed checks has $\Omega(m)$ edges since all degrees are 16. In any nonempty rectangle consisting entirely of correct tables, the bit on the singleton side of each mixed triple is fixed: fixing a member of the opposite rectangle factor fixes the XOR of its two bits. This holds whichever of $X_u,Y_v,Z_e$ is alone. On a matching these singleton bits are independent unbiased functions of disjoint pairs $(X_u,Y_v)$. Each such balanced rectangle has mass at most $2^{-\Omega(m)}$ under the **same** distribution. The argument applies if each rectangle uses its own partition.

## A packed proper DIMACS projection

The formula guesses a vertex $u=(x,y)$ and a four-bit matching label $j$, calculates $v=f_j(u)$, and asserts $Z_{(u,j)}\ne X_u\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}Y_v$. Use $t=\lceil\log_2 s\rceil$ bits per coordinate and check both coordinates below $s$. The affine maps use a constant number of shifts, additions, comparisons and modular corrections on $O(t)$ bits, followed by a sixteen-way selection. Concatenating the coordinates indexes a padded $2^t$-by-$2^t$ array of size less than $4m$. Append the four label bits for the edge array. Padding entries are fixed zeros. There is exactly one selector choice for each violated labeled edge.

Use three independent lookup libraries for the three arrays. Choose a power of two $b=\Theta(\log m)$ with $b\le(\log_2 m)/16$ for large $m$. For each library decode the low $\log_2 b$ selection bits and the remaining high bits into separate one-hot flags by prefix trees. Declare a proposed output bit $a$ and a variable $S_D$ for every $b$-bit mask $D$, constrained to mean $a=D[\text{low}]$. For every low position $h$ use two clauses to impose that equivalence conditional on its flag. For each high chunk write one implication from its high flag to $S_D$, where $D$ is the actual chunk. Write its name as its public decimal bank number followed by the $b$ ASCII digits of $D$. Thus each of the $18m$ data bits occupies **one changing ASCII low bit**, and all others are fixed. Every selection has a unique completion of the lookup variables. The range checks, selectors, arithmetic and mismatch can all be encoded by gate equivalences. Use three distinct forced-false variables for padding shorter clauses, allocate nonlibrary names outside the bank range and pin the unused numerical names as in the matrix construction. This gives proper triples and every declared identifier occurs. [Bit-once decimal lookup, forcing gadgets and dense naming](compact-bit-projection-of-matrix-mismatch.md)

There are $O(m/b)$ data clauses and high decoder nodes, $O(b2^b)$ library clauses, $O(10^b)$ vacant bank identifiers and $O(\operatorname{poly}(t))$ other gates. With $O(\log m)$ encoded bits per clause, the file has $N=O(m)$ bits. The $18m$ distinct changing positions give the matching lower bound. Include one public line of $s$ spaces following the header to identify the dimension. A parser can recover $s$ with a single pass over this line. Before allocating any tables, check that $s\ge1$ and $18s^2\le N$. This guard holds throughout the family since there are $18s^2$ distinct data bits. Checked shift-and-add multiplication can reject overflow without division: reject a doubling or addition that would exceed $N$ before performing it. Use bounded ordinal counters while matching, and reject a generated size or allocation past its physical budget. On the promised files all identifiers and the array indices fit in a word.

## One globally correct solver and all solvers' travel

Regenerate and match the public template, allowing either legal digit only at each data position. Save its bit in the three arrays. Iterate through the $16m$ labeled edges, evaluate the indicated constant number of arithmetic operations per endpoint and test its XOR. The whole procedure takes $O(N)$ native steps on the promise. In particular the mask bits must be decoded even if the circuit they name would never be activated by a guessed index. Decimal names and the public gates can be generated in $O(\log m)$ steps per clause, with ordinal-to-decimal fields precomputed by prefix extension. Scan the physical file sequentially, filling the arrays by ordinal. They use at most $18m$ words, versus at least $18m$ changing ASCII **bytes** in the file. Generate the template in a predetermined order while matching, without retaining the clauses. Precomputed decimal names use $O(1)$ packed words per ordinal, with $O(m/\log m)$ ordinals for sufficiently large $m$. These bounds leave room within $2^w$ addresses at the standard width. Cap workspace allocations at the free address range on other inputs. If the template, dimension, or allocation check fails, run an ordinary complete SAT solver on the original input, which is retained unmodified. Thus this time upper bound on the family belongs to one total correct SAT solver.

For a fixed correct solver, assign memory words to two parties by a public integer address cut through the input. Each read or write to the other party's region passes the finite control and registers, costing $O_P(w)$ bits when a word has $w=\Theta(\log N)$ bits. All mutable words remain at their owner and every initial word derives solely from public bits and its own private bits. On a consistent table the machine must return UNSAT. Every transcript for that verdict gives a rectangle of consistent tables. At a balanced cut, the probability of a transcript shorter than $cm$ is exponentially small for sufficiently small $c>0$, by counting transcripts and using the rectangle bound. Thus almost all consistent tables require $\Omega(m/w)$ ownership changes at this cut. There are $\Omega(m/w)$ distinct word cuts with between $6m$ and $12m$ data bits on the left because an entire word contributes at most $w$ data bits. There are only $O(N/w)$ input word cuts altogether. The union bound gives **one actual UNSAT run** with $\Omega(m/w)$ changes at each balanced cut, for each fixed solver.

Summing over these cuts counts at most the clipped displacement between successive actual memory accesses. This gives $\Omega(m^2/w^2)$ total travel. Moreover, on an $O(N)$-step solver, $\Omega(m/w)$ hops have length $\Omega(m/w^2)$ even after clipping: allow a sufficiently small constant times $m/w^2$ travel to every step, and each remaining hop contributes at most $O(m/w)$. Arbitrary contents at far workspace addresses can transmit the necessary information in such hops at unit RAM cost. The rectangle and trace results therefore coexist with the explicit $O(N)$ solver above. ## A variant attaining packed scan time

On the infinite subsequence $s=2^r$, a different fixed serialization lets one globally correct solver decide every promised file in $O(N/w)$ executed steps, where $w=\Theta(\log N)$ is the word width. A consistent table makes every one of its $18m$ data bits sensitive, so reading just one input word per step already gives a matching worst-case lower bound. The preceding cut proof uses only the one-to-one map from data bits to physical bits. It continues to apply to the new ordering. Consequently every solver running in $O(N/w)$ on these files makes $\Omega(N/w)$ hops of clipped length $\Omega(N/w)$ on almost every consistent table. Here the constants in the hop claim can depend on the solver. Indeed, charge a sufficiently small multiple of $N/w$ per hop for all its $O(N/w)$ steps. The remaining $\Omega((N/w)^2)$ distance must come from hops of at most the physical file length in words.

**Layout.** For large $m$ choose the largest power of four $b\le(\log_2 m)/64$ and let $\ell=\sqrt b$. Thus $b=\Theta(\log m)$, $8b\le w$ at the standard width and $\ell$ divides $s$. Tile the torus in $\ell$-by-$\ell$ squares. Take the low selector bits to be the two within-tile coordinates and the high selector bits to encode the tile coordinates, putting $j$ in the high field of the $Z$ lookup. This choice just permutes the index wires. Give each of $X,Y$ one array and put the sixteen $Z$ planes in label order, then tile order. Use the three libraries and the high-flag implications as before. Each selected mask name has as suffix the $b$ ASCII bit digits of an entire tile in fixed local row-major order. The formula guesses precisely the same violated check. Its bit length is still $N=\Theta(m)$ and every data bit still occurs once as an ASCII low bit.

**Recognizing the complete syntax.** Count the $s$-space parameter line, check the preceding header against the regenerated dimensions, and check the exact end of the generated file. Counting this line even byte by byte costs $O(s)=o(m/b)$. Guard lengths and all auxiliary allocations as above and retain the packed original file for fallback. The following bounds explain how to match the public records as well as the private suffixes in $O(m/b)$ time.

* Allocate gate identifiers consecutively above the mask banks. Number the high decoder nodes one level at a time. Knowing the beginning of the current layer, the next layer and its length gives both child identifiers by fixed shifts and additions. Each edge is an equivalence of a child with the conjunction of its parent and a selected address bit (or its complement), producing a constant number of proper clauses. Enumerating a level and its gates takes constant work per child. The $O(m/b)$ high flags and data records are the only large loops. They use fixed public bank numbers, high-flag ordinals obtainable by array indexing, and a fixed choice of padding variables. All arithmetic on tile coordinates and their concatenation uses power-of-two masks and shifts.
* The small public gate circuits for coordinates, relabeling index wires and range checks take $\operatorname{poly}(\log m)$ work to enumerate. The low-selector libraries and the vacant bank names require at most $O(\operatorname{poly}(\log m)(2^b+10^b))=o(m/b)$ work, even with per-record digit inspection. The seven forcing clauses and any repeated literals can be handled using the fixed width-three rules. Enumerate the deterministic public template in the same documented order used to serialize it, with separate fixed ordinal ranges for the decoder arrays. A counting pass through the same loops, if needed for header values and range starts, fits the same bound.
* The largest identifier is $O(m/b)$. Tabulate the ordinary decimal strings of all these ordinals in $O(m/b)$ operations: seed the one-digit positive names and extend the packed text of $h$ by one ASCII digit $c$ to obtain the text of child $10h+c$. Keep lengths and a constant number of words per string, since each has $O(\log m)$ bits. Integer ordinals are written at their own addresses in the table. Each public literal, separator and clause can now be compared to the input by constant-many word extracts, shifts and masks. A monotonically advancing address and within-word offset handle unaligned fields without integer division. Data clauses require matching their entire public prefix and suffix, including the exact bank prefix and record terminator. Their $b$ varying bytes are isolated by at most two word reads at standard width. Use a table indexed by those $8b$ bits to check that every byte is exactly ASCII 0 or 1 and return the corresponding packed $b$-bit tile. Building the validity/packing table even in $O(b2^{8b})=o(m/b)$ time suffices. Save one word for each tile of each array. These arrays, decimal tables and gates occupy $O(m/b)=o(N)$ words, leaving room for the read-only input in a standard-width RAM.

**Checking the packed bits.** For a horizontal shear write $x=\ell X+u$, $y=\ell Y+v$ with $0\le u,v<\ell$. The image of a source cell under $(x,y)\mapsto(x+\sigma(2y+c),y)$, $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$, $c\in\{0,1\}$, lands in tile
$$
 (X+2\sigma Y+\lfloor(u+\sigma(2v+c))/\ell\rfloor,\ Y)
$$
modulo $s/\ell$, with within-tile horizontal coordinate $(u+\sigma(2v+c))\bmod\ell$. There are at most four possible tile offsets for a fixed shear. Interchange axes for vertical shears. For each label, offset and $b$-bit target tile value, precompute a $b$-bit word placing its relevant entries at their original source-tile positions and putting zero elsewhere. The relative offsets and local rearrangement are independent of the source tile. Enumerating local positions for all these tables takes $O(2^b\operatorname{poly}(b))=o(m/b)$ work. Coalescing offsets modulo a small torus causes no conflict because the contributing source positions are disjoint. For each source tile read the constant number of specified $Y$ tiles, look up their rearranged fragments and OR them. XOR with its $X$ tile and compare with its $Z$ tile for that label. For an identity label the $Y$ tile can be used directly. With sixteen labels all checks take $O(m/b)=O(N/w)$ operations. Direct simulation verified 48,888 tile positions for identity and the eight shears at sides 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 with every feasible tile side among 1, 2, 4 and 8.

This gives one fast path of a complete solver. Outside the precise template, or on invalid proposed dimensions, it switches to ordinary exhaustive SAT on the preserved original. The old and tiled layouts may instead be assigned separate fast paths. The scan-time statement concerns the tiled layout at the selected dimensions.
