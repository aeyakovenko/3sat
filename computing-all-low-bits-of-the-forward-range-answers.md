# Computing all the forward range sums modulo a small integer

On the original weighted transcript, a deterministic RAM can **output the low $b$ bits of every correct answer**, before using any of the proposed answers, in
$$
 O\big(nh(1+b)+u h^2\big),\qquad h=\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil .
$$
Here $n$ counts source operations, $u$ counts updates, $1\le b=O(h)$, and the array has $O(n)$ queries. There are $u=O(n/h^2)$ updates on the Weinstein–Yu schedule. Thus for any fixed $b$ all these predictions *after the entire input has been read* take $O(L)$ deterministic word steps on the actual $L=\Theta(nh)$-bit DIMACS files. Any wrong answer differing from the truth modulo $2^b$ gives a globally sound SAT acceptance. On a promise that every absolute error is strictly less than $2^b$, comparing the residues gives the exact SAT decision, even for arbitrarily many simultaneous errors of either sign. For $b$ growing to the full answer width this estimate pays an additional factor of order $h$.

## From a static count to many exact small sums

Give each original forward query $j$ a point $(a_j,b_j)$, ordered by **decreasing** logical time, and reflect the spatial coordinates as in the [sparse transposed count](offline-verification-of-weighted-range-transcripts.md). Every original insertion defines a request on this point array: a time prefix together with two upper spatial bounds. Its selected points are exactly the query IDs that receive its weight. Rather than count them separately or report all update-query pairs, add the insertion weight simultaneously to their output words, in the ring $\mathbb Z/2^b\mathbb Z$. The same construction works modulo any $m$ with $2\le m\le 2^b$: the tables for packed lanes perform addition and subtraction modulo $m$, and the scalar case needs only comparisons and conditional subtraction.

Build the heavy prefix cells and their order-preserving bitvectors as in the static count. The geometry uses all query points regardless of their annotations or the weights to be added. A canonical $x$ node keeps the points in chronological-array order. Its $y$-trie has packed next-bit routing masks at each heavy node and lists of point IDs at its small leaves. A node with fewer than $h$ points is small. Across all pairs of depths the total length of the heavy vectors is $O(nh^2)$ bits, with $O(nh)$ heavy cells. All small-child lists together have $O(nh)$ point memberships. The construction and its $O(nh)$ word bound are in the linked proof.

For each insertion decompose its $x$ range into $O(h)$ canonical nodes. Using the stored time indices, find the length of the selected time prefix inside each node. Descend one $y$ path, routing the prefix length to a child by constant-time rank on the geometry mask. Where the upper bound includes an entire zero child, all the points to be updated in that child form an initial segment of its ordered array. At a fully contained terminal the same holds. Record this as a difference update in the child: for a nonempty initial segment of length $r$, add the insertion weight at offset zero and subtract it at offset $r$ if that offset exists. Each marker changes one addressable $b$-bit lane, which costs $O(1)$ word operations. This rule includes fully contained **small** children. At the first partially covered small child on the boundary, inspect at most $h$ point IDs, test the residual $y$ comparison, and add directly to their final answer slots. There is at most one such scan per canonical $x$ node. All insertions together cost $O(uh^2)$, no matter how many total pairs are dominated.

For each $x$ node evaluate its $y$ trie bottom-up. Take the prefix sum of the difference markers in each cell to obtain its local contribution at every point. Merge completed child vectors into their parent by depositing the zero-child results in the parent's zero mask positions and the one-child results in its one mask positions. This inverse stable partition preserves the identities of points, even at equal spatial coordinates or equal intermediate residues. Add the local vector at the parent. At a small child its marker vector covers exactly its recorded IDs, and the directly charged partial updates are already in the final array. At each $x$ root, scatter its resulting residues into the final words via its explicit chronological ID list. The $x$-axis lists have $O(nh)$ total memberships. Every contributing update-point pair was assigned to precisely one complete prefix or to one partial scan, so each answer is the correct sum in the ring.

## Word cost and scope of the transposition

Store a positive constant fraction of a machine word's bits in each payload chunk. For small $b$, a chunk has $\Theta(h/b)$ lanes. Tabulate prefix sums, lane addition, and stable filtering/deposit for chunks whose total operand bits are at most $h/2$. Extracting or concatenating an unaligned chunk needs a constant number of word operations. Tables can be constructed by direct enumeration in $O(2^{h/2}\mathrm{poly}(h))=O(nh)$ time at large sizes. For a prefix-sum chunk, add the previous chunk's terminal residue to all lanes and carry its final sum to the next chunk. Depositing uses known mask populations to advance each child stream by its consumed lanes. When $b$ exceeds a small constant fraction of $h$, use ordinary scalar lanes at $O(1)$ cost each. A constant number of native words accommodates the allowed residues. Hence the heavy-cell work is $O(nh+(b/h)nh^2)$. Small cells can be processed separately in $O(nh)$ scalar operations. Empty cells and roots with no data need only their existing links. Initialize the arrays inside the same bound. Combining the terms proves the estimate.

Equivalently, the geometry-fixed program can gather a payload in the opposite direction. In characteristic two the adjoint of gathering selected bits is depositing them into the same positions, XOR is its own addition, and the adjoint of a prefix parity broadcasts into that prefix. The difference arrays and ordered merges above implement this transposition directly on an ordinary RAM. Transposing an arbitrary data-dependent integer RAM program would require a different argument.

A recognized legal original transcript admits a deterministic $O(L)$ preliminary test for every fixed $b$: compute all residues, compare with the claims, and identify every differing query. At any flagged query one scan of its earlier updates recovers the exact numerical answer and the satisfying assignment for that query. A complete solver can invoke its usual SAT procedure if all residues agree. A transcript with a single discrepancy equal to a nonzero multiple of $2^b$ passes the residue test; the independent total-sum check catches it. With unrestricted word errors and opposite signs, both tests can miss. In particular two opposite multiples of $2^b$ cancel the total and preserve all residues. The algorithm does not compute the exact integer vector at fixed precision.

## Detecting errors in arbitrary bit positions

Taking $m=3$ identifies all erroneous query IDs in linear time on the promise that each error flips a single binary digit, **at any position chosen separately per claim**. Every such difference is $\pm2^i$ and is nonzero modulo three. Arbitrarily many claims can be altered at once. A nonzero residue still certifies SAT on every legal file. Returning UNSAT solely on residue agreement requires the promise.

Two arbitrary flipped digits per claim cost $O(L\log\log n)$ by this method. If the words have $d=O(\log n)$ bits take $m=3^{1+\lceil\log_3 d\rceil}$. For $r>0$ the exponent of three in $2^r-(-1)^r$ is $1+v_3(r)$. To see this, writing $r=3^a v$ with $3\nmid v$, cubing $-1+3s$ increases the exponent of its sum with 1 by precisely one, by the factorization $z^3+1=(z+1)(z^2-z+1)$. Raising to $v$ contributes no further factor of three to the signed difference, since its quotient by $z+1$ is $\pm v$ modulo three when $z\equiv-1$. Hence for $0<r<d$, $2^r$ is neither $+1$ nor $-1$ modulo $m$. A signed sum of two distinct powers of two is nonzero, as is a single power. Conversely a modulus detecting all such one- and two-bit errors needs at least $d$ distinct nonzero classes up to sign, one for each digit. It must have $m\ge2d$. Thus the bit width $\log_2 d+O(1)$ is optimal for **one universal modulus**.

For every fixed $s\ge2$, a modulus of $O_s(\log d)$ bits suffices for all changes of at most $s$ digits per claim. The $O_s(d^s)$ nonempty signed sums of at most $s$ distinct powers of two are nonzero integers of $O(d)$ bits. Together they have $O_s(d^{s+1})$ distinct prime divisors. A prime of magnitude $d^{O_s(1)}$ outside their union exists by the elementary prime-counting bound, and exhaustive polynomial-in-$d$ search fits $O(L)$ setup for fixed $s$. Conversely all sums over at most $\lfloor s/2\rfloor$ digit positions must have different residues: canceling common positions in two such sums would give an undetected error of at most $s$ flips. Thus this precision scale is also necessary for one-modulus comparisons at every fixed $s\ge2$. These are detection and localization results; they need no advance specification of the damaged positions.

## One deterministic solver for the whole sequence of precision tests

The quantifier over moduli can be put inside a single globally correct SAT program. On the recognized files, first apply the total-sum check. At successive stages $k=2,4,8,\ldots$, check every forward answer modulo $\Lambda_k=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,k)$ using one packed call. Return SAT at the first mismatch. Once $\Lambda_k$ exceeds a public upper bound for every absolute error, residue agreement proves UNSAT. Use ordinary SAT search on inputs outside the validated template. All original words and their claimed values can be recovered in the initial $O(L)$ scan.

For a positive file put $g=\gcd_j |A_j-F_j|$ and $d(g)=\min\{m\ge2:m\nmid g\}$. The program need not know $g$. Some residue fails as soon as a tested $k$ reaches $d(g)$. At full precision the same asymptotic upper bound follows from a standard time divide-and-conquer with a two-dimensional weighted sweep at each level. The packed clock is $O(L\min\{d(g),\log n\})$ on positive files and $O(L\log n)$ on every recognized transcript. To check the bit costs directly, $\Lambda_{2m}$ divides $\binom{2m}{m}\Lambda_m$: every extra prime power lies in $(m,2m]$, and contributes a carry at its own scale to the binomial valuation. Hence $\log_2\Lambda_k\le2k$ on these stages. In the other direction every $\binom{k-1}{i}$ divides $\Lambda_k$, since each prime power contributes at most one to the binomial valuation. Thus $\Lambda_k\ge2^{k-1}/k$. A stage $k=O(\log n)$ exceeds the $O(n^2)$ bound on absolute errors. Computing the moduli up to this stage costs only polynomial in $\log n$, and the sum of the stage precisions is $O(k)$.

In particular, on **any sequence of positive files in this family** on which this one solver's ratio of time to $L$ tends to infinity, every fixed positive integer eventually divides every annotation error, and the errors eventually sum to zero. The latter follows from the initial total-sum check. These are necessary conditions on proposed hard witnesses for this program. The exact finite obstruction for these two tests is simple. To evade through stage $k$ with a nonempty error vector, at least two entries must be wrong, and the largest absolute error must be at least $\Lambda_k$. Two opposite errors $+\Lambda_k,-\Lambda_k$ attain both minima. They can evade also the global weight gcd, value bounds, and the test for equal operands. For $n\ge\Lambda_k$ sufficiently large, use the first four scheduled updates $(1,1;1),(2,2;\Lambda_k),(3,1;1),(4,4;\Lambda_k)$, where the value after the semicolon is the weight. Immediately after the fourth update query $(2,3)$ and $(3,2)$. Their true answers are $\Lambda_k+1,\Lambda_k+2$. Supply $2\Lambda_k+1,2$ and leave every other annotation truthful. The query points are incomparable, each selected set has weight gcd one, and both claims lie between zero and the total weight inserted so far. All other queries can avoid these two coordinate pairs. Increasing $k$ scales these witnesses by the lcm of the new moduli. It preserves failure of these particular tests; other SAT algorithms can recognize or compute the two errors directly.
