# The cost of giving rejection invariants direct access to the input

Consider one fixed bounded-computation source, a RAM that reads one independent bit from each of its $m$ input words and accepts precisely when their parity is even. Take a clock $H=\Theta(m)$ sufficient for the scan. Write $F_x$ for its bounded-acceptance 3-CNF with all the switch pins set to zero in the [signed-chart construction](signed-affine-charts-on-rejecting-runs.md). Exactly the odd $x$ give negative instances. A chart has $s$ bits and largest positive native gap $g$. Its cost below is $1+s+g$. Put $L=\lceil\log_2(m+2)\rceil$. A tilde suppresses fixed powers of $L$.

* For every sound deterministic UNSAT signaler and every fixed meaning of chart descriptions independent of $x$, most odd $x$ simultaneously require $(s+1)g=\Omega(m)$ for **every** valid chart. The cut sets can be arbitrary sets of complete RAM states. This gives a worst-case cost $\Omega(\sqrt m)$ without a reference algorithm or an elapsed-time bound.
* Allow the common signed wordwise-affine row circuit to query $F_x$ directly. On the same family every signaler requires worst-case cost $\widetilde\Omega(m^{1/3})$. There is one correct SAT solver with cost $\widetilde O(\sqrt m)$ for the first model and $\widetilde O(m^{1/3})$ for the second, on every odd $x$ at the matched lengths $m=8^u$. Both rates concern charts of its actual rejecting computation. In particular the two worst-case exponents differ even with the source, solver and formulas held fixed.

The formula length is $N=\widetilde\Theta(m)$. The new comparison specializes known parity proof-complexity bounds to whole-machine inductive invariants and matches their costs in one native run. Priority for that comparison is unverified. The [two-message parity verification bound](parity-chain-verdict-checks.md) and the [challenge-count refinement](alternating-parity-certificates.md) already treat the underlying local protocols.

## One chart on the source cube

Use fixed-width clause records. The only variable bits of the bounded RAM reduction are signs pinning the initialization records for the source words. In each word only its low bit varies. The pin/switch guards split each clause into a fixed number of clauses and preserve a designated occurrence of each such sign, possibly complemented by a fixed convention. The disjunction chain and its negative pins are independent of $x$. A native input load intersects only a bounded number of clause records, even if unaligned. Hence a fixed constant $B\ge1$ bounds the number of source coordinates needed per native step, including every immutable load that step permits. From $m,i$ one can compute the designated sign position for $x_i$ in $L^{O(1)}$ time. A requested formula word is also computable from its $O(1)$ coordinates in that time. [Packed layout and initialization](packed-band-transfer.md)

The schedule and cut sets of a fixed chart are independent of the charge vector in the first model. Times and lengths may depend on $m$. At time zero use the genuine initial configuration, including length-dependent initial registers. The last cut requires a signaling controller. A halted controller can be padded by self-loops. Passing all checks then certifies a real signal by the stated time. Charts ending at the actual first signal are among these passing charts. Ill-formed schedules can be discarded. Immediate native signals would be independent of the variable input at time zero and cannot occur on this family for a sound signaler.

Fix an invalid implication across some gap for $x$. There is a concrete complete state in the entrance set whose run reaches a concrete state outside the exit set. Fix the source bits read by this segment. For every charge vector agreeing there the entire segment follows the same instructions and reaches the same state. Both endpoint membership facts persist. Consequently the failed chart has a certificate of failure fixing at most $Bg$ **bits** of $x$. Full memories used to define these certificates can be arbitrarily large. This argument is an enumeration over states, with no efficiency requirement on finding a witness. Lean verifies the replay argument and the resulting failure cylinder for arbitrary state spaces and adaptive read trees. [Formal proof](PinnedChartCylinders.lean)

For this chart forbid every such cylinder with one clause. The resulting CNF has width at most $K=Bg$. Its solutions are exactly the inputs where the chart passes all implications. Induction forces the genuine signal there, so every solution has odd parity and flipping any coordinate destroys satisfaction. Paturi, Pudlák and Zane prove that a width-$K$ CNF on $m$ variables has at most $2^{m-m/K}$ isolated solutions. Thus the conditional probability that a uniformly chosen odd $x$ admits this particular chart is at most $2^{1-m/(Bg)}$. [PPZ, Lemma 2 and its isolated-solution corollary](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/PaturiPudlakZane_1999_cjtcs.pdf)

For an integer $j\ge1$, except on a fraction at most $2^{-j}$ of the odd inputs, **every valid chart on the same input** satisfies

$$
 \frac{m}{Bg} < s+j+1+\log_2((s+1)(s+2)).
$$

Indeed at length $s$ there are at most $2^s$ strings, each with its own fixed $g$. The conditional measure of violations at that length is at most $2^{-j}/((s+1)(s+2))$. Summing telescopes. For fixed $j$ this gives the first product bound simultaneously at all lengths and gaps. In particular it applies to signed affine charts of any polynomial-time cubic SAT algorithm, for which the separate reference-based chart theorem only gives $N^{1/3-o(1)}$ at that clock. This estimate on a prescribed source also applies to arbitrarily slow signalers.

The same counting argument allows some semantic dependence on the input. Suppose a failing concrete gap can be chosen so that, after fixing its two concrete states, membership of the first and nonmembership of the second persist on cylinders fixing at most $q_+,q_-$ additional source bits respectively. Then replace $Bg$ everywhere by $Bg+q_++q_-$, using uniform upper bounds for that chart. A single row evaluator reading one input word need not imply either bound for membership of an entire cut set.

## Rows with direct input gates

Give the [signed chart](signed-affine-charts-on-rejecting-runs.md) additional ROM gates for packed words of the immutable formula. An address is computed by earlier gates. Charge the whole Boolean encoding with its wires, the list of all declared label widths and truth bits, and at least one unit per ROM gate. Gate inputs and outputs have the native address and word width, or a fixed total convention on out-of-range addresses. The same acyclic circuit is reused for each catalog number. Combining circuits may also read the formula in this model. The schedule remains input-independent.

Suppose every odd $x$ admitted a valid chart of cost at most $S$, where $1\le S\le m$. Write the acceptance condition as an existential choice of chart, a universal challenge, and an existential reply. A challenge gives a gap, a full local **transcript** of the at most $S$ instructions with only the touched work words, its endpoint controllers, and the proposed truth bits for every endpoint catalog. It supplies one label per claimed false catalog. Check the CPU path, source reads, controller conditions and reduction of the claimed truth bits to acceptance at entry and failure at exit. A malformed or nondefeating challenge passes automatically. For a well-formed defeating challenge the reply is evidence that no full entrance memory can realize it.

For each claimed true catalog impose every indexed affine equation. For each claimed false catalog impose the complement of its named equation. Input-dependent equations are generated from the **actual immutable input**, even for the outgoing row. Substitute the transcript's final data on written words and pin the initial bits of touched words. A constant failure becomes $0=1$ at a fixed address. Different work addresses are independent. At word width $w=O(L)$, an inconsistent system has a contradictory XOR of at most $w+1$ selected equations at one address. The augmented vectors lie in dimension $w+1$. Consistency is equivalent to the absence of $(0,1)$ from their span. The reply names these equations. Only the named rows must be evaluated. In particular the many complementary equations supplied through the false-catalog labels are simply available for selection. Charging all their source probes on every reply would overestimate the verifier.

The three messages have $O((S+1)L^{O(1)})$ bits in total. For fixed messages the predicate needs $O((S+1)L^{O(1)})$ source-bit queries. It checks the native block, evaluates two combining circuits, and evaluates at most $w+1$ row circuits, each using at most $S$ immutable loads. Preliminary syntax checks depend only on the messages and public dimensions. Checking the displayed schedule for all gaps can use unbounded computation on the fixed chart because it needs no charges. Universal challenges include every gap and bounded transcript. These bounds charge native-width registers, labels, catalog numbers, transcript positions and cut indices. Signal-time integers appearing in a chart have at most its description or declared output width in bits. If $S>m$ the desired lower bound is immediate.

For each message triple, expand the adaptive input queries into a DNF of accepting paths. Merge its top OR into the innermost existential. The full predicate is a depth-four unbounded-fan-in AND/OR circuit of size at most $2^{O((S+1)L^{O(1)})}$ computing odd parity. The depth-four parity lower bound gives $S=\widetilde\Omega(m^{1/3})$. More precisely, build the circuit for the capped verifier whether or not it covers all odd inputs. It never accepts an even input. Its correlation with parity is exactly its coverage fraction conditional on odd parity. For an absolute fixed exponent $A$ from the compilation, Håstad bounds this fraction by $2^{-\Omega(m/((S+1)L^A)^3)}$ when $m$ is large. In particular, reducing the cube-root cutoff by a sufficiently large power of $L$ gives coverage $o(1)$. Intersecting its complement with the earlier tail event gives odd inputs witnessing both lower bounds simultaneously. No nondeterministic UNSAT reference is used. [Håstad, Theorem 3.1](https://www.csc.kth.se/~johanh/paritycorr.pdf)

## Matching rejecting computations

Here is one fixed deterministic SAT solver on all inputs. At the selected lengths $m=8^u$ use the regular pinned layout above, padding its fixed record ranges to make its length strictly increasing and arithmetically recognizable along these lengths. For example choose a power-of-two index width ceiling and pad the number of clause slots to a power of two with a fixed number of slots per width-dependent template row. One can also enumerate the $O(\log N)$ candidate source lengths and compare their computed lengths. Successive powers of eight and monotone template capacities make those lengths distinct. For a matching length, read the designated sign occurrences as a proposed $x$. Check every word of the input against the template generated from these signs. This includes all switch pins, all unused bits and all repeated sign occurrences. If any test fails or the length does not match, use exhaustive SAT on the given formula. On a matching template scan the $m$ signs in order and signal UNSAT precisely on odd parity. This is sound on arbitrary explicit inputs and terminates with the correct verdict.

Implement template checks and parity steps in fixed-time slots of length $L^{O(1)}$ native instructions, padding after the bounded arithmetic and clearing the slot scratch at the end. Use a fixed startup of $L^{O(1)}$ work for the width-dependent templates. On the promised inputs every template test succeeds. At slot boundaries the only changing public controller data are the scan index and phase, together with one parity bit during the parity scan. Work memory at these cuts is zero except for fixed template data of $L^{O(1)}$ bits. The other registers are reset to a known convention. Fixed slot lengths can be implemented by padding each bounded loop to a known bound and using balanced branches. These choices permit a circuit of $L^{O(1)}$ size to compute a cut time, all the fixed controller fields and any bit of the fixed memory from the cut index. The final verdict instruction follows the last parity slot. The true initial state is a separate first cut.

For charts independent of $x$, partition the slots of each scan into groups of at most $a$ slots, $1\le a\le m$. The entire template scan has $\widetilde O(m)$ slots and requires no advice. For the parity scan store the genuine prefix bit at each group boundary, using $O(1+m/a)$ bits. A binary selection circuit with a shared index uses $O((1+m/a)L^{O(1)})$ encoded bits. The chart specifies the exact singleton state at every internal cut. One catalog of universally indexed single-bit equations pins every memory word to its fixed value. The combining circuit pins the controller. The final cut merely requires a signal. The chart length is $\widetilde O(1+m/a)$, and its maximum gap is $\widetilde O(a)$. No directory for all template-scan cuts is needed because their common schedule is given by arithmetic. This matches the product frontier up to logarithms, including its square-root minimum.

For input-reading rows put $t=m^{1/3}$. Use cuts every $t$ slots in each scan. In the parity scan put its $t^2$-bit macroblocks in order and store only the genuine $t+1$ macro-boundary prefix bits. At a cut let $h$ be the macroblock and $k$ the number of its completed $t$-bit packets. For catalog $0\le j<t$, compute the parity of packet $j$ of that macroblock when $j<k$, returning zero otherwise. This uses a single circuit shared over $j$. Unroll $t$ accesses to the designated source signs of the selected packet, including the cost of computing their addresses and XOR. For a zero-length label and zero mask, set the right-hand side to the **complement** of the desired truth bit, so the atom $0=e$ has that truth value. These $t$ catalogs cost $t$ truth bits and zero label bits. Only the distinct catalog evaluations, not the shared circuit description, are repeated. A separate universal catalog pins the common fixed work memory.

The combiner XORs the $t$ supplied truths and the indexed stored macro prefix, and requires equality with the native parity register. At the end of the scan use the last stored prefix and zero completed packets. On template-scan cuts it only needs the known phase state. Fix the remaining controller bits as above. The encoded circuit and all its wires have size $tL^{O(1)}$, including the binary selection of the stored prefix and the addresses computed for every ROM gate. The maximum gap is $tL^{O(1)}$. Every internal set on the real input is precisely the true singleton, so all gap implications hold also as statements about every state they admit.

## What happens to the reference exponent

Input queries during a consistency test introduce a genuine public-data term. Even on two known no-op configurations of a signaler on a fixed negative instance, one true catalog can generate $0=E(y)$ from an arbitrary explicit Boolean circuit $E$. Simultaneous solvability is precisely UNSAT of $E$. Thus a uniform faster nondeterministic test for the full inner consistency predicate would also provide the corresponding faster UNSAT reference. This observation alone says nothing about the easiest valid chart. The separation above compares the **minimum** instead, on explicit negative reductions of a fixed source. Its sharpness construction runs in near-linear time on the family, so neither the product bound nor the stronger-input model's cube-root bound implies a superlinear time lower bound for SAT.
