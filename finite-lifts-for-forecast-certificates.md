# A finite algebraic lift for certificates at infinitely many forecast widths

Fix the actual two-bank recurrence, its negative seeds and a prefix with canonical widths $W=w_0 2^h$ for a fixed odd $w_0$. Equivalently, take the odd part of the integer spelling the header and adjust the starting value of $h$. At all sufficiently large $h$ the observation time is $H=D2^{5h}+s$ with fixed $D>0$ and signed $s$, and $0\le H<2^{6h}$. A width-$w$ partial certificate consists of two explicit layered linear representations over $\mathbb F_2$ that mark true zeros and ones in the stream. At each of $h$ layers the representation reads one bit from each of the six consecutive $h$-bit blocks of a timestamp. It can leave arbitrarily many ticks unmarked. The local checker requires that the pins follow from the preceding pins and the known negative initialization, and that the observation is pinned zero. [Certificate definition and soundness proof](periodic-linear-prediction-on-dyadic-forecast-widths.md#verifying-a-partial-description-of-the-actual-stream)

**Finite criterion.** For each fixed $w$ one can compute exactly which padding lengths admit such a certificate. The set of lengths above the finite startup cutoff is ultimately periodic, even if **every** layer matrix and the two boundary vectors may be chosen afresh at every length. It is infinite exactly when there is a finite prefix of matrix layers $p$, a nonempty block $v$ of layers, a suffix $s'$ and fixed boundary vectors for which $pv^j s'$ is valid for all $j\ge0$. For a proposed triple this last assertion has a finite linear-algebra test. The argument concerns the binary padding length $h$. Repeating $v$ takes the physical width from $W$ to $2^{|v|}W$.

On the common program prefix for a target SAT exponent $k$, a passing triple of any fixed width would exclude $O(N^k)$ deterministic unrestricted-workspace RAM time for ordinary SAT. Indeed it gives valid certificates on an infinite progression of lengths, each of description size $O_w(h)$ and checking time $\operatorname{poly}_w(h)$. Under the hypothesized clock, the guest's directly checked branch would find a certificate and latch one before the very observation that it certifies zero. This is a sufficient test. We have no passing triple for any of those three SAT prefixes. Taking a cycle for a different program, including a calibration, fails this implication. ## Making the local tests independent of the number of columns

Write $B=2^h$ and $t=\sum_{j=0}^5t_j B^j$ with $0\le t_j<B$. The longer lag is $AB+c$, every other lag is constant, and the horizon is $DB^5+s$. All needed shifts, equalities and comparisons thus use offsets $\Delta=\sum_{j=0}^5 c_jB^j$ with a fixed tuple of signed integers $c_j$. A transducer from $t$ to $u=t-\Delta$ guesses the five signed carries $b_1,\ldots,b_5$; set $b_0=b_6=0$. In row $j$ the required relation is
$$
              t_j-c_j+b_j=u_j+B b_{j+1}.
$$
Initialize the six column carries to $b_j-c_j$. At each column add the corresponding input digit, subtract the output digit, check evenness, and halve. After the last column require the resulting carries to be $b_{j+1}$. All guesses and intermediate carries have magnitude bounded using only the fixed $c_j$: in ordinary row order each seam is $\lfloor(t_j-c_j+b_j)/B\rfloor$, with $B\ge2$, and within a row the update halves the previous carry up to a unit digit adjustment. Each in-range integer result gives a unique successful path, and no path succeeds on overflow or underflow. The same construction can check $0\le H-t<B^6$ by swapping signs in the digit equation. The horizon is in range after a computable fixed cutoff. Equality to a fixed-offset timestamp also has a unique successful path. These transducers have constant dimension for the chosen physical program and odd part, independently of $h$. On negative predecessor times the two seed times get pin one and **every** other negative time gets pin zero.

In dimensions at most $U=C(w+1)^3$, the local checker requires a fixed finite list of scalar series to vanish on every timestamp word. There is one test for overlapping pins, one for a missing endpoint pin, and separate tests for each incompatible product of pin and bank possibilities. Apply the interval mask in each test. A shift of a pin reads the current layer's matrix at the digit symbol produced by the transducer; direct sums and tensor products give the other operations. The coefficient matrices of each test now depend only on the current choice of layer matrices and the currently read six-bit symbol. Its initial and terminal vectors depend on the respective certificate boundary vectors and the fixed arithmetic. The tests are kept separate so failures cannot cancel. The constant $C$ can be very large and depends on the actual taps and the header's odd part. This width analysis is for explicit registers of fixed width and six timestamp blocks. ## Exhausting all layer choices at fixed width

Pad smaller matrices to dimension $w$. A layer letter $\lambda$ lists one $w\times w$ binary matrix for each of the 64 digit symbols in each pin representation. The alphabet is finite. Enumerate the finitely many initial and terminal vectors. For each failure test keep the row space $R$ spanned by all histories of timestamp symbols seen so far. If its current transition matrix is $M(\lambda,a)$, the update on a layer letter is
$$
                  R\longmapsto \sum_{a=0}^{63} R M(\lambda,a),
$$
where the sum denotes the linear span. The initial space is generated by the initial row. The final test requires every vector of $R$ to annihilate the terminal column. The update is exact for any layer sequence. In particular, the certificate designer chooses one matrix table **before** ranging over all timestamp words; the matrices cannot vary separately for each tested timestamp.

Store the tuple of spaces in canonical row-echelon form. A subspace of $\mathbb F_2^U$ has a padded basis of at most $U^2$ bits. Including the fixed number of tests, the endpoint choices and a counter saturated at the fixed startup cutoff, the resulting graph has at most $S=2^{C'(w+1)^6}$ states for an effective header-dependent $C'$. Edges correspond to possible next layer letters. Accepting walks of length $h$ are exactly valid certificates. Forgetting the letter names gives a unary finite automaton. Reachable subsets of graph vertices eventually repeat, proving effective ultimate periodicity. To decide infinitude it suffices to find a reachable positive-length cycle from which an accepting vertex is reachable. In particular, **one** accepting walk of length $h\ge S$ contains a pumpable cycle. This cutoff bounds the longest acyclic accepting walk; it is not a claim that the full set of length residues repeats within $S$ steps. The matrices on the prefix, cycle and suffix of a witness walk give the claimed $p,v,s'$. [Unary finite automata and arithmetic progressions](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1291)

## Checking a proposed lift without building the exhaustion graph

For each failure test, start with its reachable space $R_p$ after the proposed prefix. Let $T_v$ be propagation through the block $v$, summing over all timestamp symbols at each layer as above. It preserves sums of spaces. Compute
$$
 Z_0=R_p,\qquad Z_{i+1}=Z_i+T_v(Z_i).
$$
After at most $U$ strict dimension increases this stabilizes. The limit is the span of the reachable rows after $pv^j$ over **all** $j\ge0$. Propagate this single space through the suffix and test its terminal column. Do this for every failure test, and ensure the shortest candidate length is at or beyond the fixed startup cutoff, absorbing repetitions into the prefix if necessary. Acceptance proves validity after every number of repetitions. Cost is polynomial in the dimensions and in the explicit number of supplied layers. For example the ordinary row-elimination bound is $O((|p|+(U+1)|v|+|s'|)U^3)$ per test after constructing the layer matrices. A fixed proposed lift with a failing repetition already has a failing repetition at some $0\le j\le U$. To obtain a concrete counterexample, check those finitely many repetitions and fix timestamp bits from greatest to least significance while retaining at least one nonzero terminal output. This recovers a failing width, timestamp and local test. The inference allows different true registers at different widths. It propagates the possible local counterexamples. ## Calibration with both successful and unsuccessful lifts

Take $L=\{1,B+1\}$ and $R=\{2\}$, $B=2^h$, with ones at times $-2,-1$. For $h\ge2$ the genuine nonnegative ones are $[0,B-2]$ and the even timestamps in $[B,2B-1]$. Through $B-2$ the ordinary left and right taps both read one. At $B-1$ the long tap first reaches the negative seed and cancels the other left input. Thereafter the odd subsequence stays zero, since its right tap is two ticks back. At each even tick $B,\ldots,2B-2$ the ordinary left tap is zero and the long tap is one. At $2B$ the long tap reaches the zero at $B-1$, killing the even subsequence too. The stream is representable by fixed column matrices.

Compare the genuine formula to two proposed formulas that toggle its bit at $3t=B-1$ and $3t=B+1$, respectively. They pass precisely at odd and even $h$, respectively, since any first changed bit violates the recurrence and the relevant divisibility by three alternates. A two-block, four-symbol implementation finds the joint exact-length row-space cycle beginning at $h=5$ with period two. The independent span-closure test checks a one-layer increment and both parities of a two-layer increment. Direct simulation independently checked 65,520 defect bits at $2\le h\le7$.
