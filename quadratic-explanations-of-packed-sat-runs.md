# Quadratic tests on an actual run of a SAT solver

Use the packed family of length $L=\Theta(D)$ whose verdict is the generalized inner product $F(x)=\sum_{i=1}^D a_i b_i z_i$ of independent binary triples. Every input bit of its ordinary 3-CNF is affine in the $3D$ source bits.

On every input of this family, every correct deterministic RAM solver needs at least $\frac12\log_2(4/3)D$ binary quadratic tests to restrict its computation to a common instruction sequence with quadratic register and memory contents. The restriction may use tests selected separately for each input, with no charge for describing them. For any one adaptive strategy for selecting the tests, a uniform input needs at least $\log_2(4/3)D-s$ answers except on a set of probability at most $2^{-s}$. A model allowing unlimited writable memory and nonlinear word instructions still pays these charges if it is to explain the resulting values by quadratics. The matching order is attained by a solver running in $\Theta(L/\log L)$ word time with input-independent memory addresses throughout the promised family. ## The exact agreement exponent with additional cubic terms

Let $\chi(P)=(-1)^{P(x)}$ for binary functions $P$. If $Q$ is any binary quadratic and each $P_j$ is the product of three affine forms on the full input space, then
$$
 \max_{Q,P_1,\ldots,P_k}\left|\mathbb E\chi\left(F+Q+\sum_{j=1}^k P_j\right)\right|
           =(3/4)^{(D-k)_+}.                                            \tag{1}
$$
There is no requirement to take one affine form from each source block.

For $k=0$ use the stabilizer fidelity of $D$ independent CCZ states. The normalized vector with amplitudes $2^{-3D/2}\chi(F)$ is exactly their tensor product. The normalized vector with amplitudes $2^{-3D/2}\chi(Q)$ is a stabilizer state, obtained by applying single-qubit signs and controlled signs to the uniform state. Their inner product is the signed correlation. Bravyi, Browne, Calpin, Campbell, Gosset and Howard give the single-state fidelity $9/16$ in Eq. (33), show in Corollary 3 that every three-qubit pure state is stabilizer-aligned, and prove multiplicativity in Theorem 5. Fidelity is the square of the absolute inner product maximized over stabilizer states. Taking square roots gives the upper bound $(3/4)^D$. The zero quadratic meets it. [Primary paper, Eq. (33), Theorem 5 and Corollary 3](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10091572/1/q-2019-09-02-181.pdf)

For $p(y)=y_1y_2y_3$ one has
$$
  \chi(p(y))=\tfrac16\sum_{u\in\mathbb F_2^3} \chi(p(y+u)+p(y)).
$$
Indeed $\sum_u\chi(p(y+u))=6$ for every $y$, and each difference is quadratic. Substituting the three affine forms for $y$ preserves this identity even if they are dependent. The absolute coefficient sum is $4/3$ per cubic. This is the bit-function form of the one-CCZ decomposition in the same paper's Eq. (108). Expand all $k$ terms, apply the $k=0$ result and cap the correlation at one. For equality remove $\min(k,D)$ of the original disjoint triples, padding with zero products if necessary. This proves (1). Priority for this formulation as an exact biased approximation tradeoff is unverified.

## How many quadratic tests a single input requires

Let $S$ be the common solution set of $b$ quadratic test answers and suppose $x\in S$. After the tests, suppose $F$ on $S$ is a quadratic plus at most $k$ products of three affine forms. All tests and residual polynomials may be chosen for this particular $x$.

The indicator of $S$ is a product of $b$ factors of the form $(1\pm\chi(Q_i))/2$. Its expansion into quadratic phases has sum of absolute coefficients at most one. Since the residual is correct on $S$, equation (1) bounds the density of $S$ by $(3/4)^{(D-k)_+}$. In the opposite direction, the indicator is also a nonzero binary polynomial of degree at most $2b$ after multilinear reduction. A nonzero Boolean polynomial of degree $d$ has density at least $2^{-d}$. To see this, choose an occurring monomial of maximum degree. After any assignment outside that monomial its top coefficient stays one, hence the resulting polynomial has at least one nonzero evaluation. This proves, on **each** such certificate,
$$
                       2b\ge (D-k)_+\log_2(4/3).                         \tag{2}
$$
For a fixed adaptive strategy truncated at depth $b$, successful exact terminal fibers up to that depth number at most $2^b$ (including early stops, by prefix-freeness). Their union has probability at most $2^b(3/4)^{(D-k)_+}$. This probability statement assumes that the residual truly equals the verdict on each counted fiber, as it does for a correct simulation. A predictor making errors has the corresponding ordinary sign-correlation bound $2^b(3/4)^{(D-k)_+}$ when it always stops by $b$ and uses the specified residual at each leaf. Results elsewhere in the journal address adaptive quadratic tests of field multiplication and flat-by-flat affine replay. [Field multiplication test trees](trace-product-quadratic-correlation.md) · [Affine replay on the RAM](affine-dimension-along-sat-runs.md)

To apply (2), fix an actual execution. Along it, restrict source inputs by sequential quadratic tests, requiring that every remaining input follow the same instruction path and that each bit of each register and of every fixed-address work cell after the instruction admit a quadratic representative in the original source bits. The chosen address can vary across these inputs if the resulting fixed-address contents are still representable. On termination the returned bit has a quadratic representative; a fixed yes/no terminal is a constant special case. All quadratic extensions chosen only on earlier surviving inputs can be taken as full-space polynomials, since tests use them only on that earlier fiber. The final nonempty surviving set has exactly the form in (2), with $k=0$. One can alternatively permit a terminal computation using $k$ cubic terms as in (1).

Such a restriction is available for every native instruction. For a fixed finite-arity log-word ISA one can reveal the bits of each required source register, effective address and loaded word as necessary. Each revealed bit has a quadratic representative when it is queried. An address computed internally from registers that are fixed by the tests is already fixed; a subsequent read at that address supplies quadratic bits from the incoming state. The number of tested bits is $O(w)$ for a width-$w$ elementary step with constantly many word accesses. Fixing these quantities also resolves conditional control and indirect stores. A step with quadratic successor contents and a fixed next instruction costs no test. The theorem charges information discarded at a step even when the restrictions are selected with full knowledge of the actual input. It does not require the machine to implement this explanatory procedure.

## A lower bound on every run of a matrix-trace evaluator

The same certificate argument applies to the original matrix lead. Let $M=\operatorname{tr}(ABZ)$ on three independent uniform binary $d$-square matrices. On one leaf allow $k$ arbitrary products of three affine forms in *all* the input bits. If all $b$ tests are quadratic and the exact residual on their nonempty fiber is quadratic plus a sum of these products, then
$$
  2b\ \ge\ \tfrac18\max\!\left\{0,\,
           \frac{(d-(6k)^{4/9})_+^2}{12}-\frac{11}{3}\right\}.          \tag{3}
$$
If the tests themselves can each be any quadratic plus a sum drawn from the same $k$ products, (3) holds with $3b$ on the left. The list can vary arbitrarily between leaves. Along a particular execution a new product can be introduced halfway through the computation: include it in that execution's final list. Thus for every run of any exact matrix-trace algorithm, including the one furnished by the near-linear SAT hypothesis, a near-quadratic number of free cubic features across the run still requires $\Omega(d^2)$ test bits to keep the symbolic state in this class.

For the proof expand all test indicators in phases. In each term the resulting comparison with $M$ has exponent $M+Q+\sum_j c_jP_j$, using a subset of the same features. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz once per input block. Taking a finite difference in each of $A,B,Z$ kills $Q$ and all monomials missing a block. The third difference of one mixed product is the sum over the six permutations assigning its three linear factors to the three blocks, evaluated on the three differences. Every summand is a pure binary trilinear tensor. Consequently the difference from $M$ is $M-U$ for a binary tensor $U$ of ordinary rank at most $6k$. The eighth power of the absolute correlation is at most its bias, bounded by the analytic-rank estimate in the journal using the imported $9/4$ matrix exponent. The expansion coefficients have absolute sum one, so this also bounds the eighth power of the leaf density. A nonempty leaf has density at least $2^{-2b}$ for quadratic tests, or $2^{-3b}$ for tests using cubic features. This proves both versions of (3). If the features each use one affine form per designated block, the factor six improves to one. [Matrix rank, compression and analytic-rank constants](average-case-rank-of-binary-matrix-products.md) · [The SAT-derived evaluator](verifying-matrix-products-from-sat.md)

For a fixed adaptive strategy one can even allow the quadratic parts of its tests and terminal computation to be arbitrary sums of functions on pairs of input blocks. The box calculation still removes these terms. Each successful leaf then has density at most two to the power of the negative right side of (3), and at depth at most $b$ there are at most $2^b$ such leaves. The per-input density lower bound no longer applies because these tests can have unrestricted Boolean degree. These are work bounds on a specified form of symbolic simulation; $O(\log d)$ test bits can be supplied by fixing one word operand.

## A globally correct scanner using public addresses

The packed rows use $r=2^{3h}$ and $D=rh$. A solver can check the entire template sequentially and fall back to an arbitrary complete SAT procedure if validation fails. Check every fixed sign and identifier, the terminal pins, all dummy-padded pin patterns, and equality of the repeated index fields in each query gate. Before accepting the special layout, check that all table pins have their prescribed values. Build a public parity table on $h$-bit integers in $O(2^h)$ operations by the recurrence $\pi[j]=\pi[j\mathbin{\gg}1]\oplus(j\bmod 2)$. For each prescribed, publicly indexed binary triple $(a,b,z)$ in the table layout use $\pi[a\mathbin{\&} b\mathbin{\&} z]$ to validate its pin. At this stage all indexing operands derive from loop counters. On the query rows, isolate the three $h$-bit fields and update one accumulator by
$$
                   C\gets C\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}(a_i\mathbin{\&}b_i\mathbin{\&}z_i).
$$
Take the parity of the accumulator once at the end by $O(\log(h+1))$ public shifts and XORs, and return that bit. Including validation this takes $O(r)$ word steps, and all input and work-memory addresses on this family depend only on the layout and its size. The known row-edit sensitivity gives the matching lower bound $\Omega(r)$ on every promised input. Consequently a proof which charges the formation of a three-block-dependent address cannot obtain even one such access from this example.

For an explicit upper bound on the quadratic-test cost of this scanner, fix all $D$ bits of the first source block by linear tests at the outset. Every accumulated product bit thereafter is quadratic in the other two blocks. Validation comparisons on the promised template can use XOR of repeated fields or comparison with fixed constants, with the template-mismatch bits identically zero. Parse using fixed masks and shifts. For a RAM doing data-dependent branches only at template failure or final return, the queries cost at most $D+1$ including the final result bit if needed. For a nonuniform explanation selected for a particular input, its $D$ first-block bits may even be fixed in pairs by a single quadratic test: the product of the two literals asserting their actual values must be one. This costs at most $\lceil D/2\rceil+1$ tests in that convention. These bounds show the order of (2) for a true SAT algorithm and locate a gap in its constant. Obtaining work growing faster than $L$ for general SAT still requires an argument about accumulated operations beyond these $O(D)$ bits of restrictions.
