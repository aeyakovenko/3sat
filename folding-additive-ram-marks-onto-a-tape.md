# Folding additive RAM addresses onto a fixed-dimensional tape

Consider two distinct mutable registers and the instructions $L:x\gets M[y]$, $W:M[y]\gets c$, and one of $y\gets y+x,\ y\gets y-x,\ y\gets x-y$. Here $c$ is a fixed program integer. Arbitrary conditional branches may select and repeat these sites in any order. There is no running-time or workspace promise. This model cannot decide ordinary ASCII DIMACS 3-SAT in $O(N^{1+\epsilon})$ time for some fixed $\epsilon>0$. The word-width convention and original DIMACS syntax are those of the sparse-name theorem. The declared but unused variable bound in this proof may have $O(\log^2 N)$ decimal digits. [Word widths and sparse names](sparse-decimal-names-in-dimacs.md) · [DIMACS syntax](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~babic/doc/dimacs_cnf.pdf)

The proof uses a virtual hard encoding tailored to arithmetic on the original words. Sparsity of the **set** of word values alone would not justify an embedding of their additive Cayley graph on a multidimensional tape. Different integral combinations of those words can denote the same RAM address. Here the embedding is defined on addresses themselves and preserves all such identifications.

## A fixed number of byte boundaries

Start with the locally computable bounded-verifier 3-CNF for SAT on a source of length $n$, with $n\log^{O(1)}n$ clauses and $K=\Theta(\log n)$ source index bits. Increase the number of clauses to the next power of ten by appending copies of a fixed positive clause on three fresh variables. This preserves satisfiability and increases the clause count by at most tenfold. Choose the target bit length $N$ to be a public power of two of order $n\log^{O(1)}n$ with enough room for the layout below, before determining $W=w/8$ bytes per guest word. A sufficiently large fixed power of the logarithm suffices, since the body needs only $O(KW)$ bytes per clause. The prescribed $w$ is polylogarithmically computable from $N$ and is $\Theta(\log n)$. Padding fixes the file length exactly. [Locally indexed source construction](packed-band-transfer.md)

Use the aligned names and records of the constant-alphabet equality encoding. A name is a leading `1` followed by $K$ blocks, each consisting of $W-1$ ASCII zero digits and one selected binary digit as an ASCII decimal digit. The sign and leading one are in the preceding aligned word, with spaces in its other positions. A fixed terminator word ends each clause. The body admits only a fixed number of word types, each with a fixed number of runs of equal bytes. Pad the public header to a word boundary. Its largest declared name consists of a leading one and $K$ repetitions of the same length-$W$ block. Every aligned word strictly inside this periodic part has the same rotated byte pattern. The clause count now consists of a one and a run of zeros. The fixed header text, endpoints of its two numerals and header padding contribute only constantly many transition positions beyond the periodic pattern. The unused body capacity consists of spaces terminated by a newline; the final partially filled word is binary zero-padded. These last two boundaries add at most two cuts. All positions can be computed from public sizes, without examining the SAT input. [Aligned literal and header construction](communication-bound-for-public-crossed-scans.md)

Consequently there exists a public set $0=b_0<b_1<\cdots<b_m=w$ of **bit** offsets of uniformly bounded cardinality, such that every original guest word consists of one repeated byte in each of the byte-aligned fragments between cuts. Initially choose just the union of all possible byte-run boundaries. The source-dependent choice of a sign or bit can change the byte in a fragment; it does not introduce a cut outside that union. If an initial register supplies $N$, its bit representation is a power of two, so insert its bit offset as one further cut when needed. This split is used only as an additional numerical radix boundary; the original repeated-byte formula continues using the old byte cuts.

## Address coordinates and the physical head

Put $Q=2^w$. For any interval of identical bytes of value $v$ between byte offsets $l$ and $u$, its contribution to **255 times** the little-endian word value is exactly $v(256^u-256^l)$. Thus for every original word $a$ there are bounded signed coefficients $k_j$ such that

$$255a\equiv\sum_{j<m} k_j 2^{b_j}\pmod Q.$$

The word zero and fixed program literal $c$ have the same representation with a program-dependent bound. Since $x$ is either initial, loaded from the original, or loaded from a marked cell, these are all needed displacements except the optional initial values $x=N,w$. The extra bit cut handles $255N$. For $x=w$ the coefficient at $b_0=0$ is only $O(\log n)$, still within the allowed polylogarithmic overhead.

The map $y\mapsto z=255y\bmod Q$ is injective since 255 is odd. Express $z$ in mixed-radix digits $z_j$ with radices $R_j=2^{b_{j+1}-b_j}$. For $z'\equiv \sigma z+\sum_j k_j2^{b_j}\pmod Q$, $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$, let $A$ bound $|k_j|$. Starting with carry zero, the carry after digit $j$ is the floor of $(\sigma z_j+k_j+\mathrm{carry})/R_j$. Its magnitude is at most $A+2$ inductively since $0\le z_j<R_j$ and every radix is at least two. The resulting digit has cyclic distance at most $2A+2$ from $\sigma z_j$ on the $R_j$-cycle. This handles addition, either subtraction order, zero updates, and wraparound.

Store the physical tape head at coordinates $d_j=\min(z_j,R_j-z_j)$. Circular distance to zero is 1-Lipschitz on a cycle and is invariant under negation. Hence each step changes every physical coordinate by at most $2A+2$, including a reflection of the virtual address. The sign specifying which of $d_j$ and $-d_j$ is the actual digit selects a track. Break ties canonically at 0 and the antipode. There are at most $2^m$ tracks, a constant, so the coordinate/track pair identifies each address exactly. Translate the physical coordinate system by the initial folded vector so the tape head starts at its required position. The tape may be indexed in both directions; a signed-axis encoding and a further constant number of tracks implement this convention on nonnegative axes as well. The machine stores only a mark bit for each guest address. A LOAD sees either $c$ or the virtual input word, determined by that bit.

The simulator keeps the true registers in polylogarithmic auxiliary storage, computes both folded tuples for an update, and moves the physical head between them in polylogarithmic time. Public cut data and local input generation use polylogarithmic space and time per guest probe. It pads the number of dimensions to a single constant upper bound, independent of $n$. Thus a native $O(N^a)$ SAT solver of the stated form would give an ordinary SAT solver of time $n^{a+o(1)}$ with one fixed-dimensional tape, random access to the read-only input and polylogarithmic auxiliary workspace. At every fixed dimension $m$, Williams's Theorem 7.5 supplies a threshold $r_m>1$ and excludes $O(n^c)$ on this hybrid model whenever $c<r_m$. Choose $1<a<c<r_m$ so that the simulated $n^{a+o(1)}$ clock is $O(n^c)$. [Williams, model in §7 and Theorem 7.5](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf)

## Coverage of mixed arithmetic at six slots

For one LOAD, one STORE, one ALU and three branch sites, suppose the ALU uses both distinct mutable registers. Bitwise operations, single-direction shifts/rotations and comparisons already fail the compulsory-address test on the bounded-palette periodic bases, regardless of scheduling. For arithmetic addition, subtraction or multiplication, all actual data remain even on those bases unless the STORE injects a fixed odd literal. A STORE payload from a register or from even metadata consequently misses odd compulsory addresses. If a fixed odd $c$ is injected, all $x$ lie in the initial palette plus $c$ and initial register words. A self-addressed LOAD then misses compulsory addresses; a STORE addressed by $x$ or by a fixed word modifies only a sparse dictionary on the hard encoding. Multiplication keeps $y$ even because $y$ is itself an operand. The only remaining mixed arithmetic case is the additive marker machine above. Thus all allocations with a single ALU reading both mutable registers are excluded at some fixed exponent above one in this six-slot split. Unary updates and arbitrary positions of the third branch are separate issues.
